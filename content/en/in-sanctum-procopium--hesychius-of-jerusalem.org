* On Saint Procopius
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of Hesychius, presbyter of Jerusalem, an encomium on the holy martyr Procopius
the Persian; and on the response of the psalm: "Precious in the sight of the
Lord is the death of his holy ones," and on the saying of the apostle: "To
those who love God all things work together for good," and on the apostolic
saying: "Behold I send you as sheep in the midst of wolves" and what follows.
1 Christ crowned the sacrifice for Procopius, having seen him a bull
exceedingly fair and very manly and gold-horned by the multitude of the
virtues, leading the herd of the martyrs dear to him, and from authority
goring the enemies of the flock, by sight alone and by voice striking down the
untamed and murderous lions, by faith and zeal putting to flight the
many-spotted leopards of the demons, by courage and philosophy scattering the
various kinds of beasts that belch forth our death, having risen for us a
bright spring after the gloomy winter of error, and having been shown a
disciple of Paul by the deeds, and carrying the theology of John everywhere,
having imitated the hot mind of Elijah the Tishbite, having practiced from a
child the calling of Samuel as that one did, greater than Samson (for he did
not corrupt the locks of the Spirit), a parallel to David, since he cut off
the head of the many-headed Goliath, and having imitated also the ark of Noah
in the chest of the virtues, having denied as Abraham attachment to house and
race and fatherland, having become a whole-burnt offering like Isaac before
the slaughter of the martyrdom by the mortification of the body.
2 Jacob wrestled with God, but Procopius for God, and he raised a solemn
trophy throughout the whole inhabited world. Moses then crushed one gold-idol
calf, and destroyed many thousands of Jews blasphemous of the law; but
Procopius not so, but he wounded all the carved images of the idols without an
axe, and having netted many nations and cities and lands with a prophet-voiced
tongue he has offered them to Christ. He was pure as Enoch, according to Job
"blameless, true as that one and righteous and God-fearing and abstaining from
every evil deed"; for the boldness of the confession established him as true,
and the conduct of the life presented him as righteous, the end of the
contests proclaimed him God-fearing, "abstaining from every evil deed" the
abstinence from pleasures, the continence of the belly, the rejection of
luxury, the narrowness of the supper, the mortification of the body, the
many-formed and many-parted and many-mannered discipline.
3 He was then pure as Enoch, according to Job blameless; for the one who dines
on the accusations of sinners and belches forth the slanders of the righteous
was able to accuse or blame nothing of him, he did not dare to say upon him:
"Does Procopius worship the Lord for nothing?" For he did not see flourishing
flocks of sheep belonging to him, nor did Procopius possess burden-bearing
herds of camels. And from where were there wandering she-asses for the martyr?
From where yokes of oxen ploughing the earth, for one who left not even a clod
of earth for himself? But neither did the common slanderer dare to bring these
things and revile the endurance of the martyr: "Skin for skin, all that a man
has he will pay for his soul." For was the martyr wearing a chorus of sons or
a crown of daughters? Did he have children of household-born slaves, offspring
of concubines, a body flourishing in health? Was not bread his food and water
his drink? Was not the fasting continual? Was not the supper narrower than a
fast? Abel likewise, having died, was pleading against Cain; and the earth had
the body, but heaven the mouth, Hades the tongue, the judge the voice, the
clay the clay, the merchant the pearl, yet having died he was pleading against
Cain. But Procopius not so, but he intercedes with God for the enemies and
stands as advocate, he is ambassador for Greeks, he entreats for Jews, he
hastens to mediate for Samaritans and after death he does good to the
adversaries and reforges the ears of the deaf among them, he makes the sun new
for the blind, he stretches out the feet of the lame, and those who formerly
could not move their toes are seen running; for lepers do not even approach
the tombs, but before they come near they are stripped of the scales, and
having been cleansed they stand by the bridegroom as dancers for a bridal
chamber, as bridal escorts.
4 But who is sufficient to praise Procopius in order? Which of his contests
shall we take up first? Which shall we set second? Which shall we fit as
third? And where shall the fourth lie? What fitting place shall a fifth find?
What sixth crown shall we complete for him? What seventh shall we weave among
the prizes? What eighth ladder of praise shall we ascend? What ninth tower of
encomia shall we forge? Which of the words shall we be able to run past as
last? Error held the human race captive; Adam had been deceived, but he did
not receive perception of the deceit; God was not God with him, but the stones
were gods, the maker was not maker, but makers were the works of hands, the
carvings of the fingers, the iron forms, as many as the smith blew and the
smelting purified, whose belly the anvil hollowed, whose back the hammer
broadened, it peaked the shoulder and raised the neck, it forged the head and
set on a skull, whose graver deepened the hearing and arched the nostrils,
outlined the lips, carved the eyes, shaped the pupils, plaited locks, circled
brows and composed eyelids. From this the enemy skipped wantonly over life;
"You shall not murder" was played with, "You shall not commit adultery" was
laughed at, "You shall not steal" was hung up, "You shall not bear false
witness" was thought to be nonsense and a myth, "You shall not desire whatever
belongs to your neighbor" was some hostile commandment to life, and the law
was alien to the way of living.
5 But Procopius, having come forward into the midst, discloses a common
exhortation to those who are drunk: "Men, you are brothers. Why do you wrong
one another? For what reason are you ignorant, being slain by one another's
swords? And in return for what do you not know, each being shot with his own
arrows or those of the neighbor? Give me the eyes, you who are blind in
judgment, or rather first grant the feet, you who are lame in understanding.
Why do you compare the divine to idols and liken the maker of all to the
cattle and compare the one who breathes into creation to the lifeless, and
rank the planter of the Word among the irrational? And why, having left
virtue, are you drunk with pleasures? Gold is not ours, but of the earth which
we received, we had it to tread upon. Silver is a possession of clay; and what
will clay be able to benefit the rational soul? The wealth of garments is food
of moths and a deceit of the eyes imagining in vain and herding the man. Was
not the much-talked-of rich man clothed in fine linen? But a furnace and a
smelting of limbs and a fire-blazing pan of the tongue succeeded the fine
linen. Was not Herod clothed in a royal garment? But worms dined on the robe
together with the flesh. How many gold-purple tunics did Pharaoh possess? But
he is buried naked in the Red Sea. But you, if you desire purple, practice
temperance. If fine linen concerns you, accomplish righteousness. Exercise
endurance; for it is a royal necklace. Work almsgiving; for the loom of this
is woven upon the earth, but is cut in heaven. If you hasten to bind the high
crown about the head, pursue humility; for nothing more glorious than this has
been appointed with God; for this neither flee temptations nor beware of
dangers nor refuse the sweats nor fear death."
6 Do not flee temptations; "for blessed is the man who endures temptation." Do
not refuse persecutions, since "Blessed are those who have been persecuted for
the sake of righteousness, for theirs is the kingdom of the heavens." Do not
beware of danger; "For the dangers of Hades found me, and I called upon the
name of the Lord," says David, and as an ally he met him from the heavens. Do
not fear death; for just now you have heard the church singing "Precious in
the sight of the Lord is the death of his holy ones," and very reasonably. For
the farmer in no way endures to have confidence in the wheat before the
harvest, since he both suspects the cankerworm and fears the locust, he has
been afraid of the rust, he does not cease suspecting the casts of hail, he is
in agony at the wounds of the wind-borne blow; but when the wheat is taken by
the sickle and comes under the hand of the reaper and is added to the sheaves
and is carried with the straw on the wagons and arrives at the threshing-floor
and the winnowing-fan goes up and, having been winnowed, is kept sealed inside
the storehouse, then he has confidence in it and rejoices as having become
rich, he is proud as not having toiled in vain; and at one time he will open
storehouses and loosen the bolts and fling open the doors, he will receive;
and at another time, having set the wheat into the midst among the merchants,
he will boast of the labors of his own hands. In the same way then the sower
of the race, the farmer of our lump, does not have confidence in Adam, until
he sees him transferred from earth, until he passes through the temptations,
until he rides through the stadium of the dangers for the sake of philosophy.
7 But when he has departed from the earth, then he rejoices over him and has
confidence and is proud and says to him: "Well done, good and faithful
servant." Then he adds: "Enter into the joy of your Lord." Then he addresses:
"Come, you blessed of my Father." Then by the experience of the good things he
presents that "to those who love God all things work together for good";
whether affliction besieges the man, or distress holds the faithful, or
nakedness holds, or dangers surround, or a sword has been sharpened, none of
these will be able to separate the faithful from Christ, none to divide the
soldier from the king. For if affliction besieges, they rejoice seeing Christ
the president of joy; and if distress holds, looking toward the teacher they
are broadened; and if persecution is laid on, expecting heaven they do not
care for the earth, but they make themselves secure so as not to strike the
feet; and if famine is laid on, handling the bread of life they are filled;
and if nakedness holds, they have put on the Spirit, they wrap themselves in
grace and reasonably do not care for garments; and if danger has been
threatened, they know the general, they recognize the one who fights with them
and reasonably they do not endure to bridle the boldness of the word; and if a
sword has been sharpened, the tongue does not endure to be silent even having
been sacrificed; and they are ready toward each of the things that come upon
them as harmless lambs and innocent sheep and lambs, receiving with pleasure
the command of the shepherd: "Behold I send you as sheep in the midst of
wolves," that is, take courage and go, hasten with speed, gather the nations,
increase the herds, throw the wolves into confusion, display the power of the
shepherd.
8 You are not less than Moses nor second to Jeremiah nor have you been
entrusted with a worse grace than Ezekiel. With you is the one who sent
Elijah; this one will send you also as each of the prophets. See then who
Moses was and to whom he was sent, and what sort of trophy was raised and
accomplished by that one; the lawgiver was naked and was sent to respecters of
persons, but the rod of the naked one subjects the things of the armed and
masters camps; but you, instead of full armor, breastplate yourselves with the
next commandment: "Become therefore prudent as the serpents and innocent as
the doves"; fair is the prudent, and likewise marvelous is the innocent, but
joined to each it possesses the beneficial and the useful; and neither bare
prudence nor mere innocence is able to justify and save the man, and for this
reason it is necessary to acquire the prudent of the serpents and together the
innocent of the doves. For the serpent is prudent, but is not innocent, and
reasonably you behold the beast as a venomous thing to be rejected; the dove
is only innocent and for this reason easy for all unto a plot. You then mix
the innocent with the prudent and become innocent in the things of life,
prudent in the spiritual. If you buy a field or sell or transact some other of
the things of life, pursue the innocent; for to be wronged is more splendid
than to wrong, to be cheated more profitable than to cheat, for those who wish
to gain the heavenly rather than the earthly. But if you study the law, then
belch forth prudence and seek the aim of the things written, this how, and
that in return for what, what the letter of the law wishes, what the spirit of
prophecy has commanded, whether the word is simple, whether the reed of
teaching is set down in parables. And attend to what other signals besides
these you receive of the army and generalship on behalf of the one who sends.
9 "And they will deliver you to councils," but take courage, the Father is not
separated, the Son is not far off, the Spirit does not withdraw, angels
escort. "And in their synagogues they will scourge you." But you rejoice
greatly; for the wars on behalf of piety and the weals from the scourges are
more fair than the sun's rays. "And you will be led before governors and kings
for my sake, for a testimony to them and to the nations." Do not grow numb
even at this; for my sake you stand before the governors, for my glory you are
brought to kings. With you then is the one who anointed the kings, in you the
one who appointed bounds for the governors, who measured the tribunal for
these and granted the scepter to those. And still consider this also, that you
are not so much becoming ones to be led to kings and governors, but for a
testimony to them, clear to the children of the Jews, but indeed also to the
nations, that they may know the power of the one testified among you, that the
poor bridle the rulers, rustics put the consular council to shame and spit
upon the much-talked-of senate, private men put tyrants to shame and make
kings turn. "And when they deliver you, do not be anxious how or what you will
speak"; for you are tongues of the Word and pipes of wisdom and heralds of
life and orators of the one who has no need of orators, and for this reason
the following accompanies you: "For it will be given you in that hour what you
will speak." Before the hour the word of grace is not supplied to you, lest
you think the words brought from you to be your own; "For you are not the ones
speaking, but the Spirit of your Father that speaks in you"; for the contest
is for him, the prize through him; for his sake is the stadium. It is
necessary then to wait from him for the grace, to expect the word, to receive
the grace of the confession, to hope for the full armor, against which the
enemy cannot, because it is forged from God.
10 "Brother will deliver brother to death and father child and children will
rise up against parents and will put them to death." Nature is divided and
grace is gathered; the clay campaigns against itself, but the maker does not
endure to deliver up the clay. The earthen things strike against one another,
that they may come into need of the potter's wheel and thus receive both a
remembrance of the potter and a compulsion toward him. "And you will be hated
by all because of my name"; sufficient for you unto glory, enough unto
dignity, not small unto confidence, that you are hated by all; but the one
over all does not endure to betray you, but remains loving and warms the love
more hotly; "For you will be hated by all because of my name; but he who
endures unto the end, this one will be saved."
11 Hear the word, O faithful one, and keep the commandment and farm endurance
from eagerness and harvest the ear from it with much gladness. Vintage the
grape, tread the cluster in the winepress. Do not say many things: "The
terrible things are various, the grievous things hard, the things that hold
gloomy; the things from the fatherland rejoice, but the things found in a
foreign land are sullen; those who comfort are few, but many those who trip
up; philosophy is reviled and virtue is mocked; fear holds me concerning my
household, a contest of kinsmen has fallen upon me, the care of my own
besieges me." Do not say these things, do not reckon, nor loosen the sinews of
endurance, nor farm for yourself the sheaves of faintheartedness, but look
with eagerness toward this kingly cross, behold it and after this spy out this
holy bridal chamber, the Resurrection. Paint for yourself the king hanging
upon wood, a master unjustly slapped by the slaves, a lawgiver shamelessly
spat upon by those under account, the one rich in every wrapping of the robe
shamelessly stripped. But behold, in what things do you see the resurrection?
Here the linen cloths of the one who was stripped show the wealth, angels will
present the power of the one who was slapped, the voices of those who came
down from the heavens declare the glory of the one who was struck; to him be
the glory now and always and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
