FROM THE EXPOSITIONS OF DIDYMUS ON THE PROVERBS OF SOLOMON. From the
commentaries of Didymus on the Proverbs of Solomon. =39.1621= A proverb is
such as, "War is sweet to the inexperienced," and, "A drop continually falling
hollows a rock." The proverb has been spoken: For in the beginning, while the
paths were still unmarked, what are now called miles among the Romans, then
called signs, the ancients used to set at certain places of the road, and on
these they would inscribe certain riddles. They did this for two reasons: both
that the traveler might know the measure of the road, and that, reading the
inscription and occupying himself with its clarification, he might be relieved
of the toil. For a path (oimos) is called the way.

=39.1624= V. 2. "To know words of prudence" is properly of virtue, clearly,
just as the remaining translators also make clear, having rendered it not "of
prudence" but "words of understanding." Or he calls "words of prudence" the
sophistic and craftily devised ones. V. 3. The word, convicting those who put
on the appearance of righteousness and say they have it as they ought, says:
"If then you truly speak righteousness, judge uprightly, you sons of men." V.
6. I hear Theodotion's translation, which Symmachus also issued, that they
rendered "problem." What then is the dark word? That which is confused both in
word and thought and phrasing; such as that in the prophet Isaiah: "Woe to the
land of ships, beyond the rivers of Ethiopia! He who sends hostages by sea,
and papyrus letters upon the water." For none of these has either a clear
diction in parables, nor indeed an evident narration of history. And of the
riddle it is possible to understand the literal sense, but of the dark word
not even the literal. "Dark word" is a generic term; but the parable is
specific; and "dark," not that according to deprivation of light, but that
which is hidden, so that the one who is ignorant is darkened. And "sayings"
are the well-phrased utterances; but a riddle is a word signifying the truth
with concealment; such as, "Open, O Lebanon, your gates, and let fire devour
your cedars." V. 7. For true fear is the reverence according to veneration;
and understanding is good. One must punctuate here. For it is not enough for
blessedness to know the good, unless one also does it; and piety toward God is
the beginning of understanding. V. 8. Father of the righteous is God. For
everyone who does righteousness has been born of God. And our mother is the
Church, whose bridegroom is our Lord Jesus Christ. And ordinances are the
apostolic constitutions. Even if the things set forth have an anagogical
sense, yet they are also applied to parents according to nature, when being
godly they rear the children. And the teacher being a man, who has begotten
those being instructed through the Gospel, his yokefellow and mother of those
born is the Church, or rather the ecclesiastical mind and way of life. If
then, he says, you hearken to me, the proverb-writer, now as instructor and
father, and now to wisdom and virtue in place of a mother, you will be crowned
with a crown of graces, and you will adorn your neck with a collar, fashioned
of intelligible gold and ornament. For being the material of a crown, that
with which the head of the inner man is wreathed, the compass of the virtues,
happen to be named graces. In agreement with the crown, which is intelligible,
one must also take the golden collar placed upon the neck of the soul, that
is, upon its submissive part. V. 9. And he calls the mind head and neck; and a
golden collar, knowledge and the intelligible ornament of the soul; or a
golden collar is the subjection to God, and the compass of the divine oracles.

=39.1625= V. 10. Even to sin together with others brings punishment; yet
impious men are those who are complete in vice. V. 11. They hide in the earth
those whom they persuade to mind earthly things; and you will understand a
righteous man as the one who has a heavenly mind. And these things can also be
understood of Christ and of the people of the Jews. Scripture indicates
"enough" through "to swallow," as in: "lest such a one be swallowed up with
excessive sorrow." They say then: Having taken down and conquered the one who
has a heavenly mind, let us transfer him to ourselves, so as to mind earthly
things. And he is swallowed alive who has a right opinion concerning what
ought to be done, and has cast this away. V. 11. And he calls counsel the
choice according to which we choose to do the things that ought to be done, or
not; and thought that concerning the things contemplated, so as to leave them
behind, and to be made familiar with God, to keep his commandments as far as
power allows. V. 3. He who has the virtues in a state, not superficially
neglecting them, this one has them written upon the tablet of his own heart,
that is, upon the depth of his own soul, or of the continual desire, so that
the one from the opposite side may not turn you aside. V. 8. I call body the
assemblage of the powers of the soul; bones, its firm powers; for all our
bones will say: Lord, Lord. V. 16. Her right hand is the knowledge of divine
things, from which is eternal life; and her left, the knowledge of human
things. V. 18. Here again he names the cross a tree of life. For to those
sincerely attached to Christ the diverse life drips from the tree; and for
those who rest upon its foundations a strong confirmation has been fixed
unswervingly, as also for those securely established upon the Lord. And if the
wisdom of God is a tree of life, Adam has justly been forbidden to partake of
it. For wisdom will not enter into a soul that works evil; since from fruits
of righteousness a tree of life grows. And not having found to what he might
liken the one who hopes in the Lord, he turns the word upon himself, speaking
as upon the Lord: For the one who rests upon her, inasmuch as he hopes in God,
is secure. V. 20. This also now fits the Son of God, who is wisdom and
prudence of God. But also the faithful soul is earth, bearing fruit a
hundredfold; and this, from the richness of wisdom, becomes wise, God the
Father founding it upon her. For he establishes in it the introduction of the
teaching, making a fixing from wisdom in it, and showing it always advancing;
and he works heaven as a most pure creature, a dwelling of angels and of the
intellectual essences. And having received a perception of that which is, and
contemplating the precision of things, such as the great =39.1628= depth of
the judgments of God, he called these clouds, or he called the prophets
clouds, the teaching dew. Do not come to be outside the drinkable water of
life. And he calls counsel the movement concerning things to be done; thought,
that concerning the contemplative things; he called the same soul a neck,
which has borne the yoke of the Lord. And just as the neck wears the
breast-bands, so the soul will receive the prizes of God in the kingdom, as
also in this life his commandments. Even the glorious things of the impious
have been cursed; and even the more paltry things of the righteous have been
blessed. And he calls house the powers of the rational soul; and folds, those
of the irascible and the appetitive; for in them the irrational passions spend
their time. V. 8. Honor of wisdom is the keeping of the commandments; and
wisdom is Christ, whose bride is the soul that honors him through the virtues,
which will be embraced by him. V. 20. Just as for some infant child he always
sets prizes for attentions, and stirs up the one that has been frightened
somewhere outside. And springs of life are the virtues, from which is the
living water, the knowledge of Christ.

To the one advancing according to wisdom, many springs come to be, and not
one; but according to the multitude of the virtues. To those who keep these
exhortations, he says, they become life; these are the prize. V. 26. Walk the
course of life rightly, he says. Feet of the soul are its transmissive
impulses; for virtue is a mean.

V. 2. Do not attend to a deceitful teaching, which baits through fair speech,
that is, to heresy, and to idolatry, and to vain words. V. 3. Their good is
only as far as the hearing. For such is deceit: it is accustomed to hide the
destructive, and to put forward the pleasant, so as to catch; for otherwise
they would have leapt away at once. But God is not so; but he hides the
pleasant, and sets forth the toilsome. And he did not say, "It flows away,"
but, "It drips," so that the bitterness may not become cloying, yet later more
bitter than gall; not as much as the pleasant, so much the bitter; but far
more. V. 8. He called way here the mind journeying toward virtue; or perhaps
he commands us to separate virtue from vice. V. 11. That is, in old age; or
through the wearing away of the flesh he indicates incontinence as being a
certain corruption. V. 12. 14. He introduces the one who has been corrupted
blaming himself, as having hated instruction, and he says: I would have
perished and been corrupted, having worn away my flesh in prodigalities, and I
all but gave up life itself. Wherefore my punishment became manifest in the
midst of Church and Synagogue. For it is the voice of one who has repented,
and has not come to be in that in which he had all but come to be. Similar
also is, "My steps had almost =39.1629= slipped." For having come to his
senses and perceived, he says, Where is the almost? How then is it possible to
console the madness from nature? V. 15. Knowledge is both a well and a spring;
for those approaching the virtues it is a well; for those perfected according
to them, a spring. And knowledge is called both mother, and wife, and sister;
mother, because through it we have been begotten by the teacher; wife, because
dwelling with us it begets virtues and right doctrines; sister, because we
have come to be from one God. And I said wisdom is a sister. V. 16, 17. See
how he shows the ease not only by the abundance, but also by having no partner
in the enjoyment; and he doubles it and calls it a possession; for he knows
the pleasure of the thing. V. 8. Even if you imitate the natural work of the
irrational creatures; or "know," that is, go.

For many do good works from contemplation or hearing, and not from knowledge.
He calls bees prophets and apostles, whose labors are useful both to those
great in state and to the small; now he calls wax the deeds; and honey, the
contemplation of these. Just as the ram leads the she-goats, so the king-bee
the bees. Order then is a good thing throughout all of life. V. 12. Fool is
the godless, who has lost the contemplative; and lawless, who has lost the
practical. V. 1. Keep in common, that it may be yours, he says, both to live
eternal life, and to become clear-sighted. For far brighter than the light of
the eyes the keepings of the divine commandments shine forth for us. V. 3.
Hand, the complete action; and finger, the more partial. And being persuaded
by me you will have a broad heart for the reception of the virtues. V. 19. She
pretends to dwell with a husband, as living according to law; and she takes
away all the fearful and hindering things, putting forward the absence of the
one dwelling with her. V. 27. For her house is ways of Hades, leading down
into chambers of death. For those persuaded by her, becoming earthy through
her and earthly, will thus perish. And "upon a snare of Hades," the others
said "in depths." V. 22. The beginning, signifying many things, as we have
said, now indicates the eternal, and the cause, and the productive. And a
beginning is a beginning of certain things, and signifies a relation, but not
a coming-to-be of essence. For relatives are of this kind; as the right and
the left. The wisdom of God then, being essence, is not a beginning of certain
things; for even before creation it has being wisdom. But when it receives the
relation toward the generated things, then it becomes beginning of the
productive and providential ways of God, unto the works which =39.1632= have
their existence from the Creator, which contributed nothing to it toward being
wisdom; but the relation according to which it is called beginning and creator
then came to subsist, when also the things that came to be. For a beginning
does not preexist the things of which it is beginning; nor are these said to
exist after that, if the creatures have their essence in time. For being
eternal, wisdom is not subject to time.

The beginning then is yoked with the creatures. The Son of God, preexisting
the creation, being wisdom, if he says: "The Lord created me," let him not
have the sense as a coming-to-be of essence, but unto the relation toward the
creatures. For he says he has been created for works, in order to be beginning
of the productive and providential ways of God, that is, cause, and still to
speak by way of introduction. The Son of God became man, having taken the form
of a servant; and being before the ages and eternal, inasmuch as he is God the
Word, he is said to have been created because he had the birth from Mary,
having become flesh; so that those wishing to journey according to God and
toward God may use him as teacher, providing a pattern of the best life and
teaching to those who follow. And that "he created" does not indicate in every
case a coming-to-be of essence, David makes clear saying: "Create in me a
clean heart, O God." For he asks not as not having, to receive, but as having
soiled it, to take it up again cleansed. And Paul too, saying that the two are
created into one new man, indicates not a coming-to-be of essence of men, but
a union from concord; just as those who translated rendered "He acquired me."
V. 23. Instead of, He laid down both cause and foundation of the creatures;
according to, "All things consist in him, and he is before all." And the Lord
himself also says: "I am the beginning of the creation of God." For as wishing
to be foundation of the Church, he endured to become man, that it might be
established upon him unshaken; so also wishing to grant being to the creation,
he came to be according to it, introducing the aforementioned relation,
becoming for the things that would be beginning and cause; but even if, he
says, I was created according to these things, yet before all God begets me,
which is to be Son. V. 27. He calls throne his own kingdom, and winds the
intellectual spirits; or as also David: "Who walks upon the wings of the
winds;" wishing to present the lighter and incorporeal of his essence. V. 29.
See them dividing the works, wisdom and God. He was making, he says; and I was
fitting. And how strong? Not needing any under-support; for by will alone of
God they are held. V. 35. Either that together with appearing I provide life,
or that by a certain revelation condescending toward that one's =39.1633=
weakness, then he comes forth; for being life, and having come to be outside
for those able to see him, he gives life; and he says will or love, or that he
made us self-determining so as to be ready to do what one ought. V. 2. Let him
turn aside, that he may receive prudence; for the incomplete is strengthened
by the words and mysteries of wisdom. V. 5. The same food is named both meats
and bread and milk and wine. Yet the foolish are said to partake of them as of
bread and as of mixed wine; and if this, how shall we understand, "Man ate the
bread of angels"? And understand bread as the solid commandments of God, and
wine, the knowledge of him from study of the divine Scriptures; and likewise
also his divine body and precious blood. V. 5. The naturally intelligent
hears, "The sun shall not burn you by day, nor the moon by night." He calls
the cares of life heat; and those dried by it he calls grass; and the Savior
through the parable indicates the afflictions; and the things against the word
and the persecutions he called heat. V. 13. The wise man by a rebuking word
brings the heavy-hearted to perception. V. 7. Do you see that it is not
desiring that is wicked, but desiring in such a way that is wicked? And even
the hopes of the impious are wicked; for everything that he will hope is
destruction. V. 8. The "instead of him" is not as the pious being subject to
the judgment, but since the snare stands from the wicked against the
righteous, the pious is delivered from it by God, and the impious, abandoned
by the divine providence, falls in it; or that since the righteous is subject
to certain sins as a man, and the impious having come and done evil to the
righteous, delivered him from the sins and the coming punishments, and
received the retributions upon himself, and he is handed over not by someone
else, but by his own vice, as having previously handed himself over to a
reprobate mind.

=39.1636= V. 2 The "better" is not comparative, but instead of "good." V. 9.
The practical man and unlearned is more honorable than the one in word and
elegant and without practice. V. 12. The roots of the righteous will not be
taken away, that is, the good states. V. 17. Whatever he says, he says, is
confirmed by the works; and if it is more trustworthy than a work; or instead
of, manifest, tested, irrefutable, sufficient to exhibit it; and a witness of
unjust things is the one who attempts to establish whatever unjust things he
does; and deceitful, as having nothing straight. V. 25. The account of the
punishments disturbs the one just now attending the practical life; but that
of the promises gladdens. For the perfect righteous man will not fear from an
evil report, having the fear of the Lord that surpasses all. V. 26. The
righteous, knowing human weakness, forgives, even if what is from the friend
is a sin; or that recognizing that the friend is according to the image of
God, he is in agreement with himself, being moderate, and guarding himself,
and having nothing of the hostile vice, he has his reasonings unfactious. V.
2. For the one will have the end of virtue, and the others will not have ripe
fruit; and he will startle himself, not finding to stand with his own sin. V.
14. Law is the natural, from which the cause of life gushes; for a law is not
laid down for a righteous man; and punishment unto death for the one trapped
in evils; the spring of life, the works of the commandments. To the righteous
Gentiles the wealth of those of the circumcision will be added. V. 10. The
mind perceiving sin pains the soul; and houses of the impious are states and
dispositions; and tents are the choices; and there is a way that seems to be
right, that which prefers the seeming things of those that are. He considers
what ought to be done with endurance. They will have a portion, or they will
share; for the bold would not be a coward. V. 20, 23. This seems to me as not
having been set in the order of exhortation, but he reports the things that
happened; then correctively what follows; the, "He who dishonors a poor man
sins;" for the evils there are clear; nor does anyone correct, he would not
condemn such things; and who would praise the others? The one who cares for
virtue abounds in practice and knowledge. For indeed the one always
considering the things to be done will have something more; but the pleasant
and unfeeling, that is, the one who turns away from the labors, will be in
want of the virtues. V. 16. The "nest," sometimes signifies the place, and
sometimes =39.1637= the animals nesting. Either then the place of wisdom, the
contemplations in which it dwells, is more choiceworthy than knowledge falsely
so called; or the offspring of wisdom. For just as gold is better than silver,
so also wisdom than prudence; for the one is in knowing, and the other in
interpreting the things known. Or nests of wisdom, the Churches, or the
dwellings of the saints from there; and wisdom is Christ. V. 17. For having
the uninterruptedness of the actions, they have the corresponding length of
life, turning aside from evils, that is, from death. V. 29. Here he calls a
lawless man the devil, who makes trial of human beings, wishing to know who
among them are like him and wishing to act lawlessly; and leading them away
into ways, that is, into deeds not good; and he is the same who fixes his eyes
upon each of human beings, and understands accurately toward what passion
someone is inclined; and having known from the movements, he sets his snares;
and having caught he leads him away into all the evils. And he said "to
determine with the lips," because by his wicked suggestions he is eager to
complete through human beings in deeds all the things dear to him; and this
one is the furnace of vice, which burns the one who has received his baits
into all vice. V. 30. An eye seeing good things, that is, discerning; and what
the good things are must be inquired; and not seeing gold, it is grieved; or a
shining body, again it is melted; but what the good things are, hear: The
heaven, the earth, the sea and all the creation; so that you look at such
things as gladden the soul; since what is the profit for the eye to be
delighted, and nothing more to come to be, but the soul to be corrupted? V.
31. Having said the many things of his vice above, we will add to the things
said the remaining. With all his excesses he says also these things to those
persuaded by him: This matter is of necessity (having sharpened the seeing
faculty toward the evil), one must use it. Pleasure is natural, one must use
it; money is from God, one must enjoy it. And whenever he has persuaded his
subject in all things, then he makes him boast in thought, as doing great
righteousnesses, and that he suggests these things among the aged. V. 6. The
faithful, not hastening about the sensible things, is as having all; but the
one hastening about these is as having nothing; for loving silver, he will not
be filled with silver; or also, The faithful will see the words of this world,
as money of the mind spoken tropically; but the unfaithful will not even touch
a chance matter of the words because of uncleanness of soul. V. 28. Speechless
is the one who by silence hides his own ignorance, or the passion, with
himself or with those nearby, it seems.

=39.1640= V. 7. He does not complete all vice (for it is impossible), but he
does the things that can coexist; and still doing evil he did not cease. Or he
calls Christ a good thought; for he himself pledges it; and everyone who does
good things will find him; for he calls prudent the one who does every good
work. V. 24. The one who has not lived rightly hides in the soul the hands
unjustly; not wishing to work his own earth, how will he be filled with
breads? For the practical virtues hold the place of hands, bringing to our
mouth the bread that came down from heaven. This one then cannot even in the
beginning be benefited, being sluggish. V. 21. That is, the judgment covers
the defense of the impious. For God exceeds those who do well with the
recompenses. V. 26. Do not give yourself over to postponing repentance, being
ashamed of someone. For if you die, and do not have a time to repent, you will
no longer have rest. V. 23. And he introduces beside himself the envious
devil, and eats his own morsel with him, who with vainglory, or man-pleasing,
does any of the good works; for such a one has his reward. For indeed the
devil makes them manifest to human beings, that through the praise toward the
man-pleaser he may infect and deprive of the eternal goods. V. 20. The wicked
will not beget virtues and right doctrines (for these are offspring of the
soul), because they do not fear the Lord; but those who are in the fear of the
Lord will have offspring all the day, and their hope will not depart. And
these things follow the things said. For the enemies, he says, being wicked,
rejoiced at the falls of others; what you blame, do not envy. For their
seeming brightness and deceitful is as far as this life, and is quenched at
the end; and often even while they still live, having been convicted, it was
quenched. V. 7. The wise man says these things to wisdom, or the servant of
Christ to the Lord. And having said two, he asks concerning more; but the
seeming first is a request beforehand of obtaining the things he asks, saying:
Do not refuse to hear me, but give the grace; upon which he added the first
request: A vain word, the falsely named, make far from me, lest being filled I
become false. Satiety makes arrogance, and seeming to be above reproof; in
word and deed as sinless. For the, "Who sees me," instead of, "Reproves," he
says; lest being filled with wisdom, I seem to the others to be
incomprehensible, or also I seem a liar speaking the things above these. V.
15. The leech is born in the sweet of the =39.1641= waters, being a certain
worm-like animal; and it is most smooth to the touch, which, to those who take
of the water with open mouth, enters unnoticed by its smoothness; then having
wrapped itself inside some of the parts, it is not broken off before, until by
the drawing out of the blood it kills the human being; for it is a drinker of
blood, and from this it is fed and grows. To this he has now likened the
devil, who, not being able to hold human beings by force; for he uses
especially three bases of attack against them, pleasing, charming, deceiving,
and through this destroying them little by little unnoticed; for first he
leads them by the ways of false opinion toward destruction, which indeed he
also calls Hades, showing by the end of the judgment the greatness of the sin;
then wrapping them with the pleasant-feeling of incontinence, which indeed he
also calls love of women. Upon these with love of money, or greed, which
indeed he also called earth not filled with water; for such is the sandy,
insatiable. These then are the passions, which have been invented by the
wicked one toward the destruction of human beings, which he so loves and
cherishes, as his own daughters. And the fourth he calls the wearing and
custom of these; for not the one who once held a false opinion, or fornicated,
or desired more, immediately fell under the sufferings, but the one who by
custom has at last been defeated also in perception; for you would find such
those buried in the false opinions, never taking satiety, but always
stretching the passions further, so as also to urge others toward these. Just
as then both water and fire by the supply of the fitting things do not cease,
nor say, Enough, but rather become increased; and the water by the inflow of
water does not cease, but is increased; which things also you, he says, O
child, knowing, keep away from these, lest unnoticed by little, as by a most
smooth leech, you be stripped of life by pleasure. V. 19, 20. Tracks of a
flying eagle, the soul of the wise, which, being wise, is unable of itself to
know the flights of the intelligible eagle; concerning whom it has been
written: The great eagle and long in the stretch and full of claws, not having
a track of the presence of the devil, even if he flew forth to me from where I
do not know; for when the wicked one declined from me, I did not know, by my
not having a longing toward him. Such is a way of an adulteress, the
adulterated teaching. V. 22. The people from the nations, being formerly a
slave of sin and of the devil; thus: "Upon a foolish nation I will provoke
you"; and, A fool will be filled with the foods of life; and, A hateful woman,
which is a harlot; for a harlot was the Church from the nations, going after
foreign gods; and a maidservant casts out her own mistress, the Church of the
nations the Synagogue being mistress; for of this the law and the prophets
have need. Of the intellectual examination the one according to the letter is
a slave, which the Jews preferred as mistress; but the nations, transferring
the literal into the spiritual, cast out that which was as mistress among
those, the bodily observance of the law. V. 25. He says the nations, who have
no strength, and they store up for themselves through good works the eternal
=39.1644= life. And the hyraxes; a kind of fish, or the hares called gadflies,
the formerly weak nations, having fled to Christ; for a rock is a refuge for
the hyraxes, who made in rocks. And you then, he says, even if you are a weak
animal, take hold of the rock of the true faith, and direct your life toward
it. Resting upon deeds he enters into the kingdom. Therefore you also, he
says, do not because of the weakness of the faith disbelieve the kingdom of
the heavens, but believing the promise concerning it, pursue the commandments
that produce it. Lion is Christ; for all the rational ones are cattle toward
him; for Moses says: "And I am irrational (7)." The natural activity is said
to pass through unhindered. V. 31. The one offered for sins, and walking on
high, has the flock following him. "For when I am lifted up," he says, "I will
draw all to myself." The cock bears the person of a teacher, who sowing the
word in the souls, is strongly announcing the light. "Repent; for the kingdom
of the heavens has drawn near." This was the one from the beginning awakening
the hearts of human beings, and through many things pre-announcing the day of
his incarnate presence; and pressing out darkness, and clearly pointing out
the eternal light to all. Walking among females courageously, that is, in the
Churches, courageously and nobly, as he says: "Behold I am with you all the
days until the completion of the age." V. 32. Or also if you let yourself down
into the mind of the Scriptures; or, if you do not have a learning ear, but
are contradictory, will you hear? "But if anyone is contentious, we have no
such custom." V. 3. You shall not give your wealth to pleasures or foreign
teachings. Drink wine with counsel. Drinking the wine of the true vine, the
interpretation of the Scriptures, and the contemplation of the things that
are, approach with much discernment the examination of the thought. V. 6. Now
he called grief the labor. Those then who toil and are pained because of God,
whenever they are made drunk with the richness of the house of the Lord,
