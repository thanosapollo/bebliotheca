Origen's letterOrigen's letter to Africanus.
Origen to Africanus, beloved brother in God the Father through Jesus Christ
his holy child, to fare well.
Your letter, through which I was learning the things you indicated concerning
Susanna as she is current in Daniel in the Churches, seems indeed to be
somewhat brief; but in few words it has many problems, of which each needed
not a chance working-out, but so much as to overstep the epistolary character
and to have the compass of a treatise. And I, considering as far as I am able
the modest measure of my own mind, that I may not be without perception of
myself, see that I fall short of the accuracy required for the reply to your
letter; so that not even a few days of the sojourn in Nicomedia sufficed for
me toward =11.49= writing to you even according to the present diction
concerning the things you inquired and demanded of me. Wherefore, granting
pardon both to my modest measure and to the straitness from the time, read the
letter with all goodwill, contributing if anything has been left out by us.
And first you were saying that when I was conversing with our companion
Bassus, and was using the Scripture of Daniel who prophesied as a younger man
concerning the things according to Susanna, I did this as if it escaped me
that the part of the book was counterfeit. And you were saying that you praise
this pericope somehow as something graceful; but you were saying that you
blame it as being a recent and fabricated writing; and that, as not even
Philistion the mime, having invented the little writing, it used the
neighborhood of the names, of holm-oak (prinos) toward sawing (prisis), and of
mastic (schinos) toward splitting (schisis). Which things according to the
speech of the Greeks seem to be such, but according to that of the Hebrews not
at all. Know then toward these things what we must do, not concerning the
things according to Susanna only, current in the Greek according to the Greeks
in every Church of Christ, but not set down among the Hebrews; nor concerning
the, as you were saying, other two pericopes at the end of the book written
concerning the things according to Bel and the dragon, themselves also not
written in the Daniel of the Hebrews; but also concerning countless others,
which according to our modest measure, comparing our copies with the Hebrew
copies, we have found in many places. For in Daniel itself concerning the
burning furnace we found "bound"; but in our =11.52= copies there are not a
few verses overflowing beyond those in the Hebrew, of which the beginning
according to one of those current in the churches was thus: "Ananias and
Azarias and Misael prayed and hymned the Lord;" as far as: "Bless, all who
revere the Lord, the God of gods. Hymn and confess, for his mercy is forever,
and unto the age of ages. And it came to pass when the king heard them
hymning, he was also beholding them alive." And according to others: "And they
walked in the midst of the flame hymning God and blessing the Lord;" as far
as: "Bless, all who revere the Lord, the God of gods. Hymn and confess, for
his mercy is unto all the ages." And there was joined in the Hebrew: "And
those three men, Sedrach, Misach, Abdenego, fell into the midst of the fire
bound;" and: "The king Nabuchodonosor wondered, and rose up in haste, and
answered and said to his nobles." For thus Aquila, serving the Hebrew diction,
has published it, saying it; being more ambitiously trusted among Jews to have
interpreted the Scripture; whom those ignorant of the dialect of the Hebrews
are especially accustomed to use, as having succeeded more than all. And our
copies, whose diction I also set out, one was according to the Seventy; the
other according to Theodotion; and just as among both there was set the thing
concerning Susanna, a fabrication as you say, and the last pericopes in
Daniel;
so also these things in verses, to speak by conjecture, amounting to two
hundred and more. 3. And in many other holy books too we have found, here more
things set down among us than among the Hebrews, here things lacking. And for
the sake of example, since it is not possible to take in all, we shall set
=11.53= forth a few. For instance from Esther neither the prayer of
Mardochaeus, nor that of Esther, which are able to edify the one who meets
them, are current among the Hebrews; nor even the letters; nor even the one
written to Amman for the destruction of the nation of the Jews, nor that of
Mardochaeus in the name of Artaxerxes releasing the nation from death. But
also from Job, the things from: "And it is written that he will rise again,
with those whom the Lord raises," as far as the end, are not set down among
the Hebrews; wherefore neither with Aquila; but with the Seventy and
Theodotion the things equivalent to one another. And countless others too we
have found in Job, both by a little and still more in our copies than in those
among the Jews, by a little indeed, when "rising early he was offering
sacrifices concerning them, according to their number," the: "one calf for sin
concerning their souls." =11.56= And when "the angels of God came to present
themselves before God, and the devil came with them," the: "Going about the
earth and walking about in it." And further it was not among the Hebrews
after: The Lord has given, the Lord has taken away: "As it seemed good to the
Lord, so also it came to be." And more in ours than the Hebrew, when his wife
said to Job, from: "How long will you endure? saying: Behold, I will wait yet
a little time, expecting the hope of my salvation," as far as: "that I may
rest from my labors and from the pains that now hold me;" for only the sayings
of the wife have been written: "But say some word toward the Lord, and make an
end." And again very many things through the midst of the whole of Job are set
down among the Hebrews, but not among us; and often four verses or three, and
sometimes even fourteen and nineteen and sixteen. And why must I catalogue the
things we collected with much labor, in order that the difference of the
copies among the Jews and among us may not escape us? And many such things we
also observed in Jeremiah, in which we also found much transposition and
interchange of the diction of the things prophesied. And in Genesis too the:
"God saw that it was good," upon the firmament coming to be, is not found
among the Hebrews; and this is not a chance problem among them. And other
things too it is possible to find in Genesis, to which we have set =11.57=
beside them the signs called among the Greeks obeli, that such a thing may be
known to us; as again asterisks, to the things set down in the Hebrew, but not
found among us. And why must I speak concerning Exodus, where the things about
the tent and its court, and the ark, and the garments of the high priest and
of the priests, have been altered at length, so that not even the sense seems
to be similar? It is time then, if such things do not escape us, to set aside
the copies current in the Churches, and to legislate for the brotherhood to
put away the sacred books brought forward among them, and to flatter Jews, and
to persuade them that they may give us a share of the pure ones, having no
fabrication. And did Providence, having given in holy =11.60= Scriptures to
all the Churches of Christ edification, not take thought "for those bought
with a price, for whom Christ died;" whose Son being, "God did not spare," the
love, "but delivered him up for us all, that with him he might graciously give
us all things"? And toward these things consider whether it is not good to
remember: "You shall not move eternal boundaries, which your predecessors
set"? And these things I say not from hesitation to search both the Scriptures
according to the Jews, and to compare all ours with those, and to see the
differences in them. If at any rate it is not burdensome to say, we have done
this to a great extent, as far as we are able, exercising our mind in all the
editions and
their differences, together with practicing somewhat more the interpretation
of the Seventy; lest we should seem to counterfeit anything for the Churches
under heaven, and give pretenses to those who seek starting-points, who wish
to slander those in the midst, and to accuse the things that appear in common.
And we practice not to be ignorant of those among them also, so that,
conversing toward Jews, we may not put forward to them the things not set down
in =11.61= their copies, and so that we may use together the things current
among them, even if they are not set down in our books; for if our preparation
toward them in the inquiries is of such a kind, they will not despise, nor as
is their custom laugh at those from the nations who believe, as being ignorant
of the true things even written among them. Let these things then have been
said toward the things concerning Susanna not being current among the Hebrews.
And let us see also the charges you bring to the word. And first let us begin
from that which would have been able to put to shame toward not receiving the
history; which is the thing concerning the paronym of holm-oak (prinos) toward
sawing (prisis), and of mastic (schinos) toward splitting (schisis);
concerning which you indeed declared, as having grasped in what manner in
Greek utterances such things happen to sound alike, but in the Hebrew they
stand apart entirely; but I still doubt; since, having taken thought for the
things according to the place, because I too was at a loss in them, I set it
before not a few Hebrews, inquiring how among them the holm-oak is named, and
how they say "to saw"; and further into what they take over the mastic the
plant, and how they name "to split." And they were saying that they were
ignorant of the Greek utterance of the holm-oak and of the mastic; and they
were demanding that the trees be shown to them, so that they might know what
utterances they themselves assign upon these. And (for the truth is dear), I
was not at a loss to present the woods to them by sight. And another was
saying that the things of the Scriptures not named anywhere he is not able to
affirm how they are said in Hebrew; and that it is rash for the one at a loss
to use a Syriac utterance instead of the Hebrew; and he was saying that even
among the very wise some diction is sometimes sought. If then, he says, you
have something to present, the mastic how it was ever named in some Scripture,
or the holm-oak, from there we would find the thing sought, and the paronym
beside them; but if it was named nowhere, such a thing escapes us too. These
things then having been said, so far as it is not a history, by Hebrews with
whom I mixed, I am cautious to declare whether also among the Hebrews the
equivalence of the paronyms according to these things is preserved, or not;
but how you affirmed it, you yourself perhaps know. I remember at any rate
having mixed concerning more things with a Hebrew fond of learning, and styled
among them son of a wise man, reared =11.64= to succeed his father; from whom,
as the history concerning Susanna not being set aside, I was also learning the
names of the elders, as set down in Jeremiah having this manner: "The Lord
make you as he made Sedekias, and as Achiab, whom the king of Babylon roasted
in fire because of the lawlessness they did in Israel." How the one is sawn by
an angel, and the other is split? It must be said that these things are not
prophesied to them according to the present age, but unto the judgment by God
after the departure from here. For as the wicked steward saying: "My lord
delays to come;" and for this reason giving leisure to drunkenness, and eating
and drinking with the drunkards, and beating the fellow-servants, the lord
having come "will cut in two, and will set his portion with the unbelievers;"
so also these, having been styled elders, but having administered the
stewardship badly, analogously to that wicked steward being cut in two there,
certain angels appointed over the punishments will dispose these things; and
the one will split "one grown old in evil days, who judged unjust judgments,
and condemned the innocent, but released the guilty;" and the other will saw
as "being seed of Chanaan and not of Judah, the one deceived by beauty, and by
desire having the heart distorted." And I know another Hebrew too, bearing
such traditions concerning these elders, that to those in the captivity hoping
to be freed through the sojourn of Christ from the slavery under the enemies
these elders were pretending as knowing how to make clear the things
concerning Christ; and each of them in turn, with whichever woman he met, and
whom he wished to corrupt, was saying in secret as it were that it had been
given him from God to sow the Christ; then, being deceived by the hope of
bearing =11.65= the Christ, the woman was giving herself to the deceiver; and
thus the elders Achiab and Sedekias were committing adultery with the women of
the citizens. Wherefore well by Daniel the one has been called "grown old in
evil days;" and the other heard: "Thus you were doing to the daughters of
Israel, and they, fearing, were consorting with you; but a daughter of Judah
did not endure your lawlessness." For perhaps deceit and fear, being able in
the women, was making them furnish their bodies to these so-called elders. But
it is likely that toward these things you will inquire why then the history is
not current among them in Daniel, if, as you say, their wise men hand down
such things concerning it. And it must be said toward these things, that as
many things as they were able of those containing accusation of elders and
rulers and judges, they took away from the knowledge of the people, of which
some are preserved in apocrypha. And of this we will give an example, the
things related concerning Isaiah, and witnessed by the Epistle to the Hebrews,
written in none of the manifest books; for going through concerning the
prophets and the things they have suffered, the one who wrote to the Hebrews
says: "They were stoned, they were sawn, they died by slaughter of the sword."
For we will inquire to whom is referred the "they were sawn," according to
some ancient custom not only Hebrew but also Greek, said in the plural
concerning one. And it is clear that the traditions say that Isaiah the
prophet was sawn; and in some apocryphon this is current; which perhaps has
been deliberately falsified by Jews, they having interpolated some utterances
that are not fitting into the writing, so that the whole may be disbelieved;
but it is likely that someone, being pressed from the demonstration unto these
things, will use together the will of those who set aside the Epistle, as not
=11.68= written by Paul; toward whom we need other words privately for the
demonstration that the epistle is Paul's. I will then set beside for the
present from the Gospel the things witnessed by Jesus Christ concerning the
prophets, and a history witnessed by him of prophets, and a history spoken by
him, but not current in the old Scriptures, since it too contains accusation
of the lawless judges in Israel; and the diction spoken by the Savior has
thus. "Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites, because you build the
tombs of the prophets and adorn the monuments of the righteous, and you say:
If we had been in the days of our fathers, we would not have been partakers of
them in the blood of the prophets. So that you witness to yourselves that you
are sons of those who murdered the prophets. And you fill up the measure of
your fathers. Serpents, offspring of vipers, how will you flee from the
judgment of gehenna? For this reason behold I send to you prophets and wise
men and scribes. Some of them you will kill and crucify, and some of them you
will scourge in your synagogues, and you will persecute from city to city, so
that there may come upon you all righteous blood poured out upon the earth
from the blood of Abel the righteous as far as the blood of Zacharias son of
Barachias, whom you murdered between the temple and the altar. Amen I say to
you, that all these things will come upon this generation." And the next of
these too is not out of tune with the things set forth, having thus:
Jerusalem, Jerusalem, who killed the prophets and stoned those sent to her;
how often I wished to gather your children, in the manner a bird gathers her
nestlings
under the wings, and you did not wish; behold your house is left to you
desolate." And let us see at any rate whether it must not be said, in these
things, that the Savior speaks truly, but the Scriptures are not found, as
many as make clear the things related by the Savior; for those who build the
tombs of the prophets and adorn the monuments of the righteous, having
condemned the things dared by the elders against =11.69= the righteous and
against the prophets, say: "If we had been in the days of our fathers, we
would not have been partakers of them in the blood of the prophets." Let
someone then tell us, of which prophets' blood? For where has anything of such
things been written concerning Isaiah, or Jeremiah, or one of the twelve, or
Daniel? But also concerning Zacharias son of Barachias murdered between the
temple and the altar, we have learned from Jesus, but we have known it from no
other Scripture. Wherefore I think nothing else is being administered, than
that those thought to be wise, and rulers, and elders of the people took out
such things, as many as contained accusation of them before the people.
Nothing then wonderful, if also the history concerning Susanna, being true,
plotted against by unbridled elders, those not far from the choice of those
elders stole away and took out from the Scriptures. And in the Acts of the
apostles too Stephen, witnessing =11.72= upon many things, says these also:
"Which of the prophets did your fathers not persecute, and they killed those
who announced beforehand concerning the coming of the Righteous One, of whom
now you have become betrayers and murderers?" For that Stephen speaks truly
everyone whoever of those who accept the Acts of the apostles will confess;
but to show also from the Old Testament how reasonably he blames the fathers
of those who disbelieve in Jesus, as having persecuted the prophets and killed
them, is not possible from the current books of the New Testament; and Paul in
the former epistle to the Thessalonians, witnessing these things concerning
Jews, says: "For you became imitators of the Churches of God that are in
Judaea in Christ Jesus; because you also suffered the same things from your
own countrymen, just as they also from the Jews, who both killed the Lord and
the prophets, and drove us out, and are not pleasing to God, and are contrary
to all men; hindering us from speaking to the nations, that they may be
saved." And I think I have shown in the things set forth that there is nothing
out of place, that the history came to be, and that the cruelty with much
wantonness was dared by the elders then against Susanna, and that it was
written by providence of the Spirit, but was taken out, as the Spirit might
say, by the rulers of Sodom. In addition to these you were saying that Daniel
by this Scripture is said, having been seized by a Spirit, to have cried out
that the sentence was unjust, but the Scripture current as confessed from him
contains him prophesying in another manner, that is by visions and dreams and
an appearance of an angel; but nowhere by prophetic inspiration. But you seem
to me not to have observed very much the: "God having spoken of old in many
parts and in many ways to the fathers in the prophets," not only in all of
them, but also in those according to one. And having observed you will find
that to the same holy ones there have come both divine dreams and angelic
appearances, and inspirations. And it suffices for the present to use the
testimonies written concerning Jacob; who concerning divine dreams narrates
such things: "And it came to pass, when the sheep were conceiving, I also saw
with my eyes them in sleep, and behold the he-goats and the rams were going up
upon the sheep and the goats, white-spotted and speckled and ashen-sprinkled.
And the angel of God said to me in sleep: Jacob. And I said: What is it? and
he said: Look up with your eyes, and see the he-goats and the rams going up
upon the
sheep and the goats, white-spotted and speckled and ashen-sprinkled; for I
have seen as many things as Laban does to you. I am the God who appeared to
you in the place of God, where you anointed a pillar to me there, and vowed a
vow to me there. Now =11.73= then arise and go out from this land, and go away
into the land of your birth." And concerning an appearance of a superior one
while awake, these things have been said concerning him: "And Jacob was left
alone; and a man was wrestling with him until morning. And he saw that he is
not able toward him, and he touched the breadth of his thigh, and the breadth
of the thigh of Jacob grew numb, in his wrestling with him, and he said to
him: Send me away; for the dawn has come up. And he said: I will not send you
away, unless you bless me. And he said to him: What is your name? And he said:
Jacob; and he said to him: Your name shall no longer be called Jacob, but
Israel shall be your name, because you have been strong with God, and mighty
with men. And Jacob asked and said: Tell me your name; and he said: Why do you
ask this, my name? And he blessed him there. And Jacob called the name of that
place Form of God; for I saw God face to face, and my soul was saved. And the
sun rose when the form of God passed by." And that he also prophesies from
inspiration is clear from these: "And Jacob called his sons and said: Gather,
that I may announce to you what will happen to you at the last of the days; be
assembled and hear, sons of Jacob, hear Israel your father; Roubim my
firstborn, you are my strength and beginning of my children, hard to be borne
and hard, stubborn; you ran riot, do not boil over as water; for you went up
upon the bed of your father; then you defiled the couch, where you went up."
And likewise also toward the rest the blessings, being prophecies, have been
spoken from inspiration. Nothing then wonderful that Daniel too has spoken at
one time from inspiration the conviction against the elders, and at another in
dreams, as you too say, or also in a vision, and at another time an appearance
of an angel has come to him. The rest of your difficulties at any rate seemed
to me to have been said more unbecomingly, and not to hold of the reverence
fitting you; but it is not worse to set the thing written by you in the very
diction thus: "Then after having somehow thus uttered wondrously, he also
somehow convicts them most paradoxically, as not even Philistion the mime; for
the rebuke through the Spirit was not enough, but having set each of them
apart privately, he asks where he saw her committing adultery. And when the
one said under a holm-oak, he answers that the angel will saw him; to the one
who had said under a mastic, he likewise threatens that he be split." For it
is time to compare another thing like this said in the third of Kingdoms,
which you yourself too will confess to have been soundly written, to the mime
of Philistion. And the diction from Kingdoms has thus: "Then two women,
harlots, appeared to the king, and they stood before him. And the one woman
said: In me, my lord. I and this woman dwell in one house, and we gave birth
in the house. And it came to pass on the third day of my giving birth, this
woman also gave birth. And we were according to the same. There is not even
=11.76= one in our house except both of us in the house. And the son of this
woman died in the night, as she slept upon him. And she rose in the middle of
the night; and she took my son from my arms; and your servant was sleeping.
And she laid him to sleep in her bosom, and her son who had died she laid to
sleep in my bosom. And I rose in the morning to nurse my son; and that one was
dead. And behold I considered him in the morning, and behold he was not my son
whom I bore. And the other woman said: No; but your son is the dead, and my
son the living. And the other herself too was saying: No, but my son the
living; and your son the dead. And they spoke before the king, and the king
said to them: You say: This my son is the living, and this woman's son the
dead. And you say: No, but my son the living; and your son the dead. And the
king said: Take for me
a sword. And they brought the sword before the king. And the king said:
Divide the living child into two, and give the half of it to this woman, the
half of it to this woman. And the woman whose son was the living answered
toward the king, because her womb was troubled over her son, and she said: In
me, Lord; give her the living child, and do not put it to death with death.
And this one said: Let it be neither to me nor to her; divide. And the king
answered and said: Give the child to the one who said: Give it to her, and do
not put it to death with death; for she is its mother. And all Israel heard
the judgment which the king judged, and they feared from the face of the king,
because they saw that prudence of God was in him to do judgment." For if it is
necessary to declare scoffingly concerning the things current in the Churches,
it was necessary to liken the history concerning the two companions rather to
the mime of Philistion, than that concerning the reverend Susanna. And just as
it was not enough toward persuasion of the people for Solomon to say: "Give to
this one the living child; for she is its mother;" so the rebuke of Daniel
toward the elders was not enough, if the conviction from their mouth were not
taken on besides; they having said that they had seen her under some tree
lying with the young man, but not having agreed concerning what tree it was.
And since as having grasped it you were saying that from inspiration Daniel
had judged the things concerning the elders, perhaps convicting thus, perhaps
otherwise, attend; and these things according to Daniel seem to me to be like
the judgment of Solomon; concerning whom the Scripture witnesses that the
people saw that prudence of God was in him to do judgment. This could be said
also concerning Daniel: that there being prudence in him to do judgment, the
things written concerning the elders were judged. And this too nearly escaped
me, necessary to be set beside concerning the holm-oak to saw and mastic to
split; that also in our Scriptures there lie some as it were etymologies which
among =11.77= the Hebrews have it fittingly, but among us not likewise.
Nothing then wonderful, that those who interpreted the things concerning
Susanna managed to find either a name in accord with the Hebrew, for I am not
persuaded, or analogous to that which accords with the Hebrew, some paronymous
name. And how such a thing lies in our Scripture, we will present. Adam says
upon the woman built by God from the side of the man: "She shall be called
woman, because she was taken from her man." And the Hebrews say that the woman
is called "essa"; and that from the diction is made clear the "I took," as is
clear from: "Chos isouoth essa," which is interpreted: "I will take a cup of
salvation;" and "is" the man, as is manifest from: "Esre ais," which is:
"Blessed is a man." According to the Hebrews then is and essa of a man,
because from is her man she was taken. Nothing then wonderful that some,
having interpreted the Hebrew concerning Susanna, lying in secret among them
of old, as is likely, and being preserved among those more fond of learning
and more fond of truth, have either published the things of the diction
properly, or have found the analogue to the paronyms according to the Hebrew,
so that we Greeks may be able to follow them. For also upon many other things
it is possible to find some things published economically by those who
interpreted; which we have observed, examining all the editions together with
one another. =11.80= In addition to these you raise difficulty in the very
diction: "How, being captives in Babylon, being strangled, and thrown unburied
in the streets, as is related in the former captivity of Israel, their
children being taken away for castration, and their daughters for concubines,
as had been prophesied, were these judging concerning death, and these things
of the wife of Jehoiakim who had become their king, whom the Babylonian king
had made a colleague of the throne? And if not this man, but another Jehoiakim
of the people: whence did such a residence belong to a captive, and was there
a wide-spreading garden? And whence taking it were you saying:
Being strangled and thrown unburied in the streets; or, so far as is in my
knowledge, from Tobias? concerning whom we ought to have known that Hebrews do
not use Tobias, nor Judith; for they do not even have them in apocrypha in
Hebrew; as we have come to know having learned from them. But since the
Churches use Tobias, it must be known that even in the captivity some of the
captives were rich and were faring well. And Tobias himself says: "According
as I was remembering God in my whole soul, the Most High also gave grace and
form before Nemessar, and I was his buyer; and I was going into Media, and I
deposited with Gabael the brother of Gabria in Rhagae of Media ten talents of
silver." And he adds as a rich man: "In the days of Nemessar I did many alms
to my brothers. My breads I was giving to the hungry and garments to the
naked; and if I saw anyone of the race dead and thrown behind the wall of
Nineve, I was burying him; and if Sennacherim the king killed anyone, when he
came fleeing from Judaea, I buried them stealing; for he killed many in his
wrath." And consider whether the so great catalogue of the good deeds of
Tobias does not display much wealth and much abundance; and especially, that
he adds saying: "Having come to know that I am =11.81= sought to die, having
feared I withdrew, and all my belongings were plundered." But also another
captive, Dachiacharus the son of Ananiel brother of Tobias, was set over all
the accountancy of the kingdom of the king Acherdon, and over all the
administration; and it is said, "that Dachiacharus was cupbearer, and over the
ring, and administrator, and accountant." And Mardochaeus too was spending
time about the court of the king, and had such freedom toward him, that he was
even written among the benefactors of Artaxerxes. And further also in Esdras:
Neemias, cupbearer of the king and his eunuch, a Hebrew by race, asked the
things concerning the building of the temple, and obtained them; so as to be
entrusted to go away with more to restore the temple. What then wonderful,
that a garden and a house and a possession had come to be of some Jehoiakim,
whether it was costly or even moderate? For such a thing is not clearly shown
from the Scripture. But you say: "How were they judging concerning death,
being in captivity?" I do not know from where doubting that Susanna is a wife
of a king, because of the homonymy of Jehoiakim. And it must be said that
there is nothing paradoxical, great nations having become subject, that it has
been conceded by a king for the captives to use their own laws and the
law-courts. And now at any rate, Romans reigning, and Jews paying the didrachm
to them, as many things as the ethnarch among them is able, Caesar conceding
=11.84=, so as to differ in nothing from one reigning over the nation, we who
have experienced it know. And there even come to be judgments secretly
according to the law, and some are condemned to the [sentence] upon death;
neither with the entire freedom unto this, nor with escaping the notice of the
one reigning; and this, having spent much time in the country of the nation,
we have learned and have been fully assured. And yet under Romans two tribes
only are related to be, that of Judah and Benjamin; and let there be also the
Levitical. But he, Israel beside the people of Judah, was ten tribes; and it
is likely that the Assyrians, being content to have them subject, entrusted to
them their own law-courts. I found in your letter also this in the very
diction: "And upon all, of so many prophets who have gone before, and of those
next, no other has used another's spoken thought; for their word, being true,
was not impoverished; but this one, threatening the one of those two, reminds
you of the Lord saying: You shall not kill the innocent and righteous;" and I
wondered how, having many sojourns in the examinations and studies of
Scripture, you have not observed prophets using together words of prophets
almost in the very diction. For who does not know, even of the many faithful,
that there lies in Isaiah: "It shall be in the
last days, the mountain of the Lord shall be manifest; and the house of God
upon the peaks of the mountains, and it shall be lifted up above the hills,
and all the nations shall come upon it; and many nations shall go, and they
shall say: Come, let us go up into the mountain of the Lord, and into the
house of the God of Jacob; and he will announce to us his way, and we will go
in it; for from Sion will come out a law, and a word of the Lord in Jerusalem.
And he will judge in the midst of the nations, and he will judge a much
people; and they will beat their swords into plows, and their spears into
sickles; and a nation will not take a sword upon a nation; and they shall not
still learn to war." And the similar to all these, and in the same diction,
has been =11.85= written thus in Michaeas: "And it shall be at the last of the
days, the mountain of the Lord shall be manifest, ready upon the tops of the
mountains, and it shall be raised above the hills; and peoples shall hasten
upon it, and many nations shall go, and they shall say: Come, let us go up
into the mountain of the Lord, and into the house of the God of Jacob, and
they will show us his way; and we will go in his paths; because from Sion will
come out a law, and a word of the Lord from Jerusalem. And he will judge in
the midst of many peoples, and he will rebuke upon strong nations even unto
far, and they will cut down their swords into plows, and their spears into
sickles; and a nation shall no longer lift a sword upon a nation, and they
shall no longer learn to war." And further in the first of the Paralipomena
the psalm appointed in the hand of Asaph and of his brothers, of the praising
the Lord, whose beginning: "Confess and call upon him in his name," in most
things according to the beginning is equivalent to the 104th psalm, as far as:
"And among my prophets do not work evil;" and after this to the 95th from its
beginning having somehow near: "Sing to the Lord, all the earth;" as far as:
"Because he comes to judge the earth." And since to set beside the very
diction was much, we have said these things briefly toward the matter set
forth. And you will find also concerning not lifting a burden on the day of
the Sabbaths written not only in Moses, but also in Jeremiah. And concerning
the Pascha and priestly laws you will find both in Moses and in the last
things of Ezekiel. And I would have set beside the very diction too, and many
others, if I were not seeing the time of the writing toward you shut off by
the fewness of our days in Nicomedia. In addition to these you were saying
also that the character of the phrasing differs; which to me did not appear
very much. And these things I have answered; but it would have been primarily
after the charges to speak an encomium of the Scripture concerning Susanna,
going upon each diction, and showing the excellence of the things understood;
which someone privately of those who have studied the divine things with love
of learning and sufficiently will be able to compose. These things I have
answered toward the knocks, as you say, and I write in reply; would that I
could also instruct! but as it is I do not grant myself things so great. There
greets you he who contended together in the dictation of the letter, and was
present at all of it, having corrected in the things he wished, my lord and
holy brother Ambrosius. And his most faithful spouse Marcella also greets you
together with the children, and Anicetus. Do you greet our good papa
Apollinaris, and those who love us.
