* Oration to the Soldiers
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

..... hearing, O men, of the greatest glory that has come to you from your
deeds, I do not know what praise I am now to weave for you from an imperial
tongue. For such things have been told me concerning you, and such have been
announced through the report of my faithful servants. Those men made it known
to me exactly; those men, as lovers of truth, made known your excellence: how
great a courage, how great a charge, how great a nobility you showed against
the enemy, and how you were not fighting as against men, but bearing down on
them as on wretched women, and not finishing a contest and a war, but rather
making a sport of them. And yet they were mounted on horses of a speed not to
be overtaken, and fenced most strongly with weapons, weapons whose craft
cannot be copied, and they lacked nothing of all that makes for safety and for
terror. But since they were deprived of the one greatest thing, I mean the
hope in Christ, everything was proved empty for them and was in vain. For this
reason also, it is said, their dead became a spectacle upon the face of the
field, and as grass behind the reaper, and there was none to gather.

But you, taking courage in that hope and committing your own souls to it, set
up such trophies against the enemy and won such victories as have run through
the whole inhabited world, and have made you famous not in your homelands
only, but in every city. And now every tongue and every ear, the one tells
your wonders, the other is stirred to hear. I wish you still to take courage
in this, my own people, my strength, unconquered might, and to fight against
the enemy more eagerly than before; or rather, you will fight. This I know
clearly; for the very nature of things teaches me. For the man who has
contended against his adversary and has conquered does not deal with him
afterward as he did before; but having loosed all the fear that troubled him
before the trial, he charges with much boldness against one now clearly known.
Besides, you know that the enemy will not come with the same charge, having
already come to the trial of your courage, but they will shrink back and look
askance, and will take care not to suffer as those before them suffered. And
what now casts you into daring, that same thing wholly drives them into fear.

Take courage therefore, O my men, take courage, and fill your souls with
eagerness, and show the enemy what they can do who trust in Christ, and what
they who name Beliar, that is Muhammad, as their helper. For you become not
only avengers and champions of Christians, but of Christ himself, who is
wickedly denied by them. What then? Men know how to repay those who fight for
them; and will Christ not stretch out a hand to those who arm themselves
against his enemies? He, O men, he is our helper, who alone is mighty and
powerful in wars, whose sword is whetted like the lightning, whose arrows are
drunk with the blood of those who have stood against him, who shatters bows
and lays strong cities in a heap, who brings down the eyes of the proud, and
who teaches the hands of those who hope in him for war, and makes their arms a
bow of bronze, and gives them the shield of salvation.

Let us therefore set our whole hope on him; let us fence ourselves with his
cross in place of all weapons, with which you also, having armed yourselves
before, made some of Chambdan's mighty men the work of the sword, and
delivered others, like the Egyptians of old, to the water. Those the
abominable Chambdan had as those in whom he hoped; those we heard were his
whole arm and his might. You who so easily routed men so noble, what will you
prove against those left behind, who are useless and above all in terror and
afraid? From which perhaps the word spoken by the divine Isaiah does not
differ, that "the remnant shall be as a fleeing fawn and as a sheep, he says,
.. wandering."

He has not his strength from the truth; do not trust his crafts and his wiles.
He is terrible, he is a worker of evil crafts, having no firm power and
greatly fearing your assault and thrusting it off in every way; he tries by
wiles and phantasms to frighten your souls. Now he has it reported that
another force has come to him and that allies have been sent from elsewhere,
now that a mass of money has been dispatched to him from another quarter; at
another time he arranges for proud words to be noised abroad to the dismay of
those who hear. But all these things belong to a soul that is greatly afraid,
not to one that is confident; for if he had his confidence from the truth, he
would not have fled to these crafts and wiles. But now, lacking natural power,
he turns to inventions of craft. Do you not see this noble beast, the lions,
how having its mastery from nature it knows no wiles and invents no crafts?
But setting these things down as small and unworthy, and trusting in its
natural strength, it contends straight on against the adversary. But a fox,
and all such cowardly things, lacking true strength, desert to craft, and hunt
by wile, and by wile try to escape being hunted.

Would that it were possible to see his soul. Then you would have learned how
great a cowardice, how great a fear, besieges it, and how, hearing of your
power and suspecting the assault, he does not know what to become or where to
turn, even if he now pretends confidence and trust. Let these things not
trouble you, my people, nor give heed to his devices, but taking courage in
Christ rise up against the enemy. You know what a noble thing it is to fight
for Christians, how great a glory he who does this wins for himself; this is
more profitable than all else, this more illustrious than every other
ambition. O how great a longing for this holds me, how great a desire kindles
my soul, and I am already wholly given to the thing, and I dream of those very
days; I would rather put on a breastplate and set a helmet on my head, and
shake a spear in my right hand and hear a trumpet calling to the fight, than
put on diadem and purple and take a scepter in hand and hear imperial
acclamations. For these are given by God in ways he knows, and often even to
those who are not good; but those belong only to those who love virtue, only
to those who prefer glory to pleasure.

Nevertheless I shall not leave the zeal of those who fight without honor or
without reward. For I did not send my servants there in vain, but wishing to
use them as my own eyes, whom I shall now bind by oath, and I shall turn my
speech toward them. I adjure you therefore both by God and by our own head and
life, to prefer nothing before our love, or, better to say, before the noble
and the true, but to report to our Majesty concerning all things, how each man
stands in excellence and in eagerness. Or rather also to mark it in writing,
so that coming in here you may report it to us, that we may both see them with
pleasure and count them worthy of our praises and of our support; and that
those of the generals who command lesser themes be transferred to greater, and
those who command greater be honored with other gifts and rewards. And of the
tagmatic troops and of the others as well, whoever fight with good courage,
let each be honored according to his deed; and let some become tourmarchs,
some kleisourarchs, some topoteretai. And not of these only, but of the other
humble and common men as well, as many as show ways of excellence, let them
receive a worthy wage. But now we receive through you the assurance concerning
each man; and in a little while we shall have neither you nor any other
witnesses of such things, but our own eyes alone, and we ourselves being
present, ourselves seeing each man's excellence, ourselves shall also give the
prizes to those who fight.
