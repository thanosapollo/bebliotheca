* Fragment of a Synodical Letter
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

WE then keep the day unadulterated; neither adding, nor taking away. For also
according to Asia great elements have fallen asleep; which will be raised on the
day of the presence of the Lord, in which he comes with glory from heavens, and
will raise all the saints; Philip of the twelve apostles, who has fallen asleep
14 in Hierapolis, and two daughters of his, aged virgins; and his other daughter
who lived in a holy spirit, who rests in Ephesus; and still also John who leaned
upon the breast of the Lord; who became a priest having worn the petal, and a
martyr and a teacher; this one has fallen asleep in Ephesus. And still also
Polycarp in Smyrna both bishop and martyr; and Thraseas both bishop and martyr
from Eumeneia, who has fallen asleep in Smyrna. And why must one speak of Sagaris
bishop and martyr, who has fallen asleep in Laodicea? and still also Papirius
the blessed, and Melito the eunuch, who lived all things in a holy spirit; who
lies in Sardis waiting the oversight from the heavens, in which he will be
raised from the dead. These all kept the day of the fourteenth of the Pascha
according to the gospel; going out nothing, but following according to the canon
of the faith; and still I also the smaller of all of you Polycrates, according
to a tradition of my kinsmen, whom I also followed some of them. Seven were my
kinsmen bishops, and I the eighth; and always my kinsmen kept the day, whenever
the people took away the leaven. I then, brothers, having sixty-five years in
the Lord; and having conferred with the brothers from 16 the inhabited world;
and having gone through all holy scripture, I am not frightened at those who
strike. For those greater than I have said, one must obey God rather than men. I
could also remember the bishops who were present with me, whom you thought it
right to be summoned by me, and I summoned; whose names if I write, they are
many multitudes. And those who saw my small person agreed with the letter;
knowing that I did not bear gray hairs in vain, but I have always lived in the
Lord Jesus.
Eusebius brought this in book 5 Hist. cap. 24.
