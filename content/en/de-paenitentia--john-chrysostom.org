DISCOURSE ON REPENTANCE
=49.277= When he had returned from his country estate.
Homily 1.
1. Did you remember us, when we were parted from you this intervening time?
For I for my part was never able to forget you, but even having left the city
I did not leave your memory; but just as those who love splendid bodies,
wherever they go, carry about with them the longed-for sight, so also we,
having fallen in love with the beauty of your soul, always carry about with us
the comeliness of your mind. And just as painters, mixing varied colors, work
the images of bodies, so also we, mixing your zeal in the assemblies, your
eagerness about the hearing, your goodwill toward the speaker, all the other
successes as varied colors of virtue, and having sketched the character of
your soul, and set it before the eyes of the mind, we received sufficient
consolation of the absence through this imagination. And these things, both
sitting at home and rising, and traveling and resting, and going in and going
out, we turned continually, dreaming of your love; and not in day only, but
also in night we reveled in these imaginations. And what Solomon said, I sleep
and my heart is awake; this we also then suffered. For the necessity of sleep
was laying our eyelids down; but the tyranny of your love was waking the eyes
of our mind; and often I seemed to converse with you in a dream. For the soul
is naturally accustomed to imagine by night the things it reasons upon by day;
which then also happened in our case; and not seeing you with the eyes of the
flesh, I saw you with the eyes of love, and not being present to you in the
body, I was present to you in disposition, and my ears were always ringing
with your cry. Therefore even when the sickness of the body was compelling us
to spend more time there, and to enjoy the benefit of the air for the health
of the flesh, the force of your love did not endure this, but cried out, and
did not first cease from troubling, until it persuaded us to rise even before
the fitting hour, and advised us to count your company as health and delight
and every good whatsoever. And we, persuaded by this, chose rather to return
still having the remnants of the sickness, than, having cleanly put off the
weakness of the body, to grieve your love still more. For even while spending
time there I was hearing your accusations, and continual letters were bringing
these things to us; for I was attending to those accusing not less than to
those en49.278 comiasting; for those censures were of a soul that knew how to
love. And for these things I rose and ran; for these things I was never able
to cast you out of my mind. And what wonder if I, spending time in the
country, and enjoying leisure, was remembering your love, when Paul, wearing
chains, and dwelling in a prison, and seeing countless dangers hanging over
him, as if spending time in a meadow in the prison, so remembered the
brethren, and writing said: As it is just for me to think this about you,
because I have you in my heart, both in my bonds, and in the defense and
confirmation of the Gospel? Outside a chain from the enemies, and inside a
chain of the affection of the

disciples; but the one outside had been forged from iron, the one inside had
been born from love; and that one he often even put off, but this one he never
broke; but just as women who have taken experience of birth-pangs and become
mothers are continually bound to the children born, wherever they are; so also
Paul, or rather even much more vehemently than these, was always nailed to his
own disciples, and so much the more, as the births of the spirit are hotter
than the natural births. For this man too was in birth-pangs not once, but
even twice for the same, and cried saying: My little children, whom I again
travail. And yet a woman would never suffer this, nor would she endure the
same birth-pangs again, but Paul endured this, which it is not possible to see
in nature, both to take up again those once born, and to endure the sharp
birth-pangs over them. Therefore wishing also to put them to shame, he said,
Whom I again travail; all but saying: Spare me; no son a second time stretched
the maternal womb with birth-pangs, which you compel me to suffer; and yet
those birth-pangs strain in one moment of time, and when the child has slipped
from the belly they go out together; but these not so, but they remain even
whole months; for often a whole year he travailed, and Paul did not bear those
conceived; and there the pain is of the flesh, but here the pangs do not
stretch a belly, but they rend the very strength of the soul. And that you may
learn that these birth-pangs are hotter, who ever prayed to endure Gehenna for
the children born? But this man not only chooses to endure Gehenna, but even
prays to be anathema from Christ, so as to be able to bring to birth the Jews,
whom he was always and continually travailing with, and since this did not
happen, he was in pain saying: I have great grief, and unceasing pain in my
heart. And here again: My little children, whom I again travail, until Christ
be formed in you. What could become more blessed than that womb, which was
able to bear such children as to have Christ in themselves? And what more
fruitful, which brought forth the whole inhabited world? And what more
powerful, which was able to take up again those born and grown who had fallen
into abortion, and to shape them from above? For this =49.279= is impossible
in the natural births. And why did he not say: My little children, whom I
again regenerate, but I travail? And yet elsewhere he says he begets: For in
Christ Jesus I begot you. There then he wished only to show the kinship; but
here he was eager also to present the pain. And how does he call children
those not yet born? For if he was in travail, he has not yet given birth; how
then does he call them children? That you may learn that he was not travailing
these first birth-pangs, which was enough to put those men to shame. For I
became, he says, a father once, and I endured the birth owed over you, and you
too became children once; how then do you throw me again into second
birth-pangs? The pains of the births from the beginning are enough, why do you
stretch me again with the pangs? For the falls of the faithful were furnishing
him no less pain than those not yet having believed. For it was unbearable,
after the communion of such mysteries, to see them deserting to impiety;
wherefore also very sharply and more passionately than any woman he was
wailing, saying: My little children, whom I again travail, until Christ be
formed in you. And he was saying this, wishing at once both to encourage and
to frighten; for the showing that Christ was not formed in them was throwing
in agony and fear; but the hinting that it is possible for him to be formed
was again preparing them to take courage. For the saying, Until he be formed,
is of one showing both these, both that he has not yet been formed, and that
it is possible to be formed again. For if it was not possible, he was speaking
to them in vain, Until Christ be formed in you, and he was feeding them with
vain hopes.


2. These things then knowing let us also not despair, but also not be simply
remiss; for both of these are deadly. For despair does not let the one lying
down rise, but remissness makes the one standing fall; that is accustomed to
deprive of the goods procured, this does not let one be delivered from the
evils lying upon; and contempt even brings down from the heavens themselves,
but despair leads down into the very abyss of vice, just as not despairing
makes one run up quickly from there too. And consider the strength of both:
the devil was good before this; but having been remiss and having despaired he
fell into so great vice, as no longer even to rise; for that he was good, hear
what he says: I saw Satan as lightning fallen from heaven. And the comparison
of lightning shows both the brightness of the former way of life, and the
sharpness of the fall. Paul was a blasphemer, and a persecutor, and an
insolent man; but when he was eager and did not despair, he both rose and
became equal to the angels; but Judas was an apostle, but having been remiss
he became a betrayer. The robber again after so great vice, since he did not
despair, entered into paradise before all the others; the Pharisee having
taken courage was brought down from the very height of virtue; the
tax-collector not having despaired was so set upright as even to run past that
man. Do you wish me to show you also a whole city having done this? The city
of the Ninevites was thus saved entire; and yet the sentence threw them into
despair; for he did not say that, If they repent, they will be saved, but
simply, =49.280= Yet three days, and Nineveh will be overthrown; but
nevertheless with God threatening, and the prophet crying, and the sentence
having neither delay nor a condition, they did not fall, nor did they betray
the good hopes. For on this account he did not add a condition, nor did he
say, But if they repent, they will be saved, in order that we also, whenever
we hear a sentence of God being brought without a condition, may not even so
despair, nor give up, looking to that example. And not through this only is it
possible to see the loving-kindness of God, that not having added a condition
to the sentence, he nevertheless was reconciled to those who repented, but
also from the very bringing out of the sentence completed. For wishing to
increase the fear and to take hold of their much remissness, he did this, and
the very time of the repentance shows his unspeakable loving-kindness. For
what did three days work so much, as to make so great vice vanish? Do you see
how from here too the care of God appears? For this contributed the more into
the salvation of the city. These things then knowing, let us never despair;
for nothing is so strong a weapon to the devil as despair; therefore we do not
so gladden him by sinning, as by despairing. Hear then in the case of the one
who had committed fornication, how Paul feared the despair more than the sin;
for writing to the Corinthians he was speaking thus: It is actually reported
among you fornication, and such fornication as is not even named among the
nations; and he did not say, As is not even dared among the nations, but, Is
not even named; for what is unbearable to those even as far as the name, this
has been dared by you in deed. And you are puffed up? And he did not say, And
that man is puffed up, but having left the one who had sinned, he converses
with those who are healthy; just as physicians do, having left those who are
sick, they use more speech toward those belonging to them; and otherwise also
these were causes of all the madness to him, not rebuking nor striking. He
shared then the accusation, in order that the healing of the wound might
become easy. For to sin is terrible, but much more difficult is even to think
great things upon the sins. For if to be puffed up upon righteousness is an
emptying of righteousness, much more this happening upon the sins will bring
last harm to us, and


of their sins the accusation will be greater. Therefore he says: When you
have done all, say that We are unprofitable servants. And if those who do all
ought to be contracted, much more is it worthy for the one who has sinned both
to mourn and to number himself among the last. Which then also making clear he
was saying: And did you not rather mourn? What do you say? another sinned, and
I shall mourn? Yes, he says; for we are bound to one another after the manner
of a body and members; and in the case of the body even if the foot receives a
wound, we see the head bending down. And yet what is more august than this?
But it does not look to the dignity in the time of the disaster; so do you
also. Therefore Paul also exhorts: To rejoice with those rejoicing, and to
weep with those weeping. Therefore he says also to the Corinthians: And did
you not rather mourn, in order that he who did this work might be taken out
from the midst =49.281= of you? He did not say And did you not rather be
eager, but what? Did you not rather mourn, as of a common disease and a plague
having seized the city; all but saying: There is need of prayer and confession
and supplication, in order that the sickness may be driven from the whole
city. Do you see how much fear he hung over them? For since they were thinking
that the terrible thing stood as far as that man only, he sets them in agony
saying: Do you not know that a little leaven leavens the whole lump? And what
he says is of this sort: The evil going by a road will also touch the
remaining members; so then as counseling about common evils, thus one must
take thought. For do not tell me this, that that man alone sinned, but
consider that, that the terrible thing is a certain rottenness, and it grazes
upon all the remaining body. And just as when a house is burning, even those
who have not yet received the evil press no less than those who are in the
disaster and contribute all things, so that the fire pressing on may not come
also upon their own doors; so also Paul wakes these saying: It is a certain
fire, let us anticipate the terrible thing; let us quench the burning, before
it seizes the Church; and if you despise the sin as having happened in
another's body, first even so it is badly; for that man is a member of the
whole body. 3. But nevertheless consider that, that if you are remiss and
overlook, it will sometime seize you too. So that even if not for the brother,
at least for yourself be woken, and check the plague, and hold the rottenness
in advance, and cut through the pasture. These things then and the more than
these having said, and having commanded to hand him over to Satan, he says
later, when he had changed and become better: Sufficient to such a one is this
punishment which is by the many; confirm then love toward him. For since he
had set him as a common enemy and foe to all and had thrown him out of the
flock and cut him off from the body, see with how much eagerness he uses, so
as to glue and join him again. For he did not say: Love him simply, but,
Confirm love toward him, that is show the friendship firm and unmoved, a
certain hot and boiling and fired goodwill, bring in the goodwill
counterbalancing the former hatred. What has happened? tell me; did you not
hand him over to Satan? Yes, he says, but not in order that he may remain in
the hands of Satan, but in order that he may be delivered quickly from his
tyranny. But what I was saying, consider how Paul feared despair, as a great
weapon of the devil; for having said, Confirm love toward him, he also adds
the cause: Lest somehow by the more abundant grief such a one be swallowed. In
the throat of the wolf, he says, is the sheep; let us then arrive. let us
snatch him out in advance before he swallows and destroys our member. In a
surge now is the ship, before the shipwreck let us be eager to save it. For
just as when a sea is being raised and waves are being crested from every
side, the hull becomes underwater, so also a soul, when despondency walls it
round from every side, is quickly choked, if it does not have someone
stretching out the hand, and the saving grief upon the sins becomes deadly
under


the lack of measure. And see how accurately he spoke; for he did not say,
Lest the devil destroy him, but what? Lest we =49.282= be overreached by
Satan; and overreaching is to desire the things of others. Showing then that
he has become alien to him henceforth, and has set himself as belonging to the
flock of Christ through the repentance, he says: Lest we be overreached by
Satan. For if he holds him henceforth, he snatches our member, he takes the
sheep of the flock; for he put off the sin through the repentance. Paul then
knowing what the devil did in the case of Judas, feared lest this same thing
happen here too. And what did he do in the case of Judas? Judas repented; For
I sinned, he says, having betrayed innocent blood; the devil heard these
words; he knew him beginning the road toward the better and walking toward
salvation, and he feared the change. He has a loving-kind Master, he says;
when he was about to betray him, he wept over him and exhorted him with
countless things; will he not much more receive him repenting? Being
uncorrected he drew him and called him; will he not much more pull to himself
the one corrected and having recognized the sin? for on this account he also
came to be crucified. What then did he do? He threw him into tumult; he
darkened him with the excess of despondency, he pursued, he drove until he led
him upon the noose, and led him out from the present life, and deprived him of
the eagerness according to the repentance. For that, if he had lived, he too
would have been saved, is clear from those who crucified; for if he saved
those who brought him up upon the cross, and being on the cross itself he was
exhorting the Father, and was asking pardon for them of the daring, it is
quite clear that also the one who betrayed, if he had shown the repentance by
the fitting law, he would have received with all goodwill; but that man did
not endure to remain in the medicine, having been swallowed by the more
abundant grief. This then Paul also fearing, presses the Corinthians to snatch
the man from the throat of the devil. And what need to speak the things of the
Corinthians? Peter after the communion of the mysteries denied three times,
and having wept he wiped all away; Paul being a persecutor and blasphemer and
insolent, and persecuting not only the one crucified, but also all those of
that one, having repented, became an apostle. For God seeks only a brief
occasion from us, and grants us forgiveness of many sins. Let me speak also a
parable guaranteeing this very thing to you. 4. There were two brothers; and
having divided the paternal substance each, the one of them remained upon the
house; but the other having eaten up all the things given, moved into the
foreign land, not bearing the shame from the poverty. And I was led to speak
this parable, in order that you may learn that there is also forgiveness of
the sins after the baptism, if we attend; and I speak not in order that I may
throw into remissness, but in order that I may lead away from despair; for
despair works for us evils worse than remissness. This son then bears an image
of those who have fallen after the washing; and that he makes clear those who
have fallen after the baptism, is clear from there; for he is called a son;
and no one would be called a son without baptism. And he was also dwelling the
paternal house, and he divided all the paternal things; and before =49.283=
baptism it is not possible to take paternal things, nor to receive an
inheritance; so that through all these he hints to us the order of the
faithful. And he was also a brother of the one who had succeeded; and a
brother would not come to be without spiritual regeneration. This man then
having fallen into last vice, what does he say? I will go back to my father;
for on this account the father also let him go, and did not hinder him from
going away into the foreign, in order that through the experience he might
learn well of how great a benefaction he was enjoying remaining at home. For
often when God speaking does not persuade, he leaves the teaching to the
experience through the things; which then he was also saying toward the Jews.
For since having spent countless


words through the prophets, he did not persuade nor pull them, having left
them to be educated through the punishment, he says toward them: Your apostasy
will educate you, and your vice will convict you. For he ought to have been
trustworthy even before the outcome of the things; but since they were so
insensibly disposed, as to disbelieve his exhortations and counsels, checking
in advance not to be persuaded by the vice, he permits them to be educated
through the things, in order that thus he may recover them again. When then
the prodigal having gone away into the foreign, and having learned through
their works how great an evil it is to fall out of a paternal house, returned,
the father did not remember evil, but received him with upturned hands. Why
ever? Because he was a father, and not a judge. And choruses, and feasts, and
festivals henceforth, and the whole house was bright and overjoyed. What do
you say? these the repayments of vice? Not of the vice, O man, but of the
return; not of the sin, but of the repentance; not of the wickedness, but of
the change toward the better. And the greater thing, the former son was angry
at these things; but he also persuaded this one mildly saying, That you always
spent time with me, but this one was lost, and was found; he was dead, and he
lived again. And whenever it is necessary to save the one lost, he says, it is
not a time of law-courts, nor of accurate examination, but of loving-kindness
and pardon only. No physician, having left off applying a medicine to the one
who is sick, demands accounts of the disorder from him and punishment. And if
he wholly ought to give justice, he gave sufficient justice, the spending of
time in the foreign. At any rate so much time he was parted from our company,
and he continued wrestling with hunger and dishonor and the last evils.
Therefore he says, He was lost, and was found; he was dead, and he lived
again. Do not look at the present things, he says, but think the greatness of
the former disaster; you see a brother, not an alien. He returned to a father,
able to remember nothing of the former things; or rather =49.284= remembering
those things only, which are able to lead to sympathy and pity and the
affection and sparing that belongs to those who have begotten. Therefore he
did not say the things he did, but the things he suffered; he did not remember
that he ate up the substance, but that he fell among countless evils. Thus he
was seeking the sheep with so much eagerness; or rather with greater. For here
the son himself returned; but there the shepherd himself went away, and having
found he led, and he was rejoicing over him more than over all those who had
been saved. And see also how he brought him back; he did not scourge, but
carrying and bearing upon the shoulders, he gave him back again to the flock.
These things then knowing, that he not only does not turn away those
returning, but even receives them no less than those who have succeeded; that
he not only does not demand justice, but even comes into a seeking of those
who have wandered and rejoices more over the finding of these than over those
who have been saved, let us neither being in wicked things despair, nor being
in good things take courage, but both succeeding, let us fear, lest having
taken courage we fall, and sinning let us repent. For what I said beginning,
this I say also now, that both these are a betrayal of our salvation, both to
take courage standing, and to despair lying down. Wherefore Paul making those
standing secure was saying: Let him who thinks he stands look lest he fall.
And again: I fear, lest somehow having preached to others, I myself become
unapproved; and raising those lying down and waking them into more eagerness,
he testified writing to the Corinthians thus: Lest somehow I mourn many of
those who have sinned before, and have not repented; showing that not so those
sinning as those not repenting upon the sins are worthy of dirges. And the
prophet also says toward them: Does not the one falling rise, or the one
turning away turn back? Therefore David also exhorts these same saying: Today
if you hear his voice,


do not harden your hearts as in the provocation. While then the today is
being said, let us not despair, but having good hopes toward the Master, and
thinking his sea of loving-kindness, having shaken off every wicked
conscience, with much eagerness, let us also take hold of the hope of virtue,
and let us show the repentance with all excess, in order that having put off
all the sins here, we may be able to stand with freedom of speech upon the
tribunal of Christ, and to obtain the kingdom of the heavens; of which may it
come to be that we all succeed by the grace and loving-kindness of our Lord
Jesus Christ, with whom to the Father together with the Holy Spirit glory,
power, honor, now and always, and unto the ages of the ages. Amen.

=49.283= ON REPENTANCE,
And on the gloom of king Ahab, and on Jonah the prophet.
Homily 2.
1. Did you see on the former Lord's day a war and a victory, a war of the
devil, and a victory of Christ? did you see how repentance was being praised,
and the devil did not bear the blow, but feared and shuddered? =49.284= Why do
you fear, O devil, with repentance being praised? why do you wail, why do you
shudder? Yes, he says, justly I wail and am distressed; this repentance
snatched great vessels of mine. Which are these? The harlot, the
tax-collector, the robber, the blasphemer. For truly repentance snatched many
=49.285= vessels of his, and took down his very citadel, and has the mortal
blow of the repentance. You will know, beloved, from the things which the
trial showed the other day. For what reason then do we not enjoy such a
homily, and on each day come to meet into the church, greeting the repentance?
Even if you are a sinner, enter into the church, in order that you may speak
your sins; even if you are righteous, enter, in order that you may not fall
out of the righteousness; for the church is a harbor for both. Are you a
sinner? do not despair, but enter putting forward repentance. Did you sin? say
to God that, I have sinned. What toil is this? what circuit? what affliction?
what distress to speak a word that, I sinned? For if you do not say yourself a
sinner, do you not have the devil as accuser? Anticipate, and snatch his
office; for his office is to accuse. Why then do you not anticipate him, and
speak the sin, and wipe out the sin, knowing that you have such an accuser not
able to be silent? Did you sin? enter into the church, say to God that, I
sinned. I demand nothing else of you, than this only. For the divine Scripture
says: Speak you your lawless deeds first, in order that you may be justified;
speak the sin, in order that you may loose the sin. There is not in this a
toil, nor a circuit of words, nor an expense of money, nor any other such
thing; having spoken a word, be well-minded upon the sin, and say that, I have
sinned. And from where is this, he says, that if I speak the sin first, I
loose the sin? I have in the Scripture both the one who spoke and loosed, and
the one who did not speak and was condemned. Cain killed his brother Abel
being held by envy, so that murder followed upon the envy; for having taken
him into the plain he took him away. And what does God say toward him? Where
is Abel your brother? The one who knows all things asks, not being ignorant,
but drawing the one who murdered into repentance. For that he was asking not
being ignorant, he showed: Where is Abel your brother? And he said, I do not
know; am I a guard of my brother? Be it, you are not a guard; why also a
destroyer? you were not guarding; why also did you kill? Nevertheless do you
confess these things? You are also responsible for the guard. What then does
God toward him? A voice of blood of your brother

cries toward me from the earth. He convicted immediately and brought the
punishment upon him, not so much because of the murder, as because of the
shamelessness; for God does not so hate the one sinning, as the one being
shameless. At any rate coming into repentance he does not receive Cain, since
he did not speak the sin first. For what does he say? Greater is my sin than
for me to be forgiven; instead of, I sinned a great thing; I am not worthy to
live. What then does God toward him? Groaning and trembling you shall be upon
the earth; and he declared to him a terrible and hard punishment. I do not
take you away, he says, in order that the tru49.286 th may not be given over
to forgetfulness, but I make you a law being read by all, in order that the
disaster may become a mother of philosophy. And Cain was going about, a living
law, a moving pillar, being silent, and sending forth the voice more brilliant
than a trumpet. Let no one do, he says, such things, in order that he may not
suffer such things. He received the punishment because of the shamelessness,
and not having spoken, but having been convicted, he was condemned because of
the sin. For if he had confessed to himself, he would even have wiped out the
sin first.
2. And that you may learn that these things have this manner, learn how
another having spoken first loosed the sin. Let us come upon David, the
prophet and king; or rather I gladly call him from the prophecy; for the
kingship is into Palestine, but the prophecy into the ends of the inhabited
world; and the kingship was dissolved in a small time, but the prophecy bears
immortal words. It is more to be chosen that the sun be quenched, than that
the words of David be given over to forgetfulness. This man fell into adultery
and murder; For he saw, he says, a beautiful woman bathing, and he desired
her; then he led out into work the things that seemed good to him. And the
prophet was in adultery, the pearl in mire. But he did not yet know that he
had sinned; so much was he stupefied by the passion. For when the charioteer
is drunk the chariot also is borne disorderly; and what charioteer and chariot
are, this soul and body are; if the soul is darkened, the body also rolls in
mire; for as long as the charioteer stands, the chariot also is driven well;
but when he himself is slack, and is not able to become master of the reins,
the chariot itself also is seen in more terrible things. So also in the case
of the man; as long as the soul is sober and watches, the body itself also is
in chastity; but whenever the soul is darkened, the body itself also rolls in
mire and pleasure. What then David? He committed adultery; but he did not
know, nor was he being convicted by anyone, and this in last gray hair, in
order that you may learn that, if you are remiss, neither does the gray hair
benefit you, nor again, if you are eager, is the youth able to harm you. For
not the manner of the age, but the success of the purpose; since Daniel also
was twelve years, and he judged; but the elders, ancient in days, also
practiced a drama of adultery, and neither did the gray hair benefit those,
nor did the youth harm this one. And that you may learn that not in the age,
but in the purpose the things of temperance are judged, David being in the
gray hair itself, and then having fallen into adultery, worked a murder, and
was so disposed as not to know that he had sinned; for the charioteer mind had
been made drunk from incontinence. What then God? He sends toward him Nathan
the prophet; the prophet comes toward the prophet; for thus it also happens in
the case of physicians; whenever a physician is sick, he comes into need of
another physician. The same also here; the one who had sinned was a prophet,
and the one bringing the medicines was a prophet. Nathan then comes toward
him, and does not immediately from the doors convict him and =49.287= say:
Lawless and most accursed man, adulterer and murderer, you received so many
honors from God, and you trampled his commandments? Nathan said nothing such,
in order that he might not work him more shameless; for sins being published
provoke the one who has sinned into shamelessness. He comes then toward him
and weaves a drama of a lawsuit; and what does he say? King, I have a lawsuit
toward you. There was a certain rich man and there was a certain poor man;

and the rich man had herds of cattle and many flocks; and the poor man also
was having one ewe-lamb, which was drinking from his cup, and was eating from
his table, and was sleeping in his bosom; here he shows the genuine of the man
toward the woman; and when a certain stranger had come, the rich man spared

you who convict me? and who sent you to speak freely? and with what daring

And the Lord let go your sin; you condemned yourself, I forgive you the
condemnation; you confessed well-mindedly, you loosed the sin; you drew the
condemnation upon yourself, I loosed the sentence. Did you see how the thing
written was fulfilled, Speak you your lawless deeds first, in order that you
may be justified? What toil is this, to speak the sin first? 3. And you have
also another road of repentance; which then is this? To mourn the sin. Did you
sin? mourn, and you loose the sin. What toil is this? I demand nothing more of
you, than to mourn the sin; I do not tell you to cut seas, nor to bring down
into harbors, nor to travel, nor to go away an infinite road, nor a payment of
money, nor to make a passage of wild waves, but what? Mourn upon the sin. And
from where is this, he says, that if I mourn, I loose the sin? You have also a
proof of this from the divine Scripture. There was a certain king Ahab; and
this man is witnessed as righteous; and this man reigned into evils under
Jezebel his wife; this man desired a vineyard of a certain Naboth the
Jezreelite, and he sent toward him saying: Give me your vineyard which I

said: Let it not be to me =49.288= to sell you an inheritance of my fathers.
And Ahab was desiring the vineyard; but he did not wish to force him, as from
this to fall into a harming of him. And Jezebel having entered toward him, a
shameless and forbidden little woman, unclean and most accursed, says: Why are
you grieved, and do you not eat? rise, eat; I make you inherit the vineyard of
Naboth the Jezreelite. And having taken she writes a letter from the person of
the king toward the elders, saying: Proclaim a fast, and set false men against
Naboth, that he blessed God and king, that is, he blasphemed. O fast filled of
stretched-out lawlessness; they proclaimed a fast, in order that they might do
a murder. What then happened? Naboth was stoned, and he died. And Jezebel
having learned says to Ahab: Rise, let us inherit the vineyard, because Naboth
died. And he having been grieved toward the present, nevertheless entered, and
inherited the vineyard. God sends toward him Elijah the prophet: Go, he says,
say to Ahab, In return for which you murdered and inherited, thus shall your
blood be poured out, and the dogs shall lick your blood, and the harlots shall
wash in your blood. God-sent the wrath, completed the sentence, fulfilled the
condemnation. And see where he sends him; into the vineyard. Where the
lawlessness, there also the punishment. And what does he say? Ahab having seen
him says: You have found me, my enemy, instead of, You took me as liable
because

I have sinned; now you have a time to trample upon me. You have found me, my
enemy. Since Elijah was always convicting Ahab, Ahab knowing that he had
sinned, You were always convicting me, he says; but now you opportunely set
upon me; for he knew that he had sinned. He reads beside him the sentence;
Thus says the Lord, he says; In return for which you murdered and inherited
and poured out blood of a righteous man, thus shall your blood be poured out,
and the dogs shall lick it, and the harlots shall wash in your blood. Having
heard these things Ahab was looking gloomy and mourning upon the sin; he
acknowledged the injustice, and God loosed the vote against him. But first God
made a defense to Elijah, in order that he might not appear as a liar and
suffer what Jonah suffered; for Jonah also underwent such a thing. God says
toward him: Go, preach into Nineveh the city in which dwell twelve myriads of
men apart from women and children; Yet three days, and Nineveh will be
overthrown. Jonah was not wishing to go away, knowing the loving-kindness of
God; but what does he do? He turns into flight; for he says, I go away
preaching; you being loving-kind repent, and I am taken away as a false
prophet. But nevertheless the sea having taken him did not hide him, but gave
him back to the earth, and saved him again into Nineveh, just as an excellent
fellow-servant keeping a fellow-servant. For Jonah went down, he says, to
flee, and he found a ship going into Tarshish, and he gave his fare, and went
up into it. Where do you flee, Jonah? do you go away into another earth? But
the =49.289= earth is the Lord's and the fullness of it. But into the sea? Do
you not know that His is the sea, and he himself made it? But into the heaven?
Did you not hear David saying, that I will see the heavens, works of your
fingers? But nevertheless that man being held by fear fled in seeming; for
truly to flee God is impossible. Nevertheless when the sea restored him, and
he came into Nineveh, he preached saying that, Yet three days, and Nineveh
will be overthrown. And that you may learn that having this in mind he gave
himself into flight, that God being loving-kind repents upon the vice which he
spoke about them, and he himself was about to be as a false prophet, he shows
it to himself. For after the preaching in Nineveh, he went out outside the
city, and he was watching what might happen; and when he saw three days having
passed, and nowhere anything of the things threatened happening, then he moves
his first reasoning, and says: Are these not my words which I was saying, that
God is merciful and long-suffering and repenting upon vices of men? In order
then that Elijah may not suffer some such thing, such as Jonah also, God says
the cause, through which he forgave Ahab. And what does God say toward Elijah?
Did you see how Ahab went mourning and looking gloomy before me? I will not do
according to his vice. Wonderful, a master becomes an advocate of a servant,
and God makes a defense to a man on behalf of a man; Do not think, he says,
that I simply forgave him; he reshaped the manner, and I changed my wrath and
dissolved it. And in order that you may not be thought as a false prophet; for
you truly spoke; if he had not changed the manner, he had to undergo the
things of the sentence; but this man changed the manner, and I dissolved my
wrath. And God says toward Elijah: Did you see how Ahab went mourning and
looking gloomy? I will not do according to my wrath. Did you see that the
mourning wipes out sins?
4. And you have also a third road of repentance. And I spoke many roads of
repentance, in order that by the variety of the roads I may work the salvation
easy for you. And which is this third road? Humility; be humble-minded, and
you loosed the chains of the sins. And you have also a proof of this from the
divine Scripture, from the reading according to the tax-collector and the
Pharisee. The Pharisee and the tax-collector went up, he says, into the temple

to pray, and the Pharisee began to number his virtues. I am not also, he says,
a sinner as all the world, nor as this tax-collector. Wretched and miserable
soul, you condemned the whole inhabited world, why did you also afflict the
one near you? was the inhabited world not enough for you, unless you also
condemned the tax-collector? you slandered all from there, and you did not
even spare the one man. I am not as all the world, nor as this tax-collector;
twice of the week I fast, I tithe my belongings to the poor. He uttered words
of boastfulness. Wretched man, =49.290= be it, you condemned the inhabited
world, why did you also strike the tax-collector near you? were you not filled
with the accusation of the inhabited world, unless you also condemned the one
with you? What then the tax-collector? Having heard these things he did not
say: For who are you who say such things to me? from where do you know my
life? you did not keep company with me; you did not remain with me, you did
not spend time with me; why are you so arrogant? for who witnesses
good-working to you? why do you praise yourself? why do you grant to yourself?
But the tax-collector said none of these, but having bent he worshipped and
said: God, be gracious to me the sinner; and the tax-collector having been
humble-minded became righteous. But the Pharisee came down from the temple
having lost righteousness, and the tax-collector came down having acquired
righteousness; and words conquered deeds. For the one lost righteousness by
works, and the other acquired righteousness by a word of humility. And yet
that was not even humility; for humility is this, whenever someone being great
humbles himself; but this of the tax-collector was not humility, but truth;
for the words were true; for he was a sinner.
5. For what is worse than a tax-collector, tell me? he is a dealer of the
disasters of others, a sharer of the toils of others; and he does not look
upon the labor, but he shares the gain; so that last sin of the tax-collector.
For a tax-collector is nothing else than violence that has spoken freely,
lawful sin, well-faced overreaching. For what is worse than a tax-collector
sitting according to the road, and harvesting the fruits of toils of others,
and when the labors, no thought to him, but when the gain, he takes the
portion from the things he did not labor? So that if the tax-collector being a
sinner obtained so great a gift having been humble-minded, how much more the
one being virtuous and being humble-minded? So that if you confess your sins
and are humble-minded, you become righteous. And do you wish to learn who is
humble-minded? look at Paul the truly humble-minded, Paul the teacher of the
inhabited world, the spiritual orator, the vessel of election, the unwaved
harbor, the unshaken tower, the one circling the inhabited world in a brief
body, and having run round this as some winged one; look at that man being
humble-minded, the private person and philosopher, the poor and rich. That man
I truly call humble-minded, the one who drew countless labors, the one who
showed countless trophies against the devil, the one preaching and saying, His
grace toward me did not become empty, but I labored more abundantly than all;
the one who underwent prisons, and scourges; the one who netted the inhabited
world through Epistles; the one called by a heavenly voice, that man is
humble-minded saying: For I am the least of the apostles, who am not
sufficient to be called an apostle. Did you see greatness of humility, did you
see Paul being humble-minded, calling himself least? For I am, he says, the
least of the apostles, who am not sufficient to be called an apostle. For this
is truly humility, to be humbled in all things, and to call oneself least.
Think who was the one saying these words; Paul the heaven-citizen, the one
simply =49.291= wearing the body, the pillar of the Churches, the earthly
angel, the heavenly man.

For I gladly henceforth spend time upon the man, beholding the beauty of his
virtue. The sun having risen and sending forth brilliant rays does not so
gladden my sights, as the face of Paul shines upon my mind; for the sun lights
the sights, but Paul wings into the very vaults of the heavens; for he works
the soul both higher than the sun, and above the moon; for such is the power
of virtue; it makes the man an angel, it wings the soul into the heaven. Paul
teaches us this virtue; let us be eager to become zealots of his virtue. But
we must not go out of the matter lying before us; for the aim was to show
=49.292= a third road of repentance, the humility, that the tax-collector was
not humble-minded, but spoke truth, having stripped his own sins, and became
righteous, not having paid money, nor having cut seas, nor having walked a
long road on foot, nor having crossed infinite seas, nor having asked friends,
nor having spent much time, but through humility he acquired righteousness,
having been counted worthy of the kingdom of the heavens; of which may it come
to be that we all succeed, by the grace and loving-kindness of our Lord Jesus
Christ, to whom the glory and the power unto the ages of the ages. Amen.

OF THE SAME, A DISCOURSE On almsgiving, and on the ten virgins.
Homily 3.
1. Do you know, from where the word began for us the other day, or where it
dissolved, or from what subject into what the words of the former homily
ended? But I think you have forgotten where the word paused for us; and I
know, and I do not blame you in this nor do I accuse. For each of you has a
wife, and holds to children, and thinks of all the things about the house;
some also are occupied in campaigns, and others are handicraftsmen; and each
of you is occupied into various needs. But we are turned in these things, and
we practice in these things, and we spend our time in these things; so that
you are not to be blamed in this, but to be praised in the zeal, because you
do not leave us even one Lord's day, but despising all things you come to meet
into church. For this is a greatest praise of our city, not the having tumults
and suburbs, nor gold-roofed houses and dining-rooms, but the having a people
eager and woken. For we do not recognize the good birth of the tree from the
leaves, but from the fruits. For on this account we have been preferred before
the irrational animals in the having reason, and sharing in a word, and loving
a word; for a man not loving a word is much more irrational than the cattle,
not knowing through what he was honored, and from where he has the honor. And
well the prophet was saying: A man being in honor did not understand; he was
compared to the senseless cattle, and became like them. Being a rational man,
do you not love a word? tell me, and what pardon will you have? So that you
are more necessary to me than all, you who are winged toward the word of
virtue, and putting all things second to the divine words. Come then let us
also take hold of the subject, and let us say the things following of the
things spo49.292 ken the other day; for I owe you, and I gladly pay the debt;
for it does not bring poverty to me, but it gathers wealth. For in the case of
the outside things the debtors flee the lenders toward not paying back, but I
pursue into the paying back, and very reasonably; for in the case of the
outside things the paying back works poverty, but in the case of the word the
paying back begets wealth. I say a certain thing: I owe money to someone; if I
pay these, it is not possible both to be with that man and with me, but from
me it

withdrew, and it was glued to that man; but if I pay a word, it both is with
me, and you all have it; if I hold the word and do not share, then I am poor,
but when I pay, then I become more well-provided; if I do not pay the word,
then I alone am rich, but if I pay, I carry off the fruit with all of you.
Come then let us pay the debt; and what was this? The other day we were
exercising the word about repentance and we were saying, that many and varied
are the roads of the repentance, in order that the salvation may become easy
for us. For if God had given us one road of repentance, we would have put it
off saying: We are not able to go this, we are not able to be saved; but now
cutting out this occasion of yours, he has not given you one road only, nor a
second, nor a third, but many and different, in order that by the multitude he
may make easy for you the ascent into the heaven. And we were saying that the
repentance is easy, and there is no burden in it. Are you a sinner? enter into
the church, say that I have sinned, and you loosed the sin. For we also led
David into the midst having sinned, and having loosed the sin; then we set
down a second road, the mourning upon the sin, and we were saying, what toil
is this? it is not to pay money, nor to walk a long road on foot, nor any
other such thing, but only to mourn upon the sin; and we led this from the
Scripture, that God repented upon Ahab, because of his mourning and looking
gloomy; and this he himself was saying to Elijah: Did you see how Ahab went
before me mourning, and looking gloomy? I will not do according to my =49.293=
anger. Then we were also giving a third road of repentance, and we led from
the Scripture into the midst the Pharisee and the tax-collector, that the
Pharisee arrogantly boasting fell out of the righteousness; but the
tax-collector having been humble-minded came down fruitful of righteousness,
and not having spent even one labor he became righteous; he threw words, and
he took deeds. Come then let us come upon the sequence, and let us bring
forward a fourth road of repentance; and which is this? I mean the almsgiving
the queen of the virtues, the one quickly leading men up into the vaults of
the heavens, the excellent advocate. A great thing is almsgiving; therefore
Solomon also was crying: Great is a man and honorable a merciful man. Great
are the wings of almsgiving; it cuts the air, it passes the moon, it oversteps
the rays of the sun, it goes up into the very vaults of the heavens. But it
does not stand there either, but it also passes the heaven, and it runs past
the peoples of the angels, and the choruses of the archangels, and all the
higher powers, and it stands beside the royal throne itself; and be taught
this from the Scripture itself saying: Cornelius, your prayers, and your alms
went up before God. And the, Before God is, Even if you have many sins, but
almsgiving is an advocate, do not fear; for none of the powers above arrays
itself against it; it demands a debt, having its own handwriting in hands. For
it is a voice of the Master himself, that Whoever does to one of these least,
did it to me. So then however many other sins you have, your almsgiving weighs
down all of them.
2. Or do you not know in the Gospel the example of the ten virgins, how those
not having almsgiving, but having practiced virginity remained outside of the
bride-chamber? For there were, he says, ten virgins, five foolish, and five
prudent; and the prudent had oil, but the foolish did not have oil, and their
lamps were being quenched. And the foolish having come toward the prudent
said: Give us oil from your vessels. I am ashamed and I blush and I weep,
whenever I hear a virgin foolish; hearing this name I blush, after so great
virtue, after practice of virginity, after having winged the body into heaven

, after having the contest toward the powers above, and having undergone the
burning heat, after having trampled the furnace of pleasure; then they heard
foolish, and justly foolish, because having done the great, they were defeated
by the small. And the foolish came, he says, =49.294= and said to the prudent:
Give us oil from your vessels. And they said: We are not able to give you,
lest perhaps it may not suffice for us and you; not doing this through lack of
bowels, nor through vice, but through the narrowness of the time; for the
bridegroom was about to come. They themselves also had lamps; but of those
they had oil, and of themselves they did not have. For the fire is the
virginity, and the oil is the almsgiving. Just as then the fire, if it does
not have oil dropping upon it, vanishes, so also the virginity, if it does not
have almsgiving, is quenched. Give us oil from your vessels. And they toward
them: We are not able to give you. But this word is not of vice but of fear;
Lest perhaps it may not suffice both for us and for you; lest perhaps as we
seek to enter all, we all remain; But having gone away buy from those who
sell. And who are the merchants of this oil? The poor, those sitting toward
the almsgiving before the church. And for how much? For however much you wish;
I do not set a price, in order that you may not put forward poverty. However
much you have, buy for so much; do you have an obol? buy the heaven; not
because the heaven is cheap, but because the Master is loving-kind. Do you not
have an obol? give a cup of cold water; Whoever gives a cup of cold water to
drink to one of these least because of me, he shall not lose his reward. The
heaven is a trade and a business, and we neglect. Give bread, and take
paradise; give small things, and take great; give mortal things, and take
immortal; give corruptible things, and take incorruptible. If there was a
festival, both having cheapness, and abundance of foods, and the many things
being sold for a little, would you not, having sold off the substances, and
having put all things second, have become masters of that business? And where
corruptible things, you show so much eagerness; and where an immortal
business, you are so remiss and have fallen back? Give to a poor man, in order
that even if you are silent, countless mouths also may make a defense on your
behalf, the almsgiving standing and advocating; almsgiving is a ransom of a
soul. Therefore just as the basins are filled of water before the doors of the
church, in order that you may wash the hands; so outside the church the poor
sit, in order that you may wash the hands of the soul. Did you wash the
perceptible hands with the water? wash the hands of the soul with the
almsgiving. Do not put forward poverty; the widow in the last of the poverty
entertained Elijah, and the poverty did not become a hindrance, but with much
joy she received him; wherefore she also received back worthy fruits, and she
harvested the ear of the almsgiving. But perhaps the hearer says, Give me
Elijah. Why do you seek Elijah? I give you the Master of Elijah, and you do
not feed him. If you found Elijah, how would you have entertained him? It is a
sentence of Christ the Master of all: Whoever does to one of these least, did
it to me. If a king called a certain one upon the dinner, and =49.295= with
the attendants standing by said toward them: Thank this man many things
instead of me; this man fed me being in poverty and entertained me, this man
furnished many benefactions to me in a time of distress; how would not each
have spent all his money toward that man, whom the king thanked? how would he
not have made a defense? how would not each have been eager to set himself
beside him and to become a friend?
3. Did you see the power of the word? And if in the case of a king a man the
thing has so much honor, think Christ on that day calling upon angels and all
power, and saying: This man entertained me on the earth; this man benefited me
with countless things;

this man gathered me being a stranger; think henceforth the freedom of speech
upon the angels, the boast upon the peoples above. He to whom Christ
witnesses, how would he not have freedom of speech above angels? A great thing
then is almsgiving, brothers; let us greet this, of which nothing is equal; it
is sufficient both to wipe out other sins, and to drive away the judgment; you
being silent it stands and advocates; or rather you being silent countless
mouths thank on your behalf. So many goods from the almsgiving, and we neglect
and have fallen back. Give according to power bread; do you not have bread?
give an obol; do you not have an obol? give a cup of cold water; do you not
have this? mourn together with the one afflicted, and you have a reward. For
the reward is not of the necessity, but of the choice. But as we are still
conversing about this, the thought of the virgins went out for us; come then
let us return into the matter lying before us. Give us, he says, oil from your
vessels. We are not able to give you, lest perhaps it may not suffice for us
and you; but having gone away buy from those who sell. And as they were going
away to buy, the bridegroom came, and those having the lamps bright entered
together with him, and the door of the bride-chamber was shut. And the five
foolish also came, and they were knocking the door of the bride-chamber
crying: Open to us; and a voice from inside toward them of the bridegroom: Go
away from me, I do not know you. And after so many labors what did they hear?
I do not know you. This is, what I was saying, in vain and idly they acquired
the great possession of the virginity. Think, after so many labors they were
thrown out, when they bridled incontinence, when they had the contest toward
the powers above, when they laughed at the things of life, when they bore the
great burning heat, when they overstepped the pits, when they were winged from
earth into heaven, when they did not dissolve the seal of the body, when they
acquired the great form of the virginity, when they had the contest toward the
angels, when they trampled the necessities of the body, when they forgot
nature, when in a body they accomplished the bodiless things, when they
acquired the great and unconquerable possession of the virginity; then they
heard, Go away from me, I do not know you. For do not think to me that the
greatness of the virginity is small; such is the virginity, that none of the
ancients was able to keep this. For on this account the grace is great, that
the things which were terrible =49.296= in the prophets and in the ancients,
these have now become easy to despise. For what were the heaviest and
necessary things? Virginity, and contempt of death; but now even cheap girls
despise these. For the possession of the virginity was heavy thus, as no one
of the old to be able to practice this. Noah was righteous and witnessed by
God, but he consorted with a wife; and likewise Abraham and Isaac the co-heirs
of his of the promise consorted with a wife. Joseph the temperate denied the
great drama of the adultery; but this man also consorted with a wife; for the
profession of the virginity was heavy. From then the virginity became strong,
from when the flower of the virginity blossomed. No one then of the old was
able to practice virginity; for it is a great thing to bridle a body. Paint
with the word the form of the virginity, and learn thoroughly the greatness of
its virtue; still it has a war upon each day never able to be quiet; this war
is worse than the barbarian war. For the war of the barbarians has a certain
time of rest in the treaties, and sometimes they join, and sometimes not, and
there are orders and times; but in the case of the war toward the virginity
there is not a rest; for it is a devil who wars, not knowing to watch a time
of attack, not awaiting an array toward the joining, but he always stands
seeking to find the virgin naked, in order that he may bring on a mortal blow;
and the virgin is never able to cease from this war, but in herself she

carries about the tumult and the warrior. And those condemned even if they see
the ruler toward a time, are not so stormed; but the virgin wherever she goes
away, carries about the judge with herself, and bears about the enemy, and the
enemy does not give her a slackening of evening, not in night, not of dawn,
not in midday; but he always wars, suggesting pleasure, announcing marriage,
how he may drive the virtue from her, and how he may beget the vice in her;
how he may drive out her temperance, how he may sow fornication; the furnace
of the pleasure is kindled upon each hour being softly under-kindled. Think
how much the labor of the success. But those after all these things heard, Go
away from me, I do not know you. See how great a thing is the virginity;
whenever it has its sister the almsgiving, none of the terrible things
prevails over it, but it is above all. Therefore those did not enter, since
with the virginity they did not have the almsgiving. The word is worthy of
much shame; having wrestled down pleasure, you did not despise money; but a
virgin having renounced the life, and having been crucified, loves money.
Would that you had desired a man, and the accusation was not so much; for you
were desiring the consubstantial matter; but now the accusation is greater,
because you desired an alien matter. Be it, those married women show
inhumanity badly, through the occasion of the children; and if you say to
them, Give me almsgiving; I have children, she says, and I am not able. God
gave you children, you received fruit of a belly, in order that you may become
loving-kind, not in order that you may be inhuman; do not take the subject of
the loving-kindness into a starting-point of inhumanity. Do you wish to leave
a good inheritance to your children? leave almsgiving, in order that all may
praise you, and you may leave your memory much-talked. And you not having
children, but having been crucified to the life, why do you gather money?

ON REPENTANCE AND PRAYER.
Homily 4.
1. The shepherds, wherever they see the grass being deeper, continually lead
the sheep, and they do not first raise them from there, until the flocks shear
it all. And we also imitating these, this fourth day already we feed this
flock into the manner of the repentance, and not even today are we preparing
to raise; for we see there being still much plenty of pasture, and much
delight together and benefit. For not so the tresses of the trees becoming
instead of a roof for the sheep according to the midday of the day rest the
sheep, and furnish a longed-for and
beneficial shade, and they put them to sleep with much pleasure, as the
reading of the divine Scriptures recovers and cools the souls in pain and held
by despondency, wiping off the vehement and burning of the pain, and
furnishing a consolation sweeter and more delightful than all shade. For not
only in the losses of money, nor in the castings-away of children, nor in the
other such things, but also in the circumstances of the sins it itself
furnishes us much consolation. For whenever a man having been caught by sin
and having been tripped falls down, then the conscience eats him, and
continually reminding of the sin, he chokes himself with the excess of the
despondency, and is set on fire according to each day, and with countless
exhorting, if he does not admit the exhortation, having entered into the
church, and having heard that many of the saints having fallen rose, and
returned again into the former success, having taken the consolation unnoticed
he goes away. And having sinned often toward men we neither endure to uncover
the fault being ashamed and blushing, and even if we uncover, we will harvest
nothing =49.300= so much; but whenever God exhorts, and touches the heart, all
satanic grief will quickly be put to flight. Therefore also falls of the
righteous are written up for us, in order that both those succeeding, and
those sinning may gain the greatest things from these. For the one sinning
does not come into despair and giving-up of hope, having seen another having
fallen, and having been able to rise again; and the one working righteousness
will be more eager and more secure. For whenever he sees many much better than
himself having fallen down, being made temperate by the fear of their fall, he
will be in agony always, and he will show much security about himself; and
thus the one succeeding in the virtue, and the one sinning having been
delivered from the despair, the one will stand securely, and the other will
return quickly, from where he fell out. For whenever a man exhorts us being
grieved, and we seem to be consoled toward a little, we fall down again into
the same despondency; but whenever God urges through others who have sinned,
and have repented, and have been saved, he makes his own goodness clear to us,
in order that we may not doubt about our own salvation, receiving the
exhortation agreed and secure. Just as then in the circumstances of the sins,
so also in the bringings-on of the dangers, the old histories of the
Scriptures furnish a sufficient medicine to those despondent to all who wish
to attend. And whether a confiscation of money, or insolences of sycophants,
or prisons, or scourges, even if any other of the terrible things seizes us,
looking away toward the righteous who suffered the same things and endured, we
will be able quickly to come into ourselves. For in the case of the passions
of the body the looking toward those suffering stretches the disease of the
one who is sick, and often even works one not being; as some having seen eyes
suffering, shared the sickness from the sight only; but in the case of a soul
not so, =49.301= but the contrary happens, and the continually thinking those
who have suffered such things makes lighter for us the despondency upon our
own evils. Therefore Paul also exhorts those who are faithful in this manner,
not that leading into the midst the living saints only, but also those who
have died. For conversing with Hebrews about to be tripped and to fall down,
he leads the holy men into the midst, Daniel, the three children, Elijah,
Elisha, speaking thus: They stopped mouths of lions, they quenched power of
fire, they fled mouths of a sword, they were stoned, they took trial of
mockeries and scourges, and still of bonds and a prison; they went about in
sheepskins, in goat skins, being in want, being afflicted, being ill-treated,
of whom the world was not worthy. And the communion of the passions furnishes
consolation to those in pain; and just as the only suffering a terrible thing
has the evil inconsolable, so the finding another having fallen among the same
evils works the blow lighter.
2. In order then that in all the things that seem to trouble us we may not
fall down, let us attend with accuracy to the histories of the Scriptures; for
from here we will take a starting-point of much endurance, not being consoled
by the communion of those who suffered the same things only, but also by
learning the manner of the deliverance of the terrible things being brought
upon us, and after the slackening to remain again upon the same, and neither
to fall out into remissness, nor to be raised into madness. For to be
contracted doing badly and to be humbled and to show much caution, is nothing
wonderful; for this nature of the trials even forces those having a stony
heart to do this, the being grieved; but it is of a cautious soul, and having
God continually before eyes, even after the deliverance of the trials never to
fall into forgetfulness; which the Jews were continually suffering. Wherefore
also the prophet mocking them was saying: Whenever he was killing them, then
they were seeking him, and they were turning, and they were rising early
toward God. And Moses also knowing this same thing to them, was exhorting
continually saying: Having eaten and drunk and been filled, attend to
yourself, lest you forget the Lord your God. This then also happened; For
Jacob ate, he says, and was fattened, and was thickened, and the beloved
kicked. Wherefore one must not wonder at the saints through this, that with
the affliction itself being at its peak, they were thus cautious and
philosophical, but that even with the winter having passed and a calm
happening, they remained upon the same fairness and zeal. And that horse one
must wonder at most, the one able without a bridle to walk well-rhythmed; but
if, with a muzzle and bridle holding him, he is well-ordered, it is nothing
wonderful; for one must count the good order not to the good birth of the
animal, but to the necessity of the bridle. This it is possible to say also
upon a soul, that to be still with fear lying upon, is nothing wonderful; but
whenever the trials pass, and someone takes off the bridle of the fear, then
show me philosophy of a soul, and all the good order. But I fear lest, wishing
to accuse Jews, I accuse our own way of life; since we also when we were being
shaken from both famine and plague and hail and =49.302= drought and burnings
and uprising of enemies, was not the church being straitened according to each
day by the multitude of those being gathered? and much was our philosophy and
contempt of the things of life, and neither a longing of money, nor a desire
of glory, nor an appetite and love of wantonness, nor any other wicked
reasoning was then troubling, but all you gave yourselves over into piety,
with supplication and tears; and the fornicator was then temperate, and the
one remembering evil was turning upon the reconciliations, the overreacher was
being bent toward almsgiving, the angry and bold was being changed into
humility and meekness; and whenever God scattered that wrath, and led the
winter past, and made a calm from so many waves, we returned again into the
former preconceptions. And yet I was not ceasing then beside the very time of
the trials foretelling these things, and testifying in advance; but
nevertheless nothing more happened for us, but as a dream and a shadow having
passed, thus you threw out all those things of the mind. Therefore I have
feared now more than then, and what I was then saying, now I have feared more
greatly, lest we draw upon ourselves more difficult evils than the former, and
then receive an incurable blow from God. For when someone continually sinning
obtains pardon from God, then if the man gains nothing from this
long-suffering
into the deliverance of the vice, henceforth he prepares this man, so as even
not wishing to bring upon him the colophon of the evils, and to wipe him out
completely, and no longer to give a certain appointed time of repentance,

God, nothing useful happened for him, he was wiped out henceforth and vanished

Christ also about to make them vanish and to bring upon them an uncorrected

you did not wish, Behold your house is left desert. I fear then lest we also
suffer the same things, because we are made temperate neither by the alien
evils, nor by our own. And I say these things not toward you only who are now

being torn, and saying, that even if the trials passed, let the memory of the
trials remain in our souls, in order that also continually remembering the
benefaction, we may continually thank God who furnished this.
3. These things I was also then saying, and now I say to you, and through you
to those; let us imitate the saints, who neither were swallowed by the
afflictions, nor became more slack by the slackening, which many of men we now
suffer, just as the light of the ships being flooded round by every
circumstance of waves is baptized. For often poverty having come upon us
baptized us and made us underwater, and wealth having been added, again puffed

=49.303= being well disposed, whatever of the terrible things falls in,
whether famine, whether disease, whether sycophancy, whether a snatching of
money, whether anything else whatever, will be bearable and light, through the
commandment of the Master and the hope into him; just as again of the soul not
having well toward God, even if wealth flows upon, even if children are

and let us make it suitable both toward the economy of the present life, and
toward the going-away from here to there. For still a little, and the testing

Christ, having been clothed with our own actions, and seeing with our eyes,
this the tears of the orphans, and this the shameful wantonnesses, in which we
defiled our own souls, and this the groanings of the widows, and this the
buffetings of the poor, the snatchings of the beggars; and not only these, and
the things like these, but if we also practiced anything out of place in mind;
because he himself is Judge of thoughts, and critic of notions; and again;
Searching hearts and kidneys, and, Repaying to each according to his work. And

For the apostle Paul does not converse with women only, but also with men and

had the lamps quenched, were virgins in the body, but not pure in the heart,
but even if a man did not corrupt them, yet the love of money corrupted; their

the solemnity of their virginity. Wherefore Paul also was saying: In order
that the virgin may be holy both in body and in spirit; and again, To present
a pure virgin to Christ. For just as the body is corrupted by adulteries, so
also the soul is defiled by satanic reasonings, by corrupted doctrines, by
out-of-place thoughts. For the one saying, I am a virgin in the body, but in
the soul envies the brother, this man is not a virgin; for the mixing of the
envy corrupted his virginity. Again the vainglorious is not a virgin; for the

the virginity of his soul. And the one hating his brother, this man is a
man-killer rather than a virgin; and simply, each in what wicked passion he is
held, in this he corrupts his virginity. Therefore Paul banished all these
wicked mix49.304 ings, and commands us to be virgins thus, as to receive no
opposing reasoning willingly into the soul.
4. What then do we say toward these things? how may we be pitied? how may we
be saved? I say: let us take up the prayer in the heart always, and the fruits

am meek and humble in the heart, and you will find rest for your souls; and
again David: A sacrifice to God is a crushed spirit; a crushed and humbled

see something of the unexpected having fallen upon and troubling you, do not
flee down toward men, and look toward a mortal help, but having run past all
run up with your mind toward the physician of the souls. For that one only is
able to heal a heart who shaped our hearts alone, and understands all our
works; he himself is able to enter into our conscience, and to touch the mind,
and to exhort the soul. And if that one does not exhort our hearts, the things
from men are superfluous and unprofitable; just as again with God exhorting
and consoling, even if men trouble beside with countless things, they will not
be able to harm us even into the chance thing; for whenever that one makes a
heart firm, no one is able to shake it. These things then knowing, beloved,
let us always flee down toward God, the one both wishing and being able to
loose our disasters. For whenever it is necessary to exhort men, it is a
necessity for us first to meet with doorkeepers, and to exhort parasites and
flatterers, and to go away a long road; but in the case of God there is
nothing such, but he is exhorted without a mediator, without money, without
expense he nods to the supplication; it is enough only to cry with the heart
and to bring forward tears, and immediately having entered you will pull him.
And exhorting a man we have often feared, lest someone of the enemies being
present, or a friend, or someone of the adversaries hear the thing, and
another reveal the things being said, and corrupt the just; but in the case of
God it is not possible to suspect any of these. For whenever you wish to
exhort me, he says, come toward me alone with no one present, that is, cry
with the heart, not moving the lips. For enter, he says, into your treasury;
shut your door, and pray to your Father the one in the hidden; and your Father
who sees in the hidden,
will repay you in the manifest. See excess of honor; whenever you exhort me,
he says, let no one see; but whenever I honor you, I bring the inhabited world

suffered? no longer say the even how to help; for he himself =49.305= knows
accurately the thing beneficial for you. And there are many who announce
countless verses for the sake of the prayer saying: Lord, give me health of
body, double my belongings, avenge my enemy; which is filled of much
irrationality. Wherefore having left all these things one must supplicate and
exhort only according to the tax-collector saying: God, be gracious to me the

Thus then, beloved, let us philosophize with toil and humiliation, striking

lifted up to their enemies. And as much as these do this, so much God comes

5. See then, brothers, whenever some dishonor is brought upon us from someone,
let us not immediately be displeased and be despondent, but let us thank

body, therefore barrennesses of fruits, therefore famines, in order that from

afflictions we may inherit the eternal life. So then we ought to thank God
also on behalf of these, the one healing through many methods and saving our

did not cease naming them a people; they themselves were declining the
mastership, and he himself was not denying them, but was making them his own,
and was drawing toward himself saying: My people, what did I do to you? Did I
become burdensome to you, he says, or heavy and oppressive? But you do not
even have this to say; for even if this was, you ought not even so to have
leaped away. For who
is a son, whom a father does not educate? But nevertheless you do not even

Great and wonderful the thing said; for what he says is of this sort: What did

endure a master to utter. And he does not say, What did I sin into you, but,

things let us continually flee down toward him, and in all despondency let us
seek his consolation, in all disaster his loosing, his pity, in every trial
his help; for whatever terrible it may be, whatever greatness of disaster it
may be, he is able both to loose all and to lead past; and not only, but also
all security and power and good glory, and health of body, and philosophy of
soul, and good hopes, and the not quickly sinning, his goodness will furnish
us. Let us not then murmur according to the ungrateful servants, nor let us
accuse the Master, but in all things let us thank, and let us think one thing
only to be terrible, the sinning out into him. And if we are thus disposed
toward God, not disease, not poverty, not dishonor, not barrenness of fruits,
not any other of the things that seem to be grievous will receive us, but
harvesting a clean pleasure and a pure one through all, we will also obtain
the coming goods, through the grace and loving-kindness of our Lord Jesus
Christ, with whom to the Father the glory, together with the Holy Spirit, now
and always, and unto the ages of the ages. Amen.

1. How delightful the waves of this spiritual sea for us, and more delightful
than those in the open sea themselves. For those a disorder of winds, but
these a desire of hearing raises; and those being crested furnish much agony
to the steersman, but these appearing put in much freedom of speech to the
speaker. For those are proofs of a sea being winter-stormed, but these signs
of a soul being gladdened; and those being dashed toward the rocks send forth
an insignificant sound, but these being broken toward the word of the teaching
send forth a gentle voice. Thus also breaths of a west wind whenever they fall
into standing grain, and bend the heads of the ears and raise them, imitate
the waves of the sea upon the dry land. But even than those standing grains
these are more delightful; for not breaths of a west wind, but a grace of
Spirit raised your souls and warmed, and that fire, which long ago Christ was
saying, that I came to throw fire into the earth, and what do I wish, if it
was already kindled? this I see having been thrown down and burning in your
souls. Since then the fear of Christ kindled so many lamps for us, come from
here let us drop the oil of the teaching, so as for the light to become more
sufficient for us. For the time of the fasting henceforth presses toward the
end for us; for having arrived into the middles of the courses we have
henceforth come to meet toward the end. For just as the
one beginning vomited, thus the one having come toward middle has been seized
of the end. The time then presses toward the end =49.314=, and the hull
henceforth looks toward the harbor, but the thing sought is not to come toward
the harbor, but not to drag the
hull empty of the trade. I beg all of you and I entreat, let each into his
conscience reckon the business from the fasting, and if he finds much gain

festival stands, having done business a great gain let us withdraw, in order
that we may not withdraw with empty hands, in order that we may not, having
undergone a toil of fasting, lose a reward of fasting. For it is possible both
to undergo a toil of fasting, and not to take a reward of fasting; how?

do not eat meats, but we eat down the houses of the poor; whenever we are not
drunk with wine, but we are drunk with a wicked desire; whenever we continue
all the day without food, but we spend the day in unbridled spectacles. Behold
both a toil of fasting, and a reward of fasting nowhere, whenever we go up
into the theaters of the lawlessness. The word is not toward you; for I know
that you are clean of the accusation, but it is a custom of those in pain,
whenever they do not take the responsible, to send the wrath into those

What the benefit of the fasting, whenever you hinder the body from the lawful

speaking the things happening there to behave unseemly, or to be silent and to

prophetic voices, apostolic doctrines, masterly laws, to set beside all the
table of the virtue, and you make a wife more temperate with the narrations
from here, and you work a son more intelligent, and a household-servant more
