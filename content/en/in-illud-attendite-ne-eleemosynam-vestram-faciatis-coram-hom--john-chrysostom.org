On the saying, Take heed that you do not do your alms before men.
=59.571=
1. The wise and skilled craftsmen of husbandry, when with all care and
diligence they have planted the shoots in the earth, first raise the plants
themselves to height, and then thus skillfully cut away the shoots that
trouble them. The same also the lawgiver Moses did: for first he planted the
monarchy in the mind of men, and then thus cut away the polyarchy because of
the mind of men. And how did he plant the monarchy? Hear him saying: You shall
worship the Lord your God, and him only shall you serve; and again, Hear, O
Israel: the Lord your God, the Lord is one. And how he cut away the troubling
shoots of polyarchy, learn: You shall not make, he says, molten gods, nor
shall you worship strange gods; for I, I am the Lord your God. And this also
today God himself and our Saviour does. For since formerly he planted in our
souls the fair olive of almsgiving, and crowned its fruit, that is the
merciful, and blessed them saying, Blessed are the merciful, for they shall
obtain mercy; reasonably today he cuts back the vain things that trouble
almsgiving and cleanses them away. For formerly, stirring all together toward
almsgiving, he taught the poor man from the poor widow in Sarepta of Sidon not
to put forward poverty as a pretext of inhumanity; and he stirred the beggar
from the poor widow in the Gospels who cast the two mites into the treasury,
so that the beggar might contribute the wealth of almsgiving with eagerness;
and he bridled the merchant from the triple wave of the sea, to give rather to
the poor than to the sea; and he constrained the one who trades on land to
anticipate necessity with purpose, and before necessity to prefer God rather
than the robber.

When he had taught all together toward almsgiving, he saw, having accepted
from those he will praise that each is ready, like a thriving plant bent
toward almsgiving: But I am as a fruitful olive tree in the house of God; for
this reason he also takes away together the hypocrisies that trouble
almsgiving. Saying what? Take heed that you do not do your alms before men, to
be seen by them, not because we ourselves put this reading forward
opportunely, but because the former sequence of the readings sent it on. Take
heed that you do not do your alms before men, to be seen by them; otherwise
you have no reward; for this display makes their reward vanish; such a gift
deprives them of the coming goodness. For there is no passion of the soul so
bitter as that of vainglory. For when we have accomplished many of the
commandments, then the disease of vainglory grows up beside them. But the
Saviour cut it out from us by the root, teaching us to do our alms in secret.
But you will surely say: What then? we cannot do it; for it is not possible,
when giving to the poor, or bringing to the priests, or casting into the
treasury, not to be seen.

Have you then also made us more sluggish toward the gift of almsgiving for
good? Now therefore I wish to benefit the souls of my friends and brothers,
and to deliver them from every disease, =59.572= by the grace of the Lord and
God and our Saviour Jesus Christ, and to set right the manner of what is done.
For he examines the purpose of the one who does almsgiving. For this reason he
well adds, saying: When therefore you do alms, do not sound a trumpet before
you, as the hypocrites do; not that those men had trumpets, but that the mask
was of almsgiving, while the soul was campaigning for men, and they walked
toward their gaze. For they did not do alms because of God, hoping to be shown
mercy by him, and to receive the promised reward a hundredfold; but they gave
in order to enjoy the temporary glory of men. No less then than those who make
a show in the theatres, and those who scatter their own substance upon empty
glories, not for the useful fruit of the soul, but for the vain praise of men.
For that praise is often turned round into insult, and having spent much they
are greatly insulted.

Look how much money they spend at the horse-races; and when they spend much,
then they are more insulted by the least and most discarded of men. For they
smear the weakness of the horses upon the men; and the horse stumbles, but the
ambitious man is dishonored; and the irrational horse fell, but the rational
man fell away; and often lamenting he said, Why do I lose my substance in
vain? why did I endure so much toil and so many losses, and become fallen from
my possessions, that I might be insulted to the last by those who are
forbidden? O how many poor could this money that is lost in vain have
enriched! how many who had fallen could it have set up. And the ambitious
outlays flow as from some spring because of vain praise; but the revilings of
irrational men rise up as from some river. And others even despise their own
souls, and again others through the strife of the horses overthrow their own
houses.

2. But let us leave those men to be spent and reviled and to make a vain show;
and let us learn how we must spend money piously, and how we must live
devoutly, and expect the heavenly instead of the earthly. For Christ does not
forbid you to give alms, but he teaches how one must do almsgiving: Let not
your left hand know what your right hand does. And here again he does not hint
at the hands, but he said it hyperbolically, as that your evil thought may not
know what your good purpose does, lest it overturn you from the good. For
according to the divine voice the mind of men is set upon the evil things. For
this reason he says, And to be ignorant even of yourself, let this be your
great desire. Why? That your Father who sees in secret your cheerful, your
eager gift to the poor, may love you, and may repay you openly.

He has set a great and august theatre for those who do alms; and what the
merciful desire, this he has granted them with much abundance; so that even if
myriads of men see you doing alms, yet you should not attend to them, nor
desire the temporary glory from them; but if you wish, he says, to have
spectators of what is done by you, take, he says, the angels, and the
archangels, and the creator and Master of all. But if you wish to have men as
spectators of what is done, and to do your alms for display to them, you will
have a hundred or a thousand spectators applauding you. But if you strive to
escape those who praise you in the present life =59.573=, and to escape thus,
so that, even if some glorify you, you may not receive it gladly and come to
pride, but rather turn away from such men and overturn them; then on that
fearful day, with the whole inhabited world looking on, you will receive the
crowns of glory. If for God's sake, and for the wealth that is to be stored up
for you there by him, you do alms, and not for temporary glory; then as into
his own bosom God receiving your accomplishments of almsgiving, with all men
present from Adam until the consummation of the age; then with all the angels
and archangels present, when thousands and myriads of angels stand by; then
with his own undefiled hands he will call you saying: Come, you blessed of my
Father, inherit the kingdom prepared for you from the foundation of the world.
Come, for I received your toils; I became the banker of your money.

When you stretched out your hands to the beggar, I opened my own bosom; when
you gave your bread to the poor man, I was filled through that man's belly.
For I hungered, and you gave me to eat; I thirsted, and you gave me to drink.
Of such joy then are you deemed worthy, you merciful. But if you do your alms
for display, you will receive the reward from them. For if one must make a
display, first of all one must display to God, who most of all has authority
to crown, and to lead by the hand to the theatre of the angels. Who then is so
wretched, that when the king is eager to come to the spectacle of his
accomplishments, leaving that one he seats his own theatre toward the least,
and awaits a reward from them? But no Christian is such; God forbid. For
whatever we Christians do, we do having Christ before our eyes, and naming his
name; for he himself through his Gospels has given us a way of salvation, so
that doing every alms, and pursuing every virtue, attending to him, and
receiving the gifts from him, we may do them. For whether martyrs have given
over their bodies to the lawless, they gave them over for the sake of him who
said: Fear not those who kill the body, but cannot kill the soul; but rather
fear him who is able to destroy both soul and body in Gehenna; whether then
some have opened churches, or hostels throughout the whole inhabited world,
they did it for the sake of him who said, I was a stranger, and you took me
in; whether we pursue humility, we pursue it for the sake of him who said:
Blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven; whether
we give bread to the poor, we give for the sake of him who said: Whoever shall
give a cup of cold water only in the name of a disciple, shall not lose his
reward; whether we visit prisons, we visit for the sake of him who said: I was
in prison, and you came to me; whether =59.574= we clothe the widows and the
orphans, we clothe them for the sake of him who said: I was naked, and you
clothed me; whether we make distribution to the poor at all, it is for the
sake of him who said: Inasmuch as you did it to one of these least, you did it
to me; whether we bear the reproaches from the impious nations, as worshipping
him who was crucified by the Jews and who made the inhabited world alive, it
is for the sake of him who said: Blessed are you, when they shall reproach
you, and persecute you, and say every evil word against you falsely; rejoice
and be glad, for your reward is great in the heavens. Let no one therefore
suppose that a Christian ever does alms for display to men, for the reward
from them; God forbid. For once we were instructed from the evangelical
treasures, and being confirmed we believed, and having believed we were saved.

For this reason also we preach from the evangelical readings. But even when
the holy Gospels are being read, we do not listen simply nor as it chances,
but standing and sober, and receiving the commandments with all fear; we do
not dare, falling back in mind, sitting with the body, being occupied with the
neighbor's tales, to let them pass by; neither rich nor poor, neither slave
nor free, neither men nor women, neither rulers nor ruled, but even he himself
who wears the royal diadem, immediately when the Gospels are being read,
stands with all fear and with all eagerness, and he does not even allow the
diadem to remain on his head, but he bows because of God who speaks in the
holy Gospels. Saying what? I know the dignity of him who has given me the
dignity, I worship the kingdom of him who has given me the kingdom, and I
stand with fear, when his holy oracles are being read; since, he says, he
himself has given to rulers to stand at the royal victory being read. And that
the heretic may not revile, saying that only for a little time do the kings of
Christ hypocritically honor him, hear the prophet saying (for many generations
before he speaks the image now taking place): Kings shall see you, and rulers
shall be raised, and they shall arise, and all the kings of the earth shall
worship him.

Learn then and believe, O heretic, that into the endless ages the kingdom of
Christ our God remains, and his grace crowning all Christians, and leading
them by the hand in the kingdom of heaven. Thus all Christians do all things
looking toward him, and we have been persuaded by him saying: Let your light
shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father
who is in the heavens. To him be the glory and the might and the authority and
the worship with his only-begotten Son, and the all-holy and good and
life-giving and almighty Spirit, now and always, and unto the ages of ages.
Amen.
