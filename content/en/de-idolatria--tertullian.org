* Chapter 1
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

The principal crime of the human race, the highest guilt of the age, the whole
cause of the judgment, is idolatry. For although each offense keeps its own
species, although it is destined for judgment also under its own name, yet it
is written off in the crime of idolatry. Set aside the titles, recognize the
works: the idolater is likewise a murderer. You ask whom he has killed? If it
makes anything toward the ambition of the charge, not a stranger, nor an
enemy, but himself. By what snares? Those of his own error. With what weapon?
The offense of God. With how many blows? As many as there are idolatries. He
who denies that the idolater has perished will deny that the idolater has
committed murder. Likewise you may recognize adultery and fornication in the
same; for he who serves false gods is without doubt an adulterer of the truth,
because every falsehood is adultery. Thus he is also plunged in fornication.
For who, as a coworker with unclean spirits, does not go about defiled and
debauched? And so far do the holy Scriptures use the word fornication in the
reproach of idolatry.

The condition of fraud, I think, is this, if anyone snatches what is another's
or denies what is due to another, and fraud committed against a man is
certainly the name of a very great crime. But idolatry commits fraud against
God, denying him his own honors and conferring them on others, so that it
joins insult also to fraud. But if fraud as well as fornication and adultery
bring death, already in these equally idolatry is not freed from the charge of
murder. After such crimes, so deadly, so devouring of salvation, the rest
also, in some measure and separately likewise arranged, represent their own
condition in idolatry. In it also are the lusts of the world. For what
solemnity of idolatry is without ambition of dress and ornament? In it are
wantonness and drunkenness, since they are most often frequented for the sake
of food and belly and lust. In it is injustice. For what is more unjust than
that which does not know the Father of justice? In it also is vanity, since
its whole reason is vain. In it is falsehood, since its whole substance is
false. Thus it comes about that all things are found in idolatry and idolatry
in all things. But otherwise also, since all offenses savor against God, and
nothing that savors against God is not assigned to demons and unclean spirits,
to whom idols are made over, without doubt whoever sins commits idolatry. For
he does that which belongs to the stewards of the idols.

* Chapter 2
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h2
:END:

But let all the names of crimes depart into the properties of their own works;
let idolatry remain in that which it itself is. So hostile a name toward God
is enough for it, so rich a substance of crime, which stretches out so many
branches, pours out so many veins, that the matter has been taken up
especially concerning this: in how many ways the breadth of idolatry must be
guarded against by us, since in many fashions it overthrows the servants of
God not only when unknown, but even when dissembled. Most people think
idolatry is to be interpreted simply in these ways alone, if anyone either
kindles or sacrifices or offers a sacred feast or is bound by some rites or
priesthoods; just as if anyone should think adultery is to be counted in
kisses and in embraces and in the very congress of the flesh, or murder is to
be reckoned only in the pouring out of blood and in the snatching away of
life.

But we are sure how much more widely the Lord disposes these things, when he
marks adultery even in concupiscence, if anyone has struck his eye lustfully
and moved his soul unchastely, when he judges murder even in a word of curse
or insult and in every impulse of anger and in neglect of love toward a
brother. So John teaches that he is a murderer who hates his brother.
Otherwise both the devil's ingenuity in malice and the Lord God's in
discipline, by which he arms us against the depths of the devil, would stand
in a small thing, if we were judged only in those offenses which even the
nations have decreed should be punished. How will our righteousness abound
over the Scribes and Pharisees, as the Lord prescribed, unless we have looked
through the abundance of its adversary, that is, of unrighteousness? But if
the head of unrighteousness is idolatry, it is first that we be fortified
against the abundance of idolatry, while we recognize it not only in what is
manifest.

* Chapter 3
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h3
:END:

For some time back there was no idol. Before the craftsmen of this monster had
bubbled forth, there were only temples and empty halls, as to this day traces
of antiquity remain in certain places. Yet idolatry was being practiced, not
in this name, but in this work. For even today it can be practiced outside a
temple and without an idol. But when the devil brought into the world the
craftsmen of statues and images and every kind of likeness, that crude
business of human calamity both obtained a name from idols and made progress.
From then on every art which in any way produces an idol has become the head
of idolatry. For it does not matter whether a modeller forms it, or an
engraver carves it, or an embroiderer weaves it, because it is of no account
from the material either, whether the idol is formed from plaster, or from
colors, or from stone, or from bronze, or from silver, or from thread.

For since idolatry may be practiced even without an idol, certainly, when an
idol is present, it does not matter of what sort it is, of what material, of
what shape, lest anyone think that only that is to be held an idol which has
been consecrated in human shape. For this the interpretation of the word is
needed. Εῖδος in Greek means form; from it by diminution εἴδωλον was derived,
and it has likewise made among us a little form. Therefore every form or
little form demands to be called an idol. Hence idolatry is all attendance and
slavery around every idol. Hence also every maker of an idol is of the same
and of one crime, unless the people committed too little idolatry who
consecrated for themselves the image of a calf and not of a man.

* Chapter 4
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h4
:END:

God forbids an idol as much to be made as to be worshipped. By how much it
comes first that that be made which can be worshipped, by so much it is prior
that it not be made, if it is not lawful to worship it. For this cause, namely
to root out the material of idolatry, the divine law proclaims, You shall not
make an idol, and joining to it, Nor the likeness of those things which are in
heaven and which are on earth and which are in the sea, it has forbidden
servants of God such arts throughout the whole world. Enoch had gone before,
predicting that the demons and the spirits of the deserted angels would turn
all the elements, the whole census of the world, the things contained in
heaven, in the sea, in the earth, into idolatry, that they might be
consecrated in God's place against God. Human error therefore worships all
things except the very Maker of all. Their images are idols; the consecration
of images is idolatry. Whatever idolatry commits must necessarily be counted
against the craftsman of any idol whatsoever and of whatsoever idol.

Finally the same Enoch in one threat pre-condemns both the worshippers of the
idol and its makers. And again: I swear to you, sinners, that unto the day of
the blood of perdition repentance is prepared. You who serve stones, and who
make images of gold and silver and wood and stone and clay, and serve phantoms
and demons and spirits in shrines, and all errors not according to knowledge,
you will find no help from them. But Isaiah says, You are witnesses, if there
is a god besides me. And they were not then who fashion and carve, all vain,
who make things pleasing to themselves, which will not profit them. And
thereafter that whole pronouncement, how it detests the craftsmen, how the
worshippers. Its close is, Know that their heart is ash and earth, and no one
can free his own soul. Where equally David also the makers. Let those who make
them become such, he says. And why should I, a man of modest memory, suggest
anything further? What should I recall from the Scriptures? As if either the
voice of the Holy Spirit were not enough, or it were still to be deliberated
whether the Lord has cursed and condemned first the very makers of those
things whose worshippers he curses and condemns.

* Chapter 5
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h5
:END:

We will answer more at length, plainly, the excuses of craftsmen of this kind,
who ought never to be admitted into the house of God, if anyone knows that
discipline. Already that voice customarily thrown up, I have nothing else by
which to live, can be struck back more sharply: So then, must you live? What
have you to do with God, if you live by your own laws? Then also they dare to
argue from the Scriptures, that the apostle said, As each was found, so let
him continue. We can therefore all continue in sins by that interpretation.
For there is none of us who was not found a sinner, since Christ descended for
no other cause than that of freeing sins. They say likewise that the same man
commanded, according to his own example, that each should work with his own
hands for a living. If this precept is defended from all hands, I believe even
bath-thieves live by their own hands, and robbers themselves act with their
hands for a living, likewise forgers work bad letters not with their feet, of
course, but with their hands, and actors elaborate a living not with their
hands alone, but with all their members. Let the church therefore lie open to
all who are supported by hands and by their own work, if there is no exception
of arts which the discipline of God does not receive.

But someone says against the proposition of the forbidden likeness: Why then
did Moses in the desert make an image of a serpent from bronze? Figures are a
separate matter, which were laid down beforehand for some hidden arrangement,
not for the outlay of the law, but as a pattern of their own cause. Otherwise
if we interpret these things as adversaries of the law, do we not also, as the
Marcionites do, ascribe inconstancy to the Almighty, whom they destroy in this
way as changeable, while he forbids in one place and commands in another? But
if anyone dissembles that that figure of the bronze serpent hung up in that
manner designated a figure of the Lord's cross, which would free us from the
serpents, that is from the angels of the devil, while it hangs up the devil
himself, that is the serpent, slain through itself, or whatever other
exposition of that figure has been revealed to the more worthy, provided the
apostle affirms that all things then happened to the people figuratively, it
is well that the same God, as by the law he forbade a likeness to be made, by
an extraordinary precept enjoined the likeness of a serpent. If you observe
the same God, you have his law, You shall not make a likeness. If you also
look to the precept of the likeness made afterwards, then you too imitate
Moses, so as not to make any image against the law, unless God has commanded
you also.

* Chapter 6
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h6
:END:

If no law of God had forbidden idols to be made by us, if no voice of the Holy
Spirit were threatening the makers of idols no less than the worshippers, we
would interpret from our very sacrament that arts of this kind are against the
faith. For how have we renounced the devil and his angels, if we make them?
What divorce have we declared to those, I do not say with whom, but from whom
we live? What discord have we taken up against those to whom we are bound for
the sake of our maintenance? Can you have denied with the tongue what you
confess with the hand? destroy by a word what you build by a deed? preach one
God, you who make so many? preach the true God, you who make false ones? I
make them, someone says, but I do not worship them: as if he dared not worship
for any other cause than that for which he also ought not to make them, namely
for the offense of God on both sides. Nay, you do worship, you who make them
so that they can be worshipped. And you worship not with the breath of some
cheapest steam, but with your own; not with the soul of a beast expended, but
with your own soul. To them you sacrifice your talent, to them you pour your
sweat as a libation, to them you kindle your skill. You are more to them than
a priest, since through you they have a priest; your diligence is their
divinity. Do you deny that you worship what you make? But they do not deny it,
for whom you slay this fatter and greater victim, your salvation.

* Chapter 7
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h7
:END:

All day the zeal of faith will plead to this part, groaning that a Christian
comes from idols into the church, comes from an opposing workshop into the
house of God, raises to God the Father hands that are mothers of idols, adores
with these hands which outside are put to worship against God, brings those
hands to the body of the Lord which confer bodies on demons. Nor is this
enough. Let it be a small thing if they receive from other hands what they
contaminate, but they themselves also hand on to others what they have
contaminated. Makers of idols are enrolled in the ecclesiastical order. O
crime! Once the Jews laid hands on Christ; these daily assault his body. O
hands that should be cut off! It may be seen already whether it was said by
way of likeness: If your hand scandalizes you, cut it off. What hands are more
to be cut off than those in which the Lord's body is scandalized?

* Chapter 8
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h8
:END:

There are also other kinds of a good many arts which, even if they do not
touch the fabrication of idols, yet furnish, by the same crime, those things
without which idols can do nothing. For it does not differ whether you build
or whether you adorn, if you have fitted out a temple, an altar, a shrine of
one, if you have beaten out gold-leaf, or have manufactured insignia or even a
house. Greater is work of this kind, which confers not a likeness but
authority. If the necessity of maintenance is stretched so far, they have also
other kinds which, without a swerve from the discipline, that is without the
fashioning of an idol, may furnish help for a living. A plasterer knows how to
mend roofs as well and to lay on plaster and to line a cistern and to run out
mouldings and to crimp many other ornaments on walls besides images. A painter
too, and a marble-worker, and a bronze-worker, and every engraver, knows his
own breadths, which are certainly much easier. For he who draws a figure, how
much more easily does he paint a panel? He who carves Mars from linden, how
much more quickly does he put together a cupboard? There is no art that is not
near to another art. Nothing is empty of another. There are as many veins of
arts as there are lusts of men.

But there is a difference in wages and in the prices of the hand. Likewise
there is a difference in labor. A smaller wage is made up by more frequent
work. How many walls desire figures? How many temples and halls are built for
idols? But houses and palaces and baths and blocks of flats, how many? The
slipper and the light shoe are gilded daily; Mercury and Serapis are not
daily. Let it suffice for the profit of the crafts. Luxury and ambition are
more frequent than every superstition. Ambition will desire platters and cups
more readily than superstition will. Crowns too are spent more by luxury than
by solemnity. When therefore we exhort to these kinds of crafts which do not
touch an idol and what belongs to an idol, yet there are often things common
to men which are common also to idols, we ought to beware of this too, lest
anything, with us knowing it, be demanded by any from our hands (??) as a
matter of idols. But if we have granted it and have not used remedies so well
worn, I do not think we are free from the contagion of idolatry, whose hands,
not of men who do not know, are caught in the service or in the honor and use
of demons.

* Chapter 9
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h9
:END:

We notice among the arts also certain professions liable to idolatry. Of
astrologers there is not even to be speaking. But since someone in these days
has challenged us, defending for himself continuance in that profession, I
will use a few words. I do not allege that he honors the idols whose names he
has written on the heaven, to whom he has made over all the power of God, that
for this reason men do not think God is to be sought, presuming that we are
driven by the immutable decree of the stars. I put forward one thing, that
those deserter angels of God, lovers of women, are also betrayers of this
curiosity, and for that reason also condemned by God. O divine sentence,
persistent even to the earth, to which even those who do not know it render
testimony! The mathematicians are expelled, as their angels were. The city and
Italy are forbidden to the mathematicians, as heaven to their angels. The same
penalty of exile is upon the disciples and the masters.

But magi and astrologers came from the east. We know the partnership of magic
and astrology with one another. The first interpreters of the stars therefore
announced Christ born, the first gave gifts. By this name, I think, they bound
Christ to themselves. What then? Will the religion of those magi now also be a
patronage for mathematicians? Is astrology today, I suppose, of Christ; does
it observe and preach the stars of Christ, not of Saturn and Mars and of each
of the dead of that same order? But that knowledge was conceded up to the
gospel, so that, Christ having been brought forth, no one thereafter should
interpret anyone's nativity from heaven. For they offered that frankincense
and myrrh and gold to the infant Lord then as a close of sacrifice and of
worldly glory, which Christ was about to take away. What therefore a dream
suggested to the same magi, without doubt from the will of God, that they
should go into their own country, but by another way, not by that by which
they had come, that is, that they should not walk in their former sect, not
that Herod should not pursue them, who did not even pursue them, not even
knowing they had departed by another way, since he did not know either by
which they had come. So we ought to understand a right way and a discipline.
Therefore a command was given to the magi, that thereafter they should walk
otherwise.

So also that other species of magic which works by miracles, having rivalled
even against Moses, drew the patience of God even to the gospel. For
thereafter Simon Magus, already now a believer, because he was still thinking
something of his juggler's sect, namely that among the miracles of his
profession he might also traffic the Holy Spirit through the laying on of
hands, was cursed by the apostles and cast out of the faith; another magus,
who was with Sergius Paulus, because he opposed the same apostles, was
punished with the loss of his lights. This, I believe, astrologers too would
have brought back, if any had fallen in with the apostles. Yet when magic is
punished, of which astrology is a species, the species also is certainly
condemned in the kind. After the gospel you will nowhere find either sophists
or Chaldeans or enchanters or soothsayers or magi, unless plainly punished.
Where is the wise, where the scribe, where the disputer of this age? Has God
not made foolish the wisdom of this world? You know nothing, mathematician, if
you did not know that you would be a Christian. If you did know, you ought to
have known this too, that you would have nothing to do with that profession.
It itself would instruct you concerning its own danger, which sings beforehand
the climacterics of others. You have neither part nor lot in that account. He
cannot hope for the kingdoms of heaven whose finger or rod abuses the heaven.

* Chapter 10
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h10
:END:

But inquiry must be made also concerning schoolmasters, and indeed the rest of
the professors of letters. Nay, it is not to be doubted that they are
bordering on idolatry of many kinds. First, they must preach the gods of the
nations, declare their names, genealogies, fables, and whatever honorific
ornaments of them, then observe their solemnities and feast days, that they
may reckon their own revenues from them. What schoolmaster, without the tablet
of the seven idols, will still frequent the Quinquatria? He consecrates the
very first fee of a new pupil to Minerva both in honor and in name, so that,
even if he is not said to have been profaned to some idol in so many words
from an idol-sacrifice, he is to be avoided as an idolater. What less of
defilement is there than that which a gain named for the names and honors of
an idol furnishes? How the Minervalia of Minerva, how the Saturnalia of
Saturn, who must also be celebrated by little slaves at the time of the
Saturnalia. New-year gifts too must be caught at, and the Septimontium, and
the things of midwinter and of dear kinship, all honorary dues, the flowers of
the school must be crowned; the flaminicae and aediles sacrifice; the school
is honored with holidays when they have been created. The same is done on an
idol's birthday; every pomp of the devil is frequented. Who will think these
things befit a Christian, except one who will think they befit even a
non-master?

We know it can be said: If it is not lawful for the servants of God to teach
letters, it will not even be lawful to learn them, and, How is anyone to be
trained to human prudence for the time, or to any sense or act, when
literature is an instrument for all of life? How do we repudiate secular
studies, without which the divine cannot be? Let us therefore see the
necessity of literary learning; let us look to it, that in part it cannot be
admitted, in part it can be avoided. It befits the faithful more to learn
letters than to teach them; for the reason of learning and of teaching is
different. If a believer teaches letters, he without doubt, while he teaches,
commends the embedded preachings of idols, while he hands them on, affirms
them, while he recalls them, bears testimony. He seals the gods themselves by
this name, when the law forbids, as we have said, that gods be pronounced and
that this name be placed in vain. Hence the first faith is built for the devil
from the beginnings of learning. Ask whether he commits idolatry who
catechizes concerning idols. But when a believer learns these things, if he
already understands what it is, he neither receives nor admits them, much more
if he does not yet understand. Or when he has begun to understand, he ought
first to understand what he first learned, that is, concerning God and the
faith. Accordingly he will spit those things out and not receive them, and he
will be as safe as one who knowingly receives poison from one who does not
know and does not drink it. To this man necessity is counted for an excuse,
because he cannot learn otherwise. But it is so much easier not to teach
letters than not to learn them, as also a believing pupil will more easily not
approach the remaining defilements of the schools from public and private
solemnities than a master will not frequent them.

* Chapter 11
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h11
:END:

If we review the rest of the offenses concerning livelihoods, first
covetousness, the root of all evils, by which some, entangled, have suffered
shipwreck around the faith (although idolatry also has been called
covetousness by the same apostle), then lying, the servant of covetousness (I
am silent about perjury, since it is not even lawful to swear): is trading
fitted for a servant of God? For the rest, if covetousness departs, what is
the cause of acquiring? When the cause of acquiring ceases, there will be no
necessity of trading. Grant now some justice of gain, secure from the watch
against covetousness and lying: I think that gain runs into the crime of
idolatry which belongs to the very soul and spirit of the idols, which fattens
every demon. Truly, is that not the principal idolatry? Let them see to it, if
the same wares, I mean frankincense and the rest of the foreign stuff, are of
use as a sacrifice of idols even to men for medicinal pigments, and to us
besides for the comforts of burial.

Certainly when the pomps, when the priesthoods, when the sacrifices of idols
are equipped from the risks, from the losses, from the inconveniences, from
the thoughts, from the runnings about or tradings, what else are you shown to
be than a procurator of idols? Let no one contend that in this way controversy
can be made against all tradings. The graver offenses, each according to the
greatness of the danger, extend the diligence of watch, so that we withdraw
not only from these, but also from those things through which they are done.
For although it is done by others, it does not matter, if it is through me. I
ought to be necessary to no one in anything when he does what is not lawful
for me. From this, that I am forbidden to do it, I ought to understand that I
must take care that it not be done through me. Finally I observe this
prejudgment in another cause of no lighter guilt. For because fornication has
been forbidden me, I furnish others for that matter nothing either of work or
of conscience. For because I have segregated my own flesh from brothels, I
recognize that I cannot practice either pandering or places of that kind for
another's sake. So also the prohibition of murder shows me that the
gladiator-trainer too is barred from the church: nor will he not do through
himself what he supplies to another to be done.

See a nearer prejudgment. If a contractor of public victims comes to the
faith, will you permit him to remain in that business? Or if one already
faithful has undertaken to do it, will you think he should be kept in the
church? I think not, unless someone will dissemble also concerning the
incense-seller. Of course the procuration of blood comes to some, that of
odors to others. If, before there were idols in the world, still shapeless
idolatry was transacted by these wares, if even now the work of idolatry is
almost without an idol accomplished by burnings of odors, then what work is
greater, and toward the demons, than the incense-seller's. For idolatry is
easier without an idol than without the incense-seller's merchandise. Let us
thoroughly ask the conscience of the faith itself. With what mouth will a
Christian incense-seller, if he passes through the temples, with what mouth
will he spit and blow upon the smoking altars for which he himself has
provided? With what constancy will he exorcize his own nurslings, to whom he
furnishes his own house as a storeroom? If he indeed has shut out a demon, let
him not please himself concerning the faith. For he has not shut out an enemy.
He ought easily to have obtained it from him whom he feeds daily. No art,
therefore, no profession, no trading which administers anything either for the
equipping or for the forming of idols will be able to lack the title of
idolatry; unless we interpret idolatry as something else altogether than
attendance upon idols to be worshipped.

* Chapter 12
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h12
:END:

We applaud ourselves badly concerning the necessities of human maintenance, if
after faith has been sealed we say: I have nothing by which to live. For here
I will now answer more fully that abrupt proposition. It is said too late. For
it ought to have been deliberated before, after the likeness of that most
provident builder who first reckons the costs of the work with his own
strengths, lest, when he has begun, he afterward, having failed, blush. But
even now you have the Lord's sayings, examples taking away from you every
excuse. For what do you say? I shall be in want. But the Lord calls the needy
blessed. I shall not have food: but Do not, he says, think about food; and of
clothing we have the example of the lilies. I needed substance: but all things
are to be sold and divided to the needy. But provision must be made for
children and posterity: no one putting his hand to the plow and looking back
is fit for the work. But I was under contract: no one can serve two masters.
If you wish to be a disciple of the Lord, you must take up your cross and
follow the Lord, that is, your straits and your torments, or the body alone,
which is in the manner of a cross. Parents, spouses, children will have to be
left for God's sake. Do you still hesitate concerning arts and tradings and
professions even for the sake of children and parents?

It was already then shown to us that both pledges and crafts and businesses
are to be left for the Lord's sake, when James and John, called by the Lord,
leave both father and boat, when Matthew is raised from the toll-booth, when
even to bury a father was too slow for faith. None of those whom the Lord
chose said, I have nothing by which to live. Faith does not fear hunger. It
knows that even hunger is to be despised for God's sake no less than every
kind of death. It has learned not to look back at life, how much more at food?
How few have fulfilled these things? But what is difficult with men is easy
with God. Yet if we flatter ourselves concerning the gentleness and clemency
of God, so as not to grant to necessities as far as the affinities of
idolatry, but to shun every breath of it like a plague even from far off, not
in these things only which we have sent before, but in the whole series of
human superstition, whether made over to its own gods, or to the dead, or to
kings, as belonging to the same unclean spirits, now through sacrifices and
priesthoods, now through spectacles and this kind, now through feast days.

* Chapter 13
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h13
:END:

But of sacrifices and of priesthoods what should I say? Of spectacles,
however, and pleasures of that kind we have already filled a volume of our
own. In this place there ought to be a review concerning feast days and other
extraordinary solemnities, to which we sometimes subscribe by our wantonness,
sometimes by our timidness, against the common faith and discipline. On this
(??) I will first take my stand, whether a servant of God ought to communicate
with the nations themselves also in things of this kind, whether in dress, or
in food, or in any other kind of their gladness. Rejoice with those who
rejoice and weep with those who weep was said of brothers by the apostle,

weeps. So also Eleazar among the dead, having obtained refreshment in
Abraham's bosom, and the rich man on the other side, set in the torment of
fire, compensate by a rival retribution the alternating turns of evils and
goods.

There are certain days of gifts, which among some discharge a title of honor,
among others a debt of wages. Now then, you say, I will receive my own, or I
will repay another's. If men have consecrated this custom for themselves from
superstition, you, a stranger to all their vanity, why do you take part in
idol-sacrificed solemnities, as if it were prescribed for you too concerning
the day, so that you may not pay or receive what you owe a man, or what is
owed you by a man, apart from observation of the day? Give a form in which you
wish it to be done with you. For why do you also hide, when by another's
ignorance you contaminate your own conscience? If you are not unknown to be a
Christian, you are being tested, and you act against the other's conscience as
if you were not a Christian: but if you will also be concealed, having been
tested you are made over. Certainly, whether this way or that, you are guilty
of being ashamed of God. But whoever has been ashamed of me among men, I also
will be ashamed of him, he says, among my Father who is in the heavens.

But indeed most have already brought it into their mind that it must be

Further, the blasphemy which we must by every means avoid is, I think, this,
if one of us leads a heathen to a just blasphemy, either by fraud or by injury

discipline. If I wished to please men, he says, I would not be a servant of
Christ. But the same man elsewhere commands that we take care to please all.
As I, he says, please all in all things. Did he, I suppose, please men by
celebrating the Saturnalia and the Kalends of January? or by modesty and
patience? or by gravity, or by kindness, or by integrity? Accordingly when he
says, I have become all things to all, that I may gain all, was he an idolater
to idolaters? a heathen to the heathen? a man of the world to men of the
world?

But even if he does not forbid us to keep company with idolaters and
adulterers and the rest of the criminal, saying, Otherwise you would go out of
the world, he does not so let go the reins of company that, since it is
necessary that we both live together and mix with sinners, we can also sin
together. Where there is the commerce of life, which the apostle grants, there
to sin, which no one permits. It is lawful to live with the heathen, not
lawful to die with them. Let us live with all; let us rejoice together from
the communion of nature, not of superstition. We are equals in soul, not in
discipline, joint possessors of the world, not of error. But if we have no
right of communion in things of this kind with outsiders, how much more wicked
it is to frequent these things among brothers. Who can bear this or defend it?
The Holy Spirit reproaches the Jews for their own feast days. Your sabbaths,
he says, and new moons and ceremonies my soul hates. For us, to whom sabbaths
are foreign, and new moons and holidays once loved by God, the Saturnalia and
the January feasts and the midwinter rites and the Matronalia are frequented,
gifts go about, new-year presents, games sound together, banquets roar. O
better faith of the nations toward their own sect, which claims for itself no
solemnity of the Christians! They would not have communicated with us in the
Lord's day, not in Pentecost, even if they knew them; for they would fear lest
they seem to be Christians. We are not afraid lest we be pronounced heathen.
If something must also be indulged to the flesh, you have it; I will not say
only your own days, but even more. For to the heathen each feast is once a
year; to you it is also every eighth day. Pick out the several solemnities of
the nations and set them out in a row: they will not be able to fill
Pentecost.

But let your works shine, he says. But now our shops and our doors shine. You
may already find more doors of the heathen without lamps and laurels than of
Christians. Of this kind too, what seems good? If it is an honor of an idol,
without doubt the honor of an idol is idolatry. If it is for a man's sake, let
us recognize that all idolatry is for a man's sake. Let us recognize that all
idolatry is a cult toward men, since it is agreed even among their own that
the gods of the nations themselves were men before. And so it does not matter
whether that superstition is rendered to men of a former age or of this.
Idolatry has been condemned not because of the persons who are set forth, but
because of those offices which belong to demons. There are to be rendered to
Caesar the things that are Caesar's. It is well that he added: and the things
that are God's to God. What then are Caesar's? Namely those concerning which
the consultation was then being moved, whether the census should be rendered
to Caesar, or not. Therefore the Lord also demanded that the coin be shown
him, and asked whose the image was, and when he had heard, Caesar's, Render,
he said, the things that are Caesar's to Caesar, and the things that are God's
to God, that is, Caesar's image to Caesar, which is on the coin, and God's
image to God, which is in man, so that you may render money indeed to Caesar,
yourself to God. Otherwise, what will be God's, if all things are Caesar's?

Therefore, you say, is a lamp before the doors and a laurel on the posts an
honor of God? Not at all what is an honor of God, but what is an honor of him
who is honored in God's place by offices of this kind, as far as is in the
open, the working which is in secret remaining safe, arriving at the demons.
For we ought to be sure, if any are in the dark through ignorance of secular
literature, that there are even gods of doorways among the Romans, Cardea
named from hinges, and Forculus from doors, and Limentinus from the threshold,
and Janus himself from the gate: and we know, of course, that although the
names are empty and invented, yet when they are drawn into superstition they
snatch to themselves demons and every unclean spirit through the bond of
consecration. Otherwise demons have no name one by one, but they find a name
there where they also find a pledge. Even among the Greeks we read of Apollo
Θυραῖον and Antelii demons as presidents of doorways. These things therefore
the Holy Spirit, foreseeing from the beginning, also sang beforehand through
the most ancient prophet Enoch, that even doorways would come into
superstition. For we see other doorways too worshipped in the baths. But if
there are those who are worshipped in doorways, to them both the lamps and the
laurels will belong. You will have done it to an idol, whatever you have done
to a doorway.

Here I also call to witness from the authority of God, because it is not safe
to withdraw whatever has been shown to one, surely for the sake of all. I know
a brother chastised severely through a vision on the same night, because,
public rejoicings having been suddenly announced, his slaves had crowned his
door. And yet he himself had not crowned it or commanded it; for he had gone
forth before, and having returned he had blamed the deed. So far are we
estimated before God in things of this kind even from the discipline of our
household. Therefore as to what belongs to the honors of kings or emperors, we
have it sufficiently prescribed that we ought to be in all obedience,
according to the apostle's precept, subject to magistrates and princes and
powers, but within the limits of the discipline, as far as we are separated
from idolatry. For this reason also that example of the three brothers ran
before, who, otherwise obedient toward king Nebuchadnezzar, most constantly
refused the honor of his image, proving that idolatry is whatever is raised
beyond the measure of human honor after the likeness of divine loftiness. So
also Daniel, in other things supported upon Darius, was in office so long as
he was free from danger to the discipline. For lest he undergo that, he feared
the royal lions no more than they the royal fires.

Let them therefore kindle lamps daily, they who have no light; let them fasten
to the posts laurels soon to burn, they whom fires threaten; to them belong
both the testimonies of darkness and the omens of punishments. You are the
light of the world and a tree forever green. If you have renounced temples, do
not make your door a temple. I have said too little: if you have renounced
brothels, do not put on your house the face of a new brothel.

Now indeed there must be a review concerning the mere dress and apparatus of
honor. Each has his own proper habit, as much for daily use as for honor and
dignity. Therefore that purple and the gold ornament of the neck were among
the Egyptians and the Babylonians marks of dignity by the same custom by which
now the bordered togas or state robes or palmate togas and the golden crowns
of provincial priests are, but not by the same condition. For they were
conferred only in the name of honor upon those who earned the friendship of
kings. Whence also they were called the purple-clad of kings from the purple,
as among us candidates from the white toga, but not so that that dress was
also bound to priesthoods or to any offices of idols. For if it had been so,
men of such great holiness and constancy would at once have refused the
stained habits, and it would at once have appeared that Daniel did not serve
idols, and did not worship Bel or the dragon, which appeared much later. That
purple therefore was simple, and was not yet of dignity, but a mark of free
birth among the barbarians.

For as both Joseph, who had been a slave, and Daniel, who had changed his
standing through captivity, obtained the citizenship of Babylon and of Egypt
through the habit of barbarian free birth, so among us too the faithful, if it
has been necessary, the bordered toga may be allowed even to boys, and the
boy's stole, marks of birth, not of power, of family, not of honor, of rank,
not of superstition. For the rest, purple or the other insignia of dignities
and powers, dedicated from the beginning to idolatry inserted into dignity and
into the powers, have the stain of their own profanation, since besides even
the idols themselves are clothed with bordered togas and state robes and broad
stripes, and fasces too and rods are carried before them, and deservedly. For
demons are the magistrates of this age; they wear the fasces and purples,
insignia of one college. What then will you profit, if you indeed use the
dress, but do not administer its works? No one can seem clean among the
unclean. If you put on a tunic stained through itself, it will perhaps be able
not to be stained through you, but you will not be able to be clean through
it.

Now then, you who argue from Joseph and Daniel, know that old things and new,
crude and polished, begun and unfolded, slavish and free, are not always to be
compared. For they were slaves even by condition: but you, slave of no one,
inasmuch as of Christ alone, who has freed you even from the captivity of the
world, will have to act from the Lord's pattern. That Lord went in humility
and ignobility, uncertain of a dwelling: for the Son of man, he says, has not
where to lay his head; unkempt in dress, for he would not have said, Behold,
those who are clothed in soft things are in kings' houses; finally inglorious
in face and look, as Isaiah also had pronounced. If he exercised the right of
power not even over his own, for whom he performed a mean ministry, if
finally, conscious of his own kingdom, he fled from being made a king, he most
fully gave a pattern to his own by laying low every peak and dress both of
dignity and of power. For who would have used these more than the Son of God?
What fasces and how great would have led him forth, what purple would have
flowered from his shoulders, what gold would have rayed from his head, unless
he had judged the glory of the world foreign both to himself and to his own?
Therefore what he would not, he rejected; what he rejected, he condemned; what
he condemned, he counted in the pomp of the devil. For he would not have
condemned them unless they were not his: but they could not be another's,
unless the devil's, which are not God's. If you have sworn off the devil's
pomp, whatever you have touched from it, know that that is idolatry.

Or let this remind you that all the powers and dignities of this age are not
only foreign but even hostile to God, that through them punishments have been
decreed against the servants of God, through them also the penalties prepared
for the impious are unknown. But both your birth and your substance are
troublesome to you against idolatry. Remedies for avoiding cannot be lacking,
since even if they have been lacking, there remains that one thing by which,
made happier, you are not a magistrate on earth, but in the heavens.
