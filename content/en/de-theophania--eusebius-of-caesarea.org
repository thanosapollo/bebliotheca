* On the Theophany

Just as in the example among ourselves the mind in us, invisible and unseen,
of which no human being has ever known what it is and of what sort it is in
essence, seated like a king in secret within his own treasuries, deliberates
what is to be done, and a Word proceeds from him, only-begotten, as born from
the innermost recesses of a father, who is also first messenger of the
paternal thoughts to all and proclaims into the open the things deliberated in
secret by the father, and accomplishes the counsels by going forth into the
hearing of all, and then some partake of the benefit from the Word, but the
unseen and invisible mind, the father of the Word, no one has ever seen with
eyes: in the same way, or rather beyond every image and example, the perfect
Word of God the all-sovereign, as only-begotten Son of a Father, not
constituted by uttered power, nor constructed in nature from syllables of
nouns and verbs, nor signified in a voice through air being struck, but being
the living and active Son Word of the God over all, and existing in substance
as "power of God and wisdom of God," proceeds from the paternal Godhead and
kingship, being a good offspring of a good Father; and as common savior of all
he channels all things ..... 2 who else ever of those hymned from of old was
known and proclaimed beforehand as this one theologized among us, by prophetic
voices from above before countless times among the children of the Hebrews
long ago beloved of God? who also, taking in advance the place of his
theophany and the times of his coming and the manner of his life and his
powers and words and achievements, deposited them in sacred books. 3 But lest
my discourse now stretch to length over all his mighty works, let his death be
set before the eyes, which the interpreter, the wrapping of the divine Word,
the statue revealed, is confessed among all to have undergone. And the death
itself that is cried aloud has much of wonder, not having come about in common
with the rest of men. For not destroyed by disease, nor by a noose, nor by
fire, nor even at the trophy of the cross itself cut down with iron like the
rest of criminals, nor suffering at all from any human being any of the things
that customarily kill, did he undergo a violent end, but as he of his own will
merely handed over his instrument to those plotting against him, it was at
once raised up from the earth, and he, having cried with a great voice and
said that he committed the spirit to the Father, of his own accord made the
withdrawal from the body. Therefore also before the death he handed this very
thing over to his acquaintances, teaching and saying: "No one takes my soul
from me, but I lay it down of myself. I have authority to lay it down and
again I have authority to take it." And again: "I am the good shepherd and I
know my own and my own know me" and "I lay down my soul for the sheep." And
the cause of the death he sets forth in brief saying: "Unless the grain of
wheat falling into the earth die, it remains itself alone, but if it die, it
bears much fruit." Offering such proofs then at the death, of his own accord
he made the withdrawal from the body. Then when his body had been taken by the
acquaintances and given over to the fitting burial, on the third day he
received it again, he himself who had withdrawn of his own will. And again he
shows himself in flesh, in body, that very one as he was at first, to his own
disciples, with whom also having conversed a little and spent some time, he
goes up whence he also came, under their eyes the journey and ascent into the
heavens

having set out, to whom also having handed over injunctions about what was to
be done he appointed them teachers of all the nations of the highest piety.
What then remains after these things but to declare the very chief point of
the whole, of what sort of cause it had, I mean the much famed end of the life
and the manner of the passion and the great wonder of his revival after death.
And after the contemplation of these things, passing again to the proofs, we
shall confirm them through clear testimonies. Using then a mortal instrument
for the causes we have already said, as a statue befitting God, and through
this very thing as a great king through an interpreter having gone through the
human life, he accomplished all things worthily of divine power. If then after
the sojourns among men he had somehow otherwise become unseen and flown away
suddenly, having stolen away in secret the interpreter and his own statue,
having been eager to escape death by flight, and then somewhere having himself
of himself joined the mortal to corruption and destruction, he would have
seemed to all a phantom, and neither would he have accomplished for himself
the things that were fitting, being life and Word of God and power of God, yet
handing over his own interpreter to corruption and destruction, nor would the
things done by him against demons through the engagement with death have been
granted an end, nor would it have been known where he had gone, nor would he
have been believed by those who had not received him, nor would he have
appeared stronger than the nature of death, nor would he have freed the mortal
from its own nature, nor would he have been heard throughout the whole
inhabited world of men, nor would he have persuaded his disciples to despise
death, nor would he have set forth to those who pursue his teaching the hope
of the life with God after death, nor would he have fulfilled the promises of
his words, nor would he have furnished results in agreement with the prophetic
forecasts about him, nor would he have contended through the last contest of
all. And this was the one against death. Therefore for all these reasons,
since it was necessary that the mortal instrument after the sufficient service
which it rendered to the divine Word should meet an end befitting God, in this
way also death was being managed for him. For two things remaining for the
end, either to hand over the whole to corruption and destruction and make the
catastrophe of the life the most shameful of the whole drama, or to show
himself stronger than death by divine power, presenting the mortal as
immortal, the first was unfitting to the promise. For it is not proper to fire
to cool nor to light to darken; so neither to life to put to death nor to the
Word of God to act unreasonably. What reason then would it have that he who
promised life to others should overlook his own instrument being corrupted and
hand over his statue to destruction, and that he who was wooing immortality
for those who fled to him should outrage with death the interpreter of his
Godhead? Therefore the second was necessary, I mean showing himself stronger
than death. How then was this to be done? Secretly then and by stealth, or
conspicuously and manifestly to all? But the achievement done in darkness and
hidden and known to no one would have benefited no one; but being cried aloud
and heard unto all it furnished to all the benefit from the wonder. Reasonably
then, since it was necessary to show his instrument stronger than death and to
do this not into what is unnoticed but before the eyes of men, he does not
flee death; for he would have been thought a coward and weaker than death; but
through the engagement with death as with an antagonist he presents the mortal
as immortal, having taken up this last contest for the salvation and
immortality of all. For the first was being worked by him against demons for
the overthrow of the polytheistic error . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . .

And the second was coming about for the dissolving of the tyranny of death.
For it was necessary that what was stronger than the godless and demonic error
and consistent with the Word of God and a worthy honor of his own work, namely
the victory against death, should receive its due. For the demons gathered
against him and their leader together with the spirits that go about over the
earth around the air, invisible to the eyes of mortals, having given their
backs at the first encounter with him, were watching the second contest,
awaiting thereafter the last catastrophe of the life and expecting that the
end of the mortal would be like that of all men. For they would never have
expected mortal nature to become stronger than death, and that death is common
king of all who have once tasted mortal generation. But they thought him the
most terrible of all, whom no one would flee and withdraw from, but he after
the first trophies against demons was already arraying himself also against
death. And just as if someone wished to show us a vessel unburnable and
stronger than fire in nature, he would not have presented the wonder otherwise
than by handing over to fire what he had in hand and then taking it out of the
fire safe and uncorrupted, in the same way also the life-giving Word of God of
all things, wishing to show the mortal instrument, which he had used for the
salvation of men, stronger than death, and to make it a partner of his own
life and immortality, went upon a very useful economy, leaving the body for a
short time and handing the mortal over to death for a proof of its own nature,
then not long after taking it away from death again, for a demonstration of
the divine power, through which he showed the eternal life announced by him
stronger than every death. This then was the first cause; and the second was
the showing of the divine power that had dwelt in the body. For since
previously men were deifying men truly mortal who had been conquered by death
and had received a common end, and were naming heroes and gods those who had
been mastered by death, there also reasonably the philanthropic Word of God
showed himself for this cause, showing to men a nature stronger than death,
and leading the mortal after the dissolution to the second life, and
furnishing to all to see a trophy of immortality against death, and teaching
to confess as true God in death only this one who had bound on the prizes of
the victory against death. And a third cause of the saving death would be that
which secret words contain. What are these? It was a victim given over to
death as a life-ransom of the common race, a victim slaughtered for the common
flock of men, a victim to avert demonic error. When then one victim and great
sacrifice, the all-holy body of our Savior, had been slaughtered like a sheep
for the race of men and offered as a life-ransom of all nations formerly held
in the impiety of demonic error, all the power of the unclean and unholy
demons was thereafter overthrown, and all earthly and deceitful error was at
once loosed and let go by a stronger power. The saving sacrifice from men
then, the very bodily instrument of the divine Word, was being consecrated for
the common flock of men, and this was the victim thrown to death through the
slander of men, concerning which voices of sacred words cry out, sometimes
saying thus: "Behold the lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world," and
sometimes proclaiming beforehand thus: "As a sheep he was led to slaughter,
and as a lamb before its shearer is voiceless," and they teach the cause
adding: "This one bears our sins, and is in pain for us. And we reckoned him
to be in toil and in blow and in ill-treatment, but he was wounded because of
our sins, and has been softened because of our lawlessness; the discipline of
our peace is upon him. By

his bruise we were healed. All we like sheep went astray, each went astray in
his own way. And the Lord delivered him for our sins." The bodily instrument
of the divine Word was being consecrated then for these causes, but the great
high priest who is priest to the all-leading and all-sovereign God, being
other than the victim, Word of God and "power of God and wisdom of God," not
long after called the mortal back from death and presented this firstfruits of
our common salvation as a partaker of divine life and immortality, having
raised this up for all men as a trophy of victory against death and against
the demonic array and as an aversion of the human sacrifices formerly
performed, whence there was also laid upon him the title of Christ conforming
to the high priest among the Hebrews. He received then two names, of which the
name of Jesus signifies the saving sacrifice, and that of Christ according to
the custom of the Hebrews makes clear the high priest, the Word of God being
priest for us all. In addition to what has been said there would also be
another greatest cause of the saving death, which will be stated. For since it
was necessary for his disciples to see with eyes clearly a regeneration of
life after death, on which he taught them to hang their hopes, for which also
he was urging them to hold to the yoke of piety, reasonably he furnished this
for them to see with eyes accurately; for those who were going to pursue a
pious life needed first of all to receive this most necessary lesson through a
clear sight, and much more those who were about to proclaim him throughout the
whole inhabited world almost at once and to announce to all men the teaching
and knowledge of God put forward by him to all the nations. Who needed to take
a greatest persuasion of the life after death, so that fearless and without
fear of death they might eagerly take up the contest against the polytheistic
error; for if they had not practiced despising death, they would never have
prepared themselves for the terrible things. Therefore necessarily arming them
against the tyranny of death, he did not hand over the lesson in little words
and voices nor composing like men the discourse about immortality of soul
persuasively and from probabilities, but by the deed itself he showed them the
trophies against death. For of old formerly death the destroyer of the mortal
race was fearful to all; and his tyranny was thought a dissolution of the
whole nature of man, of soul together and body, and no one from of old was
able to take this terror away from men, and all cowered in fear, small and
great, rulers and slaves, kings together and peoples, and the multitudes of
all nations and tribes, at death, and there was no consolation of the evil for
men, not word, not manner, not life, not reasoning of wise men, not writings
of the ancients, not oracles of prophets, not appearances of angels; and being
stronger than all and higher, death held the victory against all, lofty,
boastful, proud, to whom the mortal race being enslaved was rolling in all
kinds of lawlessness, murders, unlawful acts, impieties, intricate error; for
death was cause of all these. For as no longer existing after death, they
lived doing things worthy of ten thousand deaths, and as in no way subject to
account because of the dissolution from death they lived an unlivable life,
not taking God into mind, not expecting the tribunals of divine judgment, not
kindling the memory of their own intellectual substance according to soul,
using one terrible president, Death, and having persuaded themselves that the
corruption of the bodies from this was a dissolution of the whole of
themselves, they proclaimed Death a rich god, whence also Plouton. And Death
was for them not a god alone, but also the things prized before this, the
things contributing to a life of pleasure for them. A god then for them was
the

pleasure of the fleshes, a god food, a god the seed into the earth, a god its
sprouting, a god the growth of tree-fruits, a god the luxury through
drunkenness, a god the desire of bodies, a god the pleasure of these. Hence
the mysteries of Demeter and Phersephatte and the seizure of Kore by Aidoneus
and again her sending up. Hence the rites of Dionysus and Heracles conquered
as by a stronger god, drunkenness, hence the adulterous orgies of Eros and
Aphrodite. Hence Zeus himself mad for women and loving Ganymede, and the
wanton fabrications of myths of pleasure-loving and passion-loving gods. And
death was cause of all these. For thinking death to be end and consummation of
the whole and dissolution and corruption of souls together and of bodies, and
that there is no other life than that of the body and the flesh, they lived a
life worse than every nature of irrational animals. Whom the all-sovereign
Word of God, having pitied, at the nod of the philanthropic Father hastened to
the defense, then as a most philanthropic king he devised the proof against
death through human nature, being himself life and Word of God and power of
God, but judging not to refute the terror of men without the one being helped.
Therefore the bodiless, using as a weapon a human and mortal body, contended
down death through the mortal. Hence the first mystery of the body itself was
being prepared, hence the trophy of the cross, hence the memory of the
immortal and eternal life was named "remembrance of him." For using a mortal
weapon and having shown a great wonder to all, he set up a trophy of
immortality against death through the mortal weapon. For he was letting the
mortal go as food to the beast, and it was at once nailed to the stake being
crucified, so that the nature of the mortal might be made known to all and
what was being done might escape neither men nor demons nor stronger powers.
For all needed to see accurately the mortal as in a greatest theater
confessing its own nature, so that next death coming on as a terrible beast
might refute himself as to what sort he ever was, and then the power of life
coming on after death might present again to all the victory against death,
having shown the mortal immortal. The power of the Word of God that held it
together was then letting the body go for a short time, and that which was
hung on the wood was immediately a corpse, but the Word the life-giver of all
was not what was being made a corpse, and the mortal was confessing its own
nature. And then the corpse now mastered by death, taken down by men, was
granted the customary care. And it was given over to a tomb according to the
laws of men. And the tomb was a newly made cave, a cave just hewn in rock, not
yet having experienced another body; for it had to be free for this one alone,
the one paradoxical corpse. And wonderful also to see was the rock standing
upright alone in a level place and containing only one cave within itself, so
that having become of many it might not overshadow the wonder of the one who
had contended down death. There then lay a corpse, the instrument of the
living Word, and a huge stone shut the cave, and death was much exulting in
this, as having taken this one too with those from of old as subject, but a
three-day time had not yet passed and life again was showing itself after the
sufficient proof of death. For if he had risen more quickly, arriving first,
he would not even have been thought to have died, but when he had truly been
lifted up and had truly died, and in time had truly received death. Then
indeed then the life-giving Word of God of all was showing the hope stored up
for all men through the regeneration of what was then mortal. What then
happens after these things? I would no longer be a teacher for you of the
things done. But the eyewitnesses of these things would be sufficient
witnesses of all, who through blood and soul at the sight confirmed the truth
of the things done and the

whole inhabited world by the power of the one witnessed filled with the piety
announced by them. These then, having become spectators of the things done
then, handed over in their own testimonies the regeneration grasped with their
own eyes. For the things done were not for them heard as far as word and
voice, but already were also contemplated in deeds and were even touched
accurately by those who had witnessed these things. Therefore having grasped
the clear thing both by autopsy and by truth and having seen the trophies of
the victory against death, reasonably they learned to despise death, and they
were teaching the same also to their own disciples, having received from the
Savior the pledges of the immortal life. 4 There was a man of reputation among
those in the armies, who had taken hold of Roman dignity and rule. And when
his genuine slave had been thrown at home paralyzed in the limbs, having seen
of what sort were the powers the Savior was showing toward others, healing
those who were ill, curing "every disease and every infirmity," having judged
by reasoning that the wonder was not according to man, he approaches as to
God, not looking to the apparent instrument of the body, through which he was
making the conversations with men, but to the unseen God who was showing forth
his own virtues through the mortal. Therefore he fell down and worshipped and
earnestly begged that he too might obtain the divine benefaction for the
child. 5 With how much authority the voice of the Savior was brought forth,
and how much virtue it presented, and of how much power it was filled, and how
great also was the philanthropy and readiness toward benefaction, so as to
promise his presence from what was at hand, and of what sort also was the
wonder of the benefaction, it is not even possible to think worthily. For not
to have uttered more to the chiliarch than "as you have believed, let it be to
you" and together with the word both to furnish the healing to his child and
to release at once from being ill the one held by death a little before, how
does it not present God truly speaking through a mortal voice? But if someone
is unbelieving toward this because of the excess of the wonder, yet he would
not reasonably take refuge against the forecast, on which a greater proof of
the deed will be shown, if someone take into mind that then there was one
Roman, the chiliarch who had come to the Savior, who showed a confession
toward him greater and more well-disposed than that of the nation of the Jews,
but our Savior prophesies that there will be many instead of one who are going
to come to him after that man, from those inhabiting both the climates toward
dawn and the east and those toward the setting sun, who through the
recognition of him and the confession will be deemed worthy of equal honor
with the forefathers of the Hebrews before God. And that the forefather of
those too, the very Abraham who is cried aloud, setting out from superstitious
fathers, changed his life, having withdrawn from the polytheistic error and
having recognized one God over all, he foretells that there will be like him
and like his children Isaac and Jacob countless among men throughout the whole
inhabited world, and of these especially the nations toward the east and those
inhabiting toward the setting sun, and he adds to these also the greatest of
the forecast: the Jews themselves, the descendants of those beloved of God,
boasting of Abraham and Isaac and Jacob, because of the contradiction and
unbelief toward him, as having been deprived of the light of knowledge, will
become cast out into the outer darkness, having proclaimed beforehand in this
way their ignorance and their last lack of learning and the deprivation of the
saving light. Where indeed it is fitting to set the mind upon the results and
to grasp with the eyes themselves that the Jews, who pride themselves on being
the race of the said men beloved of God, have become cast out not only from
the kingdom of God but also from their own sacred and royal metropolis. . . .
. . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . And instead of one chiliarch
who of old had come to the Savior, an unspeakable number of men from

all the nations, not chiliarchs only but also the multitude of the Roman camp,
as also countless rulers and governors who hold the nations and the lands, and
certain others superior to these in honor and dignity who are magnified in the
palaces, approaching like that chiliarch the Christ of God and through his
teaching having recognized the God of the men beloved of God who shone among
the Hebrews, have been deemed worthy of equal recompense with those very ones
before God the all-sovereign. 6 For when a great crowd was pressing upon Jesus
he embarked, it says, into one of the boats, which was Simon's. And having sat
in it, he interpreted to the crowds. And after sufficient teaching, since it
was necessary also to add a divine work to the words, useful to those who saw,
the Lord commands Peter to let down the nets for a catch, and he said to him:
"Having toiled through the whole night we found nothing. But at your word I
will let down the nets," and he was doing the things commanded. And when "they
enclosed a great multitude of fishes" and the nets were being torn, weighted
by the multitude, they call to help those in the neighboring boat, then having
drawn up the fishes they fill both skiffs, so that they were in danger of
being sunk, at which Peter, having wondered, was struck and confessed himself
unworthy of the saving embarking. And he, as having shown him the image of
what was to come, also makes clear the interpretation, and Jesus says to him:
"Fear not; from now on you will be catching men alive"; and to all: come after
me and I will make you fishers of men. Galilean men, cheap, rustic, knowing
nothing more than the speech of the Syrians, but also pursuing the humble and
cheap occupation of fishing, reasonably our Savior was promising to make
hunters of men and heralds of his teaching, and he has done it, not having
lied in the promise, presenting an operative power of God covering every
virtue of men. For if taking certain learned and wise men or even of the men
among the Jews who were glorious and rich he had used these as teachers of his
words, it would even have been likely that the matter had been conceived in a
more human way. Thus at any rate most men are accustomed to be subjected
either to money, being baited by the rich, or being deceived by a wrapping of
words and a show of wisdom, or being struck by the preeminences of glorious
persons. But he, not having used such disciples, but on the contrary poor and
inglorious and unskilled in speech and Syrians in voice and humble and cheap
in figure, was clear in deed as using only the divine power, which he showed
first by calling them with one voice and making them his followers and then
promising to make them seiners and heralds of men, so that instead of the nets
they had they might take from him the seine woven from all kinds of words,
legal and prophetic and of his divine teaching, and cast it into the sea of
human life and enclose as many as they find, filling their own rational nets
with every kind of rational fishes. But these things, being then heard through
a voice, were words and noises and there was nothing more. But the work of the
divine power he presented not long after, having accomplished in the deeds
these very things that had been spoken beforehand through the words, so that
in a short time the whole inhabited world of men together acquired countless
multitudes of those netted by these poor and unskilled men, and every place of
Greeks and barbarians was filled with churches through that one promise of the
divine voice, through which he promised not that he would teach his own
disciples, but that he himself would make them fishers of men, so that he not
only foreknew nor only foretold the future, but has also been shown maker of
so great a knowledge, and he spoke with the word and has done it with the
deed,

and he presented the future through a symbol and image and through the
operation itself he fulfilled it. For to those who of old formerly in darkness
of the light of truth and of the knowledge of God had been worn out in night
and had been able to hunt no one toward salvation he, having shone his own
brightness, commands when they have come to be in day and light, not in their
own skill but trusting in his word, to cast the nets into the deep. And they
gathered such multitudes of fishes that the hunting instruments were being
torn, and the boats were in danger of going into the deep from the load. And
since these things being done thus were leading Peter into amazement and no
ordinary fear, but these things, the Savior says to him, let them not yet
strike you. For these are still playthings and images of things to come. For
these fishes are voiceless and irrational and these skiffs and nets have been
constructed from soulless matter, but the patterns of these are not such. A
little later then, or rather already from here and "from now on you will be
catching men alive," having been released from this wretched and useless
fishing, becoming a hunter of rational animals instead of the irrational, and
no longer from depths of sea, but from the salty bitterness of life and from
dark recesses of atheism and vice you will draw up to intellectual light and
pure breath those who will be hunted by you, or rather you will catch them
alive through procuring life for them and not death. For the fishes from the
open sea, living formerly in darkness and deep, at once when they have
partaken of light and breath perish, but those from among men who will be
hunted by you, changing from darkness of ignorance, will be caught toward
divine life. Therefore also "you will be catching men alive from now on" he
says. These things the Savior spoke beforehand by divine power, these things
the same one showed faithful and true by the deeds. That net-caster then, the
fisher, the Syrian, instead of the hunt of fishes, how many myriads of men he
netted with nets of unspeakable words woven by divine power, it is not
possible to take in by number. And the things that appear are a sight of
things unclear. For what the long age of life before the theophany of the
Savior did not bring forth, what neither Moses the lawgiver of the Hebrews nor
the prophets of God after Moses nor countless others of old who cried out the
divine teaching to men, hunters who toiled much through the whole night before
his appearing, were able to accomplish, these things the Galilean, the poor
man, the barbarian in speech, that very Peter, accomplished. And a sample of
the things then accomplished by Peter are the churches shining through to this
day, much more than the skiffs then abounding in rational fishes, such as is
the one at Caesarea of Palestine, such as the one at Antioch in Syria, such as
the one at the city of the Romans itself. For these churches and all those
around them Peter himself is remembered to have established, and those in
Egypt at Alexandria itself again he himself, not indeed through himself, but
through Mark who had been discipled to him, established. For he himself was
occupying himself about Italy and all the nations around it, and he had
appointed his own disciple Mark teacher and seiner of those in Egypt. And set
the mind also upon the remaining disciples of our Savior, whom having promised
to make fishers of men, he showed the word in deed. Still even now at any rate
the same one operates and makes, being present everywhere on earth and filling
the whole inhabited world of men with his rational nets, from every kind of
rational fishes, barbarians and Greeks, drawing up from the deep of vice and
the darkness of atheism the souls of men, and drawing them up on each day and
at each hour to the light of the divine knowledge handed over by him . . . . .
. 7 Thus again also of John the son of Zebedee, whom having seen at the
fishing together with the father and the brother mending the nets he deemed
worthy of the same

calling and promise, throughout the whole inhabited world the name shines and
the words illuminate the souls of men through the writing of the gospel handed
over by him, translated into every kind of tongue, Greek and barbarian, and
being proclaimed so as to be heard by all the nations on each day. 8 And since
in written prophecies it has been said prophetically to his person: "Ask of me
and I will give you nations as your inheritance and the ends of the earth as
your possession," therefore as the prophetic testimony has now been fulfilled
in deed, he says to his disciples: "All authority has been given to me as in
heaven and on earth." For of the things in heaven he held the kingship from of
