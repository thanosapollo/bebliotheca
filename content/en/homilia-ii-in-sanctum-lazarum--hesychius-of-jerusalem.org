* Homily II on Saint Lazarus
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of Hesychius, presbyter of Jerusalem, a discourse on Lazarus of the four
days.
1 I love the precinct of the church, this spiritual banquet that it gathers
for me; for the assembly of the church is a banquet of God, watered from a
mystical mixing-bowl, and fed with a life-giving lamb. For since the king of
the angels sojourned upon the earth, banquets and delights have been assembled
for the faithful, and the mourning of nature has been turned back upon the
cause of the mourning, from the times that began with the Savior's sojourn;
for nature, as soon as it perceived the Master's radiance, was from the outset
filled with joy and leapings. Barren women bore forerunners of grace; in the
wombs themselves the embryos played, leaping for joy: "For the babe leaped in
her womb with gladness." A virgin was blessed for a birth without labor;
heaven from above called the Magi by a star; the earth was full of angels
rejoicing with those on earth; the demons released those they held; diseases
set the bodies free; from one side a paralytic came running to meet him, from
another a blind man rose up seeing, here a leper washed away his leprosy,
there the pool of a hemorrhage was dried, this one from a stammerer was made a
speaker, another, toward the recovery of hearing in his ears, quickly reaped a
welcome for words that had formerly been shut out.
2 And why must one mention the wine-drinking at a wedding that no vine had
planted, the dead leaping out of tombs? "Young man," he says, "I say to you,
arise"; and he was a swift hearer of the voice, as if he had been fashioned a
corpse and had not truly died. The tomb also loosed Lazarus, yet when so
called he hastened to display a course while still in his grave-bands, he who
on the fourth day had rotted in the memorial, many of his inward parts
plundered by worms, all the harmony of the body torn apart, possessing the
stench of the tomb as witness of corruption. But the fragrance of life recast
the dissolution of the inward parts, and the memorial became a womb for the
one who lay there, and, as if sown in a belly and not in a tomb, Lazarus
sprang forth newly fashioned, the old man and the new.
3 O new wonder-working! A tomb conceived a man for us unto life, wrapped in
burial-clothes as a babe in swaddling-bands; for the Master's voice,
recognized by the dead man, drew him from the memorial together with the
grave-bands: "Lazarus," he says, "come forth." And at the word the dead man
quickly came forth together with it, the members running together toward one
another in the tomb; the eye returned to its proper rank, the nose took its
own place, the cheeks hastened toward their former shape, the neck was bound
together with shoulders and head, the pairing of the hands was made ready for
motion, the harmonies of the joints were gathered, the order of the fingers
was composed, the covenants of the sinews were re-bound, the nature of the
bones was clothed, arteries were stretched with the veins, marrow was formed
again, membranes were woven, hairs were set in order.
4 I was making the course of the members slow by my speech. As a horse from a
signal, so, goaded by the Master's voice, the dead man leaped up at once,
called and fashioned and appearing from the memorial. "Lazarus, come forth."
Before saying "arise," he commands the going-out. While he still lies, he
presses him toward a course. Raise him first, Master, and then command the one
who has risen to run. Why do you proclaim an exit to the one who lies, before
the resurrection? "Yes," he says, "I command the course to the one who lies,
that I may not occupy the dead man with two things and delay the going-out. I
say to him, 'Come forth,' that he may run more sharply, having received one
appointed time for rising and for the course." And at once from there Lazarus
sprang winged from the memorial, as if sitting by the tomb as a guard rather
than as a corpse.
5 And Hades below, giving back the dead man as it seems, cried out: "Who is
this who by a voice raises the dead from the tombs as if they were sleeping?
Who is this who undoes the ancient laws of those who have died? Who is this
who stirs my dead to rise up against me? Who is this who has set forth a
proclamation of return to the dead? Who is this who accustoms the buried to
run back toward life? Who is this who so readily releases my prisoners? Who is
this who tears away all my dead into a dance? The scepter of my tyranny over
men, as I see, is gone. Rotten is my prison-house of death. Elijah went away,
taking a dead man from me as a favor to a woman. After Elijah, Elisha came
upon me and was made a double robber of my dead. Now this one, a more bitter
helper on behalf of the dead, has marched against me, calling the rotting dead
as if they were living, and I am in danger henceforth of not even ruling over
those who have rotted; for I have been plundered of a dead man whom I had
consumed. A four-day body triumphs over me, dancing with the living in its
burial-clothes. I shall be trampled hereafter, a dead man having fled from me
in his grave-bands. For who henceforth will fear me, who have been conquered
by a rotting corpse and have become as it were a keeper of deposits of the
dead?"
6 For Lazarus was a kind of deposit of the memorial, given back like gold with
the seals with which he had been laid away; bound with grave-bands he was laid
in the memorial, and with the same bonds he was given back again to life. For
the people of the Jews, being unbelieving and doubtful toward the magnificence
of the wonders, would have said even now of Lazarus as once of the blind man
who was healed: "This is he, this is not he." Therefore, as one about to give
back a deposit, and foreseeing the suspicion of the one who deposited it,
keeps the seals of the deposit, that the sight of the seals may persuade the
depositor, so also the Master Christ, giving Lazarus back to the Jews, the
four-day deposit among the dead, brings out with him the marks of the
grave-bands as of certain seals, that they, loosing them, may see that it was
not simply another, but the very one they had buried.
7 But the maiming of unbelief remained uncorrected; for having recognized the
dead man they envied the wonder and devised blind counsels: "They took
counsel," it says, "that they might kill Lazarus also, because many of the
Jews were going away on his account and believing in Jesus." O wicked counsel!
Is what happened true, O priests, or false? If it is true, you counsel in vain
to slaughter the one who can rise again. If the wonder is a fiction, you are
busying yourselves over the height of the wonder. If what happened is a rumor,
let rather the one falsely rumored to have been raised live, that all who come
near may learn from a living man that he has not been raised from the dead.
But if you slaughter him, you help the rumor, having destroyed the refutation
of the rumor, so that the counsel is double against you either way.
8 O much-groaned disease! The healing of those who are envied is a wounding of
those who envy; the benefactor is envied as if he were the one who harms; the
one who saves is an enemy as if he were the one who kills. One ought to
rejoice, not to grieve, when the dead are raised. A dead man has risen; you
have gained a pretext for joy, having found a physician who lays a plaster on
the dead. You have ready at hand, O man, the loosing of the things that
grieve. Is a child of yours ill? Call against the disease the one who raised
the dead. Does a demon trouble your child? The medicine of the passion is
present for you. Is some household servant gasping out his life beside you?
You have near you the one who tears the dead from death. How many gifts have
you given, seeking to find a helper in grievous things? You found this one
without pay, and you shut your eyes toward the finding. Therefore, since you
are a lover of loss, leave the gain to those who wish it; grant Lazarus to
live; do not become more bitter than death to the men whom that one released,
driving them toward him; do not make living with you more hopeless than a
tomb. The dead man had hope of life in the tomb, and he came back to life
without even being deprived of the grave-bands; but living with you he is in
danger of losing the gift he had in the tomb.
9 Why do I occupy the discourse with the wickedness of the Jews, when the
raising of Lazarus sends me on toward the memory of the resurrection? When
tombs are shaken, being demanded of their dead, the earth is troubled, shaken
with fear, and the crashes of trumpets from heaven raise a war-cry to men:
"For the trumpet shall sound," it says, "and the dead shall be raised
incorruptible." Flashing from there out of heaven the Judge descends; the
armies of the angels of the Judge run before, troubled by fear of the things
that come to pass: "for the powers of the heavens," it says, "shall be
shaken"; a meeting of the righteous aloft in clouds, a lament of demons
dragged to judgment, the devil in mourning led forward as a prisoner, tyrants
dragged toward a fearful audit, the robberies of the rich punished, the
endurances of the poor crowned, companies of kings making confession, the
lives of priests searched, ranks of virgins honored, hidden fornications
paraded, the dignity of the chaste honored, the wantonness of adulterers
punished, the kindness of the long-suffering crowned, the hidden conviction of
slanderers, the peoples who have genuinely repented beatified, the irony of
hypocrites scourged.
10 The word is not mine; you have all heard Paul crying out to all: "We shall
all stand before the judgment seat" "of Christ, that each may receive the
things done through the body, according to what he has practiced, whether good
or bad." All of us then, being ordered by the memory of these things: wither
pleasure, you lover of pleasure; for it is fuel of the fire of Gehenna.
Chastise the belly, you who live in luxury; for gluttony is a combustible
load, and the prophet, blaming this, cried out to the Sodomites: "But," he
says, "this was the lawlessness of Sodom, that they lived in luxury in
prosperity and fullness of bread." Hate greed, you rich man, as stubble of the
unquenchable burning. Be sinewed toward endurance, you poor man; for pious
patience is a cooling of Gehenna. Unlearn impiety, you Hellene; for thorns are
useful for fire alone. Come back to life from heretical doctrines as from
tombs, you heretic, before that voice comes that cries to the dead: "Behold,
the bridegroom comes, go out to meet him," Christ our God, "who is about to
judge the living and the dead." Let us glorify him with the Father and the
Holy Spirit, now and ever and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
