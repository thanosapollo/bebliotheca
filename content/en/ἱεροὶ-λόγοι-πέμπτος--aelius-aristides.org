In the season of summer the stomach was in a bad way, and there was thirst
night and day, and unspeakable sweat, and a loosening of the body, and two and
three would scarcely drag me when I needed to rise from the bed; and the god
signifies a departure, I being in Smyrna at this time, and it was necessary to
go out at once. And I was going out on the road toward Pergamum; and while
carriages were being procured, midday was coming on and a brilliant heat was
rising. It seemed good to us therefore, having spent the very peak of it in
the suburb, thus to complete the journey. And the impulse was as toward
Myrina, and the baggage animals were going ahead for this. But as there was a
stifling heat and at the same time from pleasure in the place and besides from
certain occupations we let go much of the hour, so that we arrive at the
lodging before the Hermes about the very setting of the sun. And I was at a
loss what to do. And since having gone in I could not bear the wretchedness of
the rooms, and attendance was not present because I had sent it ahead, it
seemed necessary to press on, and when I had crossed the river night was
already clear and a light and cooling breeze struck, and the body somehow
recovered and some tension with good cheer entered the mind, so as to converse
with the air in quiet and at the same time, judging the present things against
the day that had passed, how great a change there had been. And already in
deep evening I came to Larissa, and it happens to me, to my gladness, that the
carriages had not been overtaken and the things of the lodging were no better
than those before, but necessity was present to hold to the road and endure
through. And it was already midnight or even still further on, and we arrive
at Cyme, and everything had been shut

these men, it said, are even now pursuing. And together with these things the

memorials of the bodies, and the books of the speeches.

Having said and heard such things I returned. And having seen my foster
brother coming I renewed the times, in how many before we were at Athens
together. And when I turned as toward the acropolis, that I might go in home,
a flash darted through on the right and struck down so much beside the very
tips of my hair that I wondered if they had not been kindled. We had contended
then; and I took the sign rather as auspicious, for the young man who was with
me said that it brought glory, especially also since it had happened on the
right. And most of the buildings seemed to me to have certain added ladders,
and through these it was necessary to go up and down; so that I was somewhat
displeased at this at least, nevertheless I somehow get inside. And in this
those sending the procession for Eros had come back, and the interpreter,
having learned the sign, he too said it was auspicious and that the sacrifices
had therefore happened rightly for me. And there seemed to have been added to
me a dream as to make sacred rites for Zeus and Artemis and if for any other
of the gods. And upon all these I seemed to call Eudoxus as about to write
them down, both because they were rather long and from the wish to save them
through accuracy. These things were shown to me also concerning the later
reputation and the need to remain in place, among many others.
