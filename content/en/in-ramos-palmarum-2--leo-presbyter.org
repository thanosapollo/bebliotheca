* On the Palm Branches
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

A homily of Leontius, presbyter of Constantinople, on those about to be
enlightened and on the palms and on the raising of Lazarus.

Already the gifts of the Master's festival peep in, already the signals of the
all-praised feast run ahead, already the veils of the immaterial bridegroom
shine forth beforehand, already the delights of the universal resurrection
make an early spring, already the earnest of the resurrection shines forth
beforehand, which Lazarus having received becomes a table-companion with the
Lord, already the myrrh of the gift makes fragrant those who run to it,
already the faithful guards of the king have taken this great city, already
the faithful people, having run forth to meet the Lord, cry out saying, as you
were hearing: Hosanna in the highest, blessed is he who comes in the name of
the Lord, King of Israel, the unutterable. Blessed is he who comes and is
found with us and in no way comes to be outside of all. Blessed is he who
comes in the name of the Lord, King of Israel.

Truly that crowd recognized a kingly entrance. For just as in present times,
when a worldly king has marched against a tyrant and is returning from the
victory, all the citizens of his city meet him bearing flowers, weaving
praises, casting encomia instead of roses, and what leads them to this is not
a love of empty glory but a desire for the triumph, in the same way also in
the case of the Master Christ, as in the case of a trophy-bearing king, the
whole multitude both from the words and from the palms recognized that the one
who had arrived was a victor, or rather that <the one> being recognized was
God. For to God alone are those voices fitting which were then cast forth, and
now have been read out, and which blossom in the divine mysteries.

Hosanna in the highest. What is Hosanna in the highest? That is: Save now, you
who are in the highest. For Hosanna is interpreted Save now in the Hebrew
tongue. Hosanna in the highest. That is, as salvation above, so also love of
mankind below. Hosanna in the highest, blessed is he who comes in the name of
the Lord, King of Israel. They were pursuing an angelic ministry, treading
earth and pacing heaven, wearing a body and pursuing the things of the
bodiless, Jews in name and Christians in work, worldly in manner of life and
apostolic in beauty, having received the interpretation from above and not
from below.

For just as Peter, the chiefest of the apostles, not from his own study but
from the divine revelation from above from the Father said to the Lord: You
are the Christ, the Son of the living God, therefore also the Lord, showing to
him and blessing him and making known that he received the revelation not from
his own study but from the Father, said: Blessed are you, Simon Bar-Jonah, for
flesh and blood has not revealed it to you, but my Father who is in the
heavens: in the same way also the crowd, having received the revelation from
above, was casting fitting voices to the Lord, saying: Hosanna in the highest,
blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord, King of Israel.

And it is likely that someone will say: And from where is the proof for us
that the Jewish crowd received the testimony from above? From where? From the
voices themselves and from the fitting acclamation. For if they were casting
some plain voices, they were making the acclamation from themselves; but if
they were addressing him as Savior and King and Lord and Hosanna, it is surely
clear to everyone that they received the revelation from above.

For from where did the crowd recognize the Lord as king? He was not wearing a
worldly diadem, he was not clothed in purple, he was not bringing a host of
soldiers, horses and gold-studded chariots were not running before him, he
was not riding a chariot

spread with purple, a royal one. He was sitting on a cheap colt, a borrowed
one, a small one, bringing only twelve disciples. From where then did the
crowd know that he was a king, unless they received the revelation from above?

Therefore they did not seek a worldly diadem upon the Lord: for they knew him
as reigning without beginning. They did not seek purple: they were persuaded
by the one who said: The Lord has reigned, he has clothed himself with
comeliness. They did not seek a host of soldiers: for they were persuaded by
the one who said: Ten thousand times ten thousand angels ministered to him,
and a thousand thousands of archangels stood beside him. They did not seek
horses and chariots upon the Lord: for they recognized that he himself is the
one who took up Elijah in a fiery chariot, and knowing the evangelical horses
following him, being persuaded by the prophet who said: Behind him horses red
and white and dappled and ashen. Red horse: Matthew, as having set forth
splendidly the things of the Lord's becoming man. Ashen horse: Mark, as having
written the Gospel more floridly. Dappled horse: Luke, as having set forth
more eloquently and more variously the things concerning the Lord. White
horse: John, as having more brightly pilloried every heresy.

And Habakkuk also named the apostles horses that carry the Gospel swiftly over
all the earth, saying: And you made your horses go upon the sea, calling the
sea tropically the whole world. Therefore they did not seek upon the Lord a
chariot studded with stones, spread with purple, adorned with royal arms: for
they knew that he possessed as a chariot the cherubim and the seraphim, the
six-winged lamps, being persuaded by the one who said: You who sit upon the
cherubim, appear.

Therefore that Jerusalem crowd, having received the revelation from God, was
not scandalized by the cheap colt, but was strengthened by the Master's
presence. Therefore as soon as that faithful crowd saw the Lord mounted on the
colt, immediately pricked by prophetic predictions, taking up the palm-fronds
of the palms the crowds said to one another: Why do we delay and not go out to
meet the palm named in the Song of Songs? Let us therefore pluck the
palm-fronds of the palms: for his fruit is full of immortality, his leaves are
healings of the woman with the issue of blood, his stem is the cross-like
root.

We have heard the Church from the nations acclaiming him both as an
intelligible palm and as a mighty king. Therefore through the palms let us too
greet her and through her the Master Christ.

While the crowds were doing these things and acclaiming the Lord, the chief
priests and the Pharisees, stung very sharply, were rebuking the crowds,
saying: What are you doing, what empty talk are you talking, why are you
acting rashly? Do you come as to God to the carpenter's son? Do you offer
palm-fronds of palms to him who has nowhere to lay his head? Do you cast
kingly voices at him who sits on a colt of an ass? Are not his father and his
mother and all his brothers among us? Cease, lest the rash among you, being
condemned, be punished. For this is why we have been sent, that we may mark
the rash among you, that they may seasonably pay the penalties of their
daring.

Hearing these things that the chief priests were saying, the crowds are not
moved toward sedition, since peace is present, but they answer them, grace
working with them: Do you blame us, O Pharisees, and threaten punishment as if
we were sending forth blasphemies? Who are worthy of punishment, you who read
the Scriptures and do not understand, or we who hear and hold fast? Are we in
error, offering palm-fronds of the palms to the Lord? Is he not himself the
palm of righteousness? Has not the prophet cried concerning him: The righteous
shall flourish as a palm? Did he not himself blossom forth the recovery of
sight of the blind? Did he not grant to the lame a return of running?


Did he not give to paralytics the carrying of the bed? Did he not raise
Lazarus of the fourth day again from above? For this reason you wish to kill
Lazarus again.

Did you not roll away the stone, and by unbelief are you hardened as stones?
Did he not bid you to unbind the grave-bands, lest as in the case of the man
born blind you should lie that someone else had leapt from another tomb? You
always envy the good things, O Pharisees, and you oppose the multitude that
believes. For this reason you again planned to murder Lazarus, that you might
reap the crowd's faith. Grant that you kill Lazarus: can he not raise him
again? Let us see who will grow weary, you killing or he raising. You kill by
toil; he raises by a word. Who is more wearied?

Are we in error, O Pharisees, because we came to the Lord as to a king? Seeing
the colt we were rather strengthened; therefore we also recognize him as a
mighty king and we have the Scripture at our feet, that you may learn with
understanding that it is not we who are in error, but you who are deceived.
You have the prophet Zechariah: unfold him, fear his sickle of vengeance. Did
not this same prophet, evangelizing Judea from many years ago, cry saying:
Rejoice greatly, daughter of Zion; behold, your king comes to you, meek and
quiet, sitting upon a colt of an ass, the son of a beast of burden? Are we in
error, reading the Scripture and recognizing the king and doing good to
ourselves and turning those who doubt? You hold the prophet and you do not
wish to understand the one prophesied? Depart then, O Pharisees: for the more
you press upon us, so much the more you awaken our zeal. Therefore we do not
cease crying: Hosanna in the highest, blessed is he who comes in the name of
the Lord.

We know the name, O Pharisees. This name freed us from Egypt and made the sea
passable and peopled the desert and settled us in a land flowing with milk and
honey and provoked the prophet to cry: Let them know that your name is Lord,
you alone are most high over all the earth. And that the crowd was obedient,
but the chief priests disobedient, you just now heard the evangelist saying:
And the chief priests took counsel that they might kill Lazarus also, because
many of the Jews on account of him were going away and believing in Jesus.

Did you see the madness of the chief priests? In order to cut off the
multitude's faith, they sought to kill Lazarus also, saying to themselves: If
we kill Jesus only, but leave Lazarus alive, we gain nothing; seeing this one
they will believe in that one. Therefore, lest even after death he become a
stumbling-block to us, so that some believe in Jesus, let us kill Lazarus,
that we may wipe out his memory altogether.

But the chief priests were saying these things as vain-minded men, not
understanding that the Lord, about to accept the voluntary death for the
immortality of the world, was not going to leave Lazarus behind in a tomb.
Whence, having set up the unconquerable cross, they can no longer sack it nor
hide it: for it works through all creation. For what house is there that does
not have the cross as foundation? What ship is not steered by the cross? What
city does not have the cross as a fortress? What king is not bound about with
the cross? What virgin does not have the cross as a staff? What church is not
walled by the cross? What widow does not have the cross as a staff? What
orphan does not have the cross as a helper? The cross gives light in the
night, bears the lamp by day, stops winters of misfortunes, calls spring,
draws summer, binds marriage tight, rears orphans, increases friendship, works
through all good things.

What then does the evangelist say? For it is good also to feel out
the head of the


whole pericope. You just now heard the evangelist saying: Six days before the
Pascha Jesus came to Bethany, where Lazarus was, whom he raised from the dead.
Hear with understanding. Six days before the Pascha Jesus came to Bethany. Six
days before the Pascha. And what day is this Six days before? What day is
this? The present day, which characterizes beforehand the forerunner John. For
just as John ran before the Lord, so also this day ran before the
resurrection; and just as John, pointing with the finger to the Lord, said:
Behold the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world, so also this day,
pointing with the finger to the coming Lord's day, has cried saying: Behold
the mother of those who are about to be born again.

Six days before the Pascha. Hear the day and look around at this: you who
belong to the day, the day; you who belong to the light, the light; you who
belong to the sun, the sun; you who belong to the bride, the bridegroom. Six
days before the Pascha Jesus came to Bethany. Hear the day: Six days before
the Pascha is not set down simply; it indicates the quantity, that you may
cleanse yourself from every defilement of flesh and spirit: for this day is a
pre-purification. Six days before the Pascha, that you may cleanse yourself
beforehand from every stain, that you may dissolve enmity, that you may cease
from wrath, that you may quench slander, that you may bind love tight, that
you may widen love of the poor, that not you alone may keep the feast but the
one in need may keep it with you. For if you do not share with the one who has
need, you keep the feast, but he is gloomy. Lest that recompense of the rich
man come upon you also, who, making merry splendidly every day, did not share
even crumbs with Lazarus, share therefore from your table with the one who has
need and do good to those who lack, that you too may keep feast through all
and the poor man may feast together with you, lest the dogs become more humane
than you.

Hear with understanding. For if you do not do good to the poor man of your own
faith, who has need of consolation, and he, not having been benefited by you,
from necessity goes to a heretic, and that one consoles him, the dog has
become more humane than you: for every heretic barks against the shepherd.
What then does the evangelist say? Six days before the Pascha Jesus came to
Bethany, where Lazarus was, whom he raised from the dead. O the beneficence of
the Lord. He raises Lazarus, that he may provoke the devil. What is He raises
Lazarus, that he may provoke the devil? Hear in brief: for it is good to bring
the discourse to a close because of the work that is at hand.

When the devil saw the Lord crying, Lazarus, come forth, and all the things
under the earth dissolved, and the powers below not bearing it, as a kingly
command having appeared, or rather as a divine will working, and that at once
Lazarus's hairs were taking root from above, and the dissolutions of the
sinews were being tightened together, and the separation of the bones was
being pegged together, and the streams of the ichors were being drawn in, and
the vital spirit was entering in together, and the mistress soul was receiving
again her former dwelling: when the devil saw these things and things like
them taking place, being thrown into tumult and becoming anxious, he was
commanding the powers under him, saying: Hold out, take hold, do not prefer
him to my kingdom. What have I suffered, you unmanly ones? The powers of death
are being dissolved: for him whom I have been holding four days I give back by
force. I have fallen into a great disaster. I have begun to vomit those whom I
swallowed. I do not weep for Lazarus only; I fear that I may lose Adam also. I
have need of haste and swiftness: if I do not forestall the one who is
finishing me, he will show me empty of all my substance. I know what I will
do: I have the Jews as fellow-runners, I hire Judas, a robber always works
with me. He priced the myrrh well at


three hundred denarii: already I have persuaded Judas to price the myrrh at
three hundred denarii, but to sell the Master of the myrrh for thirty pieces
of silver. I have need of haste: I will prepare a cross. He took Lazarus; I
will drag him down whole, I will number him with the dead, that he may learn
not to campaign against a greater one.

But the powers below to him: Do you wish to drag Jesus down here whole, and do
you wish to number him with the dead? See that you are not plotting against
yourself: you have experience of him, you wrestled with him in the desert; did
you not come a third time and fall threefold? If being absent he can do so
much, if he comes down, what are you going to do?

For these things and things like them the Lord raises Lazarus, that he may
provoke the devil to hew a cross, that the curse may become a blessing and the
cross a helper for all. For the resurrection of Lazarus stung the devil,
filled the Pharisees with smoke, fully assured Martha, glorified Mary, gave an
earnest of the resurrection beforehand, and showed the disciples stronger.

And it is likely that someone will say: And how did the resurrection of
Lazarus show the disciples stronger? How? Hear in brief. You all know clearly
that while the Lord was beyond the Jordan, Lazarus died in Bethany. Wishing
therefore first of all to fully assure his own disciples that he was not in a
part but everywhere as God, and that nothing of the things that happen has
escaped him, he says to his own disciples: Lazarus our friend has fallen
asleep, but I go that I may wake him. But the apostles, having heard in a more
human way, attending to the words and not to the power, answer the Lord more
naturally, saying: Lord, if he has fallen asleep, he will be saved.
Nevertheless tell us, Master: you are here beyond the Jordan, Lazarus in
Bethany, how did you know that he has fallen asleep? But Christ to them: I
said to you that he has fallen asleep, and I rejoice for your sakes, that you
may know that I was not there in the body; consider the uncircumscribed power
of my divinity, that I am both here and there and everywhere. But that you may
become spectators not only of the words but also of the deeds, let us go to
him.

Jesus therefore, having come to the tomb, cried: Lazarus, come forth, and
immediately he came out, having recognized the voice of the Creator. He came
out having his hands bound and his feet with grave-bands, and his face was
bound about with a napkin. O the excess of the wonders. What was more to be
wondered at? The one raised from the dead, or the bound one running? But lest
the unbelieving Jews think the thing a fantasy, he commanded them to unbind
him with their own hands, saying: Unbind him and let him go. Receive him, he
says, as a witness: he himself will make known to you who I am, the one who is
Master of things heavenly and earthly and under the earth. To Christ himself
be the glory together with the Father and the Holy Spirit unto the ages of
ages. Amen.
