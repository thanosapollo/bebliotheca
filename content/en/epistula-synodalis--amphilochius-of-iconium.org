Synodal letter of Amphilochius, bishop of Iconium.
Even before the letters from your love were brought, the report had
reached us both of the unmixed purity of your faith and of its being
arrayed against the dangers, and of your endurance toward the
sufferings for Christ, and of the love toward God, and of the
firmness of the coming hope in you. But since we have also come upon
the letters of your piety, we have seen in truth the character of an
apostolic church and the zeal of good shepherds and an unwearying
earnestness befitting true Christians. For that you neither took into
account the length of the road nor brought the inquiries separately,
but made the question in common through the letters, has made us very
hopeful that, having written in agreement, you will also receive the
answers from us in agreement; and we have received the concord in
your letters as a kind of prelude of the more perfect concord. And it
was our prayer both to receive as present at the synod the most
admirable bishop Basil, who is named with all reverence, and to have
him as partner, or rather as leader, of the matters toward you. But
since an exceeding sickness of the body prevented him from arriving,
it belongs to your perfect love not to overlook even the letters of
our smallness. We have not, however, allowed your holy church to be
without a share even of his voice; but having a writing of his
composed by him specifically on this subject, we have him too
speaking together with us through the writing. What then is the
answer to the inquiry? We recognize the holy synod of the fathers at
Nicaea as a synod truly catholic and apostolic, and we keep unmoved
the faith set forth by the fathers at that time, and I pray that it
remain unshaken forever. And it became more necessary at that time
for the fathers to explain more fully concerning the glory of the
Only-begotten, since they then were eager to cut out the heresy of
Arius, newly sprouting, destroying its first seeds beforehand, before
the thorn was fully hardened. But since the question concerning the
Spirit was at that time passed over in silence, for this reason they
added nothing more fully; yet for those who read with understanding,
even the teaching concerning the Spirit in that faith is sufficient.
They dogmatized that just as one believes in the Father and the Son,
so also one believes in the Spirit, neither introducing some other
nature into the divine and blessed Trinity, nor cutting off anything
of those from the Trinity in the exposition of the faith. But since
recently Satan, attempting to shake the churches, has cast into some
a hesitation concerning the Spirit, it is necessary to run back to
the fountain of the faith, from which the fathers at Nicaea also,
having drawn, made the exposition of the faith. What then is the
perfection of our faith? The tradition of the Lord, which after the
resurrection from the dead he commanded to his holy disciples,
charging them: "Go, make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them
in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit." It
is clear that we received the commandment not only for baptizing
thus, but also for making disciples thus, so that through this
commandment both the disease of Sabellius is shut out, the three
hypostases having been clearly handed down to us, and the mouths of
the Anomoeans and of the Arians and of the Pneumatomachi are stopped,
the persons and the hypostases being shown as three, but the nature
and the Godhead being confessed as one. It is necessary therefore
that we baptize as we were taught, and believe as we were baptized,
and glorify as we have believed. For looking around much with the
mind and turning everywhere with our reasonings, we are able to
invent nothing between the Creator and the creation, so that if we
separate the Spirit from the Godhead, it is necessary to number it
with the creatures. And if we should dare to say it is a creature,
how is it possible for it to be mixed in baptism? We censure
polytheism thus as we do also atheism; and we proclaim neither three
principles nor three gods nor three different natures, but knowing
the Father as principle of all things we neither reject any of the
three hypostases, bringing forward also the dignity of the divine
scriptures and the testimonies upon each. But since to discuss these
things in detail also exceeds the measure of a letter, and we have
trusted that your love will be content with the main point of our
exposition and will add the rest from yourselves (for "Give," it
says, "occasion to a wise man, and he will be wiser"), we think that
we have written these things sufficiently. To which it is enough to
add a brief exhortation. For we exhort you to remain sons of peace,
that you may fulfill the apostolic commandment, being of one soul,
thinking the one thing, and having shared in the sufferings for
Christ. Do not give the evil one leave to sunder what has been well
joined, nor make yourselves more easily caught by the wolves,
confirming their prayers by the division. For we have learned how
many contests you have undergone for orthodoxy. Having then overcome
the threats and the constraints from the adversaries, give no
opportunity to dissension, lest hatred, slipping in little by little,
if it should have a place, set great precipices before you. But also
in the doxologies it is necessary to glorify the Spirit together with
Father and Son, and to know this, that those who commit the
unpardonable sin through the blasphemy against the Spirit in vain
decline communion with the Arians. For they will be condemned with
them. And we pray that you, having kept the body of the church uncut
without contention, may spend the remaining time peacefully in the
present age, and stand gloriously at the judgment seat of Christ on
the day of judgment.
