192 The man of God who in truth travels the way of righteousness, and the
obedient servant of the Lord, always receives progress in each good thing and
in every state of virtue, and becomes better than himself, being improved day
by day, and acquires a foundation in all the practices of virtue, as in
humility and meekness, in kindness, in endurance, in longsuffering, in love,
in faith, in hope, in temperance, in courage, in goodness. For each of them
has much depth and breadth and a work of fineness. Just as if there are in a
garden many fruit-bearing and various trees, and each tree has branches, and
again the branches have small twigs and finer twigs and a great multitude of
leaves, so in each of the virtues there lies a great fineness of working and
much depth, and each is as it were a perfect completion. And the foundation
and support and the highest point of all these, in which each one who
approaches God ought to be established and founded, is confidence toward God
and the firmness of faith, which before all things each one who wishes to
please God ought to acquire and to be established in the confidence of God.
For those who do not first acquire confidence toward God are like those who
build a house without a foundation. For those around the Maccabees also,
having greatly contended and shown nobility of soul magnanimously in many and
great wars, by faith and confidence toward God subdued their own enemies in
the bodily war; let us take them as an example for ourselves in the invisible
war "against the spiritual hosts of wickedness," that thus eager and 117
courageous and noble and great-souled we ought to be, the true warriors of
Christ; for the apostle says: "Now these things happened to them as types,
and they were written for our admonition, upon whom the ends of the ages have
come." In the various afflictions, then, and the manifold temptations raised
against us by the spirits of wickedness, we ought to be patient and to take
courage and to be longsuffering, the noble athletes and warriors of Christ, so
that we may also be able to be thankful in the consolations and gifts of
grace. For just as the visible world of men is governed by God with grace and
with justice, with grace in abundance and fruitfulness of crops and health and
temperateness of seasons and every giving of good things, and with justice and
discipline in famines and plagues and afflictions and necessities, overthrows,
hailstorms, collapses, and whatever has been appointed by God for discipline,
for justice and for the benefit of men: so also the soul that wishes to
inherit the upper world of the Godhead is governed in many ways by grace, and
at one time it rejoices in ease and gladness of the Spirit and is gladdened by
the consolation of grace, and at another it is handed over to discipline and
to various temptations and to all kinds of afflictions of the worldly spirit,
so that, having been trained through all things and found proven, and enduring
nobly in all temptations, and in nothing turned aside from well-pleasing God,
and having thus approved itself in Christ, it may be established as worthy of
God, inheriting eternal life by grace and by justice.
B Nr. 19b f. 131v21-132r21 B Nr. 19b f. 136r34-136v29. cf. Dörries s. 193
And just as in this life it is not altogether ease and fruitfulness and
fruit-bearing and peace among men, but by the yoke and measure of justice God
disciplines and has mercy and provides for the world, so also a soul that
longs to be born to the upper world is not altogether in ease and
gladness and joy of the Spirit, but by the yoke of justice and of grace God
disciplines and has mercy and 119 orders her. For if there were altogether
ease in life among men, and luxury and satiety and wealth without afflictions
and pains, men would not have borne and carried it, despising God and giving
themselves wholly over to the pleasures of the flesh. For "Jacob ate," he
says, "and was filled, grew fat, grew thick, was broadened, and the beloved
kicked." Take this also into the spiritual. If the soul lived altogether and
without ceasing only in the joy and ease and gladness of the Spirit, it would
no longer know itself, and it would be ignorant of the training and the way of
"righteousness," and it would be lifted up, and it would progress only in
conceit, exalting itself over all, and it would not even reckon itself to be a
human being. As the apostle says: in "the abundance of the revelations,"
"there was given me" "an angel of Satan, that he might buffet me, that I might
not be exalted." You see that for such a holy man the afflictions were for
advantage. Further, as the same says, "affliction works endurance, and
endurance provenness, and provenness hope; and hope does not put to shame." So
the afflictions are for advantage and for making the soul more proven.
B Nr. 20a f. 133r11-133v16 B Nr. 20a f. 137r29-138r3. cf. Dörries s. 193f.
The souls of Christians, coming into many afflictions in this world under the
evil one, are like the form of hemp. For just as the form of hemp, the more it
is beaten and tortured, is finished so much the finer and more useful and
purer, so that from it the finest threads may come to be, and the more it is
further tortured and carded the more useful it is made, so also the God-loving
and truth-loving souls of Christians, the more they enter into many
afflictions and trials and temptations from the spirits of wickedness,
themselves enduring and being longsuffering, 121 are made purer and more
useful and more proven for the spiritual work of the fineness of grace, and at
last are deemed worthy to dwell in the heavenly place of the saints. For the
soul, being proven in the many temptations and the various afflictions and
being helped by grace, enduring with longsuffering in hope of the Lord, little
by little lessens from itself every form of evil and lays aside the heavy
burden of the indwelling sin in some such way as this: just as if someone
travels a long road going off to some city, and carries a purse full of sand,
and this has a very fine hole below, and the more he walks, throughout the
whole road it flows out and he himself is lightened, and at last, when he has
arrived at the city, the heaviest sand has been emptied and he himself has
been made light and has rested perfectly from the weight of the sand: so also,
when the soul that truly contends is traveling the way of "righteousness" and
hastening toward the city of the kingdom, and carrying with herself the heavy
burden of the sin of the passions, the more she travels the way of the virtues
and clings to the Lord, so much are the sin and the passions lessened, and she
herself perceives in herself a sense of lightness and of gentleness. And if
she clings perfectly and wholly and travels the way of
"righteousness" blamelessly and well, she is deemed worthy to enter into the
very rest of the fullness of grace, having been wholly redeemed from the
heaviest burden of the sin of the passions. But first she must pass through
many temptations and afflictions, and thus she enters into the rest of the
fullness of grace, that is, she is deemed worthy to obtain passionlessness.
B Nr. 20b f. 133v17-134r9 B Nr. 20b f. 138r4-26. cf. Dörries s. 194
For just as the newly formed vessel, if it has not been put into the fire, is
unserviceable to men, or just as an infant child is unfit for the works of the
world, for it can neither build a city nor plant, nor set down seed, nor
accomplish any other 123 work of the world; for it is an infant. So also souls
untried and not proven in various afflictions by the spirits of wickedness are
still infants and untrained and (so to speak) are not yet useful to the
kingdom, as the blessed Paul says: "But if you are without discipline, of
which all have become partakers, then you are bastards and not sons." See how
much the afflictions (and the trials and the temptations) are for the
advantage of man, making the soul useful and proven. Only let one prepare
himself eagerly and nobly and courageously, bearing what comes upon him in
endurance, and let him hold to hope and to confidence, having always the
expectation of redemption and trusting in the compassion of Christ, that he
does not finally allow a soul that seeks him to be tempted beyond its power,
"but will make with the temptation also the way out, so as to be able to bear
it."
B Nr. 20c f. 134r9-135v4 B Nr. 20c f. 138r26-139v8
And further he judges this also in himself, that even if he will kill me and I
suffer a thousand other worse things, having been permitted by my Lord to
suffer and to be afflicted by the spirits of wickedness, I do not leave him.
For he is faithful, who will deliver me from every affliction "and from the
depths of the earth again" will bring me up, and thus, having given himself
wholly, entirely, to the Lord and having endured to the end, he will afterward
receive experience of the benefaction and of the help and of the recompense of
good things, as the Lord says: "But he who has endured to the end, this one
will be saved." For if in this life men cannot come to offices and
advancements, nor receive any greater office from a king, unless they first
come into much training and show many deeds of valor, having entered into war,
having learned every art of war and having been trained and having shown the
victory by experience and having raised trophies against the adversaries and
having approved themselves in all things to the king (for then they are deemed
worthy of offices and of great royal honors): how much more, with the heavenly
and true King, 125 no one is deemed worthy to receive at once the offices of
grace and of the gift of the Spirit and the heavenly gifts from God, unless
first the mind is trained throughout in the meditation of good thoughts and in
attention to the holy commandments and in confidence toward God, and thus is
deemed worthy of grace and receives a heavenly weapon (the Spirit itself) and
in it greatly approves itself against the spirits "of wickedness"; contending,
and agreeing and being united with the grace given him by God and having
become through all things an ally and fellow-warrior with the heavenly weapon
against evil, and having routed through many toils and contests and trials the
war "against the spiritual hosts of wickedness." And when he has taken the
victory against the enemies, then he is deemed worthy to obtain divine and
heavenly offices and glorious and spiritual honors with the heavenly King,
being able thereafter also to train others and to teach war. For if a common
man is taken simply from a flock, not knowing war nor having experience of
warlike matters, and is at once made a general, he is worthy of the laughter
of all, wishing to command in war while he himself does not first know
war. So also in the spiritual, the one who expounds the word of God and
presides over souls ought to be experienced in all spiritual matters, so that,
having shown by experience the working of the words he expounds, he may
through himself bring benefit and help to those who hear, each time. For the
wisdom of God and the way of Christianity and the work of grace, being
perfected in the faithful soul, is accomplished in a great fineness and
increase and progress, and when someone thinks he has finished, then he must
begin a more inward matter, the mysteries of the Spirit and spiritual
progressions coming to be in the soul, and often having thought he has
finished he has often also to begin. For if in this life there is in worldly
wisdom (I mean of elementary teachers, then of grammarians, then of rhetors,
again of sophists, finally of philosophers) and whenever someone has gone
through all the teachers, finishing one and beginning another, then he begins
to plead cases before rulers, then having succeeded over time he himself also
becomes at times a ruler, and then again he takes an assessor for himself,
lest he pass beyond what is just. If then in this world there is such fineness
of wisdom and such progress, how much more does the unspeakable and 127
boundless wisdom of God and the way of Christianity have a great fineness and
many progressions. And the more one progresses, he takes a beginning of other
matters, and enters more inwardly into the mysteries of the Spirit and the
hidden treasures of wisdom, and the more he increases and progresses in
grace and grows rich in the knowledge of God, so much also does he come to
know the "thoughts" and the craftinesses of evil, as the apostle says: "for we
are not ignorant of his thoughts." Such a hope, then, being set before us, and
so many spiritual good things having been prepared for those who wish to
contend in truth, let us also give ourselves with all our power to
well-pleasing God, so that, having obtained the spiritual gifts and having
progressed in the wisdom of God and having passed beyond every temptation and
affliction through the grace of Christ and having been perfectly deemed worthy
of sanctification, we may become worthy of eternal life in Christ Jesus our
Lord, to whom be the glory unto the ages of ages. Amen.
