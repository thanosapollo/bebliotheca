CHAP. III. And let these things lie as having been demonstrated. But the first
most impious and most abrupt problem of heretics is that written in the
Proverbs of Solomon: "The Lord created me beginning of his ways for his works;
before the age he founded me; in the beginning before he made the earth, and
before he made the abysses, before the springs of the waters came, before the
mountains were settled, and before all hills he begets me." For through this
problem they force to declare the Creator and benefactor a creature. But
better and more persuasive than their will is also the understanding of this
Scripture. For if all the chapters set forth and those arranged in the first
discourse are true and prevailing, as indeed they are, having received most
clear testimony from the divine oracles: how is it not of the most unholy and
most impossible things so to take the divine matters shown through
concealment? Is it not then quite clear, =39.808= that it is not about the
all-causing and creative wisdom, I mean of the great over all

God Word, whom no argument would be able to show, that Solomon proverbializes
the, "The Lord created me"? For he did not become so bold-hearted and arrogant
against God, who even after such sins granted pardon and had compassion, as
always toward all and toward him. But also if there is, as indeed there is,
for him a perception of the things coming about here, or said by those of
other mind: I think that even before the proper and un-evening day he
contradicts them, and accuses, and stands against, teaching that he never took
such a suspicion about him. For he says that he himself knows, as having
sought wisdom beyond a kingdom, and having obtained it, and having received
the kingdom in the part of an addition; and indeed also having heard from his
father David, that as life-giving and life the Lord did not begin to be
according to the upper generation, and as life-giving and eternal light having
shone forth subsistently and consubstantially from the paternal light, he has
not been illuminated by another; but that he has brought the discourse about
the worldly wisdom, that in words, that in generalship, that in medicine, that
in architecture, and simply that existing in every wondrous science in all
wise men. For truly, if anyone should wish to consider soberly and piously the
things preceding and the things following the, "The Lord created me": quickly,
and immediately, and from the very place he will find much refutation. For he
set first: "Beginning of wisdom, fear of the Lord; and good understanding to
all who do it;" and again: "Through me kings reign, and potentates write
righteousness; through me magnates are magnified, and tyrants through me hold
the earth." =39.809= And without wisdom, he says, which is the prudence
imparted to us from above, and presence of mind, and virtue of words, none of
the things that ought is accomplished; for the many and beautiful of thoughts
escape the one deprived of this. But the man who has partaken of this both
fears God as he ought, and does things dear to him, and legislates for man,
and this one rules that one, and has acquired many times more, and not only
himself speaking and doing is pleased, but also makes temperate those who hear
him, or who learn his work. And in the things next he set out these things
scatteredly: "The Lord made uninhabited lands, and inhabited extremities of
that under heaven. When he prepared the heaven, I was present with him; when
he marked off his own throne upon winds, when he made the upper clouds strong,
and as he set sure springs of that under heaven, in his setting for the sea
its precision." And he who made the earth from not-earth, and well crafted the
circling circles of heavens from not-heavens, and the sun accomplishing swift
courses shining everywhere of heaven and of the earth, and simply having shown
all things by a word, is only-begotten Son Word of God, who was celebrated as
power of God, and wisdom of God, and as being these things of his unoriginate
and true Father, not having a beginning, as they teach, John saying: "All
things came to be through him;" and Paul writing to Hebrews: "But toward the
Son he says: Your throne, O God, is unto the age of the age." And in between
he says: "And you at the beginnings, Lord, founded the earth, and works of
your hands are the heavens." So that it is not he, but another person who has
said all these things, and the, "The Lord created me." Then he added: "And
waters will not pass his mouth." Therefore then as wisdom was created, it is
interpreted about the intelligible mouth of the creative God Word. And again:
"When he made the foundations of the earth strong, I was with him fitting; I
was she in whom he rejoiced day by day; and I was gladdened in his face at
every time; when he was gladdened having completed the inhabited world, and he
was gladdened in sons of men." For the creative Son Word, who both foreknew
and was able beforehand in all things, being long-suffering =39.812= both upon
an unjust and ill-doctrined multitude and waiting for repentance, having
completed all things, rejoiced in the wisdom existing in his creatures, and
was gladdened in it, taking delight in his

own work; and it also was gladdened in his face. Then after other things he

chief point of the Christian faith, or rather who choose the whole, so far as

nestlings? And again: "One lacking in wits has no need of wisdom;" and again:

next he says: "With wisdom a house is built, and with understanding a city is

will I shared unchangeably, sinlessly, with men of the nature, as was said a
little before. And before all hills he generated impassibly, and
unoriginately, and unspeakably, because the godhead is beyond the incorporeal.
For the "before" especially upon it happens to be indefinite, and still also
the conjunction "and" presents this. For if he also created, how did he
generate? And if he generated, how did he create? For the creature one might
say is offspring of the one who created, as we have heard, sons of the
Highest; but the truly offspring would never be called creature of the one who
generated. For not yet until today do we know this to have been said. So that
the wisdom in the

best pursuits is found from every side to have said the, "The Lord created;"
so that also according to a forced interpretation him who became conformed to
humanity. For by the definition of the divine foreknowledge this one came to
be before every creation, and creation it names catachrestically the humanity,
as also the wisdom in the world, through the things Paul says: "Be subject to
every human creation through the Lord, whether to a king, as being superior,
or to governors, as sent through him." =39.824= And indeed the saying:
So-and-so perhaps created this, or made a bishop, or a clergy, or a camp, does
not signify an essence brought from things not being, although having a
homonymous word; but it calls those already being, or the existing matter,
into some rank, or such a placing. And the Hymnist prayed the portion existing
in him, as still about to be furnished to him, crying: "Create a clean heart
in me, O God." And Paul also the most holy writes things in accord with this
and obvious: "If anyone is in Christ a new creation; behold the ancient things
passed, all things have become new;" not by having been created just now, but
by shining forth unto what is next. And elsewhere: "That he may create the two
unto one new man;" instead of, that he may gather. And see, if also that said
to those being renewed in baptism can take part with us: "Put on the new man,
the one created according to God;" and as many such oracles one might find
examining. And it has been written metaphorically also about those who begot:
"He made sons and daughters;" and, "I acquired," he says, "a man through God."
And nevertheless he speaks generatively, not creatively. And if according to
concession the, "The Lord created me," must be taken unto the God from God,
let it be remembered again, and again let it be said and believed toward us,
that because of this he added: "And before all hills he begets me;" so as to
take away the, "he created me." For the second things in oracles, as better
and more firm, everywhere stop the things before. Competing then with these,
saying nothing =39.825= trustworthy, nor true, nor storing, but heretics using
some imported, and episodic, and burdensome teaching, he who received from him
the gift of theology of the much-longed-for and long-suffering Savior, of
whose mercy the earth is full, and teaching what sort of judgment one must
have about him, brought forth a common dogma unto all men: "In the beginning
was the Word;" that is unoriginate, which is ungenerated. Unpassable is the,
"was," conceding no non-existence beyond it to be conceived, as also has been
said in the first book. "And the Word was toward God;" instead of, According
to the Father he was incomparable, ungenerated, and existing looking within
his unattainable godhead. For the, He was toward this one, is understood not
only, He was with this one, but also comparatively, He was according to this
one. "And God was the Word;" that is, uncaused; for a cause of God,
impossible, unable, there is not. "All things came to be through him, and
without him came to be not even one;" instead of, he himself also is
ungenerated; for once for all it declared every created thing to have been
brought through him, as uncreated maker. And besides these irrefutable things,
there is also another opposite, and to which no one will be able to say
anything else even unto a thought toward an attempt. For Aquila, the one of
the interpreters, this writing having it in Hebrew, Adonai kanoni, issued
thus: "The Lord acquired me," not even himself speaking strictly; for it is
interpreted according to the precise: "The Lord nested me;" that is, he begot.
For properly from those leaping out from an egg, and not being created, is
said the, "he nested," or "he leapt out," and "he nests =39.828=" and "he
