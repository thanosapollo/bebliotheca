Homily on the Martyrs
=50.661=
The festivals of the martyrs are judged not only by the cycle of the days, but
also by the disposition of those who keep them. =50.662= What I mean is this:
have you imitated a martyr? Have you been zealous for his virtue? Have you run
in the footsteps of his =50.663= way of life? Then even when it is not a
martyr's day, you have kept a martyr's feast. For the honor of a martyr is the
imitation of a martyr. Just as those who do what is base remain without a feast
even amid festivals, so those who pursue virtue keep festival even when there
is no public gathering. For the feast is marked in the purity of conscience.
Paul made this clear when he said: Therefore let us keep the feast, not with
the old leaven of malice and wickedness, but with the unleavened bread of
sincerity and truth. So there is unleavened bread among the Jews, and there is
unleavened bread among us. Among them it is unleavened bread of flour; among us
it is purity of life, a life set free from every vice.
So the one who keeps his own life clean of filth and stain keeps festival every
day, always celebrating, even if it is not on a feast day, and even if not in
the martyrs' shrines, but sitting at home. For one can keep a martyrs' feast
even by oneself. I say this not so that we should fail to come to the tombs of
the martyrs, but so that when we come we may meet them with the eagerness that
is due, and so that we may show the same reverence not only on their days but
even apart from them. Who would not marvel today at our gathering, this
brilliant assembly, the boiling love, the warm affection, the ungovernable
longing? Almost the whole city has put in here. Neither fear of a master held
back a servant, nor the necessity of poverty a poor man, nor the weakness of
age an old man, nor the softness of nature a woman, nor the pride of wealth a
rich man, nor the arrogance of power a ruler. The desire for the martyrs has
thrown out all this inequality, both the weakness of nature and the necessity
of poverty, and with one chain has drawn so great a multitude here, and has
given them wings by love of the martyrs, so that they now spend their time as
if they were living as citizens of heaven. For you have trampled every
attachment to wantonness and shamelessness, and you burn with desire for the
martyrs. Just as when the sun's ray rises the wild beasts flee and sink into
their own dens, so when the light of the martyrs shines into your minds, every
disease is buried, and the bright flame of wisdom is kindled. But let us keep
this flame not only now, but always, even after this spiritual assembly has
been dismissed, and let us withdraw home with the same reverence, not giving
ourselves over to taverns and brothels and drunkenness and revels. You have
made the night day through the holy vigils. Do not make the day night again
through drunkenness and excess and songs of the brothel.
You have honored the martyrs by your presence, by your hearing, by your zeal.
Honor them also by a decent withdrawal, lest someone seeing you behaving
shamefully in a tavern say that you did not come for the martyrs, but to
increase your passion, to indulge a wicked desire. I say this not forbidding
enjoyment, but forbidding sin; not forbidding drinking, but forbidding
drunkenness. For wine is not a base thing; excess is the evil. Wine is a gift
of God; excess is an invention of the devil. Serve =50.664= the Lord, then,
with fear, and rejoice in him with trembling.
Do you want to enjoy luxury? Enjoy it at home, where even if drunkenness
occurs, there are many to cover it. Not in a tavern, lest you become a public
spectacle to those present and a stumbling block to others. And I say this not
commanding you to get drunk at home, but not to linger in a tavern. Consider
how great a mockery it is, after such a gathering, after vigils, after hearing
the holy Scriptures, after communion in the divine mysteries, and after a
spiritual banquet, for a man or a woman to be seen spending the day in a
tavern. Do you not know how great a punishment is laid up for the drunk? They
are cast out of the kingdom of God, they fall from the unspeakable goods, and
they are sent into the eternal fire. Who says this? Blessed Paul: Neither the
greedy, he says, nor drunkards, nor revilers, nor robbers will inherit the
kingdom of God.
What could be more wretched than the drunk, when for a little pleasure he loses
the enjoyment of so great a kingdom? Rather, the drunk cannot even harvest
pleasure. Pleasure belongs to measure; insensibility belongs to excess. The
man who does not even perceive where he sits or lies, how could he perceive the
pleasure of the drink? Unable even to see the sun itself because of the thick
cloud of drunkenness, how could he enjoy gladness? His darkness is so great
that the rays are not enough for him to dissolve that gloom. Always, beloved,
drunkenness is an evil, but most of all on a martyr's day. For besides the sin
it is also a very great insult, and madness, and a contempt of the divine
oracles. Hence the punishment would be double. If, then, after coming to the
martyrs you mean to get drunk after you withdraw from here, it is better to
stay at home, and not to behave shamefully, nor to insult the martyrs' feast,
nor to scandalize your neighbor, nor to besiege the mind, nor to add sins. You
have come to see men being scraped, streaming with blood, adorned with a choir
of wounds, stripping off the present life, flying toward the life to come.
Become worthy of the contestants. They despised life; you despise luxury. They
stripped off the present life; you strip off the desire for drunkenness. But
you want to revel? Stay by the martyr's tomb, pour out springs of tears there,
crush the mind, take a blessing from the tomb. Taking it as an advocate in your
prayers, linger always in the accounts of his struggles. Cling to the coffin,
fasten yourself to the chest. Not only the bones of the martyrs, but also their
tombs and their coffins gush with much blessing. Take holy oil and anoint your
whole body, the tongue, the lips, the neck, the eyes, and you will never fall
into the shipwreck of drunkenness. For the oil, through its fragrance, reminds
you of the martyrs' contests, and it bridles every wantonness, and holds you
=50.665= in much endurance, and prevails over the diseases of the soul. But you
want to linger in gardens and meadows and parks?
Not now, when so great a people is here, but on another day. For today is a
time of wrestling, today a spectacle of contests, not of luxury, not of ease.
You have come here not to give yourself over to softness, but to learn to
contend, to fight in the pancration, and, being a man, to beat down the
strength of the invisible demons. For no one who has come to a wrestling school
luxuriates, nor does anyone who has arrived at the time of wrestling prettify
himself, nor in the time of battle does he look for tables. So do not you,
having come to behold courage of soul and vigor of mind, a new and strange
trophy, a battle of another kind, wounds and wars, and a man's pancration,
bring in demonic deeds by giving yourself over to drunkenness and luxury after
this strange and terrible spectacle. Rather, gather the gains of the soul, and
so go home, showing to all by your look that you have withdrawn after beholding
martyrs. For just as those who come down from the theaters are clear to all,
disturbed, =50.666= confused,
softened, carrying the images of everything that happened there, so the one
returning from the spectacle of the martyrs should be known to all by his look,
his bearing, his walk, his compunction, the gathering of his mind: breathing
fire, drawn in, contrite, sober, watchful, proclaiming through the movements of
the body the wisdom within. So then let us return to the city with the fitting
good order, with an orderly walk, with understanding and self-control, with a
gentle and calm look.
For a man's dress, and the laugh of his teeth, and the step of his foot will
declare what concerns him. So always let us return from the martyrs, from
spiritual myrrh, from heavenly meadows, from new and strange spectacles, that
we ourselves may reap much ease, and become agents of freedom for others, and
attain the goods to come, by the grace and loving-kindness of our Lord Jesus
Christ, with whom to the Father, together with the Holy Spirit, be glory,
power, honor, now and always, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
