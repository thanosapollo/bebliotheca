Encomiastic Homily.
On our Father among the saints Meletius, archbishop of Antioch the
Great, and on the zeal of those who have assembled.
=50.515=

1. Casting my eyes all around this sacred flock, and seeing the whole city
present here, I do not know whom I should bless first: Saint Meletius, because
even after death he enjoys so great an honor, or your love, because even after
his end you show such goodwill toward your shepherds. Blessed is he, because
he was able to leave such affection upon you all; and you too are blessed,
because having received the deposit of love, you have kept it intact for him
who entrusted it, even until now. For already a fifth year has run by since he
departed to the Jesus whom he longed for, and as if you had seen him yesterday
and the day before, so with burning affection you have come to meet him today.

For these reasons he is to be envied, because he begot such sons; and you too
are to be envied, because you were allotted such a father. Noble is the root,
and wonderful; but the fruits of the root are also worthy of it. For just as
some wonderful root, hidden in the bosom of the earth, is itself not seen, yet
through its fruits displays the strength of its own virtue, so also the
blessed Meletius, hidden in this coffin, is himself not visible to our bodily
eyes, yet through you, his fruits, he displays the strength of his own grace;
and even if we keep silence, the feast alone and the warmth of your zeal
suffice to cry out more brightly than a trumpet the love of Saint Meletius for
his children. For he so kindled your mind toward love of him that you are
warmed even at the bare name, and are stirred at the very title. Therefore I
too now weave the name itself into my words, not simply, but on purpose and
with zeal, continually. And just as one weaving a golden crown, then setting
pearls in the density of the stones, makes the diadem more brilliant, so I
too, weaving today the crown of praises for the blessed head, weave into the
discourse, as a density of pearls, the continual naming of him, expecting
thereby to make it more longed-for and more splendid. For this is the law of
those who love, and such is the custom, that they even embrace the bare names
of those they love, and are warmed toward the very titles; which is just what
you have suffered in the case of this blessed man.

For when at the beginning you received him as he entered the city, each of you
named his own child from that man's name, thinking that through the name he
was bringing the saint into his own house; and the mothers, passing by fathers
and grandfathers and ancestors, laid the name of the blessed Meletius upon the
children that were born. For the longing of piety conquered nature, and the
children born were thereafter dear to those who planted them not only from
natural affection, but also from the disposition toward that =50.516= name.
For they thought that the name itself was an ornament of kinship, and a
safeguard of the house, and salvation for those so called, and a consolation
of longing; and just as some sitting in darkness, when one torch has been
kindled, light many lamps and each brings one into his own house, so also,
when that name fell upon the city like a light, each, as if lighting a lamp,
brought into his own house the name of that blessed man then, drawing to
himself through the naming as it were a treasure of countless goods; and what
was happening was a teaching of piety. For being continually compelled to
remember that name, and to have that saint upon the soul, they had the name as
a refuge from every irrational passion and thought; and this became so
widespread that everywhere, in the streets, and in the marketplace, and in the
fields, and on the roads, they were surrounded on all sides by this name. Nor
did you feel so much toward the name alone, but also toward the very form of
his body. What you did in names, this you also practiced upon his image. For
many engraved that holy image on the bezels of rings, and in impressions, and
on bowls, and on the walls of chambers, and everywhere, so that they might not
only hear that holy name, but also see everywhere the form of his body, and
have a twofold consolation of his absence.

For immediately upon entering, he was being driven from the city, the enemies
of the truth expelling him. But God permitted it, wishing to show both his
virtue and your courage. For when, having entered, like Moses into Egypt, he
freed the city from heretical error, and having cut off the rotten members
that were incurably diseased from the rest of the body, he restored unimpaired
health to the multitude of the Church, the enemies of the truth, not enduring
the correction, stirred the emperor then reigning and cast him out of the
city, expecting thereby to prevail over the truth and to overturn the
correction that had been made. But the opposite of what they expected came
about, and both your zeal was shown the more, and the proof of his teaching
skill shone forth: his, because in not even thirty full days he was able so to
found you in the zeal of the faith that, though countless winds assailed
afterward, that teaching remained unshaken; and your warmth was shown, because
in not even thirty full days you received the seeds sown by him with such
exactness that you sent the sowings down to the depth of the mind, and
thereafter yielded to none of the trials that were brought against you.

2. And it is worth not passing over that which happened in the persecution
itself. For when the governor of the city, driving the carriage, was going out
through the midst of the marketplace, having seated that saint beside him,
showers of stones were coming from every side upon the governor's head, the
city not enduring the separation, but choosing even to fall from the present
life rather than see that saint torn away. What then did that blessed man do
then? Seeing the casts of stones, he wrapped the governor's head in his own
garments and hid it, at once putting the enemies to shame by the excess of his
gentleness, and teaching his own disciples how great a forbearance they must
show toward those who do them wrong; and that they ought not only to work no
harm against the wrongdoers, but if danger should come upon the wrongdoers
from others, to beat this danger back also with all zeal. Who did not shudder
then, both seeing the city's mad love, and being struck with wonder at the
teacher's extreme philosophy, and his gentleness, and his meekness? For the
things happening then were paradoxical. The shepherd was being driven away,
and the sheep were not scattered; the helmsman was being cast out, and the
boat was not sinking; the husbandman was being persecuted, and the vine was
bearing more fruit.

For since you were bound to one another by the bond of love, neither onsets of
trials, nor uprisings of dangers, nor length of road, nor multitude of time,
nor anything else was able to separate you from the company of the blessed
shepherd; but he was being cast out so as to be far from his children, yet the
opposite came about. For he was bound more tightly to you by the bonds of
love; and taking the whole city with him he went away into Armenia. For the
body was settled in the fatherland; but the reasoning and the mind, as if
lightened by certain wings of the Spirit's grace, and always sojourning with
you, carried this whole people in his heart; which is just what you also
suffered. For sitting here and bounded by the city, flying each day into
Armenia by the spirit of love, and seeing the holy countenance, and hearing
the sweetest and blessed voice, you so returned again. And for this reason God
permitted him to be driven from the city at once, that he might show both your
firmness of faith, as I said before, to the enemies who war against you, and
his skill in teaching. And this is clear from what follows. For after the
first persecution, having returned, he spent here not thirty days only, but
months, and a year, and one, and two, and more. For since you had given a
sufficient proof of firmness concerning the faith, God granted you to delight
again in the father with freedom. For it was the greatest delight to enjoy
that holy sight. For not only teaching, nor speaking, but even being simply
seen, he was able to introduce every teaching of virtue into the soul of those
who saw him. When therefore he was driving in toward you, and the whole city
was moving toward the road, some came near, and touched his feet, and kissed
his hands, and heard his voice; but those hindered by the multitude and seeing
him only from afar, as if they had received a sufficient blessing from the
sight, and having nothing less than those who came near, so went away with all
=50.518= assurance; and what happened in the case of the apostles, this also
happened in his case.

For just as in the case of the apostles, as many as were not able to come
forward and be near, when their shadow was stretched out and touched those far
off, drew the same grace, and likewise went away healthy; so also now, as many
as were not able to come forward, perceiving as it were some spiritual glory
sent forth from that holy head and reaching even to those farthest away, all
went away filled with every blessing from the vision alone.

3. But when it seemed good to the common God of all to call him at last from
the present life, and to rank him in the choir of the angels, this too did not
happen simply, but imperial letters call him, God having moved the emperor;
and they call him not somewhere near, nor close by, but into Thrace itself,
that Galatians, and Bithynians, and Cilicians, and Cappadocians, and all who
dwell by Thrace might learn our good things; that the bishops everywhere on
earth, seeing his holiness as an archetypal image, and taking from him a clear
example of the ministry according to this office, might have a certain rule,
sure and most clear, according to which they must administer and govern the
Churches. For because of the city's greatness and because of the emperor's
sitting there, many from many parts of the inhabited world were then flowing
together there; and the bishops of the Churches, because the Churches, having
recovered from a long war and storm, were beginning to receive peace and calm,
were all being called there by imperial letters. Then therefore this man also
arrives there. And just as in the case of the three children, when they were
about to be proclaimed and crowned, having quenched the power of the fire,
having trampled the tyrant's pride, having convicted every form of impiety,
the theater sat for them from the whole inhabited world; for the satraps and
consuls and governors of every land were called for another reason, but became
spectators of those athletes; so also it happened then, so that the theater
might become splendid for the blessed man: the bishops who administer the
Churches of every land, having been called for another cause, arrived, and
beheld that saint. And when they had seen, and had learned exactly his piety,
his wisdom, his zeal of faith, every virtue befitting a priest brought to
completeness in him, then God called him to himself. And this happened also
for the sparing of our city. For if he had given up his soul here, the bulk of
the disaster would have been unbearable.

For who would have endured to see that blessed man drawing his last breaths?
Who would have endured to see those eyelids of the eyes being drawn down, and
the mouth being closed, enjoining last words? =50.519= Who, seeing these
things, would not have been beside himself at the greatness of the disaster?
That this might not happen, God arranged that he should give up his soul in a
foreign land, so that in the intervening time, having rehearsed the disaster,
when we should see the dead man enter, we might not be struck in the soul, the
mind having been accustomed to the lament; which is just what happened.

For when the city received that holy body, it mourned even so, and wailed
greatly; but it quickly put an end to the mourning, both from the cause
already spoken, and from that which is about to be said. For God who loves
mankind, having pity on us in the pain, quickly raised up for us another
shepherd, who with much exactness kept that man's features, and preserved the
image of all his virtue; who, having also gone up upon the throne, immediately
stripped us of the mourning garment, and quenched the pain, and rather renewed
the memory of the blessed man. And the ache was withering, but the love was
kindled more vehemently, and the despondency was utterly taken away; and yet
in the losses of those most dear it is not accustomed to happen so; but when a
woman loses a longed-for son, or even a revered husband, so long as she keeps
his memory burning, the mourning also is more vehemently nourished in the
soul; but when time coming in has softened the mourning, the prime of the
memory is quenched together with the vehemence of the pain; but in the case of
this blessed man the opposite happened: the =50.520= despondency was
altogether cast out, but the memory did not go away with the grief, but grew
more vehement. And you are witnesses, who after so long a time fly around the
body of the blessed Meletius as bees around a honeycomb; and the cause was
that the affection toward him was not of nature, but a reasoning of right
judgment. Therefore it was not quenched by death, nor dimmed by time; but it
grows, and advances toward the greater; not only among you who saw him, but
also among those who did not know him. For this is the wonderful thing, that
even as many as have been born later than his life, these themselves also are
kindled toward the same longing. You then who are elders have this advantage
over those who did not see him, namely this very thing, to have been with him
and to have enjoyed the holy company; but those who did not see him have an
advantage over you, because, not having beheld the man, they show toward him a
longing not less than yours who saw him.

Let us then pray in common, all of us, rulers and ruled, women and men, elders
and young, slaves and free, taking the blessed Meletius himself as a partner
of this prayer; for he has now greater boldness of speech, and the affection
toward us is warmer; that this love may be increased for us, and that we all
may be counted worthy, just as here we are near this coffin, so there also to
be able to be near his eternal tabernacle, and to obtain the goods laid up; of
which may it come to pass that we all attain, by the grace and love of mankind
of our Lord Jesus Christ, through whom and with whom to the Father, together
with the Holy Spirit, be glory and might unto the ages of ages. Amen.
