HOMILY ON PETER THE APOSTLE And Elijah the prophet.

=50.725=

1. Few of us are present today. What then is the cause? We are keeping the
memorial of martyrs, and no one has come to meet us. Did the length of the
road throw them into sloth? Rather it was not the length of the road, but
sloth that hindered them. For just as nothing can hinder the earnest man who
is aroused in his purpose, so everything can hinder the slothful and the one
who has given way. The martyrs poured out their own blood for the truth, and
you do not even make light of a short road? They laid down their head for
Christ, and you will not even come to meet him outside the city for the
Master's sake? The Master died for you, and you hang back for him? A memorial
of martyrs, and you are slothful and have given way? You ought to be present,
and to see the devil defeated, and a martyr conquering, and God glorified, and
the Church crowned. But this is their excuse: I am a sinner, he says, and I
cannot come. Precisely because you are a sinner, come, so that you may not
fall away from righteousness.

Who among men is without sin, tell me? But for this there is sacrifice and
Church, for this prayers and fasts, because the wounds of the soul are many;
for this the medicines against them have also been found, and for each of the
soul's wounds a fitting medicine has been prepared. You have a Church offering
sacrifices, prayers of the Fathers, the supply of the Holy Spirit, memorials
of martyrs, a gathering of saints, and many such things able to call you back
from sin to righteousness; you did not come =50.726= to the prayer of the
martyrs: what pardon have you? Nothing terrible stood in the way, and you were
hindered from being of one mind with the martyrs? But a worldly care held you?
Still greater is the charge. You did not lend a small hour to God, that you
might receive a whole day? I am a sinner, he says, and I cannot. Because you
are a sinner, for this very reason enter; or do you not know that even those
who wait upon the altar themselves are fastened to sins? For they are clothed
in flesh, and entangled with blood, [and they are bound together with bones,
and we ourselves, who sit upon the throne and teach, are entangled with sins.
But we do not despair of God's loving-kindness, nor do we ascribe inhumanity
to him; for we are all men entangled from the same things;] and we do not
decline the teaching, looking toward the sea of God's loving-kindness. And if
you, having sinned, enter, the charge is not so great; for you are under
teaching; but we, the more we excel in dignity, by so much also lie under the
charge; for it is one thing for the one being taught to sin, and another for
the one who teaches; yet even so we do not decline this, lest under pretext of
humility we fall into sloth.

And this too has come about by divine administration, that the priests
themselves should also fall under sins. And hear how it is: The teachers
themselves and the priests, if they did not sin, nor were subject to the
passions of life, would be inhuman toward the others and unforgiving; but for
this reason he prepared even the priests themselves to be enslaved to
passions, and the rulers, =50.727= so that from what they themselves suffer
they may also grant pardon to the others. And God has always administered
things thus, and not only now, but also of old, and those to whom he was about
to entrust the Church and the people, these he permitted to fall under sin, so
that from their own faults they might become loving

toward the others. For if they themselves did not sin, they would have no
pardon to give to those who sin, but having become inhuman they would have cut
all off from the Church. And that these things are of this sort, and I do not
say this by guessing, come, let us speak from the very consideration itself:
Peter was about to be entrusted with the keys of the Church, or rather he was
even entrusted with the keys of the heavens, and he was about to be entrusted
with the multitude of the people. For what does the Master say to him?
Whatever you bind on earth shall be bound in the heavens, and whatever you
loose on earth shall be loosed in the heavens. Now Peter was somewhat quietly
also severe; and if he had also had sinlessness, what pardon would those
taught by him have received? But for this reason divine grace arranges that he
fall into some sin, so that from what he himself suffered he might become
loving toward the others. And see whom he permits to fall into sin: that
Peter, the summit of the apostles, the unshaken foundation, the unbreakable
rock, the first of the Church, the unconquerable harbor, the unshaken tower.
That Peter who says to Christ: Even if I must die with you, I will not deny
you; Peter who from a divine revelation confessed the truth: You are the
Christ, the Son of the living God; this man, having entered on that night on
which Christ was betrayed, and standing at the fire warming himself, and a
certain girl, it says, coming up says to him, You also were with this man
yesterday; and Peter: I do not know this man. Just now you were saying: Even
if I must die with you; now you deny and say, I do not know this man? O Peter,
is this the whole of what you promised? Not yet knowing tortures, not yet
scourges, but simply hearing a little girl's word, you went into denial. Do
you deny, Peter? Not yet racks, not yet scourges, not yet blows, not yet
rages, not yet rulers, not yet sharpened swords, not yet decrees laid down,
not yet kings threatening, not yet deaths expected, not yet prisons and cliffs
and sea, not yet any of such things, and already you have denied, I do not
know the man? Again the girl to him: You also were with this man yesterday.
And he to her: I do not know the man of whom you speak. Who is it that tells
you that you are denying? None of those in authority, but a woman, and she a
doorkeeper, a castaway, a captive, worthy of no account: she speaks, and you
deny? O strange things! A girl that Peter; a harlot coming up disturbed
Peter's faith; Peter, the pillar, the breakwater, did not bear a girl's
threat, but she only spoke, and the pillar was shaken, and the breakwater
itself was being storm-tossed. Whom did you see, Peter, that you deny? A cheap
girl, and a contemptible doorkeeper; these you saw and you deny? Then again
the third time: You also were with this man yesterday; yet he denied =50.728=
the third time. And so then Jesus looking at him brought him to remembrance of
what had been said; and he, understanding, began to weep and to repent of the
sin.

Yet the loving one permitted him the sin; for he knew that as a man he
underwent something human; but, as I said before, this was so administered,
and Peter was permitted to sin, because he was about to be entrusted with a
great people, lest being severe and sinless he should be unforgiving toward
his own brothers. He fell under sin, so that considering his own sin and the
Master's pardon, he might also pardon the others for the sake of
loving-kindness according to a divine administration fitting to God. For he
himself was permitted to sin who was about to be entrusted with the Church,
the pillar of the Churches, the harbor of the faith, Peter the teacher of the
inhabited world was permitted to sin, so that the pardon of this man might
become a ground of loving-kindness for the others. And why have these things
been said by me? Because we too, the priests who sit upon a throne and teach,
are bound together with sins.

For this reason neither an angel nor an archangel was entrusted to serve as
priest (for they are sinless), lest through severity they should at once
strike with lightning those of the people who sin; but a man from men was
entrusted with this throne, himself also bound together with pleasure and sin,
so that when he takes someone sinning, from his own faults he may become more
loving toward that one who has sinned. For if an angel were priest, and he
took someone who had committed fornication, he would at once destroy him,
himself not entangled with this passion. For this reason if an angel had
received the authority of priesthood, he would not teach, but would at once
destroy, because he is not such, and it would carry him into anger against
such a one. But for this reason a man was entrusted, knowing his own faults,
and having them from experience, so that he may pardon those who sin, and not
be moved through anger, and the Church may have leisure through the assembly.

2. But why, standing only as far as Peter, have I spoken this subject in this
way, and do I not go up a little with the word to another person? Bring me
into the midst: I mean Elijah, that prophet, the earthly angel and heavenly
man, the one who walks on the ground and drives the heavenly things, the man
of three cubits who treads on high, and who was borne up even to the very
vaults of heaven, the steward of the waters, whose tongue became a treasury of
the waters and a key of the heavens; the poor man and the rich, the unlettered
and the philosopher; poor, because he possessed nothing, but rich, because he
held the clouds of the rain in his tongue. And he too was so severe toward
those who sinned that he once prayed that rain not be given; and what does he
say? As the Lord lives, he says, there shall be no rain upon the earth except
by my mouth. What are you doing, Elijah? What do you declare? At least pray
the Master, and so fulfill the word: As the Lord lives, there shall be no
rain, except by my mouth.

Where are the heretics who say that the Son of God prays? Wretched and
miserable and shameless man: Elijah declares, and the Son prays? the servant
commands, and the Master entreats? Do you not even grant him the honor as you
do to Elijah? =50.729= Will you not grant to the Master the same share as to
the servant? That one does not pray, nor does he entreat, but he put forth a
word of truth, and he shut the heaven. Pray first, Elijah. But what of Elijah?
I know my Master, that he hearkens to me; for I do this from zeal. O strange
and paradoxical things! Did you see a Master conquered by a servant's
goodwill? For Elijah did this from much zeal. For he saw many unseemly things
happening; he saw fornication being practiced with much wickedness. For it was
night, and on that account it also held the whole inhabited world; a very
dense cloud covered all things. For all were advancing toward evil; it was a
worldwide shipwreck, not of waters, but of wantonness; chastity was gone, and
incontinence was parading; virtue was being persecuted, and vice was taking
its pride; hills and mountains, and glens, and roads, and chambers and the air
were being defiled; the sun was being smoked, the earth was being stained,
heaven was being set at nought, the whole creation was sick from idolatry; as
in night all were walking about, paying attention to none of the created
things, they saw a stone, and they worshipped it as a god; they saw wood, and
they likewise thought this a god. A very dense night held them; they saw the
Creator, and they worshipped the creatures. Elijah alone had the lamp of
virtue, sitting as upon a mountain-peak of philosophy and practicing, alone
having the lamp of piety, yet the illumination benefited no one because they
lay in a stupor, and they were held by idolatry.

So Elijah was raging, he was being sawn, he was lamenting, he was speaking
words, no one heard; he was entreating, no one endured it; then from zeal he
wants to bring them in and

to chasten them, so that even thus, wasting with famine, they may come by
prayer to the Creator, so that the famine may become for them a ground of
piety. But nothing, he says, can teach them like famine; for thus, being
straitened from every side, they will go to the Creator of all. What then does
Elijah? As the Lord lives, he says, there shall be no rain, except by my
mouth. The word of the prophet went forth, and at once the air was changed,
the heaven became bronze, not changing its nature, but having its activity
bridled; at once the elements were being recast. The word of the prophet fell,
like a fever, into the flanks of the earth, and all things at once were being
dried, all were being laid waste, all were being made to vanish; they saw at
once herbs drying, and plants, and trees fruitful and unfruitful, those in the
plains, those by the sea; all things together drying, every living age being
made to vanish, the lament of children and the weeping of mothers, and much
despair. One word of the prophet went forth, and see how many things it
worked. All then were dying, the wild beasts, the cattle, the children, the
men, the animals, all the birds; it was a worldwide shipwreck, and the
disaster overtook the whole earth together; no one was being saved, but all
were dying from the drought, the plants were being dried, springs, rivers,
lakes, and simply all things perished. A worldwide shipwreck held the whole
inhabited world, not through water, but through drought; the heaven was bound,
and then was being held back, and it was changing the whole of nature. All
then died by a God-sent wrath and perished; but Elijah cared for no one; for
he was drunk with zeal.

So every untimely age was coming to an end. What are you doing, Elijah? Be it
so, the young men =50.730= sinned; why are the children being chastened? Be it
so, the men sinned, why do the cattle die with them? Have you clothed yourself
with so great a lack of pity? You care for none of the men; you have no wife,
no child; you despise those who are perishing. What then does God say to him?
Go, he says, to the river Cherith, and I will command ravens to feed you
there. Again I would gladly question the Jew, bringing him into the midst, in
order to show him that the law was overturning the things of the law, and it
itself had in itself neither the standing nor the upright; for it was not
truth, but a shadow; those things a shadow, these truth; those things a type,
these the realities. Elijah whom you revere, whom you expect to come, about
whom you have much discourse, and you call him a prophet, how was this man fed
by a raven? For the raven according to the law is unclean, and thus the law
declared the raven to be unclean. How then was the prophet fed by the unclean
raven? For if the law said the raven was unclean, surely the one fed from the
raven would be unclean. But this was not so, away with it, let it not be! but
Elijah was fed by a raven, thinking nothing unclean of the things created by
the Lord. Then from there, since time passed, and then the river was dried up,
he raises the prophet toward food. Go, he says, to Zarephath of Sidon, and I
will command a widow woman to feed you there; and God was doing this according
to a certain economy. For since Elijah did not know the things that were
happening (for he was sitting in one place, he did not see the worldwide
disaster, how all things were dried, how the lakes, how the rivers, how the
plants, how the trees, how the untimely, how the ripe, how the fruitful, how
the unfruitful, how those by springs, those by lakes of waters, the capture of
the birds, of the cattle, of all the others, the death of the children, the
wailings of the mothers, that worldwide disaster), God raises him, and makes
him travel an immense land from there as far as Sidon, so that even thus,
seeing the things that were happening, Elijah might ask his Master to give
rain. He therefore sends him for this reason on that long road, not as though
God were unable to feed him there, but as wishing to show Elijah the disaster,
so that he might ask him concerning rain. For he himself was able even without
this, but he did not wish to insult his own servant,

leaving that one to be the author of the evils, and himself of the goods; but
he was waiting for the servant's entreaty. Then not even so was that one bent,
but using a certain arrogance he was thus walking the road, clothed with lack
of pity, making account of no one; for as I said he was drunk from zeal.

Why have you become mindless, Elijah? Why have you put on so great an
inhumanity? Wait a little, and you yourself too will be shown to be under sin.
Because of the sin of those who dwell there you called a drought, and you shut
heaven, and you bridled the earth, and you held the course of nature, and you
are not even willing to ask concerning what has come to pass? After not long
you yourself too will be shown to be under sin, and having obtained
loving-kindness from your Master, so that you may become more loving toward
your fellow-servants.

3. And today I have come out to speak the word about these same things for
this reason, that for this reason an angel did not become priest, but a man
from man, lest those who sin be destroyed by one who is sinless. For if the
priest were an angel not sinning, he would at once have punished those who
sin, but for this reason he became a man, so that he may pardon the one of
like passions, taking a reminder from his own passions for those of the same
race. Then we brought forward that even the great ones, to whom he was about
to entrust a great people, he permitted to fall under sin, and to grant them
pardon so that afterward they too from what they were taught might become more
loving. And I brought forward Peter, such an apostle, permitted indeed to sin,
but blotting out the sin by repentance through the loving-kindness of God.
Come then, let us return also to Elijah, and let us show the sea of his
achievements, that God indeed wished to show loving-kindness, but Elijah did
not show loving-kindness; and he wished to give rain, but he was seeking a
request from the servant. What then? Elijah passed the whole road, and came to
Zarephath of Sidon, and he saw a certain widow woman gathering wood. Yet see
Elijah's philosophy and faith. Again another sea of virtue received him. He
did not say to God: To whom are you sending me? You make me undergo so many
dangers, and you send me to a widow into extreme famine? Are there not other
men more well-provided, able to console my poverty? but I walk so much land,
in order to meet a widow, into the very disaster of the evils, and not only a
widow, but also a poor one? Consider how the servant said none of these
things; for he was confident in his own Master who makes possible things from
impossible. Go, he says, to Zarephath of Sidon, and behold a widow gathering
wood. Why then do you still go on, Elijah? And why do you run up to the widow?
You saw the forecourts of poverty, do not ask the sufferings of her modest
state; you saw the entrance of poverty, ask nothing about the things within.
Where are you entering, Elijah? You saw her gathering wood, and you seek to be
fed by her? Yet having the Master's word as a pledge, he went off conversing
with the widow. And what does he say? Give me a little water, that I may
drink.

Do you see Elijah's wisdom? how he did not at once go off to the stronger
thing, but to the cheaper? he did not say, Give me bread, but Give me water.
First he seeks water, guessing that if she has plenty of water, she can also
have plenty of bread. Give me, he says, a little water. And the widow going
off brought it to him, and he drank; and thus taking courage he says: You
shall also take for me a morsel of bread, and I shall eat. And she said to
him: As the Lord lives, I have no cake, but only as much as a handful of flour
in the jar, and a little oil in the cruse, which we shall make and eat, I and
my children, and we shall die. What then does Elijah? Go, he says, and make me
a cake by itself, and let me eat, and thus you shall do for your children, and
they shall eat.

What are you doing, Elijah? Be it so, you want bread =50.732=, why do you seek
it both by itself and first? Ought you not to give thanks, that you may eat
with the children? You want to eat

alone, and to choke the children with famine? [I do not want to choke, but I
want to do a kindness;] I know the abundance of my Master. Yet the widow was
not troubled nor did she think any such unseemly thing, nor did she say: You
are the one who made this famine, and in the remnants of the famine you want
to be fed by me? nor did she say, You passed so great a world, and you came to
me to destroy my children with famine, yourself having become the cause of the
famine? but the woman, becoming a sort of Abraham, going in did according to
the word of the prophet, and one could see the widow more hospitable than
Abraham. For he, being well-provided, entertained angels; but she, expecting
death from famine, entertained the prophet. And one could see nature being
despised, and hospitality being honored; one could see bowels being cast
aside, and a prophet being received; she made a whole people of children into
a tomb; for as far as the widow's purpose went, those had died, but as far as
God's loving-kindness went, they were living and being strengthened. I do not
know how I shall praise the widow; how she despised children and practiced
hospitality, how nature itself did not grow numb, how the womb was not
altered, how the bowels were not dissolved seeing a whole people of children
about to be destroyed by famine. But she became above all things, and she
entertained the prophet. When then the prophet received and ate, thereafter he
pays back the returns; the widow sowed hospitality, and at once she reaped the
ear of hospitality waving. For what does Elijah say to her? As the Lord lives,
the jar of flour shall not fail, and the cruse of oil shall not diminish. And
the widow's right hand became a winepress, and the left a threshing-floor, and
handfuls in necessity furnishing the fruit, and through the prophet's word
feeding the widow. The widow's house became a winepress and a threshing-floor;
no rain, no shower, no spring, no summer, no heat, no constraints of winds, no
changes of seasons, but only one word going forth, and it itself brought out
from his own mind, furnishes the abundance to the widow. Then from there, that
I may cut the word short, he went off to Ahab the king. I am now speaking of
his achievements, so that if you see him having sinned, you may learn that
God's grace has loving-kindness. What then does Ahab say to him? Are you the
one who is turning Israel aside? And he to him: Not I, but you and your
father's house. Did you see a prophet's boldness, how he rebuked the king?
Then when he was sitting on the mountain, and a captain of fifty came to him
saying: Man of God, come down, the king calls you; and he to him: And if I am
a man of God, let fire come down from heaven, and let it devour you and your
fifty.

Then a second time another captain of fifty says: Man of God, come down, the
king has need of you. And what does Elijah say to him? And if I am a man of
God, let fire come down from heaven, and let it devour you and your fifty.
Then again when he came to the contest of the prayer =50.733= he called the
priests of Baal of the shame saying, Let us pray, and he said: You make an
altar by yourselves, and choose two oxen, and put wood upon the one ox, and do
not put fire, and I will do likewise; and you call upon the name of your gods,
and I upon the name of my God, and it shall be, whichever god answers by fire,
this is true God. Then the priests of the shame made an altar, and they began
to call upon Baal saying: Hear us, Baal, hear us. And when much prayer had
been made there was no one who heard (For there was no voice, and there was no
hearing), then Elijah was waiting, being long-suffering while those were
praying; and when he saw that their stretching was much, but there was no one
who hearkened, he mocks them saying: Cry with a great voice, perhaps your god
is sleeping. Then when it became midday, and the hour advanced, he says to
them: Withdraw from now, so that I too may make my whole-burnt-offering; and
he made an altar, and he put on the wood, and he said: Bring water around the
altar,

and they brought it; Double it, and they doubled it; Triple it, and they
tripled it. But see also why Elijah does this. Because it is the custom of
error to attach its own things to the truth, which the harlot women do, and
they get in first, and they call the free women harlots, so that those may not
have anything with which to insult them back. Yet Elijah is wise in this too;
why? but what I am about to say, I myself have become a spectator of: In the
altars of the idols there are holes from below the altar, and there is a
certain pit below unseen, and the workers of error go down into the pit, and
thus through those holes from below they blow the fire up onto the sacrifice,
so that many are deceived, and think the fire to be heavenly. So that Elijah
too may not be suspected of doing some such thing, he poured on the water, so
that the water might expose the hole; for of necessity where the water finds a
hole, it does not stand, but goes down. He therefore filled the altar, and he
prays saying: Hear me, Lord, in fire today; you heard me in water, hear me
also in fire. And behold, when he called, fire came down quickly from heaven,
and it devoured the sacrifice, and the stones, and the fire licked up the
water. And what does he say to the people? Seize the priests of the shame; let
none of them be saved; and they took them, and they destroyed them, four
hundred and fifty priests of Baal, and four hundred of the high places.

Jezebel the wife of Ahab heard these things that had come to pass, and she
sends to Elijah saying: May the gods do these things to me, and may they add
these, if by tomorrow I do not set your soul as the soul of one of them; and
Elijah hearing fled. Where is Elijah so great and of such stature? For my aim
is to show that he fell under sin; I speak the sin not accusing the righteous
man, but working a ground of salvation, so that when you see those men having
sinned, yet not despairing of themselves, but obtaining God's loving-kindness,
you, if you sin, may hope for a more abundant salvation. Since Jezebel said,
May the gods do these things to me, and may they add these, if by tomorrow I
do not set your soul as the soul =50.734= of one of them, Elijah heard, and he
fled a journey of forty days. O excess of cowardice! He heard a woman's word,
and fleeing he walked a road of forty days; not one day, not a second, not a
third, but as soon as the woman's word entered into the prophet, from
cowardice he does not know what he was doing, going off in so great a flight.

What is it to you, Elijah? Are you the one who shut the heaven, and bridled
the rain, who commanded the air, and called fire from above, who slaughtered
the priests, who said to king Ahab: You are the one who is turning Israel
aside, and your father's house; who said: As the Lord lives, there shall be no
rain, except by my mouth; who made the widow's house a threshing-floor and
handfuls, [who commanded the elements, you heard a harlot's word, and you
fled, and one woman leads you away captive? The two citadels were exposed by a
woman; Peter feared a girl, and this man Jezebel; they fell into the same
sins. And Elijah fled a journey of forty days. Where is your zeal, Elijah,
when you were saying, As the Lord lives, there shall be no rain, except by my
mouth? when you were rebuking the king,] when you called the fire? You did so
many things, and you did not bear one woman's word? Where is your tension,
when you were not willing to ask your Master, that he give rain upon the
earth? For he was saying to you through clear proofs: Ask me who am able to
give even without you, but I did not wish to, so that just as you became the
author of the evils, so you may also become a ruler of the goods. For you
worked, Elijah, a work full of lack of pity; but God was pitying the disaster,
because he himself is creator of all, and craftsman of all; and likewise
guardian of all, and he wished to slacken your inhumanity, and you persisted
in it. He was saying to you: I know the disaster that is happening, I know the
weepings of the mothers, I know the wailings of the children; I see the earth,
which I made, being made to vanish, and I wish to show loving-kindness, but I
do not wish to insult you, and without

your mind to send rain, lest you be the cause of the evils, but a stranger to
the goods, but I honor you. O Master's loving-kindness conquered by a
servant's goodwill! Yet he himself had much arrogance as being sinless; but
now he too was shown to have fallen under sin, God permitting and devising, so
that from the loving-kindness which he himself obtained, he too may not be
inhuman toward the others. And Elijah, it says, fled a journey of forty days.
Where are those words which, saying to the captains of fifty, he brought down
fire, and burned these men? God then wishing to show that even when he was
doing the wonders, it was not he himself who was working, but the power of
God, and see what happens. When God was at work, even kings were falling and
rulers and peoples; when God withdrew, even a woman stood over him as fearful;
God withdrew, and nature was exposed. Yet after Elijah fled forty days, he
came and was sleeping in a certain place, and God comes to him, the Master to
the =50.735= servant, the guardian and the loving one; and what does he say?
knowing indeed why he came there, yet nevertheless asking: What are you doing
here, Elijah? and why are you here? hinting at his flight, instead of: You
fled; where is that boldness? That you may learn not to be confident in
yourself. What are you doing here, Elijah? and why are you here? Yet Elijah
answers, having other things in his mind, but saying other things. For what

they have dug down your altars, and I was left alone, and they seek my soul to

did you flee, he says, Elijah; for you are not the only one who did not
worship Baal; then he adds exposing him: I have left for myself seven thousand
men, who have not bent a knee to Baal.

He therefore exposed him, that he did not flee for this, but because he was
bearing the fear of the woman; and one woman made so great and such a man a
runaway and a fugitive, so that you may learn, Elijah, when you do some
paradoxical thing, not to ascribe it to yourself, but to the power of God. Did
you see how when the grace withdrew, nature was exposed? Elijah fled a journey
of forty days. O intensification of fear! O excess of cowardice! He did not
flee one day, not a second, not a third, but forty days, and he himself
reached the utterly desert places, having no food, bringing no provision; for
being drunk from fear, he did not care for these things, but he was pursuing
the uninhabited places. The woman's word entered into the prophet, and just as
a west wind falling violently upon a sail sends the ship off by the rush; so
the woman's word entering into the prophet sent him out with much rush into
the desert. Where =50.736= is it, Elijah, the things of that boldness? where
the fearful mouth? where the tongue the charioteer of the rains; where the one
commanding both elements, at one time shutting heaven, at another calling fire
upon the sacrifice? Yet, as I said before, he was doing these things with the
grace at work; wherefore he is also exposed by God. Do you see how he was
permitted to fall by a small sin so that he might put on a whole tunic of
loving-kindness? For the rest, Elijah, you have been taught, become loving as
your Master, from what you were taught, from what you learned from your
Master. Did you see how God permitted those pillars, those breakwaters, those
towers, to fall into a small sin? lest by their own sinlessness they cut all
off from the Church; so that when they see someone having sinned, and wish to
display lack of pity toward him, they may remember their own sin, and impart
to him the same loving-kindness which they themselves also have obtained from
the Master. We have said these things not accusing the righteous, but
preparing a way of salvation for you beforehand, so that when you sin, you may
not despair of your own salvation by the memory of the righteous who have
offended, and who by repentance were not diminished from the honor.

And we spoke first of their virtues, and then we introduced the sins. You too
then, even if you are a sinner, do not be left behind from the church; and
even if you are righteous, do not be left behind, so that having the knowledge
of the Scriptures ringing in your ears you may remain righteous, remembering
the things concerning the kingdom of the heavens, and those goods which God
prepared for those who love him; because to him belongs the glory with the Son
and the Holy Spirit, now and always, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
