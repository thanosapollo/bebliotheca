OF OUR FATHER AMONG THE SAINTS GREGORY THE THEOLOGIAN
ARCHBISHOP OF CONSTANTINOPLE, ORATIONS.
ORATION I.
On the Holy Pascha and on Tardiness.
A. It is the day of Resurrection, and the beginning is favorable, and let us
be made bright by the feast, and let us embrace one another; let us say
Brothers even to those who hate us, not only to those who have done or
suffered something through love; let us forgive all things for the
Resurrection; let us grant pardon to one another, both I who was tyrannized
with the good tyranny, for this I now add, and you who tyrannized well, if you
blame me at all for the tardiness, as perhaps this is even better and more
honorable to God than the haste of others; for it is a good thing both to
withdraw a little before God, as that Moses of old, and Jeremiah later, and to
run readily to him who calls, as Aaron and Isaiah, only both piously, the one
through one's own weakness, the other through the power of him who calls.
B. A mystery anointed me; for a mystery I withdrew a little, enough to examine
myself; with a mystery I also enter in together, bringing as a fair helper of
my cowardice and weakness the day, that he who today rose from the dead may
also renew me in the =35.397= Spirit, and having clothed me with the new man,
may give me to the new creation, to those born according to God, as a good
fashioner and teacher, both dying together with Christ readily and rising
together with him.
C. Yesterday the lamb was slain, and the doorposts were anointed, and Egypt
mourned the firstborn, and the destroyer passed us by, and the seal was
fearful and venerable, and we were walled about with the precious blood; today
we have fled Egypt purely, and Pharaoh the bitter master, and the heavy
taskmasters, and we have been freed from the clay and the brickmaking; and
there is no one who will hinder us from keeping feast to the Lord our God, the
feast of the Exodus, and from keeping feast, not in the old leaven of malice
and wickedness, but in unleavened bread of sincerity and truth, bringing
nothing of Egyptian and godless dough.
D. Yesterday I was being crucified with Christ, today I am glorified together;
yesterday I was being put to death with him, today I am made alive together;
yesterday I was being buried with him, today I am raised together. But let us
bear fruit to him who suffered and rose for us. Perhaps you think I mean gold,
or silver, or woven stuffs, or stones of the transparent and precious kind,
flowing matter of earth, and remaining below, of which the wicked always have
the greater part, and the slaves of the things below and of the world-ruler.
Let us bear fruit of ourselves, the possession most precious to God and most
proper to him; let us restore to the image that which is according to the
image, let us recognize our dignity, let us honor the archetype, let us know
the power of the mystery, and for what Christ died.
E. Let us become as Christ, since Christ also became as we; let us become gods
through him, since he too became man for our sake. He took on the worse, that
he might give the better; he became poor, that we might be enriched by his
poverty; he took the form of a slave, that we might receive back =35.400=
freedom; he came down, that we might be raised up; he was tempted, that we
might conquer; he was dishonored, that he might glorify; he died, that he
might save; he went up, that he might draw to himself those lying below in the
fall of sin. Let each give all things, let each bear all fruit to him who gave
himself a ransom for us and an exchange; but he will give nothing such as
himself, understanding the mystery, and through him becoming all that that one
became for us.
F. He bears fruit for you, as you see, a shepherd; for this the good shepherd
hopes and prays, and asks from you who are under his hand, he who lays down
his life for the sheep; and he gives you himself double in place of single;
and he makes the staff of old age a staff of the Spirit; and he adds to the
lifeless temple the living one, to the very beautiful, and even heavenly, one
of whatever sort and size, yet the one most precious to himself, and him
completed with many sweats and toils, and may it be said, even worthy of the
toils; and he adds to you all that is his. O the greatness of soul, or, to
speak more truly, the love of children: the gray hair, the youth, the temple,
the high priest, the testator, the heir, the words which you longed for; and
of these not those that are in vain and flowing into air and stopping at the
hearing, but those which the Spirit writes, and imprints on tablets of stone,
or rather of flesh, not engraved from the surface, nor easily wiped away, but
marked in depth, not with ink, but with grace.
G. These things then this venerable Abraham gives to you, the patriarch, the
precious and reverend head, the lodging-place of all good things, the canon of
virtue, the perfection of the priesthood, who offers today to the Lord the
voluntary sacrifice, the only-begotten, the one from the promise. But you,
bear fruit both to God and to us by being shepherded well, being encamped in a
place of green pasture, and being nurtured upon water of rest, knowing the
shepherd well, and being known, and following him who calls pastorally and
freely through the door; but not following a stranger who climbs over through
the court, in a thievish and =35.401= plotting way, nor hearing a foreign
voice, which steals and scatters away from the truth into mountains and
deserts and pits and places which the Lord does not oversee, and which leads
away from the sound faith, that in Father and Son and Holy Spirit, the one
Godhead and power, whose voice my sheep have always heard and may they hear,
but which by counterfeit and corrupted words plunders and tears away from the
true and first shepherd; from which may it be that all of us, both shepherds
and flock, far from sickly and deadly grass, both grazing and pasturing, be
all one in Christ Jesus both now and unto the rest there; to whom be the glory
and the power unto the ages. Amen.
