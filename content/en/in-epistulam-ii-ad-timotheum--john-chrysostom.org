3. What then? he says; does God ordain all, even the unworthy? God
does not ordain all, but he himself works through all, even if they
should be unworthy, for the people to be saved. For if through an ass,
and through Balaam, through a polluted man, he spoke for the sake of
the people, much more through the priest. For what does God not do for
the sake of our salvation? For what does he not utter? and through whom
does he not work? If he worked through Judas, and through those who
prophesied, to whom he says, I do not know you, depart from me, you who
work lawlessness, and others cast out demons; much more will he work
through the priests. Since if we are going to search the lives of the
rulers, we ourselves are going to be the ordainers of the teachers, and
the things above become below, the feet above, and the head below.
Hear Paul saying: But with me it is a very small thing that I should be
judged by you, or by a human day; and again, You, why do you judge your
brother? If one must not judge the brother, much more the teacher. For
if God commanded this, you do well, and you sin if you do not do it;
but if the contrary, do not dare, nor attempt beyond the marks. Against
Aaron after the making of the calf Korah and Dathan and Abiram rose
up; what then? were they not destroyed? Let each care for his own
things. For if he has a distorted doctrine, even if he is an angel, do
not obey; but if he teaches right things, do not attend to the life,
but to the words. You have Paul both through works and through words
training you toward what is needful. But he does not give to the poor,
he says, nor does he administer well. From where is this clear to you?
Before you learn, do not blame; fear the accounts. Many things are
judged from suspicion. Imitate your Master; hear him saying: I will go
down and see whether they are completed according to their cry; and if
not, that I may know.
But if you have learned and examined and seen, wait for the judge; do
not snatch beforehand the rank of Christ; it is his to examine these
things, not yours; you are a last slave, not a master; you are a sheep;
do not then be a busybody about the shepherd, lest you also give
accounts for the things of which you accuse him. And how does he tell
me, he says, himself not doing? He does not tell you; if you obey him,
you have no reward; Christ exhorts you these things. And what do I
say? one must not even obey Paul, if he says something of his own, if
something human, but the apostle who =62.611= has Christ speaking in
himself. Let us not judge one another's things, but each his own;
examine your life. But that man ought to be better than I, he says.
Why? Because he is a priest. And what more does he not have than you?
not the toils? not the dangers? not the anguish? not the hardship?
Having these things then, how is he not better than you? But if he is
not better, ought you then, tell me, to destroy yourself? These words
are of madness. For from where, that he is not better than you? If he
steals, he says, and robs temples. From where do you know, O man? why
do you push yourself down cliffs? And you, if someone says that so and

not wishing to take on a superfluous danger; but here you not only do

every idle word that men shall speak, they shall give account of it in
the day of judgment. And altogether, do you think yourself better than

That I am not as this temple robber, and do you not empty all? I am
forced to say these things and to go through this word, not because I
care so much for those men, but because I fear for you, lest you empty
your virtue through this boasting, through this condemnation. For hear
Paul exhorting and saying: But let each prove his own work, and then he
will have the boast toward himself alone, and not toward the other.
4. Tell me, if you come to a surgery having a wound, not leaving off
to put on a drug and to heal the sore, do you busy yourself about the
physician, whether he has a sore or does not have one? and even if he
has, do you care? or because that man has one, you do not heal your
own, and you say, He ought to be healthy, being a physician; since he
is not healthy, being a physician, I too leave my sore incurable? for
if the priest is bad, will there be consolation for the one under rule?
By no means, but that man will give the appointed penalty; and you too
will give the due and fitting one; for the teacher henceforth fills a
rank only. For they shall all be taught of God, he says, and they shall
not say, Know the Lord, because all shall know me from their small to
their great. For whose sake then, he says, does he sit in front; for
whose sake then is he set =62.612= over? Let us not, I beg, speak
badly of the teachers, nor be exacting about these things, lest we
dispose ourselves badly; let us examine our own things, and we will
speak badly of no one. Let us reverence that day on which he
enlightened us. If someone has a father, even if he has ten thousand
dreadful things, he overshadows all. Do not glory, he says, in the
dishonor of your father; for it is not glory to you as a reproach. And
even if understanding fails, have pardon. But if in the case of bodily
fathers, much more in the case of spiritual fathers must these things
be said. Reverence that he ministers to you every day; he causes the
Scriptures to be read, for you he adorns the house, for you he
watches, for you he prays, for you he stands beseeching God, for you
he makes supplications, for you all his worship is. Reverence these
things, think these things, approach with all reverence. Tell me, is he
worthless? And what of that? for does the one who is not worthless
himself grant you the great goods? By no means; the whole is worked
according to your faith. Neither will the righteous profit you
anything, if you are not faithful, nor will the worthless harm you
anything, if you are faithful. God worked through oxen in the case of
the ark, when he wished to save the people.
For is the life of the priest, is the virtue, of so much contribution?
The things which God grants are not such as to be accomplished by
priestly virtue; the whole is of grace; it is his only to open the
mouth, but God works the whole; this man fills a symbol only. Think how
great the interval is between John and Jesus; for hear John saying: I
have need to be baptized by you, and, I am not sufficient to undo the
strap of the sandal. But nevertheless even with so great an interval,
the Spirit also came down, which John did not have. For from his
fullness, he says, we all
received. But nevertheless before he was baptized, it did not come
down; nor indeed did John make it come down. What then does this come
to be? That you may learn that the priest fills a symbol. No one of men
has stood so far from a man as John from Jesus; but nevertheless upon
him the Spirit came down, that you may learn that God works the whole,
that God does the whole. I wish to say something unexpected, but do not
marvel nor be thrown into tumult. What then is this? The offering is
the same, whether the first comer offers, or Paul, or Peter; it is the
same which Christ gave to the disciples, and which the priests now
make; this is in nothing less than that, because men do not sanctify
this either, but he himself who also sanctified that. For as the words
which God uttered are the same which the priest also now says; so also
the offering is the same, and the baptism which he gave. Thus the whole
is of the Faith. The spirit leaped at once upon Cornelius, since he
showed beforehand the things from himself, and brought in the faith.
And this then is a body, and that; but he who thinks this is less than
that does not know that Christ is present even now, and works even now.
Knowing these things then (for all these things have not been said to
us simply, but that we may correct your mind, and make you more secure
for what follows), show much guarding of the things said. But if we
always hear, and never do, there will be no =62.613= profit to us of
the things said. Let us attend there62.613 fore accurately, let us
attend to the things said with care, let us write them upon our
=62.614= mind, let us have them always engraved on the conscience and
let us continually send up glory to the Father and to the Son and to
the Holy Spirit.

HOMILY 10.
Hold a pattern of sound words which you heard from me in faith and
love which is in Christ Jesus; guard the good deposit through the Holy
Spirit who dwells in us. You know this, that all who are in Asia
turned away from me; of whom are Phygelus and Hermogenes. May the Lord
give mercy to the house of Onesiphorus, because he often refreshed me,
and was not ashamed of my chain, but having come to be in Rome he
sought me more earnestly, and found me. May the Lord grant him to find
mercy from the Lord in that day. And how many things he ministered in
Ephesus, you know better.

1. Not through letters only did he set down for the disciple the
things to be done, but already also through words. And this he made
clear in many places and in others, saying: Whether by word, or by
letter, as through us; and much more also here. Let us not then think
that the things concerning the teaching have been said deficiently; for
he also handed over many things to him unwritten; which then reminding
him of, he said: Hold a pattern of sound words which you heard from me.
And what is it that he says? As in the case of painters I stamped, he
says, an image for you of virtue and of all the things that seem good
to God, as having laid down a certain rule and archetype and bounds
into your soul. Hold these things then; and if you need to take counsel
about faith, or about love, or about chastening, take the examples from
there; you will not need to seek an image from others, all things lying
there. Guard the good deposit. How? Through the Holy Spirit who dwells
in us. For it is not of a human soul nor of power, having been
entrusted with so many things, to suffice for
the guarding. Why? Because the robbers are many, the darkness deep; the
devil still stands by and lies in wait; we do not know at what hour, at
what season he attacks. How then shall we suffice for the guarding, he
says? Through the Holy Spirit, that is, if we have the Spirit with us;
if we do not drive the grace into exile, it will be present. For if the
Lord does not build a house, the builders labored in vain; if the Lord
does not guard a city, the watchers kept vigil in vain. This is a wall
for us, this a fortress, this a refuge. If then it dwells, and itself
guards, what need of a charge?
That we may hold it, he says, that we may guard it, and not drive it
away through worthless deeds. Then he recounts the trials, not wishing
to cast down the disciple, but to raise him up, that if ever he too
should fall into these, he may not feel it as something strange, looking
to the teacher and remembering all the things that happened to him.
What then does he say? Since it was likely that he, having been seized,
would be left behind, and would enjoy no kindness, nor inclination, nor
help, but even be betrayed by the faithful themselves and by friends,
hear what he says: You know this, that all who are in Asia turned away
from me. It was likely =62.614= that there were many in Rome then from
the parts of Asia; but no one stood by me, he says, no one acknowledged
me, all were alienated. And see his philosophic soul. He said only the
thing that happened, he did not curse them; but the one who did this he
both praised, and he prays ten thousand goods for this man, but those
he did not curse, but what does he say? Of whom are Phygelus and
Hermogenes. May the Lord give mercy, he says, to the house of
Onesiphorus, because he often refreshed me, and was not ashamed of my
chain, but having come to be in Rome he sought me more earnestly, and
found me. See how everywhere he speaks of the shame, not the danger, so
that Timothy may not fear, although the matter was full of dangers; for
he then struck against Nero, having attached to himself one of those
devoted to him. But having come to be, he says, in Rome he not only did
not flee my meeting, but even sought me out, and found me. May the Lord
grant him to find mercy from the Lord in that day. And how many things
he ministered in Ephesus, you know better. Such must the faithful be,
hindered neither by fear, nor by threat, nor by shame, but working
together with one another, as in war to stand by and to help. For they
do not grant so much to those in danger as to themselves, making
themselves partners through the things done toward those men of the
crowns owed to those men. For example, one of those devoted to God has
come to be in affliction, suffering many dreadful things, and
contending with much endurance; you have not yet been drawn to this
contest; it is possible for you, if you should wish, even without
entering the stadium, to become a sharer of the crowns laid up for him,
by being present, by anointing, by urging, by raising up. And that this
is so, hear what he says writing elsewhere: Only you did well sharing
with me in the affliction; and again: When even in Thessalonica both
once and twice you sent to my need. And how did those absent share the
affliction with the one not present? How? Because you sent to me, he
says, the things for the need both once and twice.
And again speaking about Epaphroditus he says: That he drew near as
far as death, having gambled with his soul, that he might fill up your
lack of the ministry toward me. For as in the case of kings not those
who fight only, but also those who guard the gear share in honor, and
not simply, but they often even obtained the equal gifts, although they
did not even bloody their hands, nor set their weapons, nor even at all
see the array of the enemies; much more in the case of these
afflictions. For he who helps the athlete wasting with hunger, the one
present, the one exhorting with words, the one
providing all the other attendance, has the equal with the one
contending.
2. For do not think of Paul the athlete for me, the unconquerable and
unassailable, but some other of the many, who if he had not obtained
much consolation, much exhortation, would perhaps not have stood, would
not have contended. So that those outside the contests have become
causes of the victory for the one who has en =62.615= tered the
contests, and they would become sharers of the crowns lying upon the
victory. And what wonder, if one sharing with those still living is
deemed worthy of the same things as those contending? as then also with
those who have departed, those already laid down, those crowned, those
needing nothing, it is possible to share even after death; for hear
Paul saying: Sharing in the memorials of the saints. And how, he says,
is it possible to share? When you admire the man, when you work some of
the things for which that man was crowned, you have shared with him the
contests and the crowns clearly. May the Lord, he says, grant him to
find mercy from the Lord in that day. He had mercy on me, he says; then
he will have the recompense in that fearful and awful day, where there
is need of much mercy for us. May the Lord, he says, grant him to find
mercy from the Lord. Two Lords? By no means, but for us one Lord Christ
Jesus, and one God. Here those sick with the things of Marcion leap
upon the saying; but let them learn that this is the custom of
Scripture, and it is possible in many places to find Scripture using
this habit, as when it says: The Lord said to my Lord; and again, I
said to the Lord, You are my Lord; and elsewhere: The Lord rained from
the Lord. And this is indicative of persons of the same essence, not
divisive of nature; for not that we may think two essences differing
from one another, but that we may think two persons each being of the
same essence, he says this. And see how he also said: May the Lord
grant him. What is this? Not something else, but mercy. Since he
himself obtained mercy from Onesiphorus, he prays that he too obtain
this from God. And if Onesiphorus who threw himself beside dangers is
saved from mercy, much more we. For fearful are the accounts, fearful,
and needing much kindness, so as not to hear that awful word: Depart, I
do not know you, you who work lawlessness; so as not to hear again that
fearful one: Depart, you cursed, into the eternal fire, prepared for
the devil and his angels; so as not to hear, There is a chasm between
us and you; so as not to hear that one full of shuddering, Take him,
and cast him into the outer darkness; so as not to hear that one full
of much fear, Wicked and slothful servant.
For that school is exceedingly awful and fearful; although God is
gentle, although kindly. For he is called God of compassions, and God
of consolation, and good as no one is good, and kind and reasonable and
very merciful, and he does not wish the death of the sinner, as that he
turn and live. From where then, from where is that day full of so much
anguish? a river of fire is drawn before his face, books of our deeds
are opened, this day is as a burning oven, angels run about, and many
furnaces are set forth. How then is he kind? how merciful? how good?
Kind then even thus, and from these things especially the greatness of
his kindness is shown. For this reason he shakes so much fear upon us,
that even being crowded through these toward =62.616= the desire of
the kingdom we may at some time be raised up. And see for me how he
does not simply bear witness to Onesiphorus, but says: He often
refreshed me. As some athlete pressed by drought, by the afflictions,
he recovered, he says, and strengthened. And how many things he
ministered in Ephesus, you know better. Not only in Ephesus, he says,
but also here. For such must the one who is sober and awakened be, not
once to work nor a second time nor a third, but through the whole of
life. For as our body, having been fed once, does not have the
substance of the whole life, but needs also the daily food; so then
here too in the case of piety we need each day the help from the works.
For there is need of much mercy for us; for because of our sins even
the kind God does all those things, needing none of them, but doing all
things through us. For this reason he told out and related all things
to us, and not only related, but also confirms through works. For he
was trustworthy even from words only; but lest someone think the words
to be of exaggeration, or of threat only, he also adds the full
assurance from the works. How? Both privately and in common bringing on
the punishments. And that you may learn through the things themselves,
at one time he punished Pharaoh, at another he brought on a flood of
the waters and that total destruction, at another that of fire. And now
too we see many of the wicked being punished, and paying a penalty;
which things are examples of Gehenna.
3. For that we may not sleep, nor be remiss, nor forget the words,
through the works he reminds us and raises us up, showing here
judgments, courts, accounts. Then among men is there so much care of
the just, but with God who also legislated these things is there no
account of these? and how would these things have faith? For both in a
house and in a marketplace and everywhere it is possible to see courts.
For in a house the master judges slaves each day, and demands accounts
of them for the sins, and some things he punishes, and some he lets go.
In fields again the farmer and the wife are judged each day; in ships
the helmsman judges, in camps the general the soldiers; and one would
find many courts; in crafts the teacher to the disciple. And privately
then and in common all judge one another, and nowhere would one see
the account of the just neglected, but all giving accounts everywhere.
Then here it is so scattered both by city and by houses, and for each
one the search of the just, but there, where the right hand of God is
full of righteousness, and his righteousness is as the mountains of
God, there is there no account of the just? And how does God the judge
and just and strong and longsuffering bear it, and not demand the
punishments? Here you have the cause; he is longsuffering, he says, and
he is longsuffering drawing you to repentance; but if you remain, According
to your hardness and impenitent heart you store up wrath for yourself.
If then he is just, he renders according to desert, and does not
overlook those who suffer badly without punishment; for this is of one
who is just. If he is strong, even after =62.617= death he renders, and
in the resurrection; for this is of one who is strong. And if being
longsuffering he bears it, let us not be thrown into tumult, nor say,
Why does he not proceed here? For if this came to be, the human race
would have been snatched away beforehand, if each day he demanded
penalties of us for the things we have offended. For there is not,
there is not a day clean of sins, but either a great or a small thing
we strike against. So that we would not even have reached even a
twentieth year each, if his longsuffering and goodness were not much,
furnishing us much appointed time of repentance, so as to strip off the
things offended. Let each then entering with a right conscience into
the things done by him, and bringing all his life into the midst, look
to see whether he is not worthy of ten thousand punishments and
chastisements; and when he is indignant, why so and so doing ten
thousand worthless things does not pay a penalty, let him think of his
own things, and he will stop the indignation. For those things appear
great, since they come to be upon great and manifest things; but if he
should examine his own, he will perhaps find more. For to snatch and to
overreach is similar, whether upon gold, or
whether it comes to be upon silver; for from the same mind both are
begotten, and he who stole the small will not decline also to steal a
great thing; and if it is not present, this has not come to be from
that man, but from the chance of the matter. The poorer man who wrongs
a poor man, would not, if he were able, have overlooked even the one
who is rich, but the matter henceforth comes to be of weakness, not of
that man's choice. So and so rules, he says, and snatches the things of
those under rule. But do you not snatch? tell me. For do not tell me
that that man talents of gold, and you ten obols; since also in alms
some cast gold, and she two mites, and she brought nothing less than
those. Why? For the choice is judged, and not the gift. Then in the
case of alms you think it right that God so judge, and that you carry
off nothing less because of poverty, if you put down two mites, than
those who put down ten thousand talents of gold, but in the case of
overreaching not so? and how would it have reason? As that woman
putting down two mites, from the choice brought nothing =62.618= less,
so also you having stolen two mites are disposed nothing better than
those, but if one must even say something surprising, you overreach
more than they. For as if someone commits adultery with the king's
wife, or with the poor man's, or with the slave's, he is similarly an
adulterer, the sin not being judged by the difference of the persons,
but by the wickedness of the choice of the one who dared; so then here
too. And I would rather call an adulterer the one committing adultery
with the first comer, than the one with the queen herself; for there
the wealth and the bloom and many other things were the things drawing,
but here nothing; so that this man would rather be an adulterer. Again,
I would rather call that man a drunkard who has used worthless wine;
so also a greedy man the one who does not even despise the small
things. For the one snatching the many things would perhaps despise the
small; but the one who stole the small things, the great by no means;
so that this man is rather a thief. For he who does not despise silver,
how would he despise gold? So that when we accuse the rulers, let us
reckon our own things, and we will find rather ourselves both stealing
and overreaching more than those, if we judge these things not by the
deed, but by the choice, as then it is also just to judge. For tell me,
if someone should be brought having taken the poor man's things, and
another the rich man's, will not both pay a similar penalty? What then?
is the murderer not similarly a murderer, whether he kills a maimed and
poor man, or a rich and beautiful one? When then we say that so and so
snatched so and so's field, let us think of our own things, and thus we
will neither condemn the others, and we will admire God for the
longsuffering, and we will not be indignant that the judgment does not
come upon them, and we ourselves will be more sluggish toward the
working of evils. For when we see ourselves also liable to the same
things, we will no longer be similarly displeased, but we will stand
off from the sins, and we will attain the coming goods in Christ Jesus
our Lord, to whom with Father and Holy Spirit be glory, power, honor,
now and always and unto the ages of ages. Amen.

HOMILY 4.
You then, my child, be empowered in the grace which is in Christ
Jesus, and the things which you heard from me through many witnesses,
these commit to faithful men, who will be sufficient also to teach
others. You then suffer hardship, as a good soldier of Jesus Christ.
No one serving as a soldier is entangled in the affairs of life, that

HOMILY 7.
And know this, that in last days hard seasons will stand in; for
men will be lovers of self, lovers of money, boastful, proud,
blasphemers, disobedient to parents, ungrateful, unholy, without
treaty, without affection, slanderers, without self-control, untamed,
not lovers of good, betrayers, rash, puffed up, lovers of pleasure
rather than lovers of God, having a form of piety, but having denied
its power. And turn =62.636= away from these. For from these are those
who creep into the houses, and taking captive
little women heaped with sins, led by desires and various pleasures,
always learning, and never able to come unto knowledge of truth.
1. In the former epistle he said, that The spirit says expressly,
that in later seasons some will stand =62.637= off from the faith; and
again in another place he prophesies in the same epistle something such
going to be later. And here too again he does the same, saying: And
know this, that in later seasons hard seasons will stand in. And not
from the later only, but also from those that have arrived he shows
this; In which manner, he says, Jannes and Jambres rose against Moses.
And from reasonings again: And in a great house there are not only
golden and silver vessels. Why then does he do this? that Timothy may
not be thrown into tumult, nor any of us, when there are wicked men. If
at least they have come to be in the case of Moses, he says, and after
these they will be, it is nothing marvelous that they are also upon us.
In later seasons hard seasons will stand in. He does not say this
slandering the days nor the seasons, but the men who are then. For it
is our habit thus to say seasons wicked, and not wicked, from the
things that happen in them by the men.
And at once he sets the cause of all the evils, and the root and the
spring, and from where all the others are begotten, the contempt. He
who has been caught by this passion does not even look at his own
things. For when someone does not look at the things of the neighbor,
when he neglects that man's, how will he look at his own? For as the
one seeing the things of the neighbor will set his own well in that
man's; so the one despising that man's will also look over his own. For
if we are members of one another, the salvation of the neighbor is not
of that man only, but also of the remaining body; and the harm of the
neighbor does not stand as far as that man only, but also stretches
henceforth all with the pains. If we are a building, whatever a part
suffers, it also damages the remaining; and if it is firm, it is able
also to bear the remaining through. So then also in the Church, did you
look over the brother? you wronged yourself. How? Your member underwent
no small harm. For if the one not giving a share of money goes away
into Gehenna, the one seeing him in danger in the more necessary
things, and not stretching a hand, much more than that man will he
suffer this, by as much as the damage has also come to be in a greater;
For men will be, he says, lovers of self. This lover of self is the one
who especially does not love himself; but the lover of brother, this
one rather is the one who loves himself. From this love of money. For
the love which is broad and poured out everywhere the deadly and
niggardly thing of self-love contracts and gathers into a short space.
Lovers of money. From this boastfulness is begotten, from the
boastfulness the pride, from the pride blasphemy, from the blasphemy
madness and disobedience. For the one lifting himself against men,
easily also against God. Thus the sins also are begotten; often they
go up from below. The one reverent toward men, much more toward God;
the one reasonable toward fellow-slaves, much more toward the Master;
but the one who despises the fellow-slaves, going forward on the way
will also despise God himself.
Let us not then despise one another; for this is a bad teaching,
teaching us to despise God. And even in this itself we despise God,
when we despise one another, who commanded much eagerness to be shown
toward one another. Yet also otherwise, if you wish, I will make this
manifest from an example. Cain despised the brother, he despised at
once God also. How did he despise? See how insultingly he answers
toward God: Am I a keeper of my
brother? Esau again despised the =62.638= brother, and he despised God.
For this reason God said, that I loved Jacob, but I hated Esau.
Therefore Paul also says: Let there be no fornicator or profane, as
Esau. The brothers despised Joseph, they despised God also. The
Israelites despised Moses; for this reason they despised God also. Thus
the sons of Eli also despised the people, they despised God also. And
do you wish also to see from the contrary? Abraham spared the brother,
he also obeyed God; and it is clear from the obedience upon the son
Isaac, and from all the other virtue. Again Abel being reasonable
toward the brother, was also reverent toward God. Let us not then
despise one another, that we may not also learn to despise God; let us
honor one another, that we may also learn to honor God. The one bold
against men becomes bold also against God. And when love of money, and
love of self, and madness come together at the same, what henceforth
is left toward perfect destruction? the whole has been corrupted, and
much mire of sins flows in. Ungrateful, he says. For how will the lover
of money be grateful? to whom will he know a favor? To no one; he
thinks all enemies, wishing to take the things of all. For even if you
empty all the substance, he knows you no favor; he is indignant, that
you did not have many things, and you made him master of many. And even
if you make him master of the inhabited world, he knows you no favor;
for he thinks he has taken nothing. This desire is insatiable. For it
is a desire of a disease. And such are the desires of diseases.
2. And the one with a fever is never able to be filled, but always
in desire being drunk he is never filled, he always thirsts. Thus also
the one raging about money never knows how to fill the desire; and
never being filled, however many things you empty, he will not know you
a favor. For he will know it only to the one who has given as many as
he wishes; and this no one will be able to do; for the things he wishes
do not have a measure; then he will know a favor to no one. Nothing is
so ungrateful as a lover of money; nothing so without perception as a
lover of money; he is an enemy of the whole inhabited world; he is
indignant that there are men; for he wishes all things to be full of
desolations, so that he himself may have the things of all. And he
imagines many such things: If there came to be an earthquake in the
city, he says, then with all buried, I alone was left around, so as to
take the things of all if possible; if a plague came upon, and
destroyed all apart from the gold; if a drowning came to be, if an
inundation of sea; and he thinks of ten thousand such things; nothing
kind, but he prays that earthquake and thunderbolts and wars and
plagues, and all such things come to be. Tell me then, wretched and
miserable and more slavish than every slave, if all were gold, would
you not have been released by the gold, being destroyed by famine? if
an earthquake came to be, with the inhabited world swallowed, you too
would have been destroyed by this destructive desire; for with there
not being a man upon the earth, the things of life would not have been
put together for you. For let us set that all the men throughout the
inhabited world have vanished utterly, then their gold and silver have
somehow of themselves come around to you (for they imagine foolish and
impossible things), and the wealth of those, the gold, the silver, the
silken garments, all the gold-shot come of themselves into your hands;
what the gain? thus especially would death have overtaken you, with
bakers =62.639= not being, farmers not being, beasts feeding upon, demons
frightening your soul.
And now indeed many demons hold it; but then they would even have
led you into astonishment, and would have destroyed you at once. But I
did not wish, he says, but that there be both farmers and bakers. But
those too were going to spend. But I did not wish
them to spend. Thus this desire is insatiable. For what would be more
ridiculous than these? Did you see helplessness of facts? he wishes to
have many serving him, and he is pained that these share food, as
lessening his substance. What then, tell me? do you wish men to be of
stone? All these things are mockery, waves, and storm, and winter, and
a triple wave, and much tumult to the soul; he always hungers, he
always thirsts. Tell me then, shall we not pity this man nor weep? But
in the bodies such a thing is the most grievous chastisement, called
bulimia by the physicians, when someone being filled with many things
is not released from starving; and upon the soul shall we not weep this
same passion? For love of money is a bulimia of soul, which is filled
with many things, but never stands, but is always stretched down by the
desire. For if it were necessary to drink hellebore, or ten thousand
times to undergo something heavier than this, so as to be released from
this passion, should one not have received it eagerly? There is not a
multitude of money which is able to fill the insatiable belly. Shall we
then not be ashamed, if some have so raged about money, and we do not
even show the least part of the love about God, but God becomes more
dishonorable than silver to us? For over silver men endure both
sleeplessness and travel abroad and successive dangers and hate and
plots and all things; but we for God do not even bear to let out a bare
word, nor to take on hostility, but if it is necessary to help one of
those being worn down, shrinking from the hatreds of those in power and
the dangers, we betray the one who has been wronged; and having taken
power from God so as to help, we destroy it through not wishing to be
hated nor to be hostile; and at once the many put this forward, Love in
vain, saying, do not hate in vain. And is this to be hated in vain?
and what is better than this hate? For than the friendship through God
the hate through him is much better. For when we are loved through God,
we are debtors of the honor; but when we are hated, he himself is a
debtor to us of the reward. However much the lovers of money love gold,
they do not know a measure of love; but we even if we work some small
thing, think we have accomplished the whole. As those love gold, we do
not love God, not even the least part. Great is the accusation of those,
that they have so great a madness about gold; great is our condemnation
that we do not so rage about God, but as much honor as they allot to
the earth (for the mined gold is earth), so much we do not allot to the
Master of all.
3. Let us see their madness, and be ashamed. For what, if we too are
not burned around gold, yet we do not even need God earnestly? For here
men despise wife and children and substance and their very salvation,
and these things not knowing that they increase their own substance;
for often in the midst of the hopes having also let go the soul
=62.640= they went away having toiled in vain; but we knowing that we
will attain the thing desired, if we love him as one must love, not
even thus do we love, but we have chilled all things, the love toward
the neighbor, that toward God; from this that toward God, since also
that toward the neighbor. For it is not, it is not possible for a man
not knowing how to love to have something noble and a courageous mind.
For the under-base of all the goods is nothing else but friendship; On
this, he says, the law and the prophets hang. For as fire having
entered into matter is accustomed to cleanse through all things; so
also the heat of love wherever it should fall, takes away all things
and cuts through the things damaging the divine seed, and works the
earth clean toward
reception of the seeds. Where there is love, all the evils have been
taken away; there is not the love of money the root of the evils, there
is not love of money, there is not boastfulness; for what would someone
boast against the friend? Nothing then so makes humble as love. For we
even minister the things of household slaves to friends, and we are not
ashamed, but we even know them a favor of this slavery; we do not spare
money, nor even bodies often; for already someone has even run into
danger beyond for the one loved. There is not envy, nor evil-speaking,
wherever there is genuine friendship; for not only do we not slander
the friends, but we also stop the mouths of those who slander. All
things are calm, all things tame, not even a trace of fight and

law, he says, is love; nothing unpleasant is with it. How? For the sins

loved, but would even give his own things in addition to the one loved;
for we have a favor rather than if we should take from him.
You know these things as many as have had friends; I mean friends

one ought to love, if someone was bound to someone. And if some do not

although he had a necessity of envying. How? For from the facts he saw
the kingdom coming to him, and nevertheless he suffered nothing such;
he did not say: This is the one who will deprive me of the paternal
kingdom, but he helped prepare the rule for him, and he did not spare
the father because of the friend. But let no one think him a
father-striker; for he did not wrong the father, but he held the plots
and the injustices. He rather spared, than wronged; he did not let him
go out unto an unjust murder. He even wished to die often through him.
He did not accuse, but he even stopped the father's mouth; he did not
envy, but he even helped prepare; he did not give him a share of money
only, but also of salvation. What do I say of money? he even put
forward his soul for him. He was not ashamed of the one who begot
because of the friend, since the one attempted unjust mat =62.641=
ters, and the other was conscious of nothing such for himself. There
then =62.641= the friendship was with the just; this man then is
Jonathan. And let us see henceforth also David. There did not come to
be a season for him to render the exchanges; for that man was snatched
away beforehand from the rule of David, and before the one well treated
came into the kingdom the one doing well was destroyed. What then? in
the things in which it was possible and allowed, let us see how the
just man showed the friendship. Jonathan was good to me, he says; you
were wounded unto death for me. Then this only? for this too is not
small; yet he also snatched the son of that man, and the grandson,
often from dangers, remembering the father's favor; and he continued as
guarding and keeping a grandson of some son. Such a friendship I wish
all to have, both toward the living and toward those who have departed.
4. Let the women hear (for especially for this reason I spoke toward
those who have departed), those associating with second marriages, and
corrupting the bed of the one who has died, those who were content with
the former. Not forbidding the second marriage, nor saying it is
unbridled, do I say these things; for Paul does not let me, putting a
bridle on my mouth, and saying to the women, And if she also marries,
she has not sinned. But let us see also the next:
But she is more blessed, if she remains thus; this is much better than
that. Why? For many things. For if not to marry at all is better than
to marry, much more this is better than that. But some did not bear,
he says, the widowhood, but they fell around misfortunes; for they did
not know what widowhood is. This is not widowhood, not to associate
with second marriages, as then neither is this virginity to be
inexperienced of marriage; but what? As there the seemly and the
sitting well, so here the being isolated, the attending to the
petitions, the having been released from luxury and softness; For she
who lives softly, he says, living has died. If remaining a widow you
are going to have the same pomp, and the same fantasy, and the same
wrap, which you had with the husband living, it is better to marry; for
the mixing is not an evil, but the being a busybody. And you do not do
the thing which is not an evil, but the thing which is not indifferent,
but is under a charge, this you endure. For this reason they were
turned aside after Satan, since they did not know how to be widows
well. Do you wish to learn what a widow is, and what the dignity of the
widow? hear Paul saying: If she brought up children, if she lodged
strangers, if she washed feet of saints, if she sufficed those
afflicted, if she followed every good work. For when with the husband
having died you are wrapped in a great bulk of wealth, reasonably you
do not bear the widowhood. Transfer then this wealth into heaven, and
the burden of widowhood will be bearable for you. What then, if I have
children successors of the =62.642= paternal lot, she says? Educate
those too to despise money; transfer your own things, having allotted
them so much as suffices; educate those too to be better than money.
What then, if a multitude of slaves flows around, she says; if a swarm
of affairs, gold, silver? how shall I be sufficient toward this
guarding, needing a husband's protection? These are a pretext and a
pretense; and this is clear from many places. For if you do not desire
money, nor wish to make the things that are more, the burden is light
for you; much more burdensome than to guard is to throw around more. If
then you cut out the one, the throwing around, he says, and from the
things that are you furnish to those in need, God will shield his own
hand; and if truly standing in front of the inheritance of the orphans
you say these things, and have not acquired with love of money by
pretense of this, he who walks into the hearts knows how to set their
wealth in safety, he himself who commanded to bring up children. For it
is not, it is not possible for a house rooted by alms to suffer
something dreadful; but even if it suffers toward a season, it will end
unto good. This will rather be of a shield and a spear to the whole
house.
Hear what the devil says about Job: Did you not fence around his
inside and outside? Why? hear Job himself saying: I was an eye of the
blind, he says, a foot of the lame, a father of orphans. As the one not
splitting himself from the misfortunes of others will never even in his
own things suffer something among evils, when he learns in sharing the
pain; so the one not wishing to bear the grief from sympathy will know
all these things in his own. And as upon the body the hand, with the
foot rotting, if it does not sympathize and cleanse a wound and put on
a drug and wash off ichor, will suffer this in its own evils, and the
one not wishing to minister to another being outside the passion will
itself be liable to the passion; for from there the evil creeping will
come even upon this, and no longer will its account be about ministry,
but about healing and release; so then here too, the one not wishing to
suffer with others will himself be in pain. You fenced around, he says,
his inside and outside, and I do not dare to come upon. But he
suffered, he says, dreadful things. But those dreadful things became a
cause of many goods; the substance double, the reward greater, the
righteousness more, the crowns brilliant, the prizes bright, and the
spiritual and the things of life were increased. Did he lose children?
but he took, not the same, but others instead of those, and those too
he saved in the resurrection. If he took up the same, the things of the
number would have been lessened for him; but now having given others
instead of those, he will also present those in the resurrection. All
these things existed for him from his being ready about alms; which let
us also do, that we may attain the same goods, by grace and kindness of
our Lord Jesus Christ Amen.

HOMILY 8.

men will be lovers of self, lovers of money, boastful, proud,
blasphemers, disobedient to parents, ungrateful, unholy, without
treaty, without affection, slanderers, without self-control, untamed,

1. If someone is vexed now at the heretics, let him think, =62.642=
that even from the beginning such things have come to be, the devil

; He slept, he says, as a lion, and as a cub of a lion; who will raise

works is a form only without power; reasonably.
For as a beautiful and blooming body, when it does not have
strength, but is like those painted, so is right faith without works.
For let us set that there is some lover of money, a betrayer, rash; then
he believes rightly; what the gain if he has nothing of the things
fitting the Christian? when he does nothing of the things that
characterize piety, but out =62.644= runs the Greeks in ungodliness?
when he is upon damage of those present? when upon blasphemy of God?
when he slanders the doctrine through the works? And turn away from

And turn away from these? It is likely that even then there were some

these are, he says, those who creep into the houses, and taking captive

and never able to come unto knowledge of truth.
2. You see them using the deceit of the ancient one, the weapon with
which the devil used against Adam? Creeping into the houses, he says.
Did you see how he made the shameless clear through saying, Creeping?
the dishonorable, the deceit, the flattery? And taking captive little
women. So that the easily deceived is a little woman, and far from a
man. And to be deceived is of women, or rather not even of women, but
of little women. Heaped. See from where the being persuaded comes to be

confused. Led by various desires. He did not accuse the nature; for he
did not say women simply, but such women. What is, Various? Here he

desires, he says, that is, of money, of glory, of luxury, of

Always learning, and never able to come unto knowledge of truth. Why
does he say these things? Not pardoning them, but even exceedingly

For since they buried themselves with those desires and the sins,
their mind was hardened. And in which manner Jannes and Jambres stood
against Moses, so these also stand against the truth. Who are these
magi in the case of Moses? and how are their names nowhere brought in

against the truth, men corrupted in mind, unapproved about the faith;
but they will not advance unto more. For their mindlessness will be

to innovate and to lead astray, they will not stand of the error, but

snatch together, even if at the beginning they seem to trip some, but
they will quickly be easily caught. For that he says about this, hear
what he says: For their mindlessness will be manifest to all. From
where? From everywhere; As also that of those came to be. For even if
the things of error bloom first, they do not remain through unto the
end. For such are the things not beautiful by nature, but seeming to be
beautiful; they bloom toward a season, and are caught and destroyed.
But our =62.645= things are not such; and you are a witness of these;

followed the teaching. This about word. The conduct; this about life.
The purpose; this about eagerness and the stand of the soul. I did not
say these things, he says, and not do them, nor did I philosophize in

that I may be filled with joy, how much more with him having died? He

good contest, the course, he says, I have finished, I have kept the
faith. The contest, he says, the good. Then you too lay hold of it.
Where there is a chain, where bonds, where death, is this the good

throughout in brilliance, in glory, in honor. One must henceforth

You heard that to depart is better, and to be with Christ. =62.653=

something useful. Truly good is the contest, not simply delighting the

heaven. This course is cleaner than that of the sun, which Paul ran
upon the earth, than that which that one runs upon the heaven. And how
did he finish the course? He went around the whole inhabited world,
beginning from Galilee and from Arabia, and coming as far as the ends
of the earth; So that, he says, from Jerusalem and around as far as
Illyricum I have filled the gospel of Christ; and having run through
the whole inhabited world as some winged thing, or rather even more
vehement than a winged thing; for the winged thing simply ran through,
but this man not simply, but having the wing of the spirit, and cutting
through ten thousand hindrances, deaths, plots, misfortunes. So that he
was even quicker than a winged thing. If he were a winged thing simply,
he would even have been brought down and consumed; but since he was
lightened by the Spirit, he was carried over all the nets, as if a
winged thing having wings from fire. The faith, he says, I have kept.
For many were the things wishing to plunder him, not the friendships of
men only, but also threats and deaths and ten thousand others. But he
stood toward all, How? Being sober and watching. These then also are
sufficient toward consolation of the disciples, but he himself also
adds the prizes. And of what sort are these? Henceforth there is laid
up for me, he says, the crown of righteousness. Righteousness here
again he means the virtue as a whole. One must not then be in pain,
that I go away about to be crowned, the crown from Christ being set

pain and to fear, lest I fall aside, lest I be destroyed besides. Which
the Lord will render to me in that day, the just judge, and not only to
me, but also to all who have loved his appearing.

those rays. No one of those nodding down and burying himself into the
earth will be able to see the solar light; no one of those defiling
himself with the things of life will be able to see the sun of
righteousness; he appears to no one of those turning about in these.
Sober up a little, sober up from the depth, from the surge of life, if
you wish to see the sun, if you wish to obtain the appearing; if you
obtain this appearing of his, then you will see him with much boldness.
Philosophize now; let there not be with you a spirit of madness, that
it may not slap you vehemently and cast you down; let the heart not be
stoned, and darkness, that you may not dash the ship there; let there
not be some deceit; for the reefs work most grievous shipwrecks. Do not
feed beasts, I mean the passions; for those are more grievous than
beasts; do not take heart in the flowing things, that you may be able
to stand firmly. No one stands upon water, but upon a rock all stand
securely. The things of life are water; Waters came, he says, as far as
my soul, as a winter-torrent flowing by. The spiritual things are a
rock; For upon a rock, he says, you lifted my feet. The things of life
are slime and clay; let us drag ourselves up from these; for thus we
will be able to obtain the appearing of Christ. Whatever comes upon,
let us bear; sufficient in all things is the consolation to suffer
through Christ; let us say this divine charm, and the pain of the wound
will cease. And how is it possible to suffer through Christ, he says?
Someone slandered you simply not through Christ? if you bear it nobly,
if you give thanks, if you pray for that man, you do all these things
through Christ. If you curse, if you are impatient, if you attempt to
defend yourself, even if you are not able, it is not through Christ,
but you underwent damage, and you have been deprived of the fruit
through your choice. For it is in us both to gain from the dreadful
things, and to be harmed; this does not come to be from the nature of
the dreadful things, but from our choice. What sort of thing I say: Job
suffered so many things, he bore thankfully, he was justified, not
because he suffered, but because having suffered he bore thankfully.
Another having suffered the same things, or rather not even the same
(for there is no one who suffered such things, but much lesser),
blasphemed, was indignant, cursed the whole world, was displeased
toward God; this man was condemned and sentenced, not because he has
suffered, but because he blasphemed; and he blasphemed, not from the
necessity of the things that happened since if the necessity of the
things that happened did this, Job too ought to have blasphemed; and if
having suffered more grievous things he worked nothing such, these
things did not happen from this, but from the weakness of the choice.
We then need a strong soul, and nothing will appear grievous to us, as
with it being weak, nothing which is not grievous. According to our
disposition all things become both bearable and unbearable; let us
fortify this, and
we will bear all things easily. For even the tree, when it sends the
root according to depth, not even a vehement storm will be able to
shake it through; but if it is lying from the surface, and above the
surface, even if a small inclination of wind falls upon, it pulled it
up by the roots. Thus also upon us, if we nail our fleshes to the fear
of God, nothing will be able to shake aside; but if we simply set them
forth, even a small inclination made vanish =62.655= and destroyed.
Therefore I beg, to bear all things with much eagerness, and to
imitate the prophet who says, My soul was glued behind you. See what he
says, My soul was glued. He did not say simply, It came near, but, It
was glued. And again, My soul thirsted for you. He did not say simply,
It desired, that he may show the vehemence of the desire through these
words. And again, Nail from the =62.656= fear of you my fleshes. For he
wishes us always to be glued to him thus and to be bound together, so
as never to stand off from him. If we thus hold God, if we nail our
minds to him, if we thirst for his desire, even all things will be for
us which we wish, and we will attain the coming goods, in Christ Jesus
our Lord, to whom with Father and Holy Spirit be glory, power, honor,
now and always, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.

rather; and these in being snatched are lessened rather? Let us not
then sit down to poverty, but let us choose the wealth. The wealth of
God is that many are those who enjoy the kingdom. He who is rich, he
says, unto all, and upon all who call upon him. Increase his substance;
and you will increase, if you snatch, if you overreach, if you force.
There is truly need of force. Why then? Because the hinderers are many,
wives, children, cares, the worldly things, with these demons, and the
leader of the demons himself, the devil. There is then need of force,
need of endurance. The one who uses force is in toils. How? he endures
all things, and stands toward necessity. How? he almost attempts the
impossible. If then those who use force are such, and we do not even
attempt the possible, when will we attain? when will we enjoy the
things we are eager about? Violent men, he says, snatch the kingdom of
the heavens. There is need of force and of snatching; for it is not
simply =62.662= set forth, nor from what is ready. The one who snatches
is sober throughout, has been awakened, cares and is anxious, so as to
set upon the snatching in season. Do you not see in the wars, that the
one wishing to snatch keeps vigil through the whole night? through the
whole he is armed? If then those who snatch the things of life both
keep vigil a night, and are armed, how wishing to snatch the spiritual
things, which need more eagerness than these, do we even in day sleep
and snore, and always continue unarmed and naked? For the one turning
about in sins is unarmed and naked, as the one in righteousness is
armed. We do not fence ourselves around with alms, we do not prepare
the lamps kindled for ourselves, we do not
fence ourselves together with spiritual weapons we do not learn the way
that bears there, we are not sober, we have not been awakened; for this
reason we are able to snatch nothing.
If someone should wish to set upon a kingdom, does he not first set
under himself ten thousand deaths? not having fenced himself together,
not having practiced the warlike? does he not do all things for this,
and thus rush toward the setting upon? But we not thus, but sleeping we
wish to snatch; therefore for this reason we go away with empty hands.
Do you not see those who snatch, how they flee, how they run, how they
cut through all things? There is need of a course; for at once the
devil runs down upon you, and he commands those in front to hold you.
But if you are strong, if you have been awakened, you kicked the one,
you pushed away the other, you fled through all as a winged thing; and
if you go away henceforth from here, even if you pass over the
marketplace, and the much tumult, which is the life, and you come
toward the things raised of these, which is the coming age. For as in a
desolation, there is no tumult, no one troubling, no one holding. Were
you snatched up? there is need of a little toil after the snatching, so
as not to be taken away the thing taken; if we run, if we see nothing
of the things lying before the eyes, if we care nothing else, but so as
to run through those who hinder, we will be able to hold the things
snatched with safety. Did you snatch chastity? do not remain, flee,
become far from the devil; if he sees that he is no longer able to
overtake, he will not even pursue. Thus we too, when we no longer see
those who snatched something, as having given up, neither ourselves run
down, nor do we exhort others to hold, but we let them go away and
remain. Thus you too run vehemently at the beginning. When you become
far from the devil, he will not even attempt henceforth, but you will
be in safety, enjoying the unspeakable goods with fearlessness; of
which may we all attain, in Christ Jesus our Lord, with whom to the
Father together with the Holy Spirit be glory, power, honor and
worship, now and always, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
