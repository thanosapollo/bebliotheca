To Innocent, Bishop of Rome, John greeting in the Lord.
=52.535=
Our body is settled in one place, but the wing of love flies around
everywhere throughout the inhabited world. When we too, being separated
by so great a length of road, are near to your piety, and we keep
company with you each day, seeing with the eyes of that love the courage
of your soul, the genuineness of your disposition, the firmness, the
unshakenness, your abundant consolation that is lasting and abiding. For in
proportion as the things of the waves are raised more, and more reefs
and rocks spring up, and the storms are many, so much also does your
watchfulness increase; and neither so great a length of road, nor a
multitude of time, nor difficulty of affairs has prepared you to become
supine; but you remain imitating the best of pilots, who are then most
of all aroused when they see the waves crested, the sea being carried
to a greater height, a great roaring of the waters, deepest night in
the day. Wherefore we also know many thanks to you, and we desire even
to send you snowstorms of letters, conferring the greatest favor upon
ourselves. But since we have been deprived of this by the solitude of
the place; for not only of those arriving from there, but not even of
those spending their time in this inhabited world of ours, could anyone
easily hold converse with us, both because the place in which we are
confined lies far off and toward the very extremities, and because of
the robber-fear that walls off the whole road; we beseech you rather to
pity us for the long silence than to condemn us of indolence from this.
For that it was not through neglecting that we were silent, now having
laid hold after a long time of the most honored and beloved John the
presbyter, and Paul the deacon, we both write, and do not cease giving
thanks to you, because you have shown yourselves affectionate fathers
by the goodwill and zeal concerning us. And as for what pertains to
your piety, all things have received the fitting correction, and the
rubbish of evils and the scandals have been taken away, and the
Churches have enjoyed peace and a bright calm, and all things are borne
along with the stream, and laws that were despised =52.536= have been
avenged, and ordinances of the fathers that were transgressed. But
since in the actual deeds none of these things has come to pass, those
who dared the former things still contending with their former
transgressions, I pass over relating one by one all the things done by
them afterwards; for the narration exceeds the measure of history, not
of a letter only; but this I beseech of your unsleeping soul, even if
those who have filled all things with tumults are sick with impenitent
and incurable diseases, that those who have chosen to heal these things
may not lose heart, nor give up, having considered the greatness of the
achievement. For almost for the whole inhabited world the present
contest is set before you, for Churches brought down to the knee, for
peoples scattered, for clergy being warred against, for bishops being
driven into exile, for ordinances of the fathers that have been
transgressed. Wherefore we also beseech your diligence, both once and
twice and many times, that in proportion as the storm is greater, you
show so much the more zeal. For we expect that there will even be
something more toward correction. But if this should not come to pass,
yet you have the completed crown from the God who loves mankind, and
this will be no small consolation to those who are wronged, the
persistence of your love; since even us who are spending this third
year in exile, with famine, pestilence, wars, continual sieges,
unspeakable solitude, daily death, given over to Isaurian swords, it
is no ordinary thing that comforts and consoles: the lasting and
abiding quality of your disposition and of your boldness of speech, and
that we so luxuriate in your abundant and genuine love. This is our
wall, this our security, this an unruffled harbor, this a treasure of
countless good things, this gladness, and a ground of much pleasure.
And even if we should again be carried away to a more desolate place
than this, we shall go having this no small consolation of our
sufferings.
