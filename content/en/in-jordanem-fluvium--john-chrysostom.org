On the Jordan River.
=61.725=
Why, of all the rivers upon the earth, does Christ accomplish the
mystery of baptism at the river Jordan? It is necessary to recount the
nature of the river, so that from it you may learn the sequence of the
mystery. The Jordan, beyond all rivers, has two roots of springs; for
the Jordan does not come forth from a single spring. It has one spring
at Paneas, and the other near Paneas; and the one spring is called Jor,
and the other Dan; and coming forth little by little and being divided
from one another, the springs meet one another at a certain place, and
they make one river.
The ancients gave it a name according to the roots, Jordan, the river
born from Jor and from Dan, having at the beginnings its roots
divided, and at the end joined. Again, beyond all rivers this one does
not run into a living sea, and living water of a sea does not receive
it, but the Dead Sea receives it. This one alone among all rivers is
joined to dead and idle waters. And the Jordan is a type of our mortal
race, and how, hear. From the beginning our race has two roots, Adam
formed from earth, Eve come forth from a rib; and there is not one
place and one manner of constitution. For he was formed from earth,
having the earth as a spring; and she came to be from a rib, having
the rib as a root; another beginning of that one, and another of this
one. And they were for a time divided from one another according to
the manner of their fashioning; but they came together into marital
union, the one body from both was completed, and those being born were
no longer born either from a rib or from earth, but from intercourse.
For the two, he says, shall become one flesh.
This our formation, then, having run in the sequence of nature, had
death as its end. Our formation began from earth, and ended in death;
and this formation the Dead Sea received, the deep, the underworld,
the abyss of the dead. For what the Dead Sea is to living waters, that
Hades is to those who die. Therefore Paul says: Do not say in your
heart, Who shall descend into Hades? that is, to bring Christ up from
the dead. Around this Jordan, then, that comes forth from living
springs and ends in a Dead Sea, all the wonder-working of God was
accomplished, so that the living water might no longer run toward
death. Three times God cut this Jordan, so that its course might no
longer be into the Dead Sea, but that it might run back upon the
ancient living roots. It was cut in the time of Joshua the son of Nun;
it was cut also in the time of Elijah, when he cut it with the mantle;
it was cut by Elisha, when returning he cut it. First Joshua the son
of Nun cut the Jordan; and how he cut it, hear. He did not stop the
streams of the springs, but he turned the water backward, as the
psalmist testifies saying: What ails you, O sea, that you fled? and
you, O Jordan, that you turned backward? And the backward parts were
the springs. The Savior, then, checking our race that is running into
death, makes it no longer run into death, but return upon the ancient
living roots, not end in the Dead Sea. Mortal nature, then, is
restored to the ancient form.
Nor does even one appear who cut the Jordan without some symbol.
Joshua the son of Nun cut it having the ark. Elijah having a mantle.
Elisha, having again the


mantle of his teacher, cut it. For since mortal nature was not
sufficient by itself to stop the stream, Jesus enters first, bearing
the Master's name, and bringing with him the ark, the symbol of the
Master's body. And the Jordan appears neither to have honored Jesus,
that mere
=61.726= man, I mean the son of Nun, nor to have reverenced the Jews, nor
to have been ashamed before the priests. And that it might be shown
that it does not yield to the people, but reverences the ark, Joshua
says: Behold, the ark of him who is Lord of all the earth is passing
through the Jordan. And it shall be, when the feet of the priests who
bear the ark enter, the Jordan will fail, not reverencing the priests,
but the ark. And from where this is, hear. David asks it saying: What
ails you, O sea, that you fled? and you, O Jordan, that you turned
backward? To these things creation answers saying: At the presence of
the Lord the earth was shaken. And the face of the Lord was the form
of the ark.
For what the ark was to the law and the tablets and the jar and the
rod, that the Master's flesh was of the incomprehensible Godhead, as
bearing the Godhead in an ark. Concerning this holy ark the blessed
David said: Arise, O Lord, into your rest, you and the ark of your
holiness. And see what he does: that the Jordan was dried up, not
having lost its streams, but having stepped back into the ancient
roots. Joshua, he says, took up from the Jordan twelve great stones,
and set them beyond the river; and in their place he set other twelve
stones in the river. What does this mean, the taking away of twelve
stones, and the setting of other twelve in their place? Because, as I
said before, he was checking mortal nature; or rather he wishes to
show that he pulls up humanity from the depths, and leads it up into
heaven. For the stones that had never seen a ray of the sun, nor
received a sight of light, he spread out into light, and they were
pulled up from the deep onto land. And why did he take twelve stones
from there? Because through the twelve apostles he was going to pull
up humanity from mortal nature. Be it, then, that he took away the
stones that were below, so that he might show to the sun and to the
light and to the men after these things a clear sight of how they
crossed the Jordan; for he also indicated the reason: If your son asks
you, he says, saying: What are these stones? tell him that, We crossed
the Jordan on dry ground, God having hindered the water before our
face. But let it be, the stones that were brought up were set, that
they might appear to those after these things; those set opposite, why
were they set opposite? He took from the depths those that had been
sunk, and he gave to the deep again pledges of salvation. The stones
that were set are pledges, those that were taken are a surety.
And there is much contemplation concerning these things, both why
stones were set, and why stones were taken away. Christ both takes and
gives. He came into the world, and took up our body, having given us
his own power. Through the apostles he sets opposite in the river
twelve stones, a pledge of salvation, and an earnest of the things to
come. For he who has given someone something toward some matter
pledges it to him for the future. Since therefore this name was going
to be kept for him again, when he was going to be baptized by John, he
beforehand deposited the pledges of the manifestation, and set the
stones, those which he took from there, the patriarchs, whom he then
raised; and those which he set opposite, the apostles, whom he gave in
their place. For in the place of your fathers sons were born to you.
Thus he crossed the Jordan, and the Jordan becomes for Joshua the son
of Nun the beginning of the possession of the land. Until he passed
the Jordan, he did not inherit the promised land; until Christ passed
the Jordan, he did not begin to preach the kingdom. The beginning of
the possession of the land is the Jordan, the beginning of the
possession of the kingdom of the heavens is the Jordan. Therefore the
prophet


says: I will remember you, O Lord, from the land of Jordan. For
remembrance =61.727= is indelible. Joshua, having thus crossed, cut the
Jordan, having shown it the ark; but Elijah cut it with the mantle,
with a dead skin, and the living waters obeyed the deadness of the
mantle. Elijah cut the Jordan with the mantle; and thus he crossed, in
order to go up into the heavens. The blessed Elisha returned, having
taken the mantle of his teacher, as he asked him saying: Let the
spirit that is upon you be double upon me. He says to him, You have
asked a hard thing; yet if you see me being taken up, it shall be
yours. That is, to be counted worthy to have the operation of the Holy
Spirit double. Then he was taken up. The mantle was lifted, and it
fell upon the disciple; he took a pledge, and came =61.728= to the
river; he tried to cut the Jordan, and the water did not obey. Elisha,
seized with perplexity, says: Where is the God of Elijah, even he?
and after saying it, he struck the water a second time, and the Jordan
was divided. And why did the water not obey him at first? So that he
might not suppose that it obeyed because of his former dignity, which
he had; but rather that he called upon God and says: Where is the God
of Elijah, even he? And why did he say, Where is God? Did the prophet
not know that God is in heaven and on earth and everywhere? But
because Elijah said to him: If you see me being taken up, it shall be
so for you.
