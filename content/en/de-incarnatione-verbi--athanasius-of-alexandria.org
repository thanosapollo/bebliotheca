OF SAINT ATHANASIUS, DISCOURSE ON THE INCARNATION OF THE WORD AND HIS
MANIFESTATION TO US THROUGH THE BODY

1.1 Sufficiently in the things before these, taking up a few from many,
concerning the error of the nations about the idols and their superstition,
how from the beginning the invention of these came to be, that from wickedness
men devised for themselves the worship toward the idols; but also, by the
grace of God having indicated a few things also concerning the divinity of the
Word of the Father and his providence and power toward all things; and that
the good Father orders all things by this one, and all things are moved by him
and are made alive in him: come in sequence, blessed and truly Christ-loving
one, to the faith concerning piety, and let us also relate the things
concerning the incarnation of the Word, and let us make clear the things
concerning his divine manifestation toward us; which Jews slander, and Greeks
mock, but we worship; so that still more from the seeming cheapness of the
Word you may have greater and more abundant piety toward him.

1.2 For by as much as he is mocked among the unbelievers, by so much he
furnishes a greater witness concerning his divinity; both that those things
which men do not grasp as impossible, these he himself shows possible; and
those things which men mock as unseemly, these he himself by his own goodness
constructs as seemly; and those things which men, being sophistical, laugh at
as human, these he himself by his own power shows as divine, overthrowing the
fantasy of the idols by his supposed cheapness through the cross, and
persuading those who mock and disbelieve invisibly so as to recognize his
divinity and power.

1.3 But for the narration concerning these things there is need of the memory
of what has been said before; so that you may also be able to know the cause
of the manifestation in a body of so great and so mighty a paternal Word, and
may not think that the Savior has worn a body by a sequence of nature; but
that, being bodiless by nature, and existing as Word, nevertheless according
to the love of humanity and goodness of his own Father, for our salvation, he
has been manifested to us in a human body.

1.4 And it is fitting, as we make the narration concerning this, first to
speak concerning the creation of the wholes and the God who is Craftsman of
this, so that thus one might worthily also contemplate that the renewal of
this has come to be by the Word who according to the beginning crafted it; for
nothing contrary will appear, if through him by whom the Father crafted this,
in him he also worked the salvation of this.

2.1 The crafting of the world and the creation of all things many have taken
up in different ways, and as each wished, so also he defined. For some say
that all things have come to be of themselves and as it chanced, as the
Epicureans, who also mythologize against themselves that the providence of the
wholes is not, speaking outright contrary to the things that are clear and
appear.

2.2 For if according to them all things have come to be of themselves without
providence, all things ought to have come to be simply and be alike and not
different. For as upon one body all things ought to be sun or moon, and upon
men the whole ought to be a hand, or an eye, or a foot. But now it is not so;
we see one thing sun, another moon, another earth; and again upon human
bodies, one thing a foot, another a hand, another a head. And such an
arrangement makes known that they have not come to be of themselves, but shows
that a cause of these things goes before; from which it is also possible to
think the God who arranged and made all things.

2.3 And others, among whom is also the great Plato among the Greeks, relate
that God has made the wholes from a preexisting and ungenerated matter; for
God would not be able to make anything if the matter were not already laid
down; just as for the carpenter the wood must also lie ready beforehand, so
that he may also be able to work.

2.4 But they do not know, saying this, that they attach weakness to God; for
if he himself is not cause of the matter, but altogether makes the things that
are from an underlying matter, he is found weak, not being able without the
matter to work any of the things that have come to be; just as, to be sure, it
is also a weakness of the carpenter not to be able without the woods to work
any of the things needed. For even by hypothesis, if the matter were not, God
would not have worked anything. And how would he still be called maker and
craftsman, having gotten the making from another, I mean from the matter? And
he will be, if it is so, according to them a craftsman only and not a creator
into being, if indeed he works the underlying matter, but of the matter he
himself is not cause. For altogether he would not even be called creator, if
indeed he does not create the matter, from which also the things created have
come to be.

2.5 And those from the heresies fashion for themselves another craftsman of
all things besides the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, being greatly blind
even concerning the things they utter.

2.6 For when the Lord says toward the Jews: "Have you not read that from the
beginning he who created made them male and female? and he said: For this
cause a man will leave his father and his mother and will be joined to his
wife; and the two will be one flesh"; then signifying the one who created he
says: "What therefore God has yoked, let man not separate": how do these
introduce the creation as alien to the Father? And if according to John, who
took in all things and says "all things came to be through him, and without
him not even one thing came to be," how would there be another craftsman
besides the Father of Christ?

3.1 These things, then, these men mythologize. But the God-inspired teaching
and the faith according to Christ slanders the vain speech of these as
godlessness. For it knows that God has made the wholes into being neither of
themselves, because they are not without providence, nor from a preexisting
matter, because God is not weak; but from things that are not, and things that
exist nowhere in any way, through the Word, as he says through Moses: "In the
beginning God made the heaven and the earth"; and through the most profitable
book of the Shepherd: "First of all believe that there is one God, who created
and fitted all things, and made them from the not-being into being."

3.2 Which also Paul signifying says: "By faith we understand that the ages
have been fitted by a word of God, so that the things seen have not come to be
from things that appear."

3.3 For God is good, or rather he exists as fountain of goodness; and to a
good one envy would come to be about nothing; whence, having envied none the
being, he has made all things from things that are not through his own Word,
our Lord Jesus Christ; among whom, having had mercy before all the things upon
earth on the race of men, and having seen that it would not be sufficient
according to the account of its own coming-to-be to remain always, granting
them something more, he did not simply, as all the irrational animals upon
earth, create men, but according to his own image he made them, sharing with
them also of the power of his own Word, so that having certain shadows of the
Word and having become rational they might be able to remain in blessedness,
living the true and real life of the saints in paradise.

3.4 And knowing again the choice of men that is able to incline toward both,
he took thought beforehand and secured by a law and a place the grace given to
them. For having led them into his own paradise, he gave them a law; so that
if they should keep the grace and remain good, they might have the life in
paradise without pain and without grief and without care, besides also having
the promise of the incorruption in the heavens; but if they should transgress
and, having turned, become base, they might know themselves to undergo the
corruption in death according to nature, and no longer to live in paradise,
but outside of this remaining henceforth dying in the death and in the
corruption.

3.5 And this the divine scripture also signifies beforehand, saying from the
person of God: "From every tree that is in the paradise you will eat with
eating; but from the tree of knowing good and evil you will not eat from it;
but in the day you eat, you will die by death." And the "you will die by
death," what else would it be than not only to die, but also to remain in the
corruption of the death?

4.1 Perhaps you marvel why in the world, having proposed to speak concerning
the incarnation of the Word, we now relate concerning the beginning of men.
But this too is not alien to the aim of the narration.

4.2 For it is necessary, as we speak concerning the manifestation of the
Savior toward us, to speak also concerning the beginning of men, so that you
may know that our cause became for him a pretext of the descent, and our
transgression called forth the love of humanity of the Word, so that the Lord
both reached even to us and appeared among men.

4.3 For of his embodiment we have become the occasion, and for our salvation
he was humane and came to be and appeared in a human body.

4.4 Thus then God made the man, and wished him to remain in incorruption; but
men, having been careless and having turned away from the contemplation toward
God, and having calculated and devised wickedness for themselves, as was said
in the first things, had the condemnation of death threatened beforehand, and
henceforth they no longer remained as they had come to be; but as they
calculated they were being corrupted; and death was mastering them, reigning.
For the transgression of the commandment turned them back into that according
to nature, so that, just as they came to be not being, so also they might
reasonably undergo in time the corruption into not-being.

4.5 For if, having by nature the not-being at some time, they were called into
being by the presence and love of humanity of the Word, it was consistent that
men, emptied of the thought concerning God and turned away toward the things
that are not (for the evils are things that are not, but the good things are
things that are, since they have come to be from the God who is), be emptied
also of the being always. And this is remaining, having been dissolved, in the
death and the corruption.

4.6 For man is mortal according to nature, inasmuch as he has come to be from
things that are not. But through the likeness toward the one who is, which if
he had kept through the contemplation toward him, he would have blunted the
corruption according to nature, and would have remained incorrupt; as wisdom
says: "Attention to laws is confirmation of incorruption"; and being
incorrupt, he would live henceforth as God, as somewhere also the divine
scripture signifies this, saying: "I said you are gods, and sons of the Most
High all; but you die as men, and fall as one of the rulers."

5.1 For God not only made us from things that are not, but also granted us the
living according to God by the grace of the Word. But men, having turned away
from the eternal things, and having been turned by the counsel of the devil
into the things of corruption, have become for themselves causes of the
corruption in the death, being, as I said before, corruptible according to
nature, but by grace of the participation of the Word having escaped that
according to nature, if they had remained good.

5.2 For because of the Word who was with them, even the corruption according
to nature was not drawing near to them, as also wisdom says: "God created the
man upon incorruption, and an image of his own everlastingness; but by envy of
a devil death entered into the world"; and this having come to be, men were
dying, and the corruption henceforth was flourishing against them, and
prevailing more than that according to nature against the whole race, by as
much as it had also taken beforehand against them the threat of the divine
through the transgression of the commandment.

5.3 For also in the offenses men were not standing as far as defined bounds,
but stretching themselves little by little they have come henceforth even to
the measureless, having become from the beginning inventors of wickedness, and
having called forth death and the corruption against themselves; and later
having been turned aside into injustice and having surpassed every
lawlessness, and not standing at one evil, but devising all new things upon
new, they have become insatiable about the sinning.

5.4 For adulteries and thefts were everywhere, and the whole earth was full of
murders and plunderings. And there was no thought of law concerning corruption
and injustice; but all the evils were being practiced both one by one and
commonly among all. Cities were warring against cities, and nation was being
raised against nations; and the whole inhabited world was divided by factions
and battles, each one being contentious in the lawless acting.

5.5 And not far from these were even the things contrary to nature, but as the
Apostle, the martyr of Christ, said: "For even their females exchanged the
natural use into that contrary to nature; and likewise also the males, having
left the natural use of the female, were burned in their desire toward one
another, males in males working the unseemliness, and receiving in themselves
the recompense which was due of their error."

6.1 Because of these things, then, death having mastered more, and the
corruption remaining against men, the race of men was being corrupted, and the
rational man who had come to be according to image was disappearing; and the
work that had come to be by God was being destroyed besides.

6.2 For also death, as I said before, was henceforth strong against us by a
law; and it was not possible to flee the law, because this had been set by God
for the sake of the transgression; and what was coming to be was truly at once
out of place and unseemly.

6.3 For it was out of place that God, having spoken, should lie, so that, when
he had legislated that the man die by death if he should transgress the
commandment, after the transgression he should not die, but the word of this
should be loosed. For God was not true, if, when he had said that we die, the
man was not dying.

6.4 And it was unseemly again that the things once come to be as rational and
having partaken of his Word should be destroyed besides, and turn back again
into the not-being through the corruption.

6.5 For it was not worthy of the goodness of God that the things that had come
to be by him be corrupted, because of the deceit that had come to be for men
from the devil.

6.6 Besides, it was also among the most unseemly things that the craft of God
in men be made to disappear, either through their carelessness, or through the
deceit of the demons.

6.7 The rational ones then being corrupted and such works being destroyed
besides, what ought God to do, being good? To let the corruption be strong
against them, and death master them? And what was the need of their even
having come to be from the beginning? For they ought either not to have come
to be, or, having come to be, not to be neglected and perish.

6.8 For weakness rather and not goodness is known from the carelessness of
God, if, having made, he overlooks his own work being corrupted, more than if
he had not made the man according to the beginning.

6.9 For if he had not made, there was not the one calculating the weakness;
but having made and created into being, it was most out of place that the
works perish, and especially before the sight of the one who had made them.
=6.10= Therefore it was necessary =6.10= not to let men be carried by the
corruption, because this is unseemly and unworthy of the goodness of God.

7.1 But just as it was necessary that this come to be, so also from the
opposite sides again the reasonable thing toward God stands against it, so
that God appear true in the legislation concerning the death; for it was out
of place that for the sake of our benefit and remaining the God who is Father
of the truth appear a liar.

7.2 What then ought to come to be also concerning this, or what ought God to
do? To demand repentance of men upon the transgression? For this someone might
say is worthy of God, saying that just as from the transgression they have
come into corruption, so from the repentance they might come to be again into
incorruption.

7.3 But the repentance was keeping neither the reasonable thing toward God;
for he was remaining again not true, if men were not being held in the death;
nor does the repentance call back from the things according to nature, but
only stops from the sins.

7.4 If then there were only an offense and not a following of corruption, the
repentance would have been well. But if, once the transgression had gone
before, men were being held into the corruption according to nature, and had
been stripped of the grace of that according to image, what else ought to come
to be? Or of whom was there need toward such a grace and calling-back, except
of the Word of God who also according to the beginning had made the wholes
from the not-being?

7.5 For it was his again both to bring the corruptible into incorruption, and
to preserve toward the Father the reasonable thing on behalf of all. For being
Word of the Father and being above all, consistently he alone was able also to
re-create the wholes and sufficient to suffer on behalf of all and to be
ambassador concerning all toward the Father.

8.1 For this reason, then, the bodiless and incorrupt and immaterial Word of
God arrives into our country, not at all being far off before. For no part of
the creation has been left empty of him; but he has filled all things through
all, being with his own Father. But he arrives, condescending by his love of
humanity and manifestation toward us.

8.2 And having seen the rational race perishing, and death reigning against
them by the corruption; and seeing also the threat of the transgression
holding fast the corruption against us; and that it was out of place that
before the law was fulfilled it be loosed; and seeing also the unseemly in
what had happened, that those things of which he himself was craftsman were
disappearing besides; and seeing also the exceeding wickedness of men, that
little by little they increased it even to the unbearable against themselves;
and seeing also the liability of all men toward death, having had mercy on our
race, and having pitied our weakness, and having condescended to our
corruption, and not having borne the mastery of the death, lest what had come
to be perish and his work toward men become idle for the Father, he takes to
himself a body, and this not alien from ours.

8.3 For he did not simply wish to come to be in a body, nor did he only wish
to appear; for he was able, if he only wished to appear, even through another
better thing to make his manifestation of God; but he takes ours, and this not
simply, but from an undefiled and unstained virgin inexperienced of a man,
clean and truly unmixed of the intercourse of men. For he himself, being
powerful and craftsman of the wholes, constructs in the virgin a temple for
himself, the body, and makes this his own as an instrument, being recognized
in it and dwelling in it.

8.4 And thus, having taken the like from our things, because all were liable
to the corruption of the death, delivering it to death instead of all, he was
offering it to the Father, and doing this humanely, so that, as all having
died in him, the law against the corruption of men might be loosed (inasmuch
as the authority had been fulfilled in the lordly body, and no longer having
place against the like men); and that, as men had turned back into corruption,
he might turn them again into the incorruption, and make them alive from the
death, by the making-his-own of the body, and by the grace of the
resurrection, making the death disappear from them as stubble from fire.

9.1 For the Word, having seen together that the corruption of men would not
otherwise be loosed except through the dying altogether, and it was not
possible that the Word die, being immortal and Son of the Father, for this
reason he takes to himself a body able to die, so that this, having partaken
of the Word over all, might become sufficient for the death instead of all,
and through the Word who dwelt in it might remain incorrupt, and henceforth
the corruption might cease from all by the grace of the resurrection. Whence
as a victim and a sacrifice free of every stain, offering the body which he
himself took for himself into death, he was at once making the death disappear
from all the like by the offering of the corresponding.

9.2 For the Word of God, being above all, reasonably, offering his own temple
and the bodily instrument as a ransom instead of all, was fulfilling what was
owed in the death; and as being with all through the like, the incorrupt Son
of God reasonably clothed all with incorruption in the promise concerning the
resurrection. For even this corruption in the death no longer has place
against men because of the Word who dwelt in these through the one body.

9.3 And just as, when a great king has entered into some great city and has
dwelt in one of the houses in it, such a city is altogether counted worthy of
much honor, and no longer does any enemy or robber, setting upon it, overturn
it, but it is counted worthy rather of all care because of the king who dwelt
in one house of it: so also it has come to be upon the king of all.

9.4 For when he came upon our country, and dwelt in one body of the like,
henceforth all the plotting of the enemies against men has ceased, and the
corruption of the death that of old was strong against them has disappeared.
For the race of men would have been destroyed besides, if the Master of all
and Savior, the Son of God, had not arrived toward the end of the death.

10.1 And this great work is truly fitting and especially to the goodness of
God. For if a king, having constructed a house or a city, and this being
warred upon by robbers from the carelessness of those dwelling in it, does not
overlook it altogether, but as his own work avenges and saves it around, not
looking to the carelessness of those dwelling in it, but to what is fitting to
himself: much more the Word of the all-good Father God did not overlook the
race of men that had come to be through him going down into corruption; but he
wiped out the death that had happened by the offering of his own body, and he
corrected their carelessness by his own teaching, setting right all the things
of men through his own power.

10.2 And these things one can also confirm from the theologian men of the
Savior himself, meeting with their writings, as they say: "For the love of
Christ constrains us, having judged this, that if one died on behalf of all,
then all died; and he died on behalf of all, so that we no longer live for
ourselves, but for him who died on behalf of us and rose" from the dead, our
Lord Jesus Christ; and again: "But we see Jesus, who has been made a little
lower than angels, crowned with glory and honor because of the suffering of
the death, so that by grace of God he might taste death on behalf of every
one."

10.3 Then he also signifies the cause of its being necessary that not another
but he himself, the God Word, become man, saying: "For it was fitting for him,
for whom are all things, and through whom are all things, in bringing many
sons into glory, to perfect the pioneer of their salvation through
sufferings." And this he signifies saying, that it was of no other to bring
men up from the corruption that had come to be, except of the Word of God who
also according to the beginning had made them.

10.4 And that because of the sacrifice concerning the like bodies he himself,
the Word, also took a body for himself, this too they signify saying: "Since
then the children have shared in blood and flesh, he also likewise partook of
the same, so that through the death he might bring to nothing the one who has
the mastery of the death, that is the devil, and might set these free, as many
as by fear of death were through all the living liable of slavery."

10.5 For by the sacrifice of his own body he both set an end to the law
against us, and renewed a beginning of life for us, having given a hope of the
resurrection; for since from men into men death mastered, for this reason
again through the incarnation of the God Word the dissolution of the death has
come to be and the resurrection of the life, as the Christ-bearing man says:
"For since through a man death, also through a man resurrection of dead. For
as in the Adam all die, so also in the Christ all will be made alive," and the
things following these. For no longer now do we die as being condemned, but as
being raised we wait for the common resurrection of all, "which in its own
times he will show," the God who also worked this and granted it.

10.6 This, then, is the first cause of the incarnation of the Savior. And one
might know that his good presence toward us has come to be reasonably also
from these things.

11.1 God, who has the mastery of all things, when he was making the race of
men through his own Word, having seen again the weakness of their nature, that
it would not be sufficient from itself to know the craftsman, nor altogether
to take a thought of God, because the one is ungenerated, and the others have
come to be from things that are not, and the one is bodiless, and men have
been molded somewhere below with a body, and altogether the deficiency of the
generated things toward the grasping and knowledge of the one who made them is
much; having had mercy again on the human race, inasmuch as he is good, he did
not leave them empty of his own knowledge, lest they have even the being
without profit.

11.2 For what profit is there for those who have been made, if they do not
know their own maker? Or how would they be rational, not knowing the Word of
the Father, in whom they have also come to be? For they were going to differ
in nothing even from irrationals, if they recognized nothing more than the
things around the earth. And why also was God making these, from whom he did
not wish to be known?

11.3 Whence, lest this come to be, being good he shares with them of his own
image, of our Lord Jesus Christ, and makes these according to his own image
and according to likeness; so that through such a grace, thinking the image, I
mean the Word of the Father, they might be able through him to take a thought
of the Father, and knowing the maker they might live the happy and truly
blessed life.

11.4 But men again, being beside themselves, having been careless even thus of
the grace given to them, so far turned away from God, and so far muddied their
own soul, as not only to forget the thought concerning God, but also to
fashion other things instead of other things for themselves. For they stamped
idols instead of the truth for themselves, and preferred the things that are
not to the God who is, serving the creation beside the one who created, and
what is worst, that they were even transferring the honor of God into woods
and into stones and into every matter and men, and doing more than these, as
has been said in the things before.

11.5 And they were so impious, that they were even worshipping demons
henceforth and proclaiming them gods, fulfilling their desires. For sacrifices
of irrational animals, and slaughters of men, as was said before, they were
completing into what was due to those, binding themselves down more to the
goads of those.

11.6 For this reason, at any rate, even magics were being taught among them,
and oracles according to place were deceiving men, and all were attributing
the causes of their coming-to-be and of their being to the stars and to all
the things according to heaven, calculating nothing more than the things that
appear.

11.7 And altogether all things were full of impiety and lawlessness, and God
alone, nor even his Word, was being recognized, although he had not hidden
himself unapparent to men, nor given them a simple knowledge concerning
himself, but had spread it out for them variously and through many things.

12.1 For the grace according to image was sufficient to make known the God
Word, and through him the Father; but God, knowing the weakness of men, took
thought beforehand also of their carelessness, so that if they should be
careless of recognizing God through themselves, they might have through the
works of the creation not to be ignorant of the craftsman.

12.2 And since the carelessness of men goes down little by little upon the
worse; God took thought beforehand again also of such a weakness of theirs,
having sent a law and prophets known to them, so that if they should also
hesitate to look up into the heaven and know the maker, they might have the
teaching from the things near. For men are able to learn nearer from men
concerning the better things.

12.3 It was possible, then, that they, having looked up into the greatness of
the heaven, and having contemplated the harmony of the creation, know the
leader of this, the Word of the Father, who by his own providence toward all
things makes the Father known to all, and for this reason moves the wholes, so
that through him all may know God.

12.4 Or if this was hesitant for them, it was possible for them at least to
meet with the saints, and through them to learn the God who is craftsman of
all things, the Father of Christ; and that the worship of the idols is
godlessness and full of all impiety.

12.5 And it was possible for them also, having known the law, to cease from
all lawlessness and to live the life according to virtue. For neither was the
law through Jews only, nor were the prophets being sent through them only, but
they were being sent toward Jews, and they were being persecuted from Jews;
but they were a holy teaching-place of the whole inhabited world of the
knowledge concerning God, and of the citizenship according to soul.

12.6 There being then such a goodness and love of humanity of God,
nevertheless men, being conquered by the pleasures at hand and by the
fantasies and deceits from demons, did not nod up toward the truth; but they
filled themselves with more evils and sins, so that they no longer seemed
rational, but were thought irrational from their ways.

13.1 Men having then thus been made irrational, and the demonic error thus
overshadowing the things everywhere and hiding the knowledge concerning the
true God, what ought God to do? To be silent about so great a thing, and to
let men be deceived by demons, and them not to know God?

13.2 And what was the need of the man even from the beginning having come to
be according to image of God? For he ought either simply to have come to be as
an irrational, or, having come to be rational, not to live the life of the
irrationals.

13.3 And what need altogether was there for him to take a thought concerning
God from the beginning? For if not even now is he worthy to take it, it ought
not even according to the beginning to have been given to him.

13.4 And what also is the profit for the God who made, or what glory would
there be for him, if the men who have come to be by him do not worship him,
but think others to be the ones who made them? For God is found to have
crafted these for others and not for himself.

13.5 Then a king, being a man, does not leave the countries built by him given
over to be enslaved to others, nor to flee for refuge toward others; but he
reminds them by letters, and often also writes to them through friends, and if
need also comes to be, he himself arrives, putting them to shame henceforth by
the presence; only so that they may not be enslaved to others, and his work
become idle.

13.6 Will not God much more spare his own creatures toward their not being
deceived from him, and serving the things that are not? Especially since such
an error becomes a cause of destruction and disappearance for them, and it was
not necessary that the things once having shared of the Image of God perish.

13.7 What then ought God to do? Or what ought to come to be, except to renew
again that according to image, so that through it men might be able again to
know him? And how would this have come to be, if the Image itself of God had
not arrived, of our Savior Jesus Christ? For through men it was not possible,
since they too have come to be according to image; but not even through
angels; for not even they themselves are images. Whence the Word of God
arrived through himself, so that as being Image of the Father he might be able
to re-create the man according to image.

13.8 And otherwise again it would not have come to be, if the death and the
corruption were not made to disappear.

13.9 Whence reasonably he took a mortal body, so that both the death in it
might be able henceforth to be made to disappear, and the men according to
image might be renewed again. Therefore there was not of another toward this
need, except of the Image of the Father.

14.1 For as, when the form that has been drawn in wood has disappeared besides
from the filths from outside, there is need again that he arrive whose the
form also is, so that the image may be able to be renewed in the same matter
(for because of the drawing of that one even the matter itself in which it has
also been drawn is not thrown out, but is stamped again in it):

14.2 according to this also the all-holy Son of the Father, being Image of the
Father, arrived upon our places, so that he might renew the man who had been
made according to him, and as lost might find him through the forgiveness of
the sins, as he himself also says in the Gospels: "I came to find and save the
lost." Whence also toward the Jews he was saying: "Unless someone is born
again," not signifying the birth from women as those were suspecting, but
making clear the soul being born again and being re-created in that according
to image.

14.3 And since also idol-madness and godlessness were holding the inhabited
world and the knowledge concerning God was hidden, of whom was it to teach the
inhabited world concerning Father? Of a man someone might say; but it was not
possible for men to go under all the world under the sun, neither being strong
by nature to run so much, nor being able to become trustworthy concerning
this, nor sufficient through themselves to stand against the deceit and
fantasy of the demons.

14.4 For all having been struck according to soul and disturbed by the demonic
deceit and the vanity of the idols, how was it possible to persuade a soul of
a man and a mind of men, where they are not even able to see them? And what
one does not see, how is he able to re-educate?

14.5 But perhaps someone might say the creation is sufficient; but if the
creation were sufficient, such great evils would not have come to be. For even
the creation was, and none the less men were rolling in the same error
concerning God.

14.6 Of whom then was there need again, except of the Word of God who also
sees soul and mind, who also moves the wholes in the creation, and through
them makes the Father known? For of the one who through his own providence and
ordering of the wholes teaches concerning the Father, it was his also to renew
the same teaching.

14.7 How then would this have come to be? Perhaps someone might say that it
was possible through the same things, so as again through the works of the
creation to show the things concerning him. But this was no longer safe. Not
at all; for men overlooked this before, and no longer have they had the eyes
above, but below.

14.8 Whence reasonably, wishing to benefit men, he sojourns as a man, taking
to himself a body like those, and from the things below (I mean through the
works of the body), so that those who did not wish to know him from his
providence and leadership toward the wholes might at least from the works
through the body itself know the Word of God in the body, and through him the
Father.

15.1 For as a good teacher, caring for his own disciples, those not able to be
benefited from the greater things, altogether condescending through the
cheaper things, educates them: so also the Word of God, as also Paul says:
"For since in the wisdom of God the world did not know God through the wisdom,
God was well pleased through the foolishness of the proclamation to save those
who believe."

15.2 For since men, having turned away from the contemplation toward God and
as in a depth having been sunk, having the eyes below, were seeking God in
coming-to-be and in the things perceived, stamping for themselves mortal men
and demons as gods: for this reason the humane and common Savior of all, the
Word of God, takes to himself a body, and as a man turns about among men, and
takes on the senses of all men, so that those who think God to be in bodily
things, from the things which the Lord works through the works of the body,
from these may think the truth, and through him may calculate the Father.

15.3 And being men and thinking all things human, upon whichever things they
cast their own senses, in these they were seeing themselves being taken on,
and from every side being taught the truth.

15.4 For whether they were struck toward the creation, yet they were seeing it
confessing Christ Lord; or whether their thought had been preoccupied toward
men, so as to think these gods, yet from the works of the Savior, as they
compared those, the Savior alone was appearing among men Son of God, there not
being among those such things as have come to be from the Word of God.

15.5 And if they had also been preoccupied toward demons, yet seeing them
being driven by the Lord, they were knowing this one alone to be the Word of
God, and the demons not to be gods.

15.6 And if even toward dead their mind had already been held, so as to
worship heroes, and the gods spoken of among poets; yet seeing the
resurrection of the Savior, they were confessing those to be false, and the
Lord alone true, the Word of the Father, who also is lord of the death.

15.7 For this reason he has also been born, and appeared as a man, and died,
and rose, blunting and overshadowing the things of men who have ever come to
be through his own works, so that wherever men may have been preoccupied, from
there he may lead them up, and teach his true Father, just as he himself also
says: "I came to save and find the lost."

16.1 For once the thought of men had fallen into things perceived, the Word
submitted himself to appear through a body, so that he might transfer men
toward himself as a man, and might bend their senses toward himself, and
henceforth, those seeing him as a man, through the works which he works, he
might persuade that he is not a man only, but also God and Word and Wisdom of
true God.

16.2 And this also Paul, wishing to signify, says: "Having been rooted and
founded in love, so that you may be fully able to grasp with all the saints
what is the breadth and length and height and depth, and to know the love of
Christ that exceeds knowledge; so that you may be filled into all the fullness
of God."

16.3 For everywhere the Word having spread himself out, both above and below
and into the depth and into the breadth; above into the creation, and below
into the incarnation, and into depth into Hades, and into breadth into the
world: all things have been filled of the knowledge concerning God.

16.4 And for this reason, not even arriving at once was he completing the
sacrifice on behalf of all, delivering the body to the death, and raising it,
making himself unapparent through this. But he was also establishing himself
apparent through this, remaining in it and completing such works and giving
signs, which were making him known no longer as a man but as God Word.

16.5 For the Savior was being humane in both through the incarnation, that he
was both making the death disappear from us, and was renewing us; and that,
being unapparent and unseen, through the works he was making apparent, and was
making known that he himself is the Word of the Father, the leader and king of
the all.

17.1 For he was not indeed enclosed in the body; nor was he in a body, and not
elsewhere. Nor was he moving that, and the wholes emptied of his working and
providence; but the most paradoxical, being Word, he was not held by anything;
but rather he himself was holding all things; and just as being in all the
creation, he is outside of the all according to essence, but he is in all by
his own powers, ordering all things, and spreading out his own providence into
all in all, and making each and all together alive, encompassing the wholes
and not being encompassed, but being whole in his own Father alone according
to all things:

17.2 Thus also being in the human body, and himself making it alive,
reasonably he was also making the wholes alive and was coming to be in all,
and was outside of the wholes. And being made known from the body through the
works, he was not unapparent also from the working of the wholes.

17.3 Now it is a work of a soul to contemplate even the things outside of its
own body by reasonings, yet not also to work from outside of its own body, or
to move the things far from this by presence. Never, at any rate, does a man,
thinking the things far off, already also move these and transfer them; nor if
someone should sit upon his own house and calculate the things in heaven, does
he already also move the sun, and turn the heaven around. But he sees them
being moved and having come to be, yet he does not happen to be able so as to
work them.

17.4 Not indeed such was the Word of God in the man; for he was not bound
together with the body, but rather he himself was mastering this, so that he
was both in this and was happening to be in all, and was outside of the things
that are, and was resting in the Father alone.

17.5 And this was the marvelous, that he both was living as a man, and as Word
was generating all things alive, and as Son was with the Father. Whence not
even as the Virgin was giving birth was he himself suffering, nor being in a
body was he being stained, but rather he was even hallowing the body.

17.6 For not even being in all things does he partake of all things, but
rather all things are generated alive and are nourished by him.

17.7 For if even the sun that has come to be by him and is seen by us, going
around in heaven, is not made filthy touching the bodies upon earth, nor is
made to disappear by darkness, but rather he himself both lights these and
cleanses them, much more the all-holy Word of God, who is also maker and lord
of the sun, being made known in a body was not being made filthy, but rather
being incorrupt, he was also making alive and cleansing the body that happened
to be mortal, "who did not do sin," it says, "nor was deceit found in his
mouth."

18.1 When therefore the theologians concerning this say that he eats and
drinks and is born, know that the body, as a body, was being born and was
being nourished with corresponding foods, but he himself, the God Word who was
with the body, ordering all things, even through the things which he was
working in the body was making himself known not as a man, but as God Word.
And these things are said concerning him, because even the body that eats and
is born and suffers was of no other, but of the Lord; and because, a man
having come to be, it was fitting that these also be said as concerning a man,
so that he might appear having a body in truth and not in fantasy.

18.2 But just as from these he was being known as present bodily, so from the
works which he was doing through the body, he was making himself known as Son
of God. Whence also toward the unbelieving Jews he was crying saying: "If I do
not do the works of my Father, do not believe me; but if I do, even if you do
not believe me, believe my works; so that you may know and be knowing, that
the Father is in me and I in the Father."

18.3 For as, being unseen, he is known from the works of the creation, so
having become a man, and not being seen in a body, he would be known from the
works that it is not a man but Power and Word of God who is working these.

18.4 For his commanding the demons, and those being driven away, is not a
human but a divine work. Or who, having seen him healing the diseases in which
the race of men lies subject, still thought him a man and not God? For he was
cleansing lepers, making lame walk, opening the hearing of deaf, making blind
look up, and simply driving away all diseases and every weakness from men,
from which it was possible even for the chance one to contemplate his
divinity. For who, having seen him giving back what was lacking to those whom
coming-to-be left wanting, and opening the eyes of the one blind from birth,
would not have thought the coming-to-be of men lying subject to him, and this
one to be Craftsman and Maker of this? For the one giving back what the man
did not have from coming-to-be would be altogether clear that this one is also
Lord of the coming-to-be of men.

18.5 For this reason also in the beginning, coming down toward us, he molds
the body for himself from a virgin, so that he may not furnish a small token
of his divinity to all, that the one who molded this is himself also Maker of
the others. For who, having seen a body coming forth without a man from a
virgin only, does not consider that the one appearing in this is also Maker
and Lord of the other bodies?

18.6 And who, having seen also the essence of waters being changed, and
transforming into wine, does not think that the one who did this is Lord and
Creator of the essence of the waters of the wholes? For this reason as Master
he was also setting upon the sea, and walking about as upon earth, furnishing
to those who see a token of his mastery upon all things. And feeding also from
few so great a multitude, and himself making plenty from things without
resource, so that from five loaves five thousand were filled, and another so
much was left, he was making known nothing other than that he is also the Lord
of the providence of the wholes.

19.1 And to do all these things seemed to the Savior to be well, so that since
men were ignorant of his providence in all things and did not contemplate his
divinity through the creation, they might at least look up from the works
through the body, and take a thought through him of the knowledge into the
Father, calculating from the things according to part his providence into the
wholes, as I said before.

19.2 For who, having seen his authority against demons, or who, having seen
the demons confessing him to be Lord of these, will still have the thought
doubtful, whether this one is the Son of God and the Wisdom and the Power?

19.3 For he has not even made the creation itself be silent, but the marvelous
thing, even in the death, or rather in the trophy itself against the death, I
mean the cross, all the creation was confessing that the one being made known
and suffering in the body is not simply a man, but Son of God and Savior of
all. For the sun turned away, and the earth was shaken, and the mountains were
split, all were cowering. And these things were showing the Christ on the
cross as God, and all the creation to be a slave of this one, and witnessing
by the fear the presence of the master.

19.4 Thus then the God Word through the works was making himself apparent to
men. And it would be consistent also to relate the end of his sojourn and
going-about in a body, and to say also of what sort the death of the body has
come to be; especially since this is the chief point of our faith, and simply
all men chatter about this; so that you may know that even from this no less
is the Christ known as God and Son of God.

20.1 The cause then of his bodily manifestation, as it was possible, in part,
and as we were able to think, we have said before, that it was of no other to
change the corruptible into incorruption, except of the Savior himself, who
also according to the beginning made the wholes from things that are not; and
it was of no other to re-create again that according to image for men, except
of the Image of the Father; and it was of no other to raise the mortal
immortal, except of the Self-Life that is of our Lord Jesus Christ; and it was
of no other to teach concerning Father, and to take down the worship of idols,
except of the Word who orders all things, and of the only true only-begotten
Son of the Father.

20.2 And since also what was owed from all had to be given back henceforth;
for it was owed altogether, as I said before, to die, for which especially he
also sojourned: for this reason after the proofs concerning his divinity from
the works, already henceforth he was also offering the sacrifice on behalf of
all, delivering his own temple into death instead of all, so that he might
make all unliable and free of the ancient transgression; and might show
himself also better than death, exhibiting his own body incorrupt as
firstfruits of the resurrection of the wholes.

20.3 And do not marvel if we often say the same things concerning the same.
For since we speak concerning the good pleasure of God, for this reason we
interpret the same mind through more things, lest we seem to leave something
aside, and a charge come to be as having spoken deficiently; for it is better
to undergo a blame of saying the same, than to leave aside any of the things
that ought to be written.

20.4 The body then, as itself also having the essence common with all (for it
was a human body), even if by a newer wonder it was put together from a virgin
only, nevertheless being mortal according to the sequence of the like it was
also dying; but by the setting-upon of the Word into it, it was no longer
being corrupted according to its own nature, but because of the Word of God
who dwelt in it, it was coming to be outside of corruption.

20.5 And both were happening to come to be in the same paradoxically: that
both the death of all was being fulfilled in the lordly body, and the death
and the corruption were being made to disappear because of the Word who was
with it. For there was need of a death, and a death on behalf of all had to
come to be, so that what was owed from all might come to be.

20.6 Whence, as I said before, the Word, since it was not possible for him to
die (for he was immortal), took to himself a body able to die, so that as his
own he might offer it instead of all, and as himself suffering on behalf of
all, through the setting-upon toward it, "he might bring to nothing the one
who has the mastery of the death, that is the devil; and might set these free,
as many as by fear of death were through all the living liable of slavery."

21.1 At any rate, the common Savior of all having died on behalf of us, no
longer now as of old according to the threat of the law do we who are faithful
in Christ die by death; for such a condemnation has ceased; but the corruption
ceasing and being made to disappear in the grace of the resurrection,
henceforth according to the mortal of the body we are dissolved only in the
time which God defined for each, so that we may be able to obtain a "better
resurrection."

21.2 For after the manner of the seeds being cast down in the earth, we do not
perish being dissolved, but as being sown we will be raised, the death having
been brought to nothing according to the grace of the Savior. For this reason,
at any rate, also the blessed Paul, having become surety of the resurrection
for all, says: "This corruptible must put on incorruption, and this mortal put
on immortality; and when this mortal puts on immortality, then will come to be
the word that has been written: Death was swallowed into victory. Where,
death, is your sting?"

21.3 Why then, someone might say, if it was necessary that he deliver the body
to death instead of all, did he not as a man privately put this aside, but
even went as far as being crucified? For it was more fitting that he put aside
the body honorably, than undergo such a death with insult.

21.4 See then again whether such an objection is not human; but what came to
be by the Savior is truly divine and worthy of his divinity for many reasons;
first, that the death that happens to men comes to them according to weakness
of their nature; for not being able to remain for long, they are dissolved by
time. For this reason also diseases happen to these, and having grown weak
they die. But the Lord is not weak, but is Power of God, and Word of God, and
Self-Life.

21.5 If then he had been somewhere privately, and according to the custom of
men putting aside the body on a bed, he would have been thought even himself
to have suffered this according to the weakness of the nature, and to have
nothing more than the other men. But since he was also Life, and Word of God,
and the death on behalf of all had to come to be, for this reason as being
Life and Power he was strengthening the body in himself;

21.6 and as the death owing to come to be, not from himself, but from others
he was taking the pretext of completing the sacrifice; since the Lord ought
not even to be sick, the one who heals the diseases of the others; but neither
again ought the body to grow weak, in which he also makes strong the
weaknesses of the others.

21.7 Why then did he not also prevent the death just as also the being sick?
Because he had the body because of this, and it was unseemly to prevent it,
lest the resurrection also be hindered; yet for a disease to go before the
death was again unseemly, lest a weakness of the one in the body be thought.
Did he not then hunger? Yes he hungered because of what is proper of the body,
but he was not corrupted by famine, because of the Lord who wore it. For this
reason even if he died because of the ransom on behalf of all, yet he did not
see corruption. For he rose whole; since the body was of no other, but of the
Life itself.

22.1 But it was necessary, someone might say, that the plotting of the Jews be
hidden, so that he might keep his own body immortal altogether. Let such a one
hear, then, that this too was unseemly for the Lord; for as it was not fitting
for the Word of God, being life, to give death to his own body from himself:
so it was not suitable even to flee the one given from others; but rather even
to pursue it toward destruction, whence reasonably he neither put aside the
body for himself, nor again fled the Jews plotting.

22.2 And such a thing was not showing a weakness of the Word, but rather was
making him known even as Savior and Life, that he was both waiting for the
death toward destruction, and was hastening to complete the death given on
behalf of the salvation of all.

22.3 And otherwise, the Savior came to complete not his own death but that of
men; whence not by his own death, for being Life he did not have it, was he
putting aside the body, but he was receiving that from men, so that this too,
having come upon in his own body, he might make disappear completely.

22.4 Then also from these one might reasonably see that the lordly body has
had such an end. The Lord was caring especially concerning the resurrection of
the body which he was about to do; for this was a trophy against the death, to
exhibit this to all, and to confirm all in the wiping-out of the corruption
that had come to be from him, and henceforth the incorruption of the bodies,
of which he has kept his own body incorrupt for all as a pledge and token of
the resurrection that will be upon all.

22.5 If then again the body had been sick, and before the sight of all the
Word had been dissolved from it, it was unseemly that the one who heals the
diseases of the others overlook his own instrument wasting in diseases. For
how would he have been believed to have driven away the weaknesses of others,
if his own temple was weak in him? For either as not being able to drive away
a disease he would have been laughed at, or being able, and not doing, he
would have been thought inhumane even toward the others.

23.1 And if also without some disease and without some pain, privately
somewhere and by himself in a corner, or in a desert place, or according to a
house, or wherever, he had hidden the body, and after these things again
having appeared suddenly, he was saying that he himself had been raised from
dead: he would have seemed to say myths among all, and he would have been
disbelieved much more even speaking concerning the resurrection, there not
being altogether the one witnessing concerning his death. And a death must go
before the resurrection, since there would not be a resurrection if a death
did not go before; whence if the death of the body had come to be hidden
somewhere, the death not appearing, nor having come to be upon witnesses,
unapparent and unwitnessed was also the resurrection of this;

23.2 Or why was he proclaiming the resurrection having risen, but making the
death come to be unapparently? Or why was he driving away the demons before
the sight of all, and making the one blind from birth look up, and changing
the water into wine, so that through them he might be believed Word of God;
but not exhibiting the mortal incorrupt before the sight of all, so that he
himself might be believed being the Life?

23.3 And how also did his disciples have boldness concerning the account of
the resurrection, not having to say that first he died? Or how would they have
been believed saying that first a death had come to be, then the resurrection,
if they did not have among those toward whom they were being bold these as
witnesses of the death? For if even thus, the death and the resurrection
having come to be before the sight of all, the Pharisees then did not wish to
believe, but even forced those who had seen the resurrection to deny this:
altogether if these things had come to be hiddenly, how many pretexts of
unbelief would they have devised?

23.4 And how then was the end of the death being shown, and the victory
against this, if he had not before the sight of all called it forth and proven
it dead, emptied henceforth by the incorruption of the body?

24.1 And the things that would also be said from others, these it is necessary
for us to put forward in the defenses. For perhaps someone might say even
this: If before the sight of all and with witness his death had to come to be,
so that the account of the resurrection also might be believed, he ought at
least to have devised for himself a glorious death, so as only to flee the
dishonor of the cross.

24.2 But if he had done even this, he was furnishing a suspicion against
himself, as not being able against every death, but only against the one
devised concerning him; and none the less again there was the pretext of the
unbelief concerning the resurrection. Whence not from himself, but from a
plotting, the death was coming to be for the body, so that the death which
they themselves bring upon the Savior, this he himself might make disappear.

24.3 And just as a noble wrestler, being great in understanding and in
manliness, does not himself choose the opponents for himself, lest he furnish
a suspicion of cowardice toward some; but he gives the authority to those who
watch, and especially even if they happen to be enemies, so that toward
whichever one they themselves match, this one he himself having thrown down,
he may be believed better than all: so also the Life of all, our Lord and
Savior the Christ, was not devising a death for himself for the body, lest he
appear as fearing another; but the one from others, and especially the one
from the enemies which those thought to be terrible and dishonorable and to be
fled, this he himself receiving on a cross was bearing; so that this too
having been dissolved, he himself being the Life might be believed, and the
mastery of the death might be brought to nothing completely.

24.4 A marvelous and paradoxical thing has come to be, at any rate; for the
death which they thought to bring on as dishonorable, this was a trophy
against the death itself; wherefore he did not even undergo the death of John,
the head being divided, nor as Isaiah was he sawn, so that even in the death
he might keep the body undivided and whole, and a pretext might not come to be
for those who wish to divide the Church.

25.1 And these things toward those outside heaping up reasonings for
themselves; but if also someone of those from us, not as contentious, but as
loving to learn, seeks why he underwent not otherwise but a cross, let this
one also hear that not otherwise than thus it was advantageous for us; and
this the Lord underwent well because of us.

25.2 For if he came himself to bear the curse that had come to be against us,
how else would he have become a curse if he did not receive the death that had
come to be upon a curse? And this is the cross. For thus it has also been
written: "Cursed is the one who hangs upon a wood."

25.3 Then, if the death of the Lord is a ransom of all, and by this death the
middle wall of the fence is loosed, and the calling of the nations comes to
be, how would he have called us, if he had not been crucified? For in the
cross only does someone die with hands stretched out. Wherefore this too it
was fitting that the Lord undergo, and stretch out the hands, so that with the
one he might draw the ancient people, and with the other those from the
nations, and join both in himself.

25.4 For this he himself has also said, signifying by what sort of death he
was about to ransom all: "When I am lifted up, I will draw all toward myself."

25.5 And again if the enemy of our race, the devil, having fallen from the
heaven, wanders about the air here below, and there having authority of the
demons with him as like in the disobedience, works fantasies through them for
those being deceived, and attempts to hinder those going up; and concerning
this the Apostle says: "According to the ruler of the authority of the air, of
the one now working in the sons of the disobedience," and the Lord came so
that he might throw down the devil, and cleanse the air, and make a way for us
the going-up into heavens, as the Apostle said, "through the veil, that is of
his flesh," and this had to come to be through the death: by what other death
would these have come to be, than by that which came to be in air, I mean the
cross? For only in the air does someone die, the one being completed on a
cross. Wherefore reasonably the Lord underwent this.

25.6 For thus having been lifted up, he was cleansing the air from both the
devilish and every plotting of the demons, saying: "I was contemplating Satan
as lightning having fallen," and making a way he was renewing the going-up
into heavens, saying again: "Lift up gates, you rulers, and be lifted,
everlasting gates." For it was not the Word himself who was needing an opening
of the gates, being Lord of all, nor was anything of the things made closed to
the maker, but we were the ones needing, whom he himself was bringing up
through his own body. For as he offered it to the death on behalf of all, so
through it again he made a way the going-up into heavens.

26.1 Fitting then and suitable has the death on the cross come to be on behalf
of us; and the cause of this appeared reasonable according to all things, and
has just reasonings, that not otherwise, but through the cross the salvation
of all had to come to be. For not even thus did he leave himself unapparent
even on the cross; but exceedingly he was making the creation witness the
presence of its own Craftsman, and not enduring that his own temple the body
remain for long, but only having shown it dead by the grappling of the death
toward it, at once on the third day he raised it, bringing trophies and
victories against the death, the incorruption and impassibility that had come
to be in the body.

26.2 For he was able even at once from the death to raise the body and show it
living again; but even this the Savior, having seen beforehand well, has not
done. For someone would have said that it had not died at all, or that the
death had not even touched it completely, if at once he had exhibited the
resurrection.

26.3 And perhaps also, the interval of the death and of the resurrection being
equal, the glory concerning the incorruption was becoming unclear. Whence, so
that the body might be shown dead, the Word also underwent one day in the
middle, and on the third day showed this incorrupt to all.

26.4 For the sake then of the death being shown in the body, on the third day
he raised this.

26.5 And so that, remaining for long and having been corrupted completely,
later having raised it he might not be disbelieved as not bringing it but
another body; for someone was going even because of the time to disbelieve the
thing appearing, and to forget the things that had come to be; for this reason
he did not endure more than the three days, nor for long did he drag those who
heard him concerning the resurrection;

26.6 but while their hearings still had the word ringing, and their eyes were
still expecting, and their thought was hanging, and those who had put to death
were still living upon earth and being upon places, and witnesses of the death
of the lordly body, he himself the Son of God in a third-day interval showed
the body that had come to be dead as immortal and incorrupt; and it was shown
to all, that not by a weakness of nature of the Word dwelling in it did the
body die, but so that the death might be made to disappear in it by the power
of the Savior.

27.1 For of the death having been dissolved, and the cross having come to be a
victory against it, and it no longer being strong henceforth, but being truly
dead, a token not small and a clear faith is this, that it is despised by all
the disciples of Christ, and all set upon it, and no longer fear this, but by
the sign of the cross and by the faith into Christ trample it as dead.

27.2 For of old, before the divine sojourn of the Savior came to be, all were
mourning those who die as being corrupted; but now, the Savior having raised
the body, no longer is the death fearful, but all who believe in the Christ
trample it as being nothing, and rather choose to die than to deny the faith
into Christ. For they know truly that dying they do not perish, but live, and
become incorrupt through the resurrection.

27.3 And that devil who of old was leaping wickedly upon the death, his
birth-pangs having been loosed, remained alone truly dead; and a proof of
this, that before men will believe in the Christ, they see the death as
fearful and they cower at it. But whenever they pass over into the faith and
teaching of that one, they despise the death so much, as even eagerly to rush
upon it and become martyrs of the resurrection that has come to be against it
from the Savior. For even still being infants in age they hasten to die, and
they practice against it by the exercises not only men, but also women. Thus
he has become weak, so that even women, who were formerly deceived by him, now
play with him as dead and slack.

27.4 For as, when a tyrant has been warred down by a genuine king and bound at
the feet and the hands, all henceforth who pass by mock him, striking and
tearing him down, no longer fearing his madness and his wildness, because of
the king who conquered: so also, the death having been conquered and posted up
by the Savior on the cross, and bound at the hands and the feet, all who pass
through in Christ trample it, and witnessing to the Christ they mock the
death, taunting it and saying the things written from above against it:
"Where, death, is your victory? Where, Hades, is your sting?"

28.1 Is this then a small proof of the weakness of the death? Or is it a small
demonstration of the victory that has come to be against it from the Savior,
when the children in Christ and young maidens overlook the life here and
practice to die?

28.2 For man according to nature is cowering at the death and the dissolution
of the body; but this is the most paradoxical, that having put on the faith of
the cross he despises even the things according to nature, and does not cower
at the death because of the Christ.

28.3 And just as, fire having according to nature the burning, if someone
should say there is something that does not cower at its burning, but even
rather shows it weak, such as is said the unstained among Indians; then the
one who does not believe the thing said, if he should wish to take a trial of
the thing said, altogether having put on the unburnable and having set upon
fire, is confirmed henceforth in the weakness against the fire;

28.4 or as if someone should wish to see the tyrant bound, altogether having
come into the country and rule of the one who conquered, he sees the one
fearful to others having become weak: so if someone is unbelieving, and still
after so many things, and after the so many martyrs who have come to be in
Christ, after the mockery against the death coming to be daily from those who
excel in Christ; nevertheless if he still has the thought doubtful concerning
the death having been brought to nothing and having had an end, he does well
marveling concerning so great a thing; only let him not become hard into
unbelief, nor shameless toward the things so clear.

28.5 But just as the one who has taken the unstained knows the untouchable of
the fire toward it, and the one wishing to see the tyrant bound comes into the
rule of the one who conquered: so also the one unbelieving concerning the
victory of the death, let him take the faith of the Christ, and let him come
into the teaching of this one; and he will see the weakness of the death, and
the victory against it; for many formerly unbelieving and mocking, later
having believed, so despised the death, as even themselves to become martyrs
of the Christ.

29.1 And if by the sign of the cross and by the faith into Christ the death is
trampled, it would be clear by truth judging, that there is no other but he
himself the Christ, who has exhibited trophies and victories against the
death, and has made that one grow weak.

29.2 And if formerly the death was strong, and for this reason was fearful,
but now after the sojourn of the Savior and the death of his body and the
resurrection it is despised, it would be apparent that by him the Christ who
went up upon the cross the death has been brought to nothing and has been
conquered.

29.3 For as whenever after night a sun comes to be, and every place around the
earth is lit by it, it is altogether not doubtful, that the sun that spread
the light everywhere, he himself is also the one who drove away the darkness
and lit all things: so, the death having been despised and trampled from when
the saving manifestation of the Savior in a body came to be and the end of the
cross, it would be obvious, that he himself is the Savior who also appeared in
a body, who brought the death to nothing, and exhibits trophies against it
daily in his own disciples.

29.4 For whenever someone sees men being weak by nature, leaping forth into
the death, and not cowering at its corruption, nor fearing the goings-down in
Hades, but with eager soul calling it forth, and not cowering at tortures, but
rather preferring the rush into the death because of the Christ to the life
here; or also if someone becomes a spectator of men and females and young
children rushing and leaping upon the death because of the piety into Christ,
who is so simple or who is so unbelieving, and who has so been maimed in the
thought, as not to think and calculate, that the Christ, into whom the men
witness, himself furnishes and gives to each the victory against the death,
making it grow weak in each of those who have his faith and who wear the sign
of the cross.

29.5 For also the one who sees the serpent being trampled, knowing especially
its former wildness, no longer doubts that it is dead and has grown weak
completely, unless he has turned away the thought, and does not even have the
senses of the body healthy. For who, even seeing a lion being played with by
children, is ignorant that this one has either become dead or has lost all its
own power?

29.6 As then it is possible to see with the eyes that these things are true,
so, the death being played with and despised by those who believe into Christ,
let no one any longer doubt, nor let anyone become unbelieving, that by Christ
the death has been brought to nothing, and its corruption has been dissolved
and has ceased.

30.1 Of the death then having been brought to nothing, and the lordly cross
being a trophy against it, the things said before are not a small proof. But
of the immortal resurrection of the body that has come to be henceforth from
the common Savior of all and truly Life, Christ, clearer than words is the
demonstration through the things that appear, for those who have the eye of
the thought healthy.

30.2 For if the death has been brought to nothing, as the account showed, and
through the Lord all trample it, much more he himself first trampled it with
his own body and brought it to nothing. And the death having been made dead by
him, what ought to come to be except that the body be raised, and this be
shown as a trophy against it? Or how would the death have appeared brought to
nothing, if the lordly body had not been raised? And if for someone this
demonstration concerning his resurrection is not sufficient, let the thing
said be confirmed at least from the things that have come to be in sight.

30.3 For if someone having become dead is able to work nothing, but as far as
the tomb is the grace for him, and he has ceased henceforth, and of the living
only are the deeds and the workings toward men, let the one who wishes see
then and become a judge from the things seen, confessing the truth.

30.4 For the Savior working so many things in men, and daily from every side,
both from those inhabiting Greece and the barbarian, persuading so great a
multitude invisibly to pass over into his own faith, and all obeying his
teaching: will someone still have the thought doubtful whether a resurrection
has come to be by the Savior, and the Christ lives, or rather he himself is
the Life?

30.5 And is it of a dead one to prick the thoughts of men, so that they deny
the paternal laws, and worship the teaching of Christ? Or how, if indeed he is
not working, for this is proper of a dead one, does he himself stop those who
work and live from the working, so that the adulterer no longer commits
adultery, and the man-slayer no longer murders, and the one doing injustice no
longer takes more, and the impious henceforth is pious? And how, if he has not
been raised, but is dead, does he himself drive away and pursue and throw down
the false-gods spoken of by the unbelievers as living and the demons being
worshipped?

30.6 For where Christ is named and the faith of this one, from there all
idolatry is taken down, and all deceit of demons is proven, and every demon
does not even bear the name; but even only having heard he goes off in flight.
And this is not the work of a dead one, but of one living and especially of
God.

30.7 Besides, it would also be ridiculous, to say that the demons being driven
by him and the idols being brought to nothing are living, but the one who
drives them away and by his own power makes them not even appear, and who is
confessed by all to be Son of God, to say that this one is dead.

31.1 And a great proof even against themselves do those who disbelieve the
resurrection put forward, if the Christ who is said by them to be dead, all
the demons and the gods worshipped by them do not drive; but rather the Christ
proves all dead.

31.2 For if it is true that the dead works nothing, and the Savior works so
many things daily, drawing into piety, persuading into virtue, teaching
concerning immortality, leading up into desire of the heavenly things,
revealing the knowledge concerning Father, breathing the power against the
death, showing himself to each, and taking down the godlessness of idols; and
of these things the gods and demons among the unbelievers are able to do
nothing, but rather by the presence of Christ they become dead, having idle
and empty the fantasy; and by the sign of the cross all magic ceases, and all
sorcery is brought to nothing, and all the idols are made desert and are left,
and every irrational pleasure ceases, and everyone looks up from earth into
heaven: whom would someone say is dead? The Christ who works so many things?
But it is not proper of a dead one to work; or the one not working at all, but
lying as soulless, which is proper of the demons and idols as being dead?

31.3 For the Son of God, being living and active, works daily, and works the
salvation of all. But the death is proven daily itself having grown weak, and
the idols and the demons rather happen to be dead, so that from this no one
still doubts concerning the resurrection of his body.

31.4 And the one unbelieving concerning the resurrection of the lordly body
seems to be ignorant of power of God Word and Wisdom. For if he altogether
took a body for himself, and made this his own according to the reasonable
sequence, as the account showed, what ought the Lord to do concerning this? Or
what sort of end ought to come to be of the body, once the Word had set upon
it? For not to die it was not able, inasmuch as it was mortal, and being
offered on behalf of all into the death; for which sake also the Savior
constructed it for himself. But to remain dead it was not possible, because it
had become a temple of Life. Whence it died as mortal; but it lived again
because of the life in it, and the works are a token of the resurrection.

32.1 And if because he is not seen, it is disbelieved even that he has been
raised, it is time for those who disbelieve also to deny that according to
nature. For it is proper of God not to be seen, but to be known from the
works, just as has also been said above.

32.2 If then the works are not, well do they disbelieve the one not appearing;
but if the works cry and show clearly, why do they willingly deny the life of
the resurrection thus apparently? For even if they have been maimed in the
thought, yet at least with the senses from outside it is possible to see the
undeniable power and divinity of the Christ.

32.3 Since even a blind one, if he does not see the sun, yet at least
perceiving the heat that has come to be by it, knows that a sun is over earth.
So also those who speak against, even if they do not yet believe, still being
blind concerning the truth, yet at least knowing the power from others who
believe, let them not deny the divinity of the Christ and the resurrection
that has come to be by him.

32.4 For it is clear that if the Christ is dead, he would not have been
driving the demons, and stripping the idols; for the demons would not have
obeyed a dead one. But if they are driven apparently by the naming of this
one, it would be clear that this one is not dead, especially since demons,
even seeing the things not seen by men, were able to know if the Christ is
dead, and not at all to obey him.

32.5 But now what the impious do not believe, the demons see, that he is God,
and for this reason they flee and fall before him, saying the things which
also when he was in a body they uttered: "We know you who you are; the holy
one of God"; and: "Let be, what to you and to us, Son of God? I beg you, do
not torment me."

32.6 Demons then confessing and the works witnessing daily, it would be
apparent, and let no one be shameless toward the truth, both that the Savior
has raised his own body, and that he is true Son of God, existing from him as
from Father his own Word and Wisdom and Power, who in times later for
salvation of all took a body, and taught the inhabited world concerning
Father, and brought the death to nothing, and granted the incorruption to all
through the promise of the resurrection, having raised his own body as
firstfruits of this, and having exhibited it as a trophy against the death and
its corruption by the sign of the cross.

33.1 And these things being so, and the demonstration concerning the
resurrection of the body and the victory that has come to be against the death
by the Savior being apparent, come, let us also prove the unbelief of the Jews
and the mockery of the Greeks.

33.2 For upon these perhaps Jews disbelieve, and Greeks laugh, tearing down
the unseemly of the cross and of the incarnation of the God Word; but against
both the account will not hesitate to go, especially having clear
demonstrations against them.

33.3 Jews disbelieving have the proof from the scriptures which they
themselves also read; above and below, and simply every God-inspired book
crying concerning these things, as even the words themselves are obvious. For
prophets from above were signifying beforehand concerning the wonder according
to the virgin and the birth that came to be from her, saying: "Behold the
virgin will have in womb and will bear a son, and they will call his name
Emmanuel, which is being interpreted, God with us."

33.4 And Moses the truly great, and believed true among them, concerning the
incarnation of the Savior, having judged the saying instead of a great thing
and having recognized it true, set it saying: "A star will rise from Jacob,
and a man from Israel, and he will crush the leaders of Moab." And again: "How
good are your houses, Jacob, your tents, Israel, as shaded glens, and as
paradises upon rivers, and as tents which the Lord pitched, as cedars beside
waters. A man will come out from his seed, and will be lord of many nations."
And again Isaiah: "Before the child knows to call father or mother, he will
take power of Damascus and the spoils of Samaria before a king of Assyrians."

33.5 That then a man will appear, is announced beforehand through these. And
that the one coming is Lord of all, they signify beforehand again saying:
"Behold the Lord sits upon a light cloud, and will come into Egypt, and the
handmade things of Egypt will be shaken." For from there also the Father calls
him back saying: "From Egypt I called my son."

34.1 Nor has his death been silenced; but it is signified even very clearly in
the divine scriptures. For even the cause of the death, that not through
himself, but on behalf of the immortality and salvation of all he undergoes,
and the plotting of Jews, and the insults coming to be toward him from them,
they did not fear to say, toward no one of them being unhearing of the things
coming to be and being deceived.

34.2 They say then: "A man being in a blow, and knowing to bear weakness,
because his face has been turned away; he was dishonored and was not
calculated. He himself bears our sins, and is in pain concerning us; and we
calculated him to be in toil, and in a blow, and in ill-treatment. But he was
wounded through our sins, and has been weakened through our lawlessnesses; the
discipline of our peace is upon him, by his bruise we were healed." Marvel at
the love of humanity of the Word, that because of us he is dishonored, so that
we may become honored. "For all," it says, "as sheep we were deceived; a man
was deceived in his way; and the Lord delivered him to our sins; and he
because of having been ill-treated does not open the mouth. As a sheep he was
led upon slaughter, and as a lamb voiceless before the one shearing him, so he
does not open his mouth; in his humiliation his judgment was taken away."

34.3 Then, lest someone suppose him a common man from the suffering, the
scripture takes the suspicions of men beforehand, and relates the power on
behalf of him, and the unlike toward us of the nature, saying: "And his
generation who will relate? Because his life is taken from the earth. From the
lawlessnesses of the people he was led into death. And I will give the wicked
instead of his burial, and the rich instead of his death; because he did not
do lawlessness, nor was deceit found in his mouth. And the Lord wishes to
cleanse him from the blow."

35.1 But perhaps having heard concerning the prophecy of the death, you seek
also to learn the things signified concerning the cross. For not even this has
been silenced; but it has been made clear even very clearly from the saints.

35.2 For Moses first announces beforehand with a great voice saying: "You will
see your life hanging opposite your eyes, and you will not believe."

35.3 And the prophets after him again witness concerning this saying: "But I
as a harmless lamb being led to be sacrificed, did not know; they calculated
an evil upon me saying: Come, and let us throw a wood into his bread, and let
us wipe him from earth of living."

35.4 And again: "They dug my hands and my feet; they numbered all my bones,
they divided my garments for themselves, and upon my clothing they cast a
lot."

35.5 And a death aloft, and coming to be on a wood, would not be another,
except the cross; and again in no death are hands and feet dug through, except
in the cross only.

35.6 And since at the sojourn of the Savior even all the nations from every
side began to recognize God, they did not even leave this unmarked; but there
is also a memory concerning these in the holy letters. "For there will be," it
says, "the root of Jesse, and the one rising to rule nations, upon him nations
will hope." These things then a few toward demonstration of the things that
have come to be.

35.7 And all scripture has been filled proving the unbelief of the Jews. For
who ever of the righteous related in the divine scriptures, and holy prophets,
and patriarchs, had the coming-to-be of the body from a virgin only? Or what
woman without a man has become sufficient toward constitution of men? Did not
Abel come to be from Adam, and Enoch from Jared, Noah from Lamech, and Abraham
from Terah, and Isaac from Abraham, and Jacob from Isaac? Did not Judah from
Jacob, and Moses and Aaron from Abraham? Did not Samuel come to be of Elkanah,
not David of Jesse, not Solomon of David, not Hezekiah of Ahaz, not Josiah of
Amos, not Isaiah of Amos, not Jeremiah of Hilkiah, not Ezekiel of Buzi? Did
not each have the father as origin of the coming-to-be? Who then is the one
who has come to be from a virgin only? Because it even very much concerned the
prophet concerning the signification of this one.

35.8 And of whose coming-to-be did a star run before in heavens, and signify
the one born to the inhabited world? For Moses being born was being hidden by
the parents; and David was not even heard by those from neighbors, where not
even the great Samuel was knowing him, but was inquiring whether there is
still a son to Jesse; and Abraham having become great henceforth was known to
those near. But of the coming-to-be of the Christ the witness was not a man,
but a star appearing in heaven, from where he was also coming down.

36.1 And who ever of the kings who have come to be "before being strong to
call father or mother" was king and has taken trophies against the enemies?
Did not David become king at thirty years, and Solomon having become young was
king? Did not Joash having become of seven years pass over upon the kingship,
and Josiah still lower, having come to be about seven years, took hold of the
rule? But nevertheless these, leading this age, were strong to call father or
mother.

36.2 Who then is the one reigning almost before coming-to-be, and stripping
the enemies? And who has become such a king in the Israel, and in the Judah,
let the Jews say having searched, upon whom all the nations have set the hope
and he has had peace? And did they not rather oppose them from every side?

36.3 For as long as Jerusalem was standing together, there was an unsparing
war for them, and all were fighting toward the Israel, Assyrians pressing, and
Egyptians pursuing, and Babylonians setting upon; and the marvelous thing,
that they even had Syrians from neighbors warring against them. Or was not
David warring those in Moab, and cutting out the Syrians, Josiah guarding
those near, and Hezekiah cowering at the boast of Sennacherib, and Amalek
campaigning against Moses, and the Amorites opposing Joshua the son of Nun,
those inhabiting Jericho arraying against? And altogether unsparing were the
things of friendship of the nations toward the Israel? Who then is the one
into whom the nations set the hope, it is worthy to see; for there must be
one, since it is also impossible that the prophet lie.

36.4 And of which of the holy prophets or of the patriarchs from above has the
death come to be on a cross on behalf of the salvation of all? Or who was
wounded and was taken away on behalf of the health of all? And which of the
righteous or of the kings went down into Egypt, and by the going-down of this
one the idols of the Egyptians have ceased? For Abraham went down, and again
the idolatry was against all. Moses has been born there, and none the less
there was there the worship of those deceived.

37.1 And who of those witnessed in the scripture was dug through at the hands
and the feet, or altogether has been hanged upon a wood, and has been
completed on a cross on behalf of the salvation of all? For Abraham dying on a
bed died; and Isaac and Jacob also themselves having lifted the feet upon a
bed died. Moses and Aaron on the mountain, David has ended in the house,
having suffered no plotting from the peoples. And if he was also sought by
Saul, yet he was being saved unharmed. Isaiah was sawn, but he has not been
hanged upon a wood; Jeremiah was insulted, but he did not die having been
condemned; Ezekiel was suffering, but not on behalf of the people, but
signifying the things that will be against the people.

37.2 Then these, even suffering, were men, such as also all according to the
likeness of the nature; but the one signified from the scriptures to suffer on
behalf of all is not simply a man, but is called life of all, even if like
according to the nature to men. "For you will see," it says, "your life
hanging opposite your eyes," and: "His generation who will relate?" For of all
the saints someone, having learned, is able to relate the generation from
above, who and from where each has come to be; but of the one who happens to
be life the divine words signify the generation as unrelatable.

37.3 Who then is he, concerning whom the divine scriptures say these things?
Or who is so great, as even the prophets to announce so many things beforehand
concerning him? But indeed no other is found in the scriptures, except the
common Savior of all, the Word of God, our Lord Jesus Christ. For this one is
the one who came forth from a virgin and appeared as a man upon earth and has
the generation according to flesh unrelatable. For there is not one who is
able to say the father of this one according to flesh, his body not being from
a man but from a virgin only.

37.4 As then of David and Moses and all the patriarchs someone is able to
genealogize the fathers, so no one is able to relate the generation according
to flesh of the Savior from a man. This one is the one who also made the star
signify the coming-to-be of the body. For it was necessary that the Word
coming down from heaven have the signification also from heaven; and it was
necessary that the king of the creation coming forth be known apparently by
all the inhabited world.

37.5 At any rate he was being born in Judea, and those from Persia were coming
to worship him. This one is the one who even before the bodily manifestation
took the victory against the opposing demons, and trophies against the
idolatry. At any rate all from every side those from the nations, denying by
oath the ancestral custom and the godlessness of idols, set the hope
henceforth toward the Christ, and enroll themselves to him, as it is possible
also with the eyes to see such a thing.

37.6 For not at another time has the godlessness of the Egyptians ceased,
except when the Lord of the all, as being carried upon a cloud, went down
there with the body, and brought to nothing the error of the idols, and
transferred all toward himself and through himself toward the Father.

37.7 This one is the one who was crucified upon witness of the sun and the
creation and those who brought the death upon him; and by the death of this
one the salvation has come to be for all, and all the creation has been
ransomed. This one is the life of all, and the one who as a sheep on behalf of
the salvation of all delivered his own body into death as a ransom, even if
Jews do not believe.

38.1 For if they do not think these sufficient, let them be persuaded at least
from other things from which they themselves again have oracles. For
concerning whom do the prophets say: "I became apparent to those not seeking
me, I was found by those not asking me; I said behold I am to the nation who
did not call my name; I spread out my hands toward a people disobeying and
speaking against"?

38.2 Who then is the one who became apparent? Someone might say toward Jews;
for if it is the prophet, let them say when he was being hidden, so that later
he might also appear; And what sort is this prophet who also became apparent
from unapparent things, and spread out the hands upon a cross? Of the
righteous then no one, but only the Word of God, who is bodiless by nature and
through us appeared with a body and suffered on behalf of us.

38.3 Or if not even this is sufficient for them, let them be put to shame at
least from other things, seeing the proof thus clear; for the scripture says:
"Be strong, slack hands and loosened knees; comfort, you small-souled in the
thought; be strong, do not fear; behold our God gives back judgment, he
himself will come and will save us; then will be opened eyes of blind, and
ears of deaf will hear; then the lame will leap as a deer, and clear will be a
tongue of stammerers."

38.4 What then are they able to say also concerning this, or how altogether do
they dare even to look against this? For the prophecy signifies God
sojourning, and the signs make known also the time of the presence; for both
the blind looking up, and lame walking, and deaf hearing, and the tongue of
stammerers being made clear, they say upon the divine presence that has come
to be. When then have such signs come to be in the Israel, or where has some
such thing come to be in the Judea, let them say.

38.5 A leper Naaman was cleansed, but a deaf one did not hear, nor did a lame
one walk. Elijah and Elisha raised a dead, but a blind from birth did not look
up. For even to raise a dead is truly great, but not such as the wonder from
the Savior. Only if the scripture has not silenced the thing concerning the
leper and the dead of the widow, altogether if it had also come to be that a
lame walk and a blind look up, the account would not have passed by making
these clear also. But since it has been silenced in the scriptures, it is
clear that these have not come to be before.

38.6 When then have these come to be, except when he himself the Word of God
has arrived in a body? And when has he arrived, except when lame walked, and
stammerers were made clear, and deaf heard, and blind from birth looked up?
For this reason also the Jews then watching were saying, as not having heard
these things coming to be at another time: "From the age it has not been
heard, that someone opened eyes of a blind who had been born; if this one were
not from God, he was not able to do anything."

39.1 But perhaps even they, not being able to fight toward the apparent
things, will not deny the things written, but will confirm that they expect
these and that the God Word has not yet arrived. For chattering this above and
below, they do not blush being shameless toward the things that appear.

39.2 But concerning this they will be proven even before all things rather,
not from us, but from the most wise Daniel signifying both the present time,
and the divine sojourn of the Savior, saying: "Seventy weeks have been cut
short upon your people, and upon the holy city, to complete sin, and to seal
sins, and to wipe out the injustices, and to propitiate the injustices, and to
bring everlasting righteousness, and to seal vision and prophet, and to anoint
a holy of holies; and you will know and understand from a going-out of a word
to answer, and to build Jerusalem, until Christ a leader."

39.3 Perhaps upon the other things they are able at least to find pretexts,
and to put off the things written into a future time. But what are they able
to say toward these or altogether to look against? Where both the Christ is
signified, and the one being anointed is announced to be not a man simply but
Holy of holies, and until his presence Jerusalem stands together, and
henceforth prophet and vision ceases in the Israel.

39.4 David was anointed of old, and Solomon, and Hezekiah, but again also
Jerusalem and the place was standing together, and prophets were prophesying,
Gad, and Asaph, and Nathan, and after them Isaiah, and Hosea, and Amos, and
others. Then even they themselves who were anointed were called holy men, and
not holy of holies.

39.5 But if they put forward the captivity, and because of it say that
Jerusalem is not, what would they also say concerning the prophets? For also
of old, the people going down into Babylon, Daniel and Jeremiah were there;
and Ezekiel and Haggai and Zechariah were prophesying.

40.1 Therefore Jews mythologize, and put off the present time that is present.
For when did a prophet or vision cease from the Israel, except now when the
Holy of the holies, Christ, has arrived? For a sign and a great token of the
presence of the Word of God is this, that no longer does either Jerusalem
stand, nor a prophet is raised, nor a vision is revealed to these, and very
reasonably.

40.2 For the one signified having come, what need still was there of the ones
signifying? And the Truth being present, what need still was there of the
shadow? For this reason they were also prophesying until the
Self-Righteousness should come and the one ransoming the sins of all. For this
reason also Jerusalem was standing together for so long, so that there they
might practice beforehand the types of the truth.

40.3 The Holy of the holies then being present, reasonably both vision and
prophecy have been sealed, and the kingship of Jerusalem has ceased. For for
so long were kings being anointed among them, until the Holy of the holies was
anointed; and Moses also prophesies that the kingship of the Jews stands until
him saying: "A ruler will not fail from Judah, and a leader from his thighs,
until the things laid up for him come, and he is expectation of nations."

40.4 Whence also the Savior himself was crying saying: "The law and the
prophets prophesied until John." If then there is now among Jews a king or a
prophet or a vision, well do they deny the Christ who has come. But if there
is neither a king nor a vision, but henceforth even all prophecy has been
sealed, and the city and the temple have been taken, why do they so much act
impiously and transgress, as to see the things that have come to be, and to
deny the Christ who has done these? And why also, seeing those from the
nations leaving the idols, and having the hope upon the God of Israel through
the Christ, do they deny the Christ who has come to be from the root of Jesse
according to flesh and who is king henceforth? For if the nations were
worshipping another god, but were not confessing the God of Abraham and Isaac
and Jacob and Moses, well would they again be making pretexts that God has not
come.

40.5 But if the God who gave the law to Moses and promised to Abraham, and
whose word Jews dishonored, this one the nations revere, why do they not know,
or rather why do they willingly overlook, that the Lord prophesied by the
scriptures has shone upon the inhabited world and has been manifested bodily
to it, as the scripture said: "The Lord God has been manifested to us," and
again: "He sent out his Word and healed them," and again: "Not an elder, not
an angel, but the Lord himself saved them."

40.6 And they suffer a like thing, as if someone, having been struck in the
thought, should see the earth being lit by the sun, and deny the sun that
lights this. For what more, having come, does the one expected among them have
to do? To call the nations? But they have already been called. But to make a
prophet, and a king, and a vision cease? This has already also come to be. To
prove the godlessness of idols? It has already been proven and condemned. But
to bring the death to nothing? It has already been brought to nothing.

40.7 What then has not come to be, which the Christ must do? Or what is left,
which has not been fulfilled, so that now Jews may rejoice and disbelieve? For
if indeed, as we also see, there is among them neither a king, nor a prophet,
nor Jerusalem, nor a sacrifice, nor a vision; but also all the earth has been
filled of the knowledge of God, and those from the nations leaving the
godlessness henceforth flee for refuge toward the God of Abraham through the
Word, our Lord Jesus Christ: it would be clear even to those who are very
shameless that the Christ has come, and that he himself has simply lit all
with his own light, and has taught concerning his own Father the true and
divine teaching.

40.8 Jews then one might reasonably prove from these and from the more things
from the divine scriptures.

41.1 But Greeks one would even very much marvel at, laughing at the things not
to be mocked, and maimed upon their own shame, which having set up in stones
and woods they do not see.

41.2 Only, our account not being at a loss in demonstrations, come, let us put
even these to shame from the reasonable things, especially from which we
ourselves also see. For what is out of place, or what is worthy of mockery
among us? Or altogether that we say that the Word has been manifested in a
body? But this even they themselves will confess together not to have come to
be out of place, if they become friends of the truth.

41.3 If then they altogether deny that there is a Word of God, they do
superfluously, mocking concerning what they do not know.

41.4 But if they confess that there is a Word of God, and this one Leader of
the all, and in him the Father has crafted the creation, and by the providence
of this one the wholes are lit and generated alive and are, and he is king
upon all things, so as to be known from the works of the providence and
through him the Father: see, I ask, whether they do not move the mockery
against themselves not knowing.

41.5 The world the philosophers of the Greeks say is a great body, and they
speak truly saying. For we see it and the parts of this falling under the
senses. If then in the world being a body the Word of God is, and he has set
upon in wholes and in all the things according to part of it, what is
marvelous or what out of place if we also say that he has set upon in a man?

41.6 For if it is altogether out of place for him to come to be in a body, it
would also be out of place for this one to have set upon in the all, and to
light and move all things by his own providence; for the whole also is a body.

41.7 But if it is fitting for this one to set upon the world and to be made
known in a whole, it would be fitting also for him to be manifested in a human
body, and for this to be lit and to work by him. For even the race of men is a
part of the all. And if the part is unseemly to become an instrument of him
toward the knowledge of the divinity, it would be most out of place also for
such a one to be made known through a whole of the world.

42.1 For just as, the whole body being worked and lit by the man, if someone
should say it is out of place even in the finger of the foot for the power of
the man to be, he would be thought mindless, because giving him to extend
through and work in the whole, he prevents him also to be in the part: so the
one who gives and believes the God Word of God to be in the all, and the all
to be lit and moved by him, would not think it out of place even for one human
body to be moved and lit by him.

42.2 And if because the human race is generated, and has come to be from
things that are not, for this reason they do not think it seemly that we say
the manifestation of the Savior in a man, it is time for them also to throw
him out of the creation; for this too has come to be from the not-being into
being through the Word.

42.3 And if, even the creation being generated, it is not out of place for the
Word to be in it, then neither is it out of place for him to be in a man. For
of whatever sort they would think concerning the whole, such things it is
necessary that they also consider concerning the part. For a part, as I said
before, of the whole even the man is.

42.4 Therefore it is altogether not unseemly that the Word be in a man, and
all things be lit and moved and live by him and in him, as also the writers
among them say, that "in him we live, and are moved, and are."

42.5 What henceforth worthy of mockery do we say, if in that in which the Word
is, this the Word has used toward manifestation as an instrument? For if he
was not in it, he would not even have been able to use this. And if we have
given back beforehand that this one is in the all and in the things according
to part, what is unbelievable if in those in which he is, in these he also
manifests himself?

42.6 For just as by his own powers, whole setting upon in each and all, and
ordering all things ungrudgingly, if he wished, through sun or moon or heaven
or earth or waters or fire, no one would have said out of place that he has
made himself use a voice and make known himself and his own Father; once he
holds all things together and happens to be with all and in the part itself
and showing himself unseen: so it would not be out of place, him ordering all
things and making the wholes alive, and having wished to make known through
men, if he has used an instrument of a body of a man toward manifestation of
truth and knowledge of the Father. For even the humanity happens to be a part
of the whole.

42.7 And just as the mind, being through a whole of the man, is signified from
a part of the body, I mean the tongue, and not indeed does someone say that
the essence of the mind is lessened because of this: so the Word, being
through all things, if he has used a human instrument, this would not appear
unseemly. For if, as I said before, it is unseemly to use a body as
instrument, it is unseemly also for him to be in the whole.

43.1 Why then, if they say, did he not appear through other better parts of
the creation, and has he not used a better instrument such as sun or moon or
stars or fire or ether, but a man only, let them know that the Lord did not
come to exhibit, but to heal and teach those who suffer.

43.2 For of one exhibiting it was only to be manifested and to strike those
who see; but of one healing and teaching it is, not simply to sojourn, but to
come to be upon benefit of those who need, and to be manifested as those who
need bear, lest by the exceeding of the need of those who suffer he disturb
those who need themselves, and the manifestation of the divine become
unprofitable for these.

43.3 Nothing then of the things in the creation was deceived into the thoughts
concerning God, except the man only. At any rate, not sun, not moon, not
heaven, not the stars, not water, not ether have changed the order, but
knowing their own craftsman and king Word they remain as they have come to be;
but men only, having turned away from the good, henceforth fashioned the
things that are not instead of the truth, and have set up the honor into God
and the knowledge concerning him to demons and men in stones.

43.4 Whence reasonably, since it was not worthy of the goodness of God to
overlook so great a thing, but also men were not able to know him even in the
whole, him managing and leading, he takes for himself a part of the whole as
instrument, the human body, and sets upon this, so that since they were not
able to know him in the whole, they might at least in the part not be ignorant
of him; and since they were not able to look up into his unseen power, they
might at least from the like things be able to calculate and contemplate him.

43.5 For being men, through the corresponding body and the divine works
through it, they will be able more quickly and nearer to know the Father of
this one, comparing that the things coming to be by him are not human, but
works of God.

43.6 And if it were out of place according to them for the Word to be made
known through the works of the body, again it would be out of place for this
one to be known from the works of the all. For just as being in the creation,
he partakes of nothing of the creation, but rather all things partake of his
power, so also using the body as instrument, he was sharing of none of the
things of the body, but rather he himself was even hallowing the body.

43.7 For if indeed even the Plato marvelled at among the Greeks says that the
one who generated the world, seeing it being storm-tossed and in danger of
sinking into the place of unlikeness, having sat upon the tillers of the Soul
helps, and corrects all the stumblings: what is said unbelievably among us,
if, the humanity being deceived, the Word sat upon this, and appeared as a
man, so that he might save it around being storm-tossed through his steering
and goodness?

44.1 But perhaps they will assent to these being ashamed, but will wish to
say, that God, wishing to educate and save men, ought to have done it by a nod
only, and that the Word of this one ought not to touch a body, just as indeed
he has also done of old, when he put them together from the not-being.

44.2 And toward this objection of theirs these things would reasonably be
said, that of old, nothing existing anywhere, there came to be need of a nod
and of a will only into the crafting of the all. But when the man has come to
be, and need demanded to heal not the things that are not but the things that
have come to be, it was consistent that the Physician and Savior arrive in the
things already come to be, so that he might also heal the things that are. And
he has become a man because of this, and has used the body as a human
instrument.

44.3 Since if this manner ought not to have come to be, how ought the Word,
wishing to use an instrument, to have arrived? Or from where ought he to have
taken this, except from the things already come to be and needing his divinity
through the like? For neither were the things that are not needing salvation,
so as also to be content with a command only, but the man already come to be
was being corrupted and being destroyed besides. Whence reasonably he has used
a human instrument well, and the Word spread himself out into all things.

44.4 Then this too must be known, that the corruption that had come to be was
not outside of the body, but had come upon it, and there was a necessity that
instead of the corruption life be knitted to it, so that just as in the body
the death has come to be, so in it the life also may come to be.

44.5 If then the death was outside of the body, the life of it also ought to
have come to be from outside. But if in the body the death was knitted, and as
being with it was mastering this, there is a necessity that the life also be
knitted to the body, so that the body, having been clothed in turn with the
life, may throw off the corruption. Besides, if the Word had also come to be
outside of the body, and not in it, the death was being defeated by him most
naturally, inasmuch as the death is not strong against the life, but none the
less the corruption that had come upon was remaining in the body.

44.6 For this reason reasonably the Savior put on a body, so that, the body
having been knitted to the life, it might no longer remain as mortal in the
death; but as having put on the immortality, henceforth having risen it might
remain immortal. For once having put on corruption it would not have risen, if
it had not put on the life; and again death would not appear by itself, if not
in the body; for this reason he put on a body, so that having found the death
in the body he might wipe it out. For how altogether would the Lord have been
shown life, if he had not made the mortal alive?

44.7 And just as stubble being corrupted by fire by nature, if someone
prevents the fire from the stubble, the stubble is not burned, but the stubble
remains altogether again stubble, the stubble suspecting the threat of the
fire; for by nature the fire is consuming of it; but if someone should clothe
the stubble with much unstained, which indeed is said to be counter-affect of
the fire, no longer does the stubble fear the fire, having the safety from the
clothing of the unburnable;

44.8 in the same manner also upon the body and upon the death someone might
say: that if by a command only the death had been prevented by him, none the
less again it was mortal and corruptible according to the account of the
bodies. But lest this come to be, it put on the bodiless Word of God; and thus
it no longer fears the death nor the corruption, having as clothing the life,
and the corruption being made to disappear in it.

45.1 Therefore consistently the Word of God took up a body, and has used a
human instrument, so that he might both make the body alive, and so that, just
as in the creation he is known through the works, so also he might work in a
man, and show himself everywhere, leaving nothing desert of his own divinity
and knowledge.

45.2 For again I say the same, taking up the things before, that the Savior
has done this, so that just as he fills all things being present from every
side, so he might also fill all things of the knowledge concerning him, as
also the divine scripture says: "The whole was filled of the knowing the
Lord."

45.3 For whether someone wishes to look up into the heaven, he sees the
ordering of this; or whether he is not able into the heaven, but looks up
toward men only, he sees through the works his incomparable power toward men,
and knows this one among men only God Word. Or whether someone has been turned
away in demons, and has been struck concerning this, he sees this one driving
these, and judges this one to be master of them; or whether he has been sunk
into the nature of waters, and thinks these to be God, as Egyptians revere the
water, he sees this being changed by him, and knows the Lord to be creator of
these.

45.4 And if someone has also gone down into Hades, and has been struck toward
the heroes who have gone down there as gods, yet he sees the resurrection of
this one that has come to be, and the victory against the death, and
calculates even in those that the Christ only is true Lord and God.

45.5 For the Lord touched all the parts of the creation, and freed all things
from all deceit and proved it, as Paul says: "Having stripped the rulers and
the authorities he triumphed in the cross," so that no longer might someone be
able to be deceived, but everywhere might find the true Word of God.

45.6 For thus henceforth the man being shut in from every side and everywhere,
that is in heaven, in Hades, in man, upon earth, seeing the divinity of the
Word spread out, is no longer deceived concerning God, but worships this one
only, and through him well knows the Father.

45.7 By these things then even Greeks will reasonably be put to shame by us
from the reasonable things; but if they do not think the accounts sufficient
toward their shame, let the things said be confirmed at least from the things
appearing before the sight of all.

46.1 When did men begin to leave the worship of the idols, except from when
the true God Word of God has come to be among men? And when have the oracles
among Greeks and everywhere ceased and been emptied, except when the Savior
has manifested himself as far as earth?

46.2 And when did the gods and heroes spoken of among poets begin to be
condemned, as being only mortal men, except from when the Lord worked the
trophy against the death, and has kept the body which he took incorrupt,
having raised it from the dead?

46.3 And when was the deceit and madness of demons despised, except when the
Power of God the Word, the Master of all and of these, having condescended
because of the weakness of men, appeared upon earth? And when did the art of
magic and the teaching-places begin to be trampled, except when the
manifestations of God of the Word came to be among men?

46.4 And altogether, when has the wisdom of the Greeks been made foolish,
except when the true Wisdom of God manifested herself upon earth? For of old
all the inhabited world and every place was being deceived by the worship of
the idols, and men were thinking nothing other than the idols gods. But now
according to all the inhabited world, men leave the superstition of the idols,
and flee for refuge upon the Christ, and worshipping him as God, through him
they also know the Father whom they did not know.

46.5 And the marvelous thing, there being different and countless objects of
reverence, and each place having its own idol, and the one called god among
them not being strong to go beyond the nearby place, so as even to persuade
those from neighbors to revere him, but scarcely even being worshipped in his
own (for no other was revering the god of the neighbor, but each was keeping
his own idol, thinking it to be lord of all), the Christ only among all is
worshipped one and everywhere the same; and what the weakness of the idols has
not been able to do, so as at least to persuade those inhabiting nearby, this
the Christ has done, having persuaded not only those nearby but even simply
all the inhabited world to revere one and the same Lord, and through him God
his own Father.

47.1 And of old the things everywhere were filled of the deceit of the
oracles, and the oracles in Delphi and Dodona and Boeotia and Lycia and Libya
and Egypt and Cabiri and the Pythia were being marvelled at in the fantasy by
men; but now from when Christ is announced everywhere, the madness of these
also has ceased, and there is no longer henceforth in them the one giving
oracles.

47.2 And of old demons were beating fantasies upon men, occupying beforehand
springs or rivers or woods or stones, and thus by the tricks they were
striking the mindless. But now the divine manifestation of the Word having
come to be, the fantasy of these has ceased. For the man using even only the
sign of the cross drives away the deceits of these.

47.3 And of old the gods spoken of among poets, Zeus and Kronos and Apollo and
heroes, men were thinking gods, and they were being deceived revering these;
but now the Savior having appeared among men, those have been known to be
mortal men, and the Christ only among men has been made known as God Word of
true God.

47.4 And what might someone say concerning the magic marvelled at among them?
That before the Word sojourned, this was strong and was working among
Egyptians and Chaldeans and Indians and was striking those who see; but by the
presence of the truth and the manifestation of the Word this too was proven,
and has been brought to nothing completely.

47.5 And concerning the Greek wisdom and the big-voicedness of the
philosophers, I think no one needs an account from us, the wonder being before
the sight of all, that the wise among Greeks having written so many things and
not having been able to persuade even few from the nearby places concerning
immortality and the life according to virtue, the Christ only through cheap
words, and through men not wise according to the tongue, throughout all the
inhabited world has persuaded very full churches of men to despise death, and
to think immortal things, and to overlook the temporary things, and to look
away into the everlasting things, and to think the glory upon earth nothing,
and to claim only the immortality.

48.1 And these things said by us are not as far as words, but even from the
trial itself they have the witness of the truth.

48.2 For let the one who wishes come and contemplate the token of virtue in
the virgins of Christ and in the younger ones who keep chastity in
self-control, and the faith of immortality in the so great choir of his
martyrs.

48.3 And let him come also who wishes to take a trial of the things said
before, and upon the fantasy itself of the demons, and the deceit of the
oracles, and the wonders of the magic, let him use the sign of the cross
mocked among them, having named the Christ only, and he will see how through
him demons flee, and oracles cease, and all magic and sorcery has been brought
to nothing.

48.4 Who then and how great is this Christ, who by his own naming and presence
has overshadowed and brought to nothing all things from every side, and only
is strong against all, and has filled all the inhabited world of his own
teaching? Let those Greeks say who laugh very much and do not blush.

48.5 For if he is a man, even how has one man surpassed the power of all the
gods among them, and by his own power proven them to be nothing? And if they
say he is a magician, how is it possible for all the magic to be brought to
nothing by a magician, and not rather to stand together? For if he was
conquering magician men, or was strong against one only, well would he have
been thought among them as exceeding the art of the others by a better art.

48.6 But if against all magic simply and the name itself of it his cross has
taken the victory, it would be clear that the Savior is not a magician, whom
even the demons called upon by the other magicians flee as master.

48.7 Who then is he, let those Greeks say who have the eagerness only in the
mocking. Perhaps they would say that even he himself has become a demon, and
thus is strong. And saying this they will even very much owe mockery, being
able again to be put to shame by the former demonstrations. For how is it
possible that the one driving away the demons be a demon?

48.8 For if he was simply driving demons, well would he have been thought to
be strong by the ruler of the demons against the lesser, such things as also
Jews wishing to insult him were saying to him. But if all madness of demons
stands out at the naming of this one and is driven, it would be apparent even
in this that they are deceived, and that our Lord and Savior Christ is not, as
they think, some demonic power.

48.9 Therefore if the Savior is neither a man simply nor a magician nor some
demon, but even the suspicion among poets and fantasy of demons and wisdom of
Greeks he has brought to nothing and overshadowed by his own divinity, it
would be apparent and will be confessed among all that this one truly is Son
of God, existing Word and Wisdom and Power of the Father. For this reason
neither are his works human, but they are known above a man, and of God in
being, both from the things themselves that appear and from the comparison
toward men.

49.1 For who of men who have ever come to be put together a body for himself
from a virgin only? Or who of men ever healed such diseases, as the common
Lord of all? And who has given back what was lacking to the coming-to-be, and
made a blind from birth see?

49.2 Asclepius was made a god among them, because he practiced the medical
art, and was devising herbs toward the suffering of the bodies, not himself
molding these from earth, but by the knowledge from nature finding these. And
what toward what came to be by the Savior, that he did not heal a wound, but
molded a coming-to-be and restored the molded thing?

49.3 Heracles is worshipped as a god among Greeks, because he fought against
equal men, and took away beasts by tricks. What toward the things that have
come to be by the Word, that he was driving away diseases and demons and the
death itself from men? Dionysus is worshipped among them, because he has
become a teacher of drunkenness for men. But the Savior who is in being and
Lord of the all, having taught self-control, is mocked among those.

49.4 But let these things be. What also toward the other wonders of his
divinity? Of what man dying was the sun darkened, and the earth shaken? Behold
until now men die and have died still from above; when has some such wonder
come to be upon them?

49.5 Or, so that I may leave aside the deeds through his body, and may
remember the things after the resurrection of his body, of whom ever of the
men who have come to be has the teaching, from ends as far as ends of earth,
one and the same through wholes been strong, so that through all earth his
reverence has flown?

49.6 Or why, if indeed the Christ is a man and not God Word according to them,
is he not prevented by the gods among them from his reverence passing into the
same country, where they are, but rather he himself the Word sojourning by his
own teaching stops the worship of those, and puts their fantasy to shame?

50.1 Many kings and tyrants of earth have come to be before this one, many
among Chaldeans are related and among Egyptians and Indians having become wise
and magicians; who of these ever, I do not say after death, but even still
living was able to be so strong, as to fill the whole earth himself of his
teaching, and to educate so great a multitude from the superstition of the
idols, as many as our Savior has transferred toward himself from the idols?

50.2 The philosophers of Greeks with persuasiveness and art of words have
written many things; what then so great as the cross of the Christ have they
exhibited? For as far as their end the sophisms from them had the persuasive;
but even what they seemed living to be strong they had the rivalry in one
another, and practicing against one another they were contentious.

50.3 But the Word of God, the most paradoxical, having taught with poorer
phrases, overshadowed the very sophists, and brought their teachings to
nothing, drawing all toward himself, and has filled his own churches; and the
marvelous thing, that as a man having gone down into the death, he brought to
nothing the big-voicedness of the wise concerning idols.

50.4 For of whom ever did a death drive away demons? Or whose death ever did
demons fear as that of the Christ? For where the name of the Savior is named,
from there every demon is driven away. And who thus took away the
soul-passions of men, so that the fornicators are self-controlled, and the
man-slayers no longer hold a sword, and those held beforehand by cowardice are
made manly?

50.5 And altogether, who persuaded the men among barbarians and those
according to place of the nations to put aside the madness, and to think
peaceful things, except the faith of the Christ, and the sign of the cross?
And who else has so confirmed men concerning immortality, as the cross of the
Christ, and the resurrection of his body?

50.6 For though Greeks have lied all things, nevertheless they were not able
to fashion a resurrection of their own idols, not considering altogether,
whether altogether it is possible after death for the body to be again; upon
which especially one might even accept them, that having calculated such
things they proved the weakness of their own idolatry, and yielded the
possible to the Christ, so that even from this he might be known among all as
Son of God.

51.1 Who then of men after death or altogether living taught concerning
virginity, and did not think this virtue to be impossible in men? But our
Savior and King of all, Christ, was so strong in the teaching concerning this,
as even children not yet having set upon the lawful age to announce the
virginity above the law.

51.2 Who of men ever was able to pass through so much, and into Scythians and
Ethiopians, or Persians, or Armenians, or Goths, or those said beyond the
Ocean, or those being over Hyrcania, or altogether to go by the Egyptians and
Chaldeans, who think magical things, and are superstitious above nature and
wild in the ways, and altogether to proclaim concerning virtue and
self-control and the worship according to idols, as the Lord of all, the Power
of God, our Lord Jesus Christ?

51.3 Who not only proclaimed through his own disciples, but even persuaded
them according to thought, to change the wildness of the ways, and no longer
to revere the paternal gods, but to recognize him, and through him to worship
the Father.

51.4 For of old being idolaters, Greeks and barbarians were warring against
one another, and they were happening to be raw toward the kinsmen. For it was
not possible for anyone altogether either to pass through the earth or the sea
without arming the hand with swords, because of the irreconcilable battle
toward one another.

51.5 For even all the passing of living for them was coming to be through
weapons, and a sword was for them instead of a staff, and a support of every
help; and yet, as I said before, they were serving idols, and were pouring
sacrifices to demons, and nevertheless those who think such things were not
able to be re-educated from nothing of the superstition of idols.

51.6 But when they have passed over into the teaching of Christ, then indeed
paradoxically as in being having been pricked according to thought, they put
aside the rawness of the murders, and no longer think warlike things, but all
things are peaceful for them, and the things toward friendship are henceforth
according to heart.

52.1 Who then is the one who has done these things, or who is the one who has
joined those who hate one another into peace, except the beloved Son of the
Father, the common Savior of all Jesus Christ, who by his own love underwent
all things on behalf of our salvation? For even from above it was being
prophesied concerning the peace being stewarded from him, the scripture
saying: "They will cut their swords into plows, and their spears into sickles,
and a nation will not take a sword upon a nation, and they will not still
learn to war."

52.2 And such a thing is not unbelievable, where even now the barbarians who
have the wild of the ways inborn, still sacrificing among themselves to the
idols, rage against one another, and without a sword they do not endure to
remain no hour.

52.3 But when they hear the teaching of Christ, at once instead of wars they
turn into farming, and instead of arming the hands with swords, they stretch
them into prayers; and altogether, instead of warring toward themselves,
henceforth they are armed against devil and against demons, warring these down
by self-control and virtue of soul.

52.4 And this is a token of the divinity of the Savior; that what men have not
been able to learn in idols, this they have learned from him; and of the
weakness and nothingness of demons and of idols this is not a small proof. For
the demons, knowing their own weakness, for this reason of old threw men
together to war against themselves, lest having ceased from the strife against
one another, they turn back into the battle against demons.

52.5 At any rate, not warring toward themselves, those being discipled to
Christ array against demons by the ways and by the deeds according to virtue,
and they drive these, and they mock the devil who is ruler of these, so as to
be self-controlled in youth, and to remain in trials, and to be steadfast in
toils, and being insulted to bear, and being deprived to despise, and the
marvelous thing, that they even despise death, and become martyrs of Christ.

53.1 And so that I may say one thing which is even very marvelous as a token
of the divinity of the Savior: who ever simply a man, or a magician, or a
tyrant, or a king, was able to throw so much upon himself, and against the
whole of the idolatry and all demonic army and all magic and all wisdom of
Greeks, being so strong and still flourishing and striking all, to fight
against and at one turn to stand against all, as our Lord, the true Word of
God, who unseen proving the error of each, only from all strips all men, so
that those who worship the idols henceforth trample them, and those marvelled
at in magics burn the books, and the wise prefer the interpretation of the
Gospels to all.

53.2 For those whom they were formerly worshipping, these they leave; and the
one whom they were mocking as crucified, this one they worship as Christ,
confessing him God; And the gods spoken of among them are driven by the sign
of the cross; but the crucified Savior in all the inhabited world is
proclaimed God and Son of God. And the gods worshipped among Greeks are
slandered as shameful among them; but those who take the teaching of Christ
have the life more self-controlled than those.

53.3 These things then, and such things, if they are human, let someone who
wishes show even the things of those before as such, and let him persuade. But
if these appear and are not of men but works of God, why do the unbelievers so
much act impiously, not recognizing the Master who has worked these?

53.4 For they suffer a like thing, as if someone from the works of the
creation should not know the God who is craftsman of these. For if from his
power into the wholes they were knowing his divinity, they would have known
that even the works through the body of the Christ are not human, but of the
Savior of all, the Word of God. And knowing thus, just as Paul said, "they
would not have crucified the Lord of the glory."

54.1 As then if someone should wish to see God who is unseen by nature and not
at all being seen, he grasps and knows him from the works, so the one who does
not see the Christ with the thought, let him at least learn this one from the
works of the body, and let him test whether they are human or of God.

54.2 And if they are human, let him mock; but if they are not human but are
known of God, let him not laugh at the things not to be mocked, but rather let
him marvel, that through such a cheap thing the divine things have been
manifested to us, and through the death the immortality has reached into all,
and through the incarnation of the Word the providence of all has been known,
and the supplier of this and Craftsman himself the Word of God.

54.3 For he himself became man, so that we might be made god; and he himself
manifested himself through a body, so that we might take a thought of the
unseen Father; and he himself underwent the insult from men, so that we might
inherit incorruption. For he himself was being harmed nothing, being
impassible and incorrupt and Self-Word and God; but the men who suffer,
because of whom he also underwent these things, he was keeping and saving in
his own impassibility.

54.4 And altogether the successes of the Savior that have come to be through
his incarnation are such and so many, which if someone should wish to relate,
he is like those who look away into the deep of the sea and wish to number the
waves of this. For as he is not able with the eyes to take in all the waves,
the ones coming on passing by the sense of the one trying, so also for the one
wishing to take in all the successes in a body of the Christ it is impossible
to receive the wholes even with the reasoning, the ones passing by his
consideration being more, of which he himself thinks to have taken in.

54.5 Better then not looking toward the wholes to speak, of which not even a
part is anyone able to speak out, but still to remember one, and to leave the
wholes for you to marvel. For all things equally have the wonder, and wherever
someone looks away, from there seeing the divinity of the Word he is struck
beyond.

55.1 This then after the things said before it is worthy that you learn
thoroughly and set as a beginning of the things not said, and marvel very much
that, the Savior having sojourned, no longer has the idolatry increased, and
the one that is is lessened, and little by little ceases; and no longer does
the wisdom of Greeks advance, and the one that is henceforth disappears; and
demons no longer deceive by fantasies and oracles and magics, but only daring
and attempting they are put to shame by the sign of the cross.

55.2 And to say in a sum, contemplate how the teaching of the Savior increases
everywhere; and all idolatry and all the things opposing the faith of Christ
daily are lessened and grow weak and fall. And contemplating thus worship the
Savior over all and powerful God Word; and condemn the things being lessened
and made to disappear by him.

55.3 For as, a sun being present, no longer is the darkness strong, but even
if somewhere it is left remaining it is driven away: so, the divine
manifestation of the God Word having come, no longer is the darkness of the
idols strong, but all the parts everywhere of the inhabited world are lit by
the teaching of this one.

55.4 And just as, someone being king and not appearing in some country, but
being inside in his own house, often some disorderly, abusing his withdrawal,
proclaim themselves, and each having dressed up beats fantasies upon the
simple as king, and thus men are deceived by the name, hearing that there is a
king, but not seeing him, because especially they are not even able to go
inside of the house, but whenever the truly king comes forth and appears, then
the deceiving disorderly are proven by the presence of this one, and the men
seeing the truly king leave those who of old were deceiving them:

55.5 so also of old the demons and men were deceiving, attaching honor of God
to themselves; but when the Word of God was manifested in a body, and made
known to us his own Father, then indeed the deceit of the demons disappears
and ceases; and the men, looking away into the true God Word of the Father,
leave the idols, and henceforth recognize the true God.

55.6 And this is a token of the Christ being God Word and Power of God; for
the human things ceasing, and the saying of the Christ remaining, it is clear
among all, that the things ceasing are temporary, and the one remaining is God
and true Son of God only-begotten Word.

56.1 Let these things then from us through few, as much as toward an
elementary teaching and character of the faith according to Christ and of his
divine manifestation toward us, be set up for you, O Christ-loving man, and
you, having taken the pretext from these, if you meet with the letters of the
scriptures, genuinely setting the mind upon them, you will know from them more
perfectly and more clearly the exactness of the things said.

56.2 For those were spoken and written through theologian men from God. And
we, having learned from the theologian teachers who meet with them, who have
also become witnesses of the divinity of Christ, share also with your love of
learning.

56.3 And you will know also his second again glorious and truly divine
manifestation toward us, when no longer with cheapness, but in his own glory;
when no longer with lowliness, but in his own greatness; when no longer to
suffer, but henceforth to give back the fruit of his own cross to all he
comes, I mean the resurrection and the incorruption; and no longer is he
judged, but he will judge all, toward what each practiced through the body,
whether good things, or base; where for the good is laid up a kingdom of
heavens, and for those who practiced the base things, everlasting fire and
outer darkness.

56.4 For thus also the Lord himself says: "I say to you, from now you will see
the Son of the man sitting from the right of the power, and coming upon the
clouds of the heaven, in the glory of the Father."

56.5 Wherefore also there is a saving word preparing us into that day and
saying: "Become ready and watch, because in an hour you do not know he comes."
For according to the blessed Paul, "we all must stand beside before the
judgment-seat of the Christ, so that each may receive, toward what he
practiced through the body, whether good, or base."

57.1 But toward the search from the scriptures and true knowledge, there is
need of a good life and a clean soul and the virtue according to Christ, so
that having journeyed through it the mind may be able to obtain what it
reaches for and to grasp, as far as it is reachable for the nature of men to
learn concerning the God Word.

57.2 For without a clean thought and the imitation of the life toward the
saints, no one would be able to grasp the words of the saints.

57.3 For just as if someone should wish to see the light of the sun,
altogether he wipes and brightens the eye, cleansing himself almost like to
the thing longed for, so that thus the eye having become light may see the
light of the sun, or as if someone should wish to see a city or a country,
altogether he arrives upon the place for the sake of the seeing: so the one
wishing to grasp the thought of the theologians ought beforehand to wash and
beforehand to rinse the soul with the life, and to arrive toward the saints
themselves by the likeness of their deeds, so that having become with them by
the leading of the living-together, he may contemplate the things revealed
also to them from God, and henceforth as having been joined to those he may
flee the danger of the sinners and the fire of these in the day of the
judgment, and may receive the things laid up for the saints in the kingdom of
the heavens, "which an eye has not seen, nor an ear heard, nor have they gone
up upon a heart of men, as many as have been prepared for those" who live
according to virtue, and "who love God" and Father, in Christ Jesus our Lord,
through whom and with whom to him the Father with him the Son in holy Spirit,
honor and mastery and glory into the ages of the ages. Amen.
