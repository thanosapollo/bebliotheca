Αʹ. The fear of the Jews being upon the disciples of the Savior, John at any
rate says that the disciples were gathered together in one house, the doors
having been shut because of the fear of the Jews. How did Peter and John go to
the tomb? And this when a military guard was keeping the place, as Matthew
testified; for the custodia is a military detachment. And we will say to these
things, that the apostles went boldly to the tomb, having learned beforehand
from the Magdalene that none of those guarding the place was present there, as
was clear from the stone having been taken away from the tomb; and it was
taken away in no other way than by the angel from heaven flashing upon the
place with much light, and himself rolling away the stone, and frightening the
guards, so that they nearly even became as stone with fear; and after this, as
is likely, they took to flight, so that none of them was left behind, and the
place was free for those coming to the sight of the saving resurrection; for
this was especially the cause of the appearing of the angel. For it was not
for the sake of the resurrection that he moved away the stone, nor on account
of the stone that he appeared such, but in order that he might drive the men
away, and, having received the women coming to the sight, might announce the
resurrection to them. Of these things Matthew is witness, saying, From fear of
him the keepers were shaken, and became as dead. For the Savior, having risen,
even anticipates the angel, and does not wait for the moving away of the
stone, but even while this still lay before the door, and was sealed with the
seals of the chief priests, and the guards were surrounding the place, he was
gone from the tomb, having accomplished the resurrection from the dead by
divine power, at which hour no one knew, and at which time none of the
evangelists indicated; so that one might say opportunely also of him this
which was said by him concerning the universal consummation, in the saying,
"But of that day no one knows, not even the angels of God." Thus at any rate,
himself first having become the firstfruits of the resurrection, the =22.985=
Savior, at which hour no one knew, had been raised, escaping the notice of
all, and had been raised while the stone remained in its position. And an
angel of good things, the angel was present for men, contributing nothing to
the resurrection through his presence, but ministering great things to the
salvation of men; wherefore also he flashed forth his form, showing himself
white-robed, and first beginning the proclamation of the saving resurrection.
And just as at the risings of the sun venomous reptiles and such of the beasts
as are friends of night and darkness flee, but men pursue these as being
kindred to light, in the same manner the angel, flashing with the rays of his
light, drove away those who are enemies of truth and life, but kin and friends
of death; but to those who long for the saving resurrection he announced this
as good news, furnishing them ready leisure through the driving away of the
guards; at any rate he provided two signs for his own to see, the moving away
of the stone, and the opening of the door of the tomb, and the chasing away of
the guards; which things the Magdalene, having seen, reported to the
disciples; and they, having learned these things from her, went boldly at a
run, nothing standing in their way.
Βʹ. And how, while it was still dark, according to the evangelist John, did
the two disciples see the things within the tomb? For the same one also
indicated the hour, saying: "Early, while it was still dark," and that the two
disciples had seen the things within in the tomb; "For they entered, he says,
and believed." The Scripture testified that at the beginning of the day, when
they went to the tomb, there was still then darkness; but nevertheless it was
already early; and this "early, while it was still dark," Mary herself having
seen by herself



goes back to the disciples; then an angel comes to them. In the
intervening time then, after the first arrival, as she was going away to
the apostles, the hour likely advanced; and as those again were arriving
at the tomb, the time
increased still more, so that it was no longer dark, but already clear day, at
which the said men, having arrived, and standing outside the tomb, were able
to see through to the linens lying within; and much more having come inside,
the place now being illuminated by bright day. And it seems to me that the
linens lying within both furnish a proof that the body had not been taken by
men, which Mary supposed; for those taking away the body would not have left
the linens; nor would the thief ever have waited until he unwrapped the linens
and took the body; and at the same time they are also indicative of the
resurrection of the body from the dead. For God, who transforms the body of
our humiliation to be conformed to the body of the glory of Christ, altered
the body as an instrument of the power that had dwelt in it, changing it
toward the more divine, but the =22.988= linens, as superfluous and alien to
the substance of the body, he let go. And it seems to me that Peter and John
arrived at the tomb at a fitting time, in already clear day and bright light,
so that they might not, having come at night and in darkness, be suspected of
that which the chief priests also falsely accused them of, that having come by
night they stole him. Wherefore the men did not go by night, nor while it was
still dark, but when bright day was already shining. But if the Gospel should
say that the disciples were gathered together because of the fear of the Jews,
and then someone should counter, saying, How then did those who were shut in
go about to the tomb in clear day? we will say that those dwelling in the
midst of the city among the Jews, it was likely that they were all shut in
together, gathered in one house; but those arriving at the tomb, being outside
the city, happened to be far also from the fear of the Jews, as going to a
deserted place and one vacated of men. And perhaps also Peter and John, having
become superior to fear among the disciples, more boldly dared to go forth
from the house, the others not daring to do this, or rather only these, who
also in the other things have been testified to have been deemed worthy of
more honor than the remaining apostles.
Γʹ. But how according to John the disciples, having heard from Mary, and then
having come to the tomb, believed; but according to Luke it is said that these
words appeared before them as idle talk, and they did not believe them? The
Mary in John revealed what she had seen as a secret to the chosen of the
apostles, Peter and John only; and they again, escaping the notice of the
remaining disciples, went alone at a run to the tomb, and having seen, they
believed; and it was nothing wonderful that the chosen of the apostles, having
seen, believed, but the rest, to whom the women reported, inasmuch as they had
not received it by sight, did not believe them. At any rate immediately also
when the Savior appeared to the disciples gathered together according to John,
those who saw rejoiced; but Thomas, since he was not present and did not see,
was not persuaded. And if this one disbelieved the apostles, scarcely would
anyone blame the rest that, not yet having seen, they disbelieved the women.
And the Scripture presents much testing and accuracy of the disciples, not
readily assenting to these words, but holding back at first, until later,
having recognized the truth most fully and clearly, they were able to say:
"That which was from the beginning, which we have heard, which we have seen
with our eyes, which we have looked upon, and our hands have handled,
concerning the Word of life." And the passage would also have another sense.
For one might say that the women announcing the resurrection of the Savior, as
from hearing of the two men who appeared to them according to Luke, the eleven
did not =22.989= believe, among



whom were also Peter and John, they themselves not having believed. But to
Mary according to John saying, "They have taken my Lord from the tomb," the
two disciples did not believe this very thing, that the Savior had been taken,
before they came to the place and received this in fact. At any rate when
these entered the tomb, and saw the linens only, but the body nowhere, then
they believed; and what did they believe, if not the word of Mary who said,
"They have taken my Lord"? Wherefore he adds next: "For they did not yet know
the Scripture, that he must rise from the dead."
Δʹ. And how, two disciples according to John having come to the tomb, Peter
and John, Luke says that one only arrived, this would be solved thus. Peter
was always great in eagerness, so as to say to the Savior alone among the
apostles: "Even if I must die with you, I will not deny you"; and again alone
to ask to walk upon the waves; and alone to answer and say to him: "You are
the Christ, the Son of the living God"; wherefore also he alone of the
disciples hears: "Blessed are you, Simon Bar-Jonah." Wherefore also he first
dares to enter the tomb, having come with the other disciple whom Jesus loved,
according to John; but then, having learned from the Magdalene that the body
of the Lord had been taken from the tomb, he went together with the other
disciple; then he withdrew with him, having seen the linens within the tomb
and having believed. But according to Luke, the other disciples disbelieving,
he alone again believes the women saying they had seen the angels that
appeared; for not having disbelieved the testimonies of the women, he comes
back running to the tomb alone; and again stooping down he sees the linens
only as also before; then he went away, wondering in himself at what had
happened; and now he went away wondering at what had happened, but since he
was great in eagerness and showed more zeal than all, hastening and running
about, and looking around everywhere for the Savior with the eyes of the soul
opened, and seeking everywhere and looking about, he did not fail of his hope,
but he himself also is deemed worthy of his theophany; of this the same
evangelist Luke is witness, going on next somewhat thus and saying that the
Lord has truly been raised and has appeared to Simon. And the holy Apostle
also bears witness with him, writing thus to the Corinthians, that "He
appeared to Cephas, then to the eleven." And Cephas was Simon who is also
Peter, to whom also before the eleven the Savior appeared alone to him alone,
because of his zeal surpassing that of all.
Εʹ. Still one might inquire how according to Matt =22.992= hew the Magdalene
Mary with her namesake is said to have seen outside the tomb one angel only
sitting upon the stone; but according to John she sees two angels sitting
within the tomb; but according to Luke two men met the women; and according to
Mark a young man was seen by them. The things in John and Matthew would find
such a solution: for I think that the history in John precedes that in
Matthew, and that the Magdalene, having come first, saw the two angels sitting
within the tomb; and after these things, having come a second time to the same
place with the other Mary, she saw the angel sitting upon the stone. Or
perhaps the angel in Matthew is other, and other also the time and the place
of the seeing of the angel; and other also are his angels toward the women;
and likewise also the two angels in John who appeared within the tomb are
other than the one outside before the tomb sitting upon the stone in Matthew.
For if, Matthew having said late on the Sabbaths, and having related one
sitting before the door of the tomb upon the stone, John, having kept the same
time and the same place, had said that late on the Sabbaths, before the door
of the tomb upon



the stone two sitting were seen, there would truly be ground to charge
disagreement. And Matthew likewise, if similarly to John having kept the
morning hour, had said that within the tomb not two angels but one had been
seen, he would reasonably have seemed to write the contrary. But if the
evangelists distinguished both the times and the manners, and the persons of
those seeing, and the words of the angels, no one would reasonably blame a
disagreement of Scripture, each being true according to the account of its own
history, and indicating a narrative of different events. But the angels in
these evangelists, and the theophanies of the Savior after the resurrection,
being carried in these alone as in those who are greater and eyewitnesses and
earwitnesses of the Savior himself, would preserve this sequence. But the two
men in Luke who appeared in flashing garment, on account of the symbols of the
feast; and the young man in Mark clothed also himself in a white robe, seen on
the right and not on the left, because he announced bright and favorable
things to the women, these again would themselves be other, both from one
another and from those in the first. Wherefore these also did not name them
angels, since they did not even mention the visions of the Savior, having
yielded to those greater than themselves, Matthew and John, to write and
relate the greater things; but themselves narrating the second things, and in
time the things done later after the memory of the first, they passed over the
things said by the eyewitnesses, but filled in the things passed over in
silence by those; which were second and far inferior to the history of the
former; the Holy Spirit thus distributing to each the fitting and
corresponding narratives.
ςʹ. At any rate the women in Luke who came to the tomb =22.993= at deep dawn
and bringing the spices they had prepared, would be other than those in
Matthew and John. For it would not make sense that the Marys who had already
seen so many things should now first bring spices, as not yet having learned
the resurrection beforehand. For clearly through what he says he shows that
those who appeared also were other than those previously indicated; and the
place where they appeared was other; for they were neither within the tomb, as
John taught, nor sitting upon the stone, as the angel in Matthew; but Luke did
not even name them angels at all, but two men, except that they too were in
flashing garment, on account of the symbols of the feast; and their words to
the women are distinctive; and the women also were more, and not the two Marys
in Matthew, but simply those who had come with him from Galilee; and these too
were somewhat careless, not having shown even so much zeal as those who
endured through the night and remained at the tomb. At any rate Matthew
testifies to the Magdalene and the other Mary much vigor and remaining, saying
after his passion these things: "And there was Mary Magdalene and the other
Mary, sitting opposite the tomb"; but these on the day of Preparation sat
opposite the tomb; wherefore also they more quickly see first the angel of
good things, then also the pioneer of life himself. And the one in John, she
too first sees the angels, and after these the Savior himself; these did not
carry spices, nor were they occupied with this zeal. But the women who
followed him from Galilee, being many according to Luke, having returned on
the Preparation, prepared the spices, inasmuch as they had not yet learned
anything beforehand about the resurrection. Wherefore to these two men in
flashing garment appear and announce the resurrection as good news. But the
Savior no longer appeared to them, as he did to the one in John and those in
Matthew; so that it is fitting to consider the history distinctive. And
according to Mark very early on the first of the Sabbaths they come to the
tomb when the sun had risen, these again being other, and at another time
again, who also said



to themselves, "Who will roll away the stone for us?" and they come, and find
it rolled away, and having entered they saw a young man; where again in Luke
men but not angels, so also now not an angel is named, but a young man; so
that this narrative too is distinct, and the one who appeared other, and those
who arrived other, and the time likewise that after the rising of the sun;
which if it had also been observed in Luke, one might reasonably have demanded
the two men in him, and the same words. But if Luke took beforehand the time
before sunrise, reasonably he also relates the two men who then appeared, but
not the young man.
Ζʹ. And there being four evangelists, equal in number to these also appear the
visions recorded by them; and the times are four, and those who appeared at
=22.996= each time, distinctive; and likewise also those who saw, different;
and the words of those who appeared to them, varying. For the first time was
that in Matthew called late on the Sabbaths; and the fourth and last that in
Mark related when the sun had risen; and the middle ones that in John and that
in Luke; wherefore also those who appeared according to time are distinctive.
For late on the Sabbaths one angel from the tomb; after whom also the Savior
himself; and early while it was still dark, two angels within the tomb; after
whom again the same Savior. And at deep dawn others according to Luke, named
two men, not appearing within the tomb. Then later than all the young man,
seen by those who arrived when the sun had risen. If then all, having named
one time and having recorded the same place, did not indicate the same
appearances, one might reasonably have blamed. But if they distinguished the
times, and assigned to each time also a distinctive place, they accordingly
also recorded different visions. As if all had mentioned one appearance, and
having said one angel appeared, or having said two angels in agreement, or
again two men or one young man; then they differed as to the times, or did not
indicate the same places, there would have been room to blame even so. But now
the account remains accurate and unimpeachable, introducing the visions
exchanging upon different times and places, and their beholders again
different, the two first being other than the second; and the third likewise
other than the fourth. Immediately then after the young man's conversation
with the last women, whose names are not given, Mark adds: "And having heard
they fled, and said nothing to anyone, for they were afraid"; these then were
of such a kind and fearful, and very timid, so as not even to be persuaded by
the young man saying to them: "Go and tell his disciples and Peter, Behold he
goes before you into Galilee"; for having heard these words, they did the
contrary; but those in Luke, having turned back from the tomb, reported all
these things to the eleven. And likewise also the one in John comes to the
disciples announcing what she has seen. And only those in Mark, having come
last of all, and truly having delayed, so as to arrive after sunrise, are
deemed worthy neither to see the Savior, nor the flashing angel, nor the two
within the tomb, nor the two men in Luke; but they saw some plain young man
clothed in a white robe, having seen the vision analogously to the smallness
of their understanding; and having seen this one too white-robed for the sake
of the feast, they were nevertheless amazed; for this Mark testified of them;
although in the former cases amazement is nowhere named. And the Magdalene,
being at leisure and persevering, likely not only saw the first sights of
those who appeared to her alone, but also the young man in Mark seen by the
remaining women. And you might say the same =22.997= also concerning the
history according to Luke, who after



the arrival of the many women, and after the sight of the two men who
appeared to them, narrates saying: "And having turned back from the
tomb they reported all these things to the eleven"; to which he adds,
"And they were Mary Magdalene," and the rest. For it was not unlikely
that now again the
Magdalene, having arrived long before and remained vigorously at the tomb,
should be found, when many women who had come together from Galilee arrived at
the tomb bringing the spices, so that she too together with the rest saw the
two men, and heard their words; then returned with the many and related to the
eleven. And the account can also otherwise assign the first things to the
women from Galilee, I mean the arrival at the tomb, and the bringing of the
spices, and the appearing to them of the two men, and these men's words to
them; but the report to the eleven no longer to have been from them only, but
together from all, each relating what she had seen; among whom again the
Magdalene is with the rest, she too announcing to the disciples the things
seen privately by her alone.
Ηʹ. And I inquire also into that, how according to Mark it is said that when
the Sabbath was past they prepared spices, but according to Luke they did this
before the Sabbath on the Preparation itself. And this too, being interwoven
with the former questions, would find an interpretation similar to those; we
demonstrating that they are not the same, but other are those who before the
Sabbath on the Preparation itself, on which the Savior suffered, were occupied
about the burial, and other those after the Sabbath; for Luke has clearly
testified that these did these things on the very day of the passion, nowhere
mentioning the name of women, but simply saying women who had come with him
from Galilee; but Mark recorded by name three only who by themselves had taken
thought for the spices, not on the Preparation, but after the Sabbath was
past; and that these are other than those in Luke, the things brought in next
also show. These women then in Mark see the young man sitting on the right,
who also says to them: "Do not be afraid; you seek Jesus the Nazarene; he has
been raised, he is not here." But to those in Luke two men stood by in
flashing garment, and they bring forth other voices saying: "Why do you seek
the living among the dead?" and the rest; and those in Mark, having heard that
they should announce to the disciples the things commanded, said nothing to
anyone; but those in Luke, having gone, reported to the eleven; so that
through all these things it is gathered that they are not the same; wherefore
they also did not do the things recorded at one time.
Θʹ. "And as they were speaking these things, Jesus himself stood in the midst
of them, and says to them, Peace be to you." Here someone might be at a loss
how the Lord, having stood among the eleven disciples gathered together after
the resurrection according to Luke and according to John who in agreement
recorded these things, is no longer recorded as doing or saying the same
things in both. That the two evangelists wrote the same vision, occurring at
one and the same hour and day, must first be established; and this would
become clear to you if you observe the Scripture accurately. For John, having
first said, "And on the first of the Sabbaths Mary Magdalene comes announcing
to the disciples that she has seen the Lord and these things he said to her,"
next joins on saying: "It being evening on that day, the disciples being
gathered, Jesus came, and stood in the midst and said, Peace be to you." So
that it is clear from these things that on the very Lord's day of the
resurrection, it being evening, were done



the things in John; and according to Luke the same day and hour will be
found; at any rate he too says: "And on the first of the Sabbaths at
deep dawn, the women came to the tomb." Then next, that having seen
angels, they returned and reported to the disciples what they had seen.
And Peter, having hastened,
arrives at the tomb, and sees the linens; and these things happened at deep
dawn, on the resurrection day. And the Scripture next joining on says: "And
behold two of them on that same day were going to a village." And these were
those about Cleopas; with whom having entered Emmaus, taking bread he blessed,
and giving to them, he became invisible. Then he adds: "And having risen they
returned at that same hour to Jerusalem, and found the eleven gathered." Then
as they were speaking Jesus stood in the midst, and says to them, "Peace be to
you," and the rest. It has been shown then also from Luke that it was the same
day, and the same hour is established from the fact that in one day those
about Cleopas came to Emmaus, and from there returned to Jerusalem, evening
having already altogether overtaken them; then the appearing occurred. Since
then the same vision has appeared in both, come let us see how other things
the Savior is said in Luke to have done and spoken, and other things in John.
Likewise then in both it has been said that standing in their midst he said to
them, "Peace be to you." But distinctly in Luke it is added that they thought
they were seeing a spirit, and the rest. But even if so many things have not
been said in John, yet the things next again in both agree. For in John after
"Peace be to you," it is added, "And having said this he showed them his hands
and his side." But the cause is not carried in John, as already having been
said in Luke; and this was their thinking they were seeing a spirit; that
=22.1001= he did indeed show them his hands and his side, John having
testified, would be in agreement with the one who also set forth the cause.
Next to these, Luke says, "And while they still disbelieved for joy and
wondered, he said to them, Have you anything edible here? And they gave him a
piece of broiled fish." But these things John did not write. And when we have
come here it is fitting to apply the mind, as also in the rest, that the Holy
Spirit recorded the more august and more divine deeds and teachings of our
Savior through John, but wrote the more human things through the rest, which
indeed he has also done now. For as the disciples, having seen the Savior,
thought they were seeing not him but a spirit, and as they disbelieved him,
even after he showed them the hands and the feet, necessarily, while they
still disbelieved, having asked for something edible, he ate before them. And
these things, being more bodily, and charging much unbelief of the apostles,
and a condescension of the Savior himself, persuading them and clearly
presenting that it was he, the Spirit submitted to Luke as to one inferior to
write; but the greater things and those indicative of divine power he
presented through John, who himself also writes next these things, "The
disciples therefore rejoiced having seen the Lord"; he said therefore to them
again, "Peace be to you; as the Father has sent me, I also send you; and
having said this he breathed," and the rest. And the account in both would
have sequence, if we should reckon the things in Luke to have been done first,
and then thus after those we should join the things in John. For while they
still disbelieved he both asked for food, and not being satisfied with the
eating of the fish, he also strengthens them with words, reminding them of the
former teaching according to Luke. But when at last having been persuaded they
were fully assured that it was truly he, and they were now full of joy, then
he gives them a second peace, other and greater than the former, and commands
them to be ready for the sending, all but promising that they too would be
like him through the like work. Then next and consequently he breathes upon
them of the Holy Spirit, as



needing this for the promise brought in next; and this was the being able to
forgive sins through the power of the Holy Spirit. And thus from both
evangelists one common account will be completed, the things said in Luke
having been passed over in silence in John; and the things delivered in
writing by John having been stored in silence in Luke; and first the things
said in Luke, and next to those the things in John being joined; and thus the
things in both preserving one mind and one sense. So much for these things.
Ιʹ. But how according to Matthew an angel having appeared to the women
commanded them to announce to his disciples that He has been raised from the
dead, and he goes before you into Galilee, there you will see him, and
likewise =22.1004= also according to Mark the young man, whom they saw sitting
on the right, said these things to the women. But according to Luke in
Jerusalem itself, the eleven disciples being gathered at the same place, and
relating to one another the things concerning his resurrection, he himself
stood in the midst, and converses, and ate a piece of broiled fish before
them. And according to John also again in Jerusalem itself, not once, but also
a second time he appeared to the eleven, first on the very day of the
resurrection, in the evening; then after eight days. How then, according to
Luke and John, he having appeared so many times to the disciples in Jerusalem
itself, according to Mark and Matthew the same are commanded through the women
to go away into Galilee, as there going to see him, but not about to see him
in Jerusalem? Still, if the writing of the Gospels knew only the eleven
disciples of the Savior, even so one might say that the account lacks nothing,
if for a while, healing his disciples who disbelieved the announcements of the
women, once and a second time he appeared to them secretly as they were hiding
in Jerusalem; but in Galilee no longer in a hidden manner, nor once nor a
second time, nor to those shut in because of the fear of the Jews, but with
much boldness he made his theophany and the demonstration of his divinity,
presenting himself to them alive after the passion, by many proofs, appearing
through forty days and speaking the things concerning the kingdom of God, and
lodging with them, as Luke says in the Acts; and this is the first solution.
But since of the disciples the chosen and first filled up the chorus of the
twelve, and there was a second rank besides these, that of the seventy,
concerning whom Luke says in the Gospel: "And after these things the Lord
appointed also seventy others"; and besides these, there were also many other
acquaintances of the Savior, of whom the holy Apostle also spoke saying: "Then
he appeared to more than five hundred brethren," and these themselves also had
been deemed worthy of the name of disciples, to whom it was not possible that
he should appear after the resurrection all together gathered at the same time
in Jerusalem, reasonably two of the evangelists, Luke and John, record that he
appeared to the eleven only in Jerusalem. But the remaining two of these said
that not to the eleven only to hasten into Galilee, but simply to all the
disciples and brethren, both the Savior himself and the angel who appeared
before him commanded, indicating indefinitely and absolutely all. But the
first solution is better and more true; more then and different were the
visions that occurred to the disciples after the resurrection. And some say he
said these things, and others these, and also did them; and there are times
when speaking about the main point, the second fills up the things left out by
the former.



ΙΑʹ. And one might say, how Matthew says that the eleven, having gone to the
mountain of Galilee, saw Jesus, but John after the two visions in Jerusalem
relates that he appeared the third time not on the mountain of Galilee, but by
the sea of Galilee? I myself also agree that this is truly the third time the
Savior appeared to the disciples, the first on the very day of the
resurrection, on which Mary Magdalene saw him in the morning; and Luke also
relates this first vision. And second after eight days, that is on the other
Lord's day, on which the Savior having appeared again heals Thomas of
unbelief, having shown him the side and the hands. And since they had been
commanded to go together to Galilee, the eleven were also doing what was
commanded with the rest, and each of them was gathering, the eleven not yet
having been assembled, but still about to be, the third time by the sea of
Galilee he appears not to the eleven, but only to the seven indicated by John;
and these were Peter and Thomas and Nathanael, and the two sons of Zebedee,
and two others of his disciples; this then truly was the third time he
appeared to these, the eleven not yet being gathered. Wherefore John,
accurately not shutting out the rest also from the vision of the Savior,
indicated that after the first and second vision, he had made the third only
to those named, at the sea of Galilee. And it is not unlikely that after these
things he appeared also a fourth and a fifth time, and at other times and
often; wherefore John also does not circumscribe all the deeds of the Savior;
but going on next he says, And Jesus did many other things; at any rate after
he appeared this third time to the said seven, you would not err in saying
that a fourth vision is that in Matthew, which he recorded as having been made
on the mountain to the eleven. And after these you would not go wrong in
saying that he appeared at the same time to the eleven and the seventy
gathered together. And Paul relates that the Savior was seen after the
resurrection by still more than these, having learned this from others: "For
he appeared," he says, "to Cephas, then to the twelve, then to five hundred,
then to James, then to all the apostles, and last of all," he says, "also to
me." Do you see how many times and to how many he appeared after the
resurrection? Thus he appeared also to those hiding in Jerusalem; and before
them to those about Cleopas going to Emmaus; and before all, of men to Simon,
and of women to the Magdalene. Hence Luke in the Acts, since he showed himself
often to the disciples, observes saying that through forty days appearing to
them and lodging with them, he delivered the teachings concerning the kingdom
of God, and exhorted them to go to Jerusalem, and there to preach the word
first to the Jews; and not to depart from the city before, but to wait for the
promise of the Father, concerning which we shall treat a little later.
