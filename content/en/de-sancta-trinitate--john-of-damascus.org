* On the Holy Trinity

Of our father among the saints John of Damascus, concerning the Holy Trinity.
αʹ. Concerning the holy Trinity, consubstantial and life-giving, confess one
nature, one will, one energy, one power and authority, and lordship, because
there is also one Godhead, three hypostases, that is three persons, the
property of each person being preserved. But in the incarnate economy of the
one of the Holy Trinity, that is of our Lord Jesus Christ, two natures, of
Godhead and of humanity, two wills likewise and energies, one hypostasis, that
is one person, because he himself also is one, who before the ages was
begotten without flux and without body, and in the last of the times was
conceived from the holy ever-virgin Mary the Theotokos, ineffably and without
stain; wholly man the same and God, known in one hypostasis, impassible in
Godhead, and passible in that which was assumed; having kept sound after the
birth the signs of virginity, that is the =95.12= seals. Question. Why did the
Son become man, and not the Father, nor the Spirit? And what did he accomplish
by becoming man? Answer. The Father is Father, and not Son. The Son is Son,
and not Father; the Holy Spirit is Spirit, and not Father, nor Son; for the
property is unmoved. Or how would it be a property, if it were moved or
shifted? For this reason the Son of God also becomes son of man, he who was
incarnate from the holy Virgin, and he did not depart from the filial
property. And the Son of God became man, that he might again grant immortality
to the man he made, and eternal life for the enjoyment of the eternal goods.
Amen; so be it, so be it.
βʹ. Father and Son are not two without beginning, but without an intermediary.
Mixture works corruption, and is mixed thus, as water and wine. Union is when
some joining takes place, neither of the other being diminished nor corrupted,
but remaining in what it is; as the soul and the body. Thus also Christ, the
Word of God perfect, and man perfect, is united, but not confused. One God,
Father of a living Word, of subsistent wisdom and power and of an eternal
impress, perfect begetter of the perfect, Father of an only-begotten Son; and
one Lord, only from only, God from God, impress and image of the Godhead,
active Word, wisdom containing the constitution of all things, and power
productive of the whole creation, true Son of a true Father, invisible of the
invisible, and incorruptible of the incorruptible, and immortal of the
immortal, and eternal of the eternal; and one Holy Spirit, having existence
from God, and manifested through the Son, that is to human beings. Perfect
image of the Son and life, cause of the living, holy fountain, holiness, giver
of sanctification, in whom God the Father is made manifest, who is over all
and in all; and God the Son, who is through all. A perfect Trinity, in glory
and eternity and kingdom, neither divided nor alienated. Therefore there is
nothing created, or slavish, in the Trinity, nor adventitious, as =95.13=
formerly not existing, but later coming in. For the Son never failed the
Father, nor the Spirit the Son; but unchanging and unalterable is the same
Trinity always. For thus we believe the blessed and life-giving and
indivisible holy Trinity. Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, three persons, one
image; three impresses, one stamp; three hypostases, one Godhead; three
properties, one essence; three energies, one grace; three existences, one
equality; three recognitions, one glory; three names, one confession; three
confessions, one faith. God is an eternal and unvarying essence, creative of
the things that are. God is light most high and unapproachable, neither
graspable by mind, nor speakable by word.
γʹ. Question. In how many ways is God? Answer. God is the Father; God is the
Son; God is the Holy Spirit; the blessed nature, the unstinting goodness; the
beloved of all who have partaken of reason, the much-desired beauty, the
beginning of the things that are, the fountain of life, the unapproachable
wisdom, the unchanging nature, the undisturbed life, the manner of life
without grief, around which there is no alteration, which change does not
touch; the overflowing fountain, the unstinting grace, the inexhaustible
treasure.
δʹ. We confess our Lord Jesus Christ the only-begotten Son and Word of the
Father, light from light, true God from true God, to have been incarnate and
to have become man from the Holy Spirit, and from the holy glorious Theotokos
and ever-virgin Mary. The same perfect God, and perfect man, one and only Son,
both before the incarnation and after the incarnation; consubstantial with the
Father according to the Godhead, like us in all things, and consubstantial
with us according to the humanity, having become man without sin; before the
ages begotten from the Father according to the Godhead, and in the last of the
days the same for us and for our salvation from the holy Theotokos and
Ever-Virgin Mary, one and only Son in two natures, unconfusedly, unchangeably,
indivisibly known. For two natures came together with one another according to
a union unbroken, unconfused, unchangeable. For the flesh is flesh, and not
Godhead, even if it has become God's flesh; likewise also the Word is God and
not flesh, even if he made the flesh one with himself through the economy. We
say therefore that the natures are two, but one Christ, and Son, and Lord, the
Word of God become man and incarnate. For the Master did not diminish his own
glory =95.16= because he assumed flesh; for before he took the flesh and
humbled himself, angels alone knew him; but when he humbled himself, the whole
nature of human beings came to know him. You see how the humility did not work
a diminution, but made his glory shine more. And if any of the over-curious
says that the Godhead was on the cross, or was not, he makes it not to be in
all, and circumscribed to one place; but we say that God is uncircumscribed
and incomprehensible.
εʹ. Question. From where is God etymologized? Answer. From tho, I construct,
and I make; as maker of all things, and cause of the construction of all
things; or from theo, I run. For God is present everywhere. Or God, from
theasthai, to behold; for God beheld all things before their coming to be. And
Lord from kyros, which signifies authority. Question. What is union? Answer.
That which is according to hypostasis, and the thing that exists from
different natures. Question. Is the divine nature imparting, or participating?
Answer. Imparting. Question. What is coalescence? Answer. Confusion. Question.
By what names is the Father honored, and according to what is he called
Father? Answer. One Father, the holy God, and almighty, without beginning,
eternal, immortal, unsurveyable, uninterpretable, endless, without successor,
unmoved, unsearchable. He is called Father, because he keeps all things.
Question. By what names is the Son honored, and according to what is he called
Son? Answer. One Son, the holy Word, ineffable offspring, unspeakable Word,
unsurveyable mind, light of life, sun of righteousness, shepherd, door, way,
lamb. And he is called Son, because he is from the Father. Question. By what
names is the Holy Spirit honored? According to what is he called Spirit?
Answer. One Holy Spirit, lord, life-giving, lover of mankind, essential,
existent, hypostatic, firm, keen, without care, sanctifying power,
consubstantial with Father and Son. For he is vapor of the upper powers of
God; and he is called Spirit, because he nods and breathes wherever he will.
Question. What is Jesus Christ? Answer. Lord become man without sin. Question.
What is Christ? Answer. A symbol of Godhead and of ensouled flesh,
unchangeable union; and he is called =95.17= Christ, because he used flesh
according to us. Question. What is holy? Answer. He who sanctifies and is
sanctified. Question. What is Word? Answer. Word is essential, always present
together with the Spirit. Question. In which hypostasis do you worship the Son
of God? Answer. In the hypostasis of God the Word, one incarnate nature
worshiped, one worship, since from two that came together there is one
worshiped person. Two essences, two energies, two wills, two
self-determinations; and hypostasis is fivefold: hypostasis, and person, and
impress, and property, and individual.
