HOMILY ON VIRGINITY.
=1.1= Each of us, having received free will from God, as the holy Paul says
in the first to the Corinthians, has authority each over his own will.
=1.2= And whoever has decided in his own heart to keep his own virgin will do
well, so that, as he says, he who marries does well, and he who does not
marry, he says, does better. =1.3= And that I may speak more plainly, for
these things moved me, I shall not hesitate to set forth the good and the
better, especially for those who are winged by the heavenly and spiritual
love toward God. =1.4= For the honorable marriage is good, as being fitted
together from God; but the undefiled virginity is better, through the better
of the good; and neither is the one alien to the other. =1.5= For as the
fruits are found from the plants; nevertheless the sweet fruit turns away
the axe; and the axe, says our Savior, is laid at the root of the trees.
=1.6= Every tree therefore that does not produce good fruit is cut down.
=1.7= For Paul accuses the woman of transgression according to the
first-formed Eve, but promises that salvation shall come through
childbearing. =1.8= But he did not leave the saying ambiguous: for if, he
says, they remain in faith and love and holiness and sobriety. =1.9= Hence
then it is already clear that if they remain, they are saved; but if they do
not remain, they will be answerable in a transgressing fault.

=2.10= Let a father persuade a son, and a mother a daughter, to keep
chastity for Christ; for the offspring is common. =2.11= If however anyone
neither flatters nor wishes to persuade with zeal toward God, let him not
dare to hinder those hastening to keep chastity, whether son or daughter,
whether slave or maidservant they happen to be. =2.12= For the one desiring
to keep chastity, do not hasten to hinder, lest you sow some enmity with the
true Bridegroom Christ. =2.13= Then, if nature brings forth a soul,
blossoming purely with the body for the glory of Christ and turning toward
him, while the father together with the mother is honored on earth, not
suffering with the child that blossomed for the glory of Christ, nevertheless
the child, nourished by the pure air of chastity and watered by the dew of
the good promise, will appear with truth to the Master uncorrupt and pure and
undefiled; it itself will be glorified for honor, because from thorns and
briers a cluster has come to be; but those who begot it bring upon themselves
condemnation instead of glory. =2.14= Therefore your daughter wishes to
remain a virgin for Christ. =2.15= You ought to rejoice; but not to agree
immediately. =2.16= Do not bring forth the things from the heart, but the
things from the lips. =2.17= Not being melted by envy, but fearing the fall,
for this is what is sought, pure from the beginning; do not be ashamed of the
Bridegroom. =2.18= Then if you see the footprints fair, the movements well
ordered, the gaze modest, what the mind is, what the desire is, whether human
or heavenly, what the strains of fasting and of all piety in Christ, if you
see a heavenly love moving guilelessly, by all means thenceforth give her in
the bridal chamber to the child of the Lord God, to the spotless and
undefiled Bridegroom. =2.19= Let the father thenceforth keep the temple of
God.

=2.20= Become a priest of God the Most High. =2.21= Do not allow anything
base to approach the pure temple; do not, because you begot her, despise her,
but because you have a deposit of the Master, you ought not to be careless.
=2.22= Guard yourself, lest you grow drowsy, lest you lose the deposit of the
heavenly King. =2.23= Do not grow slack while keeping guard, lest you despair
after losing; do not smile, lest you weep. =2.24= And if it hurts, better to
hurt for Christ. =2.25= For not even if someone teaches the child the best
things without pressing hard, not moved by anger, but touching with paternal
affection and love of child. =2.26= Do not despise because you have, but
fear while guarding, in order that you may have. =2.27= Do not bring every
man into your house; for many are the snares of the devil. =2.28= Do not show
your deposit to every man; for envy is everywhere. =2.29= Assiduously forbid
conversation with men; for from conversation in Genesis the first-formed fell
with Eve.

=2.30= And if the conversation be harmless, the useless one trifles. =2.31=
For he is cunning in evil; he does not apply the drugs at once, but having
mixed the gall with honey he escapes notice. =2.32= For as one is trapped
from the lips of a harlot woman, in the same way now also the words from an
unchaste man at first stroke the throat like honey, but the ends are more
bitter than gall and more sharpened than a two-edged sword, <and> more
terrible. =2.33= And if the habit is venerable, and if the words are
pleasing to God, do not trust, being afraid; for both parts alike sprang from
the same seeds, and it is impossible to root out the plant of desire unless
sober reason quenches it. =2.34= Do not, under pretext of a vigil, having
dismissed her as if to church, send her away captive. =2.35= Nor again,
carrying torches for the sake of keeping vigil, let her quench the lamp of
chastity. =2.36= Nor, under pretext of the assemblies, having been robbed, be
robbed by the assemblies, and remain without assembly with the saints.
=2.37= Nor, having gone out on account of some who have died, let her herself
be found dead. =2.38= And attend carefully to the inner bed; for the
baleful one is everywhere: also laughter and wrath and anger and oath and
idle word and the other passions of the flesh. =2.39= Do not grant anything
at all, so that no blemish may attach to the unblemished one.

=2.40= Care for the tender shoots, that you may take away the pluckings.
=2.41= Learn what you have, that you may know to keep: a sanctuary for God, a
temple of Christ, a pure altar for the King, a Holy Spirit incarnate, pure
members of Christ, a keeping-place of the law, a disciple of the gospels, a
venerable speech of the Church of God, a right doing of the transgression of
Eve, a recall from exile, a reconciliation with men, a bride of the heavenly
King, a pledge of life. =2.42= This deposit is the cause of so many deposits;
do not therefore attend carelessly, that you may become a partaker of the
achievement. =2.43= Subjoin from the divine writings this: better is
childlessness with virtue; and the thing said to the holy Mary by the angel:
Hail, full of grace, the Lord is with you. =2.44= And gathering the kindred
of these from the seeds and benefits of the divine writings, the commandments
that glorify chastity, adding them, weaving a pure crown of words of
chastity, add them, in order that, desiring those things, she may eagerly
mount into the undefiled bridal chamber of Christ, running with the wise
virgins, so that you also, the father, may taste the palace of the kingdom,
and she may receive the crown of incorruption.

=3.45= But if she is not willing to remain so, no one compels, no one
blames. =3.46= She will blame herself, when the pains present themselves to
her and the transitory things*** escape her, when the birth pangs come
together, when she makes lamentation as of dragons and mourning as of
daughters of sirens. =3.47= For she dragged upon herself the decision of the
Lord, which he spoke to Eve in Genesis: Multiplying I will multiply your
pains and your birth pangs; and: in griefs you shall bring forth children.
=3.48= For she overlooked the Hail, full of grace. =3.49= And even if
afterward she holds fast the passing man to whom she fled, the help from him
becomes far from her. =3.50= She will blame herself, when the daughter dies
and the son is sick, when another mourning threatens her mourning that went
before, and a groan succeeds her groan. =3.51= She will blame herself, when
the husband is abroad and her griefs dwell at home, and the things for her
needs are not at hand; when the husband is delayed by his journey and the
griefs take root; when, having looked out many roads, the tears come down in
streams; when a messenger of her death outstrips the death of her longed-for
husband; when, wailing and tearing her hair, she runs now one road now
another, whether tender, whether barefoot, whether rivers meet her, the haste
of mourning overlooks these things; when, plucking her tresses, she beats her
breast; when she binds her fingers together and puts them on her knees and
falls to the ground; when her spirit fails her as she weeps and the light of
her eyes is dimmed; when she beats her breast all day and measures the dust
upon her head; when, having defiled with the ash from the hearth, she smites
her head that was crowned with garlands and sprinkled with lilies. =3.52=
And she will blame herself, when a mournful garment succeeds the bridal
robe. =3.53= And perhaps to her mourning a second mourning will be
betrothed, for the sake of a second marriage. =3.54= And if this pass by, the
troubled and much-groaning spirit of widowhood will suffice her and abound.
=3.55= Then, having accomplished all things, the wretched one, or rather
having been made wretched before all things, departs this life, which is full
of many pains. =3.56= And there perhaps other pains and second groans will
meet her: when she sees the holy virgins clothed in garments of incorruption,
holding in their hands the psaltery written in their hearts, singing the
triumphal hymn of virginity, wearing crowns of incorruption on their temples,
in return for which they left the human groaning here, dancing before Christ,
with gladness made by angels. =3.57= When Christ the Bridegroom shall show
forth his affection, when he shall groan with groaning, then again she will
blame herself, then she will repent much, and her repentance will be without
profit.

=4.58= But let not only the female sex be persuaded to keep chastity; let the
men also not be slack to be persuaded to sanctify their own bodies, thus
stringing along from the divine writings: Blessed is the man whose hope is
the name of the Lord; and: Blessed are the blameless; and: The unmarried man
cares for the things of the Lord; and: Blessed are the self-controlled, for
God will speak to them; and that corruption will not inherit incorruption;
and that he who sows to the Spirit will reap eternal life; and that those who
are in the flesh cannot please God; and that it is comely, and that it is
service devoted to the Lord without distraction; and that it is good for a
man so to be; and the word spoken to the prophet Jeremiah by the Lord, that
You shall not take a wife, and no son or daughter shall be born to you in
this place; and the word in the prophet Isaiah, the Lord saying: Let not the
eunuch say, I am a dry stick. For these things, he says, says the Lord to the
eunuchs: as many as choose the things I will, to keep their hands from doing
unrighteousness, and do not devise evil against the Lord, I will give, he
says, a place renowned in my house and within my walls better than sons and
daughters; and these also, the things cried out again by the Lord himself in
the gospels: there are eunuchs who made themselves eunuchs for the sake of
the kingdom of the heavens. =4.59= Let the soul of the young child at least
take its chief support from the holy scriptures.

=4.60= And if, recounting to them also the hardships of marriage, for the
turning away of the passions of the flesh, they thus wrestle down the
passing and raging and grass-flower-passing desire of the flesh with the
heavenly desire, they will quench the bitter of sweetness, having conquered
with sober reason. =5.61= Thus therefore let the father converse with the
son, as it were softening the vigor of the young man; then, if you see his
faith unbroken, nothing in it swayed or soft, the outward habit not blooming
disorderly in wantonness with the flesh, but the faith working with the
divine fear, imitate the patriarch: become a young Abraham offering the young
Isaac; leave the donkey and the boys, that is, the transitory employment of
this world, and lay upon him the wood, that is, the cross. =5.62= After that
likeness, lead you also your own young Isaac to the mountain, offering a
living sacrifice to the living God, that you too may become a priest of God
the Most High, offering praise to God; for with such sacrifices God is well
pleased; such priests serve God. =5.63= And if you shackle his feet, and if
you do not let him have food at hand, so that his flesh, grown fat, may not
kick, lest, fed from superfluous food and drink, things that corrupt, with
strength of body and not of soul, kicking he kick away and not receive the
wood of the cross, or perhaps scatter the altar, so that you may have hopes
upon hope, so that you may be both father and priest of the Most High; and if
you offer prayers for him, you will show the love-charm. =5.64= For this the
servant of God Job did, whom you hear sacrificing a calf yearly on account of
the thought of his sons, saying, Lest perhaps my sons have thought evil
things against God. =5.65= And if the mother makes tunics for the son, let
her not be vexed at all; for Anna, serving God, made for the holy Samuel a
little cloak yearly as for a son, as it is written in the first of Kingdoms.
=5.66= And surely the king will not fail to recompense with honor those who
honored his soldiers. =6.67= That parents also lay up treasure for their
children, Paul taught. =6.68= What is the treasure? To bring them up in
discipline and admonition of the Lord. =6.69= For thus truly is the good
treasure, and peculiar to its owner, which the thief does not approach, nor
does the moth corrupt; nor the transitory wealth of the world, which is for
men a table-talk and an unquiet and a war. =6.70= For often the owner has
also lost life itself along with it, as we have often received by hearing,
and you will see the work, that these things are so. =6.71= Thus therefore
let parents also treasure up for their own children, furnishing the things
dear to God. =6.72= And if anyone else strives to do this for God, only in
the name of a disciple, and if anyone, having drawn a cup of cold water and
strained it, offer it to a disciple of Christ, he will be an heir of the
divine kingdom. =6.73= Let all who love Christ then hasten to provide the
useful things. =6.74= But let nothing be thought more useful than the
salvation of the soul by those of sound mind. =6.75= Many are the things
that secure salvation for the soul, even down to two mites, and even to a
good word spoken over what is given. =6.76= For this guileless choice from a
true mind is more acceptable to the philanthropic God than the blood of
sacrifices. =6.77= For often the testimony borne by the all-wise God to David
in the first (book) through the holy Samuel has taught us: men look on the
face, but God looks in the hearts, especially on those lifted up on the wing
of perfect heavenly desire.

=7.78= Let virgins therefore come; for there is need of them in the kingdom,
but there is need of wise ones. =7.79= For not unjustly did he shut out
those virgins you hear of; but since they had no oil because of their own
negligence, they could not be worthy to enter in and greet the fair
(Bridegroom), nor did they strive for the better, to keep awake; and
therefore they heard not as brides, and did not reach the incorruptible
bridal chamber, and did not stretch out their palace, and therefore did not
receive the crown of the kingdom of the heavens, and did not converse with
the immortal Bridegroom; for they did not reach him. =7.80= Therefore he
said: there is need of wise ones. =7.81= If you keep virginity for Christ,
not as you will, but as he wills who is kept a virgin for you. =7.82= Do not
will the same things as those who mind the things of the world. =7.83= For
how shall he who has mortified the members on earth, being members of sin,
be [one] of those who did not yield to the truth? =7.84= What do you seek,
you who have been crucified to the world, together with those who are of the
world and in the world and love pleasure? =7.85= No one, he says, serving as
a soldier entangles himself in the affairs of life, that he may please him
who enrolled him; and no one is crowned unless he contend lawfully. =7.86=
You were buried with Christ in baptism; why do you lay down the law like one
living in the world? =7.87= You crossed the Jordan; do not turn back to
Egypt. =7.88= That is, you went out from the world in your mind; do not again
darken with earthly things your mind that has already been enlightened to
heaven. =7.89= Not the habit Jerusalem and the ways Egyptian.

=7.90= See that you cover not the wolf within with sheepskins, nor crown the
thorn with rose blossoms. =7.91= The garment is taken up; the manner of the
sheep is sought. =7.92= The leaves fall; the cluster is sought. =7.93= The
flowers outside fall away; the character appears; for the nature of the thorn
shows itself. =7.94= Thus there is need of virgins not of body, but need of
guileless manners. =7.95= Thus not the leaves feed the husbandman, but the
fruit gladdens. =7.96= Thus understand me also in all things; for there is no
need to speak of these things subtly. =7.97= For the minds of the wise,
springing toward the understanding, count the subtlety of words a burden.
=7.98= I do not exhort, then, that there be any outward habit, but let the
work of the habit go before, lest otherwise, while the works are comely, some
of us be found uncomely in the works. =8.99= Often therefore also, when a
daughter desires to hasten to the better things, a mother, caring for the
young men, or led astray by a passing beauty that is only thought so, or
perhaps also melted by envy, hastens to make the daughter a child of this age
and not to be married to God. =8.100= But everything thought terrible lies in
ambush; yet do not play the coward, O child; the eye upward, where the
longed-for one is; keep the footprints of her who went before, that Thecla
you hear of.

=8.101= Even if what is said be a digression: even if Theoclia rages, even if
Thamyris mourns, even if Alexander succeeds, even if the judge threatens, let
the love-charm not be extinguished. =8.102= For it is profitable for the
young to be crucified with Jesus Christ. =8.103= Who will separate those
loving him from the love of Christ? =8.104= Neither tribulation, nor
distress, nor judges, nor orators, nor sword, nor any other creation will be
able to separate from the love of Christ. =8.105= But even if you become
bereft of parents, you are not bereft of God. =8.106= And if your father's
house forget you, the King will desire your beauty. =8.107= Come then,
whether you be very rich, or hard to look upon, or poor, or a hireling.
=8.108= Take courage, come; for the Bridegroom Jesus Christ is not enamored
of beauty that withers, nor does he turn away poverty. =8.109= For the same
grace calls the small and the great, but it tests the choices, and thus
receives those armed with virtue by sober reason. =8.110= These things also
hold in the case of a son hastening to present his body: the father raises
hatred against his own child, not being able to know such an achievement.
=8.111= He reckons, according to the divine writing, the wisdom of God
foolishness. =8.112= But do not crouch, O young man; do not prefer a mortal
father or mother or anyone else. =8.113= He declared those worthy of
himself, as it is written in the gospel, whom Jesus called: Leave the ship,
leave the father and the nets for the catch, even if the hopes of life are
many, even if Zebedee rages. =8.114= Do not prefer anything at all to God,
nor rather to your own salvation, that you may be with John, bearing the
worthiness of the throne beside him. =8.115= For thus God promised that those
who left father or mother or brothers or fields or houses would sit upon
twelve thrones in the kingdom of the heavens. =9.116= He said these things
to virgins, he said these things to those hearing; for he himself shut up
all, and that all might run together to him. =9.117= And he commanded these
things not only [in] those keeping virginity, but also: whoever has left wife
and children and the rest will inherit a hundredfold and eternal life.
=9.118= He did not say separating, but those having, so that they should not
be as having, because the season is straitened; not loosing the bond, but
sowing sobriety, that he may drown incontinence. =9.119= And since we <do
not> bear the marks of Christ, and do not reject the loosing, except in word,
perhaps some of us are gathered together in the church in vain.

=9.1= And this I will not leave unwitnessed. =9.121= Come, I will set forth
from the gospel. =9.122= He likened his own kingdom to a dragnet cast into
the world and gathering very many from every multitude. =9.123= When on the
shore, he says, you pick out the good things and cast the rotten outside.
=9.124= He himself says: Many are called, but few are chosen; not few because
of the choosing, but because the chosen are not found. =9.125= For as on the
threshing floor many things are heaped together, but few are carried into the
storehouse, so in the Church many of us are gathered together, but few will
be brought into the kingdom. =9.126= For the ship of the kingdom makes its
way and steers into the deep, and the north winds do not play. =9.127= The
ship does not envy; the light one is carried away. =9.128= Let no one then be
light, lest he become as chaff separated off outside. =9.129= For no one is
found to escape notice. =9.130= For the fan is in the hand of God, who
cleanses the threshing floor: the fruit is mingled with the fruit; the chaff
is separated by the discernment of the Spirit and is delivered to fire, as
you hear. =9.131= And in another way we learned the exactness of our
examination in the gospel. =9.132= For the king came in, he says, to see
those reclining at table, and found him who seemed to escape notice. =9.133=
The king said. =9.134= He suffered it, as you heard; for the word spoken to
him was stern. =9.135= I hesitate to utter even with the tongue, and
therefore I will not send it down by word. =9.136= For those who love
learning know the fearful word spoken to that man and to those who have
become of like manner with him. =9.137= It is not the dinner that fails, but
the one reclining was unworthy of the dinner. =9.138= Not is the king
envious, but the one who came in did not make himself worthy, since, being
evil, he reclined. =9.139= Let no one then suffer this: let no one be evil,
lest he be muzzled. =9.140= Thus let those who wish to live godly muzzle the
desires of the flesh. =9.141= For Paul cries out to flee youthful desires.
=10.142= I did not desire to speak thus, but I was compelled to say it so
that, having heard the stern word, you may flee the terrible thing. =10.143=
And when must one flee? =10.144= Now is the day of salvation. =10.145=
Whatever he wishes to be with Christ, you know that there comes a time when
no one can work. =10.146= Night comes, when each shall receive the things
through the body which he did. =10.147= A day comes when no one can put off
to the morrow. =10.148= For in death there is none that remembers you, and in
Hades who will confess to you? =10.149= If then such is the boundary,
brethren, and we cannot pass by, nor is it possible for those who have passed
to escape notice, let us outrun his face in confession. =10.150= Let us
outrun, that we be not outrun. =10.151= Let us conquer the evil by the good,
that we be not found in the evils. =10.152= Let us cleanse ourselves from
every defilement of flesh and spirit, that, appearing pure to the Pure, we
may receive a pure crown through Jesus Christ our Lord, through whom be glory
to the Father forever and ever. Amen
