* Petitions of the Arians
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Petition made at Antioch to the emperor Jovian, by Lucius and Bernicianus, and
certain other Arians, against Athanasius bishop of Alexandria. First petition,
which they made as the emperor was departing to the Campus at the Romanesian
Gate.
We beseech your might, and your majesty, and your piety, hear us. The emperor
said: Who are you, and from where? The Arians said: Lord, Christians. The
emperor said: From where, and from what city? The Arians said: Alexandria. The
emperor said: What do you want? The Arians said: We beseech your might and
your majesty, give us a bishop. The emperor said: I have ordered the former
one, whom you had formerly, Athanasius, to sit on the throne. The Arians
said: We beseech your might; he is both in exile and under accusation for
many years. A soldier suddenly becoming zealous answered: I beseech your
majesty, examine yourself who they are, and from where. For these are the
remnants and the refuse of Cappadocia, the leftovers of that unholy George,
they who laid waste the city and the world. Hearing this the emperor spurred
the horse, and departed to the Campus. Second petition of the Arians. We have
accusations and proofs against Athanasius, who ten and twenty years ago was
separated by the ever-memorable Constantine and Constantius, and under the
most God-loving and most philosophical and most blessed Julian had the exile.
The emperor said: The accusations of ten and 20, and 30 years ago have
already passed. Do not speak to me about Athanasius; for I know why he was
accused, and how he was exiled. Third petition of the Arians. And now again
we have certain other accusations against Athanasius. The emperor said: The
just things will not appear from the crowding and from the many voices; but
rather choose two from yourselves, and from those of the multitude another
two. For I cannot give a decision toward each one of you. Those from the
multitude said: These are the remnants from the unholy George who laid waste
our province, and =26.821= they did not allow a councillor to dwell in the
cities. The Arians said: We beseech you, anyone you may wish except
Athanasius. The emperor said: I told you that the matters concerning
Athanasius have already received settlement; and becoming angry he said: Feri
feri. The Arians said: We beseech you, if you send Athanasius, our city is
ruined, and no one assembles with him. The emperor said: And yet having
inquired I have learned that he thinks well, and is orthodox, and teaches
well. The Arians said: With the mouth he speaks well, but in the soul he
thinks deceitfully. The emperor said: This is enough, that you have testified
concerning him, that he speaks well and teaches well. But if with the tongue
he teaches well and speaks, yet in the soul he thinks badly, he has it toward
God. For being men we hear the word; but the things in the heart God knows.
The Arians said: Command us to assemble. The emperor said: For who prevents
you? The Arians said: We beseech you, he proclaims us as heretics and
dogmatizers. The emperor said: It is his work, and of those who teach well.
The Arians said: We beseech your might; we cannot bear this man, and he has
taken away the precincts of the churches. The emperor said: So then you have
come here on account of money, and not on account of the faith? And again he
said: Go away, and keep peace. Still adding he said to the Arians: Go away to
the church, tomorrow there is a synaxis for you. And after the dismissal,
there are bishops here, and Nemesinus is here; each of you, as he believes,
shall sign. And Athanasius is here; let him who does not know the word of the
faith learn from Athanasius. You have tomorrow and the day after. For I am
going out to the Campus. And a certain scholasticus of the Cynics petitioned
the emperor. I beseech your majesty, on the pretext of bishop Athanasius the
catholicus has taken my houses. The emperor said: If the catholicus took your
houses, what has that in common with Athanasius? Another certain
scholasticus, Patalas, said: I have an accusation against Athanasius. The
emperor said: And you, what have you in common with Christians, being a
Hellene? But certain of the multitude of the Antiochenes seized Lucius, and
brought him to the emperor saying: We beseech your might and your majesty,
see whom they wished to make bishop. Another petition made at the porch of
the palace by Lucius. I beseech your might, hear me. The emperor stood and
said: To you yourself, Lucius, I say, how did you come here, by ship or on
foot? Lucius said: I beseech you, by ship. The emperor said: To you I say,
=26.824= Lucius, may the God of the world, and comet, sun and moon, be angry
with those men who sailed together with you, because they did not throw you
into the sea; and may that ship not have fair-running winds for all time, nor
when storm-tossed with those sailing in it obtain a harbor. And through
Euzoius they petitioned those about Probatius, the successors of Eusebius and
Bardio, the eunuchs, so that the unbelieving Arians might obtain an
introduction. And the emperor, having learned this, and having tortured the
eunuchs, said: If anyone wishes to petition against Christians, let him
suffer such things. And thus the emperor dismissed them.
