* On that saying, To those who love God all things work together for good
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

On the apostolic saying which says, "We know that to those who love God all
things work together for good," and concerning endurance, and how great is the
gain of the tribulations.

=51.165=

αʹ. I am so disposed today, as one who has come to you after a long time. For
even if I happened to be shut up at home because of the infirmity of the body,
yet I was so disposed as one banished far away somewhere from your love. For
he who knows how to love exactly, whenever it is not permitted to be with the
one who is loved, even if he dwells in the same city, will be disposed no less
than those who sojourn in a foreign land. And this those know who know how to
love. Pardon us then, I beseech you; for this separation has not come to us
from sloth, but the silence was of weakness of body. And I know that you all
now rejoice that we have laid aside the infirmity; but I rejoice, not that I
have laid aside the infirmity only, but that I am able again to see your
longed-for faces, and to revel in your love according to God. And just as the
many of men, after the riddance of the infirmity, seek bowls and cups and cold
streams; so to me your company has been established sweeter than all gladness,
and this is to me both a ground of health, and an occasion of gladness. Come
then, since through the grace of God we have received one another back, let us
pay you the debt of love, if indeed this can ever be paid. For this alone of
debts does not know an end; but by how much it is paid, by so much the debt is
stretched; and just as in the case of moneys we praise those who owe nothing,
so here we bless those who owe much.

For this reason also Paul, the teacher of the inhabited world, writing, said:
Owe no one anything, except to love one another, wishing us both always to pay
this debt, and always to owe it, and never to dissolve this obligation, until
we dissolve the present life. As therefore to owe moneys is heavy and
burdensome, so not always to owe this obligation is worthy of accusation. And
that you may learn that this is so, hear the wisdom of this marvelous teacher,
how he introduced the exhortation. For first having said: Owe no one anything;
then he added: Except to love one another; wishing all our debt to be emptied
here, and wanting this debt to remain undissolved continually. For this is
especially =51.166= that which holds together and binds tight our life. Since
then we have known how great is the gain of this obligation, and that in being
paid it rather increases, come, let us also today, as far as is possible, be
eager to pay the debt which we owe you, not from sloth nor from any
ingratitude, but from the infirmity that befell, having discoursed a few
things to your love, having made this same marvelous teacher of the inhabited
world the subject of the discourse; and the things which today writing to the
Romans he was discoursing, these having brought into the midst let us go over,
and after a long time let us set the spiritual banquet before your love. And
what the things that were read are, it is necessary to say, so that having
been reminded of the things said, you may receive with more ease the things
spoken by us. We know, he says, that to those who love God all things work
together for good.

What does this very preface wish? For this blessed soul utters nothing simply,
nor at random, but always applies the spiritual medicines corresponding to the
passions that lie before it. What then is it that he says? Since from every
side the many temptations were surrounding those who were then coming to the
faith, and the devices from the enemy were successive, and the plots
continual, and those who warred against the preaching were not at rest,
throwing some into prisons, others into arrests, and dragging others down into
countless other pits; for this reason, just as an excellent general, seeing
the adversary breathing with much rage, going round his own men from every
side stirs them, nerves them, anoints them, makes them more courageous, makes
them more eager to raise their hands against the enemy, and not to fear his
onsets, but standing opposite with a firm mind even to strike his very face,
if possible, and not to be struck with terror at the resistance against him;
in the same manner also this blessed one, the heaven-high soul, wishing to
stir the minds of the faithful, and being eager to set upright their reasoning
which was lying below, so to speak, thus began saying: And we know =51.167=
that to those who love God all things work together for good. Do you see the
apostolic understanding?

He did not say, I know, but, We know, drawing them also into assent of the
things said, that to those who love God all things work together for good.
Consider the exactness of apostolic words. He did not say, Those who love God
escape the terrible things, they are freed from the temptations; but, We know,
he says, that is, we have been persuaded, we have been fully assured, we have
received the proofs through the very experience of the things; We know that to
those who love God all things work together for good.

βʹ. How great a power do you suppose this brief saying to have? All things, he
says, work together for good; for do not tell me here the useful things, nor
reckon only the ease and the security, but also the opposite things, the
prisons, the tribulations, the plots, the daily assaults, and then you will
see with exactness the power of the saying. And lest I lead your love far away
somewhere, if you wish, let us bring into the midst a few of the things that
happened to this blessed one, and you will see the strength of the saying. For
when going round everywhere, sowing the word of piety, pulling up the thorns,
and being eager to plant the truth in the soul of each, having come to a
certain city of Macedonia, as the blessed Luke, who composed the book of the
Acts, related to us, a certain maidservant having an evil spirit, and not
enduring to be silent, but going round and wishing everywhere to make them
known through the demon, with much authority, by a word and a command, having
driven it out as some scourge-worthy thing, he freed that woman from the evil
demon; it being necessary that those dwelling in that city should look upon
the apostles as benefactors thereafter and saviors, and having used every
manner of attendance about them should repay so great a benefaction, they
repay them with the opposite things.

And hear with what things they repay them. Seeing, he says, her masters, that
the hope of their work had gone out, having laid hold of Paul and Silas, they
dragged them into the marketplace before the rulers, and they brought them to
the generals, and having laid many blows upon them, they threw them into
prison, charging the jailer to keep them safely.

Did you see the excess of wickedness of those dwelling in that city? did you
see the endurance and fortitude of the apostles? Wait a little, and you will
see also the love of mankind of God. For being wise and resourceful, he does
not loose the terrible things at the beginning and in the prefaces, but when
all the things from the adversaries have increased, and the endurance of his
athletes has been shown through the things themselves, then he also displays
his own inclination; so that no one may have to say, that they leap upon the
dangers for this reason, because of being confident that they will suffer
nothing unpleasant. For this very reason he both lets some go into the
terrible things, using a certain ineffable wisdom, and snatches others out, so
that through all things you may learn his surpassing love of mankind, and that
storing up greater recompenses for them, he often permits the terrible things
to be stretched.

Thus then he also does here. For after so great a wonder-working and the
benefaction, =51.168= which they displayed having driven out that shameless
demon, he permitted them both to be scourged and to be thrown into prison. For
from this especially the power of God also was shown. Wherefore also this
blessed one said: Most gladly therefore will I boast in my weaknesses, so that
the power of Christ may tabernacle upon me. And again: When I am weak, then I
am strong, calling weakness the successive temptations. But perhaps someone
might be at a loss here, for what reason they drove out the demon who was
saying nothing contrary to them, but rather even making them known; for for
many days he was crying out saying, that These men are servants of the most
high God, who announce to us a way of salvation. Do not be surprised, beloved;
for this also was of the apostolic understanding and of the grace of the
Spirit. For even if he was saying nothing contrary to them, yet lest from this
the demon, having become trustworthy, might be able also in the other things
to drag away the simpler ones, for this reason having stopped his mouth he
drove him out, not permitting him to utter the things beyond his worth. And
this he was doing, following his own Master; since also coming to him they
were saying, We know who you are, the holy one of God; and nevertheless he was
driving them out though they were saying these things. And this was coming to
pass for an accusation of the shameless Jews, that they, seeing miracles every
day and countless marvels coming to pass, were unbelieving, but the demons
were recognizing him, and were confessing him Son of God.

γʹ. But let us return to the sequence of the discourse. In order then that you
may learn how to those who love God all things work together for good, it is
necessary to read you this whole history, so that from this also you may know,
how after the blows, after the prison, the grace of God changed all things for
them into good. But let us see how the blessed Luke shows this, saying: And
the jailer, having received such a charge, threw them into the inner prison,
and secured their feet into the wood. Consider how the terrible things are
stretched, so that both the endurance of the apostles may become more
splendid, and the unspeakable power of God may be made known to all. And hear
also the next. For he adds, And at midnight Paul and Silas praying were
hymning God. See a soul given wings, see a mind sober; let us not simply run
past, beloved, the thing said. For not at random did he also mark the time for
us, saying, And at midnight, but wishing to show, that, when to all the others
sweet sleep comes upon them and puts the eyelids to sleep, and especially
those who have been set in many pains it is likely at that time to be dragged
down by sleep, then therefore, he says, when the tyranny of sleep had
prevailed from every side, then those men Praying were hymning God, making
this a greatest proof of the love concerning him.

For just as we, being troubled by bodily pains, seek the company of our
genuine ones, so that from the converse with them we may console the intensity
of the pain; so also these saints, being set on fire with the longing
concerning the Master, and offering up the sacred hymns, were not even
receiving a sensation of those =51.169= pains, but were becoming wholly of the
supplication, and they were offering up that marvelous hymnody, and thereafter
the prison became a church, and the whole place was being sanctified through
the hymnody of those saints. And one might see marvelous and unexpected
things, men bound to the wood, and in nothing hindered toward the hymnody. For
the one who is sober, and watchful, and having a boiling longing concerning
God, nothing will ever be able to hinder from the company toward the Master;
for I am a God drawing near, he says, and not a God from far off; and again
elsewhere, While you are still speaking I will say: Behold I am present. For
wherever there is a sober mind, the reasoning is given wings and is released,
so to speak, from the bond of the body, and flies up toward the one longed
for, and looks beyond the earth. and having become higher than the things
seen, he is eager toward that one; which indeed has also come to pass in the
case of these saints. For see the immediate energy of the hymns, and how even
having come to be in a prison and bound to the wood, and mixed together with
sorcerers and prisoners, they were not only in nothing harmed, but even by
this more they shone forth, and with the light of their own virtue they
illumined all those dwelling in the prison. For the voice of those sacred
hymns, entering into the soul of each of the prisoners, was remolding it, so
to speak, and reshaping it.

For suddenly, he says, there was a great earthquake, so that the foundations
of the prison were shaken, and immediately all the doors were opened, and the
bonds of all were loosed. Did you see the power of the hymns toward God? Not
only did they themselves who were offering up the hymns enjoy consolation, but
they also caused the bonds of all to be loosed; so that it might be shown
through the things themselves, how To those who love God all things work
together for good. For behold both blows, and prison, and wood, and a sojourn
with the executioners, and nevertheless these things have become a ground of
good things and an occasion of good repute, not for them only, nor for the
prisoners dwelling in the prison, but also for the jailer himself.

For the jailer, he says, having become awake, and seeing the doors of the
prison opened, having drawn a sword, was about to kill himself, having
supposed that the prisoners had fled. See for me here the love of mankind of
God surpassing every word. For what reason do all these things come to pass at
midnight? For the sake of nothing else, but so that the matter might be
managed without tumult and in quiet, and they might work the salvation of the
jailer. For when the earthquake had come to pass and the doors had been
opened, the bonds of all who were there were loosed, and not one of them was
permitted to leap out. Consider for me also another wisdom of God here. For
all the other things came to pass, the earthquake, I mean, and the opening of
the doors, so that all might learn through the works, who they were who were
then dwelling in the prison, and that they are not chance men; but it came to
pass for no one to go out, so that this might not become an occasion of
dangers for the jailer. And that this is true, hear how, when the jailer only
suspected this to have come to pass, and supposed that some had fled, he even
despised =51.170= life itself. For having drawn a sword, he says, he was about
to kill himself. But the one sober and watchful everywhere, the blessed Paul,
with his own voice snatched the lamb from the throat of the beast. For he
cried out with a great voice, saying, Do nothing evil to yourself; for we are
all here. O excess of humility! he did not think great thoughts upon the
things that had come to pass, he did not rise up against the jailer. he did
not endure to utter anything overweening; but with the prisoners, with the
executioners, with the evildoers he numbered himself, saying: We are all here.
Did you see him using much humility, and assigning nothing more to himself
than the evildoers there? See also the jailer thereafter, not approaching as
to one of the others. For having taken courage and having asked for lights, he
leaped in, and being trembling, he fell before Paul and Silas, and having led
them out, he said: Lords, what must I do, so that I may be saved? Did you see
how To those who love God all things work together for good? did you see the
device of the devil, how it was dissolved? how his devices became void? For
when they had driven out the demon, he caused them to be thrown into the
prison, thinking to hinder the course of the preaching through this. But
behold even the prison has become for them a ground of spiritual merchandise.

δʹ. Therefore we also, if we are sober, not only being in ease, but also in
tribulations, are able to gain, and then more, than in ease. For ease, for the
most part, works men more slothful; but tribulation, preparing one to be
sober, makes one also worthy of the inclination from above, and especially,
whenever through the hope toward God we display the endurance and the
fortitude in all the tribulations that are brought on. Let us not then be in
pain, whenever we are tribulated, but rather let us rejoice; for this is the
ground of our good repute. For this reason also Paul was saying, And we know
that to those who love God all things work together for good. But let us see
the boiling soul of these saints. When then they heard the jailer saying, What
must I do, so that I may be saved? did they put it off? did they postpone? did
they neglect toward the catechesis? By no means; but what do those men say to
him? Believe upon the Lord Jesus Christ, and you shall be saved, you, and all
your house.

See apostolic care. They are not content with his salvation, but through him
they wish also to net all those belonging to him into the word of piety,
giving the devil a mortal blow. And he was baptized, he and all his,
immediately, and he rejoiced with all his house having believed in God. From
this we are taught never in the spiritual things to postpone even the chance
thing, but always to consider the time that falls in our way a fitting time.
For if while it was night these saints did not endure to put it off, what
defense shall we have, running past a spiritual gain at another time? Did you
see the prison becoming a church? did you see the lodging of the executioners,
suddenly shown to be a house of prayer, and the mystagogy being accomplished
there? =51.171= So great a thing is to be sober, and never to run past the
spiritual gains, but to make every time fitting toward such merchandise. Well
then was this blessed one saying, writing, That to those who love God all
things work together for good. This saying, I beseech you, we also having
engraved in our mind, let us never be distressed, whenever we fall into some
grievous things according to this life, or circumstances, or bodily
infirmities, or some other grievous matters; but using much philosophy, let us
hold out against every temptation, knowing that, if we are sober, we are able
to gain from every side, and more from the temptations than from the eases;
and let us never be downcast, considering how great is the gain of endurance,
nor even be hostile toward those who bring the temptations upon us. For even
if those men do this fulfilling their own aim, yet the common Master permits
it, wishing us even through these things to traffic the spiritual merchandise,
and to receive the wage of endurance. If then we are able thank =51.172= fully
to bear the things that are brought on, we shall dissolve no small part of the
things we have transgressed.

For if the Master was enduring seeing this treasure and teacher of the
inhabited world falling into dangers every day, not overlooking his own
athlete, but preparing the trenches longer for him, so that he might work the
crowns more splendid for him, what would we say, we who are full of countless
sins, and through these often falling into the temptations, so that having
given the penalty for these things here, we may be deemed worthy of at least a
little love of mankind, having enjoyed on that fearful day those ineffable
good things? And reckoning this with ourselves, let us hold out nobly against
all things, so that we may both receive the wage of endurance from the Master
who loves mankind, and may be able to cut away the multitude of the things we
have sinned, and to obtain the eternal good things, by the grace and love of
mankind of our Lord Jesus Christ, with whom to the Father, together with the
Holy Spirit, be glory, might, honor, now and always, and unto the ages of
ages. Amen.
