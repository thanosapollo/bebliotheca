Of Constantine Manasses the race of Oppian through political verses in meter.
Oppian the poet was a Cilician by race from a most brilliant city called
Anazarbus, having been of thrice-blessed conspicuous parents, of Agesilaus a
father and of Zenodote a mother, the father being full of wisdom and of the
greater education and especially also of the higher. He flourished then in the
times of the ruler Severus, Severus who generated Marcus Antoninus. Whom having
sojourned in the bounds of the Cilicians, for it was a custom prevailing then
for those monarching often to go around all those under them, that they might be
set right by them, if any should limp, and all the race of Cilicians having
flowed together into the same toward his escort, and all meeting, the great
Agesilaus had been left even alone; for he was at leisure with books by night
and by day hunting the fairest of all the lessons, and whelping the son into the
like hunts. The ruler then is angry, he makes the lover of wisdom summoned; for
he thought it a despite that this one also had not been set as his escort, and
he sets him an exile into the island Melite, which the wide-gaping sea of Adria
washes around. Where Oppian being with him thirty years old wrote the hunt of
the fishes, the sea one, that of the beasts after it, then that of the birds
with more others, in fine and short-cut books, of which all-subduing time has
boasted, having spared these two completely and only those unto the hunts and
toward the sea one. And when having been brought up into Rome, the elder Rome,
he met with the king of the Romans Antoninus, for Severus had gone from earth
having left the life, and he handed over to him the books that had been toiled,
he had so great a love toward the king, as to have been ordered to show a search
toward the angers, and he seeks the bringing-down of the father from an island,
he hits the desired, he takes the begetter, and gold also is numbered to him
toward the king, setting a price of one stater for one verse. And let these
things for me stand here of the further course; for I pass by the many fleeing
length of word, that he would have died with a father having turned in
Anazarbus, a pestilential disease and destruction having fed the city, that when
he died the people set a costly statue having written a praise in elegiacs, that
he happens fittingly altogether to be a maxim-maker, and by comparing he shows
the things suspected, and last that much is the smoothness in the words, which
as a flower has a preparation of the clear, and he knows the density of the
enthymemes, which is hard for rhetors and altogether laborious.
