PROVERBS COLLECTED BY THE MOST HOLY PATRIARCH KYR GREGORY OF CYPRUS ACCORDING
TO THE ALPHABET. 1.1 This cord has drawn nothing: it is said from the
fishermen who hauled nothing. 1.2 An Aphrodisian oath. Not punishable. 1.3
Marketplace of Cercopes: of the wicked and ill-natured. For certain Cercopes
were deceivers in Ephesus. 1.4 An eagle's old age, a lark's youth. 1.5 Goods
of Cilicians: of those growing rich not well; for a certain Cilician betrayer,
having betrayed Miletus, became prosperous. 1.6 Do not despise a rustic
orator. 1.7 Garden of Adonis: of things short-lived and untimely. 1.8 At
another time to become of another sort and to follow the land: that it is
fitting to make oneself like the customs of the cities and places in which one
may come to be; and it is said from the octopuses. 1.9 Shake another oak: of
those refusing to give still; they are accustomed to take the request. =1.10=
You sing as one sailing to Delos: of those living without care and fond of
pleasure. =1.11= Salt and a bean: of those not knowing and pretending; for the
seers set out salt and a bean. =1.12= To lift the armpit: instead of to feast;
it is said from those dancing in a rustic way. =1.13= The goat the knife.
=1.14= A tearless war, and: A trophy of one sleeping: of those succeeding most
easily and contrary to hope. =1.15= Always jackdaw to jackdaw: and: Always god
leads like to like. =1.16= Horn of Amaltheia: because Zeus gave to Amaltheia
his nurse, from a goat that was feeding him, a horn, through which there
gushed forth for her everything she might ask. =1.17= You teach an eagle to
fly. =1.18= For the dice of Zeus always fall well: of those worthily honored.
=1.19= A contest admits no excuse, nor does friendship. =1.20= An eagle in the
clouds: of things hard to catch. =1.21= A milled life: of the ready and
without toil. =1.22= You stir Anagyrus: of those bringing evils upon
themselves; for a certain hero Anagyrus used to harm his neighbors. =1.23= May
such enemies always pursue me: of the despised and worth nothing. =1.24= An
eagle sees woodworms: of those despising certain people. =1.25= There is no
brine in him: of the unpleasant. =1.26= A fox is not bribed: of those not
easily caught. =1.27= Libya always brings some evil: of those always
discovering some new evil. =1.28= The springs of rivers go upward: of things
said contrariwise. =1.29= Always the fornicator calls the temperate man a
fornicator. =1.30= Fountains of silver speak: of those made conspicuous or
prospering through wealth. =1.31= Keep away from a greater man: that is, guard
yourself, in the Ionic. =1.32= An Arabian piper: who piped for a drachma, but
stopped for four. =1.33= The cap of Hades. =1.34= To touch with the tip of the
finger. =1.35= To weep with blood: by hyperbole, of those weeping exceedingly.
=1.36= The Graces are naked: that one must confer the gift without vainglory.
=1.37= You feed a whetstone: of those being fed and not making progress.
=1.38= Some labored, others had the benefit: of those inheriting the things of
others contrary to hope. =1.39= The owl utters one thing, the crow another: of
those not agreeing with one another. =1.40= To play the fox toward another
fox: of those wishing to deceive their likes. =1.41= To some a tongue, to
others grinders: of the talkative and the gluttons. =1.42= An old man's skull
is a raisin: of those useful for nothing. =1.43= Leucon says one thing, but
Leucon's ass carries another: of those whose deeds are at variance with their
words. =1.44= We were bailing out one, but another flows in: of those toiling
in vain. =1.45= I asked for water-buckets, but they denied scooping-bowls.
=1.46= At once the favor has been pitied and has died: of the ungrateful.
=1.47= If meat is not at hand, one must be content with salt-fish: that one
must be satisfied with what is present. =1.48= We were once; last year's
things are always better. 1.49a The man who flees does not wait for the sound
of the lyre: of those who ought to do each thing quickly. A Lydian man had no
troubles, but he himself came and bought them: of those drawing evils upon
themselves. =1.50= An insatiable jar. =1.51= To farm the winds: of those
toiling and partaking of nothing. =1.52= Not even the gods fight with
necessity. =1.53= A scorpion-fish instead of a perch: of those choosing the
worse things. =1.54= A fisherman struck will bring understanding: And a fool
knows having suffered. =1.55= Some sow, but others will reap. =1.56= With the
bear present you seek tracks: of things obvious. =1.57= A beetle midwifes an
eagle: of those suffering at the hands of certain cheap ones; for the beetle
makes the eagle's eggs vanish by rolling them. =1.58= An old man's jaws are a
staff. =1.59= Friends are nearest when a man is doing well. =1.60= Friends are
out of the way when a man is doing ill. =1.61= A fox drives the ox: of the
crafty and small, yet overcoming great men who are foolish. =1.62= At one time
the day is a stepmother, at another a mother: of those sometimes prospering,
and at another time faring ill. =1.63= Give and take at once: when we deal
with an untrustworthy man. =1.64= The treasure has become coals: of those
disappointed in the things they hoped. =1.65= From the slow-legged asses a
horse sprang. =1.66= All things are easy to the wise: of those who through
prudence prevail even over the difficult. =1.67= Fight with spears of silver
and you will conquer all. =1.68= Every hedgehog is rough. =1.69= You flay a
wineskin: of those doing something mindlessly. =1.70= You live a piper's life:
of those living from the things of others. =1.71= You will pay back
grape-stones, pig: that is, you will give back what you ate. =1.72= The
counsel of a bad man met with utmost ruin: of those suffering things worthy of
what they did. =1.73= Athos covers the flanks of the Lemnian cow. =1.74= From
the oar to the platform: of those suddenly attempting the greatest things.
=1.75= Not feeding yourself you feed dogs: of those lacking means to feed
themselves, but promising others. =1.76= Having eaten up a medimnus of salt:
of the ungrateful. =1.77= The wagon pulls the ox: of those doing something the
reverse way. =1.78= Wagon-sized words: of great speeches. =1.79= A human is a
Euripus, both soul and mind and character: of those changing most easily.
=1.80= The sow is lost, and the talent, and the jaw. =1.81= Uninvited, friends

the feast of the good. =1.82= The doors of the Muses have been opened. =1.83=
Office shows the man. =1.84= A cargo of salt, whence it came, there it went:

dark in the light, but bright in the darkness. =1.86= It is hard for one who
has sense to speak much among the foolish. =1.87= If the lion-skin does not
reach, sew on the fox-skin: of those not able to harm openly, but using
knavery. =1.88= You hurl a javelin with a plow: of those doing something
unconsideringly. =1.89= But the trap seems as if it will catch a trap. =1.90=
Here is Rhodes, here the jump. =1.91= Boeotian mind: of the uneducated; for
the Boeotians were always reproached for this. =1.92= Boeotian strain: of
those quiet at the beginning, but later making a disturbance. =1.93=
Touchstone: of those being examined in words, inasmuch as the Lydian stone
tests the gold. =1.94= Boeotian riddles: of the senseless; for the Sphinx was
Boeotian. =1.95= An ox on the tongue: of those being bribed; because the coin

loving themselves more than others. =1.97= It is a poor thing to set upright
an old man who fell when young. =1.98= Fruit is sweet when the watcher has
departed: of those enjoying the things of others without toil. =1.99= An old
woman plays the Bacchant: of those doing something out of season. =1.100= Old
wives' babble. 2.1 You bring forward against me a belly, the fairest reproach
of all, Which when full is lighter, but empty is heavy. 2.2 A burden of the
earth: of the useless. 2.3 War is sweet to one without experience. 2.4 An old
woman having clapped raises much dust: of those who through experience set
many matters in motion. 2.5 The ring of Gyges: of the resourceful. 2.6 Nakeder
than a peg. 2.7 A woman is general, and, A woman encamps: of things contrary
to expectation. 2.8 Do not trust a woman, not even when she has died. 2.9
Ionian laughter: of the dissolute; for the Ionians were slandered for this.
=2.10= An old fox is not caught. =2.11= An owl to Athens: of those displaying

Athens. =2.12= An old ox unlamented in the halls. =2.13= He will stand trial
even if an ass bites a dog: of those being slandered over a small thing.
=2.14= Justice begot justice and harm harm: of the litigious and those
stringing lawsuits to lawsuits; and of those falling upon dense misfortunes.
=2.15= It is shameful to strike against the same stone twice. =2.16= Dodonaean

a dolphin. =2.23= The sleep of Epimenides: of those sleeping much; for he
slept fifty years. =2.24= You have consulted at Trophonius's: of the gloomy

=2.29= In two you see three: of those seeing other things instead of other

An uninitiated Hermes. =2.37= To drink from a bored cup: of friends who have

=2.45= Then being rich he no longer takes pleasure in lentil-soup. But before,

=2.48= An elephant does not heed a mouse. =2.49= You have your foot outside

let the horse. =2.62= They were once, they were, valiant Milesians. =2.63=
More foolish than Adonis of Praxiteles: for a certain Adonis, son of

=2.75= From a bad raven a bad egg. =2.76= Adorn Sparta which you obtained:

the complete and full. =2.87= He is carried with the stream. =2.88= A crab

=2.90= The mullet fasts: of the gluttonous; and the animal is insatiable.
=2.91= The ram has paid back the cost of rearing: of the ungrateful; for the

=3.24= An ass into chaff. =3.25= He is neither rained on nor sunned: of the

The witness from home: of those bringing witnesses against themselves. =3.28=
An ass among bees: of those fallen into evils. =3.29= An ass hearing a lyre:

=3.56= Gazelles join battles against a lion. =3.57= The Rhodians the
