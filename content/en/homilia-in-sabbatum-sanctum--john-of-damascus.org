* Homily on Holy Saturday
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:
Δʹ. Of John, humble monk and presbyter of Damascus, a discourse on Holy
Saturday.
=96.601=
αʹ. Who shall speak the mighty deeds of the Lord, who shall make all his
praises heard? Who shall recount the boundless and gaping sea of his goodness?
who the incalculable love toward the servants? who the condescension beyond
mind? who the compassion concerning us, and the unspeakable care from this?
There is no one, not even if he should speak with the tongues of angels and of
men; not even if he should have gathered in himself all human understanding.
For even if the spirit is willing, yet the tongue is weak toward speaking, and
the mind is dim toward comprehension. For great, it is confessed, is the
mystery of the divine economy, not received by thought, but by faith alone,
needing a purity of soul composed of divine fear and longing. For there is no
other way to procure purification of soul than by divine fear and love. Nor
again to receive divine illumination, unless the seeing faculty of the soul
has first become purified. For the Divine is unapproachable to the profane;
only the pure in heart shall see God, as Christ, the true truth, says. Since
even in the theophany of old that came to Moses at the bush, he is first
commanded to put off the sandal, and so to approach the appearing symbol. And
the taking off of the sandals signifies the putting away of dead and
earth-bound thoughts. And again when mount Sinai was smoking at the divine
lawgiving, not all went up, but for those going up the ascent was measured by
the purification. If then upon the symbols a washing of every stain was
legislated, how much more must those who are about to approach the true and
original things make themselves sincere and God-like? Let us therefore purify
ourselves, brethren, of every earthly mind, and of all tumult, and of life's
turbidity, that we may receive clearly the light-formed flashes of the divine
Word, and may feed our souls with the spiritual bread, the food of angels, and
having come within the inner shrines, may know clearly the divine passions of
the impassible one, and saving of all the world.
βʹ. Now the mystery hidden from the ages is unveiled; now the chief point of
the divine economy =96.604= is completed; now the crown of the incarnation of
God the Word is set on; now the abyss of divine love is published. For so God
the Word loved the world, that by the Father's good pleasure he came down to
incarnation, that the immaterial one clothed himself with the weight of
material flesh, that receiving the passions in that which is by nature able to
suffer, and becoming a passion by death, he might clothe us who are passible
with impassibility. Hence hunger, and thirst, and sleep, and toil, and grief,
and agony, and fear, that is, the essential desire of life; hence cross, and
passion, and death, from our nature. For these are its natural passions, and
these of my constitution are not blameworthy, of which he did not refuse the
experience, that having been moved and mastered in him, they might become
subject to us also.
γʹ. Christ on the cross; let us come together, and be partakers of the
sufferings, that we may also become of his glory. Christ among the dead; let
us be dead to sin, that to righteousness


we may live. Christ is wrapped in swaddling-clothes and clean linen; let us be
loosed from the bonds of sin, and clothe ourselves with the divine radiance.
Christ in a new tomb; let us cleanse ourselves of the old leaven, and become a
new lump, that we may be called a dwelling of Christ. Christ in Hades; let us
go down together to the exaltation-making humility, that we may also rise
together, and be exalted together, and be glorified together, seeing God
always and being seen. You from the ages, be set free; you who are bound, come
out; you who are in darkness, be unveiled; be released, you captives; and you
blind, look up; arise, sleeping Adam, and rise from the dead; for Christ the
resurrection has appeared. But, if it seems good, let us make the beginning of
the discourse from above; for thus it would be both clearer and more
persuasive, and making the journey through a smooth path; and pray for me the
divine illumination of the Holy Spirit, without which the wise are foolish,
and with which the unlettered become wiser than the very wise.
δʹ. God is cause of all, but he himself is not from anyone, wherefore also
unbegotten; having a Word enhypostatic, co-eternal, begotten from him without
flowing and without time, never separated from the Father; perfect God, like
in all things to the one who begot him, except =96.605= unbegottenness, one in
essence and power, will and energy, kingdom and lordship; not without cause;
for he is from the Father; not beginning from time; for there never was the
Father when the Son was not; for the Father is Father of a Son, and the Father
will not be if the Son is not, having existence together with the Father,
being begotten from him without interval, and remaining in him without
departing, being wisdom of the one who begot him, and enhypostatic power, God
by nature, consubstantial with the Father, not known without the Spirit; for
the Holy Spirit also proceeds from the Father, of equal strength, of the same
completeness, of the same working, of the same power, co-eternal,
enhypostatic, coming forth not filially but processively; this is another mode
of existence, divine and incomprehensible; good like in all things to the
Father and the Son, princely, Lord, creator, God by nature consubstantial with
the Father and the Son, co-reigning, co-glorified, co-worshipped by all
creation. This is what is worshipped by us. Father, unbegotten begetter of the
Son; for he is not from anyone; Son, offspring of the Father, as begotten from
him; Holy Spirit of God and Father, as proceeding from him, which is also said
to be of the Son, as manifested through him, and imparted to creation, but not
having existence from him. One God, because one Godhead, one power, one
essence, one will, one energy, undivided in the hypostases alone that are
divided, that is, in the properties of existence. =96.608= For to the Father
alone belongs the unbegotten; to the Son alone to have been begotten from the
Father without beginning, without time, and eternally, and to proceed without
time and eternally belongs to the Spirit alone. One Trinity, simple,
uncompounded, infinite essence, incomprehensible light, unbounded power, sea
of goodness, one God in three perfect hypostases glorified without division.
εʹ. He himself made angels from things that are not, heaven, earth, and all
things in them; ethereal fire, and an abyss of waters; air, a storehouse of
breath, and a most transparent vehicle of light, a second vault made firm in
water, a mid-space of the aerial and abyssal waters, which indeed he also
called heaven; the shining sun, double-coursing worker of day and night,
lighting all things with its splendid flashes; the moon illuminating the
night, and tempering the burning of the solar radiance; these stars, ornaments
of the firmament; and all the things upon earth; flowers of every kind and of
many uses, seeding herb, fruit-bearing trees, a most beautiful adornment for
the earth; all sorts of tribes of animals swimming in the waters, great and
extraordinary sea-monsters; various


kinds of creeping things, and winged birds, having generation from waters, but
flying in the air, and dwelling on land; animals again from earth, and untamed
beasts, and tame systems of cattle; all together a demonstration of his own
magnificence of working, and a feast for the one about to be fashioned
according to the divine image.
ϛʹ. And last of all, as a kind of king, the much-celebrated animal, man,
honored with his own hand and image, having fitted the body from earth, but
having created the soul by the divine and life-giving inbreathing; which I
mean the Holy Spirit, the life-giving and creating, and perfecting, and
sanctifying all things; not having bound a soul previously existing to the
body (away with the harsh and absurd babble of Origen), but having constituted
it from non-being in the composite fashioning.
ζʹ. This man then, this man, whom he constructed according to his own image as
both intellective and rational, bearing a spirit of life, he set in communion
of his own grace, and made him citizen of paradise, which he planted toward
the east. For the children of the Hebrews name Eden delight; having granted
him a blessed and all-blissful life, freed from all care and easy, from which
all pain, grief, and groaning fled. For like some other angel dwelling on
earth, he hymned God who made him, being filled with divine thoughts, being
led through all creation to the maker of generation, for whom also he had been
fashioned. For one who says, I think, that this is the tree of life, the all
and full, undivided, bearing only the participation of the good, will not err
in the manner of anagogy.
ηʹ. But the inventor of evil did not bear it, the father of the lie, the
creator of envy, the serpent devil, to see man luxuriating in the abundance of
so many goods. He attacks him then, providing for the deprivation of the
goods. And he pretends goodwill, but trips man by deceit, and brings down a
fall, most pitiable of all falls. And see what the bait is.
θʹ. What of all things is higher? And what alone is higher? God, clearly, who
is beyond all and cause of all. What then is more desirable than to become
God? There is nothing. Having cheated man with this desire, he persuades him
to taste of the tree of knowledge, having first plundered the woman's
simplicity. For evil is somehow by nature covered with the pretext of the
good; for it also smears its own shames upon virtue; of which tree the greedy
tasting the one who knows all things profitably forbade to Adam. For he says:
From every tree that is in the garden you shall eat for food; but from the
tree of knowing good and evil, you shall not eat from it, nor shall you even
touch it; in whatever day you eat from it, you shall die by death. And if you
should wish also to understand a tree planted for testing and trial of
obedience and disobedience, and from this named tree of knowledge of good and
evil, or also providing a cognitive energy of their own nature to those who
partake, you will not miss what is fitting.
ιʹ. But the tasting of this is unprofitable before perfection. For he knew
himself to be naked when he tasted, and he contrived a covering, and was drawn
about, and was troubled toward the care of his own body, having departed from
the divine contemplation, hence he is stripped of grace, and puts on
corruption, and is turned back to earth, and becomes an exile from the divine
paradise, and is condemned to sweat, and toil, =96.612= and death. Not that
the tree begets death; for God did not make death; but that disobedience draws
death after it. Hence sin, having taken pasture,


enslaves me the thrice-wretched, and works every kind of evil in me, and
salting through itself a sweet food, gives it to death.
ιαʹ. What then? Did he who is compassionate by nature, who gave being, and
granted us well-being, overlook his own fashioning, the most precious, the
most beloved, so terribly tyrannized? By no means. But to speak apostolically,
God having spoken of old in many parts and in many ways to the fathers in the
prophets, in these last days has spoken to us in his only-begotten and
consubstantial Son, whom he sent forth into the world, that he might lead
wandering man back to the ancient blessedness. And, O the great goodness! what
happens? The unmixable are mixed; things above nature come to be; things which
eye has not seen, and ear has not heard, and have not arisen upon a man's
heart, are now seen, and heard, and spoken, and believed. What is this? The
Son of God and God becomes Son of man, mediator of God and men; not casting
away being God; unchangeable; nor feigning becoming man. For he is without
falsehood, and all guile, and deceit is everywhere alien to the straight good
pleasure; but truly he became man by nature, without any change and confusion,
that he might truly grant salvation to the nature.
ιβʹ. He is therefore perfect God; for in him dwells all the fullness of the
Godhead bodily; perfect man, that he might save the whole man; for what is
unassumed is unhealed; one and the same in two perfect natures, known without
any co-division; identified according to hypostasis with his own flesh, and
naturally consubstantial with the Father and the Spirit as God; and to us,
having become man, of the same nature and of the same race, bearing in himself
all the essence of the first Adam, from which, and in which, and which things
a man is, a mortal body, and an intellective and immortal soul, rational,
self-determining, willing and energetic; for the animal that lacks any one of
all =96.613= these is not a man; from a holy Virgin, having constituted for
himself flesh ensouled with a rational and intellective soul, and united to
this according to hypostasis, and conceived as God incarnate, one Son, and
Christ, and Lord, and having made her who bore him Theotokos.
ιγʹ. For if he were not one according to hypostasis, how did the Word become
flesh? the Holy Spirit showing this, not that the Word was turned into flesh,
but rather that he was united in flesh according to hypostasis. Or how did the
only-begotten Son, who is in the bosom of the Father, they say, declare? For
the appearing man himself declared; who if he were not also God, how shall he
be believed to be in the bosom of the Father? or how, No one has gone up into
heaven, except he who came down from heaven, the Son of man who is in heaven?
For he had not yet gone up bodily into heaven, since even after the
resurrection he said: For I have not yet gone up to my Father. Or how The one
who came down is himself also the one who went up? For he came down in
Godhead, not locally, but by condescension; and he went up bodily, being one,
the same God and man. Or how does the speaking and appearing man say: I and
the Father are one? For it is not easy for a mere man to utter such voices,
unless he were also God consubstantial with the Father.
ιδʹ. And if he should not be known in two natures, how would the same be at
once mortal and immortal, created and =96.616= uncreated, visible and
invisible, perfect God and perfect man? For these would not be properties of
one nature. For the created is not the same by nature as the uncreated, nor
the mortal as the immortal, nor the visible as the invisible, nor Godhead as
humanity.


ιεʹ. And how would Christ be of one composite nature, when the Father and the
Spirit are contemplated in a simple nature? And how will he be consubstantial
with the Father and with us according to the same nature, unless someone
should say that the Father also is consubstantial with us? Which is more
absurd than every monstrous thought. And how does the same say: He who has
seen me has seen the Father? and again: Why do you seek to kill me, a man, who
have spoken the truth to you? For he who has seen him as man only has not seen
God and the Father.
ιϛʹ. And if we should say that his energy is one, where shall we place the
bodily walking, the breaking of the loaves, the uttered word, and such things,
which are not of divine nature, but of human energy? For these came about not
in figure, but in truth of nature.
ιζʹ. And if we should suppose him devoid of natural and self-determining will
according to the human, where shall we place the natural desire of food and of
the rest, I mean of sleep, and of drink, and of such things? For these, being
moved by a law of nature, lead the irrational soul by authority toward
fulfillment; whence also the impulse toward the act accompanies the appetite;
for nature leads the irrational, wherefore they are also not accountable,
subject to no manner of punishment; but those to whom reason is present, being
moved self-determinately, rather lead nature, having by authority both to
follow and to hold back the appetite. For natural will is a rational and
self-determining appetite. And I say these things of those who keep what is
according to nature, since those who have been driven toward what is contrary
to nature from carelessness are rather led than lead, the passions having
acquired habit as a pasture of mastery.
ιηʹ. And by what will shall we say that having come into a house, and having
wished that no one know, he was not able to be hidden? For the divine will is
everywhere clear as all-powerful, but the human is surely weak and inactive.
And how, declining his own will, did he pray for the fulfillment of the
paternal will? Of what nature is praying proper? Of the created, clearly. Of
this then also is the will that is declined. Thus one =96.617= Christ is, Son
and Lord in two perfect natures, and in their natural properties, undividedly
and at the same time unconfusedly willing and doing the things of each nature
with the communion of the other, according to the unconfused perichoresis of
the natures in one another, the one of the Holy Trinity, the Son of God, and
for us having become Son of man.
ιθʹ. For since through a man death came, it was necessary that resurrection be
granted through a man. Since an intellective soul by self-determining will
worked the transgression, it was necessary that an intellective soul by
natural and self-determining will work the obedience of the Creator, and that
salvation return, through those things by which death had banished life, that
death might not think to tyrannize man.
κʹ. What then from this? Having baited man with hope of Godhead, he is baited
by a pretext of flesh, and death, having tasted a sinless body, was nauseated,
and the wretched one vomited all the food in his entrails. For God the Word
who created us, having taken up all our things for us, and having been tried
in our passions according to likeness without sin, having fulfilled the law,
and having been shown alone among men sinless, and therefore not liable to
death, and having published the power of the divine essence through the
miracles, at last comes willingly to the passion saving of all the world for
us, and gives himself, a curse for us


becoming, not being himself a curse, but rather blessing and sanctification,
but receiving our curse, he is crucified for us, and dies, and is buried. And
cursed is everyone who hangs upon a tree, says the Scripture. And: You are
earth, and to earth you shall go, God said to Adam. He becomes therefore a
curse for us, that we may receive the blessing. For as many as, he says,
received him, he gave them authority to become children of God, to those who
believe in him.
καʹ. O the wonder! he who shepherds Israel, as God before the ages, is
delivered to death by an Israelite hand as man. He who leads Israel as a
sheep, as a harmless lamb has been led to be sacrificed, and he brings a tree
of life against the tree of knowledge, and by a taste of gall mixed with
vinegar, he banished the disease that had fallen upon nature from the sweet
eating. He who sits upon the Cherubim as God, hangs upon a cross as one
judged, himself being the life of men, which the God-killers, having seen
hanging from the tree, did not believe. For they shut =96.620= their eyes, and
with their ears they heard heavily. He who fashioned man with divine hands,
all the day stretched out the undefiled hands toward a people disobedient and
contradicting, and he commits his soul to the paternal palms. And a side is
pierced with a spear of him who created Eve from the side of Adam, and he
wells forth divine blood and water, a drink of immortality, and a baptism of
refashioning. Whence the sun was ashamed, not bearing to see the intelligible
sun of righteousness being outraged. Earth was shaken, being sprinkled with
the Master's blood, shaking off the stain of idolatrous gore, and leaping with
joy at the purification. Dead rose from the tombs, prefiguring the rising of
him who was being put to death for us. The sun was quenched, and was kindled
again, creating the three-day numbering of the Master. The veil of the temple
was divided, clearly signaling the entering of the inner shrines, and the
manifestation of the hidden things. For a thief was about to tread paradise,
and the man being put to death as an evildoer, God to be believed and
worshipped by all creation; for he is truly God; and the earthly body
completed from dust to become higher than heavens, and to sit with God; and
the knowledge of the holy, and blessed, and much-hymned Trinity to be
manifested.
κβʹ. He who breathed into Adam a spirit of life, and made him into a living
soul, is laid dead, breathless in the tomb. He who condemned man to be turned
back to earth, has become numbered with those forgotten in the earth. Brazen
gates are crushed, and iron bars are broken; everlasting gates were lifted,
and the gatekeeper of Hades shuddered, and the foundations of the inhabited
world were unveiled. For he who is free of sin has been reckoned among the
dead; and he who loosed the wrappings of Lazarus is wrapped in grave-bands,
that he may loose from the bonds the man put to death by sin, and tightly
bound by its cords, and may let him go free. Now the king of glory has come
down to the tyrant, the mighty in war, whose goings forth toward us are from
the end of heaven, who ran as a giant the path in life. For he wrestles with
this by the weakness of humanity, and binds the strong one as a sparrow, his
bodyguards having become gone by the self-powerful Godhead, and he makes the
snatching of his vessels, leading up well what that one led down badly. Now
the Word goes down to the dragon, Leviathan, the apostate (and Leviathan is
interpreted dragon), the great mind =96.621= of the Assyrians, I mean of the
opposing powers, lurking in the heart of the earth; and he draws this one with
the hook of the Godhead, with the hidden body as with a kind of worm, and
those whom


he swallowed, having been very strong, he forces most pitiably to vomit,
sending away empty the one who boasts in wealth. The child born and given to
us, having gone down upon a hole of asps, throttles, and kills and destroys
the haughty and vaunting one. Now Hades has become heaven, and the underworld
is filled with light, and the darkness that formerly pursued is driven out,
and looking up is given to the blind. For upon those sitting in darkness and
shadow of death, a dayspring from on high has appeared. These things prophets,
and patriarchs, and righteous men clearly prefigured and foretold.
κγʹ. The righteous one is bound, as unserviceable. For they have counseled a
counsel that begets destruction against themselves, those who harvest the
people of the Lord, and disturb the paths of their feet. Woe to their soul!
evil according to their works shall happen to them, Isaiah says; but for us
the deed has already become truly pain-loosing and pain-healing. I give my
back to scourges, and my cheeks to slaps, and I endure the shame of spittings,
said the one speaking in Isaiah. Wherefore the fashioning of my hands shall
not be put to shame, shall not be ashamed. For I have not been reckoned to
have seized being equal to God; but being God consubstantial with the Father,
I let myself down toward emptying, and for the ungodly I am humbled toward
death, the Father having so been well pleased. For what he wills, I will,
being a co-willer with him, and a sharer of the Godhead. And I the Most High
am exalted, and in me humanity having been glorified (for thus I establish the
paternal love), that being justified in my blood, and having been granted
reconciliation toward my Father in my death, they may live in the life, and
may rest under my wings, the sheep kept for me.
κδʹ. Let us understand, we who have not seen, and let us believe by the
hearing of those who announce peace. For to us the arm of God, his all-working
power, is unveiled. For we shall be glorified if we understand, and we shall
be greatly glorified, mirroring the glory of the Lord in cheapness, and
perceiving in the inglorious form the beauty beyond mind. For even if he has
suffered from weakness, yet he lives from the power of God. If he is seen
hanging upon a tree, uncomely and inglorious failing beyond all the sons of
men, yet he himself is the radiance of glory, which earth seeing was ashamed
and mourned. This one bore our sins upon the tree, and he felt our bruise, and
he is in pain concerning us, and he bore wound, and toil, and ill-treatment,
the =96.624= exchanges of our peace. For since we wandered as sheep, turning
aside from the way of the Lord, and finishing our own path, through bowels of
mercy he has been delivered for our sins; not striving, nor crying out,
leaving us a pattern, and as a voiceless sheep he is led to death for us. You
shall see, says Moses, your life hanging before your eyes, and you shall not
believe in your life. David the ancestor of God, or rather through David the
Lord of David, proclaiming beforehand his own passion, and the life-begetting
burial, said: They divided my garments among themselves, and upon my clothing
they cast a lot. He who clothed the first fathers of the race with tunics of
skin, willingly is stripped toward crucifixion, that having stripped us of
mortality he may clothe us with the comeliness of incorruption. And he assigns
the robe by lot to the soldiers. For he is about, rising from the dead, to
send to the nations those whom he himself chose as disciples, and himself to
become a wrapping of divine baptism for the faithful; For as many as, he says,
were baptized into Christ, have put on Christ.
κεʹ. Noah, being shut in an ark, and saving by wood the seeds of a second
world, and becoming again a beginning of the race, typed Christ willingly


being buried, who washed away sin by the blood that flowed from his side with
water, and by the wood of the cross saved our whole race, and became a leader
of a common life, and of a new polity. Abraham the great patriarch, leading
Isaac up to whole-burnt offering, the one from promise, and in whom are the
promises, clearly announced beforehand the slaughter of the Master. For Isaac
himself is granted living to the father from God; but a ram held by the horns
in a plant of Sabek became a victim; and the mystery of the double, of both
the ram and Isaac, becomes a true type of Christ our God. For this one is
double and composite, being the same God and man, inasmuch as he is by nature
Son of God and God, he remained in impassibility; but inasmuch as he himself
became man beyond man, he offered himself for the world as an unblemished
victim in a plant of Sabek to the Father, that is, in a wood of remission. For
Sabek is interpreted remission. What does it mean that he became of the three
measures of fine flour a hidden cake? does it not clearly hint at the
three-day burial of the bread of life? And what the pit of Joseph formerly,
and the guard later? does it not show a tomb, and the guard upon it, most
clearly? For they set me, he says, in the lowest pit, in dark places, and in
shadow of death. And what Moses the God-seer and lawgiver? is he not hidden in
the ark, given over toward death, and taken up by the queen? Thus =96.625=
also Christ is covered in a tomb, being put to death bodily, but by his own
Godhead that reigns over all creation is led up again to life. And does not
Moses himself again strike the sea with a rod, and by the double of the blow,
the upright and the transverse, signal the figure of the cross, and go down
toward the deep, showing the Savior's descent toward Hades, and put to death
Pharaoh who pursues, and save Israel? Since Christ also put death to death,
and saves all who believe in him. And by the stretchings of the hands, turning
Amalek, and making Israel trophy-bearing, he prefigures the same mystery of
the Savior. The paradox of the manna also amazes my mind. For just as it was
hidden only on the evening of the Sabbath, so Jesus my God, and for me man,
all sweetness, and all desire, at the end of Preparation is covered in the
tomb. And Jonah, did not the Lord himself foretell as a type of himself? For
just as, he says, Jonah spent in the belly of the sea-monster three days and
three nights, so must the Son of man also spend in the heart of the earth
three days, and three nights.
κϛʹ. But someone will say: If on Preparation he endured the voluntary death,
and came to life again on the one of the Sabbaths, how shall the spending
three nights in the heart of the earth be saved? But the divine Moses speaks
thus to us, that God called the light day, and he called the darkness night.
When then the Lord was hung upon the undefiled cross, darkness came upon the
whole inhabited world, not of a cloud standing in front, and covering the
solar ray, not of the lunar body walling, as of some partition, and not
allowing the radiance to reach us; for thus those skilled in these things say
that solar eclipses come about; but of darkness having seized all the earth,
far dimmer than the palpable one of the plague of the Egyptians. For this
welling illuminating power of the solar body failed. For it was necessary that
all creation mourn the bodily death of the Creator. Wherefore also the prophet
says: The sun shall set at midday, and the light shall grow dark in the day.
And another again: In that day there shall not be light. And not day, and not
night, and toward evening there shall be light. In this darkness then, the
divine and all-holy soul of the Lord =96.628= of the sacred and


life-giving body having been divided, sojourned in the heart of the earth,
which is reckoned as a night. Then after the darkness again a day is
improvised by the creator, the sun having run again toward its own figure;
wherefore also the prophet foretold that there would be light toward evening;
then the night before the Sabbath, and the Sabbath, and that before the one of
the Sabbaths, and itself the bright and light-bearing day of holy Sunday, on
which the uncreated light comes forth bodily from the tomb, as a bridegroom
fair with the beauty of the resurrection; for the end of the Sabbaths, which
the evangelist calls late of Sabbaths, becomes a beginning of the one of the

But let us return again, from where we departed.
κζʹ. He who fashioned man from dust is brought down into dust of death, and
his life is taken from the earth. For he makes the putting away of the earthly
things, I do not mean of a body, but of the things of the body, of sleep and
toil, of hunger and thirst, of cutting and of flowing. For these entered into
our life through the disobedience. And his burial comes in peace, which he
procured for us through cross and burial, having joined the things that stood
apart, and having subjected the apostate man to the one who fashioned him.
Wherefore the wicked instead of his burial have been delivered to destruction;
Jews, after the sack of the temple and of the city, being led away as
spear-won by the enemies, no longer to run again to their own; for they are
left deserted of the divine help after this voice of the Lord, Your house is
left deserted; and demons having been stripped of the tyrannical and haughty
rule, which they ruled over us, evil ones evilly, having mastered us by the
most shameful passions. But he has inherited the spoils of the wicked, those
who have died from the ages, having set free those who were held by the yoke
of sin. For having been numbered among the lawless, he planted good law; and
the seed of the disobedient has become unto destruction, and mourning has been
exchanged from feasts, and dirges have echoed against hymns. But for us light
has risen from darkness, and life has come forth from a tomb, and from Hades
wells resurrection, and joy, and gladness, and exultation. κηʹ. And it is not
out of place, having touched also the evangelical sayings, to draw the wealth
in them. The evangelists who spoke the divine things by the Spirit, and having
Christ speaking in themselves, say: When evening had come, there came a rich
man from Arimathea, the name Joseph, a reputable counselor, a good and just
man, who also himself had become a disciple of Jesus, and was awaiting the
kingdom of God. This man had not consented to their deed, but because of the
fear =96.629= of the Jews he was hidden. This man having dared went in to
Pilate, and asked for the body of Jesus. O blessed and celebrated man! Trulyhe
says the Lord: Every tree is known from its own fruits. Being therefore good
and just, he had not consented to their counsel and deed. Truly this is a
blessed man, as God-speaking David says. For he did not walk in counsel of
ungodly, nor standing in a way of sinners did he rage, and he has not sat upon
a seat of pestilent men against the Lord, and against his Christ, having
counseled with lawless peoples. He did not say: Take, take, crucify him. He
did not utter the voice of the cowards, drawing upon himself and his own
offspring the penalty of the innocent and divine blood; but having his will in
the law of the Lord, and lying down and rising in his law always meditating,
he irrigated his own mind with the God-flowing streams of the Spirit. For he
had become a pupil of the good teacher, and he followed his tracks. O rich
man, in choice! O wise merchant, who snatched the heavenly wealth with the
earthly, and hid the treasure of life in himself


having hidden! And Pilate, he says, wondered if he had already died. He
wondered how the life has died, how the steward of the breath of men has given
over the breath. Yes, he has died, O Pilate, but willingly. For he has
authority to lay down his own soul, and he has authority again to take it.
Yes, he has died, that he may spoil death, that he may make alive the one held
greatly by fetters, that having become firstborn from the dead, he may well
forth resurrection to the dead, that the mortal may be swallowed by the life.
κθʹ. He therefore grants the body to Joseph. O unrestrained daring and
boldness, which faith and divine longing begot! The disciples having become
partakers of divine gifts, being contracted by fear are hidden, and you long
for a dead man, you imitated more quickly the teacher your leader and Lord.
For by choice you have given over your own soul to death. You did not bear to
see naked the holy body of the Lord, that which was united according to
hypostasis to the Godhead. You touched the divine coal, whose type the
Seraphim have not been able to touch. O blessed hands! O all-blissful arms,
with which having held the body of my God, you wrapped it in clean linen and
costly myrrhs! For thus, he says, it is custom for the Jews to bury. And there
was, he says, in the place where he was crucified, a garden, and in the garden
a new tomb, in which no one had yet been laid. There then because of the
Preparation of the Jews, because the tomb was near, they laid Jesus; which he
had hewn in the rock; and having rolled a great stone to the door, he went up.
Preparation then is said, as a kind of pre-arrangement =96.632= toward the
rest of the Sabbath. For the Sabbath had a ceasing of works, God having
ordained for the Hebrews to touch no servile thing at all; but for us
Preparation toward sin, and the hardships from it, the divine passion has
become. In clean linen then is wrapped the only clean and undefiled, he who
wraps the heaven in clouds, he who puts on light as a garment is laid in a
tomb, he who has heaven as a throne, and the earth a footstool, he who fills
and circumscribes all things, the only one uncircumscribed as God, and having
grasped creation in a handful, is circumscribed bodily in a tomb. Himself as
God worshipped in heaven with Father and with the Spirit, and himself as man
lying bodily in a tomb, and in soul lodging in the recesses of Hades, and
making paradise enterable for the thief, the uncircumscribed Godhead
accompanying everywhere. For even if the sacred soul was divided from the
life-giving and undefiled body, yet the Godhead of the Word after the
conception in the womb of the holy Virgin and Theotokos Mary, of an
inseparable union of the two natures having come about according to
hypostasis, has remained inseparable from both, I mean of the soul, and of the
body; and thus one hypostasis of Christ remained even in death itself, the
soul and the body subsisting in the hypostasis of God the Word, and after
death possessing this hypostasis. Therefore every knee shall bow of things in
heaven, and on earth, and under the earth, and every tongue shall confess,
that Jesus Christ is Lord to the glory of the Father.
λʹ. And why is he laid in a new tomb, in which a dead man had not yet been
laid? It seems to me, that the rising of someone of those laid of old may not
be suspected. For they are ready for mischief who begrudge their own
salvation, and most sharp toward unbelief. In order then that the resurrection
of the Lord may be shown manifest and very clear, he alone is buried in a new
and empty tomb, the spiritual rock of life, from which those who followed
drank, the ungrateful; the stone, the cornerstone and uncut by hand, is
covered in a rock that has been hewn. For easily-broken souls and
easily-poured toward pleasures do not bear


to receive the divine Word, but rather those firm, and disposed manfully
toward virtue.
λαʹ. And on the morrow, he says, which was after the Preparation, the chief
priests and Pharisees were gathered to Pilate, saying. Again the council of
lawlessness was gathered. Those who kill the prophets, and stone those sent to
them; those who terribly devour the vineyard of the Lord, the =96.633=
Israelite people I mean, and having put to death the heir Son with hatred
toward the servants. For they did not know that he would be heir of all
creation, as man. For if they had known, they would not have crucified the
Lord of glory. And what do they say to Pilate? Lord, we remembered that that
deceiver said while still living: After three days I rise. O the customary
lawlessness of the idolaters! The deceivers, and fellow-partakers of the
deceit, call the one under treaty of deceit, the slave of sin, lord, and they
name the Savior of all and Lord, the true truth, the wisdom and power of the
Father, the true light that enlightens every man coming into the world, a
deceiver. Why do you say formerly, while still living? Does he not live after
death, he who is cause of life to the living, and of being to those that are?
He was among the dead, but living, as free. Did you never hear in Jonah the
prophet the Lord saying: Yet three days, and Nineveh shall be overthrown? For
the deceit shall be overthrown, when the Lord is raised on the third day from
the tomb, and righteousness and truth shall be planted. Command therefore,
they say, that the tomb be made secure until the third day. Why are you
troubled in vain, wretched ones? Why do you fear a fear, of which there is no
fear by nature? A seal will not hold the uncircumscribed. If the seals of the
pit did not hold back the entrance for Habakkuk, they would not have turned
aside Daniel, being a prophet of God and a servant; how shall the seals be
able to hold the common Master? But truly evil is blind, and is turned more
quickly. Lest his disciples coming steal him, and say to the people, that he
was raised from the dead; and the last deceit will be worse than the first.
Who steals a dead man, O foolish? Those who dig tombs perhaps steal the
clothing of the departed; who has ever stolen a dead man? for if he should not
rise, and should lie in the prediction of the rising, how will the disciples
cling to him in vain? For every dead man is forgotten from the heart of
mortals, said the Scripture. And if by deceivers, how will he not rather be
forgotten? And how will they draw upon themselves deaths and afflictions of
every kind, and a heap of evils, as indeed they did draw, clinging to a dead
deceiver? Truly you ascribe to the true things the deceit you are considering.
λβʹ. But Pilate: You have a guard; go, he says, make it secure as you know.
And they having gone made the tomb secure, having sealed the stone with the
guard. Pilate flees the communion of the God-killers, knowing that he found no
cause in him and he gives over to them the whole of the outrage. As you know,
he says, make it secure. Let no contradiction be =96.636= left to you toward
the resurrection, I have entrusted to you yourselves the seal and the guard.
Do not then, when the predictions of the deceiver, as you yourselves say, are
shown true by the outcome of the resurrection, have terrible pretenses, and
false marvels. Now it stands upon a razor's edge. Now there is judgment, as he
himself said. For if he himself is lifted up from earth rising, you the rulers
will be cast out, and he himself will draw all to himself. These are Pilate's
words. But the shameless and ungrateful Jews, having rushed upon the tomb like
dogs, seal the stone. He therefore lies dead in a tomb, guarded by watchers
and a seal, he who created the abyss and sealed it; he who set sand as a bound
for the sea, as


a lion he rested, as a lion's cub he slept, as a king sleeping being guarded.
Who will raise him? Now I will arise, says the Lord, now I will be lifted up,
now I will be glorified; now you shall see, now you shall perceive.
λγʹ. But the women, being kindled, being burned with the love of the Teacher,
seeing all these things without covering, and being urgent to run risk before
the Lord, conquered the boldness of the apostles, not bearing to be outdone.
So where sin abounded, grace superabounded. For it was necessary that through
those by whom death was ministered, through these the resurrection also be
served. And why then do the apostles hide? It was necessary that the most
faithful witnesses of the divine economy be kept, who would be heralds to the
nations, as having been eyewitnesses and ministers of the divine mysteries.
And why is Peter also allowed to fall toward denial, this one being a zealot
and most exact guardian of the goods? He was about to be entrusted with the
rudders of the Church. In order then that he might become forgiving to those
returning from transgression, he is allowed providentially to slip the denial,
being trained by his own slip to be compassionate. But the women, being filled
like myrrh with the divine grace, and having drawn by longing Christ the myrrh
emptied for our renewal, him who was emptied divinely and anointed, and was
anointed bodily; for both the anointing and the anointed came together into
the completion of one person and one hypostasis; pursuing this one always by
love, and running unceasingly into the scent of his myrrhs, they buy myrrhs
again, and hurry toward the tomb. Wherefore they also first obtain the divine
resurrection, the just judge measuring the grace corresponding to the
eagerness.
λδʹ. Let us therefore also become like wise servants awaiting the arrival of
the master of the house, =96.637= who having received the talent, let us
multiply this with all strength; that as good slaves and faithful stewards we
may obtain the joy of the Lord. And a talent seems to me to be every gift
given to men from the divine goodness. There is therefore no one who is
altogether without a share of the divine gifts, but one is useful toward this
virtue, and one toward more, and another toward fewer; one toward high and
surpassing ones, and another toward humble and lower. To each God has
portioned a measure of faith. The powerful then shall be examined powerfully,
and from him to whom they entrusted much, they will demand much. For each is
demanded according to the measure of the power entrusted to him from God. And
he who gives knows to whom he gives, and all things are naked and laid open to
his eyes. Let us hasten therefore with all power to multiply the talent. Let
him who received the five apportion five others to the one who gave; and two,
he who has been entrusted with the two. Let one stretch a practical hand to
those who need mercy, and who labor under the burden of poverty, whoever
received this grace; and let another feed with word those wasting in famine
and dried by the burning of unbelief. Let us acquire friends from the mammon
of injustice, and let us fill the bellies of the poor, that as approachable
orators they may speak for us before the bench full of horror. Let us give
hospitality to the homeless; let us clothe the naked; let us visit the sick;
let us not hesitate to move the foot toward those in prison; let us stretch
the hand to those in distresses and griefs. Let us suffer together; let us
pour a compassionate tear; for it will quench the unending fire of Gehenna. If
we accomplish these things with a thirsting heart, the Lord will say also to
us: Come, you blessed of my Father, inherit the kingdom prepared for you.
λεʹ. You who have been called, let us be adorned brightly with a wedding
garment, that we may become partakers of the


divine wedding, and may be known worthy of the calling back, and may partake
of the fatted calf, and may share of the lamb of the Pascha, and may be filled
with the new produce of the vine, now indeed flesh of God from wheat, and
blood of God from wine, truly by the invocation and unspeakably being changed;
for the one who promised is with=96.640=out falsehood; eating and drinking
with a clean conscience, and by these sevenfold, as some gold being purified,
and being washed of the counterfeit of sin, and being bequeathed incorruption,
and being joined to God, and being deified, and delighting in his
participation, when a trumpet of the Lord shall sound, and the dead shall
rise, when a tribunal shall be seated, and on a day on which God has appointed
to judge the inhabited world in righteousness; when all things shall receive
their own restoration, no other restoration being left; when fire shall
consume the adversaries inconsumably, and the dwellings prepared before the
ages shall receive the righteous, and the bosom of Abraham, the God Word and
Lord incarnate from the bosom, I mean, of Abraham; when the earth of the
ungodly shall fall, and gloom shall receive them, and a dark fire which they
kindled for themselves, and a worm wearing them unrestingly, and weeping mixed
with the gnashing of the teeth; but the earth of the meek shall receive those
straight in heart, and having unblemished faith, bright, shining brightly as
the sun; when of the virgins, the foolish shall be most mindlessly troubled,
seeking at no time the things of the time, and hastening to adorn the lamps
that are being quenched with foreign oil, for whom the bridechamber shall be
shut. and a voice of the impartial one from within shall sound sharp: I do not
know you. And to say the same: I do not love you; for you have not loved my
brothers; you have not shown yourselves symbols of love; you have not moved
bowels of compassions toward them. Wherefore judgment is without mercy to him
who has not done mercy. For by nothing, as by mercy, am I the compassionate
one served; Nothing knows how to seize my heart, except mercy. For I will
mercy, and not sacrifice. You did not open a door of mercy to those in need,
nor shall I spread for you the entrance of my palaces. These are the tillages
of the mindless. But the prudent were prepared, and were not troubled. For
having filled their lamps with virtues of every kind, and having watered these
ungrudgingly with the oil of well-doing, and having kindled them with the
illuminating fire of orthodox faith, bright, brightly having met the
bridegroom at midnight, they dance in the divine bridechamber, and enjoy the
gladness there, being always joined intellectually to the undefiled, and being
counted worthy to converse purely with the pure.
λϛʹ. And you therefore, O divine and sacred flock of the great shepherd, and
priest, and victim, a people of possession, a royal priesthood, who have grown
rich with the common name as slaves of Christ, remembering his passions, and
words, and deeds, let us keep his life-bearing commandments. For he said:
Believe in God, and believe in me. And another: The Father will send you
another Paraclete, =96.641= the Spirit of truth, who proceeds from the Father.
Believing then, let us love him eagerly. For he who loves me, he says, shall
be loved by my Father, and I will love him, and I will manifest myself to him.
λζʹ. Let us therefore hate his enemies. Everyone who does not confess Christ
Son of God and Lord is antichrist. If anyone should say that Christ is a
slave, let us shut our ears, knowing that he is a liar, and truth is not in
him. Let us bear his reproach, as a diadem of glory. For blessed, he says, are
you, when they reproach you, and persecute, and say every evil word against
you for my sake. Rejoice, and be glad, that your wage is great in the heavens.
Thus


we shall turn aside from the mindlessness of the foolish, and we shall emulate
the wisdom of the prudent.
ληʹ. And let us become ready also toward the resurrection of the Lord from the
dead. Let us not prepare for the belly the things toward luxury. Let us not
heap many wrappings upon a body, not useless costliness of myrrhs, not revels
and drunkennesses, and their yokefellows beds and wantonnesses; let us not
think great against the poor; let us not kindle a desire of empty glory, and
spit out the sober bits of desire; let us not become mare-mad horses, raging
disorderly upon others' vessels; let us not sow into the flesh the kindlings
of the fire; let us not be stolen by the polluted bait of gold and silver, and
costly stones; let not, while others hunger, a table full for us raise the
untamed worm of Gehenna. For behold the Bridegroom arises; behold the King of
those who reign comes forth from a tomb. Let us be prepared, that we may not
be without a share of the joy. Let us become approved money-changers,
discerning the better from the worse, and let us burn the one with fire of
divine zeal and love, and let us store the other in our own treasuries. Let us
chasten anger and desire; let us wither the appetite of the belly; let us gird
ourselves with temperance through self-control and a humble heart; let us cast
down conceit through cheapness and memory of death; let us become all things
to all; let us bear insult nobly; being wronged let us rejoice in the hope.
For the fashion of this world is passing, and all that is now seen is going
by.
λθʹ. Upon all let this be minded in us, which also in Christ Jesus, who loved
us being enemies, and having loved had mercy, and having had mercy humbled
himself, and having humbled saved. For from love comes forth mercy, and from
mercy humility, and from humility salvation and exaltation. If we so live as
citizens, now we shall be freed from the griefs that lie upon us. If we throw
=96.644= off the yoke of the passions, and we shall shake off the yoke-collar
of the tyrants, and just as from the better things the grievous things came,
so also from the grievous the more useful will return, and we shall leave
behind the ancient boldness, and we shall feast purely to the Lord God a feast
of going out, and we shall be set free from the blasphemous voice that is
lifted against the one who created, and the polity of the Church shall be in
peace; and then with bright lamps we shall meet brightly the conqueror of
death, the immortal bridegroom, and the undefiled bridechamber shall receive
us, and with unveiled face we shall mirror the glory of the Lord, and we shall
delight in his beauty, with whom to the Father with Holy Spirit be glory,
honor, worship and magnificence, both now and always, and unto the ages of
ages. Amen.
