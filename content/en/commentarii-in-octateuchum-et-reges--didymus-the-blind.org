* Commentaries on the Octateuch and Kings

=39.1112= A catena of fifty-one commentaries on the Octateuch and the
Books of Kings, now for the first time issued in print, at the request of
the most pious and most serene prince of all Ungrovlachia, Lord Lord
Gregory Alexander Ghika; and by the care of Nikephoros the hieromonk
Theotokes. At Leipzig in Saxony, in the printing house of Breitkopf. In
the year 1772.
Ch. 1, v. 14. And God said, Let there be lights in the firmament of the
heaven for the illumination of the earth, to divide between the day and
between the night. Of Didymus. Whence also the lights, having come to be
on the fourth day, because of the account concerning them, inasmuch as
they are greater, are said to have been generated on this day. For the
tetrad, being in potency a decad, is honorable; for monad and dyad and
triad and tetrad, added to one another, make the decad; for throughout
all Scripture the decad is praised; for Moses also gave ten words, and
tithes are offered. And just as the water, formerly spread out over the
face of all the earth, God later on the third day gathered into one
gathering and made the seas and the rivers and the wells; so also the
light, which was created in the beginning, formerly spread out, God later
on the fourth day gathered all at once and made from it the sun and the
moon and the remaining stars. Not that the sun is something other than
the day. Ch. 3, v. 19. Thorns and thistles shall it bring forth for you,
and you shall eat the herb of the field. Of Didymus. For what that
follows from the body is not painful, pleasures and cares growing out of
it? For the Savior indicated that these are the thorns, which choke the
seed called by him, if the one who received it does not receive it into
depth. V. 25. And he stationed the cherubim, and the flaming sword that
turns about, to guard the way of the tree of life. Of Didymus. The sword
is called turning, so that when the human being has turned from vice to
virtue, it too may turn, granting the entrance. If then a desire for the
entrance should arise in anyone, he is led by the hand through these; the
cherubim hinting at knowledge =39.1116= our glory, then you will follow,
as a servant follows a master. V. 23. And I will take away the hand, and
then you shall see the things behind me; but my face shall not be seen by
you. Of Didymus. The pre-cosmic existence and divinity is indicated
through the face; but the creation and providence, through the things
behind (1). Ch. 34, v. 2. And be ready toward morning, and you shall go
up in the morning to Mount Sinai, and you shall stand for me there on the
top of the mountain. Of Didymus. For he stands not for any other, but for
God, for whom he will complete the progress that has been indicated. And
what is it to stand for God, except to become ready to hear the teaching
of God? V. 3. And let no one go up with you, and let no one be seen on
the whole mountain; and let the sheep and the oxen not graze near the
mountain. Of Didymus. He commanded those who fall short of the perfection
of Moses to remain below the mountain.
