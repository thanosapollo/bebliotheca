PSALM 131. The Lord swore truth to David. And to David who had sworn, God
swore in return when he sought to learn the place, giving as a gift even that
he should come forth from him: In order that through two unchangeable things,

prayed such things to know, wishing to learn concerning the place of the
Saviour's arrival among men, receive then yourself also the promise, and
become a minister of the saving generation. For yours will be the fruit that
is to come, coming forth from your succession and from your seed; wherefore
also to him will be given as it were the promised throne, because no other
than only the one who will come forth from you is worthy of the throne

the throne of David his father, and he will reign over the house of Jacob
forever. If your sons keep my covenant. Upon it, namely the throne of Christ,

sons, O David, that is those of the circumcision, both if they receive Christ,
and if they keep my covenant and my testimonies, not those given through the
first legislation, but these which I will teach them, which are going to be
handed over to them later sometime through the new covenant; for if =24.28=
they keep these, they too will sleep upon your throne prepared for Christ.
Blessing I will bless her hunt, her poor I will fill with bread. And according
to the anagoge, the hunt of Zion is those who like fish through

the evangelic and apostolic nets are caught day by day; and her poor are the
blessed, because theirs is the kingdom of the heavens; who eat the bread that
came down from heaven; but also her priests, having first clothed themselves
with righteousness, as he said, are clothed by God with salvation; then he
speaks concerning the holy ones, those who excel in virtue and life in the
Church; concerning whom Aquila said: Praising they will praise; and Symmachus,
Speaking well they will speak well. And upon him my sanctification will

That one was the lamp that burns and shines. And these things can also be seen
otherwise according to the times of the persecutions; and Symmachus said, his
sanctification; for the sanctification of our Saviour in the Church sanctifies
throughout the whole inhabited world.

PSALM 132. Behold now what is good or what is pleasant, but that they dwell,
etc. Not everything that is good is already also pleasant; nor is the pleasant
in every way also good; for many things are sweet, yet base; as also others
are toilsome, yet good; but concord and peace of souls that are of one mind
and of one doctrine is praised as having both at once both the pleasant and
the good; not that brothers dwell simply, but when they are upon the same,
according to the Apostle saying: That you all say the same, and that there be
no schisms among you; but that you be fitted together in the same mind and in
the same judgment; and he teaches that the good and the pleasant are not to be
set in the body, nor in pleasure of fleshes. As dew of Hermon that comes down
upon the mountains of Zion. Hermon is a mountain lying beside Lebanon, upon
which much snow is gathered, which the Word named dew; and since that dew,
gathered from many drops, is embodied into one and compacted, he reasonably
likened the concord and agreement of the Church through another example not to
the snow that remains on mount Hermon, but to that which comes down upon the
mountains of Zion; for in order that we may understand the multitude of the
snow, mount Hermon has been taken; but in order that he may show the agreement
of the citizenship according to God, he said that the dew comes down upon the
mountains of Zion. Because there the Lord commanded the blessing. There he
says among the brothers dwelling upon the same; and life not the common, but
the immortal.

=24.29= PSALM 133. May the Lord bless you from Zion. Already it has been said
to Abraham: Those who bless you have been blessed; and he who does this
blesses himself in power; and if in the case of Abraham, how much more in the
case of the Lord? for he does not need the blessing from us. And each grants a
favor to himself, and blesses himself, being blessed in return by the Maker of
all. Wherefore first it has been said: Bless the Lord; then next: May the Lord
bless you.

PSALM 134. Having the same words as the last of the odes, the present praise
reasonably lies beside it; wherefore the alleluia has been prefixed; allelu
signifying praise, and ia the Lord. And one might say that the first parts are
a hymn, as in a prologue stirring up those commanded to praise God; beginning
the praise from,

Lord, your name is forever. And he catechizes turning them from idols, and
teaches knowledge of God. For of such an exhortation those now coming from the
nations had need; whom he also calls servants, as not yet having taken up the
spirit of adoption. And it happens that these, before the bath of
regeneration, have a station in the house of God of his vestibules, but have
not obtained the innermost things; for they had an introduction, but not
perfection. Different indeed are the courts, since the lives of those being
introduced are also different; and it is of progress, after praising the name
of the Lord, to praise the Lord himself; for one must first learn that there
is a Word of God, set over the whole world, and mastering it; then learn
concerning his essence and power, and know the only-begotten Son of God.

him. And the cause of praising is that the good is not by participation, but

PSALM 135. To him who alone made great wonders. And it is wonderful even
according to the letter, if heaven by sight alone shows the wisdom of God, by
greatness and beauty and long-abiding stay; and earth washed round from every
side by the waters and keeping its own constitution; and to all the psalmist
joined the eternal mercy, since God created all things not through his own
need but through philanthropy alone. To him who struck Egypt. If you are not
persuaded, he says, that by God's providence and power the whole world and its
parts are governed, and you hold in doubt lest somehow the particular things
and human things are overlooked by him, know that he provides for things on
earth no less than for those according to heaven; he goes after, at any rate,
at times those of men who are excessively godless and impious, as once he went
after the Egyptians and the Egyptian land itself. And again he stands before
and fights for those dear to God, and not only for them, but also for their
descendants, as for those then freed through Moses from the slavery among the
Egyptians, whom the God over all himself freed, not otherwise than by using a
stretched-out hand and a strong arm; and this would be the =24.36= Word of
God, through whom both of old and now the philanthropy of the all-good God has
accomplished the benefactions toward men. Sihon king of the Amorites. And
Amorites are interpreted speakers who embitter, and their king Sihon,
temptation and provocation; for those terrible toward sophistic deceit who
embitter are speakers, wishing those who are lovers of truth to be spoken of
and sung upon deceit, having as their aim to tempt and provoke. And Bashan
being interpreted shame, signifies the

wickedness that puts the wicked to shame; whose king Og, together with
Sihon, is slain; in order that the land which they held badly, spending their
time sophistically and with a wretched habit about the nature of things,
seeming to reign over it, may be given to Israel; but the land so interpreted
having
been taken from them is given for a possession to Israel, that he may inherit
it, being a genuine servant of the cause of all. Because in our humiliation
the Lord remembered us. And in the things above Israel was shown in various
ways, not being the race of the Jews, but such a one by ways of piety; For not
all who are of Israel, these are Israel; nor the circumcision in the open in
flesh, according to the Apostle, but the Jew in secret, and circumcision of
heart not in letter but in spirit; whose praise is not from men, but from God;
and us therefore who are from the nations (for we are the circumcision and
true Israel), he redeemed from our enemies rather than those; for those who
came forth from Egypt escaped the bodily tyrants; but carrying in their own
souls the impious ways, they fell in the wilderness having committed idolatry.
But us he truly redeemed from the enemies of our soul, and he remembered us in
our humiliation rather than those; for they were slaves in bodies to the
Egyptians, but we in the souls themselves were playthings of demons, and we
were humbled under the wicked spirits. And so great is the mercy of God, that
he not only cares for the pious, and takes thought for those who draw near to
him, but also simply once for all makes providence of all on earth, and not of
rational things only but also of irrational, so as to furnish foods to all the
animals on earth, and to each kind to grant the things suitable and the things
fitted to life, and from here again to extend to heaven. Wherefore the word
after saying, Who gives food to all flesh, sends us up to heaven, saying
again: Give thanks to the God of heaven, because his mercy is forever.

PSALM 136. The present psalm is without title among =24.37= the Hebrews, and
neither of whom it is has been prefixed, nor of what kind it happens to be;
but it is a bare prophecy spoken from the person of those who came to be in
Babylon; and this too one will note, that in some of the copies, according to
the Seventy, it has been titled Of Haggai and Zechariah. Blessed is he who
will repay you your recompense! The blessing will come round to those who
render the exchanges to Babylon, and him who dashes their infants against the
rock and destroys them; for indeed in truth, the first blessed thing was never
to have seen Babylon, nor to have come to be in the confusion of her evils;
but if once someone should come to be in her having fallen away from the
better things, he would be blessed if he should do what he has suffered, and
should take the Babylonians captive in return, and should transfer the souls
themselves of the Babylonians to the fear of God. And if he should not permit
their infants to grow, but should crush them against the rock, he would become
more blessed than many. And you would not err in calling infants of Babylon
the seeds of wickedness, and the beginnings of the confusing sins; which
indeed by the saving word (for the rock was Christ) he who is blessed in these
things will be eager to destroy so that they may not grow, nor come forth into
works. PSALM 137. I will give thanks to you, O Lord, with my whole heart. As
the perfect man loves God from whole soul and power and strength, not tearing
the powers of his own love

about some other thing, but wholly hung upon God alone, so also he himself
gives thanks with whole heart, thanking God. And taking courage from such
freedom of speech of his, and knowing that in the great theatre of the life of
men, while divine angels and ministers of God look on, and behold his
progresses in God, he renders the fitting worship to God, and says: And before
angels I will sing to you. I will worship toward your holy temple. He promises
to worship toward a holy temple, showing the Church, or the heavenly
Jerusalem, of which the temple on earth was a type; then he confesses thanks
having been counted worthy of mercy and truth of God. Instead of which
Symmachus says, concerning your mercy and your truth; for having obtained
mercy of God, he has been released from types and symbols, and has been
counted worthy to worship God in spirit and truth. Let all the kings of the
earth give thanks to you, O Lord. We have often observed that of the psalms
those having as a prefix neither hymn, nor ode, nor psalm, or some other
thing, happen to be of another character besides these, of which sort is the
present one sharing in prophetic power; for through the things that went
before David having given thanks, But not I, he says, among kings alone have
been counted worthy of this, but very many such will come to be, namely after
the preaching of the Saviour; and especially when the =24.40= fullness of the
nations comes in, and the second presence of the Saviour comes to be; for then
the great glory of the Lord will be seen; and at times the things to come are
shown in the imperative and in the optative, according to a certain custom of
the Hebrews. He prophesies concerning the knowledge of God that is going to be
spread out into all nations; For there will be, he says, a time when all the
kings of the earth will give thanks to you and will hear all the words of the
mouth of God, and that they will sing in the ways of the Lord. And these
things have obtained fulfillment at no other time than after the evangelical
preaching of our Saviour. And ways are the virtues, which lead to the
knowledge of God. The Lord is high, and he looks upon the low things. David,
having learned the things spoken in him and through him by the prophetic
spirit, takes courage as God looking upon the low things, as shielding with
him in the afflictions. If I walk in the midst of affliction, you will make me
live, etc. And more clearly Symmachus said: If I walk through the midst of
affliction, you will save me round about; against the breath of my enemies you
will stretch out your hand, and your right hand will save me; the Lord will
accomplish for me.

PSALM 138. O Lord, you have tried me, and you have known me. Since therefore
also the time of our life has certain intervals measured with God, all the
time of my life, he says, and the way which I went through, you yourself have
tracked out. Your knowledge was made wonderful from me. It mounts, he says,
upon my power, and the knowledge of these things is beyond my grasp, so that I
am not able to find them out nor to speak them out. For whatever I say happens
to be small and least of the divine power, whose grasp flees my smallness and
nothingness. Your eyes saw my unworked thing. What has not yet been done by
me, he says, you behold as already completed, and those not yet born you know
as already born. And the innocent and unwicked, and my unexamined thing, can
be understood in two ways; for the cunning ought to be called a work; and an
unworked soul is that which has not yet been formed through virtue and
knowledge. Men of bloods, turn aside from me. They will take your cities into
vanity. Having said, But to me your friends were exceedingly honored, O God,
he reasonably declines the enemies of God and drives them

from himself; but also having remembered that there will be a time in which
God himself will kill the sinners, from here already he drives these too far
from himself, so that he may not become a partner of them, nor from drawing
near share in the harm from them. And since also into contentious words and
strifes they will take your cities into vanity, the divine Scriptures being
named cities, and vainly these the heterodox ta =24.41= ke, and not as they
ought; I turn away and decline from them. Try me, O God. Having said at the
beginning, O Lord, you have tried me and you have known me, now as not yet
having been tried he said, Try me, O Lord, and know my heart. And then it has
been said, You tracked out my path and my measuring-line, and you foresaw all
my ways; but just now, Search me, and know my paths; lest then he is not
saying contrary things. For then he said simply that he had been tried and
known, but now he prays to be tried, in order that his heart may be known; for
the being pure and upright in heart is greater than simply being pure and
upright; and it surpasses the simply having been tried and known, to be tried
in order that his heart may be known; but also than the simply having his ways
foreseen, greater is the being known as to his paths after being searched.
Wherefore he also added: And see if you saw lawlessness in me, that is in my
habit and disposition. It is also carried thus: And see if a way of
lawlessness is in me; that is, consider if lawlessness ever journeyed in my
soul, so that traces of its way remain with me. Nevertheless even if such
sinlessness has been accomplished for me from you and through you, still you
guided me in an eternal way, leading to immortality and incorruption.

PSALM 139. Coals of fire will fall upon them. For he will pay a penalty from
him, having such a disease. Evils will hunt an unjust man unto corruption. And
what is unto corruption? that many are hunted unto salvation, as those from
the apostles, as those from the holy men but the wicked when they are hunted
by wickedness and corruption; for every unjust man, as having been corrupted
by his own disease by his own wickedness, will pay the fitting penalty; for it
is sufficient instead of every punishment to corrupt the soul that has it. I
knew that the Lord will do the judgment of the poor. From here Solomon having
been benefited says of his father in Ecclesiastes: End of the word, hear the
whole, that God will bring every work into judgment, in every thing
overlooked, whether good or wicked; for even now, he says, as God will judge,
avenging the needy and poor now being worn down. And after these things he
turned the psalm upon a good end; wherefore also it has been titled Unto the
end, speaking the hope laid up for the righteous. And Symmachus says, beside
your face. And what is better than to see God face to face with a purified
mind? according to the beatitude of the Saviour who said: Blessed are the pure
in heart, for they will see God; which having obtained they will give thanks.

=24.44= PSALM 140. Following the preceding psalm this too has been titled; and
it is of David, and has the same power of prayer. And this has something more,
being more doctrinal, and teaching more and more useful things through the
beginning; and he says: I cried; one must

not in every way suppose that he has used a great voice and a shout; for God
hears even those who are silent; so also it is said that he said to Moses: Why
do you shout to me? But he who has acquired great doctrines concerning God,
asking something from God with surpassing disposition and faith, thus cried to
him; wherefore also taking courage he says: Since I am such, and I shout such
things, attend to my voice in my crying to you; as having pure not only the
mind, but also the voice through the lips from falsehood, and from perjury and
reviling. To which he also added accordingly: The lifting of my hands is an
evening sacrifice. And you will understand this from the Apostle saying: I
wish the men to pray lifting holy hands, without wrath and murmurings; for
that man too remembered together both the things according to mind, and the
things according to action; but the impious never lift hands; wherefore it has
been said to them: When you stretch out your hands to me, I will turn away my
face from you; and one must lift the actions to a height, and accomplish them
in a way pleasing to God. Do not turn aside my heart to words of wick edness,
to put forward pretenses in sins. Do not permit my mind, once simply
constructed, to be turned aside and inclined to the contrary; and instead of,
to put forward pretenses in sins, Symmachus rendered, to think lawless
thoughts, having interpreted more clearly the contemplation of the word. Then
again he passes over to the practical saying, with men working lawlessness,
and I will not pair with their chosen ones. Instead of which again Symmachus
interpreted more clearly saying, With men working injustice, nor may I eat
with them their sweet things. For I will not be, he says, together with these,
if you, O Lord, set a watch for the mouth, and keep the treasury constructed
for me beforehand. And chosen of these would be those who allege pretenses for
the things they do badly; those without pretense, and lawlessly acting without
cover, being worse. Their judges were swallowed beside a rock. And Symmachus:
For still also my prayer is within their vices; their judges will be plucked
out in hands of a rock; they will hear my sweetest sayings; as a farmer when
he breaks the earth, as your bones were scattered beside Hades. And now he
brings on the cause of not pairing with sinners; for their communion, he says,
is sufficient to scatter my soul, and to catch it within their vices =24.45=;
and the they will be plucked out shows that they also see their own rulers and
judges pulled down from roots, as being plucked out by the power of the solid
word. Do not take away my soul. Symmachus instead of, you will not take away,
do not empty out, that is, do not permit the good treasury laid up in my soul
to be emptied out; for then, he says, I will go away without fear, when
sinners are pierced with their own nets.

PSALM 141. Of understanding, for David, when he was in the cave, a prayer. And
the 50th has a like title thus: Unto the end, do not destroy, for David for a
monument, when he was fleeing from the face of Saul into the cave. And the
history lies in the first of Kingdoms: And David went up from there, and sat
in the strong caves of Engaddi; and it came to pass as Saul returned from
behind the aliens; and they reported to him saying, Behold David is in the
wilderness of Engaddi. And Saul took three thousand chosen men from all
Israel, and

went to seek David and the men with him, upon the face of the hunting of the
deer; and they came to the folds of the sheep upon the way and upon the cave;
and Saul went into the cave to cover his feet; and David and the men with him
sat in the inner parts of the cave. The history concerning Saul and the cave
was written before; and the name of David is interpreted in various ways, as
we have often said, and is taken in various ways; for when you find it lying
toward the person of the Lord, it is interpreted desired; but when toward the
person of those who sin and turn back, it is interpreted set at nought,
because of his sins. Now the name of David can be taken for the people that
has sinned and come to understanding, and shut itself in a cave, and praying
with tears strongly, and confessing, as this very inscription also shows; and
likewise also the psalm teaches these things. When my spirit was failing from
me. These things too have been said very fittingly to the constraint of the
time and of the place; for so far as it was in men, being held inside in the
cave, every running away and flight had been shut, and not even one hope of
help was present to him; nor was there one who delivered his soul or sought it
out. And seeing the intelligible enemies of his soul, having accomplished this
whole thing, I mean Saul's going into the cave in order that David might be
seized, he reasonably said, They hid a snare for me. But the things of the
snare had not gone forward for them because of the prayer set forth; and
taking courage from the course of his life, and from the actions and the
=24.48= ways in which he walked, being driven and warred against unjustly, he
says to God: and you knew my paths. To you, O Lord, I cried. Again here too he
confesses that he cries, not simply, but to the Lord; for otherwise it was
impossible for him to send forth a voice, the enemy being present near; and
having confessed that he has God as his only hope, he did not fall from the
aim; for hope does not put to shame; but also if there will be a land of the
living, I confess, he says, to have no place in this but you alone as my hope;
for you are God, my portion in the land of the living; as if I did not have
you, I would never have been examined in the land of the living. And land of
the living is not that which is now trodden by us, for this is a land of death
and of the dead, but that which the meek will inherit; upon which all who live
according to the eternal life spend their time. There was not one who
recognized me. There is not one who seeks out my soul. He who is neglected by
those supposed to preside over the churches and is not sought out at all will
say this opportunely; and he brings on next that which cries to God, and not
to hope upon leaders, but upon the Lord, and to have a portion in the land of
the living and not in a forgotten land.

PSALM 142. Everyone being examined in greatest temptations and surpassing
afflictions would opportunely use the prayer set forth; wherefore also the
psalm is without title. The Lord then hears all, but hearkens to few; if we
hearken to those who need us, we too will be heard; and he who takes courage
in his own prayer calls God a hearer. And others say that the psalm is not
without title, but has been titled, A psalm for David, when his son pursued
him. Yet this is not added through all the interpretations; and the things
said are not foreign; and nothing hinders them from having been said
concerning the pursuit by Saul. My spirit was in accidie upon me. The spirit
of the man that is in him is in accidie, whenever the heart is troubled having
remembered how
