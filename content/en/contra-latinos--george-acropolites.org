Discourse of the grand logothete George Acropolites against the Latins,
written by him when he was held in the West.

1.1 Men of Rome, you who spring from elder Rome, I wished indeed to call you
brothers as being of one mind and one thought, since this at any rate is the
aim of the God who for our sake took on what is ours and displayed in himself
all the properties of the flesh except only the sinful life; and this was the
great mystery concerning us men, that he might join all things in himself and
gather them into one-mindedness of the faith, which, according to the great
Paul, is not Greek, not barbarian, not Scythian, not Jew, nor any other of the
ethnic names taken up, but only and wholly Christ is their distinctive mark. I
wished then so to speak, for we too once happened to be of one mind and
thought brotherly thoughts and were directed by the same canons of piety or
even by the same standards and were equalized in all parts; but he who by envy
separated the first man from God and cast among the brothers the evil strife
unto division, so that the villainy even ended in murder (alas for our
weakness and for Satan's assault) has also divided us, O friends of Rome, who
were united, and has dissolved us who were blended undividedly. And now we
lament your separation, and, as I am persuaded, the more well-disposed and
sympathetic among you also lament it. For what is more good or more delightful
than brothers dwelling together in the same? What more gracious or more
profitable than that the same men contend with one mind for piety? What more
wretched or more pitiable than that those of one mind in Christ be torn into
discord and those united in Spirit into an unnatural division? And if where
two or three are gathered in Christ he is recognized in the midst of them, how
much more among two or three or even more nations of one mind in him will he
not be present and fill them with the goods, both those of peace found here,
whose fruit is the purification or ascent of those being perfected, and those
of the higher and better life from there?

1.2 For these reasons then we are torn by your separation and cannot bear the
passion, because being members of Christ and being led up to him as one head
and fitted together we no longer breathe with the same things nor is the whole
body moved to one work, but we are borne against ourselves into alien actions
and for the most part opposed by contradiction, nor do we set the cornerstone
in one corner, but as if fitted to one building and another we judge one set
of things, while you marvel at another. And who is the Jeremiah who will
lament us? And how many tears would he need? For if when Jerusalem was taken
and the Jews were led from it, one city and a very small nation and having
suffered the visible captivity, he sought springs and seas for tears, when
nearly the whole inhabited world is speaking against piety and sounding
contrary doctrines toward the preaching according to Christ, how many seas
would that man have needed for his tears? To me then this lament is not
fitting for men nor for those who wear this mortal garment, but for angels or
cherubim and all the powers above, that they may worthily lament us who do not
recognize the truth nor the mystery of Christ, through which he accomplished
our

salvation, but belch forth words of the belly and contend to win a victory far
more wretched than defeat. And it is not you who do this, and we who do not;
but what is the case, O Romans, I will not hide.

1.3 The passion began from you, but it has also fed on us like a herpes or a
gangrene, having begun from self-love and ended in stubbornness. But now come
and hear me; I will teach you the fear of the Lord; for another too, wise in
God, has said this is the beginning of wisdom. Why are we so ready not only
toward every discourse, I mean physical or mathematical or logical or of
another kind, but even toward theology itself, incontinent in hand and tongue
and thought? Why are we not bounded by our own limits? Why do we leap beyond
the trenches? Why do we not know ourselves as men, but think we are of a more
divine portion, of a nature lifted above the heavens and dwelling with God and
having known the things concerning him most excellently and being able to
announce them to others? Vain indeed are we, at whom demons laugh, angels
lament, and Christ condemns, and a condemnation which he predetermined in the
Gospels, weeping and gnashing of teeth and the rest of punishment. For not
having a wedding garment we recline ourselves with the guests around the
master's banquet; and this is not beyond the terrible, though it is terrible,
but if we do not rank ourselves with the first and with those beyond whom none
is, we have reckoned ourselves as nothing. O we wretched ones: leaving off
contemplating our own things, we seek the things of the nature unapproachable
even to the cherubim; leaving off keeping the commandments of the Lord,
through which we shall be saved, we have chosen to theologize; leaving off
philosophizing about nature and bodies and giving maxims about character, of
which even to succeed is gain and to miss is not harmful, we fly up to
theologizing, of which to err is the most destructive fall of the soul, and of
which recovery is as if impossible; for where failure is unpardonable, the
return is hard. O we vain ones, what is this last bait of Satan around us?
Who, since he cannot hunt us with the others (for he grows weary when opposed
by the saving spirits) casts this greatest and most dangerous snare upon those
perhaps sharper and hotter toward piety, by which he has destroyed many
multitudes even of those more distinguished in other things and has shown
those who leaped over the things below and did not proceed by steps a fall
worthy of tears.

1.4 Therefore all must hesitate to come to theology, and must fear the fear
whose being feared is altogether worthy. For what newer thing do we even
theologize? We ought to be content with the former things and with what he who
knows his own things well taught us, or even to hear the second, whose
practice and whose life have borne witness to the divine decrees, since these
too did not use their own powers about the matter nor remain in logical
methods alone. For this is human and is known to nature, and where are the
mysteries of the Godhead? And where are the things above nature from the
things according to nature, both wondered at and honored? Christ the first
theologian theologizes, and you falsify his theology? He who knows himself and
his own well, and you dare to declare even anything whatever beside him?
Christ says: "the Holy Spirit, who proceeds from the Father," and who else
would dare to declare also from the Son? For as he says he has been begotten
from the Father, and no one would ever be so bold as to say also from the
Spirit, so it is also in this. Again he himself says he sends the Paraclete,
and let us be content with the word and not counterfeit the saying; let the
syllables stand thus, which the first elementary teacher wrote down. Why do
you syllogize in vain and bring on other names instead of others? Call sending
sending for me, and proceeding proceeding for me; far better than you is he
who taught these things and commanded us to believe thus. For why did he not

say that he proceeds it, the very Wisdom and the foreknower of things to come,
who knew such things accurately and the doubt about them and the discord, so
that he might free us from many words and oppositions, but said that he would
send it to the disciples after his ascent to the Father? For if he did not
know sending as one thing and procession as another, he would not have changed
the names, just as he does not in the case of the Father nor in the case of
the name of the Son and of the relation between them, that is generation; but
he keeps these and does not interchange them with words of equal force, as
some would say. It is God who theologizes these things and about himself, and
how would I come to such boldness and shamelessness as to theologize anything
else besides these, being faint dust and clay crawling on the earth, even if I
was honored with the Spirit? Paul did not falsify the saying, he who was
caught up to the third heaven and from there into paradise and heard
unutterable things; no evangelist wrote down that the Paraclete also proceeds
from the Son, and who is so rash in his wits and unbridled in his words as to
belch this from his mouth?

1.5 And if again you take the sending into the proof and linger over the word
and identify names and say that sending is proceeding, hear again the things
toward reply. None of the names is outright the same; and even if you bring
the many-named things themselves, you will find in them a difference not
small; for looking to one and another sense of the subject those who first set
names imposed the variety of names. And you would know these things well
having come to a teacher of grammarians unfolding the syllables and uncovering
the hidden of the thought (and these things the chance comer would not
recognize offhand) and you would see also the experts not using chance names
for interpretation, if they set themselves to speak properly, but the fitting
and corresponding and not discordant. If then even in the case of things among
us and mortal the difference of names hides much sense and the expert has
recognized it and does not allow those not going deep into technical
discourses to counterfeit, how much more in the case of the nature above us,
the superessential essence, the cause of all beings, that which is known by no
one nor gives any of the things concerning it for a small grasp of it, that
which is lifted above every [transcendent] essence and power, from which every
being both is and is made alive and is made good and remains held around, held
by the ineffable power of the being-making nature? How much then in the case
of this ineffable essence must one observe the names which the first worker of
their revelation set, and say that the Spirit proceeds in the cases in which
he said proceeding, and again is sent in the cases in which he said being
sent. For who knows the mind of the Lord? Who has peered into the depths of
the Spirit? Christ said that the Spirit proceeds from the Father, and none of
those who have chosen to be pious would counterfeit the voice. Again he said
that he sends it forth, and let the name remain thus. Or what is the reason
for keeping that unfalsified, but bringing this over into another? And yet the
matter is not simple nor of the undangerous things, but of the exceedingly
most dangerous and those furnishing much destruction.

1.6 Let us also go over the compositions and teachings of the God-bearing men.
That they too had such a sense about these things is clear from the following:
for which of them was so bold as to say that the Holy Spirit proceeds from the
Son? And indeed they could have said it if they had thought any such thing,
but they remained with the sending and the giving and the supplying, and in
some places also the forthcoming. But forthcoming is not the same as
procession to those skilled in the oracles, if indeed we say that the Son also
comes forth from the Father, but it is a common voice needing some further
determination toward the

clarity. For the Son comes forth from the Father, but generately; the Spirit
also comes forth from him, but processionally. So that not wherever there is
forthcoming, there is generation or procession. And using this voice of the
Spirit the luminaries of the church, just as indeed also the aforementioned,
did not mark this with some further determination, so that we might know the
thought, which was necessary if they had thought it; for they could have said
to come forth from the Son but processionally, if they inclined toward such an
aim.

1.7 Speak then you too such voices, keeping them unaltered for utterance.
Teach that the Spirit is sent forth from the Word; for you were taught this
from the Better. Interpret also that it is supplied from him and given and
sent and comes forth; for you have been taught these things from those
greatest men, whose virtue is the mark and whose being God-borne is evident.
Basils theologize these things and Cyrils and Gregories, who have not dared in
any way at all to write the procession of the Spirit upon the Son. And how
would we so unblushingly leap upon the name and insist that we know ourselves
better than they? Where ever did you find in their writings that the Spirit
proceeds from the Son? But again the syllogisms easily found by you are
brought on, and this rotten cord of the argument, through which you gather the
absurdities, if indeed falsehoods are absurdities to those of sound mind. For
how did men so many and of such quality not know what is gathered by you? But
they knew the premises and taught them, yet what you yourself bring as
following, or conclusions, they did not receive into mind at all; for what
they have not thought, how would they set it before those being taught? Take
you too the premises as arguments, if you wish, and conclusions, and do not
through them conclude other things alien to the truth and foreign to the
church of Christ.

1.8 What is the art or science of theology, that we should follow its canons?
Our preaching is a simple teaching. For Christ taught it, the apostles widened
it, the teachers strengthened it. Again you contend to syllogize, and I set
against you the simple word. You conclude falsehoods for me, and I put forward
the true things for you. You gather for me from the premises also from the
Son, I declare to you the from the Father. This is of Christ, that I do not
know of whom; this is the teaching of the very Wisdom, that is a fiction of a
mind not sound, devising arts toward the things beyond arts and sciences; this
Christ teaches, and I have trusted the word, that you put forward, and you no
longer have me persuaded. What is this so great innovation of words? What is
this so great chatter about the divine words, as not to wish to be occupied
and display oneself in other things, but to contend to take pride in the
divine things? Of the Athenians there was nothing but to say and hear
something newer, and of the new theologians to recast the teachings of the
truth.

1.9 And if you bring the forthcoming from the sending, or the giving or the
supplying or the forthcoming, into slavery, and again theologize or
philosophize the by nature or not by nature and the according to authority or
according to conjunction and either from home or from outside and all that
belongs to the little human craft, I will let you chatter with such awkwardly
offered trifles and with one solution I will overturn your technical divisions
or subdivisions or further divisions and the necessary things following the
oppositions. In the case of the nature of God and of his division and union
and mind is weak, in which we know the limits, and thought has given up,
through
which we have found or possessed the sciences; and how would opinion have room
when those before it are darkened and have failed in the thinking? We men have
found the arts and having examined nature we put forward the arguments
physically, and by art we have known the sequences of nature. But in the case
of the things above nature what is the technical argument? What is the

scientific mind? How great is that beyond, so as to think something and
declare it, where the many-eyed close their eyes, where the cherubim shudder,
where all powers and angels are silent, knowing one song only, the praise of
the trisagion, this too without curiosity and settled above the little
technical words in simplicity? For it was necessary that for the simple, or
for the Simple, or even above simplicity itself, the odes too be simple. Why
then do we the composite and consisting of many mixtures not emulate the
simple words in the conceptions about the divine and gather the voices
according to it, so that we too may be gathered by the one creative panarchy
into one, having woven one human symphony, that we may share in the angelic?
We shall be content with what has been taught us here by Christ and those of
Christ; and if there should be anything more hidden, let us store it up for
ourselves there. Or what else would we grant to belong to the life from there,
if we knew all the things of God here? Or how would we know, being also flesh
and partaking of matter, if we should strive to reach all the divine things?

=1.10= If it were true that the Holy Spirit also proceeds from the Son, the
teachers would not have hesitated to mark it in their writings; for I cannot
say they begrudged it. But they were silent, having feared in themselves a
praised fear, and you, being who you are, are bold for the utterance? And
Christ has not said it; for I think it was not. For how, if it were, did he
not teach it to those to whom he made all theology clear? And after him none
of the teachers dared to say it, and you who say these things, who are you for
confirmation? Sending and giving and supplying and forthcoming are not the
same as procession; for if they were the same, they would not, using these in
their discourses everywhere, have been cautious about the procession; for they
did not wish not to agree with Christ. One must then remain with the words of
Christ and with the teachings after him. But you have Paul advocating for you,
teaching Spirit of Christ and Spirit of truth. And who does not say that the
Spirit is of Christ, that is of the Son? He is alien to the church of Christ
who does not speak these things with us. But to proceed also from the Son
neither the Son himself who taught the things of the Father and his own and
the things of the Spirit most clearly has said, nor has any of the teachers
decreed, so far as we know, and whose writings men set forth for teaching and
benefit of the many. You seek again, as I think, the reasons of the sending,
of the supplying, of the giving: "if they are besides the procession, what
then are they? Sending, supplying and giving are the same." Who are you who
examines about God? He himself has spoken about himself, and remain with the
same and wear down the words yourself too and keep the names unharmed by any
of those who badly counterfeit even the royal drachma, for which the Master
made the effort, that he might find it buried by the passions.

=1.11= Again you chatter, and again I theologize. Speak the sayings as they
were spoken or written, and you will not be in danger concerning the soul nor
will you contend with me toward luxury of words, if indeed some have badly
practiced luxuriating in such things, making theology an occasion of words, as
if there were not other things in which they might display their ambition.
There is nature, numbers, continuous quantities, harmony and forms of all
kinds of arguments, knowledge of soul, purification of mind, difference of
sense, and contraction of the outward things in themselves or leading-up
toward the mind. These things we must philosophize, but concerning God we must
be altogether cautious. Some of those who have wished to theologize have grown
shameless in thoughts, in words, but they were ostracized, they were cast out,
they were cut off as rotten members of the church from the head, and now they
are pitiable for their boldness and are mocked and lamented. May we too not
appear of such a portion. Let us hold fast to what has been taught by Christ,
by the apostles, by the

later teachers, the God-bearing men; let us use their words for our utterance,
let us put forward the same thoughts as teachings, lest the common enemy take
the hope of our salvation as an occasion of destruction, and whence we have
being saved, from there come the corruption. Terrible is the adversary, and if
he grows weary with the things on the left, he casts himself upon the things
on the right, and he puts forward zeal and perhaps handles the truth. He mixes
a mixing-bowl full of honey, and to those of sound mind it is known as full of
poison; he fawns with what appears and bites with what is hidden. A seal has
become common to us all who have believed in Christ. Let us all be marked with
this seal, and perhaps the destroying devil will pass us by. We are a flock of
Christ, of the good shepherd and chief shepherd; let us keep our seal
unassailable, lest we be secretly plundered by the thief and not known by the
Master. Not a few heresies have appeared at times, wishing like tares to
defile the clean wheat of Christ, but they have been rooted out by words of
truth. And the teachers have not falsified the seal at all by the words for
these things, but were content even only to cast off the alien; and the seal
was kept as it had from the beginning by nature. For what has been filled out
a second time by many canons most excellently made exact, of this they decreed
the completion as needing nothing; but they set right what was erring, and
kept what was well laid down altogether unharmed. If then you wish both to be
a sheep of Christ and to be known to all who see, bear the seal as it has been
formed to the form of piety, and do not have it counterfeit having falsified
it toward your will, lest the Lord meeting you, he who says he knows his own
and is known by those of his, justly say "I do not know you."

=1.12= But the images are on the tongue and the examples are offhand, root and
branch and the fruit and the sun and ray and radiance; for I am silent about
that of the spring, being unsuitable for taking up. I will dissolve your
arguments of the examples, if only you are willing to hear without contention
what is said by me. The Spirit according to your hypotheses has been born as
fruit, and in others again as radiance. But the otherness is great and the
difference is not simple. Why then did he who is wise in all things and knows
his own things most excellently not declare the fruit from the branch and the
radiance from a ray, but from the root and from the sun? Unless then both are
branches and both fruits, or even rays and radiances united in one another and
blended by ineffable reasons, not falsifying the properties. And how will
things bounded by place and interval have room in the unshaped, in the
unextended, in things altogether freed from sensible apprehension and
appearance? I too wish to call these three suns, the two having existence from
one, but a delimitation and a circumscription disturbs me, and that these are
not all recognized in one another. The root and the branch and the fruit also
trouble me, if in one hypostasis these are parts from which the whole has been
composed. But the sun too is an individual, and how will the three-hypostatic
nature be imaged in one individual, torn and divided into parts, of which each
neither has been composed with the nature of the whole nor has been named? But
God is the Father, God the Son, God the Holy Spirit; the two from one as
caused from a cause, the one generately, the other processionally, and grown
together with one another and always being together as consubstantial. And if
consubstantial, then altogether also of equal power, not in any way whatever
falling short of the essence of the cause, or of the energy or of the power.
And if there is also another property of both, that the one is given from each
or sent or supplied or however else you wish to call it, being cautious only
of the name of procession as indicating a beginning of substantial existence,
and each from each, you no longer on account of these things too bring on me
the procession as following of necessity,

so that the properties may be contracted. Who are you who judges these things
so finely? If you know the reason of the procession, in what it differs from
generation, then you would know the reason of the sending and of the
supplying, in what it differs from the procession; but indeed not that, and
therefore this not at all. Remain with the syllables that lie there, as I have
often said to you, hold to what has been decreed as true, lest a tittle of the
oracles be broken off for you. For if in the case of the commandments of
Christ the danger in such things is easy to bear, in the names of theology how
much more destructive this is.

=1.13= I will speak to you a word not mean, only lend me an ear and do not
fight against the truth. If the Spirit were also processible from the Son, the
Son would not have been silent about such a thing and have dogmatized it from
the Father. For if from the Son, then altogether also from the Father; for the
Son is from the Father and all the things of the Son; yet not, if from the
Father, also from the Son as following of necessity. One must then have said
the first, so that the second might follow; but having said the second he no
longer brings the first. For whose sake then did he omit the ambiguous, having
taught what is confessed by all? It seems to me, in order to free those about
to receive the preaching from such a thought, so that they might think and say
that the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father, but is in or is together with
the Son and receives the sending and the supplying or also the giving from
him, I mean the according-to-good-pleasure breathing-together and concord.

=1.14= You force me then to speak a saying which Christ did not teach, an
apostle did not hand down, a teacher did not set forth. I hesitate at the
word, and most of all I have feared, not wishing, by theologizing rashly, not
only to fall from the light of God, but also to be cast into the outer
darkness. I do not wish to be named a bold theologian, when it is possible for
me to be saved by fear; I do not accept being badly manly and falling, when it
is possible for me to be kept unwounded by contractions. Have you the boldness
and I the fear; it is good in such things to fear rather than to be bold. Even
myth did not save Icarus who dared to travel the air with waxen wings, but
cast him into the sea when the wax melted by the sun's rays. Do we then
persuade you by such words, and would you too suffer this passion of fear,
which many even better than you have feared, which both saves and does not
destroy? For when it is possible to accomplish salvation without toil, why do
we fall of our own will into the dangers? When we ought to be crowned together
with those who contended before us without toils and long words, why do we
cast ourselves upon unclear things? Do not gaze upon them with pupils that are
insatiable of the sparkles of the sun, lest you ever cast away the light of
the eyes by the brightness of the rays; grasp it through curtains or in air,
and you have the pleasure with the benefit. Do not wish to dive into an
infinite sea; be content with rivers or even with well-bounded lakes to
display the art of swimming.

=1.15= Come then let us stand together in the house of the Lord, and let us
sing a song not new (for this I will parody from the things of David), for it
is old, even if of the new and better covenant. For long ago those taught this
by Christ, who were raised in the better wisdom, set it before those who have
chosen to be pious, commanding them to keep it unbroken. Let us receive their
command as a guard of the soul, let us welcome their teachings as compositions
toward salvation, and let us sing this very song having run together to the
same, the Spirit having gathered us into one symphony, the Spirit who proceeds
from the Father, but rests in the Son or is together with the Son, and is also
sent by him and given and all such things, as the three know and the one, of
whom they have hidden the clearer of the words; and let us all shout with one
voice this common symbol of our faith, our good deposit of piety, which
unimpaired

we would keep, and let us glorify the one Godhead and kingship, to which
belongs glory, might, honor and worship unto the ages. Amen.

2.t Second discourse concerning the procession of the Holy Spirit from the
Father.

2.1 Since we have composed the first discourse by the grace of the Spirit as
far as possible, in which we have set forth with simple thoughts that it is no
longer necessary to say that the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Son, following
Christ the teacher and the fathers, come then and let us confirm it also with
proofs as is fitting; for those who rejoice in the addition will not, as I
think, be content with the simple and uncurious words, but will seek also
arguments from the lower and trampled wisdom and will demand premises and the
following conclusions, and inductions for confirmation and the things through
impossibility toward the matters at hand and all the other technical trainings
and refinements, which our mystery has surpassed or cast off. And I do not
mean the first, that in the Godhead, but also the second and lower, that in
humanity. I have heard even the theologians of the Greeks not presenting all
the things of their gods with proofs, although the things of the gods are
fictions for them, whose substance is a holding-up of thought and for whom
forgetting is destruction, but they also put forward immediate and unproved
theses and decree that they be received as true. And what has driven us to
such folly (for I hesitate to say madness), that unless we declare even that
God is with a demonstrative argument, we will no longer reckon it acceptable,
fearing lest we somehow escape notice having destroyed God together with the
arguments or having lost providence by the unproved? It was necessary then, O
men of Rome, to keep the preaching thus simple and not varied with
oppositions. But since he who long ago ostracized man from simplicity, having
deceived him by his own duplicity and brought him down into the many-turned
and many-formed of matter, has also cut the thing of our faith into some
many-cleft thoughts, and from there there is need of many words, if somehow
the false might be separated from the true, already we too, trusting in the
Holy Spirit, have entered this greatest contest, hoping from there a turn to
speak with the Spirit the things of the Spirit, from where every perfect gift
comes down.

2.2 What then is new, if he who formerly opened the mouth of the donkey, so
that a prophecy might be spoken concerning Christ, will now also cast off the
mist of our mind and scatter the darkness lying before the sun of the soul as
far as possible, so that in light we may see light, in the Spirit the things
of the Spirit? Speak to me first yourself your own things, strike me with your
missiles; for I have already put forward the shield of the truth; and I will
receive your missiles first, then I would strike you back with mine and make
trial of the power.

2.3 This one thing then appears even very strong to these men, which Christ
said in the Gospels: "But when the Paraclete comes, whom I will send to you
from the Father, the Spirit of truth, who proceeds from the Father." This then
they send forth like a two-natured arrow against those who contradict, trying
to prove from both the "I will send" and from the truth that the Holy Spirit
also proceeds from the Son. "For if the Spirit," they say, "is sent from the
Father, and is sent also from the Son in the same way, it proceeds on account
of these things also from the Son"; for even this man boasting much strength*
among the dialecticians as the same things altogether follow the same, just as
the contrary follows the contrary and the contrary the contrary. And yet
neither has this been true in all cases nor has that had the unshaken. For if
to sleep

waking is opposed, and living follows this, dying will follow sleep, and if to
the sitting it is to be ensouled, to the standing it is to be soulless. But
neither do the same things follow the same, if these have been observed in
other individuals. For to being rich there follows in one man good repute, in
another ill repute, and it benefited the one who sailed, but shipwreck will
follow another. And no one would insist that it is otherwise. Let these things
have been said toward the dialectical canons and the devices. And if you add
to the sending the "by nature" (for I hear even some of you saying such a
thing) and from there the terrible and unconquerable syllogism is woven for
you, that the sending by nature is procession, you sophize again and do not
syllogize the truth for me. And this fallacy is, as I think, besides asking
the thing at the beginning; for one ought to show through another that the
sending by nature is procession, but you have taken it as an agreed premise.
But the sending by nature is not procession, just as neither is the procession
a sending; for sending is one thing and procession another. And Christ
interprets this clearly for you; for he has named his own sending, and the
paternal procession. For if it were not one thing and another, how did he not
put forward the sending there or the procession in himself? For both are in
one passage.

2.4 And I, having also marked the time, find much difference in these things.
For the "I will send" stands of future time, but the "proceeds" of present.
And indeed if it were the same, he ought to have said this same thing
otherwise. For he who would send was present and was conversing, and it was
not another who was going to do this; for after the union the hypostasis is
one. And if he sends the Spirit eternally, that is proceeds it, he was also
then proceeding it and put forward the word thus, just as also concerning the
Father he marked it with the extended tense of time. Do you then understand
what is said by me? The Lord says that he will send the Spirit, but this he
says proceeds from the Father. Nothing then is the sameness of the procession
and of the sending, just as indeed neither of the times, by which the things
of the verbs have been characterized. And why does a conjunction not even join
the names or verbs, if they were identified altogether?

2.5 And I think you seek their difference. Take this alone as faith for me,
and God knows the reason, he who proceeds and who sends and who both proceeds
and is sent. Become first you too God for me, and then you will know the
things of God accurately; for nature will recognize itself. And if you are
unable for this, how also, not knowing well the differences of the things
below, do you mince thoughts upon the altogether unknown and unspeakable
nature?

2.6 "But the Spirit of truth is of the Son; and if he says of the Son, then
from the Son; for it is Spirit of the Father, since also from the Father." But
I will reverse the argument and say to you: that because from the Father, of
the Father, but because of the Son, not from the Son. For if from the Son, of
the Son, yet not if of the Son, from the Son. For it is not that because of
this one, also from this one of necessity, but what is from this one is also
of this one altogether, from whom it both is and is said. And if again you
demand of me the reasons of the sending and of being called of the Son, I at
least am at a loss, since I am not God by nature, and perhaps I have not even
become so by grace; and if the one who became man was well pleased even at
this, yet the first is as impossible. How then shall I make the reasons clear
to you? How shall I interpret the ineffable things for you? You command me to
drain the Atlantic sea with a small cup, or to encompass the infinite sea with
a measure, or sand by number, or the flood of the unwandering stars. And these
things I think much easier than to speak out the things concerning God which
he himself has not unveiled or made clear to men. For some things he has not
even stripped altogether, but has given small appearances, yet has not
presented how the things themselves are. For nature did not have room to
contain; for he did not begrudge the sharing to those whom he knew it to
suffice; for the divine is without share of envy. If then we should strive to
recognize what it is not permitted to recognize, we would also justly

suffer deprivation of the things of which it is permitted to be in knowledge,
having destroyed the power that is in us by the too-greedy movement.

2.7 But let my argument keep to the course, even if it has turned a little
from the matter at hand. Sending is not the same as procession, and it has
been proved by many as I have supposed; therefore the Spirit is not from the
Son on account of the sending, and in the same way also not from him on
account of being his. And how it is his and is sent by him, we shall make
clear as we go down, the Spirit supplying the strength. This first arrow of
theirs has been reckoned an arrow of infants to us; for it has been
sufficiently managed that those who have chosen to run the course of piety
without stumbling must not on account of these things dogmatize that the
sending is procession, where life is the finishing-point, just as the
turning-aside, as one might say, is the deathless death. And the things said
before fit also all sayings which claim that the Spirit is of the Son, and the
things of the apostle Paul, Spirit of Christ and Spirit of the Son and Spirit
of wisdom and all other such things, which those who dogmatize the procession
from the Son know to transfer to Christ, leaving the other thoughts. For the
solutions which we brought against in the aforementioned oppositions, these we
will also bring under in these.

2.8 Again from another side the "he will take from what is mine" tries to
strike me as beyond healing. But I will not only delay toward the healing, but
I will not even receive the wound at all. That the Spirit takes from the Son,
who of the faithful will doubt? But he takes the manifestation, the giving,
the supplying. I will say even one of your things: the having all things
whatever the Son has, the sameness of the essence, of the power, of the energy
and of the properties without all things simply, he takes also from the
Father, but the being itself and the existence; and not these only, but indeed
also all that he takes in addition from the Son. But it troubles me very much
that it was said by Christ not "he will take from me" but "from what is mine."
For both are not the same nor do they lead to the same thought when borne
without article and with article. Do you wish me to speak out what is thought
by me in such a thing? He has said "he will take from what is mine," we have
made clear the reason of the economy. For he himself came down to earth, he
himself through experience has had the human nature or knowledge or
betterment. And just as he himself says "I will ask the Father and he will
send another Paraclete," not as being less than the Father on account of the
asking, and "what I speak to you I do not speak from myself, but whatever I
have heard from my Father, those things I speak," so indeed he also says "the
Spirit, who will come to you, will take from what is mine," that is of word,
manner, teaching and all that I have set forth for the benefit of men, not as
taking just now when the "he will take" had energy, but as bringing to birth
the completion of a mystery marked out before the ages, this being made clear
by divine order and coming out into energy; for he will not come as
contradicting, but as agreeing with what has been taught toward the truth.
Thus the Spirit takes from him and announces, thus he is manifested, thus he
comes forth. These things the voices can do, not being led up to the
procession and identified with it, but signifying something other and
presenting another manner of some property and a newer relation of those among
us, which the Son has had toward the Spirit and this toward him, Paraclete
toward Paraclete, ray toward ray, the radiance from the first Father toward
the radiance from the first equal in all things. These are an undivided dyad
being given essence from the undivided one of the Father, the one generately,
the other processionally.

2.9 If you know the manner of the otherness which procession has had toward
generation, you will also know the manner of the manifestation of the Spirit
through the Son or from the Son; for we will not fight about the prepositions
in these things at all. But that is ineffable and uninterpretable and thus in
the same way altogether unclear. Interpret the first for me

you, and I will make the second clear to you. And if your mind is numb toward
clarity, why do you force me to agree with outlandish opinions? Have all the
things of God been interpreted for us by Christ and by his disciples and by
the best men in theology, or has something also been hidden underneath and
remained undiscerned, as overpassing the human mind? I think you will not say
it is not so. Be silent then yourself too in the things in which the better
have been silent, but proclaim with a bright voice the things proclaimed by
them with a piercing cry; for we must be pious using most truly the words that
lie there.

=2.10= What necessity is there to falsify them harmfully and, so that we may
appear wiser and skilled toward theology, to escape our own notice falling
into an ignorance of destruction, the most pitiable of all things? Heaven
appears to us for investigation, and earth is set forth, water, air, fire.
Imagine first the beauties in heaven, know the motions of the stars, the
wandering and the unwandering; from these you will recognize wisdom of God.
Then thus philosophize for me about the things below, examine the variety of
nature; you will learn that all things are of God. And of these you will not
find the causes or the principles or elements complete for the grasp, what the
matter is, what the form, and if they have been composed by themselves and how
both have run together, but you will think the aim of nature as a whole, if it
is different to all the different forms or like to the like, or sometimes
different to like, and sometimes like to things being differentiated; what are
the most unclear reasons of the seeds, but in the outgrowth most clear; what
is the aim for the craftsman of generation and of corruption, if indeed
everything that has come to be is corrupted, and I do not mean in men on
account of the fall, since somehow it also remains and is preserved, but also
in other animals on account of the coincidence.

=2.11= These things we must philosophize and about such things we must make
the investigations as exact, but of the words of theology we must hold as they
had from the beginning of theol[ogy]; but the things spoken by the teachers
trouble them. And I will no longer say as this of Cyril, and this of Basil and
again of Athanasius and of the Gregories and of others, but having joined all
together and set them in one passage I will bring against them all one
solution that fits. One of these theologizes that the Spirit is poured forth
from the Son, another that it comes forth, another that it is given and
another that it is supplied or sent forth, and some some things or all all
things have engraved in their own writings. I myself say that the Spirit is
poured out from the Son, I agree with those who have said it is also given,
and I do not refuse that it is supplied from him, and I do not deny coming
forth and being sent and the other of the names, neither in the utterances nor
first in the thoughts. But, O friend, it is not the case that what is poured
out and supplied and given has also been given essence from that from which it
is also poured and supplied and given. For the water from a vessel and the ray
from the fire-places are sent and given from these, but they have not been
given essence from these. And let the example in things of other essence not
disturb you; for taking up of the examples as much as fits the hypothesis we
cast off the superfluous. For where would an example be found fitting the
Godhead in every respect? Even what you say and we in others have much that is
discordant, but some small likeness toward the matter at hand, and they
characterize something of the things around the essence, I mean relation or
manner or motion or all the outward things, and they have not been born to
present the being itself of that which is above being.

=2.12= Having then from here the starting-points of the arguments we do not
think the outpouring or giving or supplying or manifestation as a manner of
the substantial existence of the Spirit, but a presentation of some other
peculiarity. Such voices we receive and put forward, but we are altogether
silent about the procession. For what

we think, we announce unto truth, confirming the knowledge by faith or making
the faith clear by the knowledge unto piety. Thus we also dissolve for you the
coming forth from the Son, thus also the being sent and supplied and given and
whatever of the names those who know these things better than we dogmatize
upon the Son, and the identity and otherness in these, who have altogether
forbidden the procession, of whose names the utterance of the announcement has
borne witness. For their knowledge has gone up above heaven, their sound has
been poured out to all the ends of the earth. For these in Christ have held
the whole with the words of piety, having whirled around or flashed around the
world under the sun in the manner of thunder or lightning.

=2.13= Since then the things of Christ have been made clear to us thus and the
solutions have been well, together with those things which all the hierophants
also taught and made clear, who have not dared to say that the Spirit proceeds
from the Son, because not every necessity appeared to them from the words, we
must also begin the rest toward the distinction. For the present then, that I
may also pre-announce to you something small of the arguments, how, what
Christ did not teach and an apostle does not know (for if he knows, he also
spoke it out, he who received all the grace of the Spirit in a fiery tongue)
the teachers did not syllogize, according to the syllogisms now easily put
forward by you, do you yourself dare to teach? Who ever are you? And though
myriads of men have passed who were distinguished in piety and wisdom and the
greatest of the divine signs as we know, no one yet was so bold as to add such
a word also upon the Son, and you grow shameless toward the attempt? You would
have been blessed if you only held to what was taught you by them; but now (I
do not know from where you start), wishing to elbow aside and surpass those
who taught, you have shown yourself to me most wretched.

=2.14= But since we have shaken the great foundations for you, in which you
even more boasted the firm and as you thought unshaken, behold I bring you
also my things, so that you may know if they are founded upon the rock of
Christ, where the streams of the rivers of those who sophize in vain cannot
sweep anything away. But first I will ask you something, and answer me toward
the question: did the Father beget the Son and proceed the Spirit at the same
time, or not at the same time? That not at the same time no one yet of those
who have wished to be pious will say; for time will intervene about the
existences of those from the Father and they will not escape the first and the
second and the other things that follow those not given essence at the same
time. And somewhere one of those who have theologized has said toward those
who set them in a beginning: "When has the Son been begotten? When the Spirit
has been proceeded" and conversely: "When has the Spirit been proceeded? When
the Son has been begotten," not distinguishing the procession from the
generation in the instant of the substantial existence. It remains then that
the generation from the Father and procession are at the same time, that is
the being begotten from him and the being proceeded. If then at the same time,
how is the one through the other? For the middle must first be, then that
through it appear. And if the things above time are far from time, these are
above time in this way, that they are at the same time and that a middle is
not found toward the existence. For together with the cause the things from it
are thought at the same time, but the caused things, if the one is through the
other, there is every necessity that they also be given essence first and
second. Both then at the same time from the same and not the one through the
other, lest there be time first and later, or a difference of essence will
intervene in the three.

=2.15= If then I do not persuade you with these, listen again to another. If
the Son is middle of the Father and of the Spirit, he is then bounded (for
every middle is circumscribed by the extremes) and I do not mean in place or
time or power or energy, which are recognized in things under nature, but in
the essence itself even if he should be infinite, being limited by infinites.
Which is also paradoxical, if the infinite is limited; for it will not be
limited in others. And by what things it is defined, it is also circumscribed;
and God has appeared to us circumscribed in essence, the newest thing, than
which what

would be more absurd? Suppose then you too my thing. Infinite then is that
from which both are, and both infinite, being infinite together with the
beginning as the that-from-which is infinite together with the caused, or
these themselves; for there will not be a bound in which they will stand. For
the Father is moved together with his offshoots [the Father] into the
undefined, and these run up together into the infinite, and according to both
they happen to be infinite; for the argument has recognized the infinity in
the things above and below. And if they have also escaped the things on either
side, yet they have also been characterized in these things above and below.
For this reason the divine is indestructible, because it does not stand when
borne in the things above nor is it ended being emptied in the things below,
imitating as it were a circular or spherical motion without moving, where the
beginning has grown together with the end and unclear is the whence and the
where and the bound and the cut; for all things are grown together and subsist
together as into one.

=2.16= Do you wish to become a hearer of another argument too: if the Spirit
also proceeds from the Son, which is the same as to say is given essence,
either the persons of the Father and of the Son have come together into one
and both have worked the procession (and how many absurdities in these
things!) or better to say an impious confusion or contraction of persons into
one and a beginning, so to speak, a divine compound that later received the
distinction, or also it has been given essence from both persons, the
unconfused division being kept. And behold the first and later and the
incomplete and the complete; and again as from a beginning the Father and the
Son were either one person first and the Spirit has taken existence as from
one and thus later the persons were distinguished, or being two they have been
contracted into one, so that the Spirit might be proceeded from both, the two
having been fused together into the completion of one consubstantial. Will any
pious man endure these things, confessing a Trinity without beginning,
timeless, consubstantial, of equal power and wishing to cast down not even one
of the three? Do you honor the Father in these things or the Spirit or the
Son? I do not know whose honor these things are, many absurdities following to
which the impious also attends.

=2.17= And if you add to me again that saying of Christ which claims "I and
the Father are one," and as from one of both you put forth the Spirit, I
wonder at you for your shrewdness and for the exact science and unfolding
about the divine of the oracles. For what does Christ say? Are I and the
Father one person? Or an individual? Or a hypostasis? Or something of the
altogether undivided things? And indeed, how Father and how Son, if both are
one? But if indeed one thus, one ought to have said one person, and thus the
persons would have been joined into the one; but having said one he indicated
the union according to essence and the uncut conjunction according to nature,
according to which the Spirit also is joined to them and the three happen to
be one. For what? Is it not according to this argument also the Son and the
Spirit one and the Father and the Spirit one? And whichever of the three you
take having cut off, will you not say one, even if you put forward two,
according to the essence, according to the nature, according to the power and
the energy and the agreement and breathing-together unto all things? But the
one itself has not stood as one thus, as to be altogether one and undivided,
but it is cut by the properties and receives distinction by the characters.
And they are one and they have not denied the two, and they are numbered by a
dyad or even a triad, and they are recognized as truly one. Do not then take
the one in two as an individual or person and join these and work a Sabellian
confusion, and in this way do not take the division as unnatural; for the
blasphemy is of Arius, or rather to say of Satan who sows such things in the
thoughts of men.

=2.18= What then is from two as from one (for the next of the argument must be
taken up) or from one as from two, is from two altogether, unless somehow you
destroy a dyad before a triad, without which a triad would neither be nor be
said. For how from one person the Spirit proceeding from Father and Son, or of
an individual or of a hypostasis? "But from two who are one according to

nature accomplishing it": very well, for having said from two you have freed
us from cares; for the Father and the Son are two, just as a triad is also
numbered with these and the Holy Spirit. But also the two are one and the
three one according to reasons which we have spoken. What then is from two is
not from one person or one hypostasis, but from two truly.

=2.19= The absurdities then which we said follow those who claim that the Holy
Spirit proceeds from two, these would also follow upon these; and it will not
suffice those who say "I and the Father are one" for putting it forth as from
one, unless they accept the fusion or the confusion. But from there again he
says "whatever the Father has, the Son also has them"; and the Spirit has had
these thus. For Christ does not speak the things partitioned to the
hypostases, but the common things and those being with them in equal honor and
by nature; since how is the Son not without beginning or uncaused, since the
Father is? How not unbegotten, because the Father is? How not Father, since
the Father is? But these things are evident even to those who wish to hear the
sacred oracles a little and known according to whatever reasons they have been
said. About which I have also wondered very much at those who put these things
forward toward the presentation of the opinion of these, whom I would not so
much bear witness to know the things of the divine oracles, as I would not
insist even in any respect, if they do not unwillingly err in the thoughts of
the sayings; whom if the first, I reckon most wretched, since presupposing
unshaken things they have chosen to found these through the words of Christ
that seem to them firm and on account of these they strike against the rock,
upon which they will also be broken and their counsels will be crushed for
them; and if the second, I would not so much blame, but I would advise them to
go themselves to teachers, from whom they will learn the necessary things of
the proofs and will not be disposed toward the science amiss. For these voices
which Christ spoke concerning himself and the Father, if someone should
suppose, a few things apart, to say also the Spirit coming down in us, would
he have on account of these things also said that the Son is begotten from
him, such as "I and the producer are one," and "whatever this one has, I also
have," and "he who has seen me has seen that one," and all such things. But
these things, showing the sameness according to all of the essence, would fit
all also to the Spirit, yet not on account of these would he fall from his
property.

=2.20= But again there disturbs you what has been said by some, that the Son
is image of the Father, but the Holy Spirit of the Son, and from there you are
forced to say that it also proceeds from the Son. I too would have wished
this, if the necessity appeared from the arguments; but now there is no longer
necessity. For consider: if the Son is image of the Father because he is Son,
and the Spirit is image of the Son, it will be for no other reason than that
he is Son. The absurdity! Do you see where the arguments have gone forward?
And if again you insist that the Son is image of the Father because from him,
according to this then also the Spirit from the Son, since it both is and is
called image of him, you have freed us from care by saying such a thing. But
you know that the "from him" is different in both even according to your
hypotheses? For there from him, but generately, here from him, but
processionally. How then will the identity of the image be kept in different
properties? Unless then the images are not taken up according to such reasons
by those who write the more divine things, but there is something else on
account of which such things have also been said. For the Son would be a
natural image of the Father as consubstantial and co-working power, having set
before us from himself the paternal characters, in which having taken on flesh
he conversed, just as he too arranging the divine things in later times said
to Philip: "he who has seen me has seen the Father." If according to such
reasons you also take the image of the Spirit, you will find it agreeing
altogether. For since after the departure of the Son from here, or restoration
to the first, from which he has been emptied without interval, the Paraclete
has come down, of the same

essence and energy and power as the Son, not teaching things sounding against
the Son as neither did the Son of the Father, he has been shown an unvarying
image of him, having displayed in himself the things of the Son just as that
one the things of the Father.

=2.21= Do you wish that I also add that even from him, granting you all things
on account of brotherly love? But this too has been supposed bold by those who
have philosophized more accurately upon theology, furnishing an emphasis of
the procession, since apart from this it must be said. And let the Son be
image of the Father, because from him, and you know the manner of the from
him; but indeed the Spirit also is called image of the Son, because from him.
But the manner is not the same; for who would dare to say generately? It
remains then to say the different and that it is especially unconquerable. I
myself also struck beforehand when I said that the Spirit comes forth from the
Son and is supplied and given. And these things you yourself also agree with
me. What other manner of the image then do you seek, if you have even with the
things said the from him, being silent only about the procession, as never
said either by us or by those before us upon the Son? Behold I have also
solved the ambiguous of the image for you, having set the arguments before you
similarly in all things. Holding such things, or rather thinking such of the
thoughts, you will never fail of the right, you will easily cast all discord
from your mind.

=2.22= I have remembered also another argument of those who contradict us, not
least the truth and the right canon of the faith. For trying to prove that
some impossible follows our arguments they go forward to such a question: "If
the Holy Spirit does not also proceed from the Son (for in this manner the
triad has stood grown together, held in one another by the hypostases, as the
Son being from the Father, and the Spirit from the Son) by what reason or
manner will the Son be joined to the Spirit? For both will even thus be united
to the Father, since from him, but how will they be joined to one another? And
where is the extreme union of the triad, the uncut cut as one might say and
the undivided division?" O you who say these things easily and belch forth
such voices, you would perhaps even suppose that we make divisions upon the
superessential triad, such as those who converse work from genera into species
or from species into individuals or however else, and you will insist that we
set these as opposites or even circumscribe the uncircumscribable by place or
gather the unbounded in something? We do not think such things nor do we
dogmatize absurdities, we are not so mindless and unlearned as not to know
that the blessed triad that lies above, the without-beginning and without-end,
lies above every essence and nature and shape and place and time and measure
and knowledge, so as to laugh at these, whether it be one of ours or of the
opponents who wish to parallel the unshaped with numbers in shapes or planes
or solids. And if we say these things are from one, that is from the Father,
even the Spirit as caused from a cause, of which the Spirit proceeding from
the Father, which is the same as to say being given essence, remains complete
with the Son in the Son, so that the Son is never without share of the Spirit
and from there it is given and supplied and all the safe of such things, what
absurdity do we claim? And what injustice is rubbed onto the triad from our
arguments, or what dishonor attends, when we set the three consubstantial and
of equal power so that from these the union also is kept, if indeed we say
that the Spirit takes in the Son its own substantial existence from the
Father, and that all voices are also saved, I mean the Lord's and the
fathers', and that nothing whatever is harmed, as from your arguments the
incomplete is brought in together and the first and later and delimitation and
circumscription and division for those who look well? No longer has any
absurdity at all happened upon our things, or anything foreign of the
teachings of Christ and alien to the right dogmas, upon which everyone founded
has stepped as upon a firm rock, upon which also Christ the church

founded, whose gates of Hades will never prevail, blasphemous mouths and
knowing nothing but to belch forth rotten words.

=2.23= Others say that the Son works through the Spirit or in the Spirit; if
you hold to the thoughts said by me, none of the oracles will appear puzzling
to you, and the casting out the demons by the finger of God, since he has been
named arm, will be known well to you, and the giving of the living water from
the Son will be thought easily, and all such of the arguments will be grasped
accurately by you. Do you wish that I too put forward to you some small
question, having taken the starting-points also from your hypotheses? You say
altogether that the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father? "Very much so." And
you say this also from the Son? "Especially." And the Father is cause, and the
Son caused? "Yes, altogether." Therefore the Spirit has stood as caused from a
cause and a caused? "And this altogether." From a former then and a later? And
if you are vexed at such voices, since there would not be former and later in
a triad as the names hint time, yet you will accept the cause and caused and
the immediate and the mediate? "Not least." If then these have stood as your
hypotheses and you confess and do not doubt at all, attend to what follows. Is
it not a different reason to say from a cause and from a caused, and another
from an immediate and from a mediate? Altogether another, and as much another
as also the Father is another and the Son another, even if not another thing
and another thing. This then we say, the from a cause and from an immediate
and from a first, which is the same as to say that the Holy Spirit proceeds
from the Father, assigning such a voice to the first cause, to the not-caused,
to the immediate, which also the Lord has spoken. But to the second and caused
and mediate, which is the same as to say to the Son, we do not give this, so
that we may present the difference of the persons and of the properties in
them, but the other voices, so that we may prove the other. Do you see how
even according to your hypotheses the procession upon the Son is harmful and
you are tripped from your own premises, but mine harms nothing of the things
that lie there. And if you force that he puts forth the Spirit as from one of
the two, you introduce a fusion of the persons and a contraction and a
confusion (and where is such a thing in mine?) so that both your dogma is
kept, the Spirit from the Son, and ours is not shaken, I mean the not
proceeding from him, that is this taking existence being given essence
together with the Son from the Father.

=2.24= For this reason our church has not even taken it broadly to say the
Holy Spirit from the Son in any way whatever, but indeed through the Son; for
this presents the mediate and the relation which it has had toward him unto
its own manifestation. For the Holy Spirit will not otherwise be manifested
unless indeed through the Son. For having first from the Father a complete
existence not with some other nor in some other but as with the Son and in the
Son, from there again it has appeared. That then must be named procession,
this manifestation. For the coming down of the Spirit from heaven upon Jesus
being baptized presents much the energy of the thought. And keeping thus as we
speak both the nature unconfused and the order well-formed and the word of the
Lord unaltered, we also come together with your thoughts.

=2.25= If then in some of those who have theologized among the Italians the
procession of the Spirit is found also upon the Son, the stumbling-block must
be dissolved thus, that on account of the thought spoken they have used the
voice more catachrestically, since if someone asked them the things said
before, they too would have agreed most easily. And if some other or even one
of our own theologians had spoken thus, he would not have another defense. And
I think this has also happened otherwise. For since our theologians, from whom
all have drawn, set the voices of being manifested and being supplied, and
still also coming forth, in their own writings, in the translation those who
trans

lated, not accurately understanding the difference of the words toward
proceeding, have fallen unreflectingly upon such a voice; whence those who
theologize among the Italians, having taken a starting-point, have used it
themselves too, to whom there is also pardon as not then speaking the things
of the voices accurately. Cyril will bear witness to me for what is said. And
this man was of Alexandria and great in the divine things and at the peak in
the contemplation in the dogmas and the science of the Spirit. For having said
in one of his writings that the Spirit is proper to the Son, and Theodoret of
Cyrus having taken hold of the saying (and this man was wise in the divine
things) lest perhaps as of the Spirit proceeding from the Son he named it
proper, he did not take hold of the voice and agree with the saying as having
declared it for this reason, but brought on another cause, that as
consubstantial with him and of equal power and always attending him and given
by him he called it proper. Can anyone speak a clearer mark of this testimony?
As foreign then the thought was also reckoned by the divine Cyril to the
church of Christ, whom we know first and most of all in theology.

=2.26= We know then that even to some of the God-speaking men the things said
thus in the divine oracles did not seem to be referred to the Spirit itself,
but to gifts of the Spirit; for the spiritual gift is also called spirit just
as the Spirit the Lord. As then the Father gives gifts of the Spirit in whom
he would wish and the Spirit itself being self-master and self-authoritative
its own to whom it would wish, so indeed also the Son gives the things of the
Spirit to whom he would nod on account of the growing-together and
breathing-together of one another. Thus then it was thought by some as I have
said. But I myself do not insist that the Spirit itself is not given from the
Son. For he has it whole in himself not as giving it essence, but as receiving
it complete in himself given essence from the Father, which we say remaining
in the Son as proceeding from the Father and from there being sent; and this
voice I think common of the utterances apart indeed from the proceeding.

=2.27= Thus then I myself also have thought and have been minded and have
spoken, being persuaded by Christ and following his teachings and apostolic
and the paternal writings. Remove then this small stumbling-block, which makes
much division in the inhabited world; cut from the common symbol the
few-syllabled word that works long and endless battles. Why do we dissolve the
common bond? Why do we cut the spiritual cord that gathers us? Why do we not
keep the threefold chain as it has been handed down to us? For this was not
only joining of souls, but also holding-together of bodies. And from when it
has been cut by envy of Satan who from the beginning begrudges our things, how
many and of what kind of evils he has done! It seems then you no longer have
memory of our ancient love, O Italians. If then you have come to forgetfulness
of this, I would remind you of it. No other of the nations has gone forward to
such concord and breathing-together as Greeks and Italians. And reasonably;
for from Greeks even the logical sciences and the lessons are to the Italians.
And from there, so that they might not be bounded by these ethnic names,
another new has been built over against elder Rome, so that from cities thus
greatest having the name in common all might be named Romans and as they have
the common of the faith they might also have that of the calling. And as from
Christ they obtained the same most honorable name, so they also brought on
themselves the ethnic. And all the other things too were common to these,
offices, laws, words, councils, law-courts, piety itself, nothing that was not
common to Romans the older and the newer. But O the change! The things of the
former breathing-together have come down to a thing most hateful, and as we
abounded in friendship, so indeed we superabound also in hatred. And now you
are disposed toward us not less

than toward the impious and the circumcised, if not even most of all, and a
sword distinguishes the things among us and a saber dwells in the one flock of
Christ, and the things of the nations rejoice, by whom the calling of Christ
is insulted, and things altogether without truce and without peace for us.

=2.28= What has procured these things? The small word set before you in the
symbol, which becoming common to the impious you have cut off and
appropriated, having falsified it a little. But we do not wish it to be
falsified at all and most justly. And you have done this in a love of rule and
boldly, which ought not. For if you had something else to think, you ought to
mix it in other things and not in the common; "do not move the bounds of
fathers" you hear the wise man advising. I at least keep the deposit as I
received it, but you counterfeit this. Who then of us is cause of the enmity?
Who sends away the peace as hated? I keep the common things unbroken, but you
insert into these even other things, so that it might be altered toward the
newer. And who of the brothers will be persuaded by you? For I will dare to
say according to Paul: if anyone should try to add one tittle in this symbol
of the faith, whether he be Greek or Italian or some angel from heaven, I at
least am silent about what follows, being cautious of the ill-omened, but
another more hostilely disposed will not spare to speak it out. For why do you
think that you accomplish such things as dear to Christ? I bear witness to you
as truly; even if what was set by you in the symbol were most true, you would
not have pardon at the time of that great and just judgment on account of
having walked autonomously in these things and having worked by yourselves
some new private road of the faith. And it has been recognized by us that what
is said does not even hold to correctness; it must therefore be taken away on
account of these things. Cut off (for again I advise you in a friendly and
brotherly way) the scandal from the symbol, lest Satan so prevail over us as
to change us into such arrogance and lifting-up, as not even through brotherly
love to come down to the just things and to hold to what belongs to the
fathers, but to reckon all things nothing toward what has been thought and
said by us. For if you will wish according to the command of the Lord not to
scandalize us, this will be accomplished easily for you. In common with one
mind and word in Spirit let us say the symbol of the faith and with one heart
and mouth let us proclaim this in all things. We do not demand of you great
and difficult things nor things striking far from the just, but things divine
and fitting, just and holy, not things granted to Satan, but the things loved
by the Spirit. For whose sake then the war, for this let the peace also be
accomplished, in which God also more rejoices. Let us then embrace one another
and clasp in concord of the Spirit and greet with a holy kiss, thinking the
same things, dogmatizing the same things, so that in the same tents, the
eternal and blessed, we may be gathered by the one Spirit. Let there be a
common joy at our union to the heavenly and the earthly; for they will
rejoice, I know well, in these things, angels and all the power that lies
above, just as just now they are distressed at the division, and those who
follow our laws or words would leap, having seen those who lead them of one
mind, but demons would look gloomy and the impious of the nations would cower.
Which may it come about most quickly, divine craftsman of the all and provider
of things that have come to be and corrector of things borne badly, the Father
the Son and the Holy Spirit, because to you belongs glory and magnificence
unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
