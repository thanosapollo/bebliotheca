THE FIRST LETTER OF SAINT MACARIUS TO MONKS

The Master of all, the philanthropic God, always grants good starting-points
to the race of men toward the knowledge of the things to come, and wishing
each to receive progress toward the better things, to whom he suggests by a
nod of kindness, always drawing toward the good the man who has been made
according to his image; for the soul having by nature the inclination toward
the good things and hastening to desire the better things, the left-hand part
of wickedness by terrible cunning always puts forward to the simpler souls a
phantasy of good things unto the darkening of the things that are truly good.
Wherefore the unspeakable love of the grace of God teaches us knowledge of
truth through the God-inspired scriptures according to the one who says: Teach
me kindness and instruction and knowledge, that thus we may be strong to
thrust away ignorance, the cause of all evils, and being guided by the light
of true knowledge may be able to drive out completely the fleshly and
irrational mind that is in us, which is strengthened deceitfully by a promise
of knowledge, having learned that the knowledge of the truth is a cause of
salvation. Since therefore you also, O most longed-for and beloved brethren,
have often asked from us to set forth a word, 234 of what manner the one who
has withdrawn from fleshly pleasures and from the way of life of this age
ought to have .... and those who have come together to the same place
according to the apostolic character in the Acts, having become a sacred
company and a band of brotherhood, having chosen to be together piously with
one another through all, both how they ought to make their common dwelling
with one another, and what constitution of manners they ought to have, and
what is the aim of the way of extreme virtue, and what is the will of God, the
good and well-pleasing and perfect, and of what sort is the course and contest
of those who desire to arrive at the perfect and who hasten to come to the
measure of spiritual stature; and that we may not stretch the word to length,
these things and the things that follow them you asked from us to be written
for you through a letter unto a continual reminder of soul-profiting
meditation, being persuaded, that is, concerning the pious dogma of the
orthodox faith, that there is one godhead of the worshipful and blessed
Trinity, one essence, one will, one glory, one worship to offer to the three
hypostases of the one godhead, just as also in the holy mystery of baptism we
piously confessed the good confession before many witnesses. For this reason
we also, having seen your great impulse toward the good and having seen the
desire toward the summit of virtue, did not shrink from making a reminder
through a few things, taking the starting-points of the thoughts from the
God-inspired scriptures themselves; and at the same time we also set beside
them the scriptural testimonies themselves unto full-assurance of undoubting
faith, that no one may think that we say something from ourselves and may
suspect us as thinking oversized things of vanity. 235 Let there be therefore
first for all who have dedicated themselves to God and who always strive to
present their bodies a living holy sacrifice well-pleasing to God, the
reasonable worship, the aim of piety set before them, the illumination [and]
unto believing according to the holy promises of the gospels and of the
remaining scriptures, giving themselves over unto every good work and unto
every practice of virtue; and to be firmly persuaded that through the grace of
Christ and the divine power it is possible for the healing and the cleansing
of the passions of dishonor that are energized in the soul and the body to
come about in us completely, because the things impossible with us

are possible with the God who has promised; for the passions of sin came upon
the soul and the body afterward through the transgression of the first-formed
Adam; and to be counted worthy of the Spirit of adoption perfectly and wholly
unto sanctification of soul and body in all full-assurance according to the
one who said that Our gospel did not come to us in word only, but also in
power and in Holy Spirit and in much full-assurance, as you know; and again
May your spirit and the soul and the body be preserved whole blamelessly in
the day of our Lord Jesus Christ; 236 of which the earnest of the unspeakable
benefaction we who have believed received at the holy baptism of the blessed
Trinity, having been entrusted with this great and undefiled mystery unto
increase and progress of the perfect inheritance and unto multiplication of
the talent. For the divine and comforting Spirit that was given to the
apostles and through them was ministered to the one and true church of God is
present from the hour of baptism, according to the analogy of faith, variously
and in many ways with each one who has come to baptism from sincere faith; yet
unto multiplication and working-out and increase each receives the mina, as it
has been said in the gospel. For just as the infant being born in this age
does not always stand in the infant age, but day by day receives the increase
by a certain necessity of sequence according to the unspeakable laws of
nature, until it arrives at a perfect man and at the completion of the
rational thoughts, in the same manner also the one being born from above of
water and Spirit ought not to stand in the infant habit of spiritual stature,
but using contest and toil and much endurance day by day because of the
wrestling toward the antagonist he must progress and increase unto the
completion of spiritual stature according to what has been said by the
Apostle: Until we all arrive at the unity of the faith and of the knowledge of
the Son of God, unto a perfect man, unto 237 the measure of the stature of the
fullness of Christ, that we may no longer be infants, tossed and carried about
by every wind of teaching in cunning toward the method of deceit, but speaking
truth in love we may grow into him in all things, who is Christ; and again
elsewhere the same says: Do not be conformed to this age, but be transformed
by the renewal of your mind unto your proving what is the will of God, the
good and well-pleasing and perfect, saying that those who have come to the
measure of stature and of rational completion of the Spirit arrive at the
perfect will of God. But in the bodily birth by a certain necessity of
sequence and by a law of nature the child being born arrives at the manly age,
providence having thus ordered by a bound of necessity and not by choice of
will that the bodily stature be completed. But in the birth from above from
the Spirit he did not ordain this sequence, but he set toil and contest and a
course with much endurance upon the self-determined choice of the man
according to what has been said by the Lord: Strive to enter through the
narrow door; and again: Use force, for the forceful seize the kingdom of the
heavens, and He who has endured unto the end, this one will be saved; and In
your endurance gain your souls; and the Apostle Through endurance we run the
contest set before us; and Thus, he says, run, that you may seize; and again
As ministers of God in much endurance and the things that follow.

238 Thus the divine grace has ordered, that by his own judgment and his own
will and toil and contest each may take on the noetic increase, according as
he believes and according as he contends; and according as he loves and
according as by bodily and psychic contest unto


all well-pleasing of the commandments he gives himself over, so much also he
acquires the participation of the Spirit unto the spiritual increase of the
renewal of the mind, receiving the salvation by grace and by divine gift, but
receiving the progress and increase unto the perfect measure of spiritual
stature by faith and love and contest of self-determined choice, that as by
grace, so also by righteousness he will be heir of eternal life, neither
receiving the increase of progress wholly through the divine power and grace
without the co-working and eagerness of the man, nor being able wholly through
his own power and eagerness and strength without the co-working and help of
the Holy Spirit to arrive at the perfect will of God and the measure of
freedom and purity. For if the Lord does not build a house and guard a city,
in vain has the one guarding and toiling and building kept watch; and again:
For not in their sword did they inherit land and their arm did not save them,
although they were warring with swords and 239 arms, but your right hand and
your arm and the illumination of your face, which things make clear the power
of God co-working with their own contest; and at the same time also cutting
out the conceit of those who think highly of themselves because of a few
virtues, the prophet taught that life comes to those who hope by faith and
love through grace.

What then is the will of God, unto which the Apostle exhorts each, calling him
to hasten to arrive? Or is it concerning those of whom the Lord has spoken,
blessing them Blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God; and
again: Become therefore you perfect, as your heavenly Father is perfect.
Through these he promises the complete cleansing of sin, which is the complete
deliverance from the passions of dishonor, and the perfect taking-up of
extreme virtue, which is cleansing and sanctification of the heart through the
participation of the perfect and divine Spirit, the full-assurance coming
about of a soul that with faith and love gives itself over to God wholly from
the whole. Just as also through David the Spirit, showing the perfect aim of
the truth and teaching those who desire to be shown sons of light and to
become heirs of life to seek this from God and to believe, says: Let my heart
become blameless in your 240 justifications, that I may not be put to shame;
he signified that those who have not yet acquired the heart blameless from
every stain and filth bear shame and dishonor; for again he says Then I shall
not be put to shame in my looking upon all your commandments; the Holy Spirit
taught that the completion of all the commandments and a perfect withdrawal
from every transgression is to possess boldness toward God. And again: A clean
heart, the prophet said praying, create in me, O God, and renew a right spirit
in my inward parts and with a governing spirit, he says, support me, which
presents the same aim. And asking again Who will go up into the mountain of
the Lord or who will stand in his holy place, he adds who these are and of
what sort they are: Innocent in hands and clean in heart; so that the Spirit
made known that the sin accomplished through deed and word and thought is to
be cut out of us completely through his own energy, and that we must strive,
praying and hoping through faith, to arrive day by day at the perfect purity
of God. For the Apostle teaching expressly of what sort the souls ought to be
that have withdrawn from marriage of flesh and from bonds of the world, but
desire to practice virginity, says: The virgin is anxious about the things of
the Lord, that she may be holy not in body only, but also in spirit,
commanding her to be outside of practical and of reasoning, 241 that is of
manifest and of hidden, sins,


[as] inasmuch as she longs to be joined as a bride of Christ to a heavenly
king, pure and unstained. For the soul that longs to be joined to him and that
desires to become one spirit with him must be made like to that blameless and
undefiled beauty according to what has been said by the blessed John: And
everyone, he says, who has this hope purifies himself, just as that one is
pure; and Become imitators of me, just as I also of Christ. For not from
manifest sins only, such as fornication or theft or murder or gluttony or
slander or falsehood or idle speech or crying-out or laughter or jesting or
love of money or greed, must the soul that has denied fleshly communion, and
has withdrawn from the world and is practicing virginity, and has confessed
herself unto joining and communion of the incorruptible bridegroom, keep
chaste, but much more she must be clean of the more violent passions of the
soul and of hidden sins, that is of desire, vainglory, man-pleasing,
hypocrisy, love of rule, deceit, malice, hatred, unbelief, envy, lifting-up,
jealousy, self-pleasing, self-love, conceit, and of the like invisible
passions of dishonor.

For Scripture knows to consider the hidden sins of the soul, if indeed we
believe the words of the Spirit, equal and like to the outward sins; For the
Lord, he says, has scattered the bones of man-pleasers, and A man of bloods
and a deceitful man 242 the Lord abhors, numbering the deceit and the murder
into one transgression; and against those who speak peace with their
neighbors, but evils are in their hearts, Give them, Lord, according to their
works. And again: For also in heart you work lawlessness in the earth. And the
Lord blaming the man-pleasers says: They do all things to be seen by men;
wherefore he also commands saying: See that you do not do your alms before
men; but if not, you have no reward with your Father who is in the heavens;
that is, do not wish to seek from men and to hunt praise or glory through the
good practices, but rather from God, whose glory is true and eternal and whose
dishonor is eternal. And the Woe to you when men speak well of you, that is
when you wish to hear well from men and to rejoice in the glories and praises
that come from them; since how was it possible that those doing good not be
manifest to all? when the Lord himself says: Let your light shine before men,
that they may see your good works and glorify your Father who is in the
heavens. Contend unto the glory of God, he says, to do every good practice and
not unto your own glory, nor seek and rejoice in the praises of short-lived
243 men, but await the praise and the glory from God alone; just as also the
blessed David says: From you is my praise, and again: In the Lord my soul will
be praised; as also the Apostle commands even unto eating and drinking to come
about unto the glory of God. For whether you eat or drink or do anything, he
says, do all things unto the glory of God; for the Lord showed those who seek
glory or praises from men to be unbelieving, having said How can you believe,
receiving glory from men and not seeking the glory from the only God? And
John, comparing hatred to murder, says: He who hates his brother is a
man-killer; and You know that every man-killer does not have eternal life in
himself. He compares the hatred in the hidden that comes about against his
brother to murder. But also the Apostle Paul, declaring those who have been
given over to the passions of dishonor because of their senseless and
disobedient heart and numbering their


working unto the worse of destruction, numbering hidden and manifest sins into
one, says: And just as they did not approve to have God in knowledge,
wherefore God also gave them over unto an unapproved mind, to do the things
that are not fitting, filled with all injustice, wickedness, fornication,
greed, malice; filled244 with envy, murder, strife, deceit, malice;
whisperers, slanderers, God-haters, insolent, proud, boasters, inventors of
evils, disobedient to parents, senseless, faithless, without affection,
implacable, unmerciful; who having known the justification of God, that those
who practice such things are worthy of death, and the things that follow. You
see how he declared not only the manifest sins that alone are abominable among
men, but with the manifest he also numbered the hidden passions of the soul,
deceit, pride, strife, malice, boasting, hatred, senselessness; and again the
Lord says: The high thing among men is an abomination with God, and He who
lifts himself up will be humbled, and he who humbles himself will be lifted
up; and Wisdom says Unclean with God is every high-hearted one. And gathering
the like things from the scriptures we would find very many things said
against the hidden passions of dishonor in the soul. For the Holy Spirit,
knowing the hidden passions of dishonor to be more violent and to exist rooted
in the soul, teaching us through what manner their deliverance is able to come
about, says through David: From my hidden things cleanse me and from alien
things spare your servant; so that through much entreaty and faith and the
perfect good impulse toward God we are able through 245 the power of the
Spirit to prevail over the hidden passions of dishonor. For just as we strive
to guard the outer man, which is the body, from the manifest sins as a temple
of God, just as the Apostle said: He who corrupts the temple of God, speaking
concerning the body, God will corrupt this one, so also the inner man, which
is the soul, must be striven for and contended for to guard from every
defilement of filthy thoughts as a bride of Christ according to what has been
said With all guarding keep your heart; for from these are the exits of life
[as a bride of Christ]. And this will be for us to accomplish when we contend
against and always contradict the wicked thoughts of vainglory and hatred and
conceit and deceit, of desire and unbelief, of jealousy and love of rule, and
searching ourselves with all exactness and keeping the mind unyoked and
unagreeing toward the hidden passions of the soul, and giving ourselves over
in all good practices with force and toil and contest because the opposing one
hinders us; for 246 the blessed Moses, hinting at the single-mannered
straightness and showing through types, made clear that the soul must not
follow two judgments, of evil and of good, but only the good; and that two
fruits must not be farmed in us, of the worse and of the useful, but working
the useful things in a single manner we must be wholly alienated from the
worse. And he says through riddles: You shall not yoke in your threshing-floor
alien animals upon the same, such as an ox and an ass, but having yoked the
animals of the same kind you shall thresh your harvest; that is, that in the
threshing-floor of your heart virtue and wickedness may not have the working
upon the same, but only virtue. You shall not weave, he says, linen into a
woolen garment nor again wool into a linen one; you shall not farm in the
country of your land two fruits upon the same nor a second of the year; you
shall not put an animal of another kind upon another unto generation, but you
shall yoke the same-kind to the same-kind. And through all these he hints
mystically that wickedness and virtue must not be farmed in us, but that the
virtues must be brought forth from us in a single manner through the more
divine generation; and that the soul must not with two spirits, the spirit of
the world and the Spirit of God,


have communion and be united, but he ordered the soul, being united and joined
only to the Spirit 247 of God, to bear as fruit the holy generations of virtue
and the fruits of the Spirit, nor for the choice to agree with two faces, the
worse and the better, but using only the good and being wholly alienated from
the evil to farm in the soul the single-mannered fruit of the virtues
according to what has been written: Therefore I was set right toward all your
commandments; I hated every unjust way; and I hated injustice and abhorred it,
but I loved your law, that thus the Lord, seeing the contest of the soul, may
by his own love of mankind make the great healing of the hidden passions
through the power of the Spirit completely in us, working the vengeance of our
noetic enemies; just as the Lord himself, going through concerning the widow
who was worn down by the adversary and who came to the unjust ruler, and who
was seeking the vengeance from him and through the shamelessness of her long
remaining obtained her vengeance, teaches us not to grow weary in the
entreaties. For he was speaking to them also a parable toward the need always
to pray and not to grow weary and the things that follow. The not growing
weary hints at the long remaining of the prayer; and after 248 these things
the Lord See what the unjust judge says; how much more will your heavenly
Father make the vengeance of those who cry to him night and day; yes I say to
you he will make the vengeance quickly. For of what sort of contest the
Apostle had on behalf of the disciples and unto what sort of spiritual measure
he was eager for them to come, praying for them from disposition, and at the
same time also making the extreme aim of Christianity manifest to all who wish
to run without toil † truth-lovingly and making evident the apostolic canon of
the new creation to those who strive to progress unto the things in front:
Admonishing, he says, every man and teaching in all wisdom, that we may
present every man perfect in Christ, unto which I also toil contending. And
again he prays that those counted worthy of the seal of the Spirit of the
promise through baptism receive increase of spiritual progress saying: For
this reason I also, having heard the faith according to you and the love unto
all the saints, do not cease praying for you and asking, that the God of our
Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of glory, may give you a spirit of wisdom and of
revelation in knowledge of him; the eyes of your heart having been illuminated
unto your knowing what is the hope of his calling and what the wealth of the
glory of his inheritance in the saints and what the surpassing greatness of
his pow249er unto us who believe. And having noted exactly the manner of the
participation and partaking of the Spirit that comes about in full-assurance
According to the energy, he says, of his power, which he energized in Christ,
having raised him from the dead; according to that energy of the Spirit, he
says, that you also may receive the full-assurance. And again in the same
letter having gone forward a little, desiring them to come unto some higher
increase and progress of spiritual perfection, he prays saying: For this cause
I bend my knees toward the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, from whom every
fatherhood in heavens and upon earth is named, that he may give you according
to the wealth of his glory, to be strengthened in power through his Spirit
unto the inner man, for Christ to dwell through the faith in your hearts,
rooted and founded in love; that you may be able to grasp with all the saints
what is the breadth and length and depth and height, and to know the
surpassing love of the knowledge of Christ, that you may be filled unto all
the fullness of God.


And in another letter teaching concerning the same things and showing to the
disciples the excess of the spiritual wealth and exhorting them to hasten to
arrive at the perfect measure he was saying: But be zealous for the spiritual
gifts; and I show you a still more excessive way. If I speak with the tongues
of the angels, but do not have love, I have become sounding bronze or a
clanging cymbal; and if I have prophecy and 250 know all the mysteries and all
the knowledge, and if I have all the faith so as to remove mountains, but do
not have love, I am benefited nothing; and if I feed out all my possessions
and give over my body that I may be burned, but do not have love, I am
nothing. And he adds of what sort the fruits of love are and how those who
have acquired this have been wholly redeemed from the passions of wickedness,
saying: Love does not envy; love does not boast; is not puffed up; does not
behave unseemly; does not seek its own; does not reckon the evil; does not
rejoice upon the injustice, but rejoices with the truth; covers all things;
endures all things; believes all things; hopes all things. And again the same:
Love never falls. And this "3never falls"3 signifies this, that those who are
in the aforementioned gifts of the Spirit and have not yet received the
redemption of the passions through the fullest and energetic love of the
Spirit are still in danger and contest and fear because of the wrestling
toward the spiritual things of wickedness, even if they have acquired
spiritual gifts. For see of what sort and how great a spiritual measure,
inasmuch as it is no longer under a fall and a passion, he showed to those who
wish to walk the way of Christianity exactly; 251 so that even tongues of
angels and prophecy and faith and all the knowledge and gifts of healings are
nothing as toward a comparison of the most extreme virtue and of the fullness
of the love of perfection, although those also are workings of the same
Spirit. But that, if someone is counted worthy of those gifts, he may not
think that through the newer energy of the grace he has arrived at the perfect
and, having supposed that he no longer needs anything, may remain away from
the perfect measures of passionlessness, being fully assured only in the
lesser gifts, for this reason he showed the aim of perfection, that each,
recognizing himself poor from so great a wealth, may unceasingly and
unyieldingly contend the good contest, and may so run the spiritual course,
until he seizes, just as he has said: Thus run, that you may seize. See how
through many things he sets the bound and aim and canon of piety and of
perfection into the manifest for all, praying for his own disciples that all
arrive at it; for again the same in another, showing the newness and change
that comes about in Christ for the faithful and those who love the Lord, says:
For neither circumcision has any strength nor uncircumcision, but a new
creation; and as many as walk by this canon, peace upon them and mercy, and
upon the Israel of God. And again: If anyone is in Christ, a new creation, the
ancient things have passed. Through one phrase of the new creation he has 252
set the apostolic canon into the manifest; and the new creation is what else
than, just as the same says elsewhere: That he may present the church to
himself glorious, not having stain or wrinkle or any of such things, but that
she may be holy and blameless, the complete purity from the shame, which is
the complete deliverance from the passions, and the full unspeakable and
mystical communion of the divine power of the Spirit in sanctification, being
energized in hearts of saints who have been counted worthy of a newer
enjoyment beyond nature and spiritual in virtues, this the Apostle taught to
be the new creation in Christ. But also the Clean out the old leaven, that you
may be a new lump; and the We keep feast not in old leaven, but in unleavened
things of sincerity and of truth, presents the same


aim. And because we are not sufficient toward the wrestling against the
principalities and the authorities and the spiritual things of wickedness he
exhorts us to take up the panoply of God; put on the breastplate of
righteousness and shoe the feet in readiness of the gospel of peace and gird
the loin in truth, having taken up upon all the shield of faith, through which
he says you will be able to quench all the fiery darts of the wicked one,
which are the energies of the defiled passions; And receive the helmet of
salvation and the holy sword of the Spirit, which is a word of God. Through
the aforementioned spiritual 253 weapons beyond our nature the Apostle showed
that we will have strength to stand toward the hard methods of the devil, and
the manner through which we are able to acquire these he noted exactly having
said: Through all prayer and entreaty praying in every season in Spirit and
unto it keeping watch in all perseverance and entreaty; wherefore he also
prays for all saying: The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ and the love of God
and Father and the communion of the Holy Spirit be with all of you. And again:
May your spirit and the soul and the body be preserved whole blamelessly in
the day of our Lord Jesus Christ. See according to how many a manner he has
set into the manifest the straight way of righteousness and the extreme aim of
Christianity, exhorting all and desiring them to arrive at it, unto which
those who love truth, looking and contending wholly without growing weary,
pray by faith and hope and every constitution of virtue and desire to come to
it. For these will be able also purely and perfectly and blamelessly to
fulfill the great commandments, upon which the law and the prophets hang, You
shall love the Lord your God from your whole heart and from your whole soul
and from your whole strength, and your neighbor as yourself, having been
delivered from all wickedness. And if we also seemed to stretch the narration
254 further concerning the aim of piety, of what sort the Lord and the
apostles and all the God-inspired scriptures hand down to us, presenting it
through many testimonies, we do not consider that we have done it out of
season, that from every manner we may be unhesitatingly and undoubtingly fully
assured, what is our hope of the faith and what is the aim and of what sort
the course is set before us, especially those who have taken up the solitary
life and have lifted themselves from worldly bonds and fleshly communion and
have chosen to keep virginity in the Lord, that each man and each woman,
attending to where he has been called and unto what sort of measure he ought
to arrive, may cease from all conceit because of certain accomplishments that
have come about, considering themselves truly to be nothing because of the
excess of the perfect measure of the calling and of the untraceable wealth of
the love of Christ, and in every manner and with every strength and with all
eagerness may always give themselves over readily unto the good, according to
the word of the Lord denying still with all the things of this age even their
own soul, taking up the cross each day with joy and gladness and following
him, having the Lord himself in all things become a type and way and pattern
and aim for us according to what has been said: Become imitators of me, just
as I also of Christ, and again: Through endurance let us run the contest set
before us, looking unto the pioneer and perfecter of the faith, Jesus, who
instead of the joy set before him endured a cross, having despised shame, and
has sat at the right of God; lest ever being fully assured 255 and resting
upon certain gifts of the Spirit or upon some few accomplishments and as
already having arrived at the perfect we may be deceived by cunning of
wickedness with conceit of righteousness, remaining away from the greater
progresses of the Spirit; but as lovers of truth until the end the toil and


the affliction and contest and course let us have unceasing, stretching always
day by day unto the things in front and forgetting the things behind, always
hungering and thirsting the righteousness, having the heart crushed, inasmuch
as we have not yet arrived at the calling to which we were called nor seized
the perfect measure of Christianity nor yet arrived at the perfect love of
Christ; for those who truly desire to obtain the extreme aim that has been set
before and shown beforehand are always wounded by the longing unto that
calling of unutterable perfection, being fully assured and remaining in none
of the lesser good things, of righteousness of fastings or of vigils or of
alms or of a participation of gifts of the Spirit, until last breath; but
giving themselves over wholly in all good and fine things and all virtues and
thus remaining in want for the sake of virtue they will never think to fulfill
a law of wisdom; but although being honorable from God they consider
themselves unworthy, and being in spiritual progress they are with themselves
as beginning, and being great they set themselves at nothing and judge
themselves as being nothing; and thus according as they believed and according
as they loved they will receive back the rewards of the gospels of the
kingdom. Such souls will be able to be well-pleasing to God and to become
heirs of the kingdom, those who have the heart crushed and are poor in the
spirit, always hungering and 256 thirsting the righteousness, aiming at the
perfect honors, they will be repaid with extreme gifts because of their
extreme love toward God. And concerning the hope of the aim, of what sort
those who have taken up the solitary life and have embraced virginity
especially ought to have, enough has been said; but concerning the gathering
