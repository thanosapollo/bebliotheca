seem to be such to human beings, believing in Christ Jesus crucified, and

we who are now on earth later in heavens, but also that if we sin we shall be

23–31 [but we proclaim Christ crucified, to Jews a stumbling-block and to
nations foolishness, but to those who are called, both Jews and Greeks, Christ
God's power and God's wisdom. ... For see your calling, brothers, that not
many wise according to flesh, not many powerful, not many well-born. But God
chose the foolish things of the world, in order that he might put the wise to
shame, and God chose the weak things of the world, in order that he might put
the strong things to shame, and God chose the ignoble things of the world and
the things that have been despised, and the things that are not, in order that
he might bring to nothing the things that are, so that no flesh may boast
before God. And from him you are in Christ Jesus, who became wisdom for us
from God, both righteousness and sanctification and redemption, in order that
as it is written he who boasts, let him boast in the Lord.] [Of Origen] We
received power from believing in Jesus Christ crucified; and as much as we
fall short in <this> faith, so much we fall short in the power of having in
ourselves the things from God. And if you wish to understand what it is I will
destroy the wisdom of the wise and the understanding of the understanding I
will set aside, understand for me someone altogether noble in the heretical
words, say Marcion or Basilides, and so seeking with power on behalf of the
heretical words that not just anyone is able to stand against him; then
understand someone sufficient according to the truth saying that which also
made us sufficient as ministers of a new covenant, and showing all those
things to be foolishness on which the deceived one had trusted. He therefore
destroys the wisdom of those, not of the ones who are truly wise, Isaiah,
Jeremiah or



Solomon; he destroys the seeming wisdom, not the true. And how also In the
wisdom of God the world did not know God through wisdom? The wisdom of God is
in law and prophets; the world did not know the Christ proclaimed in law and
prophets. For this reason at the completion of the ages he sent Jesus Christ
to be crucified on behalf of <the> race of human beings, in order that by the
foolishness of the proclamation those who have believed in Jesus Christ
crucified might believe. And he added to Not many wise the according to flesh,
knowing that there are differences of the wise, and some are wise according to
flesh and some according to spirit; and wise according to flesh are those who
have practiced little words alone and who beautify whatever is not truth as
though it were truth. Yet not even so did he shut the wise according to flesh
out from the faith; for he did not say No wise man according to flesh comes to
the word, but that they are exceedingly few. And powerful according to flesh
are those who have the authorities; and you will understand according to
spirit, if you see in what manner the Savior was not powerful according to
flesh; he was at any rate handed over and crucified from weakness; but he was
powerful according to spirit, healing every disease and every infirmity in the
people. So look at the things according to the apostles, in what manner they
were powerful according to spirit. One is powerful according to spirit also
who has lived according to the spirit and by spirit puts to death the deeds of
the body. Likewise you will understand also upon the well-born; well-born
according to flesh are those from rich and glorious fathers; but according to
spirit well-born are the sons of God concerning whom it is written But as many
as received him he gave them authority to become children of God. What then
did God choose? not the foolish things simply, but with the addition of the
world; we who believe are fools as toward the world. They therefore laugh at
us saying we are fools "Christians say a resurrection of dead and that we live
after death and that Jesus whom Jews crucified has been born from a virgin,"
and as many such things. Let those then laugh at us and call us fools; for a
soulish human being does not receive the things of the spirit; for it is
foolishness to him. God therefore chose the foolish things of the world, in
order that he might put the wise to shame. Not the wise simply, but according
to the common of the world; in order that he may say God chose the foolish
things of the world, in order that he might put the wise of the world to
shame; for truly the wise of the world are put to shame, when they themselves
worship idols but the unlettered and private persons die on behalf of not
committing idolatry. And God chose the ignoble things of the world and the
things that have been despised; here again with the addition of the world. For
what is more well-born than a soul that leaves the idols and says How false
did our fathers acquire idols and there is not in them one who brings rain.
God therefore chose the ignoble things of the world but well-born of God; only
let us succeed from whom we shall not be despised by God, concerning whom it
is written a worker of evil is despised before him. And the things that are
not, in order that he might bring to nothing the things that are; he named the
things that are not the foolish things of the world, the weak, the ignoble,
the things that have been despised; the things that are the well-born of the
world; for in the world these are somebodies, we are not, being worn down by
all and being despised. God has now chosen the things that are not, in order
that he might bring to nothing the things that are, so that no flesh may boast
and say For this reason I was chosen, that I am well-born. And from him you
are in Christ Jesus, who became wisdom for us from God both righteousness and
sanctification and redemption; for this is why Christ is all these things for
us, in order that the thing written may come to be He who boasts, let him
boast in the Lord. For if the one who boasts in wisdom ought to boast, and
Christ is the wisdom, it is clear that the one who boasts in Christ boasts;
and upon the remaining things likewise, I mean both of sanctification and of
righteousness. 9 ii 4–7 [and my word and my proclamation not in persuasive
words of wisdom, but in demonstration of spirit and of power, in order that
your faith may not be in wisdom of human beings but in power of God. And we
speak wisdom among the perfect, but a wisdom not of this age nor of the rulers

wisdom that has been hidden, which God predestined before the ages unto our

they would not have crucified the Lord of glory.] [Of Origen] In order that
someone may not say So then, since he brought the demonstrations of the faith
in power and in spirit, he had no wisdom at all, but this is the mystery of
piety, to believe only, and there is no promise of wisdom in him; he adds and
says And we speak wisdom among the perfect, but a wisdom not of this age nor
of the rulers of this age who are being brought to nothing. For it is one
thing to lead some into the faith, another to reveal the wisdom of God. We
therefore unfold the wisdom of God not to those being led in nor to those
beginning nor to those who have not yet given a demonstration of the sound
life; but whenever, having exercised the senses as one must toward discernment
of both good and evil, he becomes fit also toward hearing wisdom, then we
speak wisdom among the perfect; and I say a wisdom not of this age nor similar
to the wisdom of the rulers of this age. And at the same time he has made
clear that the lessons carried among the others are not of human beings
according to the beginning but of invisible powers who are being brought to
nothing. And I would say that poetic wisdom is of this age, and perhaps also
the rhetorical; and also the wisdom that professes to speak concerning heaven
and earth and concerning administration of the whole. If then we speak wisdom
and say it among the perfect, we do not say that of this age; for neither
through poems nor through some such art. [Of Origen] But we speak wisdom in a
mystery, things that have not been known to the rulers of this age who work
the wisdom of the wise of this world; and thus they did not know; they plotted
against our savior and Lord Jesus Christ and crucified him; for if they had
known they would not have crucified the Lord of glory. One must therefore seek
wisdom also in the word, but after the faith, after the manner of life,
according to the thing written I desired wisdom, keep commandments. 10 ii 9–11
[But as it is written Things which an eye has not seen and an ear has not
heard and upon a human heart have not gone up, the things which God prepared
for those who love him. But to us God revealed them through the spirit, for
the spirit searches all things, even the depths of God. For who of human
beings knows the things of the human being except the spirit of the human
being that is in him? so also the things of God no one has known except the
spirit of God.] [Of Origen] Scripture knows a difference of those who love God
and those who fear God, and it knows those who love as far excelling those who
fear. Some things then have been prepared also for those who fear; yet he
prepared a greater thing for those who love than the things prepared for those
who fear. For the spirit searches all things, even the depths of God. There is
a spirit which is able to search all things; the soul of the human being is
not able to search all things, but there needed to come to be a better spirit
in us, in order that, that one having come to be in us searching all things
even the depths of God, and having been mixed with us, we with that one may
search together all things even the depths of God. 11 ii 12–15 [And we did not
receive the spirit of the world but the spirit that is from God, in order that
we may know the things that have been given us by God; which things we also
speak not in words taught of human wisdom, but in those taught of spirit,
comparing spiritual things with spiritual. But a soulish human being does not
receive the things of the spirit of God, for it is foolishness to him, and he
is not able to know because it is discerned spiritually. But the spiritual one
discerns all things, yet he himself is discerned by no one.] [Of Origen] Let
us understand the things that have been given us by God; for if the spirit
that is from God teaching us does not come to be in us, we are not able to say
that we know the things that have been given us by God, for also no one knows
the things of God except the spirit of God. which things we also speak he says
not in words taught of human wisdom but in those taught of spirit and the
things that follow. All the



wisdoms of this world are words taught of human beings, which someone learns
according to each of the rational arts; but the things taught of the spirit it
does not teach; and you would not say that the teacher taught, having been
taught by the spirit concerning the things he says. For the spirit being in
him who seeks and searches the truth shines upon him, and thus always by the
renewal of the mind he finds things which he has not learned from human
beings. For this reason the Savior says Do not seek a teacher upon the earth;
for one is your teacher, the Father who is in the heavens, or one is your
guide, the Christ. A human being suggests a few things, say Paul teaching
Timothy, and Timothy having taken starting-points comes himself also to the
spring where Paul has come and draws and Timothy becomes equal to Paul. And
how someone is taught from the spirit must be understood from the apostolic
words; for comparing spiritual things with spiritual, by examining this
wording together with that wording and gathering the like things, the mind of
the scripture is as it were uncovered. For thus I understand the things of God
and become taught of spirit; not only <by> having learned the things of the
spirit that moved Isaiah, but also by having had the same spirit which also
shut and sealed the words of Isaiah; for if the spirit does not open the words
of the prophets, the things that have been shut cannot be opened. And since he
said these things, he necessarily adds that there are some human beings who do
not receive the things of the spirit of God, not because of nature as the
heterodox think but because of not having prepared themselves. For a soulish
human being does not receive the things of the spirit, for he was soulish; but
when he inclined toward piety and received the spirit, he became spiritual.
And the soulish one is not able to know because it is discerned spiritually;
he does not know for this reason <what> he hears, that it is discerned
spiritually, and the power being tested convicts the fool, as if I speak
having been taught from the spirit, and I speak with power, and the hearer
testing the power seems and does not give assent, the power being tested
convicts the fool. And it is not so much that he rejected the thing said as
that the thing said rejected him; for into a soul that works evil wisdom will
not enter nor will it dwell in a body in debt to sin. But the spiritual one
discerns all things, yet he himself is discerned by no one. The one who no
longer walks according to flesh but according to spirit and always by care
increases it and kindles the gift of God still more, prepares for himself to
become spiritual; and having become so he is sufficient to discern all things,
the things of Greeks, the things of barbarians, the things of wise, the things
of unintelligent; but no one is able to discern him because of the greatness
of his understanding and <of> his reasonings. And if you wish to recognize the
spiritual one, from their fruits you will know them. And what are the fruits
of the spirit from which one must understand who the spiritual one is? Hear
the apostle saying but the fruit of the spirit is love, joy, peace,
long-suffering, kindness, goodness, faith, meekness, self-control; where these
things are, there is the spirit, that one is spiritual; but where something of
these is lacking, that one is not yet spiritual. 12 iii 1–3a [And I, brothers,
was not able to speak to you as to spiritual but as to fleshly, as to infants
in Christ. I gave you milk to drink, and not food, for you were not yet able,
but not even now are you still able.] [Of Origen] Of those who hear some are
perfect, the spiritual, concerning whom we were also speaking formerly; but
those who are not spiritual but believe upon the Christ, as the apostle says,
infants in Christ and fleshly in Christ; for I hear In Christ from the common.
There is therefore need of lessons more deficient for those who are not more
perfect and not yet exercised in the sacred writings, which the apostle named
to be milk; for milk he says I gave you to drink not food; for you were not
yet able, but not even now are you still able. And I think there is a range in
the spiritual foods of milk, of food, of true eating, of solid food, of flesh
of the word, and of a spiritual



vegetable. And I dare to say these things being persuaded by the scriptures;
for he did not say every spiritual food is true eating, saying my flesh is
true food. What things then are not solid food, the more ethical things and as
many as in the ethical things are weaker, said according to concession not
according to command, being managed because of the weakness of the hearers.
But as many as in ethical things are a more perfect teaching in hearers who
love learning and love labor, these I would say in ethical things a solid
food, that concerning perfect chastity, that concerning virginity or
temperance, that concerning martyrdom; these are solid food. And solid food in
mystical things is that concerning Father and Son. And so also the law at one
time hands down solid food, at another more deficient. For example we come
upon the word concerning the serpents that were destroying the sons of Israel
because of the murmuring; if we read more simply, we say Let us not even
murmur, just as some of them murmured, and they perished by the serpents. But
if the hearer is able to hear a mystical word, we say who the serpents are and
who the serpent that was hung upon the wood, and how one is saved looking
toward that serpent, and thus according to each spiritual scripture. And who
those watered with milk are Peter will make clear in the epistle saying as
newborn babes long for the rational guileless milk; and in that to the Hebrews
also you have become having need of milk and not of solid food; for everyone
who partakes of milk is inexperienced in the word of righteousness; for he is
an infant; but solid food is of perfect ones, of those who because of habit
have the senses exercised toward discernment of both good and evil. 13 iii 3–5
[For where there is among you zeal and strifes and divisions, are you not
fleshly and do you walk according to a human being? For whenever someone says
I am of Paul; and another I of Apollos; are you not human beings?] [Of Origen]
Of the sins among human beings the lesser are zeal and strifes. If someone
will be in fornication, such a one is no longer fleshly but worse than
fleshly. And what such a one would be, let us see. I think there is a
difference of fleshly and of earthy; As we bore, he says, the image of the
earthy, let us also bear the image of the heavenly. If then you sin the things
toward death, you are not fleshly but earthy; but if you sin, yet not toward
death, you are not altogether earthy nor fallen away from the grace of Christ,
but you are then fleshly. And of our walks one is according to a human being,
and another according to God. If we walk as the many of human beings, we walk
according to a human being; but if according to let your light shine before
human beings, we differ from the many and we do not walk according to a human
being. Clear then is the life to the one able to see of what sort is that
according to a human being and of what sort that according to God. For when
someone says I am of Paul, and I of Apollos, and I of Cephas. And let us
attend to these. We shall see that we sometimes are not even human beings but
worse than human beings. For the one who said then I am of Apollos, was
speaking concerning a man of account and holy and blessed; the one who says I
am of Paul, speaks concerning a man a sacred and blessed apostle. But
sometimes inclinings come to be not similarly to the inclining of Paul or
Apollos, but someone is inclined sometimes to a sinner and a heterodox. If
then these are human beings and walk according to flesh, who say I of Apollos
and I of Paul; whenever you say yourself to be of someone more deficient, it
is clear that you are no longer even a human being but even worse than a human
being; and perhaps it will be said toward you Brood of vipers, offspring of
snakes. 14 iii 6–8 [I planted, Apollos watered, but God gave the increase; so
that neither is the one who plants anything nor the one who waters, but God
who gives the increase. ... and each will receive his own wage according to
his own labor.] [Of Origen] If Paul planted, he planted together with God who
plants first; if Apollos watered, he watered together with Christ who watered
first; for God plants; wherefore it is written every plant which my heavenly
Father did not plant will be rooted out; and again my Father is the farmer.
Let us therefore make fruits in order that we may not be cut out according to



the fruitless fig tree. For every tree not making good fruit is cut out and
thrown into fire. Let us hold back lest it be said concerning us I waited in
order that it might make a grape, but it made thorns. For if he says these
things concerning us, he will say also the things that follow Now then I will
announce what I will do to my vineyard. I will take away its hedge and it will
be for plunder, and I will pull down its wall and it will be for a trampling.
15 iii 9–15 [for we are God's fellow-workers; you are God's field, God's
building. According to the grace of God that was given me as a wise
master-builder I have laid a foundation, and another builds upon it. But let
each look how he builds upon it; for no one is able to lay another foundation
besides the one that lies, which is Jesus Christ; and if someone builds upon
this foundation gold, silver, precious stones, woods, hay, stubble, each one's
work will become manifest, for the day will make it clear; because it is
revealed in fire, and the fire will test of what sort each one's work is. If
anyone's work remains which he built upon it, he will receive a wage; if
anyone's work is burned up, he will suffer loss, but he himself will be saved,
yet so as through fire.] [Of Origen] Since we are not only a field but also
God's building, I seek the master-builder of the building and those who work
with him. For understanding this the apostle says according to the grace that
was given me as a wise master-builder I have laid a foundation; for Paul
established the church in Corinth <being> in no way before the apostle. And
since there were schisms among them he says that grace having been given me by
God as a wise master-builder I laid the foundation, and another builds upon
it; for if another coming after builds upon the foundation of the church, that
one is not master-builder of the church but builds upon the church; if then
you wish to see that Paul is a wise master-builder, hear him that From
Jerusalem and as far as Illyricum I have fulfilled the gospel of God, being
ambitious to proclaim not where Christ was named, in order that I may not
build upon another's foundation. And not only as a wise master-builder did he
lay down a foundation, but he also wrote as it were master-building books, how
the master-builder must build a house, of what sort the bishop must be and
presbyters and deacons and the remaining fullness of the church; for all these
things were as it were master-building laws. Another then he says builds upon
it; but let each look how he builds upon it. For this he says also to me, in
order that I may not build upon it without care, knowing that of what sort the
work is which I build upon it the fire itself will test in that day. And I
take thought lest perhaps building upon it I may build woods hay stubble. For
if I bring souls to the church without judgment, base ones, I brought to the
good foundation Christ Jesus woods, others hay, and others stubble; just as
again if some shine out from the building, and shine out so much that some are
found analogous to the things written concerning Jerusalem and the temple
chosen stones and stone of crystal and stones of sapphire and as many stones
as he named there, it is clear that I built precious stones upon the
foundation. And since <it is necessary> that the temple of God and the
building also have gold just as the temple which Solomon built having a symbol
of a temple having much gold and silver, if some other one comes being built
upon it more precious than silver he would be found gold. But I am cautious
lest ever through me wood may enter and hay and stubble, and wretched I (even
if I am otherwise judged worthy of salvation; since it is written but he
himself will be saved, yet so as through fire) may be saved thus as having
become a cause, by not having contained the grace nor having built well, of
the building having been filled with woods, hay, stubble, which the word of
God does not wish; for of what sort each one's work is the fire will test. And
I wish also otherwise to copy out For no one is able to lay another foundation
besides the one that lies, which is Jesus Christ and the things that follow.
From the time my Lord Jesus Christ sojourned, this foundation has been
proclaimed into the world; and this foundation in



Judea the apostles laid, but among the nations the apostle of the nations and
his fellow-worker Barnabas; for they gave right hands of fellowship to me and
Barnabas, in order that we unto the nations and they unto the circumcision.
Jesus Christ the word of God having therefore been proclaimed, and this
foundation having been laid down, no one is able to lay another foundation
besides Jesus Christ; and those who build upon it, if they are orthodox
through the everlasting doctrines and the divine thoughts and the good words
and the sacred deeds, build upon the foundation gold silver precious stone.
For gold has often been observed, being taken instead of mind and thoughts; if
I speak well, I build silver; if I interpret as one must and I exhort the
hearers well unto a manner of life, I build precious stones. As many however
of those who teach Jesus Christ have missed the truth, they built upon the
foundation woods hay stubble; and I would say that of those from the heresies
all the more blasphemous woods, and those who sinned less in the teaching hay,
and those some thin and that I may so name lesser stubble. Come let us also
see according to the wording. This that will be said touches each one of us;
each of us having received the word of the gospel received Jesus Christ as
foundation, in order that in all things which he does and says and thinks he
may build upon this foundation. If then we understand well and think, we build
gold; if speaking purely we say every word that is said in a holy way, we
build as silver; if every deed which we do is good, we build as precious
stone, and God judges the value of the stones and the quality. If however I
sin after the foundation, the great things of my sins will be woods, the more
deficient hay, the still more deficient stubble. And I seek lest perhaps † the
roots † and the thoughts, whenever they are mistaken, are woods; for the
thoughts are a spring of the evils; and the words hay, and the deed stubble;
for the worth of the deed is more deficient beside the reasoning; and the one
who plants and the one who waters are one. 16 iii 16–20 [do you not know that
you are a temple of God and the spirit of God dwells in you? If someone ruins
the temple of God, God will ruin this one; for the temple of God is holy;
which you are. Let no one deceive himself; if someone seems to be wise among
you in this age, let him become a fool, in order that he may become wise. For
the wisdom of this world is foolishness with God; for it is written He who
catches the wise in their craftiness; and again The Lord knows the reasonings
of the wise that they are vain.] [Of Origen] If you wish to learn a true
temple of God, seek a living stone and ones hewn clean, thrown by the word,
and standing four-square, having nothing unstable nor rolling; for even if
holy stones roll upon the earth, still the hewers do not allow them to roll
until the end. You then as living stones are built a spiritual house into a
holy priesthood, to offer spiritual sacrifices well acceptable to God through
Jesus Christ. And also with the apostle it is said that we all are one temple,
each of us being some stone from the temple. And he says thus in that to the
Ephesians So then you are no longer strangers and sojourners but
fellow-citizens of the saints and household of God; having been built upon the
foundation of the apostles and of the prophets, Jesus Christ himself being
chief corner-stone; in whom every building fitted together grows into a holy
temple in the Lord; in whom you also are built together into a dwelling-place
of God in spirit. One must therefore no stone be unfitted to the building; for
if two he says of you agree upon the earth concerning every matter which they
may ask, it will come to be for them from my Father who is in the heavens. How
much more then, if all the stones agree into one harmony and the heart and the
soul of all become one, will their prayer be able and have strength? It is
therefore good that we all say the same thing in order that we may be fitted
in the same mind and the same judgment; for thus you are a building



fitted together into a holy temple in the Lord. And each of us is able also by
himself to be a temple by another word; for if a temple is the one who has
glory of God, everyone who has in himself glory of God is according to this a
temple of God. And he has more or less glory who makes the name of the Father
in the heavens to be glorified through his deeds; wherefore it is written
those who glorify me I will glorify. Then therefore we shall especially be a
temple of God, if we prepare ourselves capable of containing the spirit of
God. Yet someone having a spirit of some sin is not able to be a temple of
God, inasmuch as the spirit of God dwells only in whom it judges to dwell. If
someone ruins the temple of God, God will ruin this one. As far as it is upon
himself each one, even if he is a stone, ruins the temple of God having
sinned, and also the one who provides some stumbling-block to the church ruins
the temple of God. And more properly the one who fornicates ruins the temple
of God, inasmuch as your bodies are a temple of the holy spirit in you which
you have from God. The clean one then is called uncorrupted, and the one no
longer a virgin corrupted, as if this work alone corrupts. If then this is a
work of corruption, let us especially guard against this. Since also the Lord
