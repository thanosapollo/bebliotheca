* On the Petition of the Mother of the Sons of Zebedee

On the remainder against the heretics, and on judgment and almsgiving, and on
the petition of the mother of the sons of Zebedee.

Eighth discourse.

=48.767=

αʹ. Yesterday we returned from war, from a war and a heretical battle, having
our weapons bloodied, the sword of the word dyed red, not having struck down
bodies, but having destroyed reasonings and Every high thing that exalts
itself against the knowledge of God. For such is the form of this battle;
therefore such also is the nature of the weapons; both of which the blessed
Paul teaching said: For the weapons of our warfare are not fleshly, but mighty
through =48.768= God to the pulling down of strongholds, destroying reasonings
and every high thing that exalts itself against the knowledge of God. It would
have been necessary, then, to tell those who were absent the falls that
happened yesterday, and to narrate the array, the clash, the victory, the
trophies; but lest we make you more careless, having passed these by, so that
you who were absent, stung by the loss, may become more earnest, today we will
take hold again of what follows; but if anyone is earnest and awake, he will
learn from those who were present what was said by us yesterday. For the
hearers showed us such eagerness that, having taken everything, they withdrew
home, and left nothing =48.769= of what was said to slip away. Those things,
then, you will learn from them; but what it is necessary to say today, these
we will tell you, setting forth in the midst the objection which the children
of the heretics brought against us. What then is this? Since formerly we were
discoursing about the authority of the Only-begotten, showing that the
authority is equal to that of the Father who begot him, and we spent much
speech on these things, they, struck by what was said, brought against us a
certain evangelical saying, spoken in one way, but suspected by them to have
been spoken in another, saying: And yet it is written: But to sit on my right
and on my left is not mine to give, but for whom it has been prepared by my
Father. But I, what I always entreat of your love, this I also today advise
and counsel, not simply to go over the letters, but to search out the
thoughts; for if anyone simply sits down with the words, and seeks nothing
more than what is written, he will err in many things. For the letters also
say that God has wings, the prophet saying that In the shelter of your wings
you will shelter me, but we will not for this reason say that that
intellectual and immortal essence is winged.

If this cannot be said of men, much more of that undefiled and invisible and
incomprehensible nature. What then is to be understood by us from the wings?
The help, the safety, the shelter, the alliance, the invincible character of
the help. Again the Scriptures say that he sleeps, speaking thus: Arise, why
do you sleep, O Lord? not that we should suspect God of sleeping; for this is
of extreme folly; but by the name of sleep showing us his long-suffering and
forbearance. And another prophet says: Will you be as a man that sleeps? Do
you see that we need much understanding as we search out the treasure of the
divine Scriptures? so that if we hear what is said simply and at random and as
it happens, not only will those absurdities follow, but also much conflict in


what was said will appear. For one says that he sleeps, another that he does
not sleep; but both are true, if you take the fitting sense. For the one who
says that he sleeps shows the intensity of his long-suffering, and the one who
says that he does not sleep shows us the undefiled character of the nature.
Since therefore we need much understanding, let us not take this saying simply
either, the "It is not mine to give, but for whom it has been prepared by my
Father." For this is not an abolition of authority, nor a mutilation of
sovereignty, but a proof of much care and wisdom and providence for our race.
For that he is lord both to punish and to honor, hear what he himself who says
these things says: When the Son of man comes, he says, in the glory of his
Father, he will set the sheep on the right, and the goats on the left, and he
will say to those on his right: Come, you blessed of my Father, inherit the
kingdom prepared for you from the foundation of the world. For I was hungry,
and you gave me to eat; I was thirsty, and you gave me to drink. And to those
on the left he will say: Depart from me, you cursed, into the fire prepared
for the devil and his angels. For I was hungry, and you did not =48.770= give
me to eat; I was thirsty, and you did not give me to drink; I was a stranger,
and you did not take me in. Did you see a judgment brought to completion, and
how he both honors and punishes, and crowns and chastises, and leads some into
the kingdom, and sends others away into Gehenna?

βʹ. And consider here too his great care for us. For when he was speaking to
those who are crowned, Come, he says, you blessed of my Father, inherit the
kingdom prepared for you before the foundation of the world; but to those who
are punished he did not say: Depart from me, you cursed, into the fire
prepared for you, but "That prepared for the devil." For the kingdom I
prepared for men, but Gehenna I did not prepare for men, he says, but I made
it ready
for the devil and for his angels. But if you have shown such a life as to
become worthy of punishment and chastisement, you would be just in reckoning
this to yourselves. And consider the inclination toward loving-kindness: for
not yet had the contestants come into being, and the crowns were prepared
beforehand, and the prizes were made ready. For inherit, he says, the kingdom
prepared for you before the foundation of the world. And in the parable of the
ten virgins one may see the like; for when the bridegroom was about to arrive,
the foolish say to the wise: Give us of your oil. And they to them: Lest it
not suffice for us and you; the Scripture not discoursing here of oil and
fire, but of virginity and loving-kindness, and it sets virginity in the rank
of fire, and almsgiving in the rank of oil, showing that virginity has great
need of loving-kindness, and without this it is not possible to be saved. And
who are those who sell this oil? Who else but the poor? for they do not so
much receive as they give. Do not then think almsgiving an expense, but an
income, nor a spending, but a business; for you receive more than you give.
You give bread, and you receive eternal life; you give a garment, and you
receive a wrapping of incorruption; you give a sharing of a roof, and you
receive the kingdom of heaven; you furnish things that perish, and you receive
things that remain continually.

And how can I show mercy, being poor, he says? Especially when you are poor,
then you can show mercy. For the rich man, drunk with the abundance of money,
and burning with a most grievous fever, and having an insatiable desire, wants
to make what he has still more; but the poor man, delivered from this disease,
and clean of the infirmity,


will more easily give from what he has. For it is not the measures of
substance, but the measures of purpose, that are naturally able to work
almsgiving; since that widow then put in two obols, and overshot those who
were flourishing in wealth, and the other widow with a handful of meal and a
little oil entertained that heaven-high soul, and for neither of these was
poverty a hindrance. Do not then make superfluous and useless excuses; for he
does not demand an abundance of contribution, but a wealth of choice; which is
shown not from the measure of what is given, but from the eagerness of those
who furnish it. =48.771= Are you poor, and poorer than all men? yet you are
not poorer than that widow, who overshot those who were rich with much to
spare. But you even lack necessary food? yet you are not more destitute than
the Sidonian woman, who, having come to the very bottom of famine, death now
being expected, and a choir of children standing round, even so did not spare
what she had, but through intense poverty bought unspeakable wealth, making
her hand a threshing-floor and her jar a winepress, causing much to spring
from little. But let us go to the matter at hand, so that we may not make
continual digressions. Since therefore the bridegroom was about to come, the
virgins were saying these things to one another. And those sent them to the
sellers; but there was no longer any time left to buy oil; reasonably.

For the sellers are only in the present life; but after the departure from
here and the dissolving of the theater there is no longer a remedy to find for
what has been done, nor pardon and defense, but there is need thereafter to be
punished; which also happened then. For when the bridegroom came, some went in
having bright lamps, but the others, having missed the entrance, were striking
the doors of the bridechamber, and they heard that fearful word: Go away, I do
not know you. Did you see him again both honoring and punishing, and crowning
and chastising, and receiving and sending away, and being lord of either part
of the judgment? This also in the vineyard, this also in the five and two
talents, and the one, one may see; for some he accepted, and set them over
more, but the other he commanded to be bound and cast out into the outer
darkness.

γʹ. But what is their sharp argument, or rather one full of much folly? that
he has authority both to punish and to crown, and to chastise and to give
rewards, but the highest presidency, he says, and the loftiest honor, this he
said is not his to give. What then if you learn that nothing is excepted from
his verdict, will you ever put away this untimely contentiousness? Hear him
again then saying: The Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the
Son. If therefore he has all judgment, nothing is excepted from the verdict;
for he who has all judgment is lord to chastise all and to crown them. But the
"he gave," here, do not hear in a human way, beloved; for he did not give to
one who did not have, nor did he beget him incomplete, nor did it come to him
afterward, but the "he gave" is of this sort: he begot him such, perfect,
complete. And he uses this word, so that you may not think two begotten gods,
but that you may know both the root and the fruit; not that you may think this
came to him afterward. For being asked elsewhere, Are you then a king? he did
not say that I received the kingdom; he did not say that the kingdom came to
him afterward; but that For this I have been born. If he was born a complete
king, it is quite clear that he is also judge and one who gives judgment; for
it belongs especially to a king =48.772= to judge, and to give judgment, and
to honor, and to chastise. And


from another place too one may see this, that he himself is lord to give even
the honors above. For when we bring into the midst the one better than all
men, and show this one being crowned by him, what pretext will be left you
thereafter? Who then is better than all men?

And who else but that tentmaker, the teacher of the world, who ran over land
and sea as if winged, the vessel of election, the bridesman of Christ, the
planter of the Church, the wise master-builder, the herald, the runner, the
contestant, the soldier, the trainer, who left everywhere in the world
memorials of his own virtue, who before the resurrection was caught up to the
third heaven, who was carried into paradise, who shared unspeakable mysteries
with God, who heard and spoke such things as it is not possible for human
nature to speak, who both enjoyed more grace, and showed greater labor? And
that he toiled more than all, hear him saying that I labored more abundantly
than they all. And if he toiled more than all, he is also crowned more; For
each will receive his own reward according to his own labor. If therefore he
receives a greater crown than the apostles, and no one has become equal to the
apostles, and this man is greater even than those, it is quite clear that he
will enjoy the highest honor and presidency. Who then is the one who crowns
him? Hear him saying: I have fought the good fight, I have finished the
course, I have kept the faith; henceforth there is laid up for me the crown of
righteousness, which the Lord, the righteous judge, will give me in that day.
The Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son. And this is
clear not only from here, but also from what follows: And not to me only, but
also to all who have loved his appearing. And whose is the appearing? hear him
saying, The grace of God that brings salvation to all men has appeared,
teaching us that, denying ungodliness and worldly desires, we should live
soberly and justly and piously in the present age, looking for the blessed
hope, and the appearing of the glory of our great God and Savior Jesus Christ.

δʹ. But the battle against the heretics has an end for us, and we have set up
the trophy, and we have taken a splendid victory, having shown from all that
was said that he is lord both to honor and to punish, having all judgment, and
crowning and proclaiming the one better than all, and in those parables
himself working both. It is necessary hereafter also to cast out the tumult of
the brethren, and to teach for what reason he has spoken thus, that "It is not
mine to give this"; for I think many are at a loss about what was said. In
order then that we may both solve the difficulty, and calm the tumult of the
soul, stretch the understanding for me, furnish me the purpose; for I need
more labor now. For it is not the same to war and to teach, to strike the
enemy, and to correct one's own, but I need more =48.773= earnestness here, so
as not to overlook the part that is lame, nor to pass by anyone who is in
tumult. For I say, but do not be in tumult at what is said, nor be troubled;
for I say not only that it is not of the Son, but not even of the Father; and
I shout these things with a great voice, and more clearly than a trumpet, that
it is not his to give, neither his, nor the Father's. For if it were his, it
was also the Father's; if it were the Father's, it was also his. For this
reason he himself also did not say simply, it is not mine to give, but what?

It is not mine to give, but for whom it has been prepared. He shows that it is
neither his, nor the Father's, but of certain others. What then is the saying?
For I think the


tumult has increased for you, and the difficulty has become more, and you are
in agony; but do not fear; for I will not stand off, until I bring on the
solution. But bear with me a little as I take the word further back; for it is
not otherwise possible to deposit all things with clarity in your purpose.
What in the world then is the saying? The mother of the sons of Zebedee, James
and John, came to Jesus as he was withdrawing toward Jerusalem with the
children, saying, Say that my two sons may sit one on your right, and one on
your left. And another evangelist says that the children were asking these
things of Christ. But there is no disagreement, nor should one pass over even
these small things; but putting the mother forward, when she had spoken and
opened a door, they too brought the petition, not knowing what they were
saying, yet saying it nonetheless. For even if they were apostles, they were
still more imperfectly disposed, like nestlings wandering as in a nest, their
wing not yet being fixed.

And this especially it is necessary for you to know, that before the cross
they were in much ignorance; therefore also rebuking them he said: Are you
also still without understanding? do you not yet perceive nor understand, that
I did not speak to you about the loaves to beware of the leaven of the
Pharisees? And again, I have many things to say to you, but you cannot bear
them now. And not only did they not know the higher things, but even what they
heard they often threw away from fear and cowardice; which rebuking them he
said: None of you asks me, Where are you going? but because I have said these
things to you, sorrow has filled your heart. And again, He will remind you of
all things, and will teach you, speaking of the Paraclete. And he would not
have said, he will remind, if they had not thrown out many of the things that
were said. And these things have not been said by me simply, but because Peter
appears, now confessing a complete confession, and now having forgotten all.

For he who said, You are the Christ, the Son of the living God, and was
blessed for these things, shortly after sinned such a sin as to be addressed
as Satan; for he says, Get behind me, Satan; you are a stumbling-block to me,
because you do not mind the things of God, but the things of men. What could
be more imperfect than one who does not mind His things, but the things of
men? For when he was discoursing with him about the cross and resurrection,
not knowing the depth of what was said, nor the unspeakable character of the
doctrines, nor being able to grasp the salvation that was to come to the
world, =48.774= taking him aside he said: Be it far from you, Lord; this shall
not be to you. Do you see how they knew nothing clear even about the
resurrection? And the Evangelist, marking this very thing, said: For they did
not yet know that he must rise from the dead. And they, not knowing these
things, much more were ignorant of the rest; such as the things about the
kingdom above, and our firstfruits, and the ascension into heaven, and they
were still some lowly ones, not yet able to fly high.

εʹ. Having then such a thought, and expecting that the kingdom would meet him
straightway in Jerusalem (for they knew nothing more than this; which another
Evangelist also marks, saying that they thought his kingdom was already
coming, suspecting it to be some human one, and expecting him to go on to
this, but not to a cross and death; for even hearing it a thousand times, they
were not able to know it clearly); since therefore they did not yet possess a
clear exactness of the doctrines, but thought he was going on to this sensible
kingdom, and would reign in Jerusalem, taking him aside on the road, thinking
they had a suitable time


they ask this petition. For having torn themselves away from the choir of the
disciples, and having turned the whole upon themselves, they ask for
presidency and to become first of the others, thinking the affairs had already
received an end, and that the whole had been accomplished, and that it was
henceforth a time of crowns and rewards; which itself also was of extreme
ignorance. And that these things are not conjecture, nor plausibilities of
words, I will bring you the proof from Jesus himself, who knows the
unspeakable things. For when they asked these things, hear what he says to
them: You do not know what you ask. What is clearer than this proof? Do you
see that they did not know what they were asking, discoursing with him about
crowns and rewards and presidency and honor, when the contests had not yet
even received a beginning? He therefore hints at two things by saying: You do
not know what you ask, one that they are discoursing about a kingdom of which
Christ had no account; for he had not promised this lower and sensible one;
and another that seeking already presidency and the highest honors, and
wishing to appear more splendid and more conspicuous than the others, they do
not seek these things even in season, but even very untimely. For that time
was not of crowns, nor of prizes, but of contests and wrestlings and toils and
sweats and wrestling-grounds and wars. What he says, then, is of this sort:
You do not know what you ask, discoursing with me about these things, not yet
having toiled, nor having stripped for the contests, the world still remaining
uncorrected, impiety prevailing, all men =48.775= having perished; you have
not yet sprung from the starting-line; you have not yet stripped for the
wrestlings. Can you drink the cup which I am about to drink, and be baptized
with the baptism with which I am baptized? calling here cup and baptism his
own cross and death; cup, because he went to it with pleasure, and baptism,
because through it he cleansed the world; and not for this only, but also
because of the ease of the resurrection.

For just as the one baptized in water rises with much ease, being hindered in
nothing by the nature of the waters; so also he, having gone down into death,
came up with still more ease; therefore he calls it baptism. And what he says
is of this sort: can you be slaughtered and die? for the time now is of these
things, of deaths and dangers and toils. Those say, We can, not even knowing
what in the world was being said, but promising in hope of receiving. He says
to them: You shall drink the cup, and you shall be baptized with the baptism
with which I am baptized; speaking thus of the death; for James was cut off
with a sword, and John died many times. But to sit on my right, and on my
left, is not mine to give, but for whom it has been prepared. And what he says
is of this sort: You shall die, and you shall be slaughtered, and you shall be
honored with martyrdom; yet to become first is not mine to give, but for the
contestants to take, through more earnestness, through greater eagerness. And
that what I say may become clearer, let us suppose there is some president of
the games; then a mother having two children as athletes comes with her
children to the president of the games, and says: say that these two sons of
mine may receive the crown; what then will he himself answer? This very same
thing, that it is not mine to give; I am president of the games, not awarding
by favor, nor by petition and entreaty of those who come, but by the end of
the affairs. For this especially belongs to a president of the games, not to
give the prizes simply and as it happens, but honoring courage. This then
Christ also does; he did not speak thus taking away the things of the essence,
but making this clear, that it is not his alone to give, but also for the
contestants to take. For if it were his alone, all men would have been saved,
and would have come to knowledge of the truth;


if it were his alone, there would not have been different honors; for he
himself made all, and cares for all alike. And that there are different
honors, hear Paul making this clear and saying: There is one glory of the sun,
and another glory of the moon, and another glory of the stars; for star
differs from star in glory. And again, If anyone builds upon this foundation
gold, silver, precious stones. For showing the variety of virtue, Paul thus
arranged the word.

And he said these things, showing that it is not for those who sleep and snore
to enter into the kingdom of heaven, but through many tribulations one must
take those prizes. For since because they enjoyed much friendship and boldness
they thought that they would also be pre =48.776= ferred to the others, lest
suspecting these things they become more careless, leading them away from this
suspicion, he says: It is not mine to give, but yours, if you should wish, to
take; so that you may show more eagerness, so that greater toil, so that much
earnestness; for I set the crowns on the works, and the honors on the toils,
and the prizes on the sweat; this is with me the best commendation, the proof
from the works.

ʹ. Do you see how I was not saying in vain that it is neither his, nor the
Father's, but of those who contend and toil and suffer hardship? For this
reason also he said to Jerusalem: How often I wished to gather your children,
as a hen her chicks, and you did not wish; behold, your house is left to you
desolate. Do you see that of those who are careless and have given up and lie
on their backs it is not possible for anyone ever to be saved? From this we
also learn another unspeakable thing, that not even martyrdom is enough to
give the highest honor and the loftiest presidency. See at any rate he
foretold to these that they will indeed be martyrs, but will not by all means
obtain the first places; for there are some who can show even greater things.

And making this clear he said: You shall drink my cup, and you shall be
baptized with the baptism with which I am baptized; but to sit on the right,
and on the left, is not mine to give; not that he sits, but to enjoy a greater
honor, to obtain the first places, to become above all, this is it, he says;
to sit then on the right and on the left, he says condescending to them in
their suspicion. For they were seeking the first places, and to appear greater
than all the others. This very thing then, he says, that you appear greater
than the others, and above all, is not to be taken from this alone; for you
shall die; but to enjoy the highest honor is not mine to give, but to those
for whom it has been prepared. And for whom has it been prepared? tell me. Let
us see who these are, the blessed, and thrice-blessed, and enjoying those
splendid crowns. Who then are these, and what having done will they appear so
splendid? Hear him saying; for when the ten were angry about the two, because
having torn away from the choir they wished to turn the highest honor upon
themselves, see how he corrects both the passion of those and of these. For
having called them he says: The rulers of the nations lord it over them, and
their great ones exercise authority over them; but among you it shall not be
so, but he who wishes to be first among you, let him become last of all.

Do you see that this is what they wished, to become first and greater and
higher, and, as one might say, rulers of them? For this reason then standing
against this, and bringing their unspeakable things into the midst, he said:
He who wishes to be first among you, let him be servant of all. If you love
presidency, he says, and the highest honor, pursue the last things, to be
cheaper than all, to be humbler than all, to be smaller than all, to rank
yourselves after the others. For this virtue gives that honor. And the example


is near, and with much abundance. For the Son of man did not come to be ser
=48.777= ved, but to serve, and to give his soul a ransom for many. For that
this makes men splendid and notable, you see the thing happening in my case,
he says, who have no need of honor or glory; but nevertheless I too accomplish
the countless goods through this. For before he took the flesh, and humbled
himself, all had perished and been corrupted; but when he also humbled
himself, he raised all to a height, he made the curse vanish, he quenched
death, he opened paradise, he put sin to death, he spread open the vaults of
heaven, he brought our firstfruits up into heaven, he filled the world with
piety, he drove out error, he brought back the truth, he set our firstfruits
upon a royal throne, he worked the countless goods, which neither I, nor all
men, would be able to set forth in speech. And before he humbled himself,
angels only knew him; but when he humbled himself, the whole nature of men
recognized him. See how humility did not work a lessening, but countless
gains, countless achievements, and made his glory shine forth greater. And if
in the case of God who is without need and need48.778 ing nothing, to be
humbled is so great a good, and brought him more household servants, and
extended the kingdom, why do you yourself fear lest you be lessened from being
humbled? Then you will be higher, then great, then splendid, then conspicuous,
when you cheapen yourself, when you do not love the first places, when you
accept to be lessened and slaughtered and to be in danger; when you pursue the
service of the many and the attendance and the care, and are prepared both to
do and to suffer all things for this.

Thinking these things then, beloved, let us pursue humility with much
eagerness, and when we are insulted and spat upon, and endure all the last
things, and are dishonored and despised, let us bear all things with pleasure.
For nothing is so accustomed to make height and glory and honor, and to show a
man great, as the virtue of humility; which may it come to pass that we,
accomplishing it with exactness, obtain the promised goods, by the grace and
loving-kindness of our Lord Jesus Christ, with whom be glory and honor and
worship to the Father and to the Holy Spirit, now and always, and unto the
ages of ages. Amen.
