* Fragments from the Book against Diodore of Tarsus
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of Cyril in a homily titled on Immediately after the affliction of those days
the sun will be darkened and the moon will not give its light and the stars
will fall from heaven; but concerning that day and hour no one knows. But when
he says this too, he does not altogether mean himself; for he did not add
exactly Nor the Son of God, but in a loosed way he says Nor the son; that you
may think something of this sort: the disciples were eager to learn all things
according to exactness, and they attempted, that I may so speak, even to busy
themselves with the very depths of the Lord. But the matter was far above
them. Therefore, lest, having refused the explanation as they wished to learn,
he seem somehow to grieve those who are genuine, he says in a shadowed way
"But concerning that day or hour, no one knows, nor the angels, nor the son,
except the Father"; that you may think thus: even if man has been called to
sonship and has been shown a new son, yet let him not seek the things above
nature, nor think it right to learn what the Father has not revealed even to
the angels; and if an angel does not know, neither will a man know even if he
has been called to sonship.
