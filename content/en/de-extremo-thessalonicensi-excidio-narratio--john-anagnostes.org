we had cast away the memory of the evils coming upon us from the capture and
we were rejoicing in the things we had obtained unexpectedly and we were
hoping that the city would take back its former prosperity, then from us, the
proverb says, "the strap was cut," and the things of the hopes were turned
about toward the contrary.]
20 [And Murad having come to sojourn at Thessalonica (a second year, I think,
he was then riding through after the capture, or a third) in order to see this
and to observe more exactly the things concerning it and to have enjoyed the
goods from it and to show himself generous in return with great gifts, and to
imitate by the generosity of mind his ancestor, that man also having mastered
Thessalonica and having repaid it with many graces: being then of such a mind
and having come with such, he, having been led by counsels that were not good,
both changes his mind and transfers the former mind, and the things which, at
the time at which we were captured, he had not done, at that time of the
season he was urged to accomplish. And so when he had seen the city and the
things of it one by one and had enjoyed every ease in it, when he had revelled
to satiety in the goods belonging to it, and had been disposed not least
lovingly toward it and had given all of us to be confident concerning the
better things for us. First he brought forth a vote such as no one, not even
having seen it in dreams, would ever have expected. And this was that all
monasteries and temples be taken, and all their revenues and the possessions
be taken away, and that our affairs come down altogether into a narrow place,
which also came out to an end not long after.

and envious mind, and they had been numbered with much exactness and

indeed the monasteries, as many as were greater and brighter toward beauty, he
granted to his own and those being in offices, and likewise also of the
houses, as many as were fairer than the others both toward beauty and size,
having left to us only the four temples, which are also called catholic, the
shepherd having brought in much haste on behalf of these. And all the
remaining houses and almost all the temples he determined to be furnished both
to those from other places who perhaps wished to choose the city instead of
that which they inhabit, and to the Turks who had migrated from the Yenitzai.
And this place is a whole plain, being distant a day's road from the western
part of the city, having had many Turkish inhabitants either by the fitness of
this or by the haste of the one who originally chose it toward dwelling, which
place indeed Murad, I know not how, having come there for viewing and hunting
of wild animals, at the time when he was sojourning in the city, makes almost
empty of those dwelling in it, having ordered these to migrate with speed and
to move house into Thessalonica. Which command indeed those appointed toward
it appeared to have fulfilled with haste, having delayed nothing after his
going abroad, but having come there and having brought all the Turks there
into Thessalonica rather by force than by persuasion and having furnished
other houses instead of those they had, according to the command. Both these
things then, I think, Murad considered and ordered that this come about thus,
in order that the city might have safety and a more exact guard from this,
being coastal and because of this needing many inhabitants, and in order that,
having the marketplace full of all wares being brought into it, it might draw
all to itself, distributing all businesses and exchanges to those in need and
easily enriching itself.]
21 [These things then having thus advanced and the Turks, numbered into a
thousand, having occupied the city and all having taken houses and temples
instead of houses and having obtained the dwelling mixed through the whole
city, the city put on the disorder as a certain mourning tunic, having been
stripped of the comeliness which it would have obtained if the evil counsel
had not found room, and having thrown away most unhappily the adornment which
it would have had not long after, and groaning, as it were with wailing, that
an earthquake did not bury it or fire consume it or water given up or burst
from cloud cover it and flood it; for this is more bearable, since at least it
is better than to be seen such, that it not stand upon the earth in any way at
all. Some then of the holy houses were changed into common lodgings, as we had
said, and to speak the prophetic word, "the altars of God had been thrown down
with axe and stonecutter's tool," and others preserve only remnants of their
first beauty and position, and some, and rather the greater number, had fallen
completely, so that it is not even known where they once were; for their
materials were plundered and added to other newer buildings and especially to
the common bath now seen in the midst of the city. And one may almost hear
from the older men that here was this temple,
and there that one, and as many beauties and graces as belonged to each. And
this the temples throughout the whole city; but the monasteries, which the
account showed earlier, stand as an occasion of mourning for those who look,
not only having been set empty of monks badly driven out and having migrated
toward other cities, but also having been stripped besides of the brilliance
through all things; for their marbles were taken from the floors, and all the
other material, which had been put together from the beginning toward their
completion and beauty, was let loose into plunder for those who wished, and
now they are seen by all as a certain unadorned spectacle and they move the
souls of all toward tears. Many then would have chosen the city instead of the
land that bore them, and it would already have been full of the pious, full of
monks, and overflowing with gladness and delight fitting the Christ-named
fullness, if such an accursed sentence had not been brought forth against it
and the things concerning it had not thus advanced. But now not only have
those who had a longing from long ago to occupy it had a repentance of the
eagerness, but even to us there is repentance of the coming into the city, we
having clearly missed of the hope; for all things have become up and down, as
the saying is, and the city like a certain ship enclosed in a sea of evils is
carried about, the things falling together each day, so to speak, troubling it
like winds and filling it with much nausea and dizziness and not allowing it
to obtain a pure fair weather, nor, having spread the sail of peace and calm,
to have reached a harbor of goods and to stand upon anchors of safety and
remain unshaken. This then would be of God and clearly of his holding of the
rudder, both to calm the towering waves of the things coming on, and to steer
toward a calm and to grant salvation and to give to back water for the rest
and "to sail a second voyage, as they say," all things having been changed
toward the better and what will benefit the city, the Trophy-bearer and Martyr
of our own having mediated and having taken hold again, so to speak, of the
tillers of the fatherland and having used his own power toward so great a sea
of calamities. And may it be not at a long time, God having looked with a
propitious eye and having shown himself humane in his accustomed way, upon the
things in which we provoke him.]
22 These things have followed up to this point, even if some of these we,
willingly, because of the time and our weakness about speaking, have judged it
necessary to leave aside. And since we were not able to thrust aside your
request and to carry off a charge of disobedience, as it was possible for us,
we have given ourselves to the present narrative; for this we were promising
even at the beginnings, to display nothing beyond our power. Whence, if we
should appear to have worked something worthy both of your desire and of the
subject, this would be yours and of your prayers on our behalf. But if again
we should seem unfortunate in all things, which it is all necessity to suffer,
whatever return would again be given back to us, it would be just that you
also have shared in this, having called us, being ignorant, toward so great a
work through thinking us to be something, we being nothing and insisting on
this everywhere. But I earnestly ask that your prayers be given back to me as
a return, most honorable father; for having known many stumblings in myself, I
need the help from your prayers toward the washing-off of these.
