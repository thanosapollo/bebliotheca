1 To the Corinthians, wisdom of God. [For he stated at the beginning the aim
for which he wrote neither against Greeks nor against others.]
"One had to consider Apollophanes": From what is acknowledged among the Greeks
he sets out the ordering of God who is cause of all, saying that from this it
is fitting to recognize God the boundary-setter of all, from the fact that the
sun and the moon have the appointed limits of the day, and run round together
with the whole circuit of heaven, that is from east to west. Or if they should
not be carried round with the heaven that contains them, while heaven and the
sphere of the fixed, unmoving stars turn circularly from east to west, but
the sun and moon themselves run the course opposite to the whole from west to
east, even so the creator and ordainer of the whole, God, is
justly recognized and wondered at. We have interpreted these things according
to Apollophanes, who says that heaven did not stand still then together with
the sun, since in the heading before this we have already stated the exact
point. [Concerning the ordering of the stars standing by the counsel of God,
and not being moved again whenever he wills, as in the case of Joshua son of
Nun and Ezekiel.]
"Whenever the sun by him": The things of Joshua son of Nun are set out in
these ( 15Δ_426 >, and he reminds holy Polycarp of this history, so that he
may set it against Apollophanes. He says then: He said to him: Learn how God,
when the sun and the moon were then at a diameter opposite each other, the sun
already setting, made the luminaries stand in place, a certain standing by a
certain ineffable power of God who is their cause. And the whole stood
motionless together with the two luminaries; for neither heaven nor the
remaining stars were going round, but they stood together with the two
luminaries, motionless. And the course of the whole and the motion did not
stand for a little only, but for a whole day, so that there came to be for
Joshua son of Nun a double day

of 24 hours, as Jesus the son of Sirach clearly says this in the hymn of the
Fathers. Then he adds: And if Apollophanes should say, Impossible, for heaven
and the remaining stars did not stand with the two luminaries, but those were
moving, while this one stood, answer him that, This especially ought to turn
you toward the true God and the knowledge of him, even him who moves the
heaven that contains the luminaries, yet makes the contained luminaries stand
motionless. For this is of a greater power: that, while heaven and the
remaining stars are moving, sun and moon stand motionless, and are not moved
with the same motion together with the whole, with which they were always
being carried round.
"Most supernatural together with the whole": He says, on the one hand, that
together with the pole remaining motionless (for this he named the whole) the
standing of the sun and the moon took place. Rather, he says, it is far more
wonderful if the pole was being carried round (for this he

indicated, saying "of the wholes, and of the stronger, and of the
containing"), while sun and moon (for these are "the contained") were not
carried round with the container.
"According to continuity it is almost tripled": He added "almost" ( 15Δ_428 >
because that day did not become triple in full (for it became of 32 hours);
that from the rising as far as the place in which it was, because the sign was
given, and ten from there toward the east, and twelve as far as the last
setting. And he used the same figure of speech here also, that either the pole
itself together with the sun in the interval of 24 hours made that
supernatural counter-revolution, or the sun alone.
"Or the whole of so much time": Compose the saying thus, it being by
hyperbaton: Or the whole turns back these opposite motions, retracing so great
an interval of time by these revolutions beyond reason. And these things are
also shown in the fourth of the "Kings," and especially in the edition
according to Symmachus, and in the second of the "Paralipomena."
"Or the sun in its own course": The annual motion of the sun makes five turns:
a northern ascent, that is from the equinoctial in spring to the summer
tropic, and a northern descent from it to the equinoctial in autumn, and a
southern descent from the equinoctial to the winter tropic, and from it again
a southern ascent to the equinoctial, and a fifth opposite to the whole.
Having spoken then of its fivefold motion, by the addition of the article, and
by adding "its," he hinted at the known and customary one. And let us
understand what is said in these by the Father thus: Let us suppose the sun on
that day at the hour of rising to be in the first degree of Aries complete,
having no minutes; it was not possible for it to return to the same degree
without completing the fivefold motion set forth, or as through 365 days and a
quarter. In the 10 hours then from the 12th in which it was, as far as the
place in which it made its rising, that is of the first degree of Aries,
having retraced in it, it fulfilled the things of the fivefold motion, that is
of the yearly period; and in the other 10 ( 15Δ_430 > hours after the
retrogression, coming to be at the place in which it was at one and a half
degrees of Aries, it likewise fulfilled the things of the same fivefold motion
by analysis, which he called analytically; for it was not possible for it
again to come to be in the one and a half degree, this of Aries, once it had
departed from it, except through the days of the year, as has been said.
"It is carried in reports": That is, in histories.
"They celebrate Mithras": The Persians call the sun Mithras. He indicated then
the extension of that day through the triple. And note besides this also
concerning the darkness that came from the sixth hour until the ninth hour
over all the earth at the time of the Lord's crucifixion, what the father
says.
"According to Heliopolis": Perhaps he means Heliopolis in Egypt; for they were
still studying there together.
"Paradoxically": For they say that an eclipse does not otherwise take place
unless the moon should fall together with the sun.
"And again it": Note from here the solution of the difficulty in the
evangelist Luke. And no one has narrated the strangeness of the manner and of
the miracle except this one alone. For when divine Luke said, "from the sixth
hour darkness came to be at the cross of the Lord, the sun being eclipsed," it
was doubted among all how he called this an eclipse, the moon being
fourteenth-day, and there not being a conjunction of sun and moon. For nearly
all the interpreters, being much later than these times, supposed that the sun
itself cast off its rays for the three hours until the ninth. Here however the
strange manner also has been stated

of the eclipse. He says then that from the east, being fourteenth-day (for it
was not a time of conjunction), it reached the sun at the sixth hour, and
falling into it ran under, thrusting it into the eastward part of the solar
disk, ( 15Δ_432 > from which the moon also came, and entered running under it
and retracing, until it shaded his whole disk. It was necessary then, if it
were an eclipse according to the usual, that both the eclipse and the clearing
should take place from the same part; as when the moon runs under the sun,
say, from the east it first obstructs the eastern part of the solar disk,
which it also began to run under; from there then, from the eastern part, the
disk of the sun also begins to be cleared, the moon running toward the west,
and first uncovering a part in passing and running past the sun, which it also
first covered, that is the eastern. But at the saving passion it did not
happen thus, but the contrary. For coming from the east the moon ran under the
sun, and they blocked his whole disk. Then it no longer passed on toward the
west, but retraced again toward the east, and first laid bare the western part
of the solar disk; for it ran back behind toward the opposite diameter of the
sun toward the east, so that the falling-in and the clearing no longer took
place from the same part. Phlegon the Greek chronographer also mentions this
eclipse in the thirteenth of the "Chronographies" in the 203rd Olympiad,
saying that it took place contrary to what is customary; yet he did not record
the manner. And our Africanus too in the fifth of the "Chronographies," and
Eusebius son of Pamphilus in the same, mention the same eclipse.

§ 1. This Demophilus was a monk, as the title shows; for they called the monks
attendants. And having played the tyrant against his own presbyter, and being
angry that he received someone who was in repentance of sins, he cast him out,
having struck the one repenting, and he thrust out the presbyter, and sat as a
self-ordained ( 15Δ_434 > presbyter. Note then that these evils also took
place even in those times. Fittingly then he says, "O noble Demophilus,"
reproaching him with disordered strength and not godly reverence, and by this
mocking the monk.
"And if ever him outside": That is, when the Scriptures say that he has come
to be in the indignation of God, and in the turning away of his face. And
first, he says, they show him to have come to be outside of gentleness, and
then thus to have suffered becoming out of the sight of God.
"From the preeminent": Note that gentleness is exceptional and preeminent for
the imitation of God, and that because of gentleness Moses is called attendant
of God.
"Of ambition": Ambition in the proper sense is to aim at and love honor. And
"Theocritus" is not someone so called, but the one judged by God for ruling
and suitable.
"And what of the ancestor of God": That he calls David ancestor of God,
because of Christ. For divine Paul says: "Set apart for the Gospel, which he
promised beforehand in holy Scriptures concerning his Son, who came to be from
the seed of David according to the flesh." Fittingly then is David ancestor of
God.
"And yet also a statute": For it has been written in the law, so as even to
raise the falling beast of the enemy.
"But upon the uphill": Upon the higher. And note the philanthropy of the
angels, and that they entreat God on behalf of nations, and that they rebuke
the apostate powers, that is the demons.

"But of the truly good": Concerning the goodness of our Lord Jesus Christ the
true God. He makes truly to be, that is he holds together toward continuance.
"And he always wills all things to come to be": Since he said all things, and
( 15Δ_436 > among all things there are also the soulless and the irrational,
by adding according to the fitness of each, he treated in advance the lurking
objection.
"And he contends": How the Lord loves all, and exhorts us not to fall away
from him, whence he is also called Paraclete.
"And of those being broken": To be broken in the proper sense is to be
snapped; and the word is also said of the beloved, who, putting off the
meeting with those who love them, use such a bodily, that is a broken, manner.
Whence it is taken also of other things of like kind.
"Nevertheless he runs toward those who approach": He sets out in these the
things of the evangelical parable of the prodigal, and the like. And
wonderfully he also says whole wholes, putting to shame the heretics then,
who said that the soul alone is saved by God, and not the body.
The Lord then, he says, as whole, having taken soul and body, has saved us
wholes who are of soul and body. And there were then heretics from Simon, as
Irenaeus and Hippolytus show. And those who dogmatize the things of Origen
also think the same. And "having grown around," that is 'having been entwined
around'.
"Namely the good": Note whom he calls the friends summoned together; and a
little later he understands these to be the good angels. And see how he
understands, "That it may be the dwelling of all who rejoice."
"But you, as your letters show": But you, he says, O Demophilus, also insulted
the priest who said that the impious and repenting one was pitiable, that is
worthy of mercy. And it seems to me that Demophilus insulted a presbyter; for
he is accustomed to call the priests presbyters, as he made clear in "On the
Ecclesiastical Hierarchy." And here too a little later going down he also
confirms what was said. And the syntax is by hyperbaton. You were insulting
the ( 15Δ_438 > priest who justified even this, that the impious one is
pitiable, that is worthy of mercy.
"You contracted the Holy of holies": You contracted, that is you humbled, and
you diminished; or not this, but you contracted, that is you gathered, as has
been said, as if you lifted, having carried from the altar, although deacons
and presbyters have been entrusted to do this, the law saying "And priests
shall wrap the holy things," as he also says in the following leaf.
"It is not lawful a priest": That a presbyter, even if he sins, must not be
called to account by a deacon or a monk, still less by laymen, and these
things apart from heresy; for above the order of monks is that of the
ministers, that is deacons. And if this, much more is it not lawful for a
bishop to be accused by anyone.
"And the priests angels": Note that priests too are angels and interpreters,
that is teachers of the divine judgments after the hierarchs; and that the
letter was written concerning a presbyter persecuted and insulted by a monk is
shown especially from here.
"Through the midst of the ministers": Note that those who seek to learn the
divine things from presbyters ought not to do this through themselves, even if
they are monks, but through the midst of deacons, and this when a fitting time
is present.
"Through whom also being": As we said above in the "Ecclesiastical Hierarchy,"
in the chapter on monastic perfection, through presbyters the monastic schema
is given to them


"The ordering": Concerning the ecclesiastical orderings, and what the liturgy
of those who live as monks is; for he calls them attendants. He says these
things also in the "Ecclesiastical Hierarchy."
"The gates of the inner shrines": For the guarding of the gates of the Church
has been assigned to deacons, as he says in the "Ecclesiastical Hierarchy," in
the chapter on the priestly perfections, a little after the beginning of the
chapter.
( 15Δ_440 > "Of the divine veils": That there were also then veils around the
altar.
"Nor do you have any of the fitting things": Note that the one who leaps in
upon the holy things disorderedly and contrary to the sacred statutes is
believed neither to see nor to hear nor to have anything from them, even if he
touches them with his hands.
"He dared; for not yet": In common, "He was justly punished," as if this one
also.
"Whenever someone attempting beyond worth": That even if someone does the
fitting things, but contrary to his own order and worth, this one too
oversteps what is reachable.
"Demons truly theologizing": Concerning the demons theologizing Jesus.
"But proclaimed out": That every bishop in another's affairs is proclaimed
out from theology.
"And the priests wrap": Who are able to gather and lift the holy things, and
that it is not reachable for all.
"And Miriam becomes leprous": Note the cause for which Miriam the sister of
Moses became leprous.
"And upon the sons of Sceva": And the demons sprang upon the sons of Sceva,
because neither believing in Christ nor having been ordained they were
exorcizing the demons, which was lawful for the exorcists alone. This lies in
the Acts of the holy Apostles.
"Nor the just things not according to worth": Note what it is, "he shall
justly pursue the just," which Moses says, and that it is not lawful to pursue
even the just things not according to worth. And you have this also in what
follows. And Origen exercises this at length in the 10th of the "On the
Lamentations of Jeremiah."

that there is impiety among the priests not being set straight.
"And I will make a defense to you": A defense toward the objection concerning

[2] for God is not in a place, fittingly then neither are the intelligible

[3] "Symbols in a Christlike way": That is the celebration of the mysteries.

§ 3. [4] "But not for Demophilus": For it is not permitted, he says, for

"And they are just": What it is to pursue the just things justly, and that the
just is assigned to angels.
[5] "And Demophilus by reason": That is, let Demophilus chasten his own

[6] And note that in all beings through the first the things according to

[7] "But let it suffice": Let reason, he says, rule anger and desire; for
these are the subordinate; for in the restraint of these the rational
authority and power appears, so as to use them not simply, but toward some
useful end.
"Lawgiver from God": He means divine Paul.
"He does not deem worthy of the Church": From the letter to Timothy.
§ 4. "Set apart the things according to worth": Note an ecclesiastical order
that is praised, that the deacons may rebuke those who live as monks, and the
( 15Δ_444 > presbyters these, and the bishops the presbyters, and the apostles
and their successors the bishops. And among the successors of the apostles the
rebuke is from those of the same rank.
"And to the hierarchs the apostles": Note that the apostles also are greater
than bishops, and the successors of the apostles, and that each ought to be
set straight by the holier of his own order. And I think the successors of the
apostles are the present patriarchs.
"I do not know how I shall weep": Note the bitterness, or rather the sympathy
of the father.
"For even if not of the good": For even if you do not think, he says, that you
are an attendant of the good God who is among us, you are altogether alien
both from us and from the whole Church. And note that perhaps this was sinned
in a sojourn of the great Dionysius, and that he himself ranked Demophilus a
monk of the Church.
"Or the double sin": Note what he calls a double sin, when someone sinning
does not know that he has sinned. And this is of Jeremiah, as he clarifies
next: "That the people has sinned two things: they have abandoned me a
fountain of life," and what follows.
"That Demophilus thinks": A great and large and sympathetic exhortation to
sinners.
"But the thearchic consecrator": He means the Lord, that he does not ordain
things like those that have been done by Demophilus.
"And these of the sinners": Saying the Apostle's, "For he did not commit sin,
nor was deceit found in his mouth"; outright, "Separated from the sinners, and
having become higher than the heavens."
( 15Δ_446 > "And he judges it right to enjoy his own": That is of his own
philanthropy, and not of darkness and punishment.
"For also to those into him": It lies in the Gospel according to Luke.
§ 5. "Away, we do not have": May many good things come to you, most
sympathetic father!
"For also the most divine": Note that he calls the disciples of the Lord
without share of the Holy Spirit, seeking to rain fire on the Samaritans, as
we find in the Gospel. How then does the Lord say to them, "You do not know of
what Spirit you are"? But perhaps he said this, showing by what grace they
were chosen to minister; and perhaps also the Father, looking to the
perfection of the gift which they received after the resurrection, has said
this, because it has been written: "For the Holy Spirit was not yet, because
Jesus was not yet glorified." And he calls holy apostle Paul consecrator, as
having ordained for him the high-priesthood of the Church of the Athenians, as
has been written in the sacred Constitutions of the apostles.
"For one must be taught, not punished": Note a fine point and especially
useful now, that those who are ignorant must be taught, and not punished, just
as we also guide the blind, we do not punish them. And some say korre is the
temple of the head, some the jaw, others some part of the head. And it is
better to call korre, as I said, the temple; Homer however calls it korse. And
korre is oxytone. Demophilus then struck the one repenting on the temple.

"For those to do injustice": Note what he says follows those who wish to do
injustice or to benefit some, even if they have not brought to deed what they
planned.
"Attempting": Those namely whom they chose to wrong or to benefit, he says
they have not altogether disposed as they wished. And it is clear that the one
is the unwillingness of those being benefited, and the other from the
feebleness of those doing injustice.
( 15Δ_448 > "Both here and there": That the righteous are not only in the age
to come, but also in the present life attendants of good angels, that is
followers, and fellow-travelers.
"The greatest of all goods": That the greatest of all goods is to be always
with God.
"Both here and after death": That sinners both in this life and in the one to
come are punished, being condemned to be with the demons by their own passions
which they preferred.
"I shall mention": Note the vision of holy Carpus, of how much horror and
exhortation it is full.
§ 6. "When I had come to be once": A fine narrative, that one must not curse
those who sin, even if they offend against God. The history then is to be
noted, containing the unspeakable compassion of our Lord Jesus Christ, and
concerning those who repent. And he also speaks concerning the holiness of
divine Carpus. And this Carpus is the one whom the Apostle mentions in the
second Letter to Timothy.
"According to the pre-initiatory prayers": The Athenians called proteleia the
prayers with the sacrifices before marriages; for they called marriage a
telos, as perfecting the human being toward life; and this it is possible to
show from many poets of comedy. They held marriage then as a mystery. And they
also said to be pre-initiated both to be pre-instructed, and as it were
pre-purified toward preparation of a mystery, as Cratinus namely in the drama
Pylaia. For especially they called the mysteries of some of their falsely
named gods tele and teletai, as perfecting and leading those being initiated
to the perfect. The great Dionysius then named what is said among the Greeks
of the impious onto the mystery of the truth, and he called pre-initiatory
prayers the most perfect petitions before the life-giving mystery for
purification and uncondemned participation of the most perfect and perfecting
gift of the sacrifices. And "he hosted as a stranger" that is, he received as
a guest as a stranger.
( 15Δ_450 > "Still of the Hilaria days": There were certain days among the
idolaters, which they called Hilaria; and some were private and festive, as
when someone married or acquired a son, and the common and public were called
Hilaria, as when a king being proclaimed provided the public Hilaria. And it
was not permitted to wear mourning things on these, but both spectacles and
sacrifices were carried out publicly each day, and those mourning ceased and
watched, and they spent the appointed days in unceasing feasts at such
Hilaria. And there was also Hilaria, a special feast of the Romans in honor of
the mother of their gods, or rather demons, as Demophilus says in "On the
Sacrifices and Feasts among the Ancients."
"Through all of life": That is until the things of the present life stand. And
it is to be noted that those dear to God must through all of life admonish
those who have gone astray, and draw them to knowledge of truth. Note it then;
for some ignorantly say the contrary, using the apostolic saying that says: "A
heretical human being after a first and second admonition refuse," not
understanding the force of what has been said, which is clarified especially
from the following sayings. For then the refusal after a first and second
admonition is reasonable, when the teacher has come to the refusal, knowing
that such a one has been turned inside out, and escapes notice, being

self-condemned. For it is clear that among the sacred teachers the state also
of those who approach is revealed. And others have thought "after a first and
second admonition" thus, that is, after the teaching from the Old and New
Testament.
"For he was accustomed into this": Note that the apostolic tradition has
handed down the nocturnal canons of the psalmodies.
"Outside of disturbance": The negative particle "not" is to be construed with
"outside of disturbance," so that the whole is such: He rises, having enjoyed
neither of sleeps being many, because they are interrupted, ( 15Δ_452 > nor
being outside of disturbance, because the sleeps also are interrupted.
"He was asking God with some thunderbolt": A prestēr is a spiral motion from
above downward of fiery air. And such a thing burns, whence it is also said
from pimpran (to burn).
"Of boundless in human form": Note that angels also appear to the saints in
human form.
"By the of their own feet": Note "by the unsteadiness of their own feet,"
which shows the easy-swaying of the choice; for not wishing, they were not
falling.
"Into the gaping": Note how the devil casts human beings into the outer
darkness, which here he calls gaping, that is a pit, either deceiving or
thrusting those who obey him. And there are times when he also takes
licentious human beings as his co-workers for the deceit of others, as also
the idolater in the case of the one now deceived, about whom the word is.
Wherefore also in the vision he sees men among the serpents, that is the
demons, dragging those who have been conquered by the devil, from whom may the
Lord free us.
"By the evil": Note "by the evil," that is of sin, which we say is properly
evil as being born in choice. And "unwilling and being forced" is not to be
understood as some necessitating power, that is the devil, as it seems to
Manichees and Messalians; but that being persuaded to do the evil, we give
place to the deceiver to attack us continually, taking the strength from us
against us; and he will be powerless, if we are not persuaded, since the help
of God is near us assisting, as what follows shows.
"And to stretch a good hand": Note the ineffable compassion of the Lord.
"Of the hand already stretched forth": Of the hand of Carpus, clearly.
( 15Δ_454 > "Not of others": Of those crucifying, namely, the Lord says to
Carpus.
"Of good and philanthropic": Note that the Lord himself also calls the angels
good and philanthropic.
ON LETTER 9
§ 1. This holy Titus is the one known to the Apostle, whom he also established
as bishop of Crete, as the Letter written to Titus himself by divine Paul
says.
"But in the Symbolic": What the "Symbolic Theology" of the great Dionysius
contains.
"They wipe in": To wipe off is to wipe in and to throw something into one's
own mind, by metaphor from painters, who, attending to the truth, paint the
images. And "fathers of the ineffable wisdom" he means the prophets.

"For thus beholding we might sephthioimen": Sephthioimen that is we might put
in reverence, we might honor, we might wonder, we might be struck. And note
upon the holy and worshipful Trinity: "A fountain of life poured into itself,
and standing upon itself"; for from here both the consubstantial is shown, and
that the monad having gone forth into Trinity stands as far as it. Thus also
the theologian Gregory says in the first discourse On the Son, and in the
second Peaceful. And note: "Always through itself beholding itself"; and this
too he says concerning the holy Trinity.
"For the things outside it": Of it, namely of the symbolic fashioning. And
here too "of how much" lies instead of "of much," and "the things outside,"
that is the things readily suggested from the wording.
"Belly of God bodily": Belly of God because of "From the belly before the
Morning-star." "Word into air," because of, "My heart has belched a good
word." "Spirit being breathed out," because of, ( 15Δ_456 > "And by the spirit
of his mouth." And "bosoms," because of, "Only-begotten who is in the bosom of
the Father." And "plantlike," that is according to the form of plants, because
of, "It is a tree of life," and, "From a shoot, my Son, you have gone up,"
and, "Jacob shall flower and shoot," taken of Christ, whose fruit of salvation
has filled the earth, and the like. Note then how belly is said of God, and
belching of the word, and such things.
"Or springs of waters": Springs of waters according to, "With you is a
fountain of life," and, "They have abandoned me a fountain of life of living
water," and as many such. And "radiances," because of, "Who being a radiance
of the glory," and, "Lord, in the light of your face they shall walk, and in
the light we shall see light," and the similar.
"And upon the intelligibles": Indicative "of the providences" is, "As an eagle
he shall cover his nest" and the things with it; and of the "gifts," as, "You
open your hand," and, "Who gives food to all flesh"; or rather this too is
indicative of providence. And of "manifestations," because of appearing in the
form of a human being to the saints in the Old, and in the form of a dove, the
Holy Spirit. And of providence also the punitive of the impious; it has been
