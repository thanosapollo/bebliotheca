Of the blessed Cyril from the 11th discourse of those Against Julian.
It is true to say that the conceited and contemptuous man is a braggart, and
will accomplish nothing, according to the voice of the prophet; for conceit
is a cutting-off of progress, according to the common saying that is also in
the mouth of the many. From the same discourse. Conceit is a cutting-off of
progress. For one who confesses =76.1060= ignorance, someone might more
easily set upon the things not yet discerned; but as for the one who is in
the opinion of knowing, seeming to know renders him both unwise and ignorant
wholly and altogether; for he does not admit the words of those who teach.
From the 12th of those Against Julian. The president of the games does not
simply grant the exceptional honors to those of the athletes who wish them,
but rather distributes the prizes analogously to the successes of those who
contend. From the same. To allot equal things to unequals is of the greatest
injustice. From the 12th of those Against Julian. It seems to me to be best
and wise not to miss the fitting time in affairs. From the 12th of those
Against Julian. That no one would choose to speak ill is, I think, quite
clear to everyone; yet this is popular and of the marketplace.
From the 14th discourse Against Julian. It is possible for every human being
to be able to succeed and to live splendidly. From the same. Man is
self-called and goes of his own will toward whatever seems good to him; it
seemed thus well to the Creator of all, so that both praise being due should
follow those who have chosen to do good, and being justly mocked by all
should follow those who go the other path. From the 14th discourse of those
Against Julian. Signifying wittily the confusion of this life and the bitter
overflowing of affairs in it, the Melodist said, This is the sea, the great
and wide. From the 14th of those Against Julian. Those who are keen toward
salvation are somehow dark and not clear to all. From the 15th of those
Against Julian. The human mind is governed by its own wills, and of its own
accord rushes toward whatever of the things to be done; and this too seemed
rightly to God the master-craftsman of all; so that even if one of us should
come to be in participation of the Holy Spirit that has taken up dwelling in
him through holy baptism, he is not for this reason released from living
according to his own judgment, and as it may seem to him; but as with free
reins he has inclined both toward the good and toward the contrary.
From the 15th of those Against Julian. Slander is somehow always an open
horse-road, and lack of control in words is in many; but I think that proofs
ought to follow the accusations which one might make against anything; and if
the one who has chosen to accuse is weak toward this, he will carry off the
reputation of calumniating.
From the 15th discourse of those Against Julian. To be well-clothed is only
boyish, and has the pleasant only in seeming, and mocks the eyes.
From the 16th of those Against Julian. For those accustomed to till the earth
best, being praised as due would reasonably follow. From the 16th discourse
of those Against Julian. And that the repayment of the reward is also
analogous to the achievements of each; equal in weight to the sweats, the
rewards will be allotted by God to those who have chosen to act bravely;
for the Savior himself also will give full assurance, saying that the
distribution of the talents did not come to be in equal measure; and setting
of those who had succeeded, the one over ten cities, and the other over five.
For the life of each is as it were weighed in the balance, and the repayment
will altogether somewhere stand equal in weight to our equity. From the 16th
discourse of those Against Julian. He who pleases men would reasonably have
the praises from them unto the force of a counter-reward; yet he will receive
of the things from God nothing at all; for the good of manliness, I mean that
upon earth, has made not him the overseer rather, but the eyes of those who
see. From the 16th discourse of those Against Julian. To insult is altogether
shameful and unbeautiful and unfitting to those who have chosen to live well,
and no such person would there be among those in desire of dignity and
equity.
From the 18th book of those Against Julian. Each of those upon the earth has
been allotted the reins of his own wills, and of his own will he rushes
either with it or upon it, that is either toward virtue, or at any rate
toward the worse. From the 18th discourse of those Against Julian. There are
some, and these are very many in life, who having supposed what [seems] to
themselves to be well, strengthen it without examination. And they think that
they speak things wise and undisputed wholly and altogether; yet they steal
the reputation of seeming to be wise and shrewd =76.1064= only so far, until
the argument from those of another mind meets them, standing up against them
with more vehement proofs. From the 18th discourse of those Against Julian.
In the "Nothing in excess" is what is well; but in the superfluous, the
slanders. From the 18th discourse of those Against Julian. To those who do
not have the equitable and admired life, it is burdensome, as it seems, that
others succeed by good living and virtue; for they make the thing an
accusation of their own baseness, and provoked by the good repute of others
they travail with the flame of envy, although they ought rather, being
zealous, to choose the better, and shaking every rope, to love to contend
with those thus splendid, not to make a pretext of love of blame, the things
which they have not been able to accomplish.
From the 19th discourse of those Against Julian. The matter of fine speech
and the splendor of diction lies very much among those outside; yet there is
in it nothing of the things that bring benefit. For they did not know the God
who is by nature God and Creator, but they bring countless gods into life.
