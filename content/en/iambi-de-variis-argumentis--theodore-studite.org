Iambs of our holy father and confessor Theodore, abbot of the Stoudios, on
various subjects

On the relics of the holy martyrs
Whoever bears the faith of her who flowed with blood will plunder the divine
grace that gushes in the relics; for touching them, as she did the hem, he
would strengthen the body and hallow the soul.

2 On the cell of our father Mourn me, cell, having a sluggish worker, the
worst alone of the possessors you have had. I do not brighten you with the
light of prayers; I do not fatten you with the stream of tears; nor again do I
cleanse you with the ways of purity. But do you, Savior, Creator,
Light-bringer of all things, raise me, awaken me unto your fear, give me a
taste of the love of your charity, wing me to be lifted above the passions,
while there is still time for well-doing, that I may offer glory, thanksgiving
to you who will the salvation of all.

3 On those who renounce the world Mortal men, let us flee from the
deceitful world.
Christ calls, let us run; for the life is fair-sailing, riding over storm,
cares, and turnings. For this care alone belongs to the monk: to reach that
calm harbor in which every toil of the grievous things has ceased. O how
all-hymned is the life that is a stranger to these! And who is a prudent
merchant that gains? He who, having left all, would choose to bear the cross.

4 On an abbot Seeing what a height the presidency is, advance according to
worth to the rank trembling; for you have chosen a work full of many dangers,
of much care and not of small thought. Look, take thought watchfully for those
you lead out; let not a nursling of yours be plundered somehow through
inexperience. Having set yourself as a pattern of good ways, you will know,
knowing that you will give the account, when Christ sits for the judgment of
all.

5 On a subordinate Come here, boxer, come to me, stand burning, bending your
neck readily obedient, wholly humble, dead in the will, making manifest every
thought of the heart, that you may remain perfectly in the stadium. Let
neither desert nor pillar frighten you, nor another order of the God-running
lives; you excel all, as it stands God-written; for you are accomplishing the
threshold of the first of the martyrs.

6 On the one who holds the second place Moses was ruler, and the son of Nun
running with him straight after him, as a strange attendant. And you, being a
second face in station, see how you hold the brotherhood together: lead,
guide, and shield with them in tension, peacefully and compassionately and
meekly, keeping the laws laid down from God, bringing forth just judgments
without offense, now sharing the pain of those who toil from passion, now
encouraging those who run from longing, that you may be counted worthy of
lawful leadership, as yourself leading the people of the Lord.

7 On the steward Having obtained the work of Stephen, the crown of the
martyrs, to preside in distributing the needs to the Christ-chosen multitude
of the brothers, give your labors to God each day, keeping each one as your
own member laboriously; for do not treat this one with affection and that one
with hate and envy, but looking ahead equally to the advantage of all, so that
no one is moved toward what is needed, but you would bring in what is fitting
of yourself, that, completing your course becomingly, without offense and with
all strength, in a God-minded way, you may find the wage of the care you
chose.

8 On the epithemonarchs You who have taken of the brotherhood both to judge a
judgment and to bring forth the just, do not bring out your decisions
according to the face, not by partiality, nor for the sake of wrath, not with
a loving nor with a hating heart, but gently and compassionately, mercifully,
with counsel and long-suffering, vigorously, having sharpened the mind purely,
as an arrow, may you strike the straight words at the mark, and hunting the
true in every word may you offer just judgments to God; for in the manner in
which you judge, you shall also be judged.

9 On the inspectors Set in order as eyes for the kindred, see and watch all
well-aimedly in days, in nights, at noondays, lest the faces of the young be
two, lest factions and tent-sharings come to be, the ruin of familiarities,
works of darkness, wantonnesses, laughters, disorders of insolence, and every
evil that bears far from God; speaking these things often to those who stand
first, without partiality, by the word of truth, may you obtain the divine
oversight unerringly, having the recompense of the good things.

10 On the canonarch Standing as leader of the lyre of the songs, appear a
psaltery to the strange music-making; trumpet the wood in season, as it should
be, and move your tongue as bearing a plectrum, always bringing in the
sequence becomingly, unconfused, well-sounding, after the manner of an
instrument, rhythmically ordering the mouth of the brotherhood; you would
assign all things to all worthily, not toward favor, not wrath or a gift of
grief, but in just word and manner; for all are members of God and of you.
Thinking thus, then, you will receive a crown from God.

11 On the taxiarch

Having received the charge of marshalling, as taxiarch of the people of the
Lord, keep order at every time and place, in the goings-in and likewise the
goings-out, in the meetings of the faces of the eminent, in the gatherings of
the temple of the Master, ranging all in a row and with reason, keeping each
one's appointed station, requiring the fitting garment and gait. If you do
these things wisely and intensely, in a God-befitting way and meekly, with
judgment, you will have grace and a great wage from God. 12 On the cellarer
Who, child, is a worker of God as you are? For you have a work far more than
the others, ruling the foods of the brotherhood. Come wholly with all strength
to the care, giving to each what is fitting in judgment. You would receive the
fires of much inquiry, bearing this one thus, another otherwise, meekly,
calmly, compassionately, mercifully. For thus you will have the crown with
Stephen.

13 On the refectorian Preparing breakfast for your brothers, child, stack well
the settings of the tables, weigh the bread; and if there should be any food,
dividing equally, but more to the greater; for the word is to count them
worthy of double honor. Spare likewise even the last-stander; for each is your
member, even if not equally. And standing by as at the Master's table, think
that you bear service to apostles. Turn, look, distributing even another
drink, that it may be well and abundant grace be yours.

14 On the cook Who, child, does not crown the cook, you who bear much toil
each day? A servile thing is yours, but a great wage; a filthy use, but a
washing of faults; fire burns you now, but does not judge the one to come.
Coming then, stand firm at the boiling at once from dawn, breaking firebrands,
rinse the pots, boiling the children's foods as of God; you would salt these
with prayers, as you will season them, that you may be blessed as Jacob of
old, and rejoice in these, completing your course. 15 On the tailors, that is
the vestiarers You are very much the governors and distributors of the
garments of the brotherhood; no small contest is yours in this, but much care
and anxiety and toil: to sew, to renew, to cleanse and wash, to give to each
what fits by reason, equally not, but equally by affection and longing, for
another is more worthy than another by toil. See then to do these things
soberly, that you may receive the debt of recompenses from God who gives
worthy gifts.

16 On the wakers Besides all else, not small is the toil for you either: to
raise up the sleepy kindred, to drive the wanderers into the midst. Therefore
whenever someone trumpets the wood, having run at once as angels into every
little sleeping-room, into a hidden place, raise them to the hymn of the
undefiled words, bringing them together into the temple of the Master in days,
in nights, at noondays, calling each of them fittingly, that he who measures
all things and distributes according to worth may give you a worthy wage.



17 On the infirmarian A divine thing it is to bear the burdens of the sick;
having obtained this, box, O my little child, warmly, eagerly to complete your
course. From dawn at once you would look upon your bedridden, healing first of
all with words; then again you would offer the gifts of foods becomingly, as
is needed for each, with discernment of reason; for he is a member: do not
pass by the neighbor. Thus serving, a great wage is yours: the unapproachable
light, a fair lot of the heavens.

18 On the sick Receive well for me the gift of the disease, child; for it is a
visitation of divine care. You would bear endurably the flame of the furnace
in the dew of thanksgiving. May you become Job, saying his words, upon which
he did not allow himself to sin even in word. And if someone should pass you
by without looking, be silent, in all things praising the gift according to
need, that you may fare well in body toward the heart.

19 On the cobblers The craft of making sandals is as the best; for it is of
the great apostle Paul. Zealous for his sweats of labors, receive warmly the
toils each day as workers of Christ, of this care, having cut hides and skins
as is needed, making the old new, and then also new ones, not by sluggishness
throwing toward misuse of reason, if anything is not worthy of throwing away,
or bearing the cutting deficiently above. For doing all things worthily you
will accomplish the course of those who bear witness. 20 On the dormitory You
who give sleep to all as a ransom of the toils which the bright day has
furnished, grant also to me, my Christ, Word of God, sleep that is light,
sweet, and swift-running, driven out from evil phantasms, but filled with good
dreams. And raise me when the wood is struck, unrelaxed, standing firm to sing
gladly, setting my feet strongly in praise, guarding my thoughts from the
craft of demons, making my tongue clear to chant vigorously, unto glory,
praise of your greatest power, that having passed the night perfectly, having
risen at dawn, I may see the light of your commandments from morning.

21 On rich and poor Know who you are, O man, and what remains for you, where
you sojourn and †I might remove† you would have. Become wholly toward the
things above in care; for there the light is full, but also judgment unending.
When you would say of the things here: breeze, stream, and night-dream, gold
is poured as dust, glory withers, the rest flows. All things here are
much-groaning, lamentable. Hold attentively, lest time roll you, of which
there is no recapture or play.

22 On worldly people Disciples of Christ, the good and meek, bearing wrath
against one another in fights, how do you enter his dread house? How will you
partake of the all-undefiled gifts
strangely? And indeed Judas, who approached in deceit, loving outwardly but
raging within, was shown a disciple who had sold the Master. Let no one then
come forward being hostile, planting a sword against him in the heart,
receiving eternal fire as a counter-gift. Knowing the things here, let us hold
them very affectionately.

23 On a traveler Coming, sit, everyone, without insolence; for it is the house
of God here, friend. And having knocked, say the cause for which you are
present, waiting a little again, being long-suffering for the answer. And
going inside with fear and longing, give your prayers to your Master. And say
nothing of the vain things of life, meeting those who have fled life. But if
anything is solemn, if anything is delightful in words, answer, speak toward
all that is fitting, that you may turn back from here purely and savingly,
running to your own.

24 On those who pass by See how you go your way, friend; the practical one
must be spoken and known. Cliffs, ambushes, many hunting-nets of the Deceiver,
and these: envies, laughters, satieties of insolence, fornication, glory, love
of money, murders, drunkenness, theft, adultery, and every other thing. Do not
attach yourself anywhere, lest you fall a strange fall; and if it has
happened, rise up with speed, since the judgment of all-devouring fire awaits
you.

25 Epigram on the monastery I have appeared a forecourt of a fortunate flock,
for I am of the first-leading forerunner of light, filled with a catch of
rational nurslings, whose life is unyoked, whose city is the pole of height,
of whom each and every one and each one wholly by longing, of whom no one has
anything, but this of all possessions; for their lot is the relation to God
alone. How safe, how noble is the course accomplished of those who have lived
in all ways cenobitically.

26 On the porter Standing within here, child, in fear, for it is a gate of
divine entrance-and-exit, give and receive the reports with discernment;
announce those of as many things as it is fitting to speak, but be silent
those of as many as bring harm; to those within, without, whether brothers or
strangers, closing, opening toward the season, to whom it is fitting, and give
need to a poor man, or if not, a word; for thus you will have a wage in the
standing-by.

27 On a brother going out to a service Before you go out this gate, child,
having set a seal upon every place of a member; go to the cares for which you
were sent, bent down [driving out harms], saying a psalm, a prayer, one of the
two, driving out the city-like harms. You would flee being raised with women,
even if it happens; and turning with speed remain within the monastery, lest
like a fish you die outside the salt.



28 On a brother returning from a service Having turned back, child, from the
care, twist back at once both flesh and your wandering mind, bringing inside
nothing vain from outside, but if anything is solemn; and this to those who
stand first. For you do not speak a word of the vain worldliness: "I saw, I
have eaten; and this and that happens," and then so-and-so and this one says
such things to you. For mixing an overturn for the neighbor with these you are
worthily called a curse of a prophet.

29 On the guesthouse Come forward, come, to the roof that hosts strangers,
traveling men, wearied with toils, may you partake of my hospitalities: of
longed-for bread, that feeds the heart, of sweet drink, that flows
ungrudgingly, and of coverings alien to cold, which my Master Christ, the
gusher of wealth, has furnished me, the God-known, friends, from his
all-blessed gifts by grace; blessing him as nourisher of the world, and making
in return only a prayer for me, that from there, by the stranger-loving
manner, I may obtain to enter the bosom of Abraham.

30 On holy icons The image which you see is of Christ; and call it too Christ,
but homonymously; for there is identity in naming, but not in nature; and the
worship of both is one, unsplit. He then who venerates this honors Christ, but
he who does not venerate is his enemy and very much so, as raging that the
drawn incarnate sight of him not be reverenced.

31 Another If we take the body of Christ to eat, drinking his life-bearing
blood, how would we not show him drawn, whose body also is figure-drawn? For
as simple, he has no circumscription; for he is God, outside every place. But
as having put on our essence, he is man, drawn by composition, having the
other unmingled according to essence and composed in one hypostasis. He who
glorifies the Word thus greatly has appeared a faithful nursling of the
church; for thinking otherwise he runs far from God.

32 Another Seeing it adorned by a form-making hand, drawn here, men, pluck the
fruit of benefit worthily, lifting the mind to their causes; for the writing
shouts great equally with a word, as: My honor is the glory of the
seeing-itself, for which I was brought in as a reminder only, guarding,
illuminating my friends, but alienating those who do not wish to venerate me
with relation from the inheritance above.

33 Another

Being fleshless before, Christ was not in sight; for no one of men, he says,
has seen God. But since he is clothed in the thickness of flesh, he who is
outside every time and place, becomes touchable, passible by the
composition; conceived as an infant from a maiden without seed, he grows,
thirsts and hungers and bears all of the things shown both in position and in
drawing. How then do you, O deranged, O wretch, not circumscribe Christ
reverently in flesh, according to his form the whole presence? But either
having changed your mind become faithful from error, or be carried far from
the court of God.

34 Another Christ is the one shown in the drawing, since he has appeared
mortal, being God by nature. For he who does not wish to look upon him thus,
says that he is embodied fantastically. 35 On the holy Theotokos Seeing you in
an icon, mother of the Lord, I approach to venerate you with fear and longing;
for truly so great a grace is yours, that by your shadow wonders gush.

36 Another Upon my arm I bear in drawing a child in mother-form, Christ the
all-king, flashing the light of incorruption and lifting the world from the
error of demons.

37 Another You, having borne God, excel angels; for him whom even they cannot
look upon, you yourself bear as Son with undefiled hands.

38 Another I bear the Son drawn in a mother-befitting way, that he may appear
a genuine child of a mother, who is raised of the drawing in a father-like
way, since he is twofold firmly in nature.

39 On the icon of Christ I have been portrayed, even if I am God by nature,
since I am also mortal according to essence; and I am venerated everywhere
even in my shadow, not yet receiving a division of the glory.

40 On the church of the Theotokos He who sees the house of the Virgin,
understand; for she has been shown a temple of God, she has temples of herself
everywhere on earth.

41 On an altar-cloth Seeing the World-maker embodied together with the one who
bore him in the drawing of the icons, be struck with wonder, how God is an
infant and does all things toward the
salvation of mortals; that having condemned corruption into himself he might
save the creature by the power of godhead; which thinking, she brings to the
Theotokos toward her ransom and her husband's an undefiled gift.

42 On the ciborium of the Forerunner Seeing me as a covering of the divine
table, think a Cherubic marshalling; for understand that Christ is being
mystagogued within, the Master of those above and of those below.

43 On the templon Seeing the divine table before your face, reverence the holy
and stand wholly in fear.

44 On the holy sanctuary Dread is this bema and full of fear, a light-bearing
temple of God, to be touched by the pure. Approach divinely, and you will
partake worthily; for the gift is fire burning the unworthy.

45 On the portals of the narthex Having opened the gates of the temple of
those here, go, offer a crushed spirit; and this is an acceptable sacrifice to
the Lord.

46 On the first entrance of the church Being about to enter the gate of the
house of God, may you be outside the drunkenness of cares, that you may find
the judge within graciously, giving you releases of the debts.

47 On a church entrance: on a cross Having me as a guard, do not be terrified
at noises of fears; for I am a dread-formed burner of demons.

48 Epigram on crosses Upon that in which I am formed, Satan has no place; for
I have taken down his power altogether.

49 Another At your sight from afar the dragon runs, seeing you as the type of
the God-woven wood.

50 Another O cross, my light, shine for me at every time, driving far the
darkness of my soul.

51 Another This one, having been fixed in the place of Golgotha, blossomed for
the world a life-giving fruitfulness.

52 Another On this the All-king, having stretched out his hands, raised the
world from the pit of faults.

53 Another Hail, trophy of the panoply above; in you every phalanx of demons
has been turned.

54 Another Hail, thrice-blessed undefiled wood; in you the mortality of the
curse has ceased.

55 Another Hail, all-light burden of monks; for in you the course of Christ is
light for them.

56 Another Hail, guardian of the place where you sit by; may you shelter
unharmed those who remain here.

57 Another Someone, forming me, even only with a finger, at once turns the
hostile demon.

58 Monostich epigrams on crosses The cross of those who see is a
lightning-bearing brightness. The cross of monks is a fair-running salvation.
The cross of those who love in a God-inspired way is a fornication-slayer. The
cross of those who weep is an eraser of faults. The cross of the purity-loving
is a most sure guard.

59 Another

Everyone who draws me, wish also to draw Christ, since he himself has been
woven with me in the passion; for he is my enemy and destroyer who does not
draw that one, even if he draws me.

60 Monostich I torch-lead mortals and I burn those who rule the dark.

61 On the Forerunner I am an image of the revered Forerunner and those who
venerate me with longing worthily, as making the honor toward that one, I keep
safe from the error of heretics.

62 On saint John the Theologian Seeing you drawn, the son of thunder, the
lightning of the Word, I am illuminated in my thoughts; for you, having
kindled a torch of unattainable words, arrive to loose the world from the
darkness of error.

63 On saint Paul the apostle Hail, great vessel of the knowledge of God, from
which all draw the divine things abundantly; for having pulled the whole world
out of error you came to height, having reached the third heaven.

64 On saint Zacharias You, having shone the greatest of the prophets, are
called a prophet of God and a sacrificer; and offering a divine fruit to the
Master you are sacrificed with a knife before the table of the Lord.

65 On saint Dionysius Being great in the divine things, thrice-blessed, you
describe the angels mystically; and completing every hierarchy you fill the
world from here with a breadth of doctrines.

66 On saint Basil You flashed with the radiance of your light-bearing life and
you brighten the world with works and words; and having taken the keys
yourself as a new Peter you are a guardian of the whole church.

67 On saint Gregory the Theologian Thundering the divine things with the cry
of the doctrines you truly sounded the under-heaven, blessed one; and having
made foolish all the heresies firmly you established the world in your words.
68 On saint Gregory the Wonderworker As some great sun having shone in life
great you scatter the night of wickedness; and working wonders by the light of
prayers you drive away swarms of demons, father. 69 On saint Epiphanius The
brightness of action showed you a morning-star by the height of the greatest
wonders, from which, flooding the streams of the doctrines, you are a triumph
of all who hold heresy.

70 On saint Ignatius the God-bearer Having Christ as love in your heart you
were shown a tent-fellow of the apostles, thrice-blessed; and burning out the
error with warm contests you are some other Paul by your letters.

71 On saint Athanasius Who would tell the contests of your toils, O
pentathlete, glory of the teachers? For having run as a great giant on behalf
of God, wearing a crown, you took down utterly evil enemies.

72 On Chrysostom Having gushed an unending spring of all-golden words you
water the whole under-sun, blessed one; and opening doors of salvation to
mortals you send the whole world out into the heavens.

73 On saint Cyril Having treasured the divine things in various ways you
enriched the world with God-inspired words; and having pulled down the profane
heresies you have appeared a herald of orthodox doctrines.

74 On saint Gregory of Nyssa Gushing an abyss of all-sweet words, blessed one,
you shook out all error in a noetic way; and being called another great Moses
you lead the people of the Lord most excellently.

75 On saint Theodore of Sykeon You, a great gift of God given to mortals, took
down every troop of demons, father; for having shone at once as a dawn
light-bringer you have appeared equal to angels by the height of life.

76 On saint Antony You have appeared a pillar of fire by the radiance of life,
guiding the world into the path of salvation; and having tripped up the
phalanxes of the demons you inhabited a desert, as some Eden of angels.

77 On saint Hilarion By the brightness of your light-bearing life you are a
morning-star shining at dawn; and brightening the world with rays of wonders
you drive away the harmful gloom of the demons.

78 On saint Euthymius You worked the desert into a city of God; for you filled
it with dense multitudes of the Christ-minded; and having flashed with the
bolts of the wonders you brighten the whole world as a light-bringer.

79 On saint Sabas You truly plowed the desert with toils, being most
beautifully heavy with various works; and having filled it with the noetic
stars you run as some sun in the midst of them.

80 On saint Dalmatos A great star having shone from some sun, you shine with
him all-bright good deeds; a support † but if they have appeared † of
orthodoxy you rule the fold of the monks from the fathers.

81 On saint Pachomius Imitating Abraham somehow, blessed one, you have become
father of many, whose race is golden; and bearing mystagogy from an angel you
have set laws for your people, as Moses.

82 On saint Arsenius Watering your soul with the pouring of your tears you
sprouted fruits altogether incorruptible; and speaking to God intensely with
bright prayers you are a great angel of the hesychasts.

83 On saint Theodosius Having stretched out a life high as heaven you have
appeared starred in a multitude of children; and multiplying the lightnings of
the wonders you scattered all kinds of clouds of demons.

84 On the holy Dios By the brightnesses of your God-shining ways you run out
as a bright star from the east; and having illuminated Byzantium most
excellently you first shepherd the flock of monks worthily.

85 On saint Christopher The Christ-martyr, Christ-minded, Christopher, giving
his own blood to God, holds a spring pouring a stream of wonder-working, which
those sick with a cough, drinking, cool the passion, like thirst, especially
whenever they come here faithfully. 86 On the church of the holy apostle Peter
For him to whom you have given, Christ, the keys of the things above, I have
built this house below.

87 On saint Andrew the apostle O first-called, glory of the apostles, the
all-great light-bringer of the fathers, keep your people together with the
shepherd.

88 On the church of Chrysostom To those who have raised your house with all
longing grant an interceding gift, father.

89 On the church of the all-famed <Euphemia> Guard those who from longing for
you have built the temple, martyr, free from harm.

90 On the Tetrakamaron You have appeared a wondrous work, altogether
beautiful; and I have spoken to you as to a living thing, church; for you bear
within a vision of icons, an unutterable sight of the spectacles above.
Receive him, glory of the apostles, all-blessed Evangelist, Theologian; and to
those who have raised this for you with longing grant grace and release of
faults.

91 On the church of the holy Theotokos at the Triton You who are near to all
for help of speed, set the voyage in order of those whom you hold warmly, who
lie before you with your prayers, Virgin.

92 On a historiated rag A dread wonder, to see Christ in a drawing, who has
done all things toward the salvation of mortals; for in the things according
to us he himself appeared strangely, coming fleshless and going above as
mortal. And for memory's sake the truly renowned Symeon offers this gift to
the Master.

93 On the icon of Christ I am Christ, shown in the drawing; and him who has
faithfully icon-worked me, as hating the heresy of the opponents, Leo the
Patrician, I shelter in all things.

94 On an enclosed monk An enclosed monk is one who, remaining outside the
passions, has shut the whole heart toward God; closing reasonings, the
wanderers of error, and sending the mind again to the spectacles above, having
prayer that burns the demons, bearing silence that mystagogues the depths, a
soul uncrushed in the joy of the hopes, a work of hands and
toil of the throat with tension, unceasing song, a crushed spirit; thus
opening with an angel-befitting sight he illuminates with rays those who look
in a God-inspired way; saying nothing of the useless things unto laughter, but
what it is for the stranger to be benefited, and fleeing even these: to speak
at length unto satiety. If then you can, you run toward these things savingly;
but if not, you would flee into the life of the living together, in which you
brighten the contests more.

95 On one who lives well Most excellent is the temperate man, who lives in the
fear of God. Cleansing of soul is better than flowing wealth.

96 On monks, dactylic and elegiac [Hail, champion men, above Achilles; this
one killed mighty Hector with the sword, but you, having armed yourselves with
the way of life of monks, taking in hand the sword of obedient holiness, slay
Belial, the great-boasting mind of darkness; from here you have departed to
heaven prize-bearing, having put off only the dead skin-coat of the things
here.]

97 Epigram to himself Humble soul, come to me, receive a word. The time is
sharp as a runner passing through, the end is near, and it is not possible to
pass by. Let us not then weary ourselves with vain cares, truly drawing into a
pierced jar, carding into fire, as it laughably stands; but if anything is
divine, if anything is saving, looking to these things and pursuing them with
longing, that we may stand with boldness before the Judge and Master of all
things, having fled the fire of Gehenna, as it is written, and running toward
the all-unutterable light.

98 Epigram in the prison of the Chalcite He has appeared a stranger of God,
whoever does not hold all the things here as strange by relation to the things
above; for thus he would somehow be shown a master of the things below. 99 In

100 In the same guard For me the body is a guard and only that; and every

101 In the same jail Look hour by hour at the end of life, and may you see

to think that one dies each day.

102 Epigram on the lamps

I am a vessel of the unsleeping lamp, bearing the over-bright rays in souls. O
lamp-bearer of the triple-shining sun, flash for us a light more than
lightning. And you who have made me of light, who have offered me here, may
you find from there my shining of unutterable light.

103 Toward reading carefully the things on the walls Know, as you pass, a
portion of the things written up; for no divine word is to be overlooked by
you.

104 Epigram on a house of women who love strangers Being very spacious in the
bowels of hospitalities, as some other Abraham the father-ruler, the women who
are mistresses of this sojourn receive a multitude of monks with longing,
putting to shame the narrow-souled passion of those who hate strangers by the
many-bowelled relation and acquiring the scepter-rule from above.

105a Epigram on strangers Everyone who nourishes strangers hosts Christ; for
which reason eagerly here may you nourish those being hosted very much, and
you shall be nourished strangely. ....... seeming to restrain my tongue from
erring; for no one has strength to bind the word of God, for which you have
long been guarding me here, Byzantis. Epigram on a certain delightful place
The place here, fair in appearance and beautiful in composition, has it in it
to produce delight. Yet nothing is delightful unto the whole, whenever it
would be judged toward the vision of God. For he himself is the all-fair
sight, the all-shining and lasting delight, whose affection if someone would
take burning, he quenches every earthly relation and, having the heart burned
in a God-inspired way, would long to arrive there and remain, where the
triple-sunned power shines divinely, where the troops of the righteous feast.
On iambs, a composition Having known the thoughts and also the phrases of the
four I was truly pleased with the well-tongued thing, as of a four-sounding
instrument of music-making fitted somehow into a fatherly hymn. Epigram on
tombs This place, being an enclosure of memorials, let it become for you a
memorial of fate, friend. And having groaned, consider with trembling, that
already a little, and you pass from life, having gone there, where you will
remain eternally, having been set there, where there is judgment of works; if
you receive these things, you will gain light of soul. On the sister This is
your tomb and the end of every course; from here you remain for Christ, he
meets with fear. Alas; how, sister, have you gone before and I remain? Where
did you sojourn, and where are you buried, and how for you? †Do you not yet
fear the things here from the dreams,† for it is not night, but there is
judgment of day. Do not forget me, toward God, if you have a word, to pass to
Christ from the much-turning life. Epitaph on worldly people O bitter tomb,
completer of life, you now have Maria together with Euphrosyne, being a bride
of this patrician house, and honored with the dignity of the greatest of those
who bear the sword upon the shoulders among the first, a noble woman, most
dear to her husband, a house-keeper again and very commandment-loving, most
beautiful in manner, most famous in race, having lately left the present life
toward twenty-three in three years of life, whom Christ has released from
corruption above, but may the Master of all things also set her spirit in the
portion of the living.

106 On a monastery of men

If someone should call the place a city of God, he would not miss the vision;
for you see, looking around it in a circle, light-bearing temples, greatly
adorned, houses upon houses, very beautifully composed, gardens, plantings,
arrayed as Eden, everything, if anything, becomingly delightful in these; in
return for which, spectator, glorify in a God-minded way, remembering Leo
worthily, the builder with God of the things here.

107 On the reception-room of the same monastery I am a reception-room and a
strange forecourt of an admirable monastery, the one called New, hosting many
unto a fortunate exchange of noble Leo, master of the things here.

108 On the same Having been hosted here by right-hand providence, may I the
stranger bring a counter-host gift, the very best of what I might have in
plenty: these four verses together with prayers.

109 On tombs I, Leo, the founder of the monastery, knowing the delightful and
shadowy thing of life, have made a good roof for those who love the sun, this
house here bearing a memory of the end; for every noble man, remembering
death, would receive light and pass by darkness.

110 Another O bitter tomb of the sweet things of life, pulling all down you do
not suffice to be filled; small, greatest, wealthy, homeless, wise,
uninitiated, ill-faced, well-fitted, grinding all you have ash equally,
bearing nothing or only the things to be done, which will stand at the
judgment-seat of God; if they are most excellent, glory to the one who
possesses them; but if they are most evil, alas also for the base-doer. O man,
then, attend to what remains for you.

111 Epigram on Stephen Here Stephen shut in his body, having lived a
twenty-fifth year in life; a shoot at once beautiful on both sides, excelling
from noble blood, and having taken a staff from the glory of palaces,
longed-for by all for goodness of manners, not knowing a wife, but remaining a
virgin, whom Christ, having lifted with speed from this world as a rose from
thorns, has with others, crowning with the right hand.

112 On a house I have been shown a house altogether delightful in form of a
most excellent and very all-famed man, who also renews the whole monastery
with longing, having offered to God a ransom for sins.

113 On one who has fallen asleep

Here, having come forward, Theophylact has been buried in the tomb, the
thrice-blessed mortal, being a eunuch, but of a right-hand race, a
fair-looking youth, and very pious, who holds the first rank in the station of
spatharioi in lengths of days of palaces. He raised the temple here and a most
fortunate monastery delightfully for God and Master as a ransom of his faults
in life.

114 On Ignas, the monastery Renowned Leo, most excellent among men, having
been cut from a spouse by a Christ-entwined longing, took the thrice-blessed
monastic life, as procuring the inheritance above, having offered the whole
house to God, to be a fortunate monastery of women, and at the ending of the
God-famed course † ode † he has been buried, awaiting the day on which Christ
will come for the judgment of all.

115 On his wife You see the tomb of Anna grown together here, as of her who
thought equally with the spouse, to suffer separation for the sake of Christ
greatly and to embrace together with children the monastic life, a
light-bearing one of heaven-acquired glory, whence she herself also awaits the
hope, to run the heaven toward Christ himself then, whenever he comes to judge
all mortals.

116 On a woman who has fallen asleep A noble woman among women both in faith
and in life is she who has laid here the famous flesh and given the spirit to
God and Master, who was well pleased that she be called Eudocia fittingly by
the name-bearing manner, as having been well-pleased with all-beautiful ways,
to carry out a solemn life with a renowned husband and in dying having been
well-pathed to incline the father who begot her toward right faith.

117 On a woman who has fallen asleep A truly useful sanctified vessel,
balancing even a gold-inlaid crown, shone with sprinkled beautiful pearls, is
she who has laid here the thrice-blessed body; for having undergone the
cutting of a young husband, another hero in strength and in glory, both having
set out toward Armenia, she judged this most excellent indeed, to give all
things to God and Master, and to wear rags in the monastic manner. May she go
most excellently therefore in life, wearing a humble heart as an adornment,
the stream of tears equal to emerald, an unsleeping eye and prayer as light;
by this she also completes the course divinely, unapproachable to the error of
heretics; and having been brought to God as a double gift she has it to
rejoice with the righteous unto the whole.

118 To the same The place is noble; for it is the tomb of Eirene who had a
peace-making life toward God.

119 On Dionysius I have appeared the tomb of Dionysius indeed, a strange monk,
a boxer, an obedient man, who has run many courses of orthodoxy and brought
out a martyr-contest end.

120 On the entrance of a monastery The first-fruits of Anna to God and Master
is this fold here and revered monastery, named of the all-pure Virgin and
bearing virgins in a bridegroom-famous way, to live ascetically, to excel in a
God-inspired way, and for a ransom of all the sins of this very one and of Leo
her spouse, together with whom also of John, a most dear child.

121 On Theophilos the deacon Much revered grace is present to you, because,
having had nothing from us, except the gift of the prayers of the sinners, it
has not yet ceased to offer us gifts, which having received we shall again in
a friendly way offer to you the friend the things of friends.

122 On Pamphilos the Thrice-sinner Pamphilos, having been shown a new
soul-destroying beast, was ostracized from the monastery of the Forerunner.

123 Letter to a grammatikos To your three-line gold-and-pearl phrasing I bring
in return an emerald-honored debt; and do you sing to me again in a
music-befitting way.

Christ, as a servant of God by deed and by best life, to all in piety piercing
through as the first; he used to wear truth in the heart together with purity,
