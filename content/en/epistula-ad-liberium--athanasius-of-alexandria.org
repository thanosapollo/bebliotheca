* Letter to Liberius
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Copy of our father among the saints Athanasius, archbishop of Alexandria,
which he wrote to Liberius, archbishop of Rome, that the God Word assumed a
perfect man for our salvation.
One God immortal, invisible, intangible; "for God is Spirit", this one
uncontained, not having a place where he is not. His Word is immortal, wisdom
incorruptible, invisible; he is called his firstborn Son, fellow-worker,
without beginning, intangible, "having neither beginning of days nor end of
life" expecting. "Through him all things came to be and without him came to
be not even one thing". This is he who formed man; this is he who showed the
ark, how it should come to be; this is he who gave the promise to Abraham;
this is he who came down to save the people, who gave the law to Moses; this
is "the God of Abraham and God of Isaac and God of Jacob", "who spoke in the
prophets", who established the old and the new covenant; who "in the last of
the days" taking flesh from a virgin, mortal though he himself is not mortal,
weak though he himself is not weak, owed to death though he himself is not
owed, visible though he himself is not visible; this was hung, not he
himself; this was buried, not he himself; this endured all the human
passions as a man, not he himself; but that one is power of God, that is,
God. He who loosed the unloosable things of Hades and abolished the dominion
of the devil went up whence he also came down; having raised that which was
buried he offered it to the Father, having freed it from death, of which it
was held. In this one we understand the Father (for one and the same in the
Godhead, in the power, in the essence, in the hypostasis, in the glory, in
the name of God) and the Son himself in the Father. This is he who after
taking the flesh was called Jesus, inasmuch as also man, that is he assumed
a man in himself, "in whom all the fullness of the Godhead dwells bodily".
This one we have not separated from the Father or from the Holy Spirit, nor
saying that the Father came down and came to be in a man. Thus one God
almighty is believed among us. And for this reason our faith is into one
God, Father almighty, and into his Son our Lord Jesus Christ and into the
Holy Spirit; and these from a unity of one Godhead, one power, one
hypostasis, one essence, one doxology, one lordship, one kingdom, one image
of the Trinity consubstantial, "through whom all things came to be". Thus we
also believe a resurrection of flesh, that is "of our body" according to the
holy scriptures, of the corruptible and mortal itself being changed into
incorruption and immortality by God. Amen.
