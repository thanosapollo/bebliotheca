* On the Term of Life

Of Theophylact Simocatta, of the former prefects, a question in
personification, that terms of life have been allotted to men, Theognostus
against Theophrastus. Theophrastus ought indeed, having clothed himself in a
reverence befitting the sacred, and wearing the robe of righteousness, and
having about him the shame of solemn gravity, to keep his tongue within the
bounds and to come to harbor at the anchors of the doctrines, and to run
somewhere inside the turning-post, and not to shoot far from the mark, nor to
sound unharmonious notes, nor to bark most unmusically, nor to describe the
circle apart from the center, or to carpenter the absurd things outside of
plumbline <and> rule, lest, legislating what is strange to the church and
erring in something great and altogether huge by the extravagance of the
attempt, he "become an outcast from the synagogue" of the greatest of the
sacred constitutions. But since he desires to reject the highways and royal
paths and is dragged down toward the less approved of the tracks, where the
thorn grows long and the bramble speaks freely and thicket has grown and a
hedge has been spread and nothing smooth or straight has citizenship, I will
lay hold of him with the staff of the word and will send him away from the
sacred enclosures as a wicked money-changer, having procured an evangelical
scourge, so that he may not, trafficking the word badly, profane the sacred
precinct of the church. But do not, men of the jury and guardians of the laws,
for this reason adjudge a blemish against me, the goad of the words having
measured the magnitudes of the boldness; for the contest is not about a small
matter for us. We have chosen today to approach the precinct of an immediate
arbitrator, since our manner is neither fond of lawsuits nor fond of Athens,
but rather fond of the gospel and unadorned and not enduring law-courts, one
who even charges us not to resist those who do injustice in insult, but even
to be obedient to those who strike; for unless we had taken up the truly
Pharaonic disobedience of resisting, which has the summons unpleasant toward
what is profitable, we would not have come down to this contest and consorted
with the dust of the wrestling-ground. For the fear is not ordinary, lest the
things that are due be somehow obelized, and visions of newer proclamations
appear to us, bringing a spectacle as of certain bogey attempts. For who
taught him that terms of life have not been established? Where of the sacred
writings has he ever heard that men have been allotted the wandering
indefiniteness of life? For if the heavenly vault has been measured
prophetically by a divine palm, and the number "of the angels of God" has been
known by the creative wisdom, that we may also weave in for you the oracle of
Moses, and "the boundaries of nations" and portions of peoples have been
allotted to the angels, and "the hairs of the head" have been numbered by God,
how have terms of life not also been fixed for men, having come into existence
by some creative care? Or did not he who created us, according to the
scripture, weigh "all things by measure and weight"? Away with the absurdity,
my good man. The sun, he says, "stood in his order" and the moon has
recognized her own setting, and "from the end of heaven is their going out and
their reaching even unto" the ends of heaven, and they were appointed "for
signs and for seasons and for years" by him who brought them forth; thus by
some order of master-craftsmanship the all-working majesty of God who brought
all things forth has ordered the whole; he who "calls the stars by names" and
who knew "all things before their generation" and who sets times and

seasons "in his own authority", did he bring forth our life destitute of
terms? Far from it. For where will you put "the time of every man has come
before me" or "the days of your death have drawn near" or "my spirit shall not
remain in the sons of men, but their days shall be a hundred and twenty
years", you who champion the unnatural indefiniteness and introduce the
strange marvels of the doctrines like certain Platonic ideas, phantoms of mind
and playthings of a light intellect, having improvised a wicked Academy for
the churches in a more Hellenic way? Or was not the descent of Israel into
Egypt also bounded by four hundred years, O you lover of the unbounded and
counterfeiter of the unalterable doctrines of the faith? And where will the
oracle championed by the psalmist go, "the days of our years are seventy years
or eighty" [of the distinction and power]? And if a spiritual law had not
bridled us not to embrace rash laughter, I would have been able to burst out
laughing with a boiling laugh, as if compelled to mock your attempts as a
ridiculous matter. Have you never heard David standing in chorus and singing
"make known to me my end, O Lord, and what is the number of my days, that I
may know what I lack"? Not, by the church, O man, "but come now, pass over",
according to the poetry, and migrate toward our things, having thrown away the
error as some orphan and unpiloted thing. Having fought against the yoke you
have been deprived of truth; but having come to acknowledge it you will
altogether have that of which you have been stripped. Have not the patriarchs
fallen asleep old men and full of days? And what does it seem to you that the
scripture indicated by this? I for my part think "the terms of life having
been fulfilled" according to that divine and great Basil. For in truth a
sparrow "shall not fall without our Father" who is in the heavens, O you noble
toward inquiry and most ready for turns of words. The "what have I to do with
you, woman? my hour has not yet come" and the flight of Christ from the Jews
because the hour of the cross had not yet come, how will they not speak
against your doctrines and like certain catapults stone your words and winnow
them like dust "which the wind drives from the face of the earth"? Who, being
drunk, handed over to you these mythical pieces of nonsense? Why, having
trafficked a cargo of empty sophistry, do you raise your brows to a great
height? What profit has been established for you, weaving "a spider's web"
according to the prophet and weaving small and insubstantial thoughts against
great doctrines? Why, having thrust aside the tunic of contemplation woven
from above, have you put on the "leather" and coarser "tunics" of the
intellect, which it is fitting for those who have run aground and are banished
from the divine luxury of the thoughts to wear, because of the emptiness of
the attempt forbidden to them? "Be put to shame by the gray hair" of the right
words; it is a coeval of the church; reverence this seniority of birth. The
newer is always somehow more dishonored by nature; the tragedians on the stage
also compose, legislating that the young man is a bad thing. Therefore do not
say that "I am younger"; you also know the poetry dishonoring the newer.
Therefore also those of the wisdom and education from without revile time as
always teeming with strange things, being sick with the newer things. "Ask"
your fathers and they will announce to you and "your elders and they will tell
you" that deaths are brought on when the terms of life are fulfilled.
Sufficiently, O men of the jury and counselors of peoples and guardians of the
laws, have I made my defense concerning the words just now; and already it is
time for me both to carry off the winning vote and to be allotted the better
portion, and, binding on the crown of the words, to dance into the inner parts
of the church and to sing with you to the Lord a victory-song, because a mouth
speaking unjust things has been stopped and a sea of error has been split and
we have somehow turned aside from

its surge, and the opposing thing, being flooded, is drowned, and rider and
bold horse have vanished, and every opposing-general word has perished. But
we, singing a paean spiritually, do not cover the triumph nor hide the trophy,
but with the angels we cry "let us sing to the Lord, for he has been
gloriously glorified".

2 Of the same, through personification, that terms of life have not been fixed
for men, Theophrastus against Theognostus. Since "an opened tomb is the
throat" of Theognostus and he has armed his tongue "as a sharpened razor" and
"wrath according to the likeness of the serpent" has inflamed his words, and
he swells with insults and boasts in wickednesses and, reviling, holds his
neck high, and says "our lips are our own, who is lord over us?" and with the
prophet he has not set at naught "a boastful tongue", but thinks too highly
"beyond what one ought to think", not becoming obedient to the inspired Paul,
"I will visit with a rod" his words "and with scourges" his sayings. For in
truth his heart has been lifted up above "the cedars of Lebanon" and he has
forgotten "David and all his meekness" and has raised the eyes of the
intellect, having gone "in great things" and not being humble-minded according
to what is written, but lifting up the soul, having stripped off the dignity
of evangelical gentleness; for we know that the divine law commands that one
treat the fool according to the stubbornness of his folly, lest, priding
themselves on the breadth of forbearance, they depart toward a greater pit of
sins by the unreproved state of the admonitions. Therefore "behold I am
against you, the insolent woman" [and education and intellect], that we may
begin from scripture and beautify the forecourts of our words with some
theological tablet. If terms of life underlie men, O mouth fond of elegance
and fond of blame, "I will ask you, and you answer me", as somewhere he who
discloses the secret things to the much-suffering one says, how do sufferings
befall populous cities when the air above the head has been corrupted and some
pestilential rottenness imparts itself to those who have consorted with it? Is
there then, is there for them a yearly term of life, according to which at the
same hour the countless races of men are netted toward death? And how have
many-thousand-burdened merchant ships often, so to speak, spread their canvas
and having struck the greatest seas perished by a wild assault of winds,
sinking with all hands, so that young men with virgins and old men "with
younger ones" were drowned in the water by some one symphony? Is there then,
was there some one immovable term for those sailing? But also when the
foundations of the earth leap aside it is possible somewhere to see, as "in
the twinkling of an eye" according to the divine apostle, infinite multitudes
perishing and every age allotted one tomb by one misfortune, the epidemic of
destruction. And why indeed shall I philosophize to you the similar things
concerning chasms of the earth? And the failures concerning the arrays of
close-combat, where will you put them in your mind, O noble and most
penetrating one, where indeed we see that often not even "one who pisses
against a wall" is saved, that I may also bring forth to you some little
saying of the sacred history? And the "men of bloods and of deceit shall not
halve their days", how is it to be understood by you, answer? "the terms being
shortened because of appetite for evils and inattention to the better".
Therefore deaths are not then brought on when the terms of life are fulfilled,
O best and most philosophical of monks; for a cutting of a term having slipped
in has altogether destroyed it somewhere. Or does not Paul also shout some
such thing

piercingly, declaring to the Corinthians "for this reason many among you are
weak and sick and a sufficient number sleep", as if indeed sin were cutting
off her own lovers untimely and sending them toward disappearance? For in
truth, according to that sacred voice, "every tree not producing good fruit is
cut out and thrown into fire and burned". I know also a people perishing
because of a census; for when David had given over the Jewish people to number
and entrusted those being governed to a census and "a peculiar people" was
being measured, he grieved the God over all, and this became a trespass for
David, and because of this the threefold suffering was commanded to the king
and he was compelled to proceed toward a choice of disaster. And since the
misfortune was inescapable and the evil had been taken in the portion of a
choice, death was voted to rule the people and the things of the disaster were
entrusted with the oversight of the populations. For "David chose death for
himself", he says, having thrust aside famine and war, saying "I will fall
into the hands of the Lord, because his mercies are very many; I will not be
trapped into the hands" of enemies. And did not the incense of Aaron and the
serpent being lifted up cut through the power of death? And did not the hands
of Moses being held aloft save Israel when it was engaged? And when Job was
being tried, did not the sons and the daughters by some sudden assault
overturn human life? Therefore not because the terms of life had been
fulfilled, but as a test of firmness of soul, the things of childlessness came
upon the athlete. And was not the little daughter of Jephthah slaughtered
because of the hardness of the vow? And how is Abel slain by a brother, O
inspired one, if the one murdered had been allotted terms of life? And if he
had been given over to death somewhere when the terms of life had been
fulfilled, why then is Cain condemned to groan and tremble, and was being kept
for the same suffering? And how will the one who has drawn a sword be
destroyed by a sword, if indeed terms of life have been fixed for men? And how
has fasting and prayer been able to deliver the one who supplicates from the
hand of death, if the terms of life have arrived and do not admit a
dissolution by sophism? And encounters with wild beasts and the things that
happen to men from what comes along, how will they be able to keep terms? Have
you not heard David hymning, crying to the God over all and saying "you have
delivered my soul from death"? Was there then, was there some one term of life
for the men who were inhabitants throughout Sodom and Gomorrah, or for the
Pharaonic people that perished at the Red Sea as at a signal? And if it is
possible to find from the scripture one asking for and receiving "life" and
"length of days", and "the seed of the ungodly" being utterly destroyed and
the transgressor being sent by withering like grass and "herbs of green",
where would the terms of life have their standing for you? Have you not heard
"the righteous shall flourish as a palm tree" and "he shall be multiplied as a
cedar in Lebanon", "yet a little and the sinner shall not be, and you will
seek his place and you shall not find it", "and I passed by and behold he was
not"? Or how is "if not because of the wrath of enemies, that they may not be
long-lived" to be omitted, you who chatter about terms of life and scorn the
teachings of Moses? Is not "the face of the Lord upon those who do evils, to
destroy their memorial from the earth"? Will not the Lord, "chastening",
chasten the righteous, not giving him over to death? Of whom "did the days
fail in vanity and the years with haste"? Not of those who cast away piety?
And concerning whom does the scripture say "let his days become few" if not
concerning those who do not fear the Lord, through whom the prophet has cried
"with head bare" that the wickedness of sinners be completed? For whom did he
appoint "the days as handbreadths"? And the "bring my soul out of prison" and
"do not bring me up in the midst of my days", how is it possible to receive
the scripture when it legislates, if indeed terms of life having the approved
thing immovably

have been laid down together with men by nature? And how has an addition been
granted as an honor to Hezekiah because of the tear? Or who will endure to
receive as a term that which is able to take a lessening or an increase, O
noble legislator and having the power even as far as insult? Truly these empty
wonder-tales of the unsought zeal are imitations of Hellenic mythology and
images of the insubstantial and high-flown fate. But you, leap and dance for
me and, singing a paean, display the things of Pan. For you have gathered
winds into your bosom by the sophisms. You think highly of images of empty
dreams and the sea has been split for you by the visions of sleep, and you
work wonders in a more Mosaic way, and commanders of three and chariots have
perished for you, and you beat the drum in your manners, being puffed up, and
you portray David in the dances, parading solemn words, not a holy tent, and
you are all but likened to those in Corybantic frenzy and have become similar
to the melancholy, having learned neither oversight of letter nor having
gathered up terms of contemplation. But if, judges, we have bound on the power
of the contest, we do not claim to be crowned by you with celery and wild
olive, but to be honored with "a shield of good pleasure" and to be made
bright with a vote of confirmation and to be adorned with the applause of the
establishment, so that we too may cry with the prophet today "truth has sprung
up from the earth and righteousness" "has looked down" from the heavens toward
our court.

Of the same, concerning the same question, a decision through personification,
the arbitrators Evangelus and Theopemptus. Of God who loves judgment and
righteousness and who gives a word "in the opening of the mouth" and who pours
grace on lips and who prepares a tongue as a reed of a scribe and who
beautifies it, and who charioteers the movements of the heart and steers the
mind toward the straight applications, and who gives "a form of knowledge" to
everyone somewhere who asks and knocks at the door of the contemplation above
and who leads into the inner parts of the thoughts, and who fills a cup of
wisdom and who makes those who thirst drunk as with a most excellent stream of
education, now each of us will ask to bring out a riddle and "a dark word" "as
light" and to shape it most clearly with beauty, and to utter "a good word"
traveling a royal road, both evangelical and smooth and carrying nothing
crooked toward an uneven error. We then, philosophizing concerning the
doctrine set before you for examination, neither say that terms of life belong
to every one and all and have the immovable thing from necessity, for this has
been established as Hellenic and proper to a tyrannical fate and alien to the
ecclesiastical legislation, nor do we champion the extravagant indefiniteness,
looking toward an abyss of infinity as discordant altogether from nature (for
the infinite is to be attributed to God alone because of the superiority of
incomprehensibility). Therefore both length of life and the shortening from
death follow us by choice. For as virtue and vice have been established as in
our power, and the kingdom of the heavens and eternal darkness and an
unsleeping "worm" and unquenchable "fire" and as many things as the unbribed
and evil-hating judgment of God has prepared beforehand toward punishment or
luxury, so also increase of life and a bringing of death have been fixed to
the human tribe by virtue or vice. Or was not also the God-formed living
being, "the first man become from earth, earthy", a mid-space of death and
immortality? For it was possible for him, if he had in no way set aside the
command, not to taste of death, or again, having been led astray through
hesitation of virtue and inattention to the better, to turn back into a
dissolution of corruption and, having sinned, to make the return toward the
bosom of the earth. Terms of life, then, underlie inasmuch as, having been
born from earth, we shall all go away into earth, but increase and cutting
accrue to the life of men either through virtue or through vice of soul. Of
whom did "the limbs" fall "in the wilderness"? Not of those seeking
meat-eating? Was not the poison dimmed by the prayer

of a faster, when the wild gourd had been taken along and the diners were
about to be in danger? Did not "Phinehas stand and make propitiation and the
slaughter ceased"? And were not Abraham and Moses and David added "in a good
old age with their fathers"? Shall not "each die in his own sin" and is "the
death of sinners evil"? Does not the scripture say "the ungodly shall be
utterly destroyed quickly", and again "he will add the years of the king day
upon day"? Did not the divine oracle teach "if you turn and listen to the Lord
your God and do all the commandments, the Lord your God will multiply you in
the offspring of your belly and in the produce of your land and in the
offspring of your cattle"? Does not the scripture say "behold I have given
before your face today the life and the death"? Did not the command of Moses
legislate "if you listen to the commandments of the Lord God, you shall live
and you shall be many and the Lord your God will bless you"? Did not the
sacred writing somewhere utter "if your heart turns aside and you will not
listen, you shall perish with destruction and you shall not become many-dayed
upon the earth"? Does not the Lord say to Moses "you shall see the land
opposite and you shall not enter", "because at the water of contradiction you
did not bless me"? And why then should we go through similar things concerning
the <same> more extensively and thicken the word with a cargo of many sayings?
We know that often both life and death are brought on through the prayer of
the righteous. Did not he who commanded the heavens and bridled rain with the
tongue and who, being carried, drove "a chariot of fire" give back the dead
child living? And did not the Shunammite receive her dead son living, the
death having somehow been destroyed because of the return of life toward him?
For in truth "women received their dead from resurrection". Did not Elisha
condemn to death children who were mocking because of the address? And did not
Ananias with the woman expire "at the feet of the apostles", Peter having
condemned the baseness of the words? And we know also children being destroyed
because of the impiety of those who bore them; for otherwise the destroying
angel would not have destroyed the firstborn in Egypt. And a certain paternal
holiness knows how to stand before children who have sinned; for because of
David in the days of Solomon the kingdom is not torn apart nor are scepters
split nor is it clothed with the penalties of idolatries. We know a
catastrophe being postponed through the turning of a king and a city and a
people; let the city of Nineveh recount how much repentance is able, although
God proclaimed through the prophet "yet three days and Nineveh shall be
overthrown". "They believed then in God and proclaimed a fast", as the
scripture says somewhere, "and they put on sackcloths and the king of Nineveh
rose from the throne and took off his robe and put on sackcloth and sat upon
ashes". "And God repented", it says, "concerning the evils which he spoke to
do to them and he did not do them". And toward the wisest among kings did not
the divine voice give an oracle "if you walk in my way and keep my
commandments and my ordinances, as David your father walked, I will multiply
your days"? And does not "I will kill and I will make to live" present the
recompense of the things we have lived? Did he not threaten, "if we do not"
turn, to stretch "the bow" and to polish "the sword", he who does not wish
"the death" of sinners but the life and the regret over the sins? Even if it
has been established as dear to the human race to despise "the wealth of his
kindness and of the forbearance and of the long-suffering". Nor is "the fear
of the Lord adds days, but the years of the ungodly shall be lessened" to be
overlooked. Of another cause is the thing concerning the children slain in
Bethlehem and the chorus-standing of those who have been martyred, concerning
which it is not of the present time to go through in the word. Nor is it
somehow outside the most gnostic greatness of mind of the one who created,
either when or how or whither

the end of the life of each of us is bounded; but the foreknowledge of the God
over all that runs through all things has not been established as a cause of
our sinning or not sinning, concerning which not a few things many in many
places of those before us have most excellently labored. And these things we
both philosophize and will philosophize again; but you, do not become
"children of wrath" for the rest, but take a spirit of meekness, enter into
your consciences, cease "from wrath" and leave the anger orphaned with David,
put to death the thought of boastfulness, cut off the members of lifting-up,
quiet the inflammation of nature, humble the thought and height of pride, make
humility your own, appropriate nothing haughty or centaur-like, "in honor"
prefer one another according to the divine apostle; the one who has hunted to
take the first places is ranked last evangelically. Let the wealth of
knowledge puff no one up, for we know "in part" and "we see through a mirror"
and through a riddle according to the divine apostle, and we will endure, as
was fitting, the thing of the word from without that has legislated for men
"we know nothing clear, but we wander". Let us flee the splinters of doctrines
and the weavings of questions and the devices of inquiries in a more simple
and more pious way, having grown dizzy at the abysses of the things not
entrusted to us, lest "he who catches the wise in their craftiness" strew "a
stone of stumbling" for the rashness of the tongue and, having stumbled
against it, we be winnowed, and then hear from Paul "where is a wise man,
where a scribe, where a disputer of this age?" and the things that follow.
Having then exchanged the doctrines together with the manners and the words,
let each of you go away having succeeded in the purpose.
