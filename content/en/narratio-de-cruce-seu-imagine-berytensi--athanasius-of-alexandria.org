OF OUR FATHER AMONG THE SAINTS ATHANASIUS, Discourse on the miracle that came
to be in Beirut, concerning the precious and venerable image of our Lord and
God. Lift up the eyes of your understanding, and see the new and paradoxical
miracle, which has come to be now in our days. Look upon the boundless
goodness of God, give him glory, consider the unspeakable love of mankind and
the greatness of his economy, and take up lamentation with gladness. Upon God
indeed nothing is strange. For in this paradoxical miracle, which came to be
in those days, all the hearts of men were astonished. Truly heaven was
astonished at the thing dared, all the hearts of men were troubled. Truly all
abysses were astonished, the sun was darkened, and the moon and the stars were
troubled, under that which was economized by the Lord. All the powers of the
heavens, hear, and be astonished, generations of generations, at what
paradoxical miracle has come to be in these days. There is a city called
Beirut, belonging under Antioch. In this city there were many multitudes of
the Jews. And near their synagogue a certain Christian, having taken a cell,
was remaining. And he made a full-standing image of our Lord Jesus Christ,
painted in much beauty. And time having passed, the Christian wished to depart
from the same cell. He sought therefore another greater cell, as having need
of it, and at the same time also that he was neighboring the Jews. And having
found another dwelling, he removed from there, taking all his belongings. And
by the economy of God, who wishes every man to be saved, and to come into
knowledge of truth, showing his wonders to all who believe in him in truth.
And the Christian having taken all his substance, as has been said before, the
image of our Lord Jesus Christ was left alone, he having forgotten it in the
cell. And a certain Jew, having taken his cell to dwell in it, where the image
of Christ the all-king was, and having brought in all his belongings into the
cell, was remaining, not having seen the image of the Lord, that it stands
there, nor did he perceive that place. And on one of the days he called a
Hebrew, =28.800= that he might eat with him; and while they were breakfasting,
the Jew who had been called, having lifted up his eyes, saw the image of our
Lord Jesus Christ, and says to the Jew who had called him: You, being a Jew,
how do you have the image of this one? and having spoken very many and
unlawful insults from his polluted mouth against the precious image of the
Master, which it is not permitted even to come into the mind of men; may it
not be! and the one called pointing out the image to the Hebrew who had
called, the Hebrew began to swear according to the Jewish faith, that Until
this hour I have not beheld this image. And the Hebrew who had been called was
silent. And having risen he went away to the Jews, their first priests and
teachers of the law, and says to them, that So-and-so the Hebrew has an image
of the Nazarene in his house, and I with my own eyes have beheld it. And they
having heard said: How are you able to prove this? He says to them: Follow me,
and I will point it out to you. And they took counsel, that all coming in the
morning should overtake the cell, in which the image of the Lord was. And
morning having come, the chief priests and their elders were gathered, and
taking the Jew, they go away into the house of the Hebrew, where was the


image of the Lord. And having arrived in the house, the chief priests and the
elders and all the crowd having leapt in from within the house, they see the
image of the Lord. Then having been filled with wrath, the Jew dwelling there
having been driven out they made him outcast from the synagogue; and having
taken down the image of our Lord Jesus Christ, they said among themselves,
that Just as our fathers mocked him, so let us also mock him. Then they began
to spit into the face of the image of the Lord, and they slapped it on the
face one by one, all of them were slapping the image of the Lord saying: As
many things as our fathers did, let us do all to his image. They say, that We
have heard, they did many mockeries to him; and let us do all. They therefore
mocked the image of the Lord with boundless mockeries, which it has not even
been dared for us to speak. Then they say: We have heard, that they nailed his
hands, this let us also do. Then having made nails they nailed the hands and
the feet of the Lord. Again they say: We have heard, that they gave him
vinegar and gall to drink with a sponge; let us do thus also. And they did
likewise, having put to the mouth of the Lord of the image a sponge filled
with vinegar. They say again: We have learned, that with a reed they struck
his head; the same let us also do. And having taken a reed, they were striking
upon his head. They say again: More exactly we know, that with a spear they
opened his side; let us not leave out one thing, but rather let us add. And
having done this, they urged a spear to be brought, =28.801= and the spear
having been brought, having taken it they pierced against the side of the
image of the Savior. And immediately there gushed forth blood and water in a
great multitude. And this paradoxical miracle having come to be, astonishment
took all those seeing the mighty works of God and the paradoxical wonders. How
unsearchable are the judgments of your righteousness, and boundless the
goodness and your much mercy, Master! Who will speak your mighty works, Lord?
who will make all your praises heard, lover of mankind, who does not wish the
death of the sinner, as that he should turn and live? Christ, glory to you.
Who is as you, Master? Who besides you doing fearful and extraordinary things?
O the miracle! O the majesty of the Savior! The powers above shuddered even
now at this. O how great long-suffering! o how much mercy! for formerly for us
and our salvation the fleshless having been incarnate from the Virgin Mary,
was crucified in that flesh, himself being impassible in the Godhead; but now
again in the image you were crucified, O Master, for conviction of the impious
and of all the unbelieving, and for confirmation of those who have believed in
you in truth. But glory to you, Master, the only one able to do all things,
together with our blessed God with the all-undefiled Father, and his Son Jesus
Christ, and the good and life-giving Spirit, now and always and unto the ages
of ages. Amen. Hear you, brethren, with fear of God and joy also the remaining
things which were economized by the Lord. For after the side of the holy and
precious image was struck with the spear, and the blood and the water gushed
forth, all the multitude of the Jews was gathered, saying to one another:
Since those who worship him rumor, that he does many healings, let us take of
this the blood and the water, and let us bring it into the synagogue, and let
us gather all those of the people who are in evil case, and let us anoint them
from it, and let us see if the things said are true. And having brought a
vessel to the side of the Lord, mocking they filled the vessel, where the
water was coming out; and they gathered all those in evil case, before all a
paralytic; whom they knew being from birth. And bringing the paralytic they
anointed him; and immediately he was knit together and the man became healthy.
And forthwith a great disturbance


came to be throughout all the city, all coming, because of the boundless
healings and wonder-workings. And all the multitude of the Jews was moved, and
they were bringing their sick, paralytics, maimed, withered, lame, and blind,
and demon-possessed, and others having all kinds of diseases; and all, being
anointed from the holy blood and water of the side of the precious image of
the Lord, their bodies became healthy. And all the chief priests and the
elders and the people of the Jews believed in our Lord Jesus Christ, our true
God, crying and saying: Great is the God of the Christians; great is the faith
of the Christians. Glory to you, Christ, whom our fathers crucified, who also
through us was crucified in your undefiled and precious image. Glory to you,
Jesus Christ, Son =28.804= of God, who does great wonders. In you we believe,
in Father, Son and Holy Spirit, Trinity consubstantial and inseparable. Be
propitious to us, and receive us, as you received the repentance of the thief
upon your holy cross. Number us the unworthy who have come to you with those
after the eleventh hour, and number us also and enlighten the eyes of our
heart, the darkened ones, which the evil devil darkened; that, having been
enlightened through your holy baptism, we also may be counted worthy of your
heavenly host. And these things they were crying saying, and proclaiming with
a great voice the wonders of God. And this report went out into all the
inhabited world concerning the precious image of our all-king Jesus Christ;
and all from east and west were bringing their sick; and all were being healed
by the goodness of God. And after these things all the multitudes of the Jews
came to the most holy bishop of the most holy Church, running to him and
saying: Number us to the holy catholic and apostolic Church, baptize us and
make us Christians. And they were crying with one accord, all from small unto
great of them with women and children saying: One God the Father, one Son the
only-begotten, one Lord; him we confess God, and we know and we recognize; him
we know God, in him we believe, who was crucified under Pontius Pilate, and
rose on the third day; and was taken up in glory before his holy disciples,
and sits at the right hand of God and Father. Having therefore glorified God
with many praises, and having shown the holy image to the archbishop of the
city there, and having reported what they did to the holy image of Christ, and
having shown the blood and water, and how it came out from the side of the
Savior; nevertheless for confirmation of all the bishop having taken from the
holy blood and water, and they having brought many sick, and anointing them,
he released them healthy. These boundless wonders having come to be, thus all
were entreating the bishop to obtain the holy baptism, and to partake of the
undefiled and immortal mysteries of the precious body and blood. Then the
bishop having taken them, and having called together all the clergy of the
most holy Church of God, and having catechized them for many days, baptized
them into the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, and
the synagogue of the Jews he made a church of our Savior. And they having
besought the most holy bishop, also the remaining synagogues, temples of God,
and martyria of his holy martyrs to dedicate [they dedicate], unto glory of
God and boast of the orthodox faith. And he also appointed from them
presbyters and deacons, subdeacons, and readers. And =28.805= thus great joy
came to be in heaven and on the earth at the paradoxical miracle of the
precious and holy image of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ upon this city;
not only passions of bodies being healed, but also


of souls having turned unto eternal life. These things I have been eager to
make known to your love of God, O most beloved brethren loved, for benefit and
hearing of those who meet with them, that your souls be confirmed from the
evil one, who rejoices at our fall, and let us be made firm upon the firm
faith of the confession of Christ. Let us therefore give glory with gladness
in compunction of heart, and thanksgivings, giving thanks to him from a pure
faith, to keep his commandments and his ordinances and of his knowledge;
because to him belongs the glory and the power in Christ Jesus our Lord, with
whom to the Father, together with the Holy Spirit, glory and the worship, now
and always, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.

OF OUR FATHER AMONG THE SAINTS ATHANASIUS
Concerning the image of our Lord Jesus Christ, of the miracle that came to be
in the city of Beirut.
Lift up the eyes of your understanding, and see this new spectacle, which has
come to be now. Look upon the boundless
miracle of God, and give glory to him to God, consider unto his unspeakable
love of mankind, the greatness of his economy, and take up lamentation with
gladness. Upon God indeed nothing is strange. For our God, even upon us, every
heart of those who hear will be astonished. Truly heaven was astonished at
this thing dared, this spectacle; abyss was troubled again, and sun was
darkened, and the stars, and the moon likewise at that which had come to be.
But again all the powers of the heavens were gladdened at the economy that
came to be by the Lord. Hear therefore, brethren, and be astonished, at what
has come to be in our days, understanding understand, and incline the ear of
your heart, and hear. There is a city called Beirut in the borders of Tyre and
Sidon, belonging to Antioch. In this city Beirut then there are many
multitudes of the Jews. And near their synagogue, being exceedingly great, a
Christian took a cell in a house from someone; in which dwelling opposite
their dining-couch, he made an image of our Lord Jesus Christ, solemnly
painted, having a full-standing image of our Lord =28.808= Jesus Christ. And a
little time having passed, the Christian sought a greater house as having
need. And this the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ economized, as I at least am
persuaded, of him who wishes all men to be saved, and to come into knowledge
of truth, showing his wonders to those who believe in him for conviction of
the impious, and confirmation of the faithful. The Christian was seeking
therefore, as I said, a greater house, and having found something from the
city, he removed there, and with joy all his things, and leaving behind by
economy of God, he left the image of the Lord, as I said. And a certain Jew
took that house on rent, where the image of the Lord was standing. And having
brought in all his things he was remaining in the house, not having seen the
image of the Lord, that it was standing there. For he did not even perceive
that place, but having entered he was remaining. And on one of the days the
same Jew called a man of his own nation to breakfast. And while they were
breakfasting, the Jew who had been called having lifted up his eyes unto the
image of the crucified, there very many and unlawful insults, from the hour at
which he saw the image of God, he spoke against it, which


I do not dare, may it not be! to write, such as the lawless Jew spoke against
our Savior, having seen the image of the Lord. And he himself assured the Jew
who had been called saying, that Until the present I have not seen this image.
And the one called was silent. And having gone away to the chief priests, he
slanders the Jew the one from the house saying, that So-and-so has an image of
the Nazarene in his house. And they having heard were filled with great wrath,
and that evening they were quiet. And morning already having come, the chief
priests take him who was slandering the Jew and a great crowd of their nation,
and they go away into the house of the Jew, where the image of the Lord was
standing. Having come upon the place, the chief priests and the elders leapt
in with the one who had informed, and they see the image of the Lord standing,
that one having pointed it out. Then having been exceedingly angered, the Jew
dwelling in the house having made him outcast from the synagogue they drove
out; and having brought down the image of our Lord Jesus Christ they said,
that Just as also our fathers mocked him then such a one. Then they began to
spit into his face of the holy image of the Lord, and they slapped it on the
face from here and from there. And slapping this, they were saying: As many
things as our fathers did, let us also do all to his image. And they say: We
have heard, that they mocked him, and let us do this. They therefore mocked
the image of God with boundless mockeries. And I am at a loss to say to you
what was then dared. Then they say: We have heard, that they nailed his hands
and his feet; this let us also do. Then they also fixed nails into the feet
and the hands of the image of the Lord. Again they say having gone mad
=28.809=: We have heard, that they gave him vinegar and gall to drink with a
sponge; let us do this also; and they did it having put to the mouth of the
Lord a sponge filled with vinegar. Again they say: We have learned, that with
a reed our fathers struck his head; the same let us also do. And having taken
a reed they were striking the head of the image. Finally then they say, as
Exactly we learn, that they opened his side with a spear; let us leave out
nothing, but let us add this also. Having made a spear to be brought, they
turned to one of them to take up the spear; and having taken it up against the
image of the Lord, immediately then a multitude of blood and water gushed
forth from it. Christ, glory to you; incomprehensible one, glory to you. Who
is as you, Master? Who besides you, God, doing fearful and extraordinary
things? O the miracle of the majesty of our Savior! truly the holy powers
above shuddered even at this. How great you are, Master, unto love of mankind!
How great you in mercy! For formerly for us and for our salvation the
fleshless having been incarnate from the Virgin Mary, you were crucified in
that flesh, the same impassible in the Godhead. But now again in your image
you were crucified, O Master, for conviction of the impious and of all the
unbelieving, and confirmation of those who believe in you in truth. But glory
to you, Master, the only one able to do all things, together with our blessed
God the Father, and the Holy Spirit. Amen. And you, children, hear the
remaining things, which his goodness economized. For after the side of the
image of the Lord was struck with the spear, and the blood and the water
gushed forth, as has been said otherwise, the chief priests and the elders of
the people say: Since those who worship him rumor that he also did many
healings, let us take his blood and the water, and let us bring it into the
synagogue, and let us gather all those of the people who are in evil case, and
let us anoint them from it, and let us see if the things said are true. Then
they brought a vessel to the side of the Lord at the place of the slaughter of
the spear, whence the blood and the water was coming out, and they filled the
vessel. And having led it away, mocking, that, as they thought, they might
insult


before all, they gathered all those in evil case, first of all a paralytic,
whom they knew him from birth, and having brought him they anointed him. And
immediately he leapt up, and the man jumped having been made wholly healthy.
Then they brought blind, and those likewise having been anointed saw again,
demon-possessed innumerable were immediately cleansed. A great disturbance
came to be throughout all the city, all running upon it because of the
boundless miracles. All the multitudes of the Jews were moved, of those
dwelling in the city, so that neither their synagogue, although being very
great, could hold them, nor the place, the multitude of the boundless people
having run together because of the boundless miracles. And all the chief
priests, and the elders, and the people, and the multitudes of the Jews, both
of men and of women and of children, believed in our Lord Jesus Christ, crying
and saying: Glory to you, Christ, whom our fathers cruci =28.812= fied, and
from us now crucified upon your image. Glory to you, Son of the living God,
who has done so many wonders. In you we believe; be propitious to us, and
receive us. These things all were crying mourning; and their cries were being
sent up, chief priests anointing, and all being healed and being made alive.
And after all had been healed, thereafter to the bishop of the Church there,
already having learned the things that had come to be, the multitudes of the
Jews ran down, crying: One God the Father, one Son of his only-begotten, one
Christ whom our fathers crucified; him we know God, in this one we believe.
Having therefore glorified God with many praises, and having shown the image
to the archbishop, and having reported what they did to the image of Christ,
they were making known the blood and the water, and how it had come out from
the side of the image, and of the boundless wonders the things that had come
to be. Thus all the multitudes were entreating to be counted worthy of the
holy baptism. Whom the bishop having taken together with the clergy there
baptized all over many days. And the synagogue he dedicated as a church of our
Savior Jesus Christ, those having besought him; and the remaining synagogues
of theirs he made martyria. And from this great joy came to be in that city,
not only of bodies having been healed, but also of so many souls having
returned from the dead unto life. These things having learned I have been
eager to make known to you, most beloved brethren, for the benefit of your
souls; that, having known also in this the power of our God and our Savior
Jesus Christ, you may be made more firm in the faith in him, and may rejoice
at his great wonders that have now come to be. Give therefore to him glory
with gladness, and compunction of heart, and with lamentation rejoicing, and
giving thanks at his majesty, because he counted us worthy of his faith, and
of his knowledge. Yes, my beloved children, let us know also in the present
the power of our boundless God and Lord Jesus Christ. And let us glorify his
goodness, of the true Son of the living God. For to him belongs honor, power,
greatness and magnificence, now and always and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
