Or because after God he named man, is the Son third after the man, and the
Holy Spirit fourth? And what then, if they also hear the Apostle saying again
in the same letter: "I charge you before God who gives life to all things, and
the Lord Jesus Christ, who witnessed the good confession before Pontius
Pilate, that you keep the commandment without spot, without reproach"? So
then, because he has now been silent about angels and the Spirit, do they
doubt about the Spirit, whether he is, and about angels, whether they are?
Yes, they doubt, so long as they have practiced such blasphemy against the
Spirit. If they hear Scripture saying in Exodus, "And the people feared
=26.565= the Lord, and they believed God, and Moses his servant," will they
number Moses with God, and after God will they not understand the Son, but
only Moses? And what if they also hear the patriarch Jacob blessing Joseph and
saying: "The God who feeds me from my youth until this day; the angel who
delivers me from all evils, bless these children"? So then, because after God
he named an angel, is the angel first before the Son, or is the Son numbered
with angels? Yes, they will understand it so again, having a corrupted heart.
But the apostolic faith is not such, nor would any Christian at all endure
these things. For the holy and blessed Trinity is indivisible and united to
itself; and when the Father is named, his Word is also present, and the Spirit
in the Son. And if the Son also is named, the Father is in the Son, and the
Spirit is not outside the Word. For there is one grace from the Father
fulfilled through the Son in the Holy Spirit; and there is one Godhead, and
one God who is over all, and through all, and in all. For so also Paul, having
said, "I testify before God and Jesus Christ," knew that the Spirit was not
divided from the Son, but was himself also in Christ, just as the Son is in
the Father; and he reasonably brought in the elect angels, so that, since a
charge was being spoken to the disciple, knowing that what is spoken by God
through Christ has been uttered in the Spirit, while the angels minister what
is from us, watching over each person's deeds, he may keep the teacher's
exhortations, as having those who oversee as witnesses of what is said. Or
perhaps he now also testifies angels because of those who always behold the
face of the Father who is in the heavens, for the sake of the little ones in
the Church, so that the disciple, knowing the guardians of the peoples, may
not neglect the Apostle's exhortations. Such, then, the sense of the divine
oracles appears to me, refuting the irrational blasphemy against the Spirit.
But they, having a persistent contentiousness toward the truth, as you write,
no longer from the Scriptures (for they do not find anything), but belching
from the abundance of their own heart, say again: If he is not a creature, nor
one of the angels, but proceeds from the Father; =26.568= then he too is a
Son, and there are two brothers, he and the Word. And if he is a brother, how
is the Word Only-begotten, or how are they not equal, but the one is named
after the Father, and the other after the Son? And how, if he is from the
Father, is he not also said to have been begotten, or that he is Son, but
simply Holy Spirit? And if the Spirit is of the Son, then the Father is
grandfather of the Spirit. Such things the dishonorable play at, being curious
and wanting to search the depths of God, which no one knows except the Spirit
of God whom they blaspheme. It would have been necessary no longer to answer
these, but according to the apostolic charge, after the warning from what has
been said before, to refuse them as heretics, or to ask them things worthy of
what they ask, and to require from them an answer such as they themselves
require from us.

Let them say then: whether the Father is from a father, and whether another
has been begotten with him, and they are brothers from one, and what name they
have, and who is Father and grandfather of these also, and the ancestors of
those? But they will say there is not. How then is he Father, let them say,
himself not coming from a Father? Or how was he able to have a Son, without
first himself having been begotten as a Son? I know that the question is
impious; but it is just to play with those who play at such things, so that at
least from such absurdity and impious questioning they may be able to perceive
their own folly. For these things are not so; God forbid! nor is it fitting to
ask in this way about the Godhead. For God is not as a man, that someone
should dare to ask human things about him. One ought, then, as I said before,
to be silent about these things, and not to take notice of such people; but
lest our silence give them a pretext for shamelessness, let them hear: Just as
it is not possible to speak of a father in the case of the Father, so it is
not possible to speak of a brother in the case of the Son. For of the Father,
as it has been written before, another God has not come to be; and there is
not another Son; for he is Only-begotten; therefore also the Father alone and
one is Father of an only and one Son; and in the Godhead alone "Father" and
"Son" stand always and are. For of men, if someone is called father, yet he
has become son of another; =26.569= and if he is called son, yet he has become
father of another; so that in the case of men the name of father and son is
not preserved in the proper sense. Abraham at any rate, being son of Terah,
became father of Isaac; and Isaac, being son of Abraham, became father of
Jacob; and in this way the nature of men is; for they are parts of one
another; and each when begotten has a part of the father, so that he too may
become father of another. But in the Godhead it is not so. For God is not as a
man, nor does he have a nature that is divided; therefore he does not beget a
Son by being divided, so that he too may become Father of another, since he
himself is not from a Father either; nor is the Son a part of the Father;
therefore he also does not beget, as he himself has been begotten, but he is
whole image and radiance of the whole; and in the Godhead alone the Father is
properly Father; and the Son is properly Son; and in their case it stands that
the Father is always Father, and the Son always Son. And just as the Father
would never be Son, so the Son would never become Father. And just as the
Father will never cease being Father alone, so the Son will never cease being
Son alone. It is madness then even to conceive and to say of the Son a
brother, and of the Father the name of grandfather. For neither was the Spirit
named son in the Scriptures, lest he be thought a brother; nor son of the Son,
lest the Father be understood as grandfather; but the Son is said to be Son of
the Father, and the Spirit Spirit of the Father; and thus of the holy Trinity
there is one Godhead and faith. Therefore on this ground too it is madness to
say that he is a creature. For if he were a creature, he would not be ranked
with the Trinity. For the whole is one God. And it is enough to know that the
Spirit is not a creature, nor is numbered with the things made; for what is
foreign is not mixed into the Trinity, but it is indivisible and like itself.
These things are enough for the faithful; knowledge among men reaches this
far; to this point the cherubim cover with their wings. But he who seeks
things beyond these and wants to search disobeys him who says: "Do not be
over-wise, lest you be struck with amazement." For what has been handed down
by faith, these things it is fitting to understand not in human wisdom but in
the hearing of faith. For what =26.572= word will be able worthily to
interpret the things that are above originated nature? Or what hearing at all
can understand things that it is not permitted for men either to hear or to
speak? Concerning then the things Paul heard, he has spoken thus; but
concerning God himself: "How unsearchable are his ways! For who has known the
mind of the Lord? Or who has been his counselor?" Abraham at any rate neither
was curious, nor examined the one speaking, but "he believed, and it was
reckoned to him for righteousness;" so Moses was called a faithful servant.
But if those who think the things of Arius, since wisdom will not enter into
their knavish soul,

cannot understand or believe concerning the indivisible and holy Trinity, let
them not on this account misinterpret the truth, nor let them say that what
they themselves cannot understand cannot even be. For they suffer a most
absurd thing: because, not being able to understand how the holy Trinity is
indivisible, the Arians make the Son one with the creation; and the Tropici
themselves also number the Spirit with the creatures. But they ought either,
not understanding at all, to be silent, and neither those to rank the Son, nor
these the Spirit, with the creatures; or to recognize what is written, and to
join the Son to the Father, and not to divide the Spirit from the Son, so that
the indivisible and truly same-natured character of the holy Trinity may
remain. Having learned these things they ought not to dare, nor in doubt to
ask how these things came to be, lest, even if the one asked is at a loss to
speak, they invent evils of mind for themselves. For it is impossible for all
originated things, and especially for us humans, to speak worthily of the
ineffable. And it is more rash again, when they cannot speak, to invent on
these matters newer words besides those of the Scriptures. Besides, such an
attempt is also mad, both of the one asking and of the one even conceiving to
answer at all. For not even about originated things would anyone asking in
this way be thought to have a sound mind. Or let those who say everything
readily dare to answer how the heaven was constituted, and of what matter, and
what is its mixture, or how the sun, and each of the stars? And what wonder is
it to convict their folly from the things above, when not even how the nature
of the woods here below, and the gatherings of the waters, and how the
fashioning =26.573= and constitution of living things, is known. But they
would not say, since even Solomon, who shared wisdom more than all, seeing it
impossible for men to find these things, said: "He has also given the whole
age in their heart, so that man may not find the work which God has made from
beginning even to end." So then, since they cannot find, do they not confess
that these things even are? Yes, they will confess, having a corrupted mind.
Therefore one might reasonably say to them: O foolish and daring in
everything, why do you not rather cease being curious about the holy Trinity,
and only believe that it is; having as teacher for this the Apostle saying:
"For one must first believe God, that he is, and that he becomes a rewarder to
those who seek him"? For he has not said how he is, but only "that he is." And
if they do not even so sink down, let them say how the Father is, so that they
may thus learn how his Word also is. But they will say it is absurd to ask
thus about the Father. Let them hear then that it is also absurd to ask thus
about his Word. Since therefore such an attempt is superfluous and even more
madness, let no one any longer ask such things, or let him learn only the
things in the Scriptures. For the examples lying in these about this are also
sufficient and enough. The Father then is called fountain and light; for "They
have forsaken me," he says, "a fountain of living water." And again in Baruch:
"Why is it, Israel, that you are in the land of enemies? you have forsaken the
fountain of wisdom;" and according to John: "Our God is light." And the Son is
called, as toward the fountain, a river; for "The river of God was filled with
waters;" and toward the light, radiance, Paul saying: "Who being the radiance
of the glory, and the impress of his hypostasis." The Father then being light,
and the Son his radiance (for especially about these things one must not
shrink from saying the same things often), it is possible to see also in the
Son the Spirit, in whom we are enlightened; for "that," he says, "he may give
you a Spirit of wisdom and revelation in the knowledge of him, the eyes of the
heart being enlightened." And we being enlightened by the Spirit, it is Christ
who enlightens in him; for "He was," he says, "the true light, which
enlightens every man coming into the world." And again, the Father being

a fountain, and the Son being called a river, we are said to drink the Spirit;
for it is written, =26.576= that "We all were made to drink one Spirit." And
being given the Spirit to drink, we drink Christ; for "they drank from a
spiritual rock that followed; and the rock was Christ." And again, Christ
being true Son, we, receiving the Spirit, are made sons; "For you did not
receive," he says, "a Spirit of slavery again unto fear; but you received a
Spirit of sonship." And being made sons by the Spirit, it is clear that in
Christ we are called children of God; for "As many as received him, he gave
them authority to become children of God." Then the Father, as Paul said,
being alone wise, the Son is his wisdom. For "Christ is the power of God and
the wisdom of God." And the Son being the wisdom, we, receiving a Spirit of
wisdom, have the Son, and in him we become wise. For so it has also been
written in the hundred and forty-fifth psalm: "The Lord looses those who have
been bound; the Lord makes the blind wise." And when the Spirit is given into
us ("Receive Holy Spirit," the Savior said), God is in us; for so John wrote:
"If we love one another, God remains in us. In this we know that we remain in
him, and he in us, because he has given us of his Spirit." And God being in
us, the Son also is in us, the Son himself saying: "I and the Father will
come, and we will make a dwelling with him." Then, life being of the Son, for
"I," he says, "am the life," we are said to be made alive in the Spirit; for
"He who raised," he says, "Christ Jesus from the dead will also make alive our
mortal bodies through his Spirit that dwells in you." And we being made alive
in the Spirit, Christ himself is said to live in us. For "I have been
crucified with Christ. I live no longer, but Christ lives in me." And again,
just as the Son said that the works which he did, the Father was working; for
"The Father," he says, "who remains in me, he does the works. Believe me, that
I am in the Father, and the Father in me. And if not, believe me because of
the works themselves." So the things which Paul worked in the power of the
Spirit, he said were the works of Christ; "For I will not dare to speak of
anything which Christ did not work through me unto the obedience of the
Gentiles, in word and work, in power of signs and wonders, in power of the
Holy Spirit." And such being the correspondence and unity that is in the holy
Trinity, who would divide either the Son =26.577= from the Father, or the
Spirit from the Son, or from the Father himself? or who is so rash as to say
that the Trinity is unlike and of another nature toward itself, or that the
Son is of another essence from the Father, or that the Spirit is foreign to
the Son? And how are these things? someone might ask again seeking: how, the
Spirit being in us, is the Son said to be in us, and the Son being in us, is
the Father said to be in us? or how, there being a Trinity at all, is the
Trinity signified in one? or how, the one being in us, is the Trinity said to
be in us? let him first himself divide the radiance from the light, or the
wisdom from the wise; or let him say how these things are. And if he cannot do
this, much more is it the daring of madmen to seek such things about God; for
the Godhead is not handed down in demonstration of words, as has been said,
but in faith and pious reasoning

with reverence. For even the things concerning the saving cross Paul used to
preach "not in wisdom of words, but in demonstration of spirit and power," and
"he heard unspeakable words" in paradise, "which it is not lawful for a man to
speak;" concerning the holy Trinity itself who is able to declare it?
Nevertheless someone will be able to treat such a difficulty first by faith,
and then also from what has been said before, I mean the image and the
radiance, and fountain, and river, and hypostasis, and impress. For just as
the Son is in the Spirit as in his own image, so also the Father is in the
Son. And indeed the impossibility of interpretation through words, and of

grasping such things, divine Scripture, consoling, has also given us such
examples, so that thus, because of the unbelief of those who dare to speak, it
may be possible to speak more simply, and to speak without danger, and with
pardon to understand, and to believe that the sanctification is one, that
which comes from the Father through the Son in the Holy Spirit. For just as
the Son is Only-begotten, so also the Spirit given and sent from the Son
=26.580= is himself also one and not many, nor one from many, but he himself
alone Spirit. For the Son being one, the living Word, his sanctifying and
illuminating living energy and gift must be one, perfect and full, which is
said to proceed from the Father, since it shines forth from the Word who is
confessed to be from the Father, and is sent, and is given. At any rate the
Son is sent from the Father; for "God so loved the world," he says, "that he
sent his only-begotten Son." And the Son sends the Spirit; for "If I go away,"
he says, "I will send the Paraclete." And the Son glorifies the Father,
saying: "Father, I have glorified you;" and the Spirit glorifies the Son; for
"He," he says, "will glorify me." And the Son says: "What I have heard from
the Father, these things I also speak into the world;" and the Spirit receives
from the Son; for "He will take of mine and will announce to you," he says.
And the Son came in the name of the Father; "And the Holy Spirit," the Son
says, "whom the Father will send in my name." And the Spirit having such an
order and nature toward the Son as the Son has toward the Father, how will the
one who says this is a creature not of necessity think the same also about the
Son? For if the Spirit of the Son is a creature, it would follow that they say
the Word of the Father also is a creature. For having imagined such things the
Arians have fallen into the Judaism according to Caiaphas. But if those who
say such things about the Spirit pretend not to think the things of Arius, let
them flee also his words, and not be impious toward the Spirit. For just as
the Son, who is in the Father, in whom also the Father is, is not a creature,
but proper to the Father's essence (for this you too pretend to say); so also
the Spirit who is in the Son, in whom also the Son is, it is not lawful to
rank with the creatures, nor to divide him from the Word, and to make the
Trinity incomplete. For the sayings then of both the prophetic and the
apostolic grace, whose sense they have falsified and deceived themselves, it
is enough through these to refute the blasphemy of the Tropici that comes from
ignorance. =26.581= And let us see further also the things themselves said in
the divine Scriptures about the Holy Spirit, and as approved bankers let us
discern whether the Spirit has something proper toward the creatures, or is
proper to God, so that we may either call him a creature, or something other
than the creatures, and proper and one of the Godhead in the Trinity. Perhaps
even so they will be ashamed, having learned how far the words of blasphemy
invented by them jar with the divine oracles. The creatures, then, have come
to be from things that were not, having a beginning of coming to be; for in
the beginning God made the heaven and the earth and all things in them. But
the Holy Spirit is said to be from God; for "No one," he says, "knows the
things of the man except the spirit of the man that is in him; so also no one
knows the things of God except the Spirit of God. And we have not received the
spirit of the world, but the Spirit that is from God." What kinship, then,
from what has been said, does the Spirit have toward the creatures? For the
creatures were not; but God is he who is, from whom also the Spirit is. And
what is from God would not be from what is not, nor a creature, lest according
to them even he from whom the Spirit is should be thought to be a creature.
Who then will endure such fools, who themselves also say in their heart that
there is no God? For if, just as no one knows the things of the man except the
Spirit that is in him, so also the things of God except the Spirit that is in
him; how would it not be blasphemous to say that the

Spirit in God is a creature, who also searches the depths of God? For from
these things such a person will learn to say that the spirit of the man is
outside the man himself, and that the Word in the Father is a creature. And
again the Spirit is and is called Spirit of holiness and of renewal; for Paul
writes: "Who was marked out Son of God in power according to the Spirit of
holiness from resurrection of the dead, Jesus Christ our Lord." And again he
says: "But you were sanctified, but you were justified in the name of our Lord
Jesus Christ and in the Spirit of our God;" when also writing to Titus
=26.584= he said: "But when the kindness and love of mankind of God our Savior
appeared, not from works in righteousness which we did, but according to his
mercy he saved us through the washing of regeneration and renewal of the Holy
Spirit, which he poured out upon us richly through Jesus Christ our Savior, so
that, having been justified by his grace, we might become heirs according to
hope of eternal life." But the creatures are sanctified and renewed. For "You
will send forth your Spirit, and they will be created, and you will renew the
face of the earth." And Paul also says: "For it is impossible for those who
have once been enlightened, and have tasted the heavenly gift, and have become
partakers of the Holy Spirit." That, then, which is not sanctified by another,
nor partakes of sanctification, but is itself participated, in which also all
the creatures are sanctified, how would it be one of all, proper to those who
partake of it? For those who say this must of necessity say also that the Son,
through whom all things have come to be, is one of all. He is called
life-giving Spirit; for "He who raised," he says, "Jesus Christ from the dead
will also make alive your mortal bodies through his Spirit that dwells in
you." And the Lord is life itself, and "pioneer of life," as Peter said; and
the Lord himself said: "The water that I will give him will become in him a
fountain of water leaping up unto eternal life. And this he said about the
Spirit, which those who believed in him were about to receive." But the
creatures, as has been said, are made alive

through him. And that which does not partake of life, but is itself partaken
and makes the creatures alive, what kinship does it have toward the originated
things? Or how would it be at all of the creatures, which are made alive in
him from the Word? The Spirit is called anointing, and is a seal. For John
writes: "And you, the anointing which you received from him, remains in you.
And you have no need that anyone teach you; but as his anointing," his Spirit,
"teaches you about all things." And in the prophet Isaiah it is written: "The
Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because of which he anointed me." And Paul
says: "In whom also having believed, you were sealed unto the day of
redemption." But the creatures are sealed and anointed by this, and =26.585=
are taught about all things. And if the Spirit is anointing and seal, in which
the Word anoints and seals all things; what likeness or property is there of
the anointing and the seal toward the things anointed and sealed? Therefore on
this ground too he would not be of all. For the seal would not be from the
things sealed, nor the anointing from the things anointed; but this is proper
to the Word who anoints and seals. For the anointing has the fragrance and
breath of the one who anoints, and those who are anointed, partaking of this,
say: "We are the fragrance of Christ;" and the seal has the form of Christ who
seals, and those who are sealed partake of this, being formed according to it,
the Apostle saying: "My little children, of whom I am again in travail, until
Christ is formed in you." And being sealed thus, we reasonably also become
partakers of the divine nature, as Peter said, and thus all creation partakes
of the Word in the Spirit. And through the Spirit we are all called partakers
of God; "For do you not know," he says, "that you are a temple of God, and the
Spirit of God dwells in you? If anyone destroys the temple of God, God will
destroy this one.

For the temple of God is holy, which you are." And if the Holy Spirit were a
creature, no participation of God would come to us in him; but then we would
be joined to a creature, and would become foreign to the divine nature, as
partaking of it in no way. But now, when we are called partakers of Christ and
partakers of God, it is shown that the anointing in us and the seal is not of
the nature of originated things, but of the Son's, who through the Spirit in
him joins us to the Father. For this John, as has been said in what went
before, wrote teaching: "In this we know that we remain in God, and he in us,
because he himself has given us of his Spirit." And if by participation of the
Spirit we become partakers of the divine nature, one would be mad who said the
Spirit is of created nature, and not =26.588= of God's. For this is why also
those in whom he comes to be are deified; and if he deifies, it is not
doubtful that his nature is God's. And still more clearly, for the overthrow
of this heresy, it is sung in the hundred and third psalm, as we said before:
"You will take away their spirit, and they will fail, and they will return to
their dust. You will send forth your Spirit, and they will be created, and you
will renew the face of the earth." And Paul writes to Titus: "Through the
washing of regeneration and renewal of the Holy Spirit, which he poured out
upon us richly through Jesus Christ." And if the Father through the Word in
the Holy Spirit creates all things, and renews; what likeness or kinship does
the creator have toward the creatures? Or how at all would he, in whom all
things are created, be a creature? For such blasphemy is followed also by
blasphemy against the Son; so that those who say the Spirit is a creature say
that the Word also, through whom all things are created, is a creature. The
Spirit is called and is image of the Son; for "Those whom he foreknew he also
predestined to be conformed to the image of his Son." Therefore, the Son also
being

according to them confessed not to be of a creature, his image would not be a
creature either. For of whatever sort the image would be, such of necessity
also he whose image it is must be. Hence reasonably and fittingly the Word is
confessed not to be a creature, being image of the Father; he then who numbers
the Spirit with the creatures will altogether number the Son also among these,
blaspheming in this the Father too because of the blasphemy against his image.
The Spirit then is other than the creatures, and has been shown rather to be
proper to the Son, and not foreign to God. For that wise question of theirs
too: If the Spirit is from God, why is he not also called Son? has already
been shown in what went before to be rash and daring, and is shown now no
less. For even if he was not called Son in the Scriptures, but Spirit of God,
he has been said to be in God himself and from God himself, as the Apostle
wrote. And if the Son, since he is from the Father, is proper to his essence,
of necessity also =26.589= the Spirit, being said to be from God, is proper
according to essence to the Son. At any rate, the Lord being Son, the Spirit
himself has been called Spirit of sonship. And again, the Son being wisdom and
truth, it is written that the Spirit is Spirit of wisdom and of truth. And
again the Son is power of God, and Lord of glory; and the Spirit is called
Spirit of power, and Spirit of glory, Scripture saying thus on each: Paul
writing to the Corinthians: "For if they had known, they would not have
crucified the Lord of glory;" and in other places: "For you did not receive a
spirit of slavery again unto fear; but you received a Spirit of sonship;" and
again: "God sent forth the Spirit of his Son into your hearts, crying: Abba,
Father." And Peter wrote: "If you are reproached in the name of Christ, you
are blessed; because the Spirit of glory and of power and of God rests upon
you." And the Lord has said that the Spirit is Spirit of truth and Paraclete;
from which it is shown that the Trinity is perfect in this. In this, then, the
Word glorifies the creation, and deifying and making sons he brings it to the

Father. And that which joins the creation to the Word would not itself be of
the creatures; and that which makes the creation sons would not be foreign to
the Son; since it would be necessary to seek another Spirit, so that this too
might be joined in that one to the Word. But this is absurd. Therefore the
Spirit is not of originated things, but proper to the Father's Godhead, in
which also the Word deifies the originated things. And that in which the
creation is deified would not itself be outside the Father's Godhead. And that
the Spirit is above the creation, and other than the nature of originated
things, and proper to the Godhead, it is possible to see again from this also.
The Holy Spirit is unchangeable and unalterable; "For a holy Spirit of
discipline," he says, "will flee deceit, and will withdraw from senseless

in all the divine Scripture you will find that the Holy Spirit said to be of

being a proper offspring of his essence, =26.628= is not a creature,

permitted to say otherwise about him, than that he is Father, so it is impious
to ask whether the Spirit is Son, or the Spirit is Son. For this reason
Sabellius was judged foreign to the Church, having dared to say of the Father
the Son, and of the Son the name of the Father. So then will anyone still
after these things dare, hearing Son and Spirit, to say: Is the Father then
grandfather, or is the Spirit then Son? Yes, Eunomians, and Eudoxians, and
Eusebians
will dare. =26.645= For once pretending the Arian heresy, they will not cease
their tongue from impiety. For who handed these things down to them? Who is
the one who taught? But no one from the divine Scriptures; but from the
abundance of their heart such madness went out. For if, since the Spirit is
not a creature (for this was shown), you ask, Is the Spirit then Son? it is
time, having learned that the Son is not a creature (for through him
originated things were created), to ask, Is the Son then Father? Or also thus:
Is the Spirit then the Son, and is the Son himself the Holy Spirit? And
reasoning such things, they will be outside the holy Trinity, and will be
judged godless, changing the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the
Holy Spirit; and transferring it, as they wish, in the likeness of human
generation, naming grandchildren and grandfathers, and fashioning for
themselves the theogony of the Greeks. But this is not the faith of the
Church, but as the Savior said, into Father and Son and Holy Spirit; Father
indeed, not able to be called grandfather, and Son not able to be called
Father, and Holy Spirit not named otherwise than thus. Upon this faith it is
not possible to say interchangeably; but always the Father is Father, and the
Son always Son, and the Holy Spirit always is and is called Holy Spirit. For
in the case of human things, it is not so, even if the Arians imagine such
things. For as it has been written, "God is not as a man," so one would say,
men are not as God. For in the case of men, the father is not always father,
nor the son always son. For the same becomes father of a son, and he himself
has become son of another, and the son being son of a father becomes father of
another. Abraham at any rate, being son of Nahor, became father of Isaac; and
Isaac being son of Abraham became father of Jacob. For each, being a part of
the parent, is begotten a son, and himself also becomes father of another. But
in the Godhead it is not so, since God is not as a man. The Father at any rate
is not from a Father; therefore he also does not beget one who will become
Father of another; nor is the Son a part of the Father, therefore he is not an
offspring unto begetting a Son. Hence in the Godhead alone the Father, being
properly and only Father, is and was and always is. And the Son is properly,
and only Son. And in their case it stands that the Father is always Father,
and that Son is always Son, both to be and to be called; and the Holy Spirit
also is always Holy Spirit; and

we have believed this to be of God, and to be given from the Father through
the Son. =26.648= For so the holy Trinity remains unalterable, known in one
Godhead. Therefore he who asks, Is the Spirit then Son? as if the name could
be changed, imagines, and procures madness for himself. And he who asks, Is
the Father then grandfather, inventing a name for the Father, wanders in the
heart. To answer still toward the shamelessness of the heretics is not safe;
for it is to fight with the apostolic exhortation; but rather to counsel, as
he himself charged, is good. These things are enough toward refutation of your
foolish talk. Play no longer into the Godhead. For it is proper to those who
play to ask the things not written and to say: Then is the Spirit Son and the
Father grandfather? So laugh the one in Caesarea and the one in Scythopolis.
It is enough for you to believe that the Spirit is not a creature, but is
Spirit of God, and in him a Trinity, Father, and Son, and Holy Spirit. And one
must neither name the Father name upon the Son, nor is it permitted to say the
Spirit is himself the Son, nor that the Son is himself the Holy Spirit; but it
is so, as has been said, and the Godhead in this Trinity is one, and faith is
one, and baptism is one, given in it, and the perfection is one, in Christ
Jesus our Lord, through whom and with whom to the Father is the glory and the
power with the Holy Spirit unto all the ages of the ages. Amen.
