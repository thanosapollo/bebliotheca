* Homily on Mercy and Judgment

OF THE SAME, ON MERCY AND JUDGMENT.
Father, bless. The world that forgets God, brethren, is held by injustice
toward the neighbor and by inhumanity toward the weak, just as the divine
Apostle says: "Since they did not see fit to have God in knowledge," =31.1708=
"God gave them over to a reprobate mind, to do the things that are not
fitting, being filled with all injustice, wickedness, greed, malice, full of
envy, murder, strife, deceit, malignity, whisperers, slanderers, haters of
God, insolent, arrogant, boastful, inventors of evils, disobedient to
parents, without understanding, without affection, without mercy." But those
whom God leads back to piety he teaches both abstinence from evils and care
for mercy toward the neighbor, just as the prophet Isaiah taught, speaking
from the person of God: "Cease from your wickednesses; learn to do good." And
the law had many ordinances concerning not harming the neighbor, and
commandments also concerning showing kindness and having mercy. For if anyone
should leave aside one of these, the other is not sufficient for the
correction of a man. For neither is a benefaction toward the needy from
unjust gains acceptable with God, nor is the one who abstains from injustices
praiseworthy if he shares none of his own with anyone. For concerning those
who do injustice and attempt to offer gifts to God it is written: "The
sacrifice of the ungodly is an abomination to the Lord"; and concerning those
who do not show mercy: "He who stops his ears so as not to hear the poor, he
also shall cry out, and there shall be none to hear." Therefore the Proverb
advises: "Honor the Lord from your just labors, and offer him firstfruits
from the fruits of your righteousness." For if you are about to offer to God
from injustice and plunder, it is better neither to acquire such a possession
nor to offer from it. For a pure gift will carry the prayer upward, as it is
written: "The prayers of those who go straight are acceptable with him." And
again, if while acquiring from just labors you do not offer to God offerings
from which the poor are fed, it will be reckoned to you as plunder, as he
says through the prophet Malachi: "The firstfruits and the tithes are with
you, and there shall be plunder in your houses." You must therefore, having
mixed mercy and judgment, acquire with judgment, and spend for mercy,
according to what is written: "Keep mercy and judgment, and draw near to your
God continually." For God loves almsgiving and judgment. Therefore he who
takes thought for mercy and judgment draws near to God. Here then let a man
examine himself, and let the rich man look at his own resources, from which
he is about to bring gifts to God: whether he has oppressed the poor, whether
he has done violence to the weaker, =31.1709= whether he has defrauded the
one subject to him, using authority in place of justice. For we are commanded
to keep justice and equity even toward slaves. Do not, because you hold
power, do violence, nor, because you are able, be greedy; but because the
things of authority are at hand for you, show the things of justice. For you
will not furnish the proof of obedience toward God and of fear in that which
you cannot do, but in that which, being able to transgress, you do not
transgress. But if, having taken away the things of the poor, you give to the
poor, it were better that you neither seized nor gave. Why do you defile your
own wealth, introducing into it gains that are not just? Why do you make your
own offering abominable from injustice, attempting to offer, in the very act
in which you are about to have mercy on another beggar? Have mercy on that
one whom you wrong.

Upon this man spend your kindness; to this one give freely, and you will
fulfill mercy with judgment. For God will not share in greed, nor is the Lord
a partner of robbers and plunderers; nor, as if unable to feed the poor, has
he left them to us; but seeking from us the just and kindly fruit-bearing,
for our own benefit. Almsgiving does not come from injustice, nor blessing
from a curse, nor benefactions from tears. God says to those who stir the
tears of the wronged: "You did the things I hated; you covered my altar with
tears, and with weeping and groaning." Have mercy from your labors, and do
not wish to do injustice, making the excuse of bringing mercy to God from
injustice. Such things are vainglory, and they look toward praises from men,
not toward the praise that is from God. Therefore the Lord well says, not to
be seen by men. For if you do almsgiving as God looking upon it, you will
keep yourself from doing it from greed, knowing that you do not gladden God
the overseer. Let us so do almsgiving, that we may receive from God. And God
repays those whom he praises; but he praises no greedy man. It is not
possible for you to bring gifts to God if you have grieved your brother. "If
you offer," he says, "your gift at the altar, and there remember that your
brother has something against you, go first, be reconciled to your brother,
and then come and offer your gift." Remember Zacchaeus the tax collector,
who, having resolved to restore fourfold if he had defrauded anyone, thus
distributed half of the remaining money to the poor. For he knew that he
wished to receive Christ, and that Christ will not otherwise accept the
liberalities toward the poor, unless the greedy wrongs against those who were
injured be loosed. And thus the Lord accepted the uprightness of Zacchaeus,
and said: "Today salvation has come to this house." And =31.1712= these
things are toward those who do alms, yet do not take thought for equity. But
toward the one who is on guard against doing injustice, yet is careless of
showing mercy, we say that "Every tree that does not bear fruit is cut down
and thrown into the fire," and such a tree will never please the heavenly
husbandman who says that "He came seeking fruit on the fig tree, and found
none," and who commands it to be cut down, that it may not occupy the ground
in vain. And he also appears condemned before God who does not restore the
pledge to the poor, and a threat comes against such a one. "For," he says,
"he who has not received back the pledge will cry to me, and I will hear him.
For I am merciful." And it was a dreadful and unlawful thing both to glean
the harvest a second time, and to glean the vineyard again, and to strip the
olive grove, because these things ought to be left for the poor. But if these
things were commanded to those under the law, what shall we say concerning
those in Christ, to whom the Lord says: "Unless your righteousness abound
more than that of the scribes and Pharisees, you shall not enter into the
kingdom of the heavens"? Therefore the Apostle exhorts us to share with those
who have not, not only from fields and revenues, but also from the labor of
the hands: "Working," he says, "the good with the hands, that you may have to
share with him who has need." And to the one who wishes to follow, the Lord
proposed also to sell all his substance for the benefit of the poor, and so
to follow him: commanding to the followers and the perfect the sudden and
complete fulfillment of almsgivings, that, having completed the ministry
through money, they may pass over to that through word and spirit; but to the
rest charging continual distributions and sharings from what they have, that
through these they may be found imitators of the kindness of God, showing
mercy and sharing and giving freely. "For give," he says, "and it shall be
given to you." And through these things he also promised them communion with
himself. For these are they who stand at the right hand of the Lord, to whom
the king, when he comes, will say: "Come, you blessed of my Father, inherit
the kingdom prepared for you from the foundation of the world. For I was
hungry, and you gave me to eat; I was thirsty, and you gave me drink; I was
naked, and you clothed me; a stranger, and you took me in; sick and in
prison, and you came to me." And when the righteous marvel and say, "When did
we do these things to you, Lord?" he will say to them: "Amen, amen, I say to
you, inasmuch as you did it to one of these least of my brothers, you did it
to me." =31.1713= For eagerness toward the saints is piety toward Christ, and
the servant eager toward the poor is found a partner of Christ; not only if,
having much, he share something, but even if, possessing little, he bring
this, and if he give the disciple only a cup of cold water to drink in the
name of a disciple. For the want of the disciples according to the world is
an occasion of true wealth for you, O rich man. For through these you are
found a fellow-worker of Christ; you feed soldiers of Christ, and you feed
them willingly, not under compulsion. For the heavenly king does not
constrain, nor does he demand tributes, but he receives those who are eager,
that giving they may receive, and honoring they may be honored, and having
shared in the temporal things they may be called into the communion of the
eternal. Let these things be in our memory at every season, and before the
eyes of the soul, that we may not let the present opportunity pass, nor,
putting off the present, wait for another, lest in waiting and delaying we be
overtaken by the end. And may the Lord grant that we be found fruitful and
watchful and mindful of his commandments, ready and unhindered for his
glorious meeting, in Christ himself our God, with whom to the Father and to
the Holy Spirit be glory, power, honor, now and always, and unto the ages of
ages. Amen.
