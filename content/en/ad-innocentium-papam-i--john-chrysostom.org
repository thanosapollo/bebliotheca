and slaughters, and countless other things; but we, knowing the laws of the
fathers, and respecting and honoring the man, and having his own letters as
well, which declare that lawsuits ought not to be dragged beyond the borders,
but that the affairs of the provinces should be tried in the provinces, did
not consent to sit in judgment, but declined it with great vehemence. But he,
as if striving to outdo his former acts, called my archdeacon, and by a great
stretch of arbitrary power, as if the Church were already widowed and had no
bishop, transferred through that man the whole clergy to himself; and the
churches were uprooted, the clerics in each being led away and being prepared
to give libels against us and being trained for accusation. And having done
these things he sent and summoned us to a court, although he had not yet
stripped off the charges against himself, which was especially contrary both
to the canons and to all the laws.

2. But we, knowing that we were not coming to a court of judgment (for
otherwise we would have come ten thousand times) but to an enemy and an
adversary, as both the things that had happened before and the things after
these made clear, sent bishops to him, Demetrius of Pessinus, Eulysius of
Apamea, Lupicinus of Appiaria, and the presbyters Germanus and Severus,
answering with the gentleness that became us, and saying that we did not
decline a trial, but an open enemy and a manifest adversary. For one who had
not yet received libels, and had done such things from the outset, and had
torn himself from Church, communion, and prayer, and was training accusers,
and transferring clergy, and emptying a church: how could he justly mount the
judge's throne, which in no way belonged to him? For it is not consistent that
the man from Egypt should judge those in Thrace, and this while he is himself
liable to a charge, and an enemy and an adversary. =52.532= Yet he was ashamed
of nothing, but, hastening to fulfill what he had planned, when we had made it
known that we were ready, even with a hundred and a thousand bishops present,
to strip off the accusations and show ourselves clean, as indeed we are, he
would not consent; but while we were absent, and calling for a synod, and
seeking a trial, and not fleeing a hearing but open hostility, he both
received accusers, and loosed those who had been made out of communion by me,
and from those very men who had not yet stripped off the charges he received
libels, and he drew up minutes, all of which was contrary to ordinance and to
the sequence of the canons. But what need is there to say much? He did not
leave off doing and contriving everything, until with all power and arbitrary
authority he cast us out of both the city and the church, toward deep evening,
while the whole people was streaming after us. Dragged by the imperial agent
of the city through the midst of the city, and hauled by force, I was brought
down and thrown into a ship, and I sailed through the night, because I was
calling for a synod for a just hearing. Who could hear these things
without tears, even if he had a heart of stone? But since, as I said before,
one must not only lament the things that are done badly, but also set them
right, I entreat your Charity to rise up, and to share the pain, and to do
everything so that the evils may stop at this point. For not even here did the
lawlessness of these men come to an end, but after these things as well he
stripped again for a further contest upon the former deeds. For when the most
pious emperor had driven them out after they had shamelessly rushed upon the
church, and many of the bishops who were present, seeing their
lawlessness, had withdrawn to their own sees, fleeing their incursion as a
fire that feeds on
all things, we were again called into the city, and into the church from which
we had been unjustly expelled, more than thirty bishops introducing us, and
the most God-beloved emperor sending a notary for this purpose, while that man
fled at once. For what reason and why? As we entered we entreated the most
God-beloved emperor to gather a synod for the vindication of what had been
done. Knowing then what he had committed, and fearing the exposure, and
imperial letters having been sent everywhere gathering all men from every
quarter, he threw himself secretly at midnight into a boat, and so fled,
taking those

with him, so as to give an account of what had been done, and not to
suppose that the things so unjustly dared from one party only, and in our
absence, and contrary to so many canons, were enough for his defense. But he
did not even endure the imperial =52.533= letters, but remained at home,
putting forward a rising of the people, and an untimely zeal of certain
persons who were supposedly clinging to him; and yet before the imperial
letters this same people had washed him with ten thousand insults. But we do
not busy ourselves with these things now; we have said them wanting to show
that he was caught in his mischief. Yet not even after these things did we
keep quiet, but we pressed on, asking that a court be held by question and
answer; for we said we were ready to show that we ourselves were without
blame, and that they had committed the utmost lawlessness. For there were
certain Syrians among those who had once been present with him, left behind
here, who had done the whole with him in common; and we approached ready to be
judged, and we often troubled them about this, asking that the minutes be
given to us, or the libels of accusations, or that the nature of the charges
be made known, or the accusers themselves; and we obtained none of these
things, but were again expelled from the Church. How could I relate the things
from this point, which now surpass every tragedy? What word will set them
forth? What ear will receive them without shuddering? For when we were putting
these things forward, as I said before, a crowd of soldiers all at once, on
the Great Sabbath itself, toward evening as the day was already hastening on,
having burst into the churches, violently drove out all the clergy who were
with us, and the sanctuary was ringed with weapons. And women who had stripped
in the houses of prayer for baptism at that very time fled naked from fear of
this harsh assault, not even being allowed to put on the modest clothing that
befits women; and many also, having received wounds, were driven out, and the
pools were filled with blood, and the sacred streams were stained red from the
blood. And not even here did the horror stand still. But the soldiers, some of
whom, as we learned, were uninitiated, having entered where the holy things
were kept, both saw everything within, and the most holy blood of Christ, as
happens in so great a tumult, was poured out upon the garments of the soldiers
just named; and as in a barbarian captivity everything was dared. And the
people were driven toward the wilderness, and the whole people spent their
time outside the city, and the churches became empty of the peoples in such a
feast, and more than forty bishops in communion with us were driven with the
people and the clergy vainly and to no purpose; and wailings, and dirges, and
fountains of tears were everywhere, in the marketplaces, in the houses, in the
deserts, and every part of the city was filled with these calamities; for
because of the excess of the lawlessness not only those who suffered, but also
those who underwent nothing of this kind, shared our pain, not only those of
the same mind, but also heretics, and Jews and pagans, and as if the city
had been taken by force, so was everything in tumults and disturbances and
wailings. And these things were dared against the will of the most pious
=52.534= emperor, when night had come on, bishops arranging them and in many
places acting as generals, who were not ashamed to have drill-masters going
before them instead of deacons. And when day came, the whole city was being
moved outside the walls under trees and glens, celebrating the feast like
scattered sheep.

4. It is possible for you from this point to reckon all the rest; for as I
said before, it is not possible to go through in speech each thing as it
happened. The hard thing is that so many and so great evils have not even now
received a release, nor even a hope of release; but the horror is stretched
out every day, and we have become a laughingstock to the many, or rather no
one laughs, even if he is ten thousand times lawless, but all mourn, as I
said, the finishing stroke of the evils, this new lawlessness. What would one
say of the disturbances of the remaining Churches? For the evil did not stop
even here,
but reached even as far as the East. For just as when a noxious humor is
poured out from the head, the remaining parts are corrupted, so also now, the
evils having begun from this great city as from a fountain, the tumults
advanced everywhere in course, and clergy everywhere rose against bishops, and
bishops against bishops, and peoples were split from peoples, and they are
about to be, and everywhere there are birth-pangs of evils, and an overturning
of the whole inhabited world. Having learned all these things, then, my most
honored and most devout lords, show the courage and zeal that become you, so
as to check so great a lawlessness that has come in upon the Churches. For if
this custom should prevail, and it should become possible for those who wish
it to go into other men's dioceses from such great distances, and to cast out
whomever one might wish, and to do by their own authority whatever they wish,
know that all things will be gone, and a kind of undeclared war will overrun
the whole inhabited world, all men striking all and being struck. Therefore,
so that so great a confusion may not overtake all that is under the sun, be
entreated to write that the things done so lawlessly in our absence, and from
one party only, and when we did not decline a trial, have no force, as indeed
they do not have by their own nature; and that those who have committed such
lawless acts, when they are convicted, be subjected to the penalty of the
ecclesiastical laws; and grant that we, who have not been caught, are not
being convicted, and have not been shown liable, may continually enjoy
your letters, and your love, and all the other things which we had before. And
if even now those who have committed such lawless acts should wish to try the
charges for which they unjustly expelled us, when minutes were not given to
us, nor libels, nor accusers appearing: if an impartial court sits, we will
both be judged, and we will make our defense, and we will show ourselves
without blame of the things brought against us, as indeed we are; for the
things now done by them are outside every sequence, and every =52.535=
ecclesiastical law and canon. And why do I say ecclesiastical canon? Not even
in the secular courts was such a thing ever dared, or rather not even in a
barbarian court, neither Scythians nor Sauromatae would ever have judged in
this way, deciding from one party only, the accused being absent, who was not
declining a trial but hostility, calling for ten thousand judges, saying that
he was without blame, and that with the inhabited world present he would strip
off the charges and show himself innocent in all things. Having reckoned all
these things, then, and having learned all more clearly from my lords, the
most devout =52.536= brethren of ours the bishops, be entreated to bring in
on our behalf the zeal that is yours. For in this way you will not grant a
favor to us alone, but also to the common body of the Churches, and you will
receive the reward from God, who does all things for the peace of the
Churches.
Farewell always, and pray for me, most honored and most holy master.
