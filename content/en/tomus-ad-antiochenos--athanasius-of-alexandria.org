The Tome to the Antiochenes of Athanasius Archbishop of Alexandria. To the
beloved and most longed-for fellow-ministers Eusebius, Lucifer, Asterius,
Cymatius and Anatolius, Athanasius and the bishops present in Alexandria from
Italy and Arabia, Egypt and Libya, Eusebius, Asterius, Gaius, Agathus,
Ammonius, Agathodaemon, Dracotius, Adelphius, Hermaeon, Marcus, Theodorus,
Andreas, Paphnutius, another Marcus, Zoilus, Menas, George, Lucius, Macarius,
and the rest, very much greeting in Christ. We are persuaded that, being
ministers of God and good stewards, you are sufficient to set in order all the
affairs of the Church; but since it has also come to us that very many who
were formerly divided from us through contentiousness now wish to be at peace,
and many, having also withdrawn from association with the Ariomaniacs, claim
communion with us; we thought it necessary to write to your courtesy these
things which we and our beloved Eusebius and Asterius have written, you
yourselves also being beloved and truly most longed-for fellow-ministers,
rejoicing at such a report, and praying that even if anyone is still left far
from us, and if anyone still appears gathering with the Arians, =26.797= this
one may leap away from their madness; so that all everywhere henceforth may
say: "One Lord, one faith." For what is so "good," as the Hymnographer said,
or what "pleasant," but "that brothers dwell together"? And our house is the
Church; and the mind ought to be the same; for thus we believe that the Lord
also will dwell with us, saying: "I will dwell in them and walk among them,"
and, "Here I will dwell, because I have chosen it." But here, where, except
where one faith and piety is proclaimed? We ourselves then from Egypt truly
wished, together with our beloved brothers Eusebius and Asterius, for many
reasons, but especially for this, to come, that we might at once embrace your
affection, and in common enjoy such peace and concord; but since, as we also
made clear in the other letters, and you can learn from the same
fellow-ministers of ours, the ecclesiastical needs detain us; we were grieved,
yet we asked the same fellow-ministers of ours, Eusebius and Asterius, to come
to you in our stead; and thanks to their piety, that, although they themselves
were able to hasten to their own dioceses, they preferred above all the
journey to you on account of the pressing ecclesiastical need. They having
then consented, we consoled ourselves, that, they and you being there, we all
consider ourselves to be present with you. Call then to yourselves all who
wish to be at peace with us, and especially those who assemble in the Palaea,
and those who are running away from the Arians, and receive them as fathers do
sons, and welcome them as teachers and guardians, and having joined them to
our beloved who are around Paulinus, require nothing more from them than to
anathematize the =26.800= Arian heresy, and to confess the faith confessed by
the holy Fathers at Nicaea, and also to anathematize those who say that the
Holy Spirit is a creature, and divided from the essence of Christ. For this is
truly to leap away from the abominable heresy of the Arians, not to divide the
holy Trinity, and say that something of it is a creature. For those who
pretend to name the faith confessed at Nicaea, but dare to blaspheme against
the Holy Spirit, do nothing more than deny the Arian heresy in words, but hold
it in thought. And let the impiety of Sabellius and of Paul of Samosata be
anathematized by all, and the madness of Valentinus and Basilides, and the
derangement of the Manichaeans; for when these things are done thus, every
evil suspicion will be removed from all, and the faith of the catholic Church
alone will be shown pure. That we then have this faith, both we and those who
have always had communion with us, we think no one either of you or anyone
else is ignorant. But since we rejoice with those who wish to be joined, with
all, but especially with those who assemble in the Palaea, and we have
glorified the Lord exceedingly, as for all things, so also for the good
purpose of these men; we exhort you that concord with these men come about on
these terms, and that nothing more than these, as we said before, either those
assembling in the Palaea be required by you, or those around Paulinus put
forward anything else or more than the things at Nicaea. And the paper much
talked of by some, as having been composed concerning faith at the synod at
Sardica, prevent it even from being read at all or put forward; for the synod
defined nothing of this kind. For some thought it right, as if the synod at
Nicaea were deficient, to write concerning faith, and they even attempted it
rashly; but the holy synod gathered at Sardica was indignant, and defined that
nothing further should be written concerning faith, but that they should be
content with the faith confessed by the Fathers at Nicaea, because nothing is
lacking to it, but it is full of piety, and that a second faith must not be
set forth, lest the one written at Nicaea be thought incomplete, and a pretext
be given to those who wish often to write and define concerning faith. Whence
even if anyone puts forward this, or another, stop such men, and rather
=26.801= persuade them to be at peace; for we have perceived nothing more in
these things than contentiousness alone. For those whom some were blaming as
saying three hypostases, because the phrases are unwritten and from the outset
suspicious, we thought it right to seek nothing more except the confession at
Nicaea; yet we examined these men on account of the contentiousness, whether
perhaps, as the Ariomaniacs say, alienated and estranged, alien in essence
from one another, and each hypostasis divided by itself, as are the other
creatures, and those begotten from men; or whether they themselves also say
them as different essences, as gold, or silver, or bronze is; or whether, as
other heretics say three principles and three gods, so these men also,
thinking thus, say three hypostases? They affirmed that they neither say, nor
have ever thought thus. And when we asked them: How then do you say these
things? or why do you use such phrases at all? they answered, because they
believe in a holy Trinity, not a Trinity in name only, but one truly existing
and subsisting, and they know a Father truly existing and subsisting, and a
Son truly essential and subsisting, and a Holy Spirit subsisting and existing,
and that they have neither said three gods or three principles, nor do they at
all endure those who say or think this; but that they know a holy Trinity, and
one Godhead, and one principle, and a Son homoousios with the Father, as the
Fathers said, and the Holy Spirit, not a creature, nor foreign, but proper and
undivided from the essence of the Son and of the Father. And having accepted
these men's interpretation of the phrases, and the defense, we examined those
accused by them as saying one hypostasis, whether perhaps as Sabellius thinks,
so these men also say, toward the abolition of the Son and of the Holy Spirit,
or as though the Son were without essence, or the Holy Spirit without
hypostasis? And they themselves also affirmed that they neither say this, nor
have ever thought thus; but we say hypostasis, considering it the same to say
hypostasis and essence; and we think one, because the Son is from the essence
of the Father, and because of the identity of nature; for we believe there is
one Godhead, and one nature of it, and not another of the Father, and foreign
from this that of the Son, and that of the Holy Spirit. At any rate there also
those accused as having said three hypostases agreed with these; and they
themselves who had said one essence interpreted and confessed the things of
those men, =26.804= and by both sides Arius was anathematized as a
Christ-fighter, and Sabellius and Paul of Samosata as impious, and Valentinus
and Basilides as alien from the truth, and Manichaeus as an inventor of evils;
and all by the grace of God, and after such interpretations, agree together
that the faith confessed by the Fathers at Nicaea is better and more accurate
than such phrases, and that for the future they would rather be content with
its words and use them. But also concerning the economy of the Savior
according to the flesh, since some also seemed to contend with one another
about this, we examined both these and those, and what these confessed, those
also agreed, that not as "the Word of the Lord came" to the prophets, so also
he sojourned in a holy man at the consummation of the ages, but the Word
himself became flesh; and being in the form of God, he took the form of a
servant, and from Mary according to the flesh he has been born man for us, and
thus perfectly and wholly the human race, being freed from sin in him, and
being made alive from the dead, is brought into the kingdom of the heavens.
For they confessed this also, that the Savior did not have a soulless body,
nor insensible, nor without mind. For it was not possible, the Lord having
become man for us, that his body should be without mind, nor has salvation
come about of the body only, but also of the soul, in the Word himself; and
being truly Son of God, he has also become son of man; and being only-begotten
Son of God, the same has also become firstborn among many brothers. Wherefore
neither was there one Son of God before Abraham, and another after Abraham;
nor was there one who raised Lazarus, and another =26.805= who inquired about
him; but the same was saying in human fashion, "Where does Lazarus lie?" and
raising him divinely; and the same was spitting bodily as man, and divinely as
Son of God opening the eyes of the one blind from birth; and suffering in
flesh, as Peter said, and divinely opening the tombs, and raising the dead.
From which, understanding also all the things in the Gospel thus, they
affirmed that they think the same concerning the incarnation and becoming-man
of the Word. These things then having been thus confessed, we exhort you not
to condemn hastily those who thus confess and thus interpret the phrases which
they say, nor to reject them, but rather receive them as they are at peace and
make defense; but those who do not wish thus to confess and interpret the
phrases, these check and put to shame, as suspicious in thought. And not
enduring these, counsel also those who interpret and think rightly not to
examine one another further nor to fight about words to no useful purpose, nor
to contend with such phrases, but to agree in the mind of piety. For those who
do not think thus, but only contend over such little phrases, and seek beyond
the things written at Nicaea, do nothing else than "give their neighbor turbid
overthrow to drink," as men who envy the peace and love the schisms. But you,
as good men and faithful servants, and stewards of the Lord, stop and check
the things that cause scandal and are strange, and prefer such peace above all
things, faith being sound; perhaps the Lord will have mercy on us, and will
join the things that have been divided, and one flock having come to be, we
all again have one leader, our Lord Jesus Christ. These things we, even if
nothing more than the synod at Nicaea ought to have been sought, nor the words
from contentiousness endured, yet for the sake of peace, and for the sake of
not rejecting those who wish to believe rightly, examined, and what they
confessed, we dictated briefly, we who were left in Alexandria, in common with
our fellow-ministers Asterius and Eusebius; for most of us had gone away to
their own dioceses. And you again in common, where you have also been
accustomed to assemble, read these things, and deign to invite all there. For
it is right that the letter be read there first, and that there those who wish
and claim =26.808= peace be joined; and then, they having been joined,
wherever it please all the peoples, your courtesy being present, the
assemblies be performed there, and the Lord be glorified in common by all. The
brothers with me greet you. I pray that you be well, and remember us to the
Lord, both I Athanasius; and likewise the other bishops who came together
signed; and those sent by Lucifer the bishop of the island of Sardinia, two
deacons, Herennius, and Agapetus; and from Paulinus, Maximus and Calemerus,
they too deacons. And there were present also certain monks of Apollinarius
the bishop, sent by him for this. And each of the bishops set forth, to whom
the letter was written, is: Eusebius of the city of Virgilli of Gaul, Lucifer
of the island of Sardinia, Asterius of Petra of Arabia, Cymatius of Paltus of
Coele Syria, Anatolius of Euboea. And those who sent the letter: both pope
Athanasius, and those present with him in Alexandria, both Eusebius himself
and Asterius; and the rest: Gaius of Paratonium of nearest Libya, Agathus of
Phragoneos and part of Elearchia of Egypt; Ammonius of Pachnemunis and of the
remaining part of Elearchia, Agathodaemon of Schedia and Menelaites;
Dracontius of Little Hermopolis, Adelphius of Onuphis of the Lychni, Hermion
of Tanis, Marcus of Zygra of nearest Libya, Theodorus of Athribis, Andreas of
Arsenoites, Paphnutius of Sais, Marcus of Philon, Zoilus of Andro, Menas of
Antiphrus. To these Eusebius also signed in Latin, of which the interpretation
is: I Eusebius the bishop, according to your accuracy, the things confessed by
both sides agreeing with one another concerning the hypostases, have myself
also consented; and moreover also concerning the incarnation of our Savior,
that the Son of God has also become man, having taken on all things without
sin, such as our old man consists of, I have confirmed according to the tenor
of the letter. And since the paper of Sardica is said to be outside, for the
sake of not seeming to set forth anything beside the faith at Nicaea, I also
consent, that the faith at Nicaea may not seem to be excluded through this,
nor that it is to be produced. I pray that you be well in the Lord. =26.809= I
Asterius agree with the things written above, and I pray that you be well in
the Lord. And after this tome was sent from Alexandria, thus signed by those
mentioned before, after these things they themselves also signed: I Paulinus
think thus, as I received from the Fathers, a perfect Father being and
subsisting and a perfect Son subsisting, and the Holy Spirit having
subsistence, perfect. Wherefore I also accept the interpretation written above
concerning the three hypostases, and the one hypostasis, that is essence, and
those who think thus. For it is pious to think and confess the holy Trinity in
one Godhead. And concerning the becoming-man of the Word of the Father which
came about for us, I think thus, as it is written, that, according to John,
the Word became flesh; not according to the most impious who say that he has
suffered a change, but that he has become man for us, having been born from
the holy Virgin Mary and Holy Spirit. For the Savior had a body neither
soulless, nor insensible, nor without mind; for it was not possible, the Lord
having become man for us, that his body should be without mind. Whence I
anathematize those who set aside the faith confessed at Nicaea, and who do not
say that the Son is from the essence of the Father, and homoousios with the
Father. And I anathematize those who say that the Holy Spirit is a creature
that has come to be through the Son. And further I anathematize the heresy of
Sabellius and of Photinus, and every heresy, walking in the faith at Nicaea,
and in all the things written above. I Carterius pray that you be well.
