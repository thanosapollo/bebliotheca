On the holy protomartyr Stephen.
=59.699=
All the contests of the martyrs are glorious, and the contest of the
righteous is marvelled at everywhere; but where the renowned stadium of
Stephen the protomartyr is held, there even the speechless tongue toward
praise =59.700= is stirred. For whom does the blessed Stephen not call to
praise, who even by the name alone signifies his own dignity, and by the
name itself has already obtained beforehand the honour of the victory?
For in this both the =59.701= wrestling, and the victory, and the honour
are proclaimed by the martyr's name. Stephen, who is first of the
contests, and first of the praises, and exceptional among the praises,
who follows in the footsteps of the Master, and became the beginning of
those who contend.
For this one the Saviour first cultivated as a martyr unto salvation;
this one the death of Christ at once persuaded to despise even death
itself; this one the Master's side caused to pour out even his own blood.
For what he knew the Master to have suffered on behalf of servants, these
things he endured to suffer on behalf of the Master himself, not waiting
for a season, not having seen forerunners, not having beheld a contest.
For since he showed his own way of life as following, everywhere to
contend together with the Master, and to work a like end, keeping, as a
disciple, the teacher's order. And look, I pray you, at the blessed one
imitating the Master himself in both works and words. The power of the
signs glorified the Saviour; the martyr's grace also is blessed in this.
For Stephen, it says, full of grace and power, did wonders and signs
among the people. The Lord is marvelled at as he teaches; the martyr is
praised as he speaks in public. For beginning from the vision concerning
Abraham, and going through in sequence accordingly, he mentions the more
crucial persons and events, and introduces the prophecy unto Christ, and
shows the Jews always angering God. But just as Christ had Pharisees
contradicting, so also the martyr had those from the synagogue opposing
him. For there arose, it says, certain of those from the synagogue called
of the Libertines and Cyrenians and Alexandrians, and of those from
Cilicia and Asia, disputing with Stephen; and they were not able to
withstand the wisdom and the spirit by which he spoke. The Master is
accused concerning the temple; the martyr also is slandered in this. For
this man, it says, does not cease speaking words against this holy place,
and the law. Outside the city the Master is crucified; outside the city
the martyr also is stoned. For casting him out of the city they were
stoning him. Jews crucify Christ, Jews also stone Stephen. The Master
grants pardon as he is crucified; for Father, he says, forgive them this
sin; for they do not know what they do.
The martyr also entreats similar things concerning them. For kneeling, it
says, he prayed saying: Lord Jesus Christ, do not lay this sin to their
charge. Neither in words nor in works did the martyr, following the
Master, omit anything, but he showed both the forbearance of the soul and
the courage of endurance; whence he is also deemed worthy of this
=59.702= divine vision. For looking intently, it says, into heaven, he
saw the glory of God, and Jesus standing at the right hand of the Father.
Great is this stadium of the contests. For the athlete wrestles below,
and the president of the games looks down from the heavens; this one
contends boxing here, and that one from above contends together standing.
For he does not behold the Saviour sitting, but standing. O how great is
the grace of the dignity! The Master stands while the martyr contends.
For the form of a president of the games, even the posture, signifies the
greatness of the contests. For then the presidents of the games rise from
their thrones, when the heat of the contests is kindled; then the
firmness of those who contend is proven, when the debt of honour is paid.
And this is the honour of the Saviour, that the martyr should even be
preferred above the angels. For looking intently, it says, into heaven,
he saw the glory of God, and Jesus standing at the right hand of God. He
saw the invisible, not only place and glory, but even the very one longed
for, where even the host of angels fears to stoop and look. There the
martyr fixes his gaze, where the Cherubim cover those pure faces, the
Seraphim do not dare to look up with their eyes into the boundless
height. He was shown therefore in this respect higher than the angels,
loftier than the powers, beyond the thrones and the dominions. For the
Master's voice was drawing him, having promised beforehand and saying:
Where I am, there also my servant shall be. This one is first servant of
the Saviour, as also first martyr of the contest. And the strange thing
in the events is that this righteous man's martyrdom is not fulfilled by
fire, nor by iron, nor by wild beasts, nor by other terrors, but by the
casting of stones alone. It was necessary that Stephen be adorned with
stones alone, and receive the grace of the truth from the addition of the
stones. For as in gold, the casting of the stones was set in the body,
and especially in the head flowing round on every side with blood.
Seeing, therefore, many have become martyrs.
For Stephen is much longed for by those who contend, both procuring the
honour and teaching the contest; for he guides by his own wrestlings, and
exhorts by his own name. Wherefore also from his own deeds, before Paul,
he cries: Become imitators of me, even as I also of Christ. For I
followed, he says, in word, and in life, and in end, the Master of all.
Come, you also imitate, accomplishing each of these things with me; for
it is possible and profitable for those who will. And I am a witness, who
both first contended after the Master, and first in heaven beheld the
hidden things. For I saw Jesus standing at the right hand of the Father,
and I was looking about at that which had come to pass: The Lord said to
my Lord, Sit at my right hand, until I make your enemies a footstool of
your feet to him be the glory and the power unto the ages of ages Amen.
