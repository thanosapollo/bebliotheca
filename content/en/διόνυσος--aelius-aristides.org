Let Asclepius himself lead, he who showed the dream, and let Dionysus
himself lead, for whom one must dance, and Apollo leader of the Muses,
father of the one and brother of the other, as the story goes. Let us
then leave the perfect hymns and speeches about Dionysus to Orpheus and
Musaeus and the ancient lawgivers; but we ourselves, as if for a token
that we were not of the uninitiated, let us address the god with a
measured voice. In every way both lengths and shortnesses and anything
whatsoever in nature is dear to him. And somehow the things from the
dream also come to pass, that one must be resourceful about speeches.

consorts with Semele; and when Semele was with child, Zeus, wishing
himself to become both for Dionysus, father and mother, conveys Semele
from the earth to Olympus through fire, and himself taking up his own
seed and having sewn it into his thigh carried it ten months from the
beginning, having his dwelling in Nysa above Ethiopia. And when it was
the due season, having called the Nymphs he looses the stitch, and thus
Dionysus is born belonging to his father from both sides. So
exceptionally did Zeus honor him above all gods and men. For neither
did he himself become both these things for any other, nor did any other
for any other. For this reason then they say the god is both male and
female, because the father too had a share of each nature toward him.
And I have already heard from some another account about these things,
that Zeus himself is Dionysus. And what could you say beyond this? And
he is like him in nature and in form. For he is as it were twin in every
way toward himself. For both among youths and among maidens * * and
again as among males both beardless and Briseus, and both warlike and
peaceful exceptionally among the gods. And they also give him Pan as
dancer, being the most perfect dancer of the gods, as Pindar hymns and
the priests in Egypt have learned. And they say that he alone of the
gods reconciled Hera to her son, having brought Hephaestus unwilling
into heaven, and that too having set him on a donkey. And that there is
a riddle in the story is clear, and it is also clear where the riddle
ends, that the power of the god is great and invincible, and he could
even give wings to donkeys, not horses only; just as some Laconian poet
also attributed to him the milking of lions' milk; and nothing then will
be so firmly bound, not by disease, not by anger, not by any fortune,
which it will not be possible for Dionysus to loose. But even the former
enemy becomes a drinking-companion, and the old man will grow young
again, and will drink when the god stirs. These things again both Sileni

bear witness, and the dancers about him; and the ease of his power,
fennel-wands instead of a spear and a fawn-skin instead of a lion-skin
having been provided for him, and a cup instead of a hollow shield, as
if fighting and drinking do the same thing for Dionysus. And there is
not much in the middle between the contest and the victory-songs. And
they say he subdued Indians and Tyrrhenians, hinting, it seems to me,
through the Tyrrhenians at the parts toward the west, and through the
others at the place of the earth toward the east, as ruling over it all.
And Bacchants going forth for him even in place of horsemen and archers,
things following the equipment which we just now hymned, and at the same
time they also seal this, that of Dionysus' polity there were even
women's armies together with those of the men, and it is Dionysiac not
so much to make the men feminine as to set the women into the rank of
men; so great and many-sided and all-harmonious he is. And it is clear
from this too. For having shared with Aphrodite he opens theaters and
becomes leader of banquets and thiasoi, but having come into the same
with Ares he becomes armed; and indeed becoming fiery together with
Athena and Hephaestus. And the Kerykes and the Eumolpids set him as
assessor to the Eleusinian goddesses, overseer of fruits and of
nourishment for humans. And what it seems to me fitting to add to this,
for he alone of the sacred apparitions went forth in the Median danger.
Thus being a friend of mankind in all things he leans toward the Greeks.
Then having mixed with Nymphs he has danced and dances many and all
kinds of things among humans, catching the things within more accurately
than Eurycles, and he makes a dancer even if one was unmusical before.
And he skips and plays and wishes to sing, and these things even from
couch and pallet. Thus the god is by nature able to do everything. And
the wondrous tyrant of humans, Eros, having drawn from Dionysus'
springs, goes over all the earth using Dionysus as guide, nor for him
are there seats apart from this one, nor works, nor beds. Then he
watches over the bounds of night and day, the
