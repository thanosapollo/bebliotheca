Of the same, to the same.
( 15Β_328 > Your sacred letter, holy Father, came and comforted me as I
was being torn by many scourges of grief over what has befallen from the
slander of those who do not fear the Lord, the noble guardian of the
virtues, lord (=0577=) George; on the one hand bringing good news of
your health, longed for by all; on the other announcing the firmness of
the right confession toward the Lord Jesus Christ, our true God. Which,
confirmed by the testing of sufferings, has been changed for you into
immutability, since you have set at nothing the ill fortune of the
present life, whose good fortune you long ago held in contempt, and have
shifted the whole desire of the soul toward the things hoped for alone;
which faith and a good conscience are by nature fitted to characterize:
the one guarding the pious word, stained in no way by a false opinion;
the other fashioning the life well-pleasing to God, accomplished by the
keeping of the commandments.
For there is need of both, I mean of word and of life, for the one who
has been eager to prefer the companionship of wisdom above all things;
so that we may also piously proclaim the mystery of the unchanged
self-emptying of the God of all through flesh for our sake; confessing
Christ in truth God and man together: the one being without cause
through himself,

and the other having become later for our sake. Neither laying aside
what he was, on account of what he became; for he is unchanged. Nor
denying what he became, on account of what he was; for he is a lover of
mankind. For it was possible for him alone, who is in the proper sense
by superabundance ( 15Β_330 > of power, to become without change and
without confusion what he was not, and to remain in the proper sense
both what he was and what he became: by the extreme unity of his own
hypostatic monad not admitting division, because of the difference of
the things from which he was composed that is naturally preserved in him
after the union; if indeed, united hypostatically to flesh intellectually
ensouled, he was shown by the wonders remaining without change what he
was; and by the sufferings he appeared preserving without alteration
what he became; and through both, I mean wonders and sufferings, he
granted us the abolition of sin and the grace of deification. And let us
honor the calling of grace by a manner of life made splendid by the
commandments, shaken in no respect by any pleasure or by any pain
whatsoever; and let us show through what we do or suffer that we are
held by God alone, who gave himself for us as ransom and exchange,
having entrusted to him our whole life, both the present and the future;
as being from him by the reason of coming-to-be, and existing through
him by the manner of conduct; and coming to rest in him by the mystery
according to grace of the hoped-for deification; and let us receive the
ever well-being through what now seems to be ill-being, having shown,
through the things we suffer, our hidden disposition toward God, which
is altered in no way by the painful things that befall us.
(=0580=) Let us not then grow weary in the afflictions, honored Father,
knowing that affliction works patience, and patience testing, and
testing hope, and hope does not put to shame, having as sure the eternal
enjoyment of the goods for which we suffer; but let us bear what happens
nobly, looking to the pioneer of our salvation, Jesus; who, being true
God, willingly accepted death for our sake; so that suffering we may
rejoice, having become connatural ( 15Β_332 > with him through death,
leavened toward resurrection of indissoluble life. Since it has been
established as the will of divine law that we, doing good, suffer
unjustly, as disciples of Christ unto the condemnation of sin; which the
intelligible serpent, having persuaded man by deceit to transgress the
divine commandment and to prefer the temporal to the eternal, brought
in; whom we received authority to trample, keeping the grace that keeps
us, guarded by the keeping of the commandments; which is accomplished
only by voluntary and involuntary hardship of flesh, by sober reasoning,
for those who love the Lord. Having nailed all your desire to him, you
do not feel the grievous things, holy Father; having as victor over the
present affliction the joy of the things hoped for; which may we all
obtain, as Christ our great God and Savior distributes these things to
us, as he knows, according as each is worthy; by the intercession of his
undefiled and all-holy ever-virgin Mother, and of all the saints. Amen.

Of the same, to Julian the Scholastic of Alexandria, concerning the
ecclesiastical dogma according to the incarnation of the Lord.
( 15Β_334 > The honored letter of my God-guarded master filled me, the
humble and sinful, with great joy and spiritual gladness, and with me,
so to speak, the whole holy catholic church of God, bringing us good
news of the firm and unshaken mind, of himself and of my master with
him, lord Christopemptus the most wise Scholastic, concerning the right
and pious and saving confession of the faith according to Christ our
true God;

of the faith; him who became man for this, that he might gather the
nature of men to himself, and stop it from being borne badly toward
itself, or rather rising and divided against itself, and having no
standing, because of the unstable motion of the mind about each thing.
Wherefore we all, giving thanks to God for you, most honored ones, make
unceasing petitions, even if we happen to be sinners, that he keep you
unshaken and (=0581=) unmoved for all time in the saving confession of
the faith; believing that the Son and Word of God and Father, from men,
for men, according to men without sin, having truly become man, was not
changed from being God even when he became man; nor yet has he been
diminished from being truly man, having remained what he was, and is,
and always will be by nature God; but ( 15Β_336 > being God according to
essence and nature through himself, he has without change according to
economy for our sake truly become man, namely by assumption of flesh
having an intellectual and rational soul. Wherefore he is also God by
nature and truly the same, as being this and that in truth without lack
the same; inasmuch as none of the natures brought together toward the
union according to hypostasis in the mystery of his incarnation, that is
perfect becoming-man, has in any way denied itself because of the union,
but has kept its own essential reason and definition, that is the
natural property toward the other unconfused and unchanged after the
union. For the union does not abolish the difference of the things
brought together into one hypostasis; but it wholly drives out their
division into parts.
The things from which Christ is being therefore preserved undiminished
in Christ according to nature even after the union: how is it not just
that the things from which Christ is be confessed even after the union,
according to some who do not see that it is unsafe and dangerous, and
has loss for every soul, to fight against the things that are clear, and
to deny the truth that plainly is; and that this belongs only to those
who do not expect resurrection and judgment at all. Since to whom is the
word of truth not evident and manifest, as simple and without excess,
and having nothing riddling whatsoever? For if Christ is God and man in
fact and in truth after the union, and would not be said to be God and
man only by naming and title; it is clear that he who says and thinks
Christ to be God and man together after the union has truly confessed
together with the names the natures of which the names are; if indeed he
does not think the titles to lie on Christ as bare, and wholly empty of
realities. What then has the holy catholic Church of God done that is
absurd, if she should say that the things from which Christ is are
preserved in Christ according to nature by an unbreakable union,
unconfusedly and undividedly even after the union? ( 15Β_338 > And what
has she thought foreign to the teaching of the holy Fathers, if she
neither holds that the divine nature of the Word has been changed into
the nature of the flesh, nor that the nature of the flesh itself has
passed over into the nature of the Word because of the union, and
teaches the faithful to think and say this after the union? And what
rather does she not confirm of the paternal tradition, teaching to think
thus, and confessing one Lord and one Christ, and one Son the same, and
one incarnate nature of God (=0584=) the Word himself who became flesh,
with flesh having the rational and intellectual soul, taking the phrase
according to the interpretation of our holy and blessed Father Cyril;
understanding by saying "incarnate" that the indication of our essence
is brought in, and not denying the nature of the flesh of the Word
because of the union? For the things preserved according to nature
unconfusedly and unchangedly and undividedly by an unbreakable union in
Christ even after the union without any diminution whatsoever, it is
altogether most fitting and most just that they also be confessed after
the

union. For it is a property of true faith to confess what we have been
taught to think. If then the things from which Christ is are preserved
according to nature after the union, it is altogether just to think them
to be in Christ after the union. And if it is just to think that the
things from which Christ is are preserved after the union, it would be
far more most just and together most fitting, and most zealous for all
who care for salvation, also to confess them; if indeed the indication
of true faith is the confession according to it without lack. And these
things concerning these. For there is no need to say more, when I make
the word toward you, and toward those who through grace are ignorant of
nothing of the things that look to the right faith of Christ.
And concerning the things you have written to me, most honored ones, I
have reported to the blessed servant of God, the all-praiseworthy
prefect, also our honored lines; and he promised readily ( 15Β_340 > to
fulfill every command of yours. And I do not cease reminding him, until
he should altogether bring your command to completion. And may the Lord
and God of all and Savior Jesus Christ, who willingly accepted death for
our sake, and by his own blood redeemed us from the authority of
darkness, and gave us the new commandment that we love one another as
much as we also have been loved; himself hold your right hand, and guide
you into every good work, and make straight your steps according to him;
bearing you in fullness of fruits of righteousness into the wonderful
place of his tent, where the dwelling of all who rejoice.
LETTER 18. ... TO ASCETIC WOMEN WHO HAVE FALLEN AWAY FROM THE CATHOLIC
CHURCH ...
18. Of the same, in the person of George the all-praiseworthy prefect of
Africa, to ascetic women who have fallen away from the catholic Church
in Alexandria.
( 15Β_342 > I for my part thought you to have been truly well
established, and to have the bases of the soul unmoved from the right
and pious confession and hope according to the blameless and unfailing
faith of Christ, reverencing the grace of God that called you, and
uniting you to the living and sound and blameless (=0585=) body of the
holy catholic and apostolic Church; and already having strengthened you
when you had wasted away with a wasting disease of error and ignorance,
and having made you a useful member of the common body of the Church;
according to which the pious and right and true and saving word of the
apostolic faith is proclaimed in its prime, making all under heaven
within itself; and always bringing and adding what is lacking to what
has already been gathered; and showing one soul and tongue of all from
the ends of the earth to its ends, by the spirit according to the
concord and unison of the faith; which the creator of men, the Word of
God, having become man from men, for men, according to men, truly by
assumption of flesh having a rational and intellectual soul, and not by
change of godhead, namely without sin, taught us men to confess and
embrace, denying none of his natures whatsoever after the union, that is
after the becoming-man; ( 15Β_344 > neither the divine, according to
which he is and remains always God through himself and consubstantial
with the Father; nor the human, according to which the same has truly
become man for our sake, as a lover of mankind, and is consubstantial
with us. For he was not changed from what he was and is, and will always
be by nature God; nor yet did he alter the nature of what he became,
man, by an unbreakable union of ensouled flesh, being one and the same
God and man; not being God and man only by naming and title, but in fact
and
in truth God and man both existing in the proper sense, and truly named.
Having the one and unique according to hypostasis from the union, but
not according to nature, as the Fathers teach, saying, "For even if the
both together is one, yet not by nature, by the coming-together";
according to which economically for our sake, as good, he accepted to
become composite, keeping unharmed even after the incarnation the simple
according to nature, according to which he is of one nature with the
Father and remains consubstantial. For godhead and manhood are not the
same. For even if the Word of God became man, yet he became man by
assumption of flesh intellectually united to him hypostatically without
break. Wherefore the same is in the proper sense both God and man, as
being that and having become this; and existing this and that without
lack; neither confused by the extreme uniqueness of the hypostasis,
according to which the reason of difference is wholly absent; nor
divided by the pure otherness of the natures from which he is composed;
according to which the reason of difference has in no way been abolished
because of the union.
The difference of the natures from which Christ is being therefore
preserved unconfusedly even after the union, there is not, as I think,
wholly any word able to show that it is just that the quantity of the
things naturally differing and preserved in Christ by ( 15Β_346 >
(=0588=) an unbreakable union not be confessed; because every difference
altogether introduces with itself a quantity of certain things differing
from one another in some respect. For no one, even if he be ingenious
and most resourceful, will ever be able, thinking well and making even a
little account of truth, to say that there is difference without
quantity; knowing clearly that every difference is either of the
otherness of certain things according to essence, or of the unlikeness
of certain things according to quality and property; which the quantity
of the underlying realities, whether they are according to union or also
wholly without this, produces. For in those in which the one and the
same reason is not contemplated in every way, there is an indication of
quantity, of which difference is a property. If then in the case of
Christ it follows to say, as the Fathers teach, the wholly unique
according to hypostasis by reason of the union; and it is consequent and
fitting to say the quantity by the difference of the natures from which
Christ is, preserved in Christ unconfusedly and undividedly even after
the union; it is clear that, just as he who does not say that Christ,
because of the union according to hypostasis, is one incarnate nature of
God the Word, understood rightly according to the interpretation of our
most holy Father and teacher Cyril, does not believe that the union has
taken place; so also he who does not confess the two natures after the
union, from which Christ is, cannot say that the difference is
preserved. For the difference after the union is of the natural
otherness of the things from which Christ is, and not of unlikeness
according to quality alone, without the natures underlying the
qualities; without which it is among the impossible things for quality
to be by itself; lest we introduce a bare, or rather more properly to
say, a false and nonexistent difference.
Persuaded that we hold this confession exactly according to the Fathers
of the faith, I did not hesitate to write at any time to all everywhere,
to patriarchs and bishops and rulers, and to our most pious and
all-peaceful emperors themselves, for thanksgiving ( 15Β_348 > to God,
making known to all your genuine turning and faith toward Christ. And I
never thought, as I said, that you would so quickly be moved from your
saving calling. But since, because of my sins, the evil one who from the
beginning by deceit has been dragging the race of men down to ruin and
destruction has been able to persuade you also to prefer the lie to the
truth; and more than God, and than the goods stored up for the saints,
for the sake of an evil custom, to gratify deceiving men who have been
taught only to bait toward death; and has been able to turn you back to
your own vomit, as one might say prophetically, like dogs, I enjoin, if
indeed you are so incurably disposed, and have proposed to the end
to turn away from the grace of Christ; for the present give the
(=0589=) gifts coming to you from me to Theopemptus the man, sent by me
for this and this alone with a written mandate, so that you may have no
doubt about this. And again, if anything presents itself to our most
pious and Christ-loving and all-peaceful emperors, and to the most holy
patriarchs, who learn all that concerns you through me, as I come with
God before their serenity, concerning your apostasy, and such
wickedness and ingratitude, I shall judge it well that it take place.
For I shall not keep silent so great an evil. But if, taking to mind the
dread tribunal of Christ, at which in the presence of angels and
archangels and every company of the heavenly hosts, and of all human
nature, the case assembled by me against you concerning this will be
tried, you repent, and return again to God who called you in grace; I
wish you already to have the very property granted you by me; and I
shall not cease, as long as I am managed to live this present and
temporal life, inventing ways for your more abundant establishment; and
I would not refuse to give myself as a ransom for your souls, if a time
should call this to take place of necessity. And ( 15Β_350 > I shall
give it readily, God calling me to this through grace; if only you
restore yourselves again to Christ God; and well heal the cutting and
separation that has come to you from his body through the return.
LETTER 19. ... TO PYRRHUS THE MOST HOLY PRESBYTER ...
19. Of the same, to Pyrrhus the most holy presbyter and hegumen.
( 15Β_352 > Having read your holy letter, God-honored Father, I
recognized your whole mind qualified through and through by the Spirit;
and I discerned you to have stood so far outside natural understanding,
as I grasped you to have laid hold of the divine wisdom that is above
nature. For through the letters I seemed as it were to see your
God-wise mind itself present, flashing only with the undefiled rays of
the Spirit; by which he is by nature transformed toward the more divine,
who is counted worthy to be adopted as son by the Father of all
(=0592=); and redeeming me from ignorance in the divine dogmas as much
as I formerly seemed to hold to knowledge of them. Wherefore, wondering
at your sagacity in all things, I cried out and uttered only that
voice which a woman of old cried to the Lord, struck by the power of his
words: Blessed is the womb that bore you, and the breasts which you
sucked. And I bless not only her who bore you, honored Father, as
counted worthy to become mother of such a one; but also your mind that
always conceives and is pregnant with and gives birth to the pious word;
as having been shown a womb able to contain the Word above nature
according to grace, because of the appropriation of the mind toward the
Word himself. And indeed according to your compact habits given forth
from your heart, with which as with breasts according to both practice
and contemplation you nourish the Word, growing together by the supply
of pious thoughts and ways; ( 15Β_354 > and, to speak paradoxically,
making the increase of the nourishing mind its own deification. For what
else had I to utter toward you, honored Father, seeing you become
begetter of such words; through which, as from a certain mountain of the
height of knowledge, you made known the judgment brought and issued upon
the divine dogmas as God-inscribed tablets through the new Mediator and
great Moses among us and priest of priests, and leader of the divine
priesthood throughout the whole inhabited world; which the holy and only
worshipped Trinity dictated as through an instrument, the aforesaid high
priest, and
bound the Churches still more toward concord, setting at nothing what
seemed to many to have taken place in the city of Alexander as an
innovation concerning the faith.
For the judgment given wholly preserved this meaning, according to which
our God-bearing Fathers handed down to the holy Churches to believe;
saying one and the same God Word both before flesh and after flesh;
which he himself united to himself for our sake, intellectually ensouled,
according to hypostasis, assumed from the holy Theotokos and
Ever-Virgin Mary; himself namely counting it worthy to become the seed
of his own incarnation; so that he might both truly become man; and show
by the seedless conception and the uncorrupt birth the nature being
innovated and suffering no diminution by the innovation. For the mystery
was a self-chosen emptying of God through goodness toward men, but the
voluntary condescension through flesh was not a falling-away of
godhead; for he remained what he was, even having become what he was
not; for he is unchanged. And he preserved what he became, remaining
what he was; for he is a lover of mankind. Through the things he worked
in a manner befitting God, showing what he became unaltered; (=0593=)
and through the things he suffered in a manner befitting man, confirming
that what he was was not changed. For he worked the divine things
fleshly, because through flesh not without a share of natural energy;
and the human things divinely, because by will authoritatively, and not
by circumstance ( 15Β_356 > he admitted the experience of human
sufferings. For neither the divine things divinely, because he was not
bare God; nor the human things fleshly, because he was not a mere man.
For this reason the wonders were not without suffering; and the
sufferings did not exist without wonder; but the one, if I may dare to
say, were not impassible; and the other were plainly wonderful; and both
paradoxical, because both divine [and human] as coming forth from one
and the same incarnate God Word, confirming through both in reality the
truth of the things from which, and which, he was. For what is completed
from certain things unconfusedly by a union according to a natural
coming-together both keeps the natures from which it is composed
unchanged, and preserves undiminished their constitutive powers, unto
the completion of one work; whether what takes place was a suffering or
a wonder, according to the image of your wonderful example, Father, and
one fitted to the mystery of the divine incarnation, of the fired sword,
whose cutting we know to be burning, and whose burning we know to be
cutting. For there has been a coming-together of fire and iron according
to hypostasis, neither departing from the power according to nature
because of the union toward the other; nor yet possessing this after the
union as loose, and separated from that of the thing composed and
subsisting together.
This accuracy of the divine dogmas, then, I found wisely woven into your
letters, to which, setting my mind according to the moderate power of
understanding that unites me, I prayed to be made sufficient to keep it,
and to hold it in the mind, not admitting circumscription by any
forgetfulness. But you, God-honored Father, have compassion on me your
servant, and as a lover of mankind grant the due pardon, since I am
unable toward the undertaking of the things commanded. And do not
condemn me of disobedience; but rather accept me as one eager to become
aware of my own ignorance. For rashness is hateful, and the first
offspring of the last ( 15Β_358 > ignorance; according to which the
disease of pride is by nature shown, fashioning a habit contrary to God
in those seized by it; and receive my refusal for this, clean of every
passion; and do not thrust me away as I put forward to you the same
words which Moses first and Jeremiah later put forward to God in such
things; the one saying, I beseech you, Lord, (=0596=) I am not
sufficient from before yesterday nor from the third day, nor from when
you began to speak to me; I am weak-voiced and slow of tongue. And the
other saying, He who is, Master Lord, I do not know how to speak,
because I am younger. For in truth I am weak-voiced and
slow of tongue, and deprived of the understanding for speaking the good
things; having neither a mind that applies itself to the divine things,
nor the spoken word able to serve the magnitude of the things inquired.
For I am still young in the passions, and I possess the rising of the
flesh that overreaches the law of the spirit. And whenever, even if we
grant by hypothesis that I lack nothing of the power in these things,
who is so bold and daring as to add further ordinances to things so
divinely decreed hierarchically by divine vote? I rather ask you to
clarify for me by your honored letters with a definition what energy is,
and in how many ways energy, and what besides this is the result of
energy; and which of these we say, toward both the work and the
practice, is the difference, so that I may have the force of the things
written to know. For I have not yet been able to set my mind exactly
upon what and how named or understood one must take the one energy. For
we do not simply put forward meaningless sounds, but we signify thoughts
by the sounds. For which cause I have often found the God-speaking
Fathers advancing sounds, but thoughts in no way; because the mystery of
our salvation exists not in syllables, but in thoughts and realities.
For the one they did, caring for peace; the other, establishing souls in
the truth. And these things concerning these.
( 15Β_360 > And I beg, honored Father, of your holiness, that you
kindly receive the divine Fathers who bring this moderate letter, who
have given themselves to this voyage because of a need profitable to the
common order of monks; and that you accept to become all things to them,
as many as the exact reason of the divine commandment wishes, of those
who desire the divine things, and have the incarnate Word as the most
unique pattern of their own life. For they are worthy of your (=0597=)
zeal for this, because they draw the divine yoke of virtue and knowledge
blamelessly. And I fear, writing these things, lest I carry away the
reproach of untimely boldness and of tastelessness among those who do
not know you, Father, and have not taken experience of your
condescension; and it be said also concerning me justly, what one of the
wise outside our word said, seeing one of the tasteless puffed up at
something:
A wise man, smiling, drove a fool to insolence.
In all things then, holy Father, pardon me your servant, and by your
all-holy prayers propitiate Christ God for me, whom you serve through
all by both purity of mind and accuracy of life.
LETTER 20. ... TO MARINUS THE MONK.
20. Of the same, to Marinus the monk.
( 15Β_362 > I for my part, knowing the multitude of my own faults, and
seeing the condemnation that will reasonably succeed me for them after
departure from this life; and as it were already contemplating that
great and dread tribunal gathered according to the soul, at which each
of us will receive the requitals of what we have done according to
worth; thought to have silence as a housemate, in every way sparing, as
it seems to me, the divine words above me, lest I be condemned still
more and more justly, as putting forward words of God that have been
put to death by me through inactivity in good works, and through this
unable to furnish the life in grace to those who hear; and unable to
show the ways of the virtues in the conduct of me who speak; and I
become liable to the coming threat, as not only myself in myself through
my evil ways having
murdered the life-giving words of God, but also furnishing to those who
hear, as a living image of contempt toward them, and manifest to all, my
whole evil manner of life. I then, as I said, knowing and fearing these
things, shook off the eagerness for speaking and writing the divine
words, whose working I do not have, judging it a very great injustice,
and a ruinous loss, to gather praises in vain from those who hear.
But since he who is great in prudence, and in all ( 15Β_364 > things
honored by me, the most holy hegumen, has overcome the vote of my
silence by the many charms of divine reasonings, whom I was not able to
thrust away because of the beauty of the virtues that sits well on him
according to divine understanding; and he forced me to write this
moderate letter to your piety, having related certain parts of your
accomplishments according to God, and having benefited me greatly; I
thought it necessary to say those things in summary, as many as are
profitable and saving for you and for me, and for all who wish to hear.
(=0600=) David the great prophet and king, showing to those who have
proposed to love God, and have chosen the God-inspired salvation above
all things on earth, the truly gnostic way of the divine virtues, and
opening the practical door of knowledge, says: The beginning of wisdom
is fear of the Lord; and good understanding to all who do it. And if
fear has been established as the beginning of wisdom, the end of wisdom
is by nature the good understanding, for those who do it. And if these
things have this manner, let us be eager with all power to acquire the
fear of God, and the good understanding. The fear, that we may flee the
defilements of sin, shaking them from the soul by expectation of the
eternal punishments; and the understanding, that we may be shown
scientific doers of all the divine wills. For he who fears the penalties
for transgressions of the divine commandments is not caught by the
passions that defile his soul; and he who has the good understanding
inwardly, distinguishing the temporal from the eternal according to the
due reason, becomes crowned with all the divine goods, preparing in no
way that the knowledge in him be shown idle. For as fear in those who
truly fear God becomes destroyer of the unclean passions; so also the
understanding of those truly intelligent becomes fashioner of all the
virtues. Therefore let us fear God, and let us do nothing of the things
he does not wish; and let us love his understanding; and let us not
neglect to do anything of the things he wishes. For he who ( 15Β_366 >
fears God hates all lawlessness, and he who has housed the good
understanding loves all righteousness. For as hatred toward lawlessness
signifies the one who fears; so also love toward righteousness makes the
intelligent one manifest.

prudence; which I pray to be kept through all for my God-guarded master,
being a guardian of his soul and of his body.
And him who brings this letter of my nothingness, having the prudence of
the soul more elder than the age of the body, I ask to be received
fittingly because of the understanding that sits well on him, as laying
claim to the divine zeal of the words, and not thinking it unworthy to
have a genuine relation concerning me the humble.


TO ANASTASIUS THE MONK*
( 15Β_450 > Yesterday on the eighteenth of the month, which was the holy
Pentecost, the patriarch made known to me saying:
Of which Church are you? Of Byzantium?Of Rome? Of Antioch Alexandria?
Of Jerusalem? Behold all have been united with the provinces
under them. If then you are of the catholic Church, be united, lest
innovating a strange road in the life, you suffer what you do not
expect.
Toward whom I said: Catholic Church, the right and saving confession of
the faith toward him, God of all declared to be, having blessed Peter
for the things he well confessed. Yet let me learn the confession, upon
which the union of all the Churches has taken place; and of what has
taken place well, I am not alienated.
And they say: Even if we do not have a command concerning this, we speak
so that you may become altogether without excuse. We say two energies
because of the difference, and one because of the union.
Have you said the two have become one because of the union, or besides
these, I said, another?
No, they say, but the two one because of the union,
We have been released from affairs, I said, having fashioned for
ourselves a faith without hypostasis, and a nonexistent God. For if we
confuse the two into one because of the union, and again divide them
into two because of the difference, there will be neither a monad nor a
dyad of energies, being abolished by one another
always, and making unenergetic him to whom they have been attached, and
wholly nonexistent.
For what has no motion from nature inalienable, and not by any reason of
change altered and falling over, has been deprived of every essence
according to the Fathers, not having an energy that characterizes it
essentially. This then I cannot say, nor was I taught by the holy
Fathers to confess. Do what seems good to you, being those in authority.
Therefore hear, they said: it has seemed good to the master and to the
patriarch, through a precept of the pope of Rome, that you be
anathematized as not being persuaded, and that you carry away the death
defined by them.
May what was defined by God before every age receive its end in me,
bringing him glory known before every age, I answered them having heard
this.
And toward your knowing, and making an addition of prayer to God and of
petition, I have made the things made known evident to you, asking you
to make these known to the lord uncle, and to our holy fathers there
with him, for the same cause.
