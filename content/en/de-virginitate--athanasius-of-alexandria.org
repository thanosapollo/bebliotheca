Of Athanasius the Great, On Virginity, that is, On Ascetic Practice.

Beginning of a word of salvation to the virgin.

1 First of all believe in one God, Father Almighty, maker of things visible
and invisible; and in his only-begotten Son Jesus Christ, who is from the
essence of the Father, equal in power to the Father in all things, who is
before the ages; and in the Holy Spirit who is in the Father and the Son, sent
from the Father and given through the Son, Father and Son and Holy Spirit,
three hypostases, one Godhead, one power, one baptism. For our God himself,
the Father of all, in six days made the heaven and the earth and the sea, and
all things in them. For he himself ordered the heaven with sun and moon and
most beautiful stars. And he founded the earth becomingly upon the waters,
ordering it with plants and trees of every kind; and at his command
honey-flowing rivers go forth and everlasting springs. And he commanded the
earth and brought forth flesh and made all the beasts of the earth according
to kind and the birds of heaven according to kind. He commanded the waters,
and brought forth fishes and made the great sea-monsters. And last of all he
made man and delivered all things to him for service. For the Lord God said to
his Son: "Let us make man according to our image and according to likeness";
and the Lord God formed man, taking dust from the earth, and set him in the
paradise of delight. And the Lord God cast an ecstasy upon Adam, and he slept,
and he took one of his ribs and filled up flesh in its place, and he built the
rib which he took into a woman, and brought her to Adam, and Adam said: this
now is flesh of my flesh, and bone of my bones; she shall be called woman,
because she was taken from her man. For this a man shall leave his father and
his mother and shall be joined to his wife, and the two shall be one flesh.

2 Hear, handmaid of Christ, and all who wish to be saved, and give ear to my
words; and let your ears receive God-breathed words. "For this mystery," he
says, "is great," as the blessed Paul said, that everyone who is joined to the
wife, both are one body. So again every man or woman joined to the Lord is one
spirit. For if those who are joined to the world leave father and mother and
are joined to mortal human beings, how much more ought the virgin who
practices continence to leave all earthly things and be joined to the Lord
alone. And the apostle himself also bears witness to my word, saying: "The
unmarried woman cares for the things of the Lord, that she may be holy in body
and in spirit, but she who has married cares for the things of the world, how
she may please her husband, and she is divided." This then I say, that every
virgin or widow practicing continence, if she has a care in this world, this
care itself is her husband; and if she has possessions, if she has goods, this
anxiety itself defiles her mind. For just as through the husband the body is
defiled, so also worldly attachments stain the soul and the body of her who
practices continence, and she is not holy in body and in spirit. But she who
cares for the work of God, her bridegroom is Christ. For she who is betrothed
to a corruptible man does the will of her husband; for thus it has been said,
that "the woman does not have authority over her own body, but the husband."
And again: "as the church is subject to the Lord, so also the wives to the
husbands in everything." For from the worldly things themselves, if we wish,
we also understand the things above. But she who is joined to the heavenly
bridegroom works the will of her own bridegroom.

3 And this is the will of Christ: that the one joined to him be carried about
by nothing at all of this age, care for none of the earthly things, but only
bear the cross of him who was crucified for him, and have care and anxiety
night and day to hymn him with unceasing hymns and doxologies, to have the eye
of the mind enlightened, to know his will and to do it, to have a simple
heart, a pure mind; to be merciful, so that, as he himself is compassionate
and merciful, so we also may follow after him; to be meek and quiet,
forbearing, rendering to no one evil for evil; enduring many insults, as he
himself was insulted by the Jews and endured to suffer blows and tortures; for
he himself suffered these things; for being struck by the servant of the high
priest, he did nothing, but only said: "If I spoke evil, bear witness
concerning the evil; but if well, why do you strike me?" Could not he who
commanded the earth to swallow Dathan and Abiram alive also swallow alive this
one who stretched out the hand and struck his own Creator? But he endured,
leaving us a pattern, that we might follow in his steps. But you, O man,
cannot bear to be insulted by a man of your own kind? Imitate your Master. For
if he, being God, bore for you to be struck by a sinful man, do you take it
ill that a man like you reviled you, and seek to repay him? O much
mindlessness and great folly! For this reason punishment also has been
prepared for us, and we kindle the fire for ourselves; because even being
rational we liken ourselves to the irrational animals. He came into this world
with much humility, and being rich he became poor for us, that we by his
poverty might become rich. And being God he became man for us, and was born of
the Theotokos Mary, that he might free us from the tyranny of the devil.

4 Therefore he who wishes to be saved makes himself foolish in this world,
that he may be called wise with God. For men call those who know how to give
and take and buy and sell, and to trade and to hold back the things of the
neighbor and to defraud and to lend and to make one obol into two, these they
call prudent; but God calls such people fools and senseless and sinners. For
hear, he says, what God himself has said through Jeremiah the prophet: "This
people are foolish sons to do evil; but they have not known to do the good."
And the blessed Paul: "The wisdom of this world is foolishness with God; and
he who wishes to become wise, let him become a fool, that he may become wise."
And again the same says: "Brothers, do not become children in your minds, but
in malice be infants." For God wishes them to be fools toward earthly things,
but prudent toward the heavenly; for our adversary the devil himself is
prudent in malice, and we must come against him prudently so as to conquer his
malicious designs. For the Savior says in the Gospels: "Be prudent as the
serpents and simple as the doves." And he who is called prudent with him, this
one is he who is skilled to do the will of God, and to keep his commandments.

5 Humility is a great medicine of salvation; for Satan was not brought down
from the heavens on account of fornication or adultery or theft, but pride
cast him down into the lower parts of the abyss; for thus he said: "I will go
up, and I will set my throne before God, and I will be like the Most High";
and for this word he was cast down, and his inheritance became the eternal
fire. Pride therefore is in the devil, but humility in Christ; for the Lord
himself says that "he who wishes to be great among us, let him be servant of
all." For God is of the humble.

6 Let us love fasting exceedingly; for fasting and prayer and almsgiving are a
great safeguard; for they rescue a man from death. For just as through food
and disobedience Adam was cast out of paradise, so again through fasting and
obedience he who wishes enters into paradise. In this virtue adorn your body,
O virgin, and you will please the heavenly bridegroom; for those who are
joined to the world and who beautify their own bodies with myrrhs and incense
and fragrances and in costly garments and gold, in order to please men, cannot
please God. But Christ needs none of these things from you, except only a pure
heart and an undefiled body bruised under fasting. And if some come and say to
you, do not fast frequently, otherwise you will become weaker; you will not
believe them nor will you obey them; for the enemy suggests it to them.
Remember what is written, when the three children and Daniel were taken
captive by Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon and other young lads with them, and
the king appointed them to eat from his table and to drink from his wine; but
Daniel and the three children themselves did not wish to be defiled from the
king's table, but they say to the eunuch who had received them: "Give us of
the seeds of the earth, and we will eat." And the eunuch says to them: I fear
your food and your drink, lest your faces appear gloomy before the king more
than all the lads who eat from the king's table, and he will condemn me; but
they said to him, try your servants about ten days, and give to us; and he
gave them pulse to eat and water to drink; and he brought them in before the
king, and their appearance was seen as choice beyond the lads who ate the
king's table.

7 You see what fasting does; it both heals diseases and dries bodily fluxes
and casts out demons and drives away evil thoughts and makes the mind brighter
and a pure heart and a sanctified body, and presents the man before the throne
of God; and, lest you think that these things are said simply, you have a
testimony in the Gospels spoken by the Savior; his disciples asked him saying:
Lord, show us in what manner the unclean spirits are put to flight. And the
Lord said: "This kind is not cast out except in fastings and prayers."
Everyone therefore who is troubled by an unclean spirit, if he understands and
uses this medicine, I mean fasting, at once the evil spirit, being straitened,
withdraws, fearing the fast. For the demons take great delight in surfeit and
in the rest of the body. Great is the power of fasting, and great achievements
come about through it, since from where also do men accomplish great powers,
and signs come about through them, and God grants healings to the sick through
them, if not altogether through ascesis and humility and a good way of life?
For fasting is the life of angels, and he who uses it has an angelic rank. And
do not think, beloved, that fasting is thus simply; for not he who fasts from
foods only has succeeded, but he who abstains from every evil deed, to this
one the fast is reckoned; for if you fast and do not keep your mouth from
speaking an evil word or irascibility or falsehood or perjury, or if you speak
against your neighbor; if these things come out of the mouth of the one
fasting, he has gained nothing, but has even lost all his labor. You
therefore, handmaid of Christ, and all who wish to be saved, if you fast,
purify yourself from all love of money; because he who loves silver cannot
love God. "For the love of money is the root of all evils."

8 Flee vainglory and boastfulness strongly. If a thought suggests to you that
you have become great and fair, advanced in virtue, do not believe it; for it
is the enemy who hinders and suggests vainglory; you will not therefore endure
the thought that praises you; but if the thought suggests to you saying, do
not wish to toil so painfully, you can be saved, you will not listen to it;
for it is the enemy who suggests slackness and negligence, that he may cast
you down from your virtuous way of life; for many are the devices that come
from the enemy to the servants of God; for he suggests to men to come and
speak words of praise so that his heart may be lifted up. But you, do not
accept the praise of men; and if someone says to you "you are blessed," say to
him, if I go out of this body having finished well, then I shall be called
blessed; for now I do not believe myself that I am blessed; for we men change
like a wind. And often he suggests to you to despise those who eat; do not
believe him; for he is alien; consider yourself least of all, that you may
lead many into the kingdom of the heavens, and you will be exalted with God;
and the enemy also suggests a great ascesis, that he may make the body weak
and useless. Let your fasting therefore have a measure; fast the whole year
without any compulsion; and at the ninth hour of the day, having continued in
hymns and prayers, partake of your bread with a vegetable prepared with oil;
all things without soul are pure.

9 But you, O virgin, let no one learn of your ascesis, not even of your own
kinsfolk; but if you do anything, do it in secret; and your Father who is in
heaven, who sees in secret, will repay you; but if you make your life
manifest, vainglory is born to you and it damages; but if you find a soul of
one mind, laboring for God, as you also do, to this one alone reveal it in
mystery; there there is no vainglory; for you have spoken, that a soul may be
saved; you will receive a great reward, if a soul is saved through you. To
those who have a desire to hear, speak the things that profit. But if one
hears and does not do, speak nothing. For the Lord says: "Do not give the holy
things to the dogs, nor cast your pearls before the swine"; for God calls dogs
and swine those who live in a dishonorable life; for the words of God are
precious pearls, given to the worthy alone.

10 O blessed soul, that has heard these words written in this book and does
them! I bear witness to everyone who hears these words and does them, that his
name shall be written in the book of life, and he shall be found in the third
rank of the angels. If you pray or chant or read, sit by yourself; let no one
hear, except you alone. And if you have one or two virgins of one soul; for
Christ says: "Where two or three are gathered in my name, there I am in the
midst of them." Put away the womanly mind and take courage and manliness. For
in the kingdom of the heavens there is not male and female, but all the women
who have been well-pleasing receive the rank of men. Forget the youthful
fashions, that you may receive the honor of the good widow. For the holy Paul
says: "Honor widows, those who are truly widows; but she who is truly a widow
and left alone has hoped in God and continues in supplications and prayers
night and day. But she who lives in luxury is dead while she lives. Let a
widow be enrolled not less than sixty years, having been the wife of one
husband, if she has brought up children, if she has lodged strangers, if she
has washed the feet of saints, if she has relieved the afflicted, if she has
followed after every good work. But refuse younger widows; for when they grow
wanton against Christ, they wish to marry, having condemnation because they
have set aside the first faith."

11 But you, if you do not do the youthful fashions, you are not called
younger, but you are even called an elder and you have honor as an older
woman; let the substance of your garments not be costly. Let your outer
garment be black, not dyed in dye, but of its own natural color or of onyx
hue; and the maphorion without fringe, likewise of the same color; and woolen
sleeves covering the arms as far as the fingers of the hands, the hairs of the
head cut round, and a woolen headband, binding the head, and a hood and a
scapular without fringe. And if you meet a man, let your face be covered,
bending down, and you shall not lift your face to a man, except only to your
God. When you stand for prayer have your feet covered in sandals; for this
very attire is priestly; you shall not undress naked; and night and day let
your garment be covering your flesh. Another woman shall not see your body
naked without any necessity; nor shall you yourself consider yourself with the
body uncovered. For from the time you appointed yourself to practice
continence for God, your body is sanctified and a temple of God. It is not
right therefore that the temple of God be uncovered by anyone. You shall not
go to a bathhouse while healthy without any necessity, you shall not dip your
whole body in water, because you are holy to the Lord God; and you shall not
defile your flesh in any worldly thing, but only wash your face, and your
hands and your feet; when you wash your face, you shall not wash with the two
hands nor shall you rub the cheeks of your face nor shall you put herb nor
nitre nor the like of these. For worldly women do these things; but you shall
wash in clean water.

12 You shall not anoint your body with costly myrrh, nor shall you put
precious spices upon your garments. And if your body becomes weaker, use a
little wine for the stomach. And if, which may it not be, you fall into
affliction, take care of yourself; do not give place to men to say that this
affliction came about from her ascesis, but before anyone says it to you, take
thought for yourself, until you rise quickly, and again draw your rule.
Continue the whole time of your life in fastings and prayers and almsgivings.
Blessed is he who hears these things. Night and day let the word of God not
depart from your mouth. And let your work always be meditation of the divine
Scriptures. Have a psalter and learn the psalms. Let the rising sun see the
book in your hands, and after the third hour complete the synaxis, because at
this hour the wood of the cross was fixed; at the sixth hour complete the
prayers with psalms and weeping and supplication, because at that hour the Son
of God was hung upon a cross; at the ninth hour again in hymns and doxologies
with tears, confessing your trespasses, beseech God, because at that hour the
Lord hanging upon a cross gave up the spirit. And after the synaxis of the
ninth eat your bread, having given thanks to God at your table thus: Blessed
is God who nourishes me from my youth, "who gives food to all flesh"; fill my
heart with joy and gladness, that always having all sufficiency we may abound
unto every good work in Christ Jesus our Lord, with whom to you be glory,
honor, power with the Holy Spirit unto the ages of the ages, amen.

13 And when you sit at the table and come to break the bread, having sealed it
thrice, giving thanks thus say: We give thanks to you, our Father, for your
holy resurrection; for through Jesus your child you made it known to us; and
as this bread existing scattered upon this table and having been gathered
became one, so let your church be gathered from the ends of the earth into
your kingdom, because yours is the power and the glory unto the ages of the
ages, amen. And if two or three virgins are present with you, let them give
thanks over the bread set forth and let them pray together. But if a
catechumen is found at the table, let her not pray together with the faithful,
nor shall you sit to eat your bread with her, nor again shall you sit to eat
your morsel with more careless women and jesters without necessity; for you
are holy to the Lord God, and your food and your drink are sanctified; for
through the prayers and the holy words it is sanctified. Devout and
God-fearing virgins shall eat with you; you shall not dine with proud women,
nor shall you acquire a boastful woman as a friend. For the divine Scripture
says: "He who touches pitch shall be defiled, and he who has fellowship with a
proud person shall be likened to him." When a rich woman is sitting with you
at the table, if you see a poor woman, you shall call her to eat and you shall
not be ashamed before the rich woman. Do not love the glory of men above the
glory of God; for God is of the poor and of the despised. Blessed is the soul
that keeps these things.

14 It is not good for a younger woman to keep company with a younger;
otherwise they do nothing good; for one disobeys the other and one despises
the other. But to be under an older woman is good. For the older woman does
not condescend to the wishes of the younger. Woe to a virgin who is not under
a rule. For she is as a ship not having a helmsman. For with its necks broken,
not having the guide, it is thrown by the waves hither and thither, until it
strikes against a rock and is at once destroyed; so is every virgin not having
one whom she fears. Blessed is the virgin who is under a rule; for she will be
as a vine in paradise bearing fruit; and her husbandman coming cleanses her
branches and waters and nips the rotten herbs around her. And she, having the
one who labors, gives her precious fruit in due season. Keep also and do the
doxologies at the table, and your food and drink shall be sanctified; when
therefore you rise from the table, again giving thanks say thrice: "The Lord
is merciful and compassionate, he has given food to those who fear him. Glory
to the Father and to the Son and to the Holy Spirit." And after the doxology
again complete the prayer saying thus: "God the Almighty and our Lord Jesus
Christ, the name above every name, we give thanks to you and we praise you,
because you have deemed us worthy to partake of your good things, of the
fleshly foods. We pray and we beseech you, Lord, that you may also grant us
the heavenly foods; and grant us to tremble and to fear your fearful and
honorable name, and not to disobey your commandments; place your law and your
ordinances in our hearts, and sanctify our spirit and our soul and our body
through your beloved child, our Lord Jesus Christ, with whom to you be glory,
honor, power unto the ages of the ages, amen."

15 For there are more of the worldly, who are mindless and are fed as the
irrational animals, rising early and seeking whom they will defraud, whom they
will overpower, that they may fill their shameful belly; these do not know how
to glorify God at the table. Concerning these the divine Paul said: "These are
the enemies of the cross of Christ, whose end is destruction, whose god is the
belly, and their glory is in their shame, who mind earthly things; but our
commonwealth is in the heavens." These are worse than both the wild beasts and
the cattle; for the cattle and the wild beasts know God who made them, and
they bless him; but men, who were formed by his hands and who bear his image,
do not know him who made them; but they confess him with their mouth, yet by
their works they deny him; "You believe that there is a God? You do well, and
the demons believe and shudder; faith without works is dead." For what does a
man profit, confessing that there is a God, yet by his evil works denying him?
How does he confess that he has a master, not serving him? For this reason he
hears his lord, and the servants know those who bought them and they honor
them, and we ought to honor him, not in word only but also in work; for our
Lord Jesus Christ himself bore witness in the Gospel saying: "Not everyone who
says to me Lord, Lord, shall enter into the kingdom of the heavens." And
again: "You shall not take the name of the Lord your God in vain." And again
he commanded us saying: "Let everyone who names the name of the Lord depart
from unrighteousness." And do you wish to know that the wild beasts and the
cattle know God, and bless him? Hear the Holy Spirit commanding them in hymns:
"Bless the Lord, you wild beasts and all cattle"; if they did not bless, he
would not have commanded them. And not they only bless God, but also all the
creation that appears and is seen, both, confess him unceasingly.

16 And you therefore, handmaid of God, whether you rise, or sit, or do some
work, or eat, or go to sleep upon your bed, or rising, let the hymn of God not
depart from your lips. Blessed are the ears that receive these words. And if
the twelfth hour comes, you shall complete a greater and longer synaxis with
your virgins of one soul; and if you do not have one of one soul, complete it
alone, God being present and hearing. It is good to pour out a tear before
God. Remember the twelfth hour, because at it our Lord went down into Hades;
and seeing him it shuddered and was astonished saying: Who is this, who has
come down in authority and great power? Who is this who crushes the bronze
gates of Hades and has shattered the adamantine bars? Who is this who came
down from the heavens and was crucified and is not held by me, death? Who is
this who looses the bonds of those held by me? Who is this who by his own
death dissolves me, death?

17 For this reason we ought to give heed to ourselves in that hour, and with
tears in the night to beseech the Lord; for the tear is a great virtue, a
great achievement, great sins and lawlessnesses are wiped out through tears,
and the holy Gospel bears witness to me; for when the Savior was delivered to
the Jews, Peter with an oath denied him a third time before a cock crowed. But
the Lord turned and looked at Peter, and Peter remembered the word of the
Lord, as he said to him: "Before a cock crows, you will deny me thrice," and
going out he wept bitterly. You see the medicine of tears, you have seen what
lawlessness it wiped out. For what is worse than this evil, that a third time
with an oath he denied his own Master, and he wiped out so great a lawlessness
through tears. You see how great a power tears have. For those things were
written for our admonition, that we, having followed, may inherit eternal
life. Not the many have the gift of tears, but as many as have the mind above,
as many as forget earthly things, as many as do not make provision for the
flesh, who do not know at all whether there is a world, who have put to death
the members upon the earth; to these alone is given mourning of tears. For
having a pure mind and a sharp-sighted glance of the understanding, still
being upon the earth they see the punishments in Hades and the eternal
tortures, in which the sinners are punished, and the eternal fire and the
outer darkness, the weeping and the gnashing of teeth. And they also see the
heavenly gifts, which God has granted to the saints, and the glories and the
crowns and the holy robes and the royal garments and the luminous treasuries
and the unspeakable delights and the eternal life, and what more shall I say?
And the greatest wonder of all, that he who has a pure mind sees God himself
with the inner eyes. How then will you not wish to weep and to mourn, you who
see these things? For he weeps and laments, that he may be delivered from the
terrible punishments; and again he weeps and asks, praying, that he may be
deemed worthy of those heavenly good things.

18 For this reason the saints hated this world, knowing what good things they
are about to inherit. So then he who has rest in the world, let him not hope
to receive the eternal rest; for the kingdom of the heavens is not of those
who rest here but it is of those who have passed through this life in much
affliction and straitness; for they did not receive it as a gift, but with
great toil and noble sweats those who were deemed worthy acquired it. It does
not concern them, however much they may toil here; for having entered there
they forget the labors and the pains, as many as they suffered in this vain
world, from the much and unspeakable rest given to them. What do you say, O
man? Behold two ways have been set before you, the life and the death;
wherever you wish, go. And behold fire and water; wherever you wish, stretch
out your hand; it is upon you, if you wish to acquire life, and it is upon
you, if you wish to acquire death. Death therefore is the world, and life is
righteousness. Far then is the world from righteousness, as far as death from
life; if therefore you go in the world, you go in death and you become outside
of God according to the divine Scripture. If you go in righteousness, you have
gone in life, and death shall not touch you; for there is not death with the
righteous, but a translation; for he is translated from this world into the
eternal rest; and as one might come out from a prison, so also the saints come
out from this wretched life into the good things prepared for them; "which eye
has not seen and ear has not heard, and upon the heart of man has not come up,
which God has prepared for those who love him"; but the sinners both toil
badly here and there again the fire awaits them; and such must be wept for
doubly, because both here they are in straitness and there they do not receive
the spaciousness. For this reason the divine Scripture has said: "Wherever the
ungodly turns, he vanishes." For things are strait for him from every side;
both there pains, and here afflictions; for there is not a man who does not
toil in this wretched life; both the poor and the rich and the slave and the
free and the sinner and the righteous, all alike toil, and one meeting shall
happen to all, both to the sinner and to the righteous, here in this world.

19 But there it is not so, but another and another order; for other is the
toil of the righteous in this world, and other of the sinner. For the
righteous toils, not that he may fill a belly, nor does he make provision for
the flesh at all, nor does he reckon that he wears flesh; but he toils night
and day seeking God, not being sated with much sleep, not filling the soul
with bread and water, wandering in deserts, bruising the body in much
hardship, until he receives the unfading crown laid up for him. But the sinner
toils and labors not for the sake of righteousness, but for the sake of this
wretched flesh, for the sake of the shameful woman, toiling in this way and
that, not being content with the things present, spending time in wickedness
and envy. But of these things the foolish understand nothing; for matter and
the many cares of the world have blinded them, and they wander, until the
severe soldier is sent upon them. Who does not regard faces nor will he take
gifts. For their souls shall be led with violence by unmerciful angels and
they shall receive their sentence from God. For being empty they also toiled
unto empty things in this world; they worked the things of the earth, for this
reason they also went into destruction. For they did not remember God while
they were upon the earth, nor did it concern them to remember the fear of God;
for this reason neither does it concern him about them. For God is righteous,
and his judgment is righteous; for when he comes to judge the world, then he
will render to each according to his works. Blessed is the heart that receives
these things.

20 At midnight you shall rise, and you shall hymn the Lord your God; for at
that hour our Lord rose from the dead and hymned the Father; for this reason
at that hour it was commanded us to hymn God. And rising first say this verse:
"At midnight I rose to confess to you upon the judgments of your
righteousness"; and pray, and begin to say the whole fiftieth psalm, until you
finish, and let these things be appointed for you each day. And say as many
psalms as you can say standing; and according to each psalm let a prayer and a
kneeling be completed, with tears confessing to the Lord your sins and asking
that they may be forgiven you. And after three psalms say the alleluia. And if

21 And let us keep love, which is greater than all. "You shall love the Lord
your God from your whole heart, and from your whole soul, and your neighbor as
yourself; on these two commandments hang the whole law and the prophets"; God
is love and he first loved man, and he delivered himself up for us, that he
might redeem us from all lawlessness. If therefore our Lord himself died for
us, we also ought to lay down our souls for one another. God is love, and he
who has love has God. For he himself has said: "By this all will know that you
are my disciples, if you love one another"; for however much a man may toil
and not have love toward the neighbor, he has toiled in vain. And thus you
shall show the love toward the neighbor, not in word only but also in work.
You shall not hold wickedness of anyone in your heart; otherwise your prayer
does not go up pure; "for the sun shall not set upon your wrath"; have
meekness, have patience, long-suffering, childlikeness. For the Lord says:
"Unless you turn and become as the children, you shall not enter into the
kingdom of the heavens."

22 You shall not be grieved at any hard thing happening to you, nor shall you
be grieved at a loss, nor at an insult; "for the grief of this world works
death." You shall be grieved only for your sins, but for another small matter
you shall not be grieved. Do not raise your voice being angry toward anyone;
for a handmaid of the Lord ought not to fight. A curse shall not go out of
your mouth, nor insult, nor evil-speaking. For your mouth is sanctified in the
hymns and doxologies of God. It is not good for you to go forth without great
necessity. Love quiet as much as you can. Do not forget the servants of God,
nor let them be wiped out of your heart. If a holy one comes into your house,
receive him thus as the Son of God; for our Lord Jesus Christ says: "He who
receives you, receives me." If a righteous man enters into your house, you
shall meet him with fear and trembling, and you shall bow down before his feet
upon the earth; for you will not worship him, but God who sent him. And you
shall take water and wash his feet and with all reverence you shall hear his
words. You shall not be confident in your chastity, lest you fall; but fear;
for as long as you fear, you never fall. It is profitable for her who
practices continence to eat her own bread alone. If you sit with virgins at a
table, eat everything set forth with them; for if you do not eat, you are
found as condemning them. You shall not announce your ascesis. And if they
also drink wine, and you do not drink, drink a little for their sake. But if
they are great older women and they compel you to drink too much, do not
listen to them, but say to them: you have worn out your youth in much ascesis;
but I have not yet even reached the first step. Concerning hospitality and
almsgiving you have no need to be admonished. For you will do it of yourself.

23 In church be silent, and speak nothing, but attend only to the reading. If
a thought comes up into your heart, that you should do some deed, do not do it
rashly, lest the enemy mock you. Do all things with the counsel of those
greater than you. When you chant or when you pray, do not let foreign thoughts
enter into your heart. I beseech you, beloved, attend and hear these
commandments written in this book; and do not only with the appearing eyes
consider the things written, but also with those within. And attend to each
commandment, and do it. For if you keep these things, you shall be deemed
worthy of the royal bridechamber; do not say in your heart, How can I do these
things? Let not your thought take cowardice; but with all eagerness you shall
keep them; for to those who fear God the commandments of God are not heavy. At
every hour let oil not be lacking to your lamp, lest the bridegroom come and
find it quenched. For you do not know when he comes, whether at first sleep or
in the morning. Be therefore ready; that when he comes, you may meet him with
the prudent having the oil in your lamp, that is your good works. Every hour
remember your departure; have death before your eyes each day; remember before
whom you must stand.

24 Ascesis is heavy and continence hard to bear; but nothing is sweeter than
the heavenly bridegroom. Here we toil a little, but there we shall receive the
eternal life; for the holy Paul says: "The sufferings of the present time are
not worthy to be compared with the glory about to be revealed to us." It is
good to flee from a crowd and to withdraw alone. Continence is a great virtue,
chastity is a great boast, great are the praises of virginity. O virginity,
incomprehensible wealth! O virginity, unfading crown! O virginity, temple of
God and dwelling-place of the Holy Spirit! O virginity, precious pearl, hidden
among many, but found by few alone! O continence, friend of God and praised
among saints! O continence, hated by many, but known to those worthy of you! O
continence, fleeing death and Hades, and held by immortality! O continence,
joy of prophets, and boast of apostles! O continence, life of angels, and
crown of holy men! Blessed is he who holds you, blessed is he who perseveres
in your endurance; because having toiled a little he shall rejoice much in
you. Blessed is he who has fasted this whole time, because dwelling in the
Jerusalem above he shall dance with angels and shall rest with holy prophets
and apostles.

25 These things I have written to you, beloved sister, dancer of Christ, for
the strengthening and benefit of your soul. Do not therefore turn aside from
these things that are said, to the right or to the left. For whoever hears
these words and despises, there is a great judgment for him. But you, most
honored sister who acquire this book, may God grant you to keep these things,
to walk in them with the understanding enlightened, the mind pure, the eyes of
the understanding enlightened, that you may receive the unfading crown, which
God has prepared for those who love him through our Lord and Savior Jesus
Christ, to whom be the glory unto the ages of the ages, amen.
