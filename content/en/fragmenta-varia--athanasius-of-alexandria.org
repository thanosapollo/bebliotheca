* Various Fragments

Of the same (Athanasius) against Valentinus, being in many things in agreement
with the heretics shown before.
How do you say Christ has been wrapped in a heavenly flesh, and not a human,
although the Apostle says: He who sanctifies and those who are sanctified are
all from one? For which cause he is not ashamed, he says, to call them brothers,
saying: I will announce your name to my brothers. And from one, of whom? Of Adam
clearly, according to the genealogy of Luke; and again: Since the children have
partaken of flesh and blood, he himself similarly held of the same. And taking
away also the supposition of a heavenly flesh, he added: For he does not surely
take hold of angels, but of seed of Abraham; whence he ought to have been made
like the brothers in all things. And many such things. But the Lord, they say,
has said in the Gospels: No one has gone up into heaven, except he who came down
from heaven, the Son of man, who is in heaven; and the Apostle again: The first
man from earth, earthy; the second man, the Lord, from heaven heavenly. For
behold from both sides he is named a heavenly man. Toward whom we will say that
since Christ, being double in the natures, is unique as to the hypostasis, he
makes the things of each nature his own. And as the Apostle, having said
concerning the Christ-killers that: If they had known, they would not have
crucified the Lord of glory, does not say the Godhead has suffered, which is
Lord of glory, but the taking-on; so also upon these sayings the Son himself and
God is said to have suffered, because of the one hypostasis of the Godhead,
which also became a hypostasis to the human nature; and to have suffered
according to his human nature, and to have come down from heaven, and to be in
heaven, according to the hypostasis of the Godhead; and to be Son of man
according to the nature later taken on, after the descent from heaven. And again
the same Lord because of the one hypostasis; second man according to the united
humanity, and from heaven according to the Godhead; for if we do not understand
each fittingly, we will subject the Apostle to contradictions. For how is he
like us the earthy in all things? And how is he our brother, who has been
wrapped in a heavenly flesh? And other myriads of unfitting things will follow.
