more ought you to do this over our good things. For if your achievements are

For patience appears from much time, not in two and three days. And he does

patience. What kind, then, is this? That which is through persecutions. And
that he hints at this is clear from what he adds and says: In all your
persecutions and the afflictions which you endure. For they were living with
enemies continually trying to injure them from every side, and they displayed
a patience firm and unmoved. Let those be ashamed who, for the patronage of
men, shift over to other doctrines. For while the preaching was still at its
beginning, poor men living from daily labor took on enmities from men in
public life and the first of the city, when there was nowhere a king, nor a
magistrate, who was a believer, and they underwent an undeclared war, and not
even so were they shaken.

A token of the righteous judgment of God. See how he gathers consolation for
them. He said that We give thanks to God, he said that We glory among men.
These things too are good. But what the one in pain seeks most is this:
deliverance from evils, and punishment of those who treat them badly. For when
the soul is weak, it seeks these things most. For the philosophic soul does
not even seek this. What then is it that he says, A token of the righteous
judgment of God? Already he has hinted at the recompense on both sides, both
that of those who do evil and that of those who suffer it: as if he said, so
that, when he crowns you and punishes them, the justice of God may be shown.
At the same time he also consoles them, showing that from their own labors and
sweats and according to the principle of righteousness they are crowned. But
first he sets down their part. For even if someone vehemently desires
vengeance, still he first longs for the prizes. For this reason he also adds,
saying: That you may be counted worthy of the kingdom of God, for which you
also suffer. So then this does not happen because those who do injustice are
more powerful, but because it is necessary to enter the kingdom in this way.
For through many tribulations, he says, we must enter into the kingdom of God.
If so be that it is righteous with God to recompense affliction to those who
afflict you, and to you who are afflicted rest with us, at the revelation of
the Lord Jesus from heaven with angels of his power. The "if so be" here
stands for "since," which we too put in the case of things strongly admitted
and indisputable: that is, exceedingly righteous. If it is righteous, he says,
with God to take vengeance on these, he will certainly take vengeance: as if
he said, if God cares about affairs, if God takes thought. So he himself also
puts this "if so be" as upon things admitted: as if someone said, if God hates
the wicked: speaking thus for this reason, that he may compel them to say that
he hates them. For such verdicts especially are indisputable: as even those
men themselves know that it is righteous. For if this is righteous among men,
much more with God. To recompense, he says, affliction to those who afflict
you, and to you who are afflicted rest with us.

3. What then? Is the recompense equal? By no means. But see through what
follows how he shows it to be more vehement, and the rest much greater. See
also another consolation =62.476=: for he says that those who are partners of
the afflictions have partners also in the recompense (this


is what "With us" means): joining them in the crowns with those who have
accomplished countless and greater things. Then he also adds the time, and
through the description he leads their minds upward, all but opening the
heavens already by the word, and setting them before their eyes, and he places
around him the angelic host, and from the place and from those with him he
makes the image broader, so that they may breathe a little. And to you who are
afflicted rest with us, he says, at the revelation of the Lord Jesus from
heaven with angels of his power, in flaming fire, giving vengeance to those
who do not know God, and to those who do not obey the Gospel of our Lord Jesus
Christ. If those who do not obey the Gospel pay a penalty, those who besides
not obeying also afflict you, what will they not suffer? But see his
intelligence with me: he did not say here, to those who afflict you, but, To
those who do not obey. So that even if not on your account, on his own account
it is necessary, he says, to punish them. This then has been said for full
assurance, that they must certainly be punished. But the former was said so
that they too might be honored. Belief, then, is produced by the full
assurance concerning the punishment. Pleasure is produced by the fact that
they suffer these things for what has been done to them. These things have
been said to those people. But they also fit us. When then we are in
afflictions, let us reckon these things. Let us not rejoice at the punishment
of others as being avenged, but as ourselves being delivered from such
chastisement and punishment. For what profit is it to us when others are
punished? Let us not, I beseech you, have such souls; let us be urged toward
virtue from the kingdom. For the one who is exceedingly virtuous is led
neither from fear nor from the kingdom, but for Christ's sake himself, as Paul
did. Still, let us consider the goods in the kingdom, the evils in Gehenna,
and even thus let us regulate and school ourselves, thus let us lead ourselves
toward the things to be done. When you see something useful and great in the
present life, think of the kingdom, and you will count it nothing. When you
see something fearful, think of Gehenna, and you will laugh at it. When you
are held by bodily desire, reckon the fire, reckon also the pleasure of the
sin itself, that it is worth nothing, that it does not even have pleasure. For
if the fear of the laws laid down here has such strength as even to draw us
away from wicked deeds, much more the memory of the things to come, the
immortal punishment, the everlasting chastisement. If the fear of the king on
earth draws us away from so many evils, how much more that of the eternal
King? How then can we have the fear continually? If we continually hear the
Scriptures. For if a dead man merely seen so contracts our mind, how much more
Gehenna and the unquenchable fire? How much more the worm that does not end?
If we always reason about Gehenna, we will not quickly fall into it. For this
reason God threatened chastisement: as, if reckoning it had no great gain, God
would not already have threatened it. But since the memory of it has power to
accomplish great things, for this reason, like some saving medicine, he laid
the threat of it in our souls. Let us not then overlook so great a gain
=62.477= born from this, but let us turn this over continually, at lunches, at
dinners. For conversation about pleasant things does not benefit the soul at
all, but makes it more slack. Conversation about painful and gloomy things
cuts off all that is dissolved and flowing in it, and turns it and contracts
it when it is loosened. He who converses about theaters and mimes has not
helped it at all, but has even kindled it more and made it more rash. He who
is anxious about other people's affairs and busybodying has often even rubbed
dangers onto it from this curiosity. But he who converses about Gehenna will
have no danger, and he makes it more sober. But you have feared the


burden of the words? Have you then, if you are silent, quenched Gehenna? Have
you, if you speak, kindled it? Whether you speak or do not speak, the fire
will boil. Let it be spoken of continually concerning it, so that you may
never fall into it. It is not possible for a soul anxious about Gehenna to sin
quickly. For hear the best advice: Remember, he says, your last things, and
you will never sin forever. It is not possible for a soul fearing about
accounts not to be slow about faults. For fear flourishing in the mind lets
nothing worldly be in it. For if a word moved about it so humbles and
contracts, will a reasoning dwelling continually in the souls not cleanse a
soul more than any fire? Let us not remember the kingdom so much as Gehenna.
For fear has more strength than the promise. And I know that many would have
despised countless goods, if they were delivered from the chastisement: seeing
that even now it is enough for me not to be punished, not to be chastised. No
one of those who have Gehenna before their eyes will fall into Gehenna. No one
of those who despise Gehenna will escape Gehenna. For just as among us those
who fear the courts will not be caught by courts, while those who despise them
are especially those who fall into them: so also there. If the Ninevites had
not feared the overthrow, they would have been overthrown. But since they
feared, they were not overthrown. If in the time of Noah they had feared the
flood, they would not have been flooded. And if the Sodomites had feared, they
would not have been burned. It is a great evil to despise a threat. He who
despises a threat will quickly feel the trial through the facts. Nothing is so
useful as to converse about Gehenna. It makes our souls purer than any silver.
For hear the Prophet saying that Your judgments are before me continually. And
Christ also converses about it continually. For even if it grieves the hearer,
still it benefits him the most.

4. For such are all things that benefit. And do not wonder. For medicines and
foods too, after first grieving the sick man, then bring the benefit. But if
we cannot bear the heaviness of words, it is quite clear that we will not bear
the affliction through facts. If no one bears the words about Gehenna, it is
quite clear that, if persecution overtook us, no one would ever have stood
toward fire, toward iron. Let us train our hearing not to grow soft. For from
this we will also come to the facts. If we are habituated to hear fearful
things, we will also be habituated to endure fearful things. But if we are so
loosened as not even to bear words, when will we stand toward the facts
=62.478=? Do you see how the blessed Paul despises all things here, and the
successive dangers? Why? Because he had practiced even to despise Gehenna for
the things that seem good to God. That man did not even think the trial of
Gehenna to be anything because of the longing for Christ. We, for the things
that profit us, do not even bear the words about it. Now then, having heard a
little, you go away. But I beg you, if there is any love in you, to turn over
the words about these things continually. They will not be able to harm, even
if they do not benefit. But they will certainly also benefit. For the soul is
also formed toward the conversations. Evil communications, he says, corrupt
good manners. Therefore good communications benefit. Therefore fearful
communications also make sober. For the soul is a kind of wax. For if you
bring on cold communications, you have hardened it and made it stiff. If you
bring on fiery ones, you have softened it. And having softened it, you shape
it toward whatever you wish, and you engrave the royal image. Let us then
block our ears toward the communications that are in vain. The evil is not
small. From this all the evils come. If our mind had practiced attending to
divine words, it would not have attended to others. And not attending to
others, it would not even have gone after the wicked deeds. For word is a road
toward the works. First we think, then we speak, then we act. Many


often even while being sober have come from shameful words to shameful deeds.
For our soul is neither good by nature nor evil, but by choice it becomes both
this and that. Just as the sail, wherever the wind blows, carries the ship: or
rather just as the helm carries the ship, if the wind is from a fair quarter:
so also the reasoning, if the good words are borne from a fair quarter, will
sail without danger. If they are contrary, it will often even sink the
reasoning. For what the winds are to ships, this the words are to souls.
Wherever you wish, you transfer it and turn it. For this reason someone
advising says: Let every narrative of yours be in the law of the Most High.
Therefore I beseech you, taking the children from the nurse, let us not
accustom them to old wives' myths, but from first age let them learn that
there is a judgment: that there is chastisement, let it be fixed in their
mind. This fear, rooted together, works great goods. For a soul that has
learned from first age to be shaken by this expectation will not quickly shake
off this dread. But like some obedient horse, having the reasoning of Gehenna
sitting upon it, walking in good order, it will both speak and say useful
things. And neither youth, nor wealth, nor orphanhood, nor anything else will
be able to harm it, having so firm a reasoning, able to hold out toward all
things. With these words let us also regulate ourselves, and wives and slaves
and children and friends, if possible even enemies. For with these words we
can cut away most of the sins, and it is better to spend time in painful
things than in pleasant ones. And this is clear from there. For tell me, if
you come into a house where marriages are being performed, for a while you are
pleased at the sight, but later going away you melt yourself with grief, that
you do not have so much. But if you enter a house of mourners, even if they
are exceedingly rich, going away you will rest more. For =62.479= you have not
received envy from there, but consolation and comfort over your poverty. You
have seen through the facts that wealth is no good, poverty no evil, but they
are indifferent affairs. So also now if you converse about luxury, you rather
strike the soul that perhaps does not have luxury. But when you speak against
luxury, and throw the words of Gehenna into the midst, the thing delights you,
and it will breed much pleasure. For when you consider that luxury will not be
able to help us at all toward that fire, you will not seek it. And if you
reckon that it is even accustomed to kindle it more, you will not only not
seek it, but you will also turn away and beat it off. Let us not then flee the
words about Gehenna, so that we may flee Gehenna. Let us not flee the memory
of the chastisement, so that we may not be chastised. If that rich man had
thought of that fire, he would not have sinned. But since he never remembered
it, for this reason he fell into it. Tell me, O man, being about to stand at
the tribunal of Christ, do you speak everything rather than about this? And
having a matter toward a judge, often only as far as words, you speak of
nothing else, not night, not day, not season, not hour, but continually about
that matter. But being about to give an account of your whole life =62.480=
and to undergo audits, you cannot even bear others reminding you about the
trial? Therefore for this reason everything has been ruined and corrupted,
because, being about to stand at a human tribunal concerning affairs of life,
we move everything, we ask everyone, we are anxious about this continually, we
do everything for this: but being about to come to the tribunal of Christ not
long from now, we do nothing either through ourselves or through others, we do
not ask the judge. And yet that one gives us much time, and does not snatch us
away in the midst of


the sins, but that goodness and lovingkindness leaves nothing undone of the
things that belong to himself. But nothing more happens. For this reason the
punishment is greater. But may it not come to be. Therefore, I beseech you,
even if only now let us sober up, let us have Gehenna before our eyes, let us
think of those inexorable accounts: so that, reckoning these things, we may
both flee vice and choose virtue, and may be able to obtain the goods promised
to those who love him, by grace and lovingkindness.

HOMILY III.
Who shall pay a penalty, everlasting destruction from the face of the Lord,
and from the glory of his strength, when he comes to be glorified in his
saints, and to be marveled at in all who have believed.

1. There are many men who have their good hopes not in abstaining from
sins, but in thinking that Gehenna is not vehement as it is said, but milder
than what has been threatened, and temporary, not everlasting, and they
philosophize much about this. I for my part have it to show from many places
not only that it is not milder than what has been threatened, but that it is
also much more vehement, and to reckon it even from the very words about
Gehenna. Still I move no discourse about these things now. For the fear even
from bare words is enough, even if we do not unfold their meaning. But that it
is not temporary, hear Paul now saying concerning those who do not know God,
nor believe in the Gospel, that they shall pay a penalty, everlasting
destruction. How then is the everlasting temporary? From the face, he says, of
the Lord. What is this? What easy thing does he wish to say here? For since
they were then greatly puffed up, being rich, there is no need, he says, of
much trouble, but it is enough for God only to be present and to be seen, and
all come to be in chastisement and punishment. His presence will be light to
some, punishment to others. And from the glory, he says, of his strength, when
he comes to be glorified in his saints, and to be marveled at in all who have
believed. What do you say? Is God glorified? Yes, In all, he says, his saints.
How? For when those who puff greatly see those scourged by them, those
despised, those laughed at, these being near him, it becomes his glory. Or
rather this is glory both of them and of him: of him, because he did not
abandon them, but =62.480= showed them splendid: of them, because they were
counted worthy of so great honor. For just as it is his wealth that there are
faithful, so also it is his glory that there are those who are going to enjoy
his goods. It is the glory of the good one to have those to whom he may impart
the benefaction. And to be marveled at, he says, in all who have believed.
That is, through those who believed. See again the "in" is "through." For
through those he is shown admirable. For when he leads the pitiable and
wretched and those who have suffered countless terrible things, and have
believed, into so great splendor, his strength is shown then, that even if
here they seem to have been abandoned, still there they enjoy much glory. Then
especially all the glory of God is shown and the strength. And how, hear. For
he adds, Because our testimony to you was believed in that day. To which end
we also pray always concerning you. That is, when those who have suffered
countless terrible things are brought into the midst, so as to start aside
from the faith, and have not yielded, but have remained faithful, God is
glorified. Then the glory of these also is shown. Since then many play-act the
faith, bless no one before the end. For also in that day those who have
believed are shown. To which end we also pray, he says, always concerning you,
that our God may count you worthy of the calling,

and may fulfill every good pleasure of goodness, and work of faith in power.
That he may count you, he says, worthy of the calling, showing that many have
also been cast off. For this reason he added, And may fulfill every good
pleasure of goodness. Since even the one who had put on filthy garments was
called, but he did not remain in the calling. For this reason he has even more
become a cast-off from the calling into the bridechamber. Since also the
=62.481= five virgins were called. Arise, he says, the bridegroom is coming.
And they prepared themselves, but they did not enter. Making clear therefore
what calling, he added: And may fulfill every good pleasure of goodness, and
work of faith in power. This calling we seek, he says. See how gently he
brings them down. For so that they may not be made vain by the excess of the
praises, as having accomplished great things, and become slothful, he shows
that something still is lacking to them, as long as they are in this life:
which also writing to the Hebrews he said: You have not yet resisted as far as
blood, struggling against sin. Into every, he says, good pleasure: that is,
pleasing, persuasion, full assurance: as if he said, that the persuasion of
God may come to be, that nothing may be lacking to you, that you may be as he
wishes. And work, he says, of faith in power. What is this? The patience of
the persecutions, that we may not be exhausted, he says. That the name of our
Lord Jesus Christ may be glorified in you, and you in him according to the
grace of our God, and of the Lord Jesus Christ.

2. See: he said glory there, he said it also here. He said that they
themselves are glorified, so as even to boast. He said what was much more,
that they also glorify God. He said that they will receive that glory. But he
also says it here. For when the master is glorified, the slaves also are
glorified. For those who glorify the master are much more themselves
glorified, both by this very thing, and apart from this. For affliction on
account of Christ is glory, and everywhere he calls the thing glory. And by
how much we suffer something dishonorable, by so much more we become splendid.
Then again showing that this very thing also is of God, he says, According to
the grace of our God and of the Lord Jesus Christ: that is, God himself has
given us this grace, that he may be glorified in us, and may glorify us in
him. How is he glorified in us? Because we prefer nothing to him. How are we
glorified in him? Because we have received power from him so as not even to
yield to the terrible things brought on. For at the same time, when a
temptation happens, both God is glorified, and we. For they glorify him,
because he so nerved us. And they marvel at us, because we prepared ourselves
worthy. And all these things come from the grace of God. But we ask you,
brethren, concerning the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ and our gathering
together to him, that you be not quickly shaken from the mind. When the
resurrection will be, he has not said. But that it will not be now, he has
said. And our, he says, gathering together to him. This too is not small. Do
you see how the exhortation also again is with praise and urging, by the Lord
appearing altogether with us ourselves and all the saints? He converses here
about the coming of Christ, and about our gathering together. For these things
will be together. He lifts their mind. That you be not shaken, he says,
quickly. Nor be troubled, neither through spirit, nor through word, nor
through letter, as through us, as that the day of the Lord is present. Here he
seems to me to hint that some go about having forged a letter supposedly from
Paul, and showing this they say that the day of the Lord has come, so that
they may lead many astray from this. So then, that they may not be deceived,
Paul secures them, =62.482= through what he writes and says: Do not be
troubled, neither through spirit, nor through word. And what he means is this:
even if someone having a spirit of prophecy says this, do not believe. For
when I was with you, I said these things to you. So you


must not be persuaded otherwise, from the things you were taught. Or through
spirit. Thus he calls the false prophets speaking by an unclean spirit the
things they spoke. For these, wishing to be believed more, were attempting to
deceive not only through persuasive speech. For this he shows saying, Neither
through word. But they also displayed a forged letter as from Paul, declaring
this same thing. For this reason, showing this also, he added, Neither through
letter, as

through us. Having then secured them from every side, thus he sets out his own
judgment, and says: Let no one deceive you in any way, because unless the
apostasy come first, and the man of sin be revealed, the son of perdition, the
one who opposes and exalts himself over every so-called god or object of
worship, so as to sit himself in the temple of God as God, showing himself
that he is God. He converses here about the antichrist, and reveals great
mysteries. What is the apostasy? He calls the antichrist himself apostasy, as
going to destroy many and make them stand off: So that, he says, if possible
even the elect would be scandalized. And he calls him a man of sin. For he
will work countless things and will prepare others to work terrible things.
And he says he is a son of perdition because he himself also is destroyed. But
who is this? Is it Satan? By no means. But some man receiving all his energy.
And the man shall be revealed, he says, who exalts himself over every
so-called god or object of worship. For that one will not lead to idolatry,
but he will be some opponent of God, and he will dissolve all the gods, and
will command to worship him instead of God, and he will sit in the temple of
God, not that in Jerusalem only, but also in the Churches everywhere. Showing,
he says, himself God. He did not say, saying, but, trying to show. For he will
also work great works, and will display marvelous signs. Do you not remember
that while still being with you I said these things to you?

3. Do you see that it is necessary to say the same things continually, and to
pour on with the same words? For see, even when he was present they heard him
saying these things, and again they needed a reminder. For just as having
heard about the afflictions, For even when we were, he says, with you, we told
you beforehand that we are going to be afflicted: still they were forgotten,
and again he steadies them through the letters: so also having heard about the
coming of Christ, they again needed letters that regulate them. He reminds
them then, and shows that he says nothing strange, but the things he always
said. For just as in the case of farmers the seeds are thrown down once, but
they do not remain continually, but they need much preparation, and if they do
not, having stirred up the earth, wrap the things thrown down, they have sown
for the seed-picking birds: so also we, if we do not wrap the things thrown
down by continual memory, have thrown everything into the air. For the devil
also snatches away, and our sloth destroys, and sun dries, and =62.483= rain
floods, and thorns choke. So it is not enough, having thrown down once, to be
rid of it, but much care and attendance is needed for the one who is going to
gather the fruits, driving off the birds, cutting out the thorns, filling the
rocky places with much earth, preventing every damage and walling it off and
taking it away. But in the case of the earth everything is of the farmer. For
that lies soulless, being ready only to suffer. But in the case of this
spiritual earth, by no means. Not everything is of the teachers, but, if not
the more, at least the half is also of the disciples. It is ours then to throw
down the seed, yours to do the things said, to show the fruits by memory
through the works, to pull up the thorns by the roots. For wealth is truly a
thorn, having no fruit, ugly even to be seen, and unpleasant to use,


grieving those who touch it, not only itself having no fruit, but also
hindering what is sprouting. Such is wealth: not only itself does not bear
everlasting fruit, but also holding those who wish it hinders them. Thorns are
foods of irrational things, of camels. They are food of fire and expense
useful for nothing. Such also is wealth, useful for nothing, except to burn a
furnace, to kindle that day that burns as an oven, to feed the irrational
passions, remembering of wrongs and anger. For such also is the camel that
eats the thorns. For it is said by those who know such things that there is
nothing among the cattle so heavy-tempered and sullen and remembering of
wrongs as the camel. Such is wealth. It feeds the irrational passions of the
soul, but it pricks and wounds the rational ones, as in the case of thorns.
This plant is something hard and rough, it grows of itself. But let us also
see where it grows, so that we may cut it out: in the precipitous parts of the
fields, in the stony, in the dry, where there is no moisture. When then
someone is rough and precipitous, that is, unmerciful, the thorn grows in him.
But when the farmer's children wish to cut them out, they do not do this with
iron, but how? Sending fire upon them, thus they draw out all the evil of the
earth. For since it is not enough to cut from above, the root remaining below,
nor even to take out the root (for from the evil it remains in the earth, and
as when some plague has fallen into a body the remnants still remain), the
fire, drawing up from above all that moisture of the thorns, like some poison,
draws it out through the heat from the flanks of the earth. For just as the
cupping-glass, being applied, draws all evil toward itself: so also the fire
draws up all evil from the thorns, and makes it clean. For what reason then
have I said these things? Because it is necessary to cleanse the sympathy
toward wealth from every side. There is also among us a fire, drawing this
evil from a soul, I mean that of the spirit. If we send this upon it, we will
be able to dry up not only the thorns, but also the moisture from them. As, if
they are fixed in, everything becomes in vain. For see: a rich man has entered
here, or also a rich =62.484= woman? She does not care how she will hear the
oracles of God, but how she will display herself, how she will sit with pomp,
how with much glory, how she will surpass all the others by the costliness of
the garments, and by figure and glance and gait she will make herself more
solemn, and all her care and anxiety is, Did so-and-so see? Did she marvel?
Have I been well adorned? And she does not make the care about this only, but
so that the garments may not rot, so that they may not be torn, and all the
care is about this. Likewise also the rich man enters to show himself to the
poor man, and to set him in fear through the wrapping of the garments, through
having many servants. And they stand by driving off. But the one from much

HOMILY IV.
And now you know that which restrains, that he may be revealed in his own
time. For the mystery of lawlessness is already at work, only he that
restrains now until he comes to be out of the midst: and then the lawless one
will be revealed, whom the Lord Jesus will consume with the spirit of his
mouth, and will bring to nought by the appearing of his coming: whose coming
is according to the energy of Satan.

1. Someone would reasonably seek first what that which restrains ever is, and
after these things learn why ever Paul sets it down so unclearly. What then
ever is That which restrains him being revealed, that is, that which hinders?
Some say the grace of the Spirit, others the Roman rule: to whom I most of all
accede. Why? Because if he wished to say the spirit, he would not have said it
unclearly, but clearly, that even now the grace of the Spirit holds him, that
is, the gifts. And otherwise he ought already to have come, if he was going to
come when the gifts failed. For they have failed long ago. But since he says
this about the Roman rule, he reasonably hinted, and for now he speaks
shadowed. For he did not wish to take on superfluous enmities and useless
dangers. For if he had said that a little later the rule of the Romans would
be dissolved, they would already immediately even have buried him as a pest,
and all the faithful, as living and campaigning for this. For this reason he
did not say thus, nor that it will be quickly, although he always says it. But
what? That he may be revealed in his own time, he says. For the mystery of
lawlessness is already at work. He speaks here of Nero, as being a type of the
antichrist. For this one also wished to be thought a god. And he well said the
mystery. For it is not openly, as that one, =62.486=.30 nor shamelessly. For
if before that time there was found, he says, one who was not far behind the
antichrist in wickedness, what wonder if there will already be one? Thus then
he spoke shadowed, and he did not wish to make him clear, not through
cowardice, but teaching us not to take on superfluous enmities, when there is
nothing pressing. Thus then he also says here: Only he that restrains now
until he comes to be out of the midst: that is, when the Roman rule is lifted
out of the midst, then that one will come. And reasonably. For as long as the
fear of this rule exists, no one will quickly be subjected. But when this is
dissolved, he will attack the anarchy, and will attempt to snatch both the
rule of men and that of God. For just as the kingdoms before this were
dissolved, for example that of the Medes by the Babylonians, that of the
Babylonians by Persians, that of the Persians by Macedonians, that of the
Macedonians by Romans: so also this by the antichrist, and that one by Christ,
and he will no longer hold. And these things Daniel deposits for us with much
clarity. And then, he says, the lawless one will be revealed. And what after
these things? The consolation is near. For he adds: Whom the Lord Jesus, he
says, will consume with the spirit of his mouth, and will bring to nought by
the appearing of his coming, whose coming is according to the energy of Satan.
For just as fire simply coming on makes the little animals even before its
presence, being far off, grow numb and consumes them: so also Christ by the
command only and by the presence will consume the antichrist. It is enough


for him to be present, and all these things have been ruined. He will stop the
deceit =62.487= even having appeared only. Then making clear who this is,
Whose coming is according to the energy of Satan: In all power, he says, and
signs and wonders of a lie. That is, he will display all power, but nothing
true, but all things toward deceit. He said these things beforehand, so that
those then may not be deceived. And wonders, he says, of a lie: either
falsified, or leading to a lie. And in all deceit of unrighteousness in those
who are perishing. Why then, he says, did God allow this to happen, and what
economy is this? And what is the gain of that one's coming, arriving for our
ruin? Do not fear, beloved, but hear him saying: He has strength in those who
are perishing, who, even if that one had not come, would not have been
persuaded. What then is the gain, he says? That these very ones who are
perishing will be silenced. How? Because even if that one had not come, they
would not have believed in Christ. He comes then to convict them. For so that
they may not be going to say that Since Christ said he was God, and yet he
nowhere said it openly, still since those after these things preached, we did
not believe: since we heard that there is one God from whom are all things,
for this reason we did not believe: this pretext of theirs then the Antichrist
will take away. For when that one comes, and commanding nothing sound, but all
things unlawful, is believed from false signs only, he will sew up their
mouth. For if you do not believe in Christ, much more you ought not to have
believed in the Antichrist. For that one said he had been sent from the
Father, but this one the contrary. For this reason Christ said, I came in the
name of the Father, and you did not receive me. If another comes in his own
name, that one you will receive. But we saw signs, he says. But also in the
case of Christ many and great things happened. Therefore much more you ought
to have believed in him. And yet concerning this one many things were said
beforehand, that he is the lawless one, that he is the son of perdition, that
his coming is according to the energy of Satan. But concerning that one the
contraries, that he is Savior, that he brings countless goods. Because they
did not receive the love of the truth so as to be saved, for this reason God
will send them an energy of error, so that they may believe the lie: that all
may be judged who did not believe the truth, but took pleasure in the
unrighteousness.

2. That they may be judged. He did not say, That they may be
chastised. For
even without this they were going to be chastised, but, That they may be
judged, that is, condemned at the fearful court, so as to be without defense.
And who are these? He himself interpreted, adding, Those who did not believe
the truth, but took pleasure in the unrighteousness. And he calls Christ love
of truth. Because, he says, they did not receive the love of the truth. For
both things were, and he came for both, both loving men and for true affairs.
But taking pleasure, he says, in the unrighteousness. For he came for the ruin
of men, and to do them injustice. For what will he not work then? He will move
all things, he will trouble all things, both for the sake of commands and of
fear. He will be fearful from every side, from the authority, from the
cruelty, from the unlawful commands. But do not fear. In those who are
perishing, he says, he will have the strength. For Elijah also will come then,
securing the faithful. And this Christ says, Elijah comes and will restore all
things. For this reason it has been said about John, In =62.488= power and
spirit of Elijah. For he neither worked signs, as Elijah, nor wonders. For

the mind of the hearers, he no longer made them think the present things

HOMILY V.
But the Lord is faithful, who will establish you, and will guard you from the
evil one. And we have trusted in the Lord upon you that the things we command
you, you both do and will do. And the Lord direct your hearts into the love of
God and into the patience of Christ.

1. Neither having committed the whole to the prayers of the saints ought they
to be idle, and run toward wickedness, and lay hold of none of the things that
bear toward virtue. =62.492= Nor again, working the good things, ought they to
despise that alliance. For great things it can do, great is the prayer on our
behalf, but when we also work. For this reason Paul also, praying for them,
again sets down the trustworthiness from the promise, and says: But the Lord
is faithful, who will establish you, and will guard you from the evil one. For
if he chose you unto salvation, he will not lie, nor will he let you perish
altogether. But so that he may not


through these =62.493= things lead them into sloth, and thinking the whole to
be of God they themselves sleep, see how he also demands the cooperation from
them, saying: And we have trusted in the Lord upon you, that the things we
command, you both do and will do. Faithful then, he says, is God, and
promising to save, he will certainly save, but as he promised. And how did he
promise? If we wish, and hear him: not simply, nor, as woods and stones, being
idle. And well he has also set down, We have trusted in the Lord, that is, We
trust his lovingkindness. Again he brings them down, hanging the whole there.
For if he had said that We believe you, the praise was great, but he would not
have shown them hanging all things upon God. And if he had said, We have
trusted in the Lord, that he will guard you, and had not added Upon you, and,
The things we commanded you do and will do, he would have made them more idle,
having thrown the whole upon the power of God. For it is necessary to throw
the whole upon him, but ourselves also working, having stepped into the labors
and the contests. And he shows here that even if virtue alone is also enough
to save, still it must be lasting, and be present with us until we come to
last breaths. And may the Lord direct your hearts into the love of God, and
into the patience of Christ. He praises them again, and prays, showing his
care. For since he is going to step into rebuke, with these he smooths their
hearts beforehand, both by saying that I am confident that you will hear, and
by demanding prayers from them, and by again praying countless goods upon
them. And may the Lord direct, he says, your hearts into the love of God. For
many are the things that turn aside from the love, and many are the paths that
draw out from there. And first the wickedness of mammon, sending as it were
some shameless hands upon our soul, and taking hold tightly, draws and drags
even those not wishing from there. Then vainglory, and afflictions often and
temptations have turned us. For this reason, like some wind, we need the help
of God, so that our sail may be sent along as by some vehement wind toward the
love of God. For do not tell me that I love him even beyond myself. These are
words. Show through the works, if you love him beyond yourself. Love beyond
the silver, and then I believe that you also love beyond yourself. But he who
does not despise money on account of God, how will you despise yourself? And
why do I speak of money? He who does not despise greed, which one ought to do
even without the commands of God, how will you despise yourself? And into the
patience, he says, of Christ. What is, Into the patience? That we may endure,
as that one endured, or that we may do those things, or also that with
patience we may wait for him, that is, that we may be prepared. For since he
promised many things, and he himself comes to judge living and dead, let us
wait for him and endure. And wherever he says patience, he certainly hints at
affliction. For this is to love God, to endure, and not to be troubled. And we
command you, brethren, in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, that you withdraw

For this is, In the name =62.494= of our Lord Jesus Christ. Having said thus,
he shows the fearfulness of the command. Through Christ, he says, we command.
So then Christ nowhere ordered to be idle. That you withdraw, he says, from
every brother. Do not tell me the rich man, do not tell me the poor, do not
tell me the holy. This is disorder. Walking, he says, that is, living. And not
according to the tradition which they received from us. He says tradition that
through the works. And properly he always calls this tradition. For you
yourselves know how it is necessary to imitate us,


that we did not act disorderly among you, nor eat bread as a gift from anyone.
And yet, even if I ate, it was not as a gift. For worthy, he says, is the
worker of his hire. But in labor and toil, working night and day so as not to
burden any of you. Not that we do not have authority, but that we may give

commanded this to you, that if anyone does not wish to work, let him not even

which was fearful and having danger, but he says to abound and to be
ambitious, which was of one urging toward virtue. That you may walk
becomingly, he says. But here nothing of this kind, but, If anyone does not
wish to work, he says, let him not even eat. For if Paul, not having
necessity, but having authority to be idle, and having taken up so great a
work, still worked, and did not simply work, but night and day, so as to be
able even to help others, much more the others ought to do this. For we hear
of some walking among you disorderly, working nothing, but being busybodies.
This here. But there in the former, That you may walk becomingly, he says,
toward those outside. For what reason? Perhaps nothing of this kind had yet
happened. For also elsewhere exhorting he said: It is more blessed to give
than to receive. But the, Walk becomingly, is not about disorder. Therefore he
also added: That you may have need of no one. But here he also sets down
another necessity, so as to do the good and useful toward all. For going
forward he says, Do not grow weary doing good. For the one who is idle, and
able to work, must altogether be a busybody. And almsgiving has been given
only to those who cannot suffice themselves from the work of the hands, or to

says work here that through the hands. And so that you may not suspect

such we command and exhort through our Lord Jesus Christ. Since he had laid
hold very strongly, wishing to make the word more gentle, he added: Through

3. For if even to be with many taking is worthy of shame, when they also give
rebuking and withdraw, of how much reproach is this? How is it enough to bite
a soul? For if only giving more sluggishly, or murmuring, they set on fire
those who take (do not tell me about the shameless beggars, but about the
faithful): if they were even going to rebuke giving, what would they not have
worked? Of how much punishment would it not have been worthy? But we not thus,
but as having been most greatly wronged, thus we insult those who beg, we turn
away. You do not give. Why do you also grieve? Admonish, he said, as brothers,
do not insult as enemies. He who admonishes the brother does not do this
publicly, he does not parade the insult, but privately and with much
attention, and grieving, and being bitten, and weeping and wailing. From a
brotherly mind then let us give, from a brotherly =62.496= choice let us
admonish: not as grieving over the giving, but as grieving over that one
transgressing the command. Since what is the gain? For if even after the
giving you insult, you ruin the pleasure of the gift. But when you neither
give and you insult, how much evil have you not worked upon that wretched and
miserable one? He came as going to be pitied by you. And having received the
mortal blow he went away, and wept more. For when he is forced through want to
beg, and through begging is insulted, see how great the punishment of those
who insult will be. He who dishonors a poor man, he says, provokes the one who
made him. For tell me: he himself let him be poor on account of you, so that
you may have to heal yourself, and you insult the one who is poor on account
of you? Of how much ingratitude are these things? Of how much thanklessness is
this work? Admonish, he says, as brothers. And after the giving he ordered to
admonish. But when even without the giving we insult, what defense will there
be for us? And may the Lord of peace himself give you the peace continually in
every place. See how, when he dictates the things to be done, he seals them
with the prayer, as some seals of the things stored, setting down the petition
and
the supplication. May he give you, he says, the peace in every place. For
since from these things it is likely that fights happen, those becoming
harsher, and these no longer likewise providing to such, reasonably he prayed
this now, having said May he give you the peace continually. For this is what
is sought, so as always to have it. What is, In every place? He wishes them to
be at peace from every side, so as to have from nowhere a starting-point of
contention. For everywhere peace is a good, where at least even toward those
outside. For hear him saying elsewhere: If possible, what is from you, be at
peace with all men. For nothing is so fit toward accomplishing what we wish as
the peaceful and untroubled, as to have been delivered from all hatred, and


to have no one as an enemy. The Lord be with all of you. The greeting with my
hand of Paul, which is a sign in every letter. Thus I write. The grace of our
Lord Jesus Christ be with all of you. Amen. He says he writes this in every
letter, so that it may not be possible for anyone to counterfeit them, the
subscription lying as a great proof. And he calls the prayer a greeting,
showing that they then did all things spiritual, and when it was necessary to
greet, the affair was with gain. And a prayer not simply a symbol of
friendship. From this he began, and into this he ended, fencing the things
said on both sides with great walls, both setting secure foundations, and he
added the end secure. Grace to you, he says, and peace. And again, The grace
of our Lord Jesus Christ be with all of you. Amen. This the Lord also promised
saying to the disciples, See, I am with you all the days until the completion
of the age. But this happens when we wish. For he will not altogether be with
us, if we make ourselves far. I will be with you, he says, continually. Let us
not then drive away the grace. He wishes us to withdraw from every brother
walking disorderly. Great then was this evil, to be torn from the fullness of
the brothers. With this then he punishes all, as also =62.497= writing
elsewhere to Corinthians he said: With such a one not even to eat, he says.
But now the more do not think it great, but all things have been confused and
corrupted. With adulterers, with fornicators, with the greedy we mix simply
and as it happens. If it was necessary to withdraw from the one fed idly only,
how much more from the others? And so that you may learn how fearful it was to
be separated from the gathering of the brothers, and how much gain it brings
to those who receive the rebuke well-minded, hear how that one puffed up by
the sin, who had driven to last wickedness, who had fornicated a fornication
such as is not even named among the nations, who was without feeling of the
wound (for this is excess of distortion): this one then of such a kind was so
bent and contracted, that Paul said: Sufficient for such a one is this rebuke
by the more. So confirm love toward him. For as a member torn from the rest of
the body, so it was then.

4. And the cause, and from where this was fearful then: because even to be
with them was thought a great good to them. For as inhabiting one house, and
being under one father, and partaking of one table, so they inhabited then in
each Church. To fall then from so great love, how great an evil was it? But
now it does not even seem to be great, because nothing great is thought, when
we are with one another. What was in the order of a punishment then, this
through the much cooling of love happens even without punishment now, and we
withdraw from one another in vain, and from coldness. For the cause of all the
evils is that there is not love. This has dissolved and made vanish all the
solemn and splendid things of the Church, in which one ought to exult. Great
is a teacher's confidence, when from his own achievements he has it to rebuke
the disciples. Therefore Paul also said: For you yourselves know how it is
necessary to imitate us. And he ought to be a teacher of the life more than of
the word. And let no one think this to be boastfulness. For having been set
into necessity he spoke this, and toward the common usefulness. That we did
not act disorderly, he says, among you. From this do you not see the humility,
or he calls a gift also good order? We did not act disorderly, he says, among
you, nor eat bread as a gift. He shows here that they also perhaps were poor.
And do not tell me, But not all were poor. For he converses about poor men and
those who do not otherwise have the necessary food, except from the work of
the hands. For he did not say, That they may have from fathers, but, That


working they may eat their own bread. For if I the herald, he says, of the
word of teaching feared to burden you, much more he who benefits you nothing.
For this is truly a burden. And it is a burden, when someone does not give
with much eagerness. But he does not hint at this, but as of those not having
easily. For why do you not work? God gave you hands for this, not so that you
may receive from others, but so that you may provide to others. And the Lord,
he says, be with you. This it is possible even =62.498= for us to pray upon
ourselves, if we do the things of the Lord. For hear Christ saying to the
disciples: Going, make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them into the
name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, teaching them to
keep all things, as many as I commanded you. And see, I am with you all the
days until the completion of the age. Not only have these things been said
toward those, but also toward us. For that it has not been promised to those
only is clear from saying, Until the age of the completion, but also to those
who walk in the tracks of those. What then does he say toward those who are
not teachers? Each of you, if he should wish, is a teacher, if not of another,
still of himself. Teach yourself first. If you teach to keep all things, as
many as he commanded you, through this you will have many who emulate. For
just as the lamp, when it itself becomes bright, can kindle countless: but
extinguished it will provide light neither to itself, nor will it be able to
kindle other lamps: so also in the case of the pure life, if the light in us
is bright, we will make countless both disciples and teachers, being set forth
as an archetype. For not so will the words from me be able to benefit those
who hear, as our life. For let there be, if you wish, some man a friend to
God, and shining in virtue, and having a wife. For it is possible even having
a wife to please God and children and household slaves and friends. This one,
tell me, will he not be able much more than I to benefit all? For they will
hear me once or twice in the month, or not even once, and the things they
hear, perhaps having guarded as far as the threshold of the church, they
immediately throw off. But seeing that one's life continually, they gain great
things. For when having been insulted he does not insult in return, has he not
almost fixed and engraved the reverence of his gentleness into the soul of the
one insulting? And even if he does not immediately confess the benefit, from
the anger either being ashamed or being put to shame, still he immediately
takes a perception. And it is impossible for an insulting man, even if he is a
beast, having mixed with some forbearing man, not to go away having gained
great things. For the good things even if we do not do them, still we all
praise and marvel. The wife again if she sees the husband gentle, always
gaining great things being together, and the child. It is possible then for
each to become a teacher. For build, he says, one another one the one, as you
also do. For see: a loss has fallen upon the house? The wife is troubled, as
being weaker and fond of ornament? If the husband is a philosopher and a
mocker of loss, he both consoles that one, and persuades her to bear nobly.
Then, tell me, will he not benefit her much more than our words? For to speak
is easy for everyone, but to act when we have been set into need is
exceedingly difficult. For this reason human nature is accustomed to be
regulated more from the works. And so great is the excess of virtue, that even
a slave has often benefited a whole house together with the master.

5. For not in vain nor simply does Paul continually order them to practice
virtue, and to be obedient to the masters, not caring so much for the service
of the masters, as so that the word of God may not be blasphemed and the
teaching. And when it is not blasphemed, it will quickly also be marveled at.
And I know many houses, that they gained great things from the virtue of the
slaves. And if a household slave lying under authority could


regulate the master, much more a master slaves. Share this service with me, I
beseech you. I converse with all in common. You privately with each, and let
each be entrusted with the salvation of the neighbors. For that it is
necessary to stand in front of those of the house in these things, hear where
Paul sends the wives. And if they wish to learn something, he says, let them
ask their own husbands in the house. And he does not lead them to the teacher.
For just as in the lessons of letters there are also teachers among the
pupils, so also in the Church. For he wishes the teacher not to be troubled by
all. Why? Because thus there will be great goods, not only that the labor will
be light for the teacher, but that each of the pupils also, having been
anxious, can quickly become a teacher, having this care. For see how much
service the wife brings in. She keeps the house, and cares for all the things
according to the house, she stands over the maidservants, she clothes with her
own hands, she makes you be called a father of children, she delivers from
brothels, she helps toward self-control, she stops the sting of nature. But do
good to her also yourself. How? In the spiritual things stretch a hand. The
useful things you hear, these, like the swallows, carrying with the mouth,
lead away, and put into the mouth both of the mother and of the nestlings. For
how is it not out of place in the other things to think yourself worthy of the
first places, and to hold the space of the head, but in the teaching to leave
the order? The ruler of the ruled ought not to surpass in the honors, but in
the virtues. For this is of a ruler. For that is of the ruled, but this is an
achievement of the ruler himself. If you enjoy much honor, this is nothing
toward you. For you received it from others. If you shine with much virtue,
this is wholly yours. You are head of the wife. Therefore let the head
regulate the rest of the body. Do you not see that it does not so much surpass
=62.500= the rest of the body by the place, as by the providence, like some
steersman, directing it all? For in the head are both the eyes of the body and
those of the soul. From there the seeing flows to these, from there the
ruling. And the rest is ordered unto service, but it itself lies unto
commanding. All the senses have from there the beginning and the spring. The
vocal organs are sent up from there, the seeing, the smelling, all the touch.
For the root of the nerves and of the bones has been composed from there. Do
you see that it surpasses more by the providence than by the honor? Thus let
us also rule the women. Let us conquer them not by seeking more honor from
them, but by their being more greatly benefited from us. I have shown that
they do us no small benefactions, but if we wish to give the return in the
spiritual things, we conquer. Since in the bodily things it is not possible to
bring in something equivalent. What then? Do you bring in much money? But that
one guards it. And this providence of hers is equivalent to that, and thus it
needs her. Why? Because many having acquired much, since they did not have one
guarding, lost all. But for the sake of the children you both share, and the
benefaction from each is equal. Or rather that one has a more laborious
service in these things, continually bearing the offspring, and being cut by
the pangs. So only in the spiritual things will you be able to conquer. Let us
not then care how there will be money for us, but how the souls with which we
have been entrusted we may present with confidence to God. For in regulating
those we will also benefit ourselves the most. For he who teaches another,
even if nothing else, still in the speaking is pricked, and especially when he
sees himself being liable to these things, for which he rebukes others. Since
then we benefit both ourselves, and those, and through those the house, and
this seems good to God chiefly, let us not grow weary both of our own, and of
the soul of those who serve us taking care, so that for all


we may receive the recompense, and with much wealth we may arrive at the holy
city our mother, the Jerusalem above: from which may we never fall, but having
shone in the best way of life with much confidence be counted worthy to see
our Lord Jesus Christ, with whom to the Father together with the Holy Spirit
be glory, power, honor, now and always, and unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
