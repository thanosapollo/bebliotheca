OF JOHN LAURENTIUS LYDUS ON CELESTIAL SIGNS
PROOEMIVM
The learned among the Hebrews divide the account concerning foreknowledge in
two, into signs and portents; and they wish signs to be the things constituted
in the heights, but portents the things appearing on earth as contrary to
nature, such as that which is reported at the so-called exodus of the Hebrews
concerning Moses himself: "and he saw that the bush was burning, but the bush
was not consumed" ̣Exod. 3, 2̣, and again: "Moses threw the rod upon the earth
and it became a serpent" ̣Exod. 4, 3̣, and again: "Moses brought his hand into
his bosom and his hand became as snow" ̣Exod. 4, 6̣, then again: "and God said
to Moses: you shall take of the water of the river upon the dry and it shall
be blood" ̣Exod. 4, 9̣; then going on: "and the Lord said to Moses: if Pharaoh
shall speak to you saying \"give a sign or a portent\", take your rod and
throw it upon the earth and it shall be a dragon" ̣Exod. 7, 8. 9̣. And not
these only, but still another more terrible portent, the change of all the
Egyptian water into gore ̣Exod. 7, 20̣ and the multitude of the frogs ̣Exod.
8, 9̣ and the so-called gnats and the gadflies, which they themselves call
dog-flies ̣Exod. 8, 17. 18̣, and the destruction of the cattle ̣Exod. 9, 3. 6̣
and the ulcerous dust, blisters and locusts; all these would justly be called
portents; but signs the things appearing from things above, such as that which
is carried in the same writing: "and the Lord rained the hail upon all the
land of Egypt; and the hail was, and the fire flaming in the hail" ̣Exod. 9,
23. 24̣, and again: "and Moses stretched out his hand upon the heaven and
there came darkness, gloom and a tempest upon all the land of Egypt" ̣Exod.
10, 22̣. Such then is the account concerning signs among the Hebrews; from
which, I think, the other nations also came to know to call these tokens
celestial signs; for Zeus is called the air according to the natural
philosophers, because he is life-giving, so that a celestial sign is the sign
of the air, just as the mild and calm figure of the air is called fair
weather. One must not therefore, being disturbed at the names, have the
simpler leap away from learning; "I will give," he says, "signs in the heaven
above and portents upon the earth below; blood and fire and vapor of smoke;
the Sun shall be turned into darkness and the moon into blood ̣Joel 3, 3. 4.
Act. ap. II 19. 20̣." ........ having promised to do this. Of the opinion of
the many I too was formerly, and I thought the things written about these
matters by the ancients were letters only. But since experience showed the
truth concerning them, and the rising of the comet that happened formerly (and
it was of the so-called horseman figure) and the inroad of the ill-starred
Persians that came about through this, which went as far as the parts of the
Orontes ̣a. 540 p. Chr.̣, but took as short a return as possible (for it made
plain also the victory of the most mighty king), I was led by the facts
themselves and by the witness from them to write concerning such things,
thinking on the one hand that the matter would be for my memory, and on the
other judging it to be out of place and full of envy that such great labors
laid down by the ancients into the accounts concerning these things should
remain hidden for ever. 2 And I think it fitting for one who wishes to write
concerning such things to say both whence the grasp of such things began, and
from where it had its starting-points, and how it advanced so far as even, if
it is lawful

to say, to surpass the Egyptians. For of these, after the great Zoroaster,
Petosiris, having woven the things in genus into the specifics, forces many
things according to himself to be handed down, yet he does not hand these down
to all, but only to those according to himself, or rather to as many of them
as are more fit for conjectures. And Antigonus after him distinguished and
articulated the tradition, but having declined toward the density of the lines
in astronomy he set down in the writing an untold crowd and one filled with
every unclarity. For the things said by Aristotle are most well known. But
Heliodorus and Asclation, and still Odapsus the Theban and Polles of Aegium
and the most divine Ptolemy before them, were not able all the way to cast out
the ancient unclarity of the matter, although they were very eager to do even
this. And since for us, I mean those from Italy, Tages became founder of the
matter, it is consistent to use his words, or rather the meaning of them; for
those things, being composed in the more ancient names, are somehow hard to
follow and not very clear. And we shall use also the rest, both Tarchon the
sacrificer and Tarcytus the initiator and Capito the priest, so as to weave
from the things said by all these a certain polished harmony of the matter.
One must therefore first relate who this Tages is and who the rest are, and
how such things were entrusted to letters contrary to what prevailed in the
sacred rites. 3 Tarchon, having the name in this way, was a sacrificer, as he
himself has been brought in upon the writing, one of those taught by Tyrrhenus
the Lydian. For indeed these things are made clear by the letters of the
Tuscans, Evander the Arcadian not yet at that time having appeared in those
places. And the type of the letters was of another sort, and not at all well
worn for us; for otherwise nothing of the secret and more necessary things
would have remained hidden until the present. Tarchon then says upon the
treatise, which some suspect to be of Tages, since there in a certain dialogic
converse Tarchon supposedly asks, and Tages answers as attending each time to
the sacred rites, how there happened to him at a certain time while plowing
something wonderful, such as no one has even heard of as having come about in
all time; for a child was given up from the furrow, seeming just now to have
been born, but in no need of teeth and of the other marks in maturity; and the
child was Tages, whom indeed it seemed to the Greeks to be a chthonic Hermes,
as also Proclus the successor says somewhere. And this has been veiled
allegorically beside the priestly law, since the account concerning more
divine things is not given openly because of the unholy, but is handed down
now mythically and now parabolically; for instead of saying that a most
perfect soul and one in no need of its own energies came upon matter, he says
a newborn infant was given up from the furrow. And Tarchon the elder (for
there was also a younger, who campaigned in the times of Aeneas), taking up
the child and setting him in the sacred places, thought it right to learn from
him something of the secrets. And having obtained what he asked he composed a
book from the things said, in which Tarchon inquires in this customary speech
of the Italians, and Tages answers remaining in ancient letters and ones not
very known to us at least in the answers. But as far as has become possible
for me, both from the Tuscans and from the others who interpreted them, I mean
Capito and Fonteius, and Apuleius and Vicellius and Labeo and Figulus, and
Pliny the naturalist, I shall try to go through these things toward you. 4 And
our zeal is to speak concerning both solar and lunar overshadowings (for thus
they call the ecliptic slips of the lights), and of comets and of the
difference according to them, and of furrowings and of shooting stars, and of
lightnings and thunders and thunderbolts and of the other aerial

possible to take from the most many-learned history that both several suns at
the same time and moons have appeared, this coming about from reflection.

and likewise also so many moons in the consulship of Gaius Annius and Gnaeus
Domitius; by which it was shown outright that Gauls and Sauromatians would

99dt ON THE SIGNIFICATIONS OF THE SUN AND MOON
One must know first that the sun alone has universal energies, the other
celestial signs having local ones. For it has been given once for the sun to
be covered by the interposition of the moon, and for her by the counter-fall
of the earth, and for him in the conjunction of all, but the moon whenever she
is full-shining. And for this no one would dispute. And this is said to have
been known beforehand among the Greeks by Thales of Miletus in the forty-ninth
olympiad, in the hundred and seventieth year of the founding of Rome later,
and among the Romans by Sulpicius Gallus, one day before the defeat of Perseus
the Macedonian. And under Vespasian Caesar in fifteen days it happened to both
the lights to suffer this. Yet this affection does not always foretell the
same things; for neither are the eclipses of one form, the sun governing Asia,
and the moon Europe *, and especially whenever the eclipse comes about toward
the so-called malefics, the royal signs being harmed and the help of the goods
being idle. For an eclipse of either of the lights happening in Taurus or
Virgo or Capricorn according to the accounts set forth, a lack of fruits and
especially of the ripe ones happens, inasmuch as Virgo is ear-bearing, and
Taurus plows, and Capricorn first tastes of such fruits; and if such a thing
comes about in Gemini or Libra or Aquarius, the triangle being human-formed,
plagues and famines will strike; and whenever it happens in Cancer or Scorpio
or Pisces, destructions of crowds and standings of wars and destruction of sea
things will lie upon. And at the royal triangle, I mean Aries and Leo and
Sagittarius, an eclipse coming about, some one of those about the kingship
itself must suffer such a thing as happens about the triangle, that being
additionally observed; so that whenever an eclipse comes about in Leo it
signifies a certain harming for the kings, and it turns away the Nile. So that
it is reasonable also to wonder at the providence of God, that through the
whole time of this most fortunate kingship ̣Anastasiị an eclipse upon this
sign did not come about. These things then have been said on behalf of the
solar eclipses.

And one must observe toward the universal circumstances the colors at the
eclipses either of the lights themselves or of the systems coming about about
them, such as rods or certain such things. For things appearing black or
pale-green become significative of the things said upon the nature of Kronos,
and white of those upon that of Zeus, and tawny of those upon that of Ares,
and yellow of those upon that of Aphrodite, and variegated of those upon that
of Hermes; and if the resulting peculiarity of the complexion appears in the
whole bodies of the lights or in the whole places about them, the outcome will
be about the most parts of the countries; but if from any part whatsoever,
according to that part only at which the inclination of the peculiarity also
comes about. And if this is going to happen about the east or the west, it
will become clear from the suffering light itself, in that the sun belongs
throughout to Asia, and the moon to Europe. And useful toward the forecasts of
the particular significations would also be the observations of the signs
coming about about the sun and the moon and the stars. One must then observe
the sun toward the daily conditions as he is rising, and toward the nightly as
he is setting, and toward those that stretch on according to the
configurations toward the moon, each configuration as a rule signifying
beforehand the condition until the next. For the sun rising or also setting
clean and unshadowed and stable and cloudless is indicative of a fair-weather
condition; but having the circle variegated or fire-colored or sending out red
rays either circling outward as upon himself, or having the so-called parhelia
clouds from one part, and the figure of the clouds tawny and as it were
lengthening long rays, he is indicative of violent winds and of such toward
whichever angles the aforementioned signs come about. And black or pale-green
rising or setting with cloudiness, or having a halo about himself at one part,
or from both parts parhelia clouds and rays either pale-green or black, he
shows storms and rains. And that at least is also clear even to those who
belong to the people, that whenever rising he as it were lurks under and
scatters the rays of the clouds supposedly running out and dripping through,
he declares that the rain mixed with hail will burst down. And whenever
setting he seems to be muddy and not dark-blue, he will be a messenger both of
rains together and of winds and especially of winds, according to the manners
and at once the places we speak of; for the breath first stretching up to the
aerial things, it is likely that he is kindled more and pours the rays in the
manner of a torch being pushed around by a certain wind. And rising clean he
foretells that the air will be both northern at once and clear; for "Boreas"
is "aether-born" according to the poet; for blowing upon the higher of the
heights and pushing the dense and misty toward earth, he makes the sun shine
out clean and unshadowed, consuming the densifications and by the thin of the
breath cleaning this leeward zone and more the one toward us and making it
brighter. And the Roman Campestrius, following the Petosirian traditions ̣fr.
8 ed. Riess. p. 342̣, says that the sun rising silver-formed and
dull-complexioned, or pale, but wholly remaining to be seen, shows wars and

of the necessities, and standings both for fathers toward children and again
for the children toward those who begot them and the plots against one
another. And these will be universal whenever the accident is according to the

places the thing that has happened seems to look toward; and if

the sun should fail according to the so-called counter-disk, he threatens the

And one must observe the moon either before three or after three days of the
conjunction and of the full moon and of the half. For appearing thin and clean
and having nothing about herself, she is indicative of a fair-weather
condition. And if she should also redden moderately and have the unlighted of
the circle translucent and somewhat moved, she is indicative of winds,
according to wherever she especially makes the inclination. And if she should
seem pale and thick, she is indicative of storms and rains. And one must also
observe the halos coming about about her. For if there should be one and this
clean and gently fading, she signifies a fair-weather condition. And if there
should be two or even three, they show storms; those through winds, if they
should be tawny and as it were bursting under; and those through snows, if
misty and thick; and if also pale-green and black and as it were being broken,
those through both and so much greater as they should also seem more. And the
runnings-through and the dartings of the stars, if they should come about from
one angle, show the wind from that; and if from the opposites, instability of
breaths; and if from the four, storms of every kind as far as thunders and
lightnings and such things. And likewise also the clouds being like flocks of
wool become sometimes preindicative of storms; and the rainbows constituted
according to seasons signify a storm from fair weather, and fair weather from
storm. And as a rule, to speak concisely, the peculiar-colored appearances
coming upon the air universally show such things.

So many things concerning sun and moon and the things happening according to
each of them. And one must go on then to the first and almost only occasion of
the treatise for us, and we mean the comet stars, going through the things
shown on their behalf, just as has become clear to us from the aforementioned
books; for coming about at the ecliptic times or also at any time whatsoever
they will make clear to those who wish to watch such things, both through the
parts of the zodiacal circle according to which such constitutions come about,
and through the inclinations according to the figures of the hair, the places
upon which the things that will come out through them strike; and through the
constitution itself and as it were formation both the form of the outcome and
the genus about which the affection will happen, and through the time of the
remaining the extension of the accidents; and sometimes also through the
relation toward the sun the beginning; for appearing morning-wise for a long
time they signify more quickly * and so much greater as the constitution comes
about from more parts and for a long time. For the universal of the outcomes
either through the

ecliptic things it is, or they are seen happening through the appearances of
the comets and especially whenever before the rising of the sun they cast the
hairs toward anything; and especially whenever with the hairs they cover some
of the wandering stars. For bringing the hairs upon that of Zeus, they
complete the disturbances about the royal houses; and whenever they bring the
hairs upon that of Ares, diseases and deaths of those in arms, and still also
standings of wars they complete; and upon that of Aphrodite it signifies
destruction of women and deaths of queens and they make separations; and upon
that of Kronos holdings-back of fruits, or scarcities; and upon that of Hermes
they will provide disturbances to contracts and unpleasant reports to the
crowd. For it is impossible for a comet to shine around the sun and the moon.
And if it appears, either before risings or about settings, it will make the
harming about the difference of the climates; for in the risings it will harm
the things about Asia, and toward the setting the things about Europe. Yet
those who spent time about these things presented neither the end nor the
swift substance of the times. And there come about also from the monthly
conjunctions and full moons, whenever the so-called malefics witness to these,
the goods not being configured together, certain few oppositions, which are
easily loosed because such things are not completed according to the eclipses
of the lights.
And different are the looks of the comets, as also different other things. And
they do not complete the same things; but all are evils. For the comet is
fiery and with the hairs bloodlike; and the bearded one has the hairs from
below in the manner of a beard; and the javelin is like a javelin and as iron
is shaken, of which appearing Judaea was taken, as has been said also to
Eusebius son of Pamphilus; and the sword is shorter, but pale and having the
rays sword-bearing; and the disked is like a disk and the form as electrum;
and the jar is full of smoky light, and the figure like the name; with which
the horned having the locks horn-formed; besides these the torch-bearer like a
burning torch; there is indeed the horse, the one also called horseman,
sprinkling the rays in the manner of a horse's mane, and sharpest beside the
others, wherefore it also obtained such a name. Ptolemy still mentions a
trumpet in the things toward Syrus, concerning which we have nothing to say;
for we do not find among another such a condition. And there come about also
other comets, the so-called goats, having certain clouds lying about them in
the manner of fleeces or flocks of wool; and at the beginning they appear
thus, but they are completed into a figure of spears. And all these are of a
nature to appear not more than upon seven days; nor at random upon any climate
of the heaven, but about the galaxy only according to Aristotle. And the
typhoons at least are according to the southern pole, and these are fiery
spheres and as it were bonds of fire; and these did not appear upon the Roman
times, but appearing before them they shook Egypt. Whence Aristotle wishes
them to be completed from an underlying wet substance; and through this to
appear sparsely upon Egypt because of the underlying dryness. And all the
others upon the north, even if they look toward risings or settings.
And about the crown another comet rises, which whenever it appears threatens
the movement of the Persians. And it appeared also when Nero was beginning his
madness. And besides these torches also shine out in the heaven or rather in
the air (for no star has been fixed to the heaven) and they appear whenever
they fall out; such as happened under Germanicus Caesar, when a theater was

ETHNOGPARHIA ASTPOLOGICA
And since certain being carried uneducatedly say that not upon every place the
same things are energized toward the risings or settings of the stars, come
let us release them from the error although they fight against. And perhaps
through short things it is possible to provide this to them. The signs then
having taken through under one the whole heaven of the wanderers, not

from the whole energizing the same things to one another upon earth, one must
watch of what sort of the climates upon earth it has been stationed to
complete under what sign; for thus the true will not flee those who seek not
toward hatred. To Aries Britain, Galatia, Germany, Bastarnae, Coele Syria,
Idumaea. To Taurus Cyclades, the coastal of Asia Minor, Cyprus, Parthians,
Medes, Persia. To Gemini Hyrcania, two Armenias, Matiane, Cyrenaic, Marmarice,
lower Egypt. To Cancer Bithynia, two Phrygias, Colchic, Numidia, Carthage,
Africa. To Leo Italy, Apulia, Gallia, Sicily, Phoenicia, Orchenia or
Antorchenia. To Virgo Greece, Achaea, Crete, Babylon, Mesopotamia, Assyria. To
Scorpio middle Syria or Commagene, Cappadocia, Mauretania, Gaetulia. To Libra
Bactria, Caspeiria, Serice, Thebais, Oases, Troglodytic. To Sagittarius
Tyrrhenia and Celtic, Spain, fortunate Arabia the great. To Capricorn
Macedonia, Thrace, Illyris, Indic, Ariane, Gedrosia. To Aquarius Sauromatians,
Oxiane, Sogdiane, little Arabia, middle Azania, Ethiopia. To Pisces Lydia,
Cilicia, Pamphylia, Phazania, Nasamones, Garamantic.
