On the cross, spoken on holy and great Friday; and on the confession of the
thief, and that we must pray for our enemies.
=49.407= Homily 2.
αʹ. We keep a feast today and a festival, beloved; for our Master is upon the
cross, pierced with the nails. And do not be surprised if, though the thing is
gloomy, we keep a feast; for all spiritual things are contrary to human
custom. And that you may learn this accurately, the cross formerly was a name
of condemnation and punishment, but now it has become a thing honorable and
longed for; the cross formerly was a cause of shame and chastisement, but now
it has become an occasion of glory and honor. And that the cross is glory,
hear Christ saying: Father, glorify me with the glory which I had with you
before the world was, calling the cross glory. The cross is the sum of our
salvation; the cross is the cause of countless goods. Through this we who were
formerly dishonored and fallen have now been received into the rank of sons;
through this we no longer wander, but have come to know the truth; through
this we who formerly worshiped wood and stones have now come to know the
Creator of all; through this we who were slaves of sin have been brought up
into the freedom of righteousness; through this the earth has now become
heaven. This has freed us from error, this has led us by the hand to the
truth, this has made reconciliation of God with men, this, having drawn us up
from the depth of wickedness, has led us up to the very summit of virtue, this
has quenched the error of the demons, this has overthrown the deceit. Through
this there is no longer smoke and savor and pouring out of the blood of
irrational animals, but everywhere spiritual worship, hymns and prayers;
through this demons flee, through this the devil is put to flight, through
this human nature contends toward the angelic way of life, through this
virginity has its citizenship upon earth. For from the time when he came forth
from a Virgin, the nature of men came to know the way of this virtue. This has
enlightened us who sat in darkness, this has reconciled us who had been made
enemies, this has made those who were far off to be near, this has made his
own those who were alienated, this has made those who had become strangers
into citizens of heaven, this has become for us the destruction of wars, this
has been established for us as the security of peace. =49.408= Through this we
no longer fear the fiery darts of the devil; for we have found the fountain of
life; through this we are not in widowhood; for we have received the
bridegroom; through this we no longer dread the wolf; for we have come to know
the good shepherd; For I am, he says, the good shepherd. Through this we no
longer shudder at the tyrant; for we have run to the king. Do you see of how
many goods the cross is the cause for us? Reasonably then we keep a feast.
Thus also Paul exhorts to keep a feast, saying: Therefore let us keep the
feast, not with old leaven, nor with the leaven of malice and wickedness, but
with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth. And for what reason do you
command us to keep a feast, O blessed Paul? Tell the cause. Because our
Passover has been sacrificed for us, he says, Christ God. Do you see how the
cross is a feast? Have you learned that he commands to keep a feast because of
the cross? For on the cross he was sacrificed; and where there is a sacrifice,
there is


the taking away of sins, there reconciliation with the Master, there feast and
joy. Our Passover, he says, has been sacrificed for us, Christ. Where was he
sacrificed, tell me? Upon a high cross. New and strange is the altar, since
the sacrifice also is strange and altered. For the same one is both sacrifice
and priest; sacrifice according to the flesh, priest according to the spirit;
the same both offered and was offered. Hear again Paul saying: For every high
priest taken from among men is appointed for men in things pertaining to God.
Whence it is necessary that this one also have something to offer. Behold for
the present he offers. But elsewhere again he says: So also Christ, having
been offered once to bear the sins of many. Behold here he was offered, there
he offered himself. Did you see how he became both sacrifice and priest, and
the altar was the cross? But it is necessary to learn for what reason the
sacrifice is not offered in the temple, I mean in the Jewish temple, but
outside the city, outside the walls. For outside the city he was crucified, as
some condemned man, that that might be fulfilled which was spoken by the
prophet, that He was numbered with the lawless. For what reason then outside
the city, upon high, and not under some roof? Not simply this either, but that
he might cleanse the nature of the air. For this reason also upon high, and no
roof lying over him, but heaven lying over him instead of a roof, that the
heaven might be cleansed once, the sheep being sacrificed on high. Heaven then
was cleansed, =49.409= and the earth also was cleansed. For the blood dripped
from the side upon the earth, and cleansed all its pollution. But under a
roof, for this reason, the sacrifice is not offered; and for what reason not
in the Jewish temple itself? This again not simply, but lest the Jews should
monopolize the sacrifice, lest you should think that the sacrifice is offered
for that nation alone; for this reason outside the city, outside the walls,
that you may learn that the sacrifice is catholic, that you may learn that the
offering is for the whole earth, and that the cleansing is common to all our
nature. For to the Jews for this reason God commanded them to leave all the
earth, and to offer in one place, to pray in one place; since all the earth
then was unclean from the smoke and the savor and the idolatrous bloods and
the other Greek pollutions; for this reason he appointed one place for them.
But Christ having come, and having suffered outside the city, cleansed all the
earth, made every place a house of prayer. Do you wish to learn how all the
earth has now become a temple, and how every place has been established as a
house of prayer? Hear again the blessed Paul saying: In every place lifting up
holy hands without wrath and doubting. Did you see how he cleansed the
inhabited world? did you see how we can lift up holy hands everywhere? For all
the earth has now become holy, or rather even holier than the holy things
among the Jews. How? Because there an irrational sheep was offered, but here a
rational one. As much then as the rational is better than the irrational, so
much more also is the sanctification here. Therefore truly the cross is a
feast. βʹ. Do you wish to learn also another of its greatest achievements,
surpassing all human understanding? Paradise, which had been closed, today he
opened; for today he brought the thief into it. Two greatest achievements:
both he opened paradise and he brought in the thief; he restored to him the
ancient fatherland, he brought him back to the ancestral city. Today you shall
be with me, he says, in paradise. What do you say? You are crucified and
nailed to the cross with nails, and you promise paradise? how then do you
grant such things? And yet Paul says that He was crucified from weakness; but
hear what follows: But he lives, he says, from the power of God. And again
elsewhere: For my power is made perfect in weakness. For this reason now on
the cross, he says, I promise, that through this also you may thoroughly learn
my power.


For since the thing is gloomy, that you may not become downcast attending to
the nature of the cross, but looking to the power of the one crucified may be
bright and glad, for this reason there he displays his own strength to you.
For not by raising a dead man, not by commanding the sea, not by rebuking
demons, but being crucified, nailed, insulted, spat upon, reviled, mocked,
being made a spectacle by all, he had strength to draw the wicked mind of the
thief to himself. See his power shining from both sides. He shook creation, he
split the rocks, he made the soul of the thief, more insensible than rocks,
softer than wax. Today you shall be with me in paradise. What do you say? The
cherubim keep paradise and the flaming sw =49.410= ord, and you promise the
thief entrance there? Yes, he says. For I am Master even of the cherubim, and
I have authority over flame and gehenna and life and death; wherefore he also
says: Today you shall be with me in paradise. If those powers see the Master,
they immediately give way and withdraw. And yet no king would ever endure to
seat a thief, a man, or some other of his fellow-servants with himself, and so
drive into the city; but the loving Master did this. For entering into the
holy fatherland, he brings in the thief with himself, not dishonoring
paradise, God forbid, with the feet of the thief, but rather honoring it by
this. For it is an honor to paradise to have such a Master, so powerful and
loving, as to be able to make even a thief worthy of the delight that is in
paradise. For when he called tax-collectors and harlots into the kingdom, he
was not doing this by shaming the kingdom, but honoring it most of all, and
showing that such is the Lord of the kingdom of the heavens, as to make even
harlots and tax-collectors so approved as to appear worthy of the honor and
gift there. Just as then we marvel at a physician when we see that, delivering
men who have incurable sufferings from the illness, he has restored them to
pure health; so also marvel at Christ, beloved, and be astonished, that taking
men who have incurable sufferings of soul, he had strength both to deliver
them from wickedness and to declare those who had gone on to the last
wickedness worthy of the kingdom of the heavens. Today you shall be with me in
paradise. Great honor, much the magnitude of the loving-kindness, unspeakable
the excess of the goodness; for greater honor than entering into paradise is
entering with the Master. What has happened, tell me? what such thing did the
thief display, that all at once from the cross he should appear worthy of
paradise? Do you wish me to tell it briefly, and show the gratitude of the
thief? When Peter denied below, the chief of the disciples, then that one
above, being upon the cross, confessed. And I said this not accusing Peter,
God forbid, but wishing to show the magnanimity of the thief, and the
surpassing philosophy. That one did not bear the threat of a cheap girl; but
this one, seeing a whole people raging and standing around and shouting and
hurling countless revilings at the one crucified, did not look to the insult
of the one crucified, but running past all these things with the eyes of
faith, and leaving aside the lowly things and the hindrances below, he
recognized the Master of the heavens, saying those brief words which declared
him worthy of paradise: Remember me in your kingdom. Let us not simply run
past what has been said, nor be ashamed to take as teacher the thief, whom our
Master was not ashamed first to bring into paradise; let us not be ashamed to
take as teacher a man who before all the race of men appeared worthy of the
citizenship in paradise. Let us then examine


each of the words, that we may learn from this also the power of the cross.
For he did not say to him, as to Peter and Andrew: Come, and I will make you
fishers of men; nor did he say to him, as to the twelve disciples, that You
shall sit =49.411= upon twelve thrones judging the twelve tribes of Israel.
Rather he did not even think him worthy of a word at all. Nor did he see
miracles, not a dead man being raised, not demons being driven out, not the
sea yielding to his command, nor did he converse with him about a kingdom; and
from where did he even know the name of the kingdom? Let us see his great
understanding. The other thief, he says, was reviling him; for there was also
another thief crucified with him, that that might be fulfilled which was
spoken by the prophet, that He was numbered with the lawless. For the
ungrateful Jews wished also to slander his glory, and from every side they
insulted the things that were happening; but through all things the truth
grew, and through the very hindrances became more splendid. The other thief
then was reviling him; but one of the evangelists says that both were reviling
Jesus; and this too is true, which especially heightens this one's gratitude.
For it is likely that he reviled at the beginning, but all at once displayed
so great a change. The other thief then, he says, was reviling him. Did you
see a thief and a thief? both on the cross, both from wickedness, both from a
robber's life, but not both in the same things; but the one inherited a
kingdom, and the other was being sent into gehenna. So also yesterday, a
disciple and disciples; but the one was preparing for betrayal, and the others
were making ready for service; and that one said to the Pharisees: What will
you give me, and I will deliver him to you? but these came to Jesus saying:
Where do you will that we prepare for you to eat the Passover? So also here, a
thief and a thief; but the one was reviling, and the other stops his mouth;
the one blasphemes, and the other rebukes, and this seeing him crucified,
condemned, the people below reviling, shouting loudly; but nevertheless none
of these things turned him aside, nor led him away from the fitting glory, but
he even attacks the other thief fiercely, and says to him: Do you not even
fear God? γʹ. Did you see the boldness of a thief? did you see how even on a
cross he does not forget his own craft, but through the confession itself he
plunders the kingdom? Do you not even fear God, you, he says? Did you see
boldness on a cross? did you see philosophy, did you see piety? For that he
was in himself, that he had his wits entirely, with the nails driven into him,
and enduring the unbearable pains from the nails, is he not worthy to be
marveled at for the noble mind? I would justly say that he is worthy not only
to be marveled at, but also to be called blessed. For not only did he not turn
toward the pains, but leaving aside his own affairs, he was concerned with
those of another, and was eager how he might snatch that one from error, and
become a teacher on the cross. Do you not even fear God, you, he says? All but
saying to him: Do not attend to the court below, do not judge from the things
seen, do not look only at the things happening; there is another invisible
judge, that court is unbribable, not able to be cheated. Do not then look at
this, that he was condemned below; but the things above are not such. For here
in the court below even the guiltless are often condemned, and the guilty are
released, and the righteous are condemned, and =49.412= the unjust escape. For
many of men destroy the things of those being judged, some willingly, some
unwillingly; for either not knowing the just and being deceived, or seeing it,
but being corrupted by money, they have betrayed the truth, and have voted
against the guiltless. But above there is nothing of this kind. For God is a
righteous judge, and the judgment


of him shall go forth as light; it has no darkness, it has no concealment, it
admits no turning aside. For lest that one should say that he was condemned
below, why do you speak for him? he raised him to the court above, to the
fearful tribunal, to the unbribable judgment-seat, to the judge who cannot be
cheated, he reminded him of that fearful tribunal. Look there, he says, and
you will not bear the condemning verdict, nor will you stand with the men
below, but you will marvel, and you will accept the judgment from above. Do
you not even fear God, you, he says? Did you see the philosophy of a thief,
did you see understanding, did you see teaching? All at once from the cross he
flew up to heaven. And see him already fulfilling an apostolic law, and not
looking only to his own things, but doing and busying himself with all things,
so as both to deliver that one from error and to bring him back to the truth.
For having said, Do you not even fear God, you, he added, That we are in the
same judgment. Consider a confession brought to completion. What is, That we
are in the same judgment? In the same punishment, he says; for we too are on a
cross. Reproaching that one then, you put yourself forward before him with the
revilings. Just as then the one who is in sins, and condemning another,
condemns himself before that one; so also the one who is in misfortune, and
reproaching another's misfortune, reproaches himself before that one. We are
in the same judgment, he says. He reads to him an apostolic law, speaking
gospel words: Judge not, that you be not judged. That we are in the same
judgment. What are you doing, O thief? do you make him a partner of yours,
saying, We are in the same judgment? No, he says; I add the correction through
what follows: And we indeed, he says, justly; for we receive things worthy of
what we have done. For lest having heard that he said, We are in the same
judgment, you should think that he made him a partner of the sin, for this
reason he added the correction, and says: We indeed justly; for we receive
things worthy of what we have done. Did you see a perfect confession on a
cross? did you see how through the words he washed off the sins? did you see
how he fulfilled that prophetic command: Say you your lawless deeds first,
that you may be justified? No one compelled him, no one accused, no one
attacked; he himself becomes his own accuser; for this reason he no longer had
any other accuser. For anticipating he seized the rank of the accuser, and
made a spectacle of himself saying: We justly; for we receive things worthy of
what we have done; but this one has done nothing amiss. Did you see piety
intensified? When then he had accused himself, when he had made a parade of
his own affairs, when he had spoken in defense of the Master saying that We
indeed justly, but this one has done nothing amiss; then he also dared to
bring forward the sup =49.413= plication, saying: Remember me, Lord, when you
come in your kingdom. He did not dare first to say, Remember me, until through
confession he had cleansed himself of the filth of the sins, until having
condemned himself he had made himself guiltless, until through the accusation
he had laid aside the offenses. Do you see how great confession is even on the
very cross? Hearing these things, beloved, never despair of yourself, but
considering the unspeakable magnitude of the loving-kindness of God, hasten
toward the correction of the things sinned. For if he counted the thief being
on a cross worthy of so great honor, much more will he count us worthy of his
own loving-kindness, if we should be willing to make confession of the
offenses. That then we too may enjoy the loving-kindness from him, let us not
be ashamed to confess our own sins; for great is the strength of confession,
and much is its power. For behold this one also confessed, and found paradise
opened; he confessed, and received boldness, the one who was in robbery, to
ask a kingdom. Until that time the


kingdom of the heavens he did not ask. From where, tell me, O thief, do you
mention a kingdom? for what such thing do you see now? Nails and a cross are
the things seen, and accusation and jeers and revilings. Yes, he says; for the
cross itself seems to me to be a symbol of a kingdom. And for this reason I
call him king, because I see him being crucified; for it belongs to a king to
die for those who are ruled. He himself said: The good shepherd lays down his
life for the sheep; therefore also the good king lays down his life for those
who are ruled. Since then he has laid down his life, for this reason I call
him king. Remember me, Lord, when you come in your kingdom. δʹ. Do you wish to
learn how the cross is also a symbol of a kingdom, and how august the thing
is? He did not allow it to remain upon the earth, but drew it up and brought
it into heaven. From where is this clear? He is going to come with it at the
second coming. But let us see also how he is going to come with it. Hear
Christ himself saying: If they say to you, Behold Christ is in the inner
rooms, behold in the wilderness, do not go forth; speaking of his second
coming, because of the false christs, because of the false prophets, because
of the Antichrist, lest anyone being deceived should fall in with that one.
For since before Christ the Antichrist comes, lest seeking, he says, the
shepherd, you should fall in with the wolf, for this reason I tell you marks
of the shepherd's coming. For since his first coming took place in secret, do
not think, he says, that the second also will be such. For that one reasonably
took place in secret; for he came seeking the lost; but this one is not so;
but how? tell and teach. As the lightning, he says, comes out from the east,
and appears as far as the west, so also shall be the coming of the Son of man.
All at once he shall appear to all; no one shall need to ask. For as when
lightning appears, we do not need to examine whether lightning has happened;
so when his coming takes place, we shall not need to examine whether Christ
has arrived. But the thing sought we have not yet even now said, whether he
comes with the cross. Hear then this too, himself making it clear. =49.414=
Then, he says, that is, when I come, the sun shall be darkened, and the moon
shall not give her light. For so great then shall be the excess of the light,
that even the brightest stars shall be hidden. And the stars too, he says,
shall fall, and thus the sign of the Son of man shall appear in heaven. Did
you see how great is the excess of the sign, how great the brightness, how
great the radiance? The sun is darkened, the moon does not appear, the stars
fall; but that alone appears, that you may learn that it is brighter even than
moon and more splendid than sun. For just as when a king is entering, the
armies going before carry the signs upon their shoulders, and announce
beforehand the entrance of the king; so when the Master of creation is
descending from the heavens, the armies of the angels and the archangels go
before carrying that sign, and announcing to us the kingly entrance. Then, he
says, also the powers of the heavens shall be shaken, speaking of the angels
and the archangels, and all the invisible powers; for trembling shall take
them, and much fear and agony. And for what reason, tell me, have those powers
also been set in fear? Reasonably. Fearful then shall be the court, all the
nature of men is going to be judged and to give accounts and to stand at a
fearful tribunal. For what reason then do the angels also tremble then, and
those bodiless powers fear? for they are not going to be judged. Just as when
a judge is condemning those who are liable, and sitting upon a high tribunal,
not only those who are liable, but also those of the order itself who stand
by, who are conscious of nothing in themselves, nevertheless fear and shudder
because of the


fear of the judge; so also then, our nature being judged and being required to
give accounts of the offenses, even the angels who are conscious of nothing in
themselves, and the remaining powers, fear and tremble because of the threat
of the judge. But this we have come to know; and for what reason and why does
he come having the cross? Learn the cause of this also. That through the very
works those who crucified may learn their own ingratitude, for this reason he
shows them the very proof of their madness. And that you may learn that he
carries it for this reason, that he may lay hold of them, hear again the
evangelist himself saying: Then shall appear the sign of the Son of man, and
all the tribes of the earth shall mourn, seeing the accuser, and recognizing
the sin. And why do you marvel if he comes carrying the cross? he comes
carrying the very wounds with himself. From where is it clear that he carries
the very wounds with himself? Hear the prophet saying: They shall look on him
whom they pierced. For just as he did in the case of Thomas, wishing to
correct the unbelief of the disciple, and showed him the prints of the nails,
and the very wounds, and says, Put your finger, and bring your hand, and see,
that a spirit has not flesh and bones, that he might show him that he truly
rose; so also then he carries the wounds and the cross, that he may show them
that this is that one who was crucified. Great then is the good and saving,
and a clear proof of the loving-kindness of God. εʹ. Or rather not the cross
alone, but also the very words upon the cross show his =49.415= unspeakable
loving-kindness. And hear the very words. For those who crucified him standing
by and boiling with wrath, Father, he says, forgive them this sin; for they do
not know what they are doing. Did you see the loving-kindness of the Master?
being crucified, he was entreating for those who crucified; and yet those were
mocking and making a spectacle of him saying: If you are the Son of God, come
down from the cross. And yet for this reason he does not come down from the
cross, because he is the Son of God; for this reason he came, that he might be
crucified for us. Let him come down, they say, from the cross, that we may
see, and believe in him. See words of shamelessness and pretexts of unbelief.
He did a thing greater than coming down from the cross, and they did not
believe, and now they say: Come down from the cross, and we will believe you.
For far greater than coming down from the cross was the raising of a dead man,
with the stone lying upon the tomb; far greater than coming down from the
cross was leading Lazarus out of the memorial, being four days, wrapped in
grave-clothes, with these. Did you see words of folly? did you see excess of
madness? but attend with accuracy, I entreat, that you may know the surpassing
loving-kindness of God, and how Christ uses even their very folly as an
occasion for pardon. Father, he says, forgive them the sin; for they do not
know what they are doing. For this all but shows, through what he says: They
are foolish, and they do not know what they are doing. And those were saying:
If you are the Son of God, save yourself; but he was eager how he might save
those who were reproaching him and making a comedy of him and reviling.
Forgive them, he says, this sin; for they do not know what they are doing.
What then? did he forgive them the sin? He forgave those who were willing to
display repentance. And if he had not forgiven them the sin, Paul would not
have become an apostle; if he had not forgiven them the sin, three thousand
and five thousand would not at once, nor would the many myriads of the Jews
have believed later. For hear what James says to Paul in Jerusalem: You see,
brother, how many myriads there are of Jews who have believed. Let us then
imitate, I entreat, we too the Master, and let us pray for the enemies. What
yesterday


I exhorted, these things I exhort also now again; knowing the magnitude of the
achievement, imitate your Master; that one was being crucified, and he was
entreating for those who crucified. And how can I, he says, imitate the
Master? If you will, you can; if it were not possible, he would not have said:
Learn from me that I am meek and humble in heart; if it were not possible for
a man to imitate, Paul would not have said: Become imitators of me, as I also
of Christ. But you do not wish to imitate the Master? imitate your
fellow-servant, I mean Stephen, who first opened the doors of martyrdom; that
one imitated the Master. For just as the Master hanging in the midst of those
crucifying him was entreating for those who crucified, so also the servant
being in the midst of those stoning, being struck by all, and receiving the
showers of the stones, leaving aside the pains that come from these said:
Lord, do not stand this sin against them. Did you see how the Son speaks, and
how the servant prays? that one says: Father, forgive them this sin; for they
do not know what =49.416= they are doing; this one says: Lord, do not stand
this sin against them. And that you may learn that he does this eagerly, he
did not simply pray, nor perfunctorily, nor standing, but having set his
knees, with contrition, with much compassion. Do you wish me to show you also
another fellow-servant praying a greater prayer for the enemies? hear the
blessed Paul saying; for after saying how many things he has suffered, that
Five times I received from Jews forty minus one, three times I was beaten with
rods, once I was stoned, three times I was shipwrecked; and having enumerated
the chain and the plots which he endured from them each day, he too says: I
was praying to be myself anathema from Christ for my brothers, my kinsmen
according to the flesh, who are Israelites. Do you wish to see others also,
not from the New, but also from the Old, doing this same thing? For this is
especially the marvelous thing, when those who were not commanded to love the
enemies, but to knock out an eye for an eye, and a tooth for a tooth, and to
defend themselves with equal things, these arrive at the apostolic summit.
Hear then what Moses says, who was often stoned by the Jews: If you forgive
them the sin, forgive; and if not, blot me also out of the book which you have
written. Do you see each of the righteous setting the safety of the others
before his own salvation? You have not sinned; for what reason do you wish to
share their punishment? I do not feel the well-being, he says, when others are
suffering badly. And it is possible to see another also making such a prayer.
For I bring forward many examples on purpose, that even thus we may correct
ourselves, and may banish this grievous disease of our own soul, I mean
praying against the enemies. Hear also the blessed David, when God being
provoked and having sent an angel who would punish the multitude, what he
says, seeing the angel now showing the naked sword and about to bring the
blow: I the shepherd have done evil, and these the flock, what have they done?
Let your hand be upon me, and upon the house of my father. Do you see again
kindred achievements? Do you wish me to show you another also doing this?
Samuel the prophet was insulted by the Jews, was set at naught, was so
dishonored that God wished to console him. Attend, I entreat, with accuracy.
For God said to him: They have not set you at naught, but me. What then did
that one who was set at naught, who was dishonored, who was despised, who was
insulted? Hear what he says: But far be it from me to sin against the Lord by

achievement, following the Master? Let us then see from the beginning the
things said. The Master says: Father, forgive them the sin; for they do not
know what they are doing. Stephen said: Lord, do not stand this sin against
them. Paul says: I was praying to be myself anathema from Christ for my
brothers, my kinsmen according to =49.418= the flesh. Moses likewise: If you
forgive them the sin, forgive; and if not, blot me also out of the book which
you have written. David says: Let your hand be upon me, and upon the house of
my father. Samuel likewise: But far be it from me to sin by ceasing to pray
for you. Of what forgiveness then shall we obtain hereafter, those from the
New, those from the Old, as if all pushing us to the prayer for the enemies,
if we shall not be eager with all haste to accomplish this? Do not then be
idle, I entreat. For as much as the examples are more, so much, if we do not
imitate, the punishment is greater. For far greater is praying for the enemies
than for the friends; praying for the friends does not benefit us so much as
praying for the enemies. Hear Christ saying: If you love those who love you,
what grace is there for you? do not even the tax-collectors do the same? When
then we pray for the friends, we have not yet become better than the
tax-collectors; but if we love the enemies, and pray for the enemies, we
become like God according to human power. For you shall be, he says, like your
Father who is in the heavens, because he makes his sun rise upon evil and
good, and rains upon righteous and unrighteous. When then we have the examples
both from the Master and from the servants, let us be zealous, let us
accomplish the virtue; that we also may be counted worthy to obtain the
kingdom of the heavens, and at this fearful table with more boldness, having
cleansed our own conscience, may also obtain the promised goods, by the grace
and loving-kindness of our Lord Jesus Christ, with whom to the Father,
together with the Holy Spirit, be glory, power, honor, now and always, and
unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
