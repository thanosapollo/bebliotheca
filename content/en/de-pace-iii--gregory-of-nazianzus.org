ORATION XXIII.
Third peace oration, on the agreement which we of the same mind made after the
conflict.
A. Hot is zeal, gentle the Spirit, philanthropic is love, or rather
self-philanthropy; long-suffering is hope. Zeal kindles, the Spirit soothes,
hope waits, love binds together, and does not allow the good in us to be
scattered, even if we are of a scatterable nature, and one of three, either
remaining as it is, or when moved being settled, or when it has gone away
returning; just as of plants those which are forcibly pulled aside by hands,
then released, run back again to themselves and to their first nature, and
show what is their own, being bent aside by force, but not being set upright
by force. For by nature vice is ready at hand, and the course toward the worse
is great, a stream running down a slope, or some straw easily kindled toward a
spark and a wind, and becoming a flame, and being consumed together with its
own offspring. For fire is the offspring of matter, and consumes the matter,
as vice does the wicked, and it departs together with the food. But if someone
should come to be in a habit of some good, and be made of it, it is more
laborious to fall away than to become good from the beginning. Since every
good confirmed by both time and reason becomes nature, just as also the love
in us, with which we worship the Love that truly is, and which we have loved,
and have set over all of life.
B. Where then are those who watch our things carefully, both those well and
those otherwise, not that they may judge, but that they may abuse; nor that
they may rejoice with us, but that they may rejoice over us, and slander the
good things, and make a tragedy of the base, and have a defense of their own
=35.1153= evils, the wounds of those nearby? Would that they judged justly!
For there would be even some use of bile, according to the proverb, if by fear
of enemies it were possible to be more secure; but now together with the
enmity there is also the vice that darkens the reasonings, by which not even
the blame has credibility. Where then are those who hate equally both Godhead
and us? For this is the most magnificent of the things we suffer, that we are
in danger together with God. Where ever for us are the gentle judges of their
own things, and the precise examiners of others', that they may here too lie
against the truth? Where ever for us are those who have the wounds, and
reproach the bruises? those who mock the stumbles, and themselves suffer the
falls? those who wallow in the mire, and take pleasure in our stains? those
who are blinded by the beams, and bring forward the motes, which neither pain
much while they lie in, nor are hard to be packed off and blown away from the
sight?
C. Come, take part in our secret things; we call you into the council, even
while being hated; we use the enemies as arbitrators (O the arrogance, or the
boldness!), that you may go away ashamed and defeated (what more paradoxical
than these things?), having learned our health from the things in which we
were sick. For we did not differ about Godhead, but we contended for good
order; nor did we dispute which of the impieties one must rather choose,
whether that which contracts God, or that which cuts, whether the Spirit alone
from the divine essence, or also the Son together with the Spirit, the one
portion, or the two, of impiety. For these, to take them in a summary, are the
present sicknesses; since they set ascents in the heart, not of confession,
but of denial; nor of theology, but of blasphemy. For one is more ambitious
than another in the wealth of impiety, as if fearing not to be impious, but
the moderate things and those more philanthropic than others.
D. But we are not so. But concerning Godhead we both think the same and agree,
not less than the Godhead toward itself, if this is not a great thing to say,
and we have become one lip and one voice, in the opposite way from those who
built the tower of old. For they were of one mind for evil; but for us the
things of concord are for everything best, that with one accord with one mouth
we may glorify the Father, and the Son, and the Holy Spirit, and this may be
said concerning us, that God is truly in us, the one who unites those who
unite him, and =35.1156= glorifies those who glorify; and that it may not only
be said, but also believed. But there are other things about which we
differed; badly even about these, for I will not deny it (for one ought to
give the evil one no passage or handle, nor freedom to the evil tongues), yet
not so much as it seems to those who slander our things. For since it was
necessary even to sin something, being men, this is what we stumbled in: we
have become excessively fond of being shepherds, and we cannot find which of
two goods is more choiceworthy, until we agreed to admire both equally. This
is our charge; concerning these things let whoever wishes either call us to
account or let us go; this is the safety of the heretics; but beyond this
nothing, not even if you wish it very much. Flies will make oil stink, says
the one who said it, dying in it and rotting in it; but envy will wish the
good things, yet will not be able. For the strongest of all things, as it
seems to Esdras and to me, is the truth.
E. Our own things then we have both settled among ourselves, and we will
settle. For it is not possible for children to judge fathers badly, and at the
same time the common Trinity mediating, for which we are warred against, and
for whose sake we will not war. I am the surety of the peace, the small one of
so great a matter, since the Lord gives grace to the humble, and he humbles
the high unto the earth. But what is this to you, our common reconcilers? For
you are reconcilers, and you grant this even unwillingly. For you are not
pious, if we are bad about something small or greater; but we are not
praiseworthy for the vice, if indeed we stumble in something, and you are no
less impious, even if we sin, unless even more, by as much as you are heavier
upon those who stumble. And that you may know us agreeing in all things, and
may learn through these things that we will also always agree, the thing seen
has persuaded, as I think, a reasonable father, and an obedient child, sitting
together with one another and being well-matched; and if there was any spark
in you of goodwill and of concord, having kindled this, let the word also
persuade. Him then you have heard, and the wonder is still ringing in my ears;
and I know that greater in you than that poured into the =35.1157= air is that
stored in the souls; and you will hear us again, if you long for it again; and
if the things published often are not sufficient for anyone toward proof, and
the trials, and the stonings, both those which we have already suffered, and
those for which we have been prepared, judging not the suffering a loss, but
the not suffering; and so much the more, as we have tasted of the dangers for
Christ, of which we have also brought the best fruit, the increase of this
people.
F. What then do you wish? Are you persuaded of this, and we need no trouble,
nor a second theology, and do you spare my weakness, by which I scarcely even
utter these things to you? or must one, as with the heavy of the ears, sound
the same word often, that at least by the persistence of the voice we may
speak into ears of those who hear? You seem to me to challenge the word
through the quiet. For that silence is consent, the proverb also teaches.
Therefore receive a word of both, from one soul and one mouth. And I am
distressed that not having gone up upon some mountain of the high ones, nor
having taken a voice worthy of the desire, I utter these things to all who
think badly at once, as in a common theater, the world: Sons of men, how long
will you be heavy-hearted? Why do you love vanity, and seek a lie, introducing
not one, nor a simple nature of Godhead, but either three alienated from one
another and torn apart, and it is not wonderful to say, even fighting by
excesses and deficiencies; or one, but some petty and narrow one, not having
it to be a beginning of great things, as not having been able, or not having
been willing; and this in two ways, either through envy, or through fear; the
one, that nothing of equal honor may be introduced with it, the other, that
there may not be an enemy and one that fights? And yet by as much as God is
more honorable than creatures, by so much is it more magnificent for the first
cause to be a beginning of Godhead than of creatures; and to come through an
intervening Godhead upon the creatures, or the contrary, that Godhead should
subsist for the sake of these, which seems good to those who are overly
inquisitive and lofty.
G. For if we were going, while confessing the dignity of Son and Spirit,
either to introduce these as without beginning, or to refer them to another
beginning, there would truly be fear lest God be dishonored, or the anti-god
be in danger from us. But if however much you exalt the Son, or the Spirit,
=35.1160= you will not set him above the Father, nor alienate him from the
cause, but you lead up there the fair offspring, and the wondrous procession;
I will ask you a little, you lover of generation and lover of the
beginningless, which God dishonors more, the one who sets a beginning of such
things as you yourself introduce, or the one who sets a beginning not of such,
but of those like in nature, and of the same mind, and such as our word
wishes? But for you your son is a great thing and the greatest toward honor,
and so much the more, as he would take after the father in all things, and be
a genuine impress of the one who begot, and you would not accept rather to be
master of countless slaves than begetter of one child; and for God is there
something else greater than to happen to be Father of a Son, which is an
addition of glory, not a taking away, and likewise projector of the Spirit? Or
do you not know that you, setting a beginning of creatures, I mean of Son and
Spirit, neither honor the beginning, and you dishonor the things from it? The
one, because you introduce a beginning of small things, and unworthy of
Godhead; the others, because you make them small, and not creatures only, but
even more dishonorable than all creatures; if at least it was for the sake of
these that he came to be, and at some time, as instruments for a craftsman
before the crafted things, formerly not existing, nor would they have come to
be otherwise, unless God wished to create something through them, as if the
wishing were not sufficient. For everything that is for the sake of something
is more dishonorable than that for which it has come to be.
H. But I, introducing a beginning of Godhead timeless, and inseparable, and
unbounded, honor both the beginning, and the things from the beginning
equally; the one, because it is beginning of such; the others, because they
are thus and of such a kind, and from such a one, being divided neither by the
once, nor by nature, nor by the venerable; being one dividedly, and divided
conjunctly, even if this is a paradoxical thing to say; no less praiseworthy
for the relation toward one another, than each contemplated and taken by
itself. A perfect Trinity from three perfect, the monad having been moved
because of the richness, the dyad having been surpassed (for it is beyond the
matter and the form), from which bodies are, and the triad having been defined
because of the perfect, for it is first to surpass the composition of a dyad,
that the Godhead may neither remain narrow, nor be poured into the infinite.
For the one is unambitious, the other unordered; and the one is wholly Jewish,
the other Greek and polytheist. =35.1161=
I. And I consider that too, and perhaps not uneducatedly, nor ignorantly, but
even very carefully, that for you there is not even one danger in introducing
the Son as generated. For the unbegotten will not suffer anything of the
bodily and material things in begetting, because he is not even a body, and
this the common conceptions about God concede. So why do we fear a fear where
there is no fear, and are impious in vain, as the saying is? But for me there
is a danger of being deprived of Godhead, if I should accept the creature. For
what is created is not God, nor is the fellow-slave lordly, even if it takes
the first places of slavery and of creation, and this alone is treated
philanthropically while being insulted. For he who deprives of the due honor
does not honor more by what is given than he dishonors by what is taken away,
even if what is done has a pretence of honor.
J. And if passions are fabricated for you about the generation, they are also
for me about the creation; for I do not even know the thing created as created
without passion. But if he has not been begotten according to you, nor created
according to the true account, receive the remainder of the argument for you,
you who dare to say almost the equal through the appellation of creature. For
you then nothing is unattempted, nor undared, you evil umpire and arbitrator
of the Godhead; for you had no other way to be well thought of, than by
casting God far from lordship, as here those tyrannical in manner, and greedy,
do to the weaker. But I will utter one voice, the same and short. The Trinity
is truly a Trinity, brothers. And a Trinity, not an enumeration of unequal
things (or what prevents one from naming even a decade, and a hundred, and a
myriad, composed with so many? for the things numbered are many, and more than
these), but a comprehension of equals and of equal honor, the appellation
uniting the things united from nature, and not allowing the things that are
not dissolved to be scattered by a number that is dissolved.
K. Thus we think, and thus we hold, so as to concede to know how these things
have toward one another of relation and of order to the Trinity alone, and to
those to whom the Trinity would reveal it, having been purified, either now or
later; but ourselves to know one and the same nature of Godhead, recognized by
the beginningless, and by generation, and by procession, as by the mind in us,
and word, and spirit =35.1164= (so far as to liken the intelligible to the
sensible, and the greatest to the small, since no image attains to the truth),
agreeing with itself, always the same, always perfect, without quality,
without quantity, timeless, uncreated, incomprehensible, never leaving itself,
nor going to leave; lives and life, lights and light, goods and good, glories
and glory, true and truth, and spirit of the truth, holies and holiness
itself; each God, if it is contemplated alone, the mind separating the
inseparable; the three God, contemplated with one another by the sameness of
the movement and of the nature; neither having left something above itself,
nor having surpassed something else; for there was not; nor going to leave
something after itself, or going to surpass; for there will not be; nor
receiving with itself something of equal honor; for nothing of the created,
and slave, and participating, and circumscribed things attains to the
uncreated, and lordly, and imparting, and infinite nature. For some are
altogether far, and others approaching in some measure and going to approach;
and this not by nature, but by participation, and at what time, when serving
the Trinity well becomes above the slavery; unless this itself is also the
freedom and the kingship, to know lordship well, and not to confuse the things
that stand apart, by lowliness of mind. And for those for whom to serve is so
great, how great is their lordship? and if to know is blessedness, how great
is that which is known?
L. This is what the great mystery wants for us; this, the faith and
regeneration into Father, and Son, and Holy Spirit, and the common name, a
denial of atheism and a confession of Godhead. For this is the common name. So
that to dishonor any of the three or to separate it is to dishonor the
confession, the one the regeneration, the other the Godhead; the one the
deification, the other the hope. You see what things the Spirit grants us when
theologized, and with what it damages us when set aside. For I forbear to
speak of the fear, and the threatened wrath, not for those who honor, but for
those who dishonor. These things have been philosophized toward you in brief,
dogmatically, but not controversially; in the manner of fishermen, but not of
Aristotle; spiritually, but not maliciously; ecclesiastically, but not in the
marketplace way; beneficially, but not ostentatiously; that you may know us
thinking the same, you who make public speeches against us, and =35.1165=
celebrate, and agreeing in this only, inspired as one, breathing as one; and
do not, as those who starve, pick at our small things, whether one must call
them stumbles, or even playthings; as it is of the utmost misfortune, not to
have the safety in one's own strong things, but in the rotten things of
others.
M. Behold we give right hands to one another in your sight. Behold the works
of the Trinity, which is glorified and worshipped by us alike. This will make
you better and more orthodox. Would that we might even be heard, and might
this day become a called day, a holy one, not of contradiction, but of
spaciousness; not a memorial of trial, but a feast of victory; that our
thinking the same with one another, and with almost all the world, of which
some things were sound, and some have now received the health, and some are
beginning to be healthy, this may become for you too a cause of salvation, and
of refashioning. O holy Trinity, and worshipful, and long-suffering! for
long-suffering is she who has borne so far those who cut you. O Trinity, of
whom I have been counted worthy both to be a worshipper, and a herald
unfeigned from of old! O Trinity who will sometime be known to all, to some by
the illumination, and to others by the punishment! May you receive these too
as worshippers, who are now insulters; and may we lose no one, not even of the
least, even if I should have to be deprived of something of the grace; for I
do not dare to speak as much as the Apostle.
N. But these things are not dear to you? And the tongue is torn, and is in
labor with the contradiction? We shall see this too sometime, or those for
whom more leisure remains than for us; we shall know also your clever
offspring, or abortions, when having broken or crushed the eggs of the asps
with a hard and resistant word, we expose these as wind-eggs and empty, and
bring into the midst the basilisk of impiety hidden in them; even if a
basilisk, yet dead and incomplete, and unmoved, having died in the
labor-pains, and not existing before being born, to speak according to you,
that I may grant you also something small; not more hateful for the conception
than pitiable for the miscarriage. This will give us, I know, the one who gave
to tread upon asp and basilisk, and to walk upon serpents and scorpions; who
will also crush Satan swiftly under our feet, whether as lightning fallen from
heaven because of the ancient brightness, or as a serpent fleeing because of
the later crookedness, and the change into the things that creep on the
ground, that we may breathe a little from evils =35.1168=, pain and grief and
sighing having fled away completely, both now and later, in Christ Jesus our
Lord, to whom be the glory and the might unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
