To Bonus the patrician and deputy of the emperor
Of old Heracles had Eurystheus as overseer of petty contests, as it seems to
me. For what common good did life gain from a boar slain or a lion choked?
Today, by a better exchange, the world-saving Heracles entrusts the world's
struggle to you, his fellow in arms. He knows you not as a skilled archer for
killings, but as a great craftsman of saving. Hence he reasonably decrees that
you should complete the contests that stand in place of all, and not from
compulsion. For how could a soul suffer force that has wounded with love and
been wounded? But where the toils rather run together and incline to one end,
there the heat of those who love is kindled the more, and the press of
necessities brings them proof of a surer longing, and the toils escape the
notice of those who love, having stolen themselves away in the bond of
affection, or been stolen. For if a material joining of bodies makes divided
flesh one, how shall anyone speak the union of souls, where spirit runs with
spirit, and when the immaterial organs have been composed from a noble
intercourse of mind, one soul attends upon two bodies? We know that
blameworthy desires do not hold such power, but are like a dream. Even if some
fire-bearer kindle them, he has turned the torch to ash in a little while.
Even if one come in swift and winged, he is lifted like a bird and flies off
at once; drawing the bow toward pleasure, as the saying goes, he has loosed a
wound upon the wounded only. But love that is established and master of the
mind, having made the mutual bond one substance, knows no hesitation, puts
forward no fear, does not reckon the unseen turns of fortune, does not shrink
from chance upon her sphere, but as if composed within the heart it often
sails with us and travels with us, and counts all that is far as near, and is
reckoned present with the absent, and shows the shared bond everywhere. For it
is not split, nor is it divided, but it is outside and within, as the master
is in you, even if he seems to stand apart.
But O clear eye of the inhabited world, who have the mind as a chariot of
inborn light: even if you have gone apart toward the Persian cloud and are
locked with barbarian hail, come to us, and Rome's world sees the sun. Make
time new: shine in winter, and you will steer the spring. Pity us, whom you
redeemed of old, for whom you had so many labors of birth-pangs, wishing to
beget with a strange seed the world left childless by a tyrant. Pity us as
blameless infants. For even little children often grow bold in the simplicity
of their baby lispings; yet the bond of affection overpowers what causes pain.
Nature, once pricked, has pardon, even if the infant do some childish thing.
For the mother who bore the child is often struck, and turns kindly back
toward the child, and remembering again, as it seems, her former birth-pangs,
she covers and shelters all; she has no true grief at the child's blow, yet
she shapes fear upon her face, fashioning anger into mere instruction. A
father too will bear blows, and even welcomes them with more pleasure, if he
looks upon his children gloomy. Yet these things are not like your power, you
who have not raised your fear against us even as far as a seeming anger, but
kindle the things of love so far that you run the inhabited world in a little
while, and labor without yielding against the winter of so many barbarians,
and seem to take that long despondency for delight. For there is no idle place
of your care for us to find: you run over all as in a circuit of the world's
sickness, and you yourself have been set everywhere as surgeon to the common
wounds of the sick, pressing yourself with




others' disasters, you who have the toils of the toiling more than they. For
you have always been made substance for us in the toils, bearing the
weaknesses that stand in place of all: danger from barbarians, fear from
enemies, hatreds from without, cares from within, sweats on land, tumults at
sea, battles of tyrants, assaults of subjects, winter and burning heat and the
burdensome vigils that fall in between. In a word, body, wits, heart, your
whole self you have nailed to toils, so that the common body may not bear
toils. And you labor much and fight the disease, stealing, drawing away the
cause of the corruptions. You have not spared the places that work the
sickness, burning here, cutting there with the sword. Yet you keep the living
heart, making purges from hidden channels, diverting the force of the disease,
and wishing to draw the confluence of the passion toward a puncture in places
without danger. Even so, become now a helper of the critical hour, and guard
the clear pupil of the whole inhabited world, the City that all eyes behold.
For when a double pain has entered, as they say, the fierce pain has dimmed
the other. Your branches will persuade you, master, the soul-leading pearls of
life, pouring out tears as pearls. For being, as they were sown, of sound
mind, they have one consolation of hope: your swift coming, with God. Receive
from them the groans from the deep, urgent ambassadors of the world's
salvation, ambassadors who are absent, yet persuade the more by not being
present and being longed for the more. For time did not grant them, perhaps,
the face-to-face petition that was due. There was fear, as of two eyes, that
the commonwealth, loving two luminaries, might by the loss of the clear sun
and of the moon that shines through him suffer a double eclipse in one City.
But O you who overtake the course of the sun at mid-heaven, leaping forward
with the speed of a change: even if you paint an angry face, you flee the
fiction and will not linger in it, you who have been raised to such gentleness
that you even seem to slacken your authority. Give a hand to those who cry
out, and arriving before the time, raise those who are urgent to fall. Make
our wastes cities: for wherever you appear, all things become cities. Pity in
due season a mother's milk, you who now for our sake are an old man even
before your time; and now, having moved from the places of the dawn-star, make
morning in the quarter toward the west, and hasten, if need be, to forestall
the sun before the turning of the day, so that every barbarian fortune may
shudder at you, and every land and City may wonder at you, and the whole world
from immaterial colors may paint you in an ever-visible image, crowning you a
second time from words.
These words, then, the time of necessity has thought fit to speak with labor.
But the Word who shaped all things by the word, who does all things as he
wills for our good, may he form your mind and set it straight, as he himself
knows, as he judges it to profit, and may he show as a purification of your
faults the flood of sweat you have dripped in place of all. But O decline of
the diseases that have passed, may you remain, bringing all to recovery, and
power to the power that begot you. May he come more quickly †eimenodromos†
[...] while this one pours the stream of tears [...] of him who drew for you a
second and more God-fitting birth from dew of fire.
