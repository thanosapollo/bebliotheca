* Second Encomium on the Forty Martyrs

OF THE SAME, AN ENCOMIASTIC ORATION ON THE FORTY MARTYRS, DELIVERED IN THE
MARTYRIUM.
The hoplites of the Romans, according to ancestral law and an ancient custom
which sons, having received it from their forefathers, keep even until the
present, at the beginning of the present month putting on the panoply and
going forth to a certain plain sufficiently spread out and level, where it is
possible both to extend a course of horses, and to practice the tactics, and
to be exercised in the whole armed discipline, both make a remembrance of the
year and keep the day as a festival. But I, completing a memorial of martyrs,
and having proclaimed this to you on the day before, having armed through the
memorial the forty soldiers of Christ, who surpassed all eagerness, today set
them forth as wonders to those able to see: an ornament of the Church, and a
gladness of peoples, and a glory of God who strengthened them.
And it is altogether best, and exceedingly profitable, that both the young be
nourished and the men come to their prime together upon the narratives of
virtue. Hearing is the most timely part of the senses, and no less instructive
than sight. For through the ears it clearly introduces learning into the
souls; and it is no small danger whether the narratives happen to be good or
base. For of whatever kind those things that are spoken are, such a phantasy
must of necessity arise in the reasonings; and thought, and a continuous
contemplation of the mind, leads the man into desire of doing the things he
reasons. Therefore prepare for me a hearing that is calm and not carried
aloft; that both the blessed martyrs may be honored with the things that are
fitting, and you through the memorial may be trained in the pious and
God-loving.
But a double contest lies before me, and a fear lest I insult the subject by
the weakness of the exposition. For if one should consider the magnificence of
the very matter in hand, it bears the prizes of victory against every speech
=46.776=; or if one should consider him who before us, through his own wisdom,
crowned the saints: a man whose wonder is common to the inhabited world; an
exact canon of both the education among Christians and that from without, a
statue of philosophy, a type and a danger of bishops, a teacher consistent in
works and words; having among all men an uncontested good repute, except
perhaps among those with whom not even Christ is without blasphemy. For as no
one contradicts that the sun gives light and heat, so neither will anyone
contradict that the great Basil has been adorned with every beauty of virtue.
High then is the praiser of the high, a holy attendant of the holy, having
according to the power present to him fulfilled the honor to the champions.
But I must not for this reason be silent, since the one who went before
proclaimed the wondrous things greatly. For the aim now is not a contest with
him who has spoken, but a care for your benefit, you who hear; and each one
benefits altogether as he is able, the enjoyment of the greater things being
supplied from those who are richer.
The forty then were soldiers, having obtained as the order of life to serve as
hoplites for the emperor of the Romans, but Christians in the faith, and pious
in the religion. And when the one then in power, being one of the polytheists,
the demons having suggested to him that bitter counsel, was driving out
Christians by a new law and writing, having commanded all the subjects either
to offer frankincense as a sacrifice to the demons, or, not doing this, to be
condemned to death, and before the end to endure many mutilations in the whole
body: then indeed then the blessed ones, having made the tyrannical cruelty
and the God-hating law an occasion of their own valor, and having broken away
from the other soldiers, having assembled an exceptional and Christ-loving
phalanx marshaled by the power of the Spirit, openly set themselves against
the polluted writings; and all in concert, as from one tongue, cried out our
faith, saying that they thought little of this temporary life, and that they
give over their bodies as surrendered to various forms of punishments.
The servant then of the violent law, more tyrannical still, having learned the
resistance of the saints, hastened to contrive a punishment equal in weight to
their eagerness, having devised for unyielding souls a new and unexpected
cowardice. If I threaten them with the sword, he says, the fear is small for
striking them down, and they will in no way yield; for they are men who have
lived in arms from childhood, and are accustomed to bear iron. If I bring
other punishments upon them, they will endure nobly, not being inexperienced
in blows and wounds; nor is fire fearful to those who are disposed, as these
are, to resistance. Therefore it was fitting that a punishment be discovered
which will have both the operation of the pain grievous, and the time long and
drawn out.
What then did the sophist of evils carefully counsel against the saints? And
having taken thought, =46.777= he found an aerial danger, which the season and
the country offered ready to him. For the season was winter; and the place,
Armenia, the neighboring country, as you know, hard-wintered; not even
furnishing the hour of summer as warm to those who dwell there, but being
cherished so far only as to nourish the ear of grain; and the plant of the
vine among them is even unknown; and he who has not gone far from home does
not know a cluster; and this man asks about a grape as we do about the things
that come to be among the Indians. There, snow being present, the sower cuts
open the earth, and a snow-fall overtakes the harvest; and winds strip the
reaper, unless he, having bound himself tightly with his garments, contends
against the violence of the blasts. And autumn and spring almost are not,
being swept aside by winter as by a bad neighbor toward that one's domination.
Upon that country then, and in the present month, having stripped the saints,
he set them in the open air in wholly naked bodies, opposite the king of the
Assyrians and the Babylonian furnace, taking vengeance on the disciples of
piety. And there is nothing similar in pain, a burning fire, and a frost that
freezes together and withers; for the one belongs to iron, leading swiftly to
death; but the other, having the pain equal in weight, stretches out the end.
For altogether slower are the sufferings that come about from cold, such as
quartans, and cancers, and carbuncles, and as many things as the
philosophizing physicians have declared that the cold matter produces in the
bodies. Thus those who are colder and fat are more sluggish and have a body
hard to use; and the irrational among the animals likewise. The hotter are
swift and easily moved toward the pressing need; but those who are otherwise,
slow, and bound with numbness. Thus a horse is swift, being aroused toward the
course by a hissing before the whip; the ass is slow, and barely accomplishing
the journey through clubs. Sharp is the leopard, flying through the glens with
a dry and hot body; the bear, on the contrary, lingering with thicker limbs.
And these things I have physiologized for you not out of season, but that you
may learn thoroughly the endurance of the men, having examined the manner of
the punishment.
Winter makes rivers navigable by ships to be traveled on foot, and changes the
flowing water into the hardness of stones; and it dissolves rocks, whenever it
reaches the depth; and it injures opposite bodies equally, making the soft
into stone, and dissolving the hard. Thus wine, whenever it is frozen, is
shaped toward the jar that contains it, and the soft oil stands solid, having
been formed toward the place of the vessel; but glass and potsherd, having
been broken around, are crushed to pieces with the liquids. And animals that
have obtained a mountain and open-air life, some are utterly destroyed, and
others by the excess of the evil forget even their wildness. For stags and
gazelles lodge in folds; neither then fearing dogs, nor fleeing those who draw
near. For the greater terror always casts out the cowardice of the lesser
fears =46.780=. And birds settle in among men, and remain under the roof. And
men dig the sea, and cut the water with stone-working tools; and those near
one another do not mix. Such a season became a weapon to the tyrant against
the martyrs.
For it was necessary, as it seems, that the great and harmonious phalanx of
the blessed should contend in a new manner of punishment, that they might
acquire a glory of piety incomparable toward the many of the martyrs. They
stood then, and were being frozen, their limbs trembling, and having the
purpose unshaken, furnishing a contest-spectacle to angels, men, and demons
... Angels were awaiting the separation of the souls, that having received
them they might lead them up to their own lot. And men were looking for the
end, testing the power of the common nature: whether through fear and hope of
the future we should have strength to prevail over such great pains. And
demons were paying close attention to the things coming about, desiring to see
the fall of the athletes, and the unmanly giving-up toward the dangers. But
their hope was put to shame, through God who strengthens.
For they were looking at the mutilations, both each at his own, and all at one
another's. One was lying with foot or fingers having fallen away; another was
lying, the innate heat having been chilled to the end. And as the most violent
of the winds, having blown upon a kindred place, then having shaken down the
high things of the trees, bring them to earth roots and all: so the array of
the blessed was laid low by the winter: the well-born plants of paradise, the
adornment of the race, the roots of our sprouting, the hoplites of Paul, the
bodyguards of Christ, the overthrowers of the altars, the builders of the
Churches, those who enrolled themselves to serve as hoplites against
barbarians, and who completed the contest toward the common enemy of humanity.
And death was coming about for them not toward violence and unavoidable
necessity, but even very ready and in their power was the salvation, if they
had even wished to grow soft toward the resistance. For there was a bath from
nearby, which was neighboring by design to the place of the punishment; and
the door was opened, and those standing by were calling. For the tyrant, being
a sophist of persecution and of various knavery, set near the bait of
transgression for those being frozen, and was advising them to desert toward
the healing, as his father did the first-formed toward the tasting of the
tree. But they then even more stretched their manhood, knowing that endurance
then furnishes the trial, whenever, enjoyment being set forth in one's power,
it spares it; which in the times above the divine Daniel, having done, has
been shown. For being invited to take abundant and pleasant foods and drinks,
by hatred and turning-away from the things sacrificed to idols, he loved
=46.781= the eating of seeds; and fasting he was blooming, and without food he
was more well-fleshed than those living in luxury; for this is an exceptional
gift of God, to grant to the faithful servants in the wants the things beyond
hope.
But since envies have been fixed beside the high things of affairs, the
blessed company was in danger of being mutilated, and we all but lost the
fullness of the fourth decade. For one, having given up because of the frost,
at the very end of the victory, alas! left his fellow-soldiers, and went over
to the art of the tyrant, to the bath, the lover of life having already spared
the flesh that was withered; for he both fell from the hope, and did not
attain the temporary life, having died immediately upon the transgression: a
wretched Judas among the martyrs, neither a disciple, nor rich, and having
drawn the noose upon himself besides; and let no one wonder. For such knavery
is customary to the devil against those who yield to him; he deceives and
flatters in various ways, that he may throw down; and having thrown down, he
immediately tramples, and mocks the one who lies. And he revels over the
misfortune with the shame, and is gladdened at the shame of the one who was
baited. Therefore the Psalmist also named him enemy and avenger, signifying
the fickleness of the manner from the opposition of the names. For that one
never becomes a covenanted friend of men; but he then plays the friendly,
whenever he wishes to put on a mask of deceit.
But the helper of our weakness, and the husbandman of good things, both of
words and of deeds, found according to Abraham a sheep for himself for
sacrifice, a household one from those making war, a confessor from those
blaspheming, and a martyr from the company of those persecuting. For one of
the executioners of the tyranny, having been counted worthy of a vision of
angels who had come upon the martyrs, and having been shone round by the
appearing of the holy spirits, as once Paul by the glory of Christ, when he
was going on the road to Damascus, immediately changed his purpose, and having
stripped himself of his garment, mixed himself with those being frozen. And
having joined all things in a small turn of time he became a proselyte, a
confessor, a martyr, having washed in his own blood the washing of
regeneration, in blood that had been made dead and had not flowed; a crowned
man, a man worthy of the whole; through that man I have the forty for the
Church of God. Through the noble one the array of the martyrs is not docked.
Through the proselyte we keep a perfect festival upon a perfect number. And
the devil suffered a graceful thing and worthy of laughter; for as he was
stealing the soldier, he was plundered of the persecutor and executioner.
Then what are the things after these? The thrice-blessed reached where they
were hastening. But the lord of the punishment, not bearing the victory of the
martyrs with endurance, was warring against the dead bodies; having commanded
that the houses of the holy souls be given over to fire, and through one deed
he was imitating wild beasts and bitter men. For the former =46.784=, when the
men being pursued have fled, tear the garment thrown toward them; and the
latter, when the enemies have withdrawn, burn and destroy the houses. And one
of the martyrs might have said justly toward him: No longer, vain one, do I
fear your cruelty. For I was fearing so long as the souls were present with
those being frozen, lest the excess of the torture should conquer the manhood
of the pious; but this fear having passed, use the remaining clay as you will;
and even if you taste of the fleshes, I take no thought. For by how much you
show the cruelty raging, by so much more you crown the victory of those who
have gone away.
For the noble assaults of those set against them become exact proofs of the
manhood of those who prevailed. But why do we linger long? The bodies were
burned, and the fire received them in turn. And the world divided that
dust, and the remains of the furnace; and almost every land is blessed with
these holy things. I too have a portion of the gift, and I laid the bodies of
my fathers beside the relics of the soldiers; that in the time of the
resurrection they may be raised with the helpers of good boldness of speech.
For I know that they have power, and I have seen clear proofs of their
boldness of speech toward God; and I wish to say one of the things that come
to wonder of their operation:
Of a village belonging to me, in which the relics of these thrice-blessed ones
have been laid to rest, there is a certain neighboring small town; they call
it Ibora. In this then, according to the law customary to the Romans, a roll
of soldiers being present, a certain one of the hoplites arrived at the
village aforesaid, having been given by the taxiarches toward the guard of the
place; that he might restrain the onsets and the insults of his
fellow-soldiers, which those serving as hoplites are accustomed to bring upon
the country folk from boldness. And this man was sick in the one of his two
feet, and was lame; and the suffering was chronic and hard to heal for him.
And when he came within the martyrium and the rest of the saints, and having
prayed to God, he called upon the intercession of the saints: by night there
appears to him a certain man of worthy dignity, and having spoken certain
other things, "You are lame," he said, "O soldier, and have you need of
healing? but give me to feel the foot." And having taken hold of him in a
dream, he drew it violently. And as the nocturnal vision was doing this, in
waking there came about so great a noise, as would come about if a bone had
slipped from the natural harmony, then been fitted in by force; so as to
awaken both those sleeping with him and the soldier himself, and the man was
raised immediately, and was walking healthily according to nature, as * he was
accustomed. This wonder-working I saw, having met with the man himself
announcing it toward all, and proclaiming the benefaction of the martyrs, and
hymning the philanthropy of the soldiers.
And if it is necessary to add also something of the things belonging to me by
exception, I will speak. For when we were about to complete the first festival
upon the relics, and to rest the coffin in the holy shrine, my mother =46.785=
(for she was the one gathering and adorning the feast for God); and she *
commanded me to come toward the sharing of the things being done, both living
far off, and still being young, and being numbered among the lay. And as
things are accustomed to happen upon affairs that press, being without
leisure, I the mindless one received the call more heavily, blaming the
mother, that she had not postponed the festival to another time, but having
moved me from many cares, she had drawn me aside to this, and before one day
of the assembly. I arrived at the place; and there being a vigil in a garden,
where also the relics of the saints happened to be honored with psalmodies, to
me sleeping nearby in a certain little room there appears in a dream a
vision of this kind: I seemed to wish to enter into the garden, where in
waking the vigil was being completed. And when I had come about the door, a
multitude of soldiers was seen sitting before the entrance; and all at once
having stood up, stretching rods over, and charging threateningly, they were
not granting the entrance; and I would even have taken blows, if one, as it
seemed, more philanthropic had not asked me off. And when sleep left me, and I
came to a reckoning of the offense upon the call, I perceived toward what the
fearful vision of the soldiers was bearing; and with many lamentations I
bewailed my own vanity; and upon the very chest of the relics I poured a
bitter tear, that God might be well-disposed toward me, and the holy soldiers
might grant the amnesty.
These things I have said, that we may be persuaded that the martyrs live, and
are bodyguards of God, and assessors, who today have benefited and adorned our
Church; and this quadragesima that has the memorial of the forty martyrs is
more brilliant and greater, and the month more illustrious of the months, and
the miserable winter no longer seems to me hard; nor do I complain against the
wildness of the present season. For this, having become a weapon of the
persecutor, completed for me this holy phalanx. And as the mother of the seven
Maccabees, through having a God-loving soul rather than a flesh-loving,
neither blamed the cruelty of Antiochus the tyrant of Syria, nor was
distressed toward the punishments and the misfortunes of the sons; but she
received the desolation as a benefaction, and the childlessness as a help; nor
indeed did Stephen, being stoned, think he was being harmed, but rather being
made alive: so we too owe thanks to the God-fighters from the good outcome, in
return for those things of which they became causes to us of so many good
things, even if they were not acting with the aim of those benefiting, but of
those making war. For even enemies benefit, not wishing it, things not less
honorable than those of genuine friends.
The devil benefited Job more than he harmed him. The king of the Assyrians
became a benefactor of Daniel. And let the three children confess thanks to
the furnace; and let Isaiah, having been sawn, hymn the Hebrews. And let
Zechariah, having been slain between the temple and the altar, honor the
self-slayers; and let John, having been cut off at the head, proclaim Herod a
benefactor; and the apostles those who bound and scourged; and let all martyrs
love =46.788= those who persecute; for if those had not opened the stadium,
the athletes would not have published the manhood.
And what would one more admire of the men set before us toward eulogy? the
multitude, or the manhood, or the unlying concord? And let us not pass by
insensibly, nor ungratefully, first the number. For he who has so many
ambassadors would never go away without effect of prayer and supplication,
even if he be exceedingly burdened with sins. And of this thought and hope God
is a witness, in the discourse toward Abraham, when he was receiving the
supplication on behalf of the Sodomites, having sought not forty righteous,
but ten, toward the intercession of a city about to be made to vanish.
But we, according to the Apostle, having so great a cloud of martyrs lying
around us, let us bless ourselves, rejoicing in the hope, continuing in the
prayer, sharing in the memorials of the saints. For forty martyrs, strong
shield-bearers against the enemies, and trustworthy intercessors of the
supplication toward the Master: with the hope of these let a Christian take
courage, both when the devil is stitching temptations, and when wicked men are
rising up, and when tyrants are boiling with wrath, and when the sea is
growing wild, and when the earth is not bearing the things it has been
appointed to generate for men, and when heaven is threatening disasters. For
toward every need and circumstance the power of these suffices, and it
receives rich grace from Christ; to whom is due all glory unto the ages. Amen.
