tongue, but the discourse being shown trustworthy from trustworthy facts. 19
For that the equinox stood before the truly first time, this is clear from
God's creation. For first we seek a month because of the first time; and the
first time ought to have as its beginning the first day and night arranged in
equality. For no other beginning was fitting for the first time than the
equality of the things mentioned; for it was necessary, when time was about to
be created and day and night had just come into being, that their existences
be shown defined in equality, and then thus that irregularity be entrusted to
motion; for if motion had the irregular, creation 20 ought to have shown the
equality. Nevertheless I wish to set this forth from Scripture: "In the
beginning," he says, "God made the heaven and the earth," then


it adds that there was darkness and light was not yet, "and God said, Let
there be light, and there was light"; this light first came to be when
darkness had seized all things. God therefore said, "Let there be light, and
there was light, and God divided between the light and the darkness, and God
called the light day and the darkness he called night"; you have a first day
and a first night. What then? How did he say that "God divided between the
light and 21 between the darkness," which he arranged into night? For
"between" makes toward the two extremes. For since all things that God created
he created in a defined nature, reasonably he also brought forth the first day
and night in a defined and equally-having circumscription; and having
completed the measure he saw that it was very good, and then thus, as I have
already said before, he left motion thereafter to work the irregularity. If
then the first standing came to be in equality, therefore the equinox is the
beginning of the first time, as has been demonstrated. 22 Then after the
equinox, consider what I say, on the fourth day sun and moon come to be, the
luminaries are outside the equinox, on the sixth day he forms man; and then on
the seventh he rests from all the works, on the following, which is also the
first, as though the whole had now been completed he left it to be carried in
order. Keep this; for these things being kept and your memory standing by, the
interpreting discourse must succeed. 23 God thus made man, and as a good God
he made so great and such a man. Do not look at his folly, but imagine the
first constitution; for he made him "according to the image" of himself. And
that he might show his nature competing with the stars, he indicates that he
also set him naked in paradise, not needing a garment. For not yet had insult
come together with the sight, so that man should also need covering, but
Scripture testifies that he made man "and set him in paradise," and that they
were naked "and were not ashamed," but just as sun and moon take pride in
their own nakedness, so also these were left to be beautified with natural
comeliness. 24 God, then, having brought in so great a benefaction upon this
one and having established him living delicately in paradise, and having
richly imparted to him of his own graces, also seeks his perception according
to what is just, and judged that the grace should be honored by obedience; and
since he saw that it was impossible for the gift to be preserved for him <if>
not by refuge and honor toward God who had given it, he defines a law as a
guard of his nature, that by memory and help of the lawgiver he may be kept as
by certain good medicines, and he promises him immortality if the law is kept,
but death if he transgresses. 25 But these things being so, what came to be
came to be, and man, persuaded by the envy of the devil to transgress the law,
becomes corruptible and is set outside paradise, and thereafter, having given
himself to multitude, he both lived a beastlike life and became a receptacle
of atheism and wantonness; and because of this prophets cried out both
concerning his first dignity and after these things his unworthiness: "Man,
being in honor, did not understand; he was compared to the senseless beasts
and became like them." 26 These things being so and nature being destroyed,
the only-begotten Son of God, wishing with the Father's counsel to lead man
back and to snatch him from the impending judgment, also devises a time of the
help and a manner, a time that he may stand over the healing in due season,
and a manner that he himself may become suitable toward the restoration of the
cleansing, and having become man while remaining God he endured the universal
Passion. I have spoken often concerning this sacrifice, I was saying many
things formerly, making the demonstrations from the Jews themselves toward
Jews and from the Greeks themselves toward Greeks, and averting


this victim and destructive both of demons and of dangers and of
enemies and of all shamefulness by the facts, that I may not again, taking up
just now the discourse toward those, hinder myself from the 27 sequence of
the discourse that lies before us. Since then the only-begotten Son of God
wished to provide resurrection for the fallen man and to renew him and by
his own Passion to recreate him unto the first manner, see what he does.
Since he himself was creator of the first, he was going to be also healer
of him when he had fallen, for the correction of the whole nature. He
brings himself to the Passion, and takes over the whole time toward renewal,
the very time which

since he had also established a most pure time at the forming of the first
man, but this first time was defiled through the transgression, being
corrupted together with the one who transgressed, and thereafter showed the
following age defiled, to take over the first time itself at the setting-right
of man, that together with this, by the cleansing from the Passion, he might

might show all things, both the renewal of man and the purity of the age, and

back, and having done this at the Passion and having recovered the lost man,

according to the ancient sequence at the Passion, "on the first of the

beginning of the sensible light and now correspondingly as ancient beginning
of the intelligible light of the resurrection he set in front. 37 And having
kept all these things according to the first creation, he also kept the

For all these reasons Christ kept the ancient appointed dates at the new
Passion; because of this then we also keep all the times for a showing of the
mystical reasonings, honoring the imitation. But the Savior, accomplishing the
true Pascha in order to deliver all from the impending judgment, reasonably,
as at a true and proper Pascha, brought together all the aforementioned times
with accuracy in the week according to the ancient, that he might accurately
accomplish the recapitulation; but we, since the appointed dates cannot always
thus run together, doing an imitation of the true Pascha, gather as far as
power allows the imitation of the times around the place, having yielded the
accurate constitution of the times 39 to the prototype Pascha of the Savior.
Whence, the only-begotten having been sacrificed once and having sufficed for
the economy, a lamb is no longer sacrificed, but the Savior, coming to be at
the Passion, gives bread and cup as an imitation of the most proper sacrifice,
accomplishing the one as his own body, the other as the blood by unspeakable
invocations, and commanding to keep the Pascha in these types. Correspondingly
then to the imitation we also gather the times in imitation as far as power
allows, collecting both equinox and fourteenth according to the moon and the
three-day period around the place, the fourteenth from necessity according to
the week, as the ancient showed, and the three-day period itself, but the
equinox around the time of the Pascha, as coming together with it. 40 The
Savior, having displayed the most proper Pascha, showed it to be averting of
the evils and demons and idolatry and all plague hanging over us, and
wrought our nature free toward the recovery of blessedness; and since he
commanded, coming to be at the times and the Passion, that we too,
imitating this, do it upon the symbols which he gave, saying: "Do this in
remembrance of me," reasonably
each year at the same remembrance, coming to be after the equinox, for the
salvation of us all and the turning-away of the evils that come upon us and
the participation of the heavenly graces, imitating the Savior we 41
accomplish the Pascha in the most manifest brightness of the whole. For this
reason we have put off the ignorance of the heretics and that of the Jews at
the mystery of the Pascha, always taking over for this the equinox and seeking
the fourteenth after the equinox and thus Preparation and Sabbath and the
Lord's day named after the Lord, as has often been said, because the Lord
himself kept these appointed dates in order to show the riddle of the sheep,
which the law called Pascha, accomplished upon him, having been seasonably
substituted. 42 See then, brother, the necessity of the matter for a showing
of the truth. Moses commanded that in the first month on the fourteenth of the
moon the sheep be slaughtered, which the prophets cried out as a type of the
Savior, whence indeed they also took it for the Savior himself and said: "As a
sheep he was led to slaughter and as a lamb before its shearer is dumb; by his
bruise we were healed," wherefore seeing this the forerunner John cried:
"Behold the lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world." Since then it
was consistent that, the clear thing having come, the type should cease,
Christ asked no other appointed date in another than the slaughter of the
sheep, that he might show the 43 substitution. Because of this he joins all
the times, as I said, and the fourteenth runs together with the Preparation;
and, the law commanding that the sheep be slaughtered "toward evening" and
"between the evenings," the Savior also observes day and hour of the
slaughter, the day the Preparation, on which man was created, and the hour the
ninth, and around the ninth itself he expires upon the cross. For they say
that "toward evening" is from the seventh hour


they say after the sixth, and "between the evenings," if you begin from the
seventh, is the completed center of the ninth, in which also the wise among
the Hebrews record that the sheep is sacrificed. In this hour then of the
slaughter of the sheep, on the Preparation, the Savior, giving up the spirit,
both displayed the substitution of the riddle and clearly made the
recapitulation through the harmony of the times 44. And so great was the
necessity of the substitution, that, it being a feast of the Pascha, the Jews
were not able, for the honor of the feast, to transfer the pollution, which
others indeed would have done, but once having rushed willingly toward the
murder they had the judgment using the impulse together, as they did not know,
toward the most manifest unveiling of the substitution. 45 And there is kept
for you also the cause of the full moon according to the Passion, that, since
the sun was going to be darkened when such a pollution was coming to be
against the Savior from the sixth hour until the ninth and to turn back its
own rays like eyes, that it might not see the pollution, the full moon was
appointed <toward> the Passion, so that an eclipse of the sun might not be
thought by the Greeks according to a running-under of the moon, but, it being
so far distant from the sun, necessity <at> the one who suffered be confessed
that the darkness that came to be "from the sixth hour" according to the
history of the Gospels at the Passion of the Savior occurred. And
prophetically from above, taking it beforehand, he cried; Amos at any rate
says: "And it shall be in that day, says the Lord, the sun shall set at midday
and the light shall grow dark in the day"; and since this day was going to be
divided by light and darkness, Zechariah again another prophet says: "And it
shall be in that day, there shall not be light, and that day is known to the
Lord; and not day and not night; toward evening there shall be light," and he
makes the whole of it ambiguous 46 by the succession in turn of the darkness
and the light. And since the prophet cried that the sun would be darkened at
midday and some at times were going to disbelieve whether this truly came to
be at the Savior or not, there is under your eyes a great proof now being
seen, that looking from the proof at the restoration you may be released from
the question. What then follows today? That the Master's voice toward the Jews
threatening may be shown, Amos says, putting forward the Passion of the Savior
the beloved of God and setting against it their lawlessness: "The sun shall
set at midday and the light shall grow dark in the day, and I will turn your
feasts into mourning and all your songs into lamentation, and I will bring up
sackcloth upon every loin and baldness upon every head, and I will set them as
mourning for a beloved and those with them as a day of grief." For of the
sun's setting at midday he has set as signs a turning of the feasts into
mourning and of the songs into lamentation, and God said he would do all these
things because of his own beloved; for such mourning, he says, there will be
and such necessity of judgment as will correspond 47 to the dignity of the
beloved who was insulted. See then: if, from when the Savior suffered, the
sufferings do not follow, if their feasts were not turned into mourning, if
their songs not into lamentation, the sun did not set at midday; but if you
behold the great wonder, that Christ was crucified in a feast of the Pascha,
and the judgment for this reason changed the feasts into mourning, the Romans
having come on, and all the Jews having fled together as into a common snare,
Jerusalem, and in the very feasts, as the history says, having been consumed
by sword and fire and famine and unending wailings, so that, they being hemmed
in by so many evils, the change both of the feasts into mourning and of the
songs into lamentation expressly came to pass, it is clear that, these things
having come out before our eyes, the sun set at midday; for this is what the
things said signify. For two years at any rate the war both consumed the Jews
in mourning and has extended the mourning of Judea even until today. For where
is the temple? where the priesthood? where the kingdom? where the stronghold?
where the city of the law?


All is gone, all is quenched, all has been taken from the foundations <and>
with the sun having set has turned into mourning. 48 Since then these things
were done for the sake of great mysteries at the Savior's suffering, because
of this we also, trying at each Pascha to renew the signification of the
mystery, also gather the appointed dates as far as power allows, and we take
over also the fourteenth, not only because of the remaining contemplations
that have been said and those not said, but also because of the sun's setting
at midday, that the darkness may appear not a work of Greek art but of
justice, as has often been said, coming to be not with the moon having run
under but rising opposite according to diameter to the solar course and thus,
as one might say, being moved, that the fourteenth may be for all an eternal
sign of the wonder that occurred, we take over also Preparation and Sabbath
and Lord's day because of the causes said before, and completing the whole
contemplation of the mystery by the concurrence of the appointed dates we also
send away both the folly of the Jews and the madness of the heretics who do
not gladly accept this manner, and we put forward a complete character of the
recapitulation of the whole at the sacred rite of the Pascha. For in all these
appointed dates Christ having suffered, it is necessary that we likewise keep
the Pascha as far as power allows, and, if any of these should be lacking, the
imitation limps dangerously. 49 Since then you have heard sufficiently
concerning these things, it would be necessary thereafter also to speak the
reason for which now the extension of the Pascha comes to be, which I promised
when I began the discourse. We have said, brother, that at the Passion one
must take together both the spring equinox and the fourteenth according to the
moon, not before the equinox, and the Preparation and the Sabbath and the
Lord's day. If then it should ever happen that the fourteenth according to the
moon falls before the equinox, we invalidate that one, and we seek another
that ought to come to be after the equinox, and it happens in this that an
intercalary month falls in for the generation of the fourteenth after the
equinox. 50 Now, brother, for this Pascha that lies before us the fourteenth
falls as two days before the equinox and becomes invalid; wherefore we seek to
complete the month of the moon and to take over the following fourteenth, that
in this we may work the Passion according to imitation of the Savior; we
therefore need an intercalary month. 51 But just as we have seen that, if the
fourteenth according to the moon falls before the equinox, we need not this
but fittingly the one after the equinox, so again, the fourteenth according to
the moon having come together with the Lord's day, we do not keep this Lord's
day as resurrectional, but we need the following Lord's day, because the
fourteenth is of the Passion, for on this Christ suffered, the Preparation
having come together, and, just as we do not recognize the Preparation as
resurrectional, neither the fourteenth yoked with it, but the Lord's day that
ought to be defined at the resurrection has been appointed to be pure of a
Passion-day. If then the Passion-number of the fourteenth should fall together
with it, leaving this we pursue the following as pure of Passion. For the
imitation according to the Savior at the Passion and the resurrection lying
before us, we always keep the Lord's day which we are going to define at the
resurrection free of Passion as the Master did, taking the Passion upon those
before this. Since you have seen this, attend thereafter to what I say. 52 Now
as I said, the fourteenth according to the moon falls two days before the
equinox; we have necessity to pass this by, and to wait for the fourteenth
after this as after the equinox; but this fitting


fourteenth runs together with the twenty-sixth of the seventh month [since]
and falls upon a Lord's day. Since then the fourteenth falls together with
this very Lord's day, we transfer the feast of the resurrection to the
following Lord's day found free of the Passion-number, and thus joining a week
to the twenty-sixth of the seventh month we keep the resurrectional on the
second of the eighth month. And this is the cause of the extension. 53 And if
someone should wish to contradict concerning the fourteenth now coinciding
with this very Lord's day on the twenty-sixth of the seventh month, let him be
sent to examine the contradiction, if he wishes, taking also the wise among
the Greeks as witnesses to this, that the fourteenth occupies the whole Lord's
day, both this and the night after it, until near the following second day. 54
And let no one say that the Pascha has never come to be at so great an
extension. We have many remembering witnesses of this too. Then the things
that are truly never never fit with science, but the reason of the science is
always sought, and often it comes quickly, often more quickly, and again more
slowly, and no one who has knowledge attends to the irregularity of the times
but to the harmony of the reasons. How often, do you think, in the former
times was "It has never happened" said, and the art prevails. And what is the
discrepancy? For those who find fault confess that Pascha has often come to be
on the twenty-ninth of the seventh month. What remains then is about three
days or four, which they hesitate to yield to the science. Where then
thereafter is the difficulty from the paradox of the extension? 55 But they
wonder that the Pascha comes to be thus irregularly, at least as far as the
time is concerned. But this is not a wonder of those who have knowledge. You
have seen, brother, poetic compositions or tragic verses, how some are short,
some shorter, some extended more. The ignorant altogether wonder at these as
simply so disposed; but the craftsman who knows the harmony of meters knows
the evenness of the art, even if the appearance of the 56 verses should seem
irregular. For instance at any rate, brother, now the resurrectional comes to
be on the second of the eighth, but for the coming year it comes to be on the
seventeenth of the seventh month, and again for the following on the ninth of
the seventh month, and again for the third year the resurrectional will be
recognized on the twenty-ninth of the <same> month, and no one is harmed
toward these things, no one is grieved toward these things, but each seeks the
harmony of the art and is gladdened by the reason of the science. 57 For the
whole of what is aimed at is, that the fourteenth according to the moon may
not come to be before the spring equinox, and that the Lord's day defined
toward the resurrectional may be free of the fourteenth, on account of which
also the questions arise for those who do not compute the calculation
accurately. For the fourteenth must occur in the week before the defined
resurrectional, but if it falls into the breadth of the week, ease of the
finding is opened, but if it falls on the Lord's day, in this there is need of
accurate attention; for those who do not inquire sometimes work the error of
taking not a fourteenth but a fifteenth according to the moon to exist, which
indeed has also happened in the present case. 58 We therefore ought altogether
to stand and to recognize that even now the extension comes to be in harmony.
We ought to recognize these things, and before the coming week let him have
the judgment pure and know that this is the first of the seven weeks that
ought to be fasted. And we have fulfilled the discourse of the promise upon
this, only if the force of the things said should remain with the memory. And
may God grant both that all the mysteries be kept in your hearts and you in
Christ, with whom glory is offered to God and Father with the Holy Spirit now
and always and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
