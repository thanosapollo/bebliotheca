* On Lazarus

Discourse of Amphilochius, bishop of Iconium, on Lazarus of the fourth day.
Come, let us bring the evangelist John into the midst again; for it is good to
contemplate the firstfruits of the resurrection. For you were just now hearing
him say: Six days before the Passover Jesus came to Bethany, where Lazarus
was, whom he raised from the dead. Did you see the firstfruits of the
resurrection prefigured in Lazarus, which the faithful crowd recognized? The
crowds recognized it, and the chief priests reviled the wonder-working,
turning it into envy. Truly they begrudged the resurrection of Lazarus,
because after his resurrection, which you heard, they took counsel to kill
him. Did you see the excess of envy? Him whom the Lord raised, they planned to
kill, not knowing that even if they kill him, it is again a light thing for
the Lord to raise his friend.
They took counsel to kill Lazarus; nothing so stung the Jews as the
resurrection of Lazarus. This miracle alone they were not able to slander. The
recovering of sight by the man born blind they slandered, saying: This is he;
this is not he; it is one like him. The raising of the daughter of Jairus they
slandered, saying that she had fallen and had not been given over to complete
death. The resurrection of the widow's only son they slandered, saying that he
had feigned death and had not been swallowed by the tyranny of death. The fig
tree that withered they slandered, saying that it had been stripped of fruit
and not that it had been withered by the Master's word. The changing of the
water into wine they slandered, saying that those at the supper were already
drunk and did not know the mockery. The resurrection of Lazarus alone they
were not able to slander.
They knew Lazarus; the man was well known. They followed the funeral, they saw
the tomb being sealed, they consoled Martha and Mary, the sisters of Lazarus,
according to custom, as at a funeral banquet of death. They knew that he had
been laid in the tomb for four days exactly, and that a four-day corpse is
disjointed on every side, the flesh having wasted, the bones having been
unpegged, the sinew-frame having been torn apart, the entrails having been
scattered, the belly having been poured out. Knowing these things, when they
suddenly saw the resurrection of Lazarus whole, complete as from a lathe,
shining with the return to life, they were troubled. They all but fainted,
finding no words of pretext, as to how they would slander the resurrection of
Lazarus, the Lord's own contrivance having arranged this. What is this, "the
Lord's own contrivance having prepared this"? Hear in brief. When Lazarus put
off the present life by nature, the Lord was not present in Bethany with
bodily eyes, yet by the Godhead present everywhere and filling all things.
For after four days from the burial of Lazarus, Jesus came to Bethany to enact
the resurrection of his friend. Hence also, when the Lord arrived in Bethany,
as Martha the sister of Lazarus heard, she ran, she hastened, she fell down in
entreaty, offering a heretical embassy. For having fallen down she said to the
Lord: Lord, if you had been here, my brother would not have died. And now I
know that whatever you ask of God, God will give you. But the Lord himself,
being a sea of longsuffering, answered saying: What are you saying, Martha?
You call me Lord, and you do not understand Lord? Was I not here? Wait a
little; for soon you will learn from your brother. For your brother will rise.
I pardon you, Martha; for you are drunk with grief (as he said to her: one who
is drunk, even while calling, goes astray); when you sober, then you will come
to your senses. For your brother will rise. But Martha, even so carried along
by human words, answered the Lord saying: I know that he will rise on the last
day. But the Lord to her: Why do you stammer, O Martha? Why do you put off the
resurrection to far-off hopes? The Resurrection is conversing with you, and
you converse about the resurrection? I am the resurrection and the life; he
who believes in me, even if he dies, shall live.
While the Lord was saying these things, almost the whole city of Bethany ran
down to the Lord's coming, not to marvel but to laugh. For they had no hope of
raising a four-day dead man. What then did the Lord do? When he saw so great a
multitude run down, and that they were worthy and sufficient for witness of
the one about to be raised by him, he answered and said to them: Where have
you laid Lazarus? Did the Master of the place not know the place? He knew the
place, but he contrived the manner, so that all in turn, compelled, might
follow Jesus, that having received the resurrection with their own eyes they
might become undoubting heralds. Where have you laid him? And those asked said
to him: Come and see.
And as the Lord was walking and hastening to the tomb, the crowd following
behind disputed among themselves saying: What does this man want to look at
the tomb of Lazarus? If he is the Son of God, he will raise this one. For of
those who had newly died, whom he raised, we do not know. How then do we know
whether they were given over to complete death? Let him raise this one, and we
receive knowledge that he is truly the Son of God. Hence Martha too, carried
along by these words, came to the Lord, herself also saying: Where are you
going, Master? Why do you want to see the tomb of Lazarus? Why do you boil up
our pain again? Why do you stir up the mourning? Why do you want to see the
tomb? For you will profit nothing, Master; already he stinks, for he is of the
fourth day. But the Lord to Martha: Did I not say to you that if you believe
you will see the glory of God? You say to me: Already he stinks. Your sister
makes him fragrant with myrrh, and you smoke with unbelief? I want to see the
tomb of my friend. Having said this, the Lord went to the tomb. And having
drawn near to the tomb, as soon as he saw the monument, immediately he wept.
What need was there to weep for one whom he was about to raise? He wept. Why?
Hear with understanding. All things the Lord contrived wisely; for he wept at
the tomb, so that he might make public the mind of those who followed, what
suspicion they have concerning him. Therefore also when they saw him weep, the
crowds following, suffering human things again, began to dispute among
themselves and say: See how he loved him. He who raised the dead and gave eyes
to the blind could not have brought it about that this one also should not
die, if indeed he loved him so? But the Lord, when he saw them still speaking
human things, answered them: It was a light thing for me to hold back the life
of Lazarus, as also that of Elijah and of Enoch, but none of you knew that by
my will the life of Lazarus was being held back. For this reason I allowed him
to be swallowed by death, so that you might learn exactly that I have
authority over life and death. And immediately the Lord said to them, as you
all know clearly: Take away the stone. He was commanding Jews: Take away the
stone. See how he contrived all things wisely, establishing them as exact
witnesses of the resurrection of Lazarus. Take away the stone. He was
commanding the disciples to move mountains by a word, and he himself did not
roll away a stone? But contriving all things wisely, as I said before, for
their benefit, he said to the Jews: Take away the stone, so that from the very
corpse-bearing stench they might learn that he had decayed and had not been
stolen. Take away the stone.
And when they had rolled it away with murmuring (for the corpse-bearing,
ungovernable stench stung them), and when those present had rolled away the
stone, immediately the Lord cried out saying: Lazarus, come forth. The Master
is the one who calls; there is none who contradicts. Lazarus, come forth; give
full assurance to Martha your sister that I command and do not entreat.
Lazarus, come forth. Before I come down, come out; in you the pledge, in me
the fullness. Lazarus, come forth. And you, O friend, hearing "Lazarus, come
forth," do not suppose that the Lord has thrown out many voices. Once he spoke
and he raised the one he formed; for he neither wept as Elijah nor was at a
loss as Elisha. With a single-sounded voice he woke the one sleeping beside
him, saying: Lazarus, come forth. One word, and diverse the wonders. The Lord
only called: Lazarus, come forth, and immediately the flesh was filled out,
the hairs were replanted, the joints were bound together, the veins were
filled again with pure blood. Hades below, beating his breast, sent Lazarus
forth. The soul of Lazarus, being held back and summoned by the holy angels,
was recast, what was its own into its own. And the most glorious of all was
that he was bound on every side, the feet and the face with grave-bands, and
he walked unhindered. Therefore the Lord also more effectively commanded the
crowds present, saying: Loose him and let him go. See the Lord's contrivance
and how he orders all things wisely. For the full assurance of those present
he said: Loose him and let him go; as you rolled away the stone and showed the
tomb and shared in the smell, so also loose the burial wrappings; keeping the
one as a blessing, and guarding the other as witnesses. If the chief priests
and the Pharisees disbelieve that Lazarus has risen, show the burial
wrappings; display the face-cloths, as the paralytic the pallet. But let us,
bearing the palm branches, say: Hosanna in the highest; blessed is he who
comes in the name of the Lord. To him be the glory and the power, with the
all-holy and life-giving Spirit, now and always and unto the ages of ages.
Amen.
