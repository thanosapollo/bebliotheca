Of our father among the saints John Chrysostom, a letter to monks

The divine apostle says: Pray without ceasing, without wrath and reasonings;
for every thought that separates the mind from God, even if it seems to be
good, is wholly the devil, not to say it is of the devil; for the whole
contest of the devil is this, that he may separate and beguile the mind away
from God and drag it off into worldly cares and pleasures; he dictates
commandments and good deeds from within in the heart, and certain other
reasonable or even unreasonable thoughts, to which one ought not to attend at
all.

And the whole contest of the soul is not to separate the mind from God, nor to
pair with and consent to the unclean thoughts, nor to attend to the things
which the all-imitating and ancient painter, the devil, depicts in the heart,
now forms, now faces and shapes. And after all these things the devil takes
form, and the wretched man, standing in one place, thinks he is somewhere
else, being deceived, and seems to see certain persons and to speak with faces
and to arrange matters which are of diabolic delusion. One must therefore make
the mind secure and drive it as a charioteer and bridle it, and chastise every
thought and every operation of the evil one through the invocation of the name
of our Lord Jesus Christ. And where the body stands, there let the mind also
be, so that between God and the heart nothing else may be found as a dividing
wall or as a fence darkening the heart and separating it from God.

And if at some time he also seizes the mind, one must not linger with the
thoughts, lest the consent to the thoughts be reckoned to him as a deed before
the Lord on the day of judgment, when God will judge the hidden things of men.
Be still therefore continually and remain with the Lord our God, until he has
pity on us, and seek nothing else except mercy alone in a humble and pitiable
heart. Seek and cry from morning until evening, if possible, and the whole
night: Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on us. I exhort you
therefore, force your mind into this work until death. For this work needs
much force, because narrow is the gate and straitened is the way that leads to
life, and those who force their way enter into it; for the kingdom of heaven
is of those who use force. I exhort you therefore, do not separate your hearts
from God, but remain and keep it with the remembrance of our Lord Jesus Christ
always, until the name of the Lord is planted within in the heart; and think
of nothing else except that Christ may be magnified in you. I exhort you
therefore, never cease the rule of this prayer, but whether you eat or drink
or travel or do anything, cry without ceasing: Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God,
have mercy on us, so that this remembrance of the name of our Lord Jesus
Christ may stir up war against the enemies. For through the remembrance the
soul that uses force is able to find all things, whether evil or good; but
first it is able to see the evils within in the heart, and then the good.

For the remembrance is able to humble him. The remembrance is able to convict
the sin that dwells in us, and the remembrance is able to consume it. The
remembrance is able to move all the power of the enemy in the heart, and the
remembrance is able to conquer it and root it out little by little; for as the
portion of the name of our Lord Jesus Christ descends into the depth of the
heart, the dragon that holds the pastures



humbles, and it saves and gives life to the soul. Remain therefore without
ceasing in the heart, crying the name of the Lord Jesus, so that the heart may
swallow the Lord and the Lord the heart, and the two may become one. But this
work is not of one day or two, but there is need of much time and season and
contest and toil, until the enemy is cast out and Christ dwells within; for
our wrestling is not against blood and flesh, but against the principalities,
against the powers, against the spiritual things of wickedness, as the apostle
says.

Of the same. Thus must the one who prays pray, as gathering together and
straining his whole mind, to call upon God with a pained soul, and not
stretching out long words, nor extending the supplication into length, but
speaking few and simple words; for being heard lies not in the multitude of
words, but in the sobriety of the mind. And this can be seen from the things
concerning Anna, the mother of Samuel. The one who prays must therefore not
stretch out long words, and must pray the prayers continually. Christ through
Paul commanded it at short intervals. For if you stretch the speech into
length, having often grown faint you have given the devil much license to
approach and trip you up and lead the mind away from the things being said.
But if you make the prayers continual and frequent, you will be able both to
occupy the whole time by the frequency and to make the prayers with much
sobriety.

Of the same. Do you wish to learn vigil of prayer and sobriety and assiduity?
Go to Anna, learn what that woman did. They all rose, it says, from the table,
and immediately that woman did not turn to sleep, nor to rest. Whence it seems
to me that even sitting at the table she was light and not weighed down with
any foods; for otherwise she would not have let fall so many tears. And it was
not because she let out a loud and great voice, but because she cried great
things within in the heart. For her voice was not heard, it says, and God
heard her; for there is no such need of voice as of mind, nor of stretching
out of hands as of a strained soul, nor indeed of posture, but of intent. For
even if you are outside the church, cry and say: Lord Jesus Christ, have mercy
on me, even if not moving the lips, but crying with the mind; for God hears
even those who are silent. Place is not sought, but manner. And wherever you
are, pray. You are a temple; do not seek a place. There was sea in front,
Egyptians behind, and in the middle Moses speaking nothing. And God says to
him: Why do you cry to me? You also then, when temptation comes upon you, flee
to God, call the Master Jesus. For is he a man, that you should go away to a
place? God is always near. While you are still speaking he will say: Behold, I
am here. You have not yet finished the prayer, and he himself grants the
request. For if you have the mind purified of the unseemly passions, whether
you are in the marketplace, or on the road, or at sea, or in an inn, or
wherever you are, you will be able, having called upon God, to obtain the
request.

Of the great Basil. Often, he says, I asked and did not receive. Entirely
because you asked badly, or faithlessly, or with a wandering mind, or for the
things that do not profit you. And if you often asked even for the things that
profit, yet you did not remain; for it is written: In your patience possess
your souls, and he who has endured to the end, this one shall be saved. For if
you do not ask with toil and much persistence, you do not receive. For one
must first desire, and having desired, seek in truth, in faith and patience,
the things that profit each, being judged in nothing by one's own conscience
as asking carelessly or slothfully; and then you receive, when God wills. For
he knows the things that profit you better than you, and perhaps for this
reason he delays, dealing wisely with your assiduity toward him, so that you
may know what a gift of God is, and may keep what is given with fear. For
everything that one acquires with much toil, he is eager to keep, lest having
lost it he lose also his much toil, and the grace of the Lord



having set it aside, become unworthy of eternal life. For what did it profit
Solomon, having quickly received the grace of wisdom and having lost it? Do
not therefore lose heart, if you do not quickly receive your request. For if
the good Master knew that, receiving the grace quickly, you would not lose it,
he was ready even before the asking to grant it to you; but now, caring for
you, he does this. For if the servant who received the talent and kept it
sound was condemned because he did not work it further, how much more shall
the one who lost it be condemned?

Of the same. Attend to yourself, and look around yourself from every side.
Keep sleepless the eye of the mind toward your own guard. You pass through the
midst of snares. Hidden nooses from the enemy have been fixed from every side.
Look around therefore at all things, that you may be saved, as a gazelle from
nooses; for that animal is unconquerable to the nooses through sharpness of
sight, whence it is also named from keen-sightedness. See then that you not
appear worse than the irrational creatures toward your own guard, lest, caught
in the traps, you become prey of the devil, taken alive by him unto his will.
Attend therefore to yourself.

Of the same, concerning undistractedness of mind. This at any rate one must
know, that neither the keeping of any other commandment, nor love itself
toward God, nor that toward the neighbor, can we accomplish, if the mind is
wandering now to one thing, now to another. But also the practice of
well-pleasing toward God according to the Gospel of Christ is accomplished in
withdrawal from the cares of the world and in complete estrangement from the
distractions of thoughts. Thus also the Lord bore witness to the disciples
after the sincere and undistracted disposition, saying: You are not of this
world.

And from the contrary he testified that the world is unable to receive the
knowledge of God, nor to contain the Holy Spirit. For, he says, Righteous
Father, and the world has not known you; and the Spirit of truth, whom the
world cannot receive. Therefore the one who is truly going to follow God must
be loosed from the bonds of attachment to this life. And this is accomplished
through complete withdrawal and forgetfulness of the old habits. And having
done this, it is fitting to keep our own heart with all watchfulness, lest we
ever cast out the thought concerning God, or defile the remembrance of his
wonders with fantasies of vain things, but through continual and pure
remembrance to have it imprinted in our souls, to carry about the holy thought
of God as an indelible seal. For thus love toward God comes to be ours, at
once stirring us toward the working of the Lord's commandments, and itself
again being preserved by them toward what is lasting and unfailing. And the
Lord shows this, saying at one time, If you love me, keep my commandments, and
at another, If you keep my commandments, you will remain in my love. And still
more compellingly: As I have kept the commandments of my Father and remain in
his love. And the one who corrupts in the working the exactness of the
commandment is clearly weak concerning the remembrance of God.

Of the same. And undistractedness also contributes to the soul, and dwelling
apart; for to have a mixed life is harmful. Therefore, that we may not receive
provocations through ears or through eyes, and that we may be able to persist
in prayer, let us first dwell apart; for thus we shall also overcome the
former habit, in which we lived
as strangers to the commandments of God, and the saying: If anyone comes to
me, let him deny himself. When would anyone be able to fulfill one of these?
For we must, having denied ourselves and taken up the cross of Christ, thus
follow him. And denial of oneself is the complete forgetfulness of things past
and the withdrawal from one's own wills. But if we leave behind for ourselves
any earthly remembrance in some perishable substance, the mind here being as
it were buried in some mire, it is necessary that the



soul be without sight of God and unmoved toward desire of the heavenly goods
and of the good things laid up for us in promises.

And it is not permitted at all to be anxious even about the necessary things
themselves, such as food and clothing. The word grants that these hold a man
as with wicked thorns, the thoughts of the world hindering the fruit-bearing
of the seed sown by the farmer of our souls, our Lord Jesus Christ having
said: These are they who fell among the thorns, who are choked by cares and
wealth and pleasures of life and do not bring fruit to perfection.

Of the same. For we shall neither give any time to sins, nor leave a place for
the enemy in our hearts, having God as inhabitant through continual
remembrance.

Of the same. And the attention of the mind and the habit of being distracted
would easily come about for such children through right training, if they were
continually examined by those overseeing them, where they have the thought and
what they turn over in the reasonings. For the simplicity of the age and its
guilelessness and unsuitability for lying easily confesses the secrets of the
soul, and so as not to be continually caught in the forbidden things, such a
one would flee the unseemly thoughts, fearing the shame from the rebukes.

Of the same. And God said: Let us make man according to our image; he means
the inner man. But you will say: He does not speak to us about thoughts; he
said man according to the image of God, but how is the thought a man? Hear the
apostle saying: Even if our outer man is being destroyed, yet the inner is
being renewed day by day. I therefore recognize two men, one who appears
and one hidden in the one who appears. We have therefore an inner man, and we
are twofold, and then we truly say that we are men within, but the outside is
not. But having the perfection in the rational part of the soul, so that the
body is an instrument of the soul of man, and man in the proper sense is that
according to the soul itself. And as to Let them rule,
then: does it rule with body or with reason? Is the ruling principle in soul
or in flesh? But in what is the ruling principle, if not in the abundance of
reason? For by as much as man falls short in the power of the body, by so much
he excels in the constitution of reason. He did not say Let us make man
according to our image, and let them be wrathful; for the passions were not
taken into the image of God, but reason, master of the passions, was given you
to rule the irrational things, so as to master the irrational passion. If then
you rule the beasts, why do you not also rule the beasts within? If you rule
the things outside, do not leave the things within unruled. You were created a
ruler then; do not however have wandering thoughts, nor be light and unstable
in mind. It would be out of place, if you should bring down the things that
fly outside, yet yourself, being so lightly and distractedly lifted up, should
be brought down.

Do not therefore, being puffed up, be lifted above what you are and think
greater things than human nature, nor, being praised, be inflated, nor seek
glory, nor think yourself to be some great thing, since you will be as an
unstable bird, carried about here and there. Rule the thoughts in you, that
you may become lord of all; for the rule given us over the animals ought to
train us toward ruling ourselves. As there is no profit of a house, nor of a
city, nor of doors, if there are not those who guard and know when to close
and when to open, so neither is there profit to a monk of the other
achievements, if reason is not a doorkeeper of his own counsels. Having been
entrusted with the closing and the opening of the heart with exactness and
much skill, and knowing what things one must bring out, and what things one
must bring in, even if some thought forces its way, one must choke it within
and not allow it to be brought out into words, but dry up this root and have
the door secure and the guard exact. And not allow the wicked counsels to be
born, and when they are being born, to choke them.



Wherefore God also slays the wicked thoughts within, saying: He who has looked
at a woman to lust after her, and the rest. You see how he does not allow them
to sprout, nor to strike a beginning, not desire, not anger? For he says: He
who is angry with his brother shall be liable to the gehenna of fire. Let the
doors be shut, he says, and the wicked thoughts will quickly be mastered. For
from wrath and desire every sin comes to be for the soul. For this reason
David also prays to God, saying: Do not incline my heart. Not that God
inclines it (God forbid!), but what he says is of this sort: do not allow it
to be inclined or overturned into wicked thoughts; for from there is the
fountain of evil and of wickedness.
