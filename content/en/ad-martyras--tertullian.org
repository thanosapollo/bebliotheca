* Chapter 1
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Among the nourishments of the flesh, blessed martyrs-elect, which both our
lady mother the Church from her breasts and the several brothers from their
own means supply to you in prison, take also something from us which may serve
for the feeding of the spirit as well. For it does no good that the flesh be
fattened and the spirit starve. Nay, if what is weak is cared for, what is
weaker ought not equally to be neglected. Nor am I so great as to address you.
Still, even the most accomplished gladiators are exhorted not only by their
trainers and overseers, but even by unskilled and idle persons from a
distance, so that often hints called out from the crowd itself have been of
use.

First, therefore, blessed ones, do not grieve the Holy Spirit, who entered the
prison with you. For if he had not entered with you now, neither would you
have been there today. And therefore take care that he remain there with you,
so that he may lead you from there to the Lord. The prison is indeed also the
devil's house, in which he keeps his household. But you have come into prison
for this, that you may trample him even in his own house. For already, having
joined battle outside, you had trampled him. Let him not therefore say: They
are in my place; I will try them with cheap hatreds, with failings, or with
quarrels among themselves.

Let him flee your sight and lurk in his depths, contracted and numb, like a
snake driven out or smoked out. Nor let it go so well for him in his own
kingdom that he set you at odds, but let him find you fortified and armed with
concord, because your peace is war to him. That peace some, not having it in
the Church, have been accustomed to beg from the martyrs in prison. And
therefore you ought to have it among you, and cherish it, and keep it, so
that, if it chance, you may also be able to grant it to others.

* Chapter 2
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h2
:END:

Let the remaining hindrances of the mind equally have escorted you as far as
the threshold of the prison, as far as your parents also. From then on you
have been separated from the world itself; how much more from the world and
its affairs? Nor let this dismay you, that you have been separated from the
world. For if we consider that the world itself is rather a prison, we shall
understand that you have gone out of prison rather than entered into prison.

The world has greater darkness, which blinds the hearts of men. The world puts
on heavier chains, which bind the very souls of men. The world breathes out
worse uncleanness, the lusts of men. Finally the world holds more guilty
persons, namely the whole race of men. Last of all it awaits not the judgments
of a proconsul, but of God. Therefore you, blessed ones, should think
yourselves, if it chance, transferred from a prison into a watch-house. It has
darkness, but you yourselves are light. It has bonds, but you are loosed unto
God. A sad thing breathes there, but you are an odor of sweetness. A judge is
awaited, but you are to judge even the judges themselves. Let him be saddened
who sighs after the fruit of the world. A Christian even outside prison has
renounced the world, but in prison he has renounced the prison also. It makes
no difference where you are in the world, you who are outside the world. And
if you have lost some joys of life, it is a trade to lose something in order
to gain greater things. I say nothing yet of the reward to which God invites
the martyrs. Meanwhile let us compare the very manner of life of the world and
of the prison, whether the spirit does not gain more in prison than the flesh
loses. Nay more: through the care of the Church and the brothers' love-feast,
the flesh does not lose what is due to it; and besides, the spirit obtains
those things which are always useful to the faith.

You do not see alien gods, you do not run upon their images, you do not share
the solemn days of the nations by mixing with them, you are not beaten by
filthy steam of sacrifices, you are not struck by the shouts of the
spectacles, by the atrocity or fury or shamelessness of those who celebrate
them, your eyes do not fall upon the places of public lusts; you are free from
scandals, from temptations, from evil recollections, and now even from
persecution. The prison furnishes this to a Christian, which the desert
furnished to the prophets. The Lord himself more often spent time in
withdrawal, that he might pray more freely, that he might withdraw from the
world: lastly he showed his glory to the disciples in solitude. Let us take
away the name of prison; let us call it a withdrawal. Even if the body is shut
in, even if the flesh is detained, all things lie open to the spirit. Wander
in spirit, walk abroad in spirit, and do not set before yourself shady
racecourses or long colonnades, but that way which leads to God. As often as
you walk that way in spirit, so often you will not be in prison. The leg feels
nothing in the stocks when the mind is in heaven. The mind carries the whole
man about and transfers him where it will. But where your heart shall be,
there will your treasure be also. Therefore let our heart be there where we
wish to have our treasure.

* Chapter 3
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h3
:END:

Grant now, blessed ones, that prison is troublesome even to Christians. We
were called to the military service of the living God already then, when we
answered to the words of the sacrament. No soldier comes to war with luxuries,
nor proceeds from a bedchamber to the battle-line, but from tents,
unencumbered and girt up, where every hardness and roughness and
unpleasantness is established. Even in peace they already learn to suffer war
by labor and hardships, walking about in arms, running over the field,
throwing up a ditch, forming the tortoise, sweating. All things stand by
sweat, lest bodies and minds take fright from shade to sun and from sun to
frost, from tunic to breastplate, from silence to shouting, from quiet to
tumult. Accordingly you, blessed ones, whatever this hardness is, assign it to
the exercise of the virtues of mind and body. You are about to undergo a good
contest, in which the living God is president of the games, the Holy Spirit is
the trainer of the course, the crown of eternity the prize of angelic
substance, citizenship in the heavens, glory for ever and ever. Therefore your
overseer, Christ Jesus, who anointed you with the Spirit and led you forth to
this wrestling-ground, wished to set you apart before the day of the contest
from a freer condition to a harder treatment, that strength might be confirmed
in you.

For indeed athletes also are set apart to a stricter discipline, that they may
have leisure for building up strength. They are held back from luxury, from
richer foods, from more pleasant drink; they are compelled, tortured, wearied:
the more they have labored in their exercises, the more they hope of victory.
And they, says the apostle, that they may obtain a corruptible crown. We,
about to obtain an eternal one, interpret the prison for ourselves as a
wrestling-school, that we may be led forth to the stadium of the tribunal well
exercised by all hardships, because virtue is built up by hardness, but is
destroyed by softness.

* Chapter 4
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h4
:END:

We know from the Lord's precept that the flesh is weak, the spirit ready. Let
us not therefore flatter ourselves, because the Lord granted that the flesh is
weak. For on that account he said beforehand that the spirit is ready, to show
what ought to be subject to what, namely that the flesh should serve the
spirit, the weaker the stronger, that from it even the flesh itself may take
on fortitude. Let the spirit confer with the flesh about their common
salvation, thinking now not of the hardships of prison, but of the contest
itself and the battle. The flesh will perhaps fear the heavy sword and the
high cross and the rage of beasts and the extreme penalty of fires and every
device of the executioner in torments; but let the spirit set over against
itself and the flesh these things, bitter though they are, yet received by
many with even mind, nay even sought of their own accord, for the sake of fame
and glory, and not by men only but also by women, that you too, blessed women,
may answer to your sex. It would be long if I were to enumerate one by one
those who, led by their own mind, have done themselves to death with the
sword. Concerning women, one is at hand. Lucretia, who suffered the violence
of rape, drove a knife into herself in the sight of her kinsfolk, that she
might win glory for her chastity. Mucius burned his own right hand on the
altar, that fame might keep this deed of his.

The philosophers did less: Heraclitus, who smeared himself with ox dung and
burned himself, likewise Empedocles, who leaped down into the fires of Mount
Aetna, and Peregrinus, who not long ago threw himself on a pyre; while women
also have despised fires: Dido, when after her most beloved husband she was
being forced to marry, likewise the wife of Hasdrubal, who, Carthage already
burning, lest she see her husband a suppliant of Scipio, flew down with her
children into the fire of her fatherland. Regulus, a general of the Romans,
captured by the Carthaginians, when he had refused that he alone be exchanged
for many Carthaginian captives, preferred to be given back to the enemy and,
packed into a kind of chest and pierced from every side with nails from
without, felt so many crosses. A woman of her own will sought out beasts, and
certainly asps, snakes more horrible than a bull or a bear, which Cleopatra
sent upon herself, lest she come into the hands of the enemy. But the fear of
death is not so great as that of torments. Therefore the Athenian harlot was
given over to the executioner, who, being privy to a conspiracy, when she was
on that account tortured by the tyrant, both did not betray the conspirators
and last of all spat her own tongue, chewed up, into the face of the
tyran(??)t, that the torments might know they could do nothing against her,
even if they continued further.

For it is not hidden that what today among the Lacedaemonians is the greatest
solemnity, the διαμαστίγι(??)σις, that is, a whipping. In which rite before
the altar the noble youths one and all are struck with whips, their parents
and kinsfolk standing by and, that they may persevere, exhorting them. For it
will be counted an ornament and a glory with a greater title indeed, if the
soul rather than the body has yielded to the blows. Therefore if earthly glory
is allowed so much from the vigor of body and mind, that it despise sword,
fire, cross, beasts, torments under the reward of hum(??)an praise, I can say
that these sufferings are moderate toward the obtaining of heavenly glory and
a divine reward. Is a glass pearl worth as much as a true one? Who then would
not most gladly spend as much for the true as others for the false?

* Chapter 5
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h5
:END:

I leave aside now the motive of glory. Even among men, affectation itself and
a certain disease of the mind have already trampled underfoot all these same
contests of cruelty and torment. How many idle men does the affectation of
arms hire out to the sword? Certainly they go down to the wild beasts
themselves by affectation, and from bites and from scars they seem to
themselves more handsome. Already some have even bound themselves over to the
fires, that they might complete a set stretch in a burning tunic. Others have
walked about among the hunters' thongs with most patient shoulders. These
things, blessed ones, the Lord has not admitted into the world without cause,
but to exhort us both now and to put us to shame on that day, if we have
shrunk from suffering for the truth unto salvation what others have affected
for vanity unto perdition.

* Chapter 6
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h6
:END:

But let us omit these examples of constancy that come from affectation. Let us
turn to the very contemplation of the human condition, that those things also
may instruct us, if any things are to be met with constancy which have been
accustomed to befall even the unwilling. For how often have fires burned men
alive? How often have wild beasts, both in their own woods and in the midst of
cities, having slipped from their cages, devoured men? How many have been put
to death by robbers with the sword, by enemies even with the cross, tortured
first, nay even finished off with every insult? There is no one who cannot
suffer even for a man's sake what he hesitates to suffer in the cause of God.
To this indeed even the present times may be documents for us, how many and of
what sort of persons meet unexpected ends, unexpected to their births and
dignities and bodies and years, for a man's sake, either from the man himself,
if they have acted against him, or from his adversaries, if they have stood
for him.
