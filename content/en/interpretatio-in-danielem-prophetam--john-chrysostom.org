CHAPTER I.
And the king said to Asphanez his chief eunuch to bring in from the sons of
the captivity of the sons of Israel, and from the seed of the kingdom, and
from the Phorthommin. Phorthommin he says is either a nation or a race; rather
a nation, barbarian perhaps. This is permitted, so that from comparison the
power of God may appear; and what happened in the case of the many happened
also in the case of wisdom. For so that no one may charge Persian wisdom with
the things that take place, others also learn with them for a refutation. For
from comparison especially among the foolish things are judged. Therefore God
works from comparison everywhere; and when he speaks about himself, he does
not refuse to set himself beside the gods and compare; and they themselves
say: There is none like you among gods, Lord. In whom there is no blemish in
them, and fair in appearance, and understanding in all wisdom. And this is a
hindrance toward temperance and philosophy. And why does he seek such as
these, so as even by the good condition of the limbs, and by the beauty of the
face, to conquer all the others? Let us hear. If a king seeks such, a
barbarian king, how much more God, who is a lover of the beauty of the soul?
If those who have a blemish in the body would not be worthy to stand before
that one (For in whom, he says, there is no blemish in them), much more it
does not befit those who have a blemish in the soul to stand before God. And
reasonably he seeks the strong as well, for the inner ministries, he says, or
he means also the strength of the soul, that is, those able to stand before
the king. And why does he seek those fair in appearance? For those things are
for use, I mean wisdom =56.194= and understanding of mind; but this, for what?
As a barbarian and a man of this life, he seeks this from much love of honor;
for the philosopher ought to seek only the things from the soul. For just as
we seek beautiful clothes for no use, so he also seeks beautiful faces, as
statues. And why did God make beauty? Hear another saying: For from the
greatness and beauty of created things the maker is beheld analogously. And
one might see many things made for us in the body not for use only, but also
for bloom. For the colors, and the good complexions, are for this sake, not
for use only. For it is possible even to be dark, and nothing of use be
overlooked. And hair too is for this in us; so Paul also says: For if a man
have long hair, it is a dishonor to him. And a neck upright, and having a
measured interval; and all things have been brought out for us toward this
seemliness, so that if you cut away a little from the whole, you ruin the
beauty, while the use remains. Whence especially the Creator also fitted this
living being for us toward beauty; and not this only, but all the others as
well. But of this beauty he gave more to some, less to others. And many, after
they came to be, not having it from themselves, he clothed with grace. And
from the very placement you will see the beauty, as the eyes lying in a high
place, as the iris, and having within the round turned as on a lathe, the
mixture of colors, the exact =56.195= ness, the genuineness, the being white.
But beauty has become a stumbling-block, he says. Not from its own nature, but
from the slackness of those who are caught. For do not, he says, study
another's beauty. He did not simply say, Do not study beauty, but added,
Another's, as to enjoy one's own he praises. For why did it not unhorse
Joseph, nor make him a fool, nor fill him with madness and pride? Let a hind
of friendship, he says, and a foal of your graces converse with you. This too
is a bond of marriage, so that men were struck toward bodies. For since a
toilsome and wretched [lot] for us


he prepared the life, he also granted a certain consolation. From this there
is in us a kindling of the love that holds all things together. For God was
eager and took much trouble, so that the bond of marriage might remain
unbroken. And yet this, he says, became a stumbling-block from the beginning.
For the sons of God, seeing the daughters of the Hebrews [l. of men], that
they were fair, took them for themselves. This did not become a
stumbling-block, but their incontinence. He made them fair, not so that the
daughters might become shameless, but so that each might cherish his own. And
understanding, he says, in all wisdom; that is, eager, receptive toward all
wisdom; to teach them letters and the tongue of the Chaldeans. Moses then,
being a private man, was reared as a king; but these, being of royal race,
were brought up in the rank of household slaves. Well is it arranged
beforehand that they learn letters and the tongue of the Chaldeans, so that
when Daniel is about to speak with the king about those great things, no one
coming in between may corrupt any of what is said. And why does this also
happen? So that you may learn his philosophy, and see at once from the
beginning how he is stronger than the belly. And another would have said, I am
a captive, I have the necessary food from nowhere, God will surely pardon. But
this man is not so; for he served God not by some wage and fear only, but also
by love, enjoying much care, and the time was not short. Three years they
philosophized, the three years they fasted. Do you see understanding? Where it
was necessary to guard himself, he is extremely exact, and foreseeing, and not
simply throwing himself, but entreating and asking; but where nothing harmed,
he did not refuse to learn a barbarian tongue and such wisdom. For the
learning was not a charge, but the using. So most of all he would have learned
the wisdom that was with himself; from comparison again, so that he may learn
that there is no other wisdom such as that of the Hebrews, so that he may be
strong =56.196= er. And even if it were unlawful, here too he would have stood
and contended. Did you see that this man's virtues [come] from where the
gluttons [come], from where those who prefer garlic to the manna? From here
Daniel, being a philosopher, appeared. And there were among them of the sons
of Judah, Daniel, and Ananias, and Azarias, and Misael. And the chief eunuch
set names on them, on Daniel Baltasar, and on Ananias Sedrach, and on Azarias
Abdenagon [scr. Abdenago], and on Misael Misach. On Daniel, he says, he set
the name Baltasar. And their god was so called, or rather the king's son was
so called. Did he dare then even to call the captive so? Most of all he would
have dared; yet it is not the same name, but the difference is great, as in
the case of Joseph, his father worshipped him. And what great thing is it that
he was called by this name? do we not see even now many private men called by
the names of kings? But not in the palaces, he says. And why does the change
of names take place? See the affairs managed from every side. He does not
first see the dream, until the three years are fulfilled. Do you see that God
manages? Why then? So that he may enjoy more boldness. And yet he would have
been more clearly wondered at, and would have appeared, one might say, if he
had seen the dream before the three years. But the decree would not have come
even against them, and otherwise Daniel would not even have been believed. For
this reason in the small and lesser things the eunuch takes a trial of God's
goodwill toward them, so that when they ask him for the greater things, he may
not disbelieve and spit it out, and so that they may learn to speak well, so
that they may enjoy more boldness. Do you not see this happening from David?
how the king disbelieved him when he promised to take a foreigner, judging the
affairs from his age? See then the cause of the way, this man having learned
it. Moses and Daniel learned the things of the barbarians exactly. For so that
they may not seem by ignorance to prefer their own to what is foreign, for
this reason he lets them share in those things too, so that when you see, or
rather hear Moses saying that There is no wisdom


such as ours, you may not think the verdict to be of affection, nor of love,
but of right judgment. For one cannot say that through a grudge against those
who taught him he leapt away from their education. For both enjoy much honor,
and they prefer their own. Which Paul too, wondering, said: He did not choose
to have a temporary enjoyment of sin, accounting the reproach of Christ
greater wealth than the treasures in Egypt. =56.197= And Daniel set in his
heart not to be defiled at the table of the king, and in the wine of his
drink, and he asked the chief eunuch that he might not be defiled. And God
gave Daniel into mercy and into compassion before the chief eunuch. See this
man as a ruler of the good things. So from the very start he showed that he
was someone great and wonderful. For he calls this one by the better name. And
in the things in which it was possible, he kept the law. Who else, tell me,
would have thought the royal table to be a pollution? See him from the
preludes at once displaying the philosophy. And he asked, he says, the chief
eunuch that he might not be defiled. See how he is without love of honor. He
did not say, I will give my soul, but he asked, if possible, to escape notice.
Why do I love honor? he says. But Joseph was not so, nor Moses. What then?
Shall we condemn those men? God forbid; for they did not know what it was. For
there was not yet according to the law a refusal of foods. See him both
refuting and philosophizing, and in the small things displaying the
philosophy. And what the apostles said, These things you ought to have done
first, and not leave those undone. And he did these things, not because they
were idol-offerings, but because they were forbidden by the law. How then did
he persuade? See how at once he has resolved the difficulty for you. God, he
says, gave Daniel into mercy and into compassion before the chief eunuch. This
happened also in the case of Joseph; there too Joseph was pitied and found
grace with the chief cook; yet both were slaves, and in barbarian households
[f. houses]. For the things said were truly enough to dissolve the king's
anger. What do you say? do you name the master's table accursed? have you
yourself appeared cleaner to us? do you not know that for this you have also
learned a Chaldean tongue and letters, so that you may change over to our
rank? And from where did the eunuch respect him at all? The child was easy to
despise, a captive. For even if he was worthy of respect, the danger did not
let him appear worthy of respect. For this reason, having said that He gave
him into mercy, he tells the words of the eunuch and the fear again. How? It
was not possible, but forbidden, unless grace from above managed all things.
And Daniel said to Amelasar, whom the chief eunuch had set over Daniel,
Ananias, Azarias, and Misael: Try then your servants =56.198= ten days, and
let them give us of the seeds of the earth, and we will eat, and we will drink
water; and let our appearances be seen before you, and the appearances of the
young men who eat the table of the king; and as you see, do with your
servants. And he heard him, and tried them ten days. And after the end of the
ten days their appearances were seen good, and they themselves strong in the
flesh above the young men who ate the table of the king. Great the boldness,
greatest the eagerness, great the understanding, great the faith. Try your
servants ten days. Then so that you may not think the brightness of the face
to be of the nature of the seeds, see then water that does not nourish. And
they did not simply appear in good condition, but even in better condition
than those who enjoyed the royal table. And that meat and wine are accustomed
to nourish more than all things is clear to everyone. See from the preludes at
once the grace blooming from the choice of the children, [and] from the grace
of God. For the choice is from the not wanting, and the grace from the being
able. And let them be seen, he says, before you. We leave the judgment to you.
And the grace is light and easy; be convinced through the facts. For I know
clearly;


and before the end I do not give a verdict, for your sake. See how he also
trained the household servants at once, and taught that he is someone who
loves God. And he did not simply say, Do with us, but, With your servants; and
beginning he spoke thus: Try your servants. They did not refuse to display
human honors, where piety was in no way harmed; since Paul also does this.
Beginning his defense, he starts from praises of the judge, saying thus:
Obtaining much peace through you; and he uses civic affairs. And Nathan too,
prophesying, worships David, and Jacob Pharaoh, and Abraham those who dwelt
with him. And this man himself, Live, he says, forever. See a word full of
flattery. But I would not call this flattery, but understanding and wisdom. So
Paul also says: Walk in wisdom toward those outside, buying up the time. So
Christ also taught: Render the things of Caesar to Caesar, and the things of
God to God. What then? were the seeds not unclean? By no means, nor the water.
And they continued doing this three years. And after the end, he says, of the
days which the king said to bring them in, the chief eunuch brought them in to
Nebuchadnezzar. And there were not found of all of them like Daniel, and
Ananias, and Misael, and =56.199= Azarias. And they stood before the king; and
in every word of wisdom and knowledge, in which the king inquired of them, he
found them ten times above all the enchanters and the magi who were in all his
kingdom. And after the end of the days, he says, their appearances were seen
good, and they advanced into beauty and good condition. See the affairs
contrary to nature; see the Creator opening a little his own working. For just
as that one would most appear a molder, who is able not only to recast the
bronze and shape it, but no less the one able to set right the statue already
made; so also in the case of God and those children one might see. For to show
the bodies in good condition with such food was no less a proof, in the
account of creating, than to make a man from earth. From where the good color?
from where the flower gleaming? from where the strength? And you know that
drinking water also pulls down strength, and eating seeds. For he did not even
bear to eat bread. And the interval is not small between worked grain and
unworked. For strength does not come from eating only, but also from the
things given being digested; and these were not according to nature to be
digested. See these signs taking their beginning not from love of honor in
those who ask, but having their subject from a necessary need. For he did not
simply, with nothing set forward, leap upon the trial, but necessity calling
him to this. So the soul of the children was also far from love of honor. And
yet who, having such faith, and being in the midst of barbarians, would not
have wished to display to those in power the goodwill that God has toward
them? But not these. See also in the case of the accusing elders again, how it
comes from necessity.

CHAPTER II.
In the second year of the kingdom of Nebuchadnezzar, Nebuchadnezzar dreamed a
dream, and his spirit was amazed, and his sleep became away from him. And yet
this is the twelfth year. For if it was three years after the city was taken,
and that was taken in the ninth, this is the twelfth year. Some say that by
the same sign among the Hebrews both this number and that come about. Either
it is also a scribal error, or in the second year the =56.200= children were
to stand by. But it would not have reason. And a more puzzling thing happens.
And what is this? Not knowing what the dream is. And this happens clearly. For
if this had not happened, the wisdom of Daniel would not have appeared. For we
see that he would also have been called, and would have said the future; those
men were saying


too. When there is no outcome, who is true and who is false? And this one must
exercise in other cases. But let this very dream be brought into the midst;
let him say what Daniel said, let those men say the opposite. From where is it
clear that he lies, or tells the truth? Already then he gives the test. But in
the case of Joseph it did not happen so, but that man tells the dream; for the
time was near. And it is worth wondering how in Egypt the wise of the
Egyptians did not wish to fabricate something from the safety of it, but said
they were at a loss. And if they do not know how to distinguish dreams, where
else are they to be trusted? Otherwise this did not need to happen there; but
in the prophecy of Joseph it happened openly, and especially in the case of
the eunuchs. And see these not calling Daniel, but choosing rather to die than
to see that man succeeding. Was the dream shown for this only, that Daniel
might be wondered at? I at least would not say so. Since even if this were the
only thing, the achievement was great and wonderful, that so much knowledge of
God should come about. Yet this is not the only thing. But what? So that this
man too might be made sober, learning that his race will not rule through all
(for if even when these things were said he did not let go of his arrogance,
much more if these things had not been said); so that he might know God as
Lord of all. For since they were rather given to dreams, for this reason these
things have come about. From this he persuades them about the things to come,
and since they honored the gods most of all by this, by seeing the future
beforehand. For all their magic was about this. And the decree went out, and
the wise were being killed; and they sought to kill Daniel and his friends.
And then Daniel answered counsel and judgment to Arioch the chief cook of the
king, who went out to slay the wise of Babylon. And he inquired of him saying:
Ruler of the king, concerning what has this shameless judgment gone out from
the face of the king? Did you see boldness? did you see courage? what things
he says to the one who has authority to slay? And he grieves for the others.
It has no reason, he says, nor pretext, nor is a color laid on the command;
=56.201= for we call these shameless. And Arioch made the word known to
Daniel. And Daniel went out, and asked the king that he give him time, and
that he report the interpretation of the dream to the king. Wonder how that
man allowed it. See everywhere all being persuaded by him. Whence how did he
suspect that he was speaking truth? how did he not say, all were refuted, they
confessed that this is not of human nature; and you, being a barbarian, from
where do you think to prevail? But when God manages and prepares the affairs
beforehand, be at a loss about nothing. Otherwise it was also without danger
to come upon him later. Why did God not reveal it to him at once? First, so
that the matter might become evident, so that they might be set in much need.
For even if he was a prophet, still he did not know. Again he makes a defense
to you through the just, showing that he did not give to those who were about
to be in danger without enduring prayer, much more he will not give to you.
For this reason Paul asks for the prayers everywhere, writing, Continuing in
prayer. For a pure life is not enough, if [not] prayer also is present. See
again his great faith. This is a second contest; and again Daniel leads the
matter, and asks for time, prepared for much endurance and prayer. So he did
not ask to be heard at once; that man gave the grace, he communicated it to
his friends. Then a mystery was revealed to Daniel in a vision of the night.
And Daniel blessed the God of heaven, and said: Let the name of God be blessed
from the age, and unto the age, because the wisdom and the understanding and
the strength are his. And he himself changes times and seasons, sets up kings
and removes them, giving wisdom to the wise, and prudence to those who ask for
understanding. He himself reveals deep and hidden things, knowing the things
in the darkness; and the light is with him. To you, the God of my fathers, I
confess, and I praise, because you have given me wisdom and understanding, and
now you have made known to me what we asked of you. Not yet openly


was it revealed to him, but still in a vision; for the prophet is trained
beforehand. And see his boldness. What is this shameless judgment, he says? It
seems to me that even before the finding he had stopped the chief cook from
the slaughter, both by accusing the attempt, and by promising to find a
release from the terror. What then was revealed to Daniel? For there is
preeminence even among saints; therefore he too is preferred. And how did he
see? In a vision, he says, not according to human wisdom. And well does he
call it a mystery, which was unknown to all. And he blessed the God of heaven.
He means the Almighty; that is, the one who is able even there in the land of
the barbarians. Nowhere a sacrifice, and a temple, and an altar, but a good
choice; and all things were accomplished. See, after receiving the request, he
did not first run to the court of the king, until he confessed the greatest
thanks to the one who had given; not as we =56.202= often, from the joy of
what is achieved, even forget gratitude. But this man is not so; but he
blessed God, and said: Let the name of God be blessed from the age, and unto
the age. For we, he says, are temporary and short-lived, we do not send up the
blessing for this time only, but for all; not for the time we live, but both
for this, and for that before this, and for the future. He must always be
blessed, both when he appears and when he does not appear. For his providence
is stretched through all. See how through the thanksgiving he also shows the
knowledge of the dreams. Because the wisdom, and the understanding, and the
strength are his; that is, the knowing all things, and the foreknowing. For
here he means this: he himself knows all things, he is ignorant of nothing.
What then? Is this only? does he have foreknowledge only? And he did not say,
he has, but, It is his, wishing to set before us the natural good, that these
things belong to him from nature. What then? does he only foresee, and not
also do? Yes, he also does. He changes seasons and times. Not meaning the
turnings [of the year], but the changes of affairs. He sets up kings, and
removes them. That he works changes. Is this only then, that he foresees and
does? not also another greatest thing, that he also imparts this knowing to
others? Giving wisdom to the wise; not to those who were wise before this, but
to those [able] to receive. If anyone wise does not have the wisdom from that
one, he is not wise. Do not think it is a craft of the Chaldean [art]. And
prudence, he says, to those who ask for understanding. Then let us see whether
it has come to him from science, or according to nature. For this too he says:
He himself reveals deep and hidden things; he did not say, he finds, but, He
reveals to others things that are deep and hidden to us, things standing away
from a long time and concealed. Knowing the things in the darkness; and the
light is with him. See what he says. As David says: As its darkness, so also
its light. He means the abundance of knowledge, that is, even if they are
unclear, there is no darkness to him, or that he is their light. How does he
know the things in the darkness? As if having light with himself. It is with
him always. He speaks in a human way. Just as there is nothing in darkness for
the one who lights a lamp, so neither for God; or rather, if anything more, as
for the one who has the light in his eyes, for the one who always carries it
about it is light. To you, the God of my fathers, he says, I confess, and I
praise, because you have given me wisdom and understanding. He remembered the
fathers now in season, wishing through those to put him to shame, as if
someone should remind a fierce lover of the beloved. I confess, he says, I
give thanks, and I praise, because you have given me wisdom and understanding.
He means the former. And now you have made known to me what we asked of you.
Perhaps it is likely that he =56.203= asked other things as well, so as to
make the interpretation known to him too. Because the vision of the king, he
says, you have made known to me. And at once Daniel came to Arioch, whom, he
says, the king had set to destroy the wise of Babylon, and said to him: Do not
destroy the wise of Babylon; but bring me in before the king, and I will
report to the king the interpretation of the vision. He ran to him and says:
Do not destroy the wise of Babylon. Who would have taken thought for those
men?


See the philanthropy and gentleness of the prophet. But he would not have been
heard, if he had not added that too: Bring me in, he says, and I will report
to the king the interpretation of the vision. Then Arioch, he says, brought
Daniel in with haste before the king, and said to him: I have found a man from
the sons of the captivity of Judah, who will report the interpretation to the
king. From the sons of the captivity, he says, I have found a man. He was not
ashamed of the race. For when a greater necessity lies on, none of these
things is sought, but all the pride that is in prosperity is put down. For the
one who is sick would never busy himself about the physician's good birth, and
a man otherwise in danger would not inquire whether the one about to snatch
him from the dangers is well-born or whoever he happens to be, but seeks one
thing only, the deliverance. Who would not be ashamed, who would not blush,
seeing all those in the fatherland being slain, and the captives puffed up and
thinking great things? None of these things, he himself thought of none, but
in haste that man called, and this one asked, no longer with the same
madness. For since the king was refuted through the trial as seeking foolish
[f. useless] things, what does he say? And the king answered and said to
Daniel, whose name was Baltasar: Are you able to report to me the dream which
I saw, and its interpretation? He speaks with more equity. He did not say: If
you are not able, you will suffer the same. What then Daniel? And Daniel
answered before the king, and said: The mystery which the king seeks is not of
wise men, magi, enchanters, Gazarenes a power to report to the king; but there
is a God in heaven revealing mysteries, and he has made known to king
Nebuchadnezzar what must come to be at the last of the days. See the
understanding of the prophet. He did not say at once: I am able to report to
you; but what the king needed to learn before all, this he says. The mystery,
he says, which the king asks, is not of wise men, magi, enchanters, Gazarenes
a power to report to the king; there is a God in heaven revealing mysteries.
He makes a defense for those badly slain, showing that it is not he himself
who is speaking. I did not say this, he says, that this is not of magi, in
order to show myself more splendid than those, but so that you may learn that
neither do I speak according to human nature. But there is a God in heaven.
Not shutting him up there, but as speaking for now to a barbarian, and leading
him away from the earth, not as your people who turn about the earth. And he
has made known to king Nebuchadnezzar what must come to be at the last of the
times. See, he speaks through riddles, and he includes the whole head of the
sight =56.204= in the preludes, and he lets his mind go, saying nothing
burdensome, nor heavy. Your dream, he says, and the visions of your head on
your bed; that is, your reasonings on your bed went up, what must come to be
after these things. And the one who reveals mysteries has made known to you
what must come to be. He speaks according to the suspicion of the many, as if
the dreams stand over the head, or as if the reasoning part is there, or of
the head, of the eyes, so that he may say: You furnished the cause. Nor did he
simply say that God showed you, but, You were reasoning what must come to be
after these things. For since he had mastered the inhabited world, he was
reasoning whether he himself would send the kingdom on to all, or would die.
For somehow the greatness of the rule is accustomed to bring us also to
forgetfulness of nature, that he is mortal. So it is likely that he, having
fallen into the sea of his own achievements, did not strongly believe that he
would die. This at least another king also suffered. Therefore someone also
said to him, But you are a man, not God, speaking to the Tyrian. And see how
he refutes him without a blow. For he did not say that You suspected this, but,
What must come to be after these things. You were thinking these things and
reasoning what then will be. On your bed. When no one was troubling, but the
soul was in quiet; when most of all the crowd of reasonings comes in upon us,
using both the quiet and the idleness. For this reason it is a custom with the
many


to spend that time on prayer, since the soul is also at leisure, and the harm
is great if we are slack. And the one who reveals mysteries has made known to
you what must come to be. See, already a second time he mentions God, and not
as it happened, but there he says: The one who is in heaven; and here, The one
who reveals mysteries. And to me this mystery was not revealed in the wisdom
that is in me above all the living, but for the sake of making the
interpretation known to the king, and so that you may know the reasonings of
your heart. All but saying: The beginning of the finding is not from me, nor
even this itself from having learned from another more than the others. For he
did not do this because he saw a wise man. For if even when these things were
said by him, he worshipped him as God; what if this had not been? But for your
sake, he says. You do not owe me thanks, but I owe you; for so that you might
learn, I learned. See how he makes him familiar toward God, and the wonder
that is about to be about him, and having taken the affection beforehand he
ascribes it to God. For the one who has learned that God did this honoring
