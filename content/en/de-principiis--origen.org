On free will, and the solution and interpretation of the scriptural sayings
that seem to abolish it
3.1.1 Since in the ecclesiastical preaching there is contained the word
concerning the just judgment of God, which also, being believed to be true,
summons those who hear it to live well and in every way to flee sin, clearly
as they agree that the things worthy of praise and blame are in our power,
come, let us also treat a few things privately concerning free will, a problem
most necessary as far as possible. And in order that we may understand what
free will is, we must unfold its conception, so that when this has been made
clear the thing sought may be accurately set forth. 3.1.2 Of things that are
moved, some have the cause of the movement in themselves, and others are moved
only from outside. Now those that are moved only from outside are portable
things, such as woods and stones and all matter held together by habit alone.
Let there be excepted from the account for now the speaking of movement as the
flux of bodies, since there is no need of this for the present purpose. And
animals and plants and, in a word, whatever is held together by nature and
soul have in themselves the cause of being moved; from among which they say
the metals also are, and besides these fire also is self-moved, and perhaps
the springs too. And of those that have in themselves the cause of being
moved, some they say are moved out of themselves, and some from themselves:
the soulless out of themselves, and the ensouled from themselves. And the
ensouled are moved from themselves when an impression arises that calls forth
impulse. And again in some of the animals impressions arise that call forth
impulse, a phantastic nature moving the impulse in an ordered way, as in the
spider an impression of weaving arises and impulse follows toward weaving, its
phantastic nature calling it forth in an ordered way to this, and nothing else
after its phantastic nature being trusted in the animal, and in the bee toward
molding wax. 3.1.3 The rational animal, however, also has reason in addition
to the phantastic nature, which judges the impressions and rejects some and
accepts others, in order that the animal may be led according to them. Hence
since in the nature of reason there are starting-points for contemplating the
noble and the shameful, following which, having contemplated the noble and the
shameful, we choose the noble and turn away from the shameful, we are
praiseworthy when we give ourselves over to the practice of the noble, and
blameworthy in the contrary case. Yet it must not be unknown that the greater
part of the nature ordered toward all things is in some measure in the
animals, to a greater or to a lesser degree; so that the work in hunting dogs
and in war-horses is somewhere near, so to speak, to the rational. Now to have
this or that of the things from outside fall upon us, moving in us such or
such an impression, is admittedly not among the things in our power; but to
judge to use what has happened in this way or otherwise is the work of nothing
other than the reason in us, either working us from the starting-points toward
the impulses that call us to the noble and the fitting, or turning us toward
the contrary. 3.1.4 And if someone should say that the thing from outside is
of such a kind that it is impossible to look against it when it has become
such, let this one attend to his own passions and movements, whether there
does not come to be a good-pleasure and assent and inclination of the ruling
part toward this or that through these persuasions. For it is not, for
example, the woman, appearing to the one who has judged to be continent and to
hold himself back from mixings, and calling him forth to do something against
his purpose, that becomes a self-sufficient cause of setting aside the
purpose; for having altogether given good-pleasure to the tickling and to the
smoothness of the pleasure, not having wished to look against it nor to ratify
what had been judged, he practices the unrestrained thing. But someone else,
conversely, the same things having happened to the one who has taken up more
learning and has trained; for the ticklings and the provocations happen, but
reason, inasmuch as it has been strengthened to a greater degree and nourished
by practice and confirmed by the doctrines toward the noble, or has come to be
near to being confirmed, beats back the provocations and slackens the desire.
3.1.5 And to blame the things from outside, when these things happen to us
thus, and to release ourselves from accusation, having declared ourselves like
woods and stones dragged by the things from outside that moved them, is not
true nor fair-minded, and such a word is of one who wishes to counterfeit the
conception of free will. For if we should ask him what free will was, he would
say that it was if nothing of the things from outside met me, when I had
proposed this or that, calling me forth toward the contrary. And again to
blame the bare constitution is against what is evident, since an educating
word takes the most unrestrained and the wildest, if they should follow the
exhortation, and changes them; so that the exhortation and the change toward
the better have come to be by far, the most unrestrained often becoming better
than those who formerly did not seem by nature to be such, and the wildest
changing toward so much gentleness that those who have never yet become wild
in this way seem wild by comparison with this one who has changed toward the
gentle. And we see others most stable and most solemn, from a distortion
toward worse ways of life, being struck out of the solemn and the stable, so
as to change them into unrestraint, often beginning the unrestraint in the
middle of life and falling into disorder after the naturally unstable part of
youth has passed. Therefore the word shows that the things from outside are
not in our power, but to use them thus or in the contrary way, having taken
reason as judge and examiner of how one must meet these or those of the things
from outside, is our work. 3.1.6 And that to live well is our work, and that
God asks this of us as being not his nor coming from some other, or, as some
think, from fate, but as our work, the prophet Micah will bear witness saying:
"has it been announced to you, O human, what is good? or what does the Lord
seek from you but to do judgment and to love mercy and to be ready to walk
with the Lord your God?" and Moses: "I have set before your face the way of
life and the way of death; choose the good and walk in it" and Isaiah: "if you
are willing and listen to me, you will eat the good things of the earth; but
if you are not willing and do not listen to me, a sword will eat you; for the
mouth of the Lord has spoken these things" and in the Psalms: "if my people
had heard me, and Israel if it had walked in my ways, I would have humbled
their enemies in nothing, and I would have laid my hand on those who afflict
them", as if the "to hear" and "to walk in the ways" of God were in the
people's power. And the savior too, saying "but I say to you not to resist the
wicked" and "that whoever is angry with his brother will be liable to the
judgment" and "whoever looks at a woman toward desiring has already
committed adultery in his heart", and if he gives any other commandment, he
speaks as if it is in our power to keep the things commanded, and reasonably
we will be "liable to the judgment" if we should transgress them. Hence he
also says "everyone who hears these words of mine and does them will be
likened to a prudent man, who built his house upon the rock" and the rest,
"but the one who hears and does not do is like a foolish man, who built his
house upon the sand" and the rest. And saying also "to those on the right:
come to me, you blessed of my father" and the rest, "for I hungered and you
gave me to eat, I thirsted and you gave me to drink" he very clearly gives the
promises as to those who are causes of being praised, and from the contrary to
the others as blameworthy compared with them he says "go, you cursed, into the
eternal fire". And let us see how Paul too discourses with us as
free and as being ourselves causes of destruction or salvation: "or do you
despise the wealth of his kindness and of his forbearance and of his
long-suffering, not knowing that the kind thing of God leads you to
repentance? But according to your hardness and unrepentant heart you store up
for yourself wrath in a day of wrath and of revelation and of just judgment of
God, who will render to each according to his works: to those who by endurance
of good work seek glory and honor and incorruption, eternal life, but to those
from faction, who disobey the truth but obey injustice, wrath and anger,
affliction and distress upon every soul of a human who works the evil, of Jew
first and of Greek; but glory and honor and peace to everyone who works the
good, to Jew first and to Greek". There are then countless things in the
scriptures that present free will very clearly. 3.1.7 But since certain
sayings from the old and the new drag around toward the contrary, that is,
that it is not in our power to keep the commandments and be saved and to
transgress them and be destroyed, come, setting out from these too in part,
let us look at their solutions, so that from the things we set out someone,
having selected for himself according to the like all the things that seem to
abolish free will, may examine the things concerning their solution. And
indeed the things concerning Pharaoh have moved many, about whom God, giving
an oracle, says: "and I will harden the heart of Pharaoh" several times. For
if he is hardened by God and through being hardened he sins, he is not himself
cause of the sin to himself; and if this, Pharaoh is not free either. And
someone will say that from the like those who are destroyed are not free nor
will they be destroyed from themselves. And the thing said in Ezekiel, "I will
take out their stony hearts and I will put in fleshy ones, so that they may
walk in my orders and keep my just requirements", might move someone as if God
gives the "to walk in the commandments and to keep the just requirements" in
having taken away the thing that hinders, the "stony heart", and having put in
the better, the "fleshy". And let us see also the thing from the gospel, what
the savior answers to those who asked "why he speaks in parables to the many":
"in order that", he says, "seeing they may not see, and hearing they may hear
and not understand; lest they turn, and it be forgiven them". And still also
in Paul the "it is not of the one who wills nor of the one who runs, but of
God who has mercy", and in other places: "and the willing and the working are
from God", and in other places: "so then he has mercy on whom he wills, and
whom he wills he hardens. You will say to me then: why does he still find
fault? For who has resisted his will?" [and "the persuasion is from the one
who calls" and not from us,] "yes indeed, O human, who are you, who answer
back to God? [and again:] will the thing molded say to the one who molded it:
why did you make me thus? or does the potter not have authority over the clay
to make from the same lump one vessel unto honor and one unto dishonor?" For
these things by themselves are sufficient to throw the many into confusion, as
if the human is not free, but God saves and destroys whomsoever he himself
wishes. 3.1.8 Let us begin then from the things said concerning Pharaoh as
being hardened by God, in order that he might not send out the people; with
which there will be examined together also the apostolic saying: "so then he
has mercy on whom he wills, and whom he wills he hardens". And since some of
the heterodox use these, themselves too almost abolishing free will through
introducing natures that are destroyed, incapable of being saved, and others
that are saved, having no power toward being destroyed, and they say that
Pharaoh, being of a nature that is destroyed, is for this reason hardened by
God, who has mercy on the spiritual but hardens the earthy, come, let us see
whatever it is they also say. For we will ask them whether Pharaoh was of an
earthy nature. And to those who answer we will say that the one of earthy
nature altogether disobeys God; and if he disobeys, what need is there for his
heart to be hardened, and this not once but several times? unless perhaps
since it was possible for him to be persuaded, and he would altogether have
been persuaded, inasmuch as he was not earthy, being put to shame by the
wonders and signs, and God needs him for displaying the mighty works upon the
salvation of the many while he disobeys to a greater degree, for this reason
he hardens his heart. And these things will be said first toward them for the
overturning of what they suppose, that Pharaoh would be of a nature that is
destroyed. And the same must be said toward them also concerning the thing
said in the apostle. For whom does God harden? those who are being destroyed?
as about to suffer what, if they were not hardened? or clearly about to be
saved by their not being of a nature that is destroyed? And whom does he also
have mercy on? is it those who will be saved? and how is there need of a
second mercy for them, once they have been constituted as about to be saved
and altogether about to be blessed through the nature? unless perhaps, since
they admit destruction if they should not be mercied, they are mercied, in
order that they may not receive what they admit, the being destroyed, but may
come to be in the place of those who are saved. And these things toward those.
3.1.9 And one must raise a difficulty toward those who think they have
understood the "he hardened", what in the world they say God hardens a heart
by working, and with what purpose he does this. For let them keep also a
conception of God, according to the sound as of one just and good, but if they
do not wish, let it be conceded to them for the present, of one just; and let
them present how the good and just, or the just only, appears justly hardening
a heart of the one who is destroyed through being hardened, and how the just
becomes cause of destruction and of disobedience of those who are punished by
him through being hardened and disobeying him. And why does he even find fault
with him saying: "but you do not wish to send out my people. Behold I strike
all the firstborn in Egypt and your firstborn", and all the other things that
have been recorded as God saying through Moses to Pharaoh? For it is necessary
that the one who believes that the scriptures are true and that God is just,
if he is fair-minded, contend how in such expressions he may be clearly
understood as just. For if someone, having signed off, stood with uncovered
head toward the creator's being wicked, other words would be needed toward
him. 3.=1.10= But since they say they are disposed concerning him as
concerning one just, and we as concerning one good and just together, let us
consider how the good and just would harden the heart of Pharaoh. See then
whether through some example, which the apostle used in the letter to the
Hebrews, we can present how by one working God "has mercy on one and hardens
another", not proposing to harden, but through a kind purpose, which the being
hardened follows because of the underlying of vice, of the evil beside
themselves, being said to harden the one who is hardened. "For earth", he
says, "that has drunk the rain that comes upon it <often> and brings forth
herb fit for those for whom it is also farmed, partakes of blessing from God;
but bringing forth thorns and thistles, it is rejected and near a curse, whose
end is unto burning". Therefore the working according to the rain is one; and
while the working according to the rain is one, the farmed earth bears fruit,
and the neglected and fallow bears thorns. And it would seem ill-sounding to
say that the one who rains: I made the fruits and the thorns in the earth, but
if it is also ill-sounding, yet it is true; for if rain had not come to be,
neither fruits nor thorns would have come to be, but when this has flowed in
seasonably and in measure both have come to be. "Bringing forth" at any rate
"thorns and thistles, the earth that has drunk the rain that comes upon it
often is rejected and near a curse". Therefore the good of the rain has come
also upon the worse earth, but the underlying, being neglected and unfarmed,
sprouted thorns and thistles. Thus then also the wonders that come to be by
God are as it were rain; and the different purposes are as it were the farmed
earth and the neglected, being one by nature as earth <...> 3.=1.11= And just
as if the sun also, sending forth a voice, said that I melt and I dry, the
being melted and the being dried being contraries, it would not be saying a
falsehood
in view of the underlying, the wax being melted from the one heat, and the
clay being dried; thus the one working that comes to be through Moses
convicted the hardness of Pharaoh through his vice, but persuasion of the
"mixed" Egyptians, who set out together with the Hebrews. And the having been
recorded little by little as if the heart of Pharaoh was being softened,
saying: "but you will not stretch far, for you will go a journey of three
days, and you leave your women", and all the other things he said, yielding
little by little toward the wonders, makes clear that the signs were working
something even upon him, yet it was not accomplishing the whole. And these
things would not even have come to be, if the "I will harden the heart of
Pharaoh" as understood by the many were being worked by him, clearly by God.
And it is not out of place to comfort such things also from custom, the kind
masters often saying to the household slaves who are being worn down through
the kindness and long-suffering: I made you wicked, and: I have become cause
to you of such great sins. For one must hear the character and the force of
what is said, and not slander through not hearing down the will of the word.
Paul at any rate, having examined these things clearly, says to the one who
sins: "or do you despise the wealth of his kindness and of his forbearance and
of his long-suffering, not knowing that the kind thing of God leads you to
repentance? But according to your hardness and unrepentant heart you store up
for yourself wrath in a day of wrath and of revelation and of just judgment of
God". For the things the apostle says to the one who sins, let them be said to
Pharaoh, and these would be understood as altogether fitting when announced to
him, "according to his hardness and unrepentant heart storing up wrath for
himself", the hardness not having been convicted thus nor having become
manifest, if the signs had not come to be, or had come to be, but not so many
and so great. 3.=1.12= But since such accounts are hard to persuade and are
thought to be forced, let us see also from a prophetic word what those say who
have experienced much kindness of God and †not† lived well, but after these
things have sinned. "Why did you lead us astray, Lord, from your way? For what
did you harden our heart, so as not to fear your name? Turn back for the sake
of your slaves, for the sake of the tribes of your inheritance, in order that
we may inherit a little of your holy mountain"; and in Jeremiah: "you deceived
me, Lord, and I was deceived, you were strong and you prevailed". For the "for
what did you harden our heart, so as not to fear your name?" said by those who
call upon being mercied, being said in such a character is of this sort: for
what did you spare us so far, not visiting us upon the sins, but leaving us
until our failings come to a greatness? And he abandons the many unpunished,
both in order that the characters of each may be examined from what is in our
power, and in order that the better may become manifest from the testing that
has come to be, and the rest, not having escaped notice of God ("for he knows
all things before their generation") but of the rational beings and of
themselves, may later obtain a way of healing, not having known the
beneficence if they had not condemned themselves; which profits each, in order
that he may perceive his own particularity and the grace of God. And the one
who does not perceive his own weakness and the divine grace, even if he is
benefited, not having experienced himself nor having condemned himself, will
think that the thing supplied to him from the heavenly grace is his own feat
of manhood. And this, having produced conceit and inflation, will be a cause
of downfall; which we think has come to be also around the devil, having
granted to himself the advantages he had when he was blameless. "For everyone
who exalts himself will be humbled, as everyone who humbles himself will be
exalted". And consider that for this reason the divine things "have been
hidden from wise and understanding", "in order that", as the apostle says,
"all flesh may not boast before God"; "and they have been unveiled to
infants", to those who after infancy have come to the better things and
remember that they have come to so much
blessedness not so much from their own cause as from the ineffable
beneficence. 3.=1.13= Therefore the one who is abandoned is abandoned by
divine judgment, and God is long-suffering upon some of those who sin not
unreasonably, but as it being profitable to them, as toward the immortality of
the soul and the unbounded age, that they not be co-worked quickly unto
salvation, but be brought to it more slowly after having experienced many
evils. For just as physicians also, being able to heal someone more quickly,
when they suspect that there is a hidden poison around the bodies, work the
contrary to healing, doing this through wishing to heal more securely,
thinking it better to hold someone for much time in the being inflamed and
being ill for the sake of his recovering health more firmly than to seem to
make him strong more quickly, and later for it to come up again and for the
quicker healing to become temporary; in the same way God too, knowing the
hidden things of the heart and knowing beforehand the things that will be,
through long-suffering permits, perhaps even through the things that happen
from outside drawing out the evil in secret, for the sake of cleansing the one
who through neglect has made room for the seeds of sin, in order that someone,
having vomited them when they have come to the surface, even if he has come to
be in evils to a greater degree, may later be able, having obtained a
cleansing after the vice, to be reconstituted. For God manages the souls not
as toward, say, the fifty-year span of the life here, but as toward the
endless age; for he has made the intelligent nature incorruptible and akin to
himself, and the rational soul is not shut out from the healing as in the life
here. 3.=1.14= Come, let us use also such an image from the gospel. There is a
rock having little and surface earth, into which if the seeds fall they flower
quickly, but having flowered, since "it has no root", "when the sun has risen
they are scorched and dried". And this rock is a human soul hardened through
neglect and turned to stone through vice; for a "stony heart" has been created
by God for no one, but it becomes such from wickedness. Therefore just as if
someone should accuse the farmer for not having thrown the seeds more quickly
upon the rocky earth, seeing some other rocky earth that has received the
seeds and is flowering, the farmer would answer that I will sow this earth
later, having put on the things that can hold the things that will be sown,
the slower and more secure being about to be better for this earth than the
one that has received more quickly and more on the surface, we would be
persuaded as by the farmer speaking reasonably and as having done
scientifically; thus also the great farmer of all nature postpones the
well-doing that would have been thought quicker, in order that it may not
become on the surface. But it is likely that someone would bring against us
toward these things: but why does "some of the seeds fall upon" a soul that
has the earth from the surface, being as it were a "rock"?
being as it were a "rock", a soul? And toward this too it must be said that it
is better for this one who has wished the better things more rashly and has
not traveled upon them by a road to obtain what he has wished, in order that
having condemned herself upon this she may be long-suffering later to receive
the farming according to nature over much time. For the souls are unbounded
for us, as one might say, and unbounded are their characters, and as many as
possible are the movements and the purposes and the attempts and the impulses;
of which there is one steward only, the best, and knowing the seasons and the
fitting helps and the leadings and the ways, the God and father of the wholes,
who knows how he leads even Pharaoh through so many things and through
drowning. At which the management of Pharaoh does not end, for it was not
dissolved when he was drowned; "for in the hand of God are both we and our
words and all prudence and knowledge of works". And these things moderately
unto a defense concerning the "heart of Pharaoh has been hardened" and
concerning the: "he has mercy on whom he wills, and whom he wills he hardens".
3.=1.15= And let us see also the thing from Ezekiel saying: "I will take out
the stony hearts from them and I will put in fleshy ones, so that they may
walk in my just requirements and keep my orders". For if when God wills he
"takes out the
stony hearts" and puts in "fleshy" ones, so that "his orders may be kept" and
the commandments be observed, it is not in our power to put away vice. For the
"stony hearts to be taken out" is nothing other than that the vice, according
to which someone is hardened, be taken around from the one from whom God
wills; and the coming to be of a "fleshy" heart, in order that someone may
"walk in the orders" of God and "keep" his commandments, what else is it than
to become yielding and not resistant toward the truth and practical of the
virtues? And if God announces that he does these things, and before he takes
away "the stony hearts" we do not put them away, it is clear that it is not in
our power to put away vice; and if we do not practice something in order that
the "fleshy heart" may come to be in us, but it is a work of God, living
according to virtue will not be our work, but altogether divine grace. These
things the one who abolishes what is in our power from the bare sayings will
say. But we will answer that one must hear these things thus, saying that,
just as the one who is in ignorance and lack of education, perceiving his own
evils either from exhortation of the teacher or otherwise from himself, gives
himself over to the one he thinks can lead him by the hand toward education
and virtue, and when this one gives himself over the educator announces that
he will take out the lack of education and will put in education, not as if
there is nothing toward being educated and fleeing the lack of education upon
the one who has brought himself to be healed, but as announcing that he will
make better the one who wishes; thus the divine word announces that it will
take out the vice of those who approach, which it named "stony heart", not of
those not wishing, but of those who have presented themselves to the physician
of those who are ill; just as in the gospels those who are ill are found
coming to the savior and asking to obtain healing and being healed. And it is,
for example, the "the blind to look up" a work according to the having asked,
having believed they can be healed, of those who have suffered, but according
to the restoration of the seeing of our savior. Thus then the word of God
announces that it will produce knowledge in those who approach, "having taken
out the stony" and hard "heart", which is the vice, for the sake of someone's
"walking" in the divine commandments and "keeping" the divine "orders".
3.=1.16= After these things there was the thing from the gospel, when the
savior said "for this reason he speaks in parables to those outside, in order
that seeing they may not see, and hearing they may not understand; lest they
turn, and it be forgiven them". And the one from the contrary will say: if
altogether having heard the clearer things these certain ones turn, and they
turn thus, so as to become worthy of forgiveness of sins, and it is not in
their power to hear the clearer words, but in the teacher's, and for this
reason he does not announce more clearly to them, "lest they see and
understand", it is not in their power to be saved; and if this, we are not
free concerning salvation and destruction. Now a persuasive defense toward
these things would have been, if the "lest they turn, and it be forgiven them"
were not added, that the savior did not wish those who will not be noble and
good to understand the more mystical things and for this reason "he spoke to
them in parables". But now, the "lest they turn, and it be forgiven them"
lying there, the defense is more difficult. First then the place must be noted
toward the heterodox, who hunt such sayings from the old covenant, where there
appears, as they themselves daring say, cruelty of the creator or a purpose
that is defensive and retributive of the worse things, or whatever they wish
to name such a thing, only in order that they may say there is not goodness in
the one who created; but they do not encounter the new in like manner nor
fairly, but they send past the things near to those they think are blameworthy
from the old. For clearly even according to the gospel the savior is shown, as
they themselves claim upon the former things, not speaking clearly for this
reason, in order that the humans may not turn, and having turned become worthy
of forgiveness of sins; which by itself is of no less than the
such things accused from the old. And if they seek a defense concerning the
gospel, it must be said toward them, if they do not do blameworthily in
standing dissimilarly upon like problems, and according to the new not
stumbling but seeking a defense, but according to the old concerning the
things near, when it is necessary to defend similarly to those from the new,
accusing. From which we will bring them together through the likenesses to
thinking that all are letters of one God. Come then, let us also furnish a
defense toward the present as far as possible. 3.=1.17= We were saying also
when examining concerning Pharaoh that sometimes to be healed more quickly
does not come to be toward the good for those who are healed, if having fallen
by themselves into hard things they should be easily released from those into
which they have fallen; for despising the evil as easily healed, not guarding
a second time against falling into it, they will be in it. Therefore upon such
God the eternal, the knower of hidden things, who "knows all things before
their generation", according to his kindness postpones the quicker help toward
them and, so to speak, helping them he does not help, this being profitable to
them. It is likely then that "those outside" also, about whom the word is,
having been seen by the savior according to the present as not going to be
firm in the turning, if they should hear the things said more distinctly, have
been managed by the lord not to hear the deeper things more clearly; lest
having turned more quickly and been healed in obtaining forgiveness, having
despised the wounds of vice as easy and easily healed, they fall into them
again and more quickly. And perhaps also paying justice for the former sins,

themselves through caution often daring to confess the having saved the ship,

having, by being animals, the being moved and, say, moving these limbs, hands
or feet, we would not reasonably say we have from God this specific thing,
either the being moved toward striking or killing or taking away the things of
others, but we received the generic, the being moved, from God, and we use the

the creator, and we use the willing either upon the finest things or upon the

4.1.τ Concerning the God-inspired of the divine scripture
4.1.1 Since <then> examining concerning so great matters, not being content
with the common conceptions and the evidence of the things seen, we take on
besides into the demonstration that appears to us of the things said the
witnesses from the writings believed by us to be divine, both of the so-called
old covenant and of the so-called new, and we try by word to strengthen our
faith, and we have not yet discussed concerning the scriptures as divine:
come, let us treat a few things concerning these too as in an epitome, setting
out the things that move us as concerning divine letters unto this. And first
at least of using from the letters themselves and the sayings shown in them,
concerning Moses and Jesus Christ, the lawgiver of the Hebrews and the
introducer of the saving doctrines according to Christianity, these things
must be treated. For of as many lawgivers as possible having come to be among
Greeks and barbarians, and of teachers announcing doctrines that promise the
truth, we have historically found no lawgiver able to produce zeal in the
remaining nations concerning receiving his words; and of those who promise to
philosophize concerning truth having brought in much preparation with the
seeming logical demonstration, no one has been able to produce the truth
thought by him in different nations, or in noteworthy multitudes of one
nation. And yet the lawgivers would have wished to strengthen the laws that
appeared to be noble if possible among all the race of humans, and the
teachers that the truth they imagined be spread everywhere of the inhabited
world. But as not being able to call those from the other dialects and the
many nations unto the keeping of the laws and the reception of the lessons,
they did not even attempt to do this from the beginning, having considered not
unwisely concerning such a thing's being impossible for them. But all Greece
and barbarian land according to our inhabited world has countless zealots, who
have left the ancestral laws and the gods that are thought, of the keeping of
the laws of Moses and of the discipleship of the words of Jesus Christ; and
yet those who add themselves to the law of Moses are hated by those who bow to
the images, and those who have received the word of Jesus Christ are in danger
even unto death in addition to being hated. 4.1.2 And if we attend how in very
few years, those who confess Christianity being plotted against, and some
being killed through this, and others losing their possessions, the word has
been able, and yet
not even with the teachers being numerous, "to be preached everywhere of the
inhabited world", so that "Greeks and barbarians, wise and unintelligent" have
added themselves to the piety through Jesus, we will not hesitate to say that
the matter is greater than according to a human, with all authority and
persuasion of the one who taught concerning the word of Jesus going to be
strengthened; so that one would reasonably think his voices oracles, such as
that "you will be led before kings and governors for my sake, unto a witness
to them and to the nations" and "many will say to me in that day: Lord, Lord,
did we not eat in your name and drink in your name and cast out demons in your
name? and I will say to them: depart from me, you who work lawlessness, I
never knew you". For to have said these things, uttering in vain, so that they
not become true, was perhaps likely; but when the things said with so much
authority have come out, it shows that God having truly become human has
handed over saving doctrines to humans. 4.1.3 And what need is there to say
also that it was prophesied [the Christ] then that "the rulers from Judah" who
are said "and leaders from his thighs will fail, when he comes for whom it is
laid up", clearly the kingdom, "and the expectation of the nations sojourns"?
For clearly from the history it is evident and from the things seen today,
that from the times of Jesus Jews have no longer been named kings, all the
Jewish matters in which they were proud, I mean both those according to the
temple and the altar and the worship performed and the garments of the high
priest, having been dissolved. For the prophecy that says has been fulfilled:
"the sons of Israel will sit many days there being no king nor there being a
ruler, there being no sacrifice nor there being an altar nor priesthood nor
lights". And we use these sayings toward those who in being afflicted from the
things said in Genesis by Jacob toward Judah claim that the ethnarch, being
from the race of Judah, rules the people, those from his seed not going to
fail, until the sojourn of Christ which they imagine. For if "the sons of
Israel will sit many days there being no king nor there being a ruler, there
being no sacrifice nor altar nor priesthood nor lights", and from when the
temple was razed, there is no sacrifice nor altar nor priesthood, it is clear
that "a ruler has failed from Judah and a leader from his thighs". And since
the prophecy says: "a ruler will not fail from Judah nor a leader from his
thighs, until the things laid up for him come", it is clear that "he has come
for whom the things laid up, the expectation of the nations". And this is
clear from the multitude of the nations that have believed through Christ into
God. 4.1.4 And in the song of Deuteronomy too it is shown prophetically the
election of the unintelligent nations that will be through the sins of the
former people, having come to be through no other than Jesus. "For they", he
says, "made me jealous upon not God, they angered me in their idols; and I
will make them jealous upon not a nation, and upon an unintelligent nation I
will anger them". And it is possible to grasp very clearly in what way those
who are said "to have made God jealous" the Hebrews "upon the not God" and "to
have angered him in their idols" "were angered" unto jealousy "upon the not a
nation", the "unintelligent nation", which God "chose" through the sojourn of
Christ Jesus and of his disciples. "We see" then "our calling, that not many
wise according to flesh, not many powerful, not many well-born; but God chose
the foolish things of the world, in order that he may shame the wise, and God
chose the unborn and the things that have been set at nought and the things
that are not, in order that he may abolish those things that formerly were,
and that" "Israel according to flesh" (called by the apostle "flesh") "may not
boast" "before God". 4.1.5 And what need is there to say concerning the
prophecies in Psalms concerning Christ, of a certain song inscribed "for the
beloved", whose "tongue" is said to be "a reed of a scribe writing swiftly.
Beautiful in beauty beside the sons of humans", since

using the prophetic words concerning him, we demonstrate together that the

4.1.7 And it would be much now to collect the most ancient prophecies
concerning each of the things that will be, in order that the one who doubts,

sun and moon and stars; and not so evident in the things according to human

is clear to the one who has lifted the eyes that "the word and the preaching"

above-heavenly power striking us unto reverencing the one who created us only,

having been manifested both through prophetic scriptures and through the
