ORATION 21.

On the great Athanasius, bishop of Alexandria.

1. Praising Athanasius, I will praise virtue. For it is the same, both to
speak him and to praise virtue, because having gathered all virtue in himself
he had it, or, to speak more truly, he has it. For all who have lived
according to God live to God, even if from here they have depar =35.1084= ted.
According to which God is also heard as the God of Abraham, and of Isaac, and
of Jacob, as not God of the dead, but of the living. And praising virtue, I
will praise God, from whom virtue is for men, and the being led up toward him,
or being led back through the kindred illumination. For though there are many
and great things for us, indeed one could not say of what size and how many,
which we both have from God and will have: this is greatest and most
philanthropic, the inclination toward him and the familiarization. For what
the sun is to things perceptible,
this God is to things intelligible. For the one lights the seen world, the
other the unseen; and the one works the bodily sights sunlike, the other the
intellectual natures godlike. And as this one, furnishing to both those who
see and those who are seen, to the former the power of seeing, to the latter
that of being seen, is himself the most beautiful of things seen: so God,
creating for those who think and those who are thought, for the former the
thinking, for the latter the being thought, is himself the highest of things
thought, at whom every desire stands, and beyond whom it is carried nowhere.
For he has nothing higher, nor will he have at all, not even the most
philosophical mind and most penetrating, or most curious. For this is the last
of things desired, and of those who have come to be of it, rest of all
contemplation. 2. Whoever then it has been granted, having cut through matter
and this fleshly thing by word and contemplation, whether one must call it
cloud or covering, to be with God, and to be mixed with the most unmixed
light, as far as is reachable for human nature: this one is blessed, both of
the ascent from here, and of the deification there, which genuine
philosophizing grants, and the becoming above the material dyad, through the
unity understood in the Trinity. But whoever has become worse by the pairing,
and has been so held together with the clay, as not to be able to look toward
the rays of truth, nor to become above the things below, having come to be
from above, and being called toward the things above: this one is wretched to
me of the blindness, even if he flows well in the things here; and so much
more, as he would be more played with by the good flow, and persuaded that
something else is beautiful before the being beautiful, reaping a wicked fruit
of a wicked opinion, either to be condemned to gloom, or to see as fire him
whom he did not recognize as light. =35.1085= 3. These things have been
philosophized by few, both of those now, and of those of old (for few are
those of God, even if all are creatures), lawgivers, generals, priests,
prophets, evangelists, apostles, shepherds, teachers, and every spiritual
fullness and system, and among them all, also by the one now praised. Whom
then do I mean by these? Such as that Enoch, Noah, Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, the
twelve patriarchs, Moses, Aaron, Joshua, the Judges, Samuel, David, Solomon up
to a point, Elijah, Elisha, the prophets before the captivity, those after the
captivity; and these last in order, and first in truth, as many as concern the
incarnation of Christ, or the assumption, the lamp before the light, the voice
before the Word, the mediator before the Mediator,

mediator of Old Covenant and New, John the great, the disciples of Christ,
those after Christ, either set over a people, or manifested through word, or
made known through signs, or perfected through blood. 4. Of these Athanasius
rivaled some, of some he fell a little short, and there are some whom he even
surpassed, if it is not bold to say; and of some the word, of some the
practice, of some the gentleness, of some the zeal, of some the dangers, of
some the more, of some all things imitating, and taking another beauty from
another, as those who paint forms with excess, and gathering them into one,
his own soul, he perfected one form of virtue from all, having conquered those
terrible in word by practice, and the practical by word; or if you wish, in
word surpassing those well-reputed in word, and in practice the most
practical; and those holding moderately in both, by the excess about the one;
and those extreme according to the other, outrunning them by both. And if it
is great for those who went before, to have become a pattern of virtue for
him, it is not less toward eulogy for our beautiful one, to have become a
pattern for those after him. 5. To say and to marvel at all his things would
perhaps be longer than according to the present impulse of the word, and a
work of history, not of eulogy; which also to hand over to a writing of its
own as both instruction and sweetening for those later, is a work of prayer
for me, as the one =35.1088= he wrote of the life of the divine Antony, a
legislation of the monastic life, in the fiction of a narrative. But having
gone through a few from many of his things, and as many as memory now
improvises for us as more familiar, that we may both discharge our longing,
and fill out what is fitting for the festival, we will leave the more to those
who know. For it is not otherwise holy, nor safe, that lives of the impious be
honored by memories, and those who excelled in piety be sent on in silence;
and these things in a city, which scarcely even many examples of virtue would
save, playing as with the horse-races and the theaters, so also with the
divine things. 6. That man was reared at once in the divine habits and
teachings, having philosophized few of the encyclical things, so as not to
seem altogether inexperienced of such things, nor to be ignorant of what he
judged to despise. For he did not endure that the noble and ambitious of the
soul be occupied in the vain things, nor to suffer the same as the
inexperienced of the athletes, who striking the air more than the bodies, miss
the prizes. And having practiced every Old Book, and every New, as not even
one other, he is rich in contemplation, he is rich in brilliance of life, and
he weaves both together wonderfully, the truly golden chain, and for the many
unplaitable; using life as a guide of contemplation, and contemplation as a
seal of life. For the beginning of wisdom is fear of the Lord, a kind of first
swaddling; and wisdom having gone beyond the fear, and having led us up into
love, makes us friends of God and sons instead of slaves. 7. And having been
reared thus and educated, as indeed even now those about to stand over a
people ought, and to handle the great body of Christ, according to the great
counsel and foreknowledge of God, which from far off lays down the foundations
of great things, he is enrolled in this great tribunal, and becomes one of
those drawing near to the God who draws near, and is deemed worthy of the
sacred station and order, and having gone through all the sequence of the
grades, that I may cut short the things in the middle, he is entrusted with
the presidency of the people, the same as to say, the oversight of the whole
inhabited world. And I cannot say whether he receives the priesthood as a
prize of virtue, or as a spring and life of the Church. For she, failing by
thirst of the truth, had to be given to drink, =35.1089= as Ishmael, or, as
Elijah, from the torrent, the earth having been chilled by drought, to be
refreshed, and breathing little

to be made alive again, and a seed to be left for Israel; that we may not
become as Sodom and Gomorrah, whose wickedness is famous, and more famous the
destruction, they having been flooded with fire and brimstone. For this a horn
of salvation was raised for us already lying down, and a cornerstone, binding
us to himself and to one another, was thrown in in season, or a purifying fire
of the worthless and wretched matter, or a farming winnowing-fan, by which the
light of the doctrines and the heavy is distinguished, or a sword cutting out
the roots of wickedness; and the Word finds his own ally, and the Spirit takes
hold of the one who will breathe for him. 8. Thus then and for these things,
by vote of the whole people, not according to the wicked type that later
prevailed, nor murderously and tyrannically, but apostolically and
spiritually, he is led up to the throne of Mark, successor no less of the
piety than of the presidency; for in the one he is found many from that man,
and in the other immediately after him; which indeed one must also take as
succession in the proper sense. For the one is of like mind and of like
throne, the other of opposite opinion and opposite throne; and the one has the
name, the other the truth of succession. For not the one who used force, but
the one who was forced is successor; nor the one who broke the law, but the
one put forward lawfully; nor the one opining the opposite things, but the one
of the same faith; unless one should say successor thus, as disease of health,
and darkness of light, and storm of calm, and ecstasy of understanding. 9. And
since he is put forward thus, thus he also disposes the rule. For he does not
at once both take the throne, as those who have seized some tyranny, or an
inheritance contrary to expectation, and insult through satiety. For these
things belong to the bastard and interpolated priests, and those unworthy of
the profession; who having brought nothing in beforehand to the priesthood,
nor having first toiled for the beautiful, are shown at once both disciples
and teachers of piety, and before being purified they purify; yesterday
temple-robbers, and today priests; yesterday outside the holy things, and
mystagogues today; old in wickedness, and improvised in piety; =35.1092= which
is a work of human grace, not of that of the Spirit; who, when they have gone
through all things forcing, last of all they also tyrannize piety; of whom not
the manner is trusted for the grade, but the grade for the manner, the order
being exchanged by far; who owe the sacrifices more for themselves than for
the ignorances of the people; and in every way they sin one of the two, either
by needing pardon, pardoning without measure, so that wickedness may not even
be cut back, but even be taught, or by the harshness of the rule, covering
their own things. Of which neither was that man; but he was high in the works,
and low in the thinking; and in virtue unapproachable, but in encounter even
very approachable, gentle, without anger, compassionate, sweet in word,
sweeter in manner, angelic in form, more angelic in thought, calm to rebuke,
instructive to praise; and to damage neither of the beautiful things by lack
of measure, but to make both the rebuke fatherly, and the praise ruling;
neither the soft slack, nor the austere astringent; but the one equity, the
other prudence, and both philosophy; needing least of word through the manner,
sufficient for training; and least of rod through the word; and still less of
cutting, through the rod striking moderately. 10. How should I paint the man
for you? Paul wrote beforehand; this, in the things in which he hymns the
great high priest, who has passed through the heavens (for the word will dare
for me even as far as these, since Scripture knows to call Christs those
living according to Christ); and this, in the things in which writing to
Timothy he legislates toward him, typing by the word the one who will stand
over the episcopate. For if you should set the law as a canon beside the one
praised in these, you will know clearly the straightness. Come then celebrate
with me as I labor about the word, and the more to run past

wishing, but being held now by one thing now by another, and not having to
find the winning thing, as in a body equal and beautiful from every side; for
always what has fallen to me appears more beautiful, and this snatches the
word. Come then divide his beautiful things for me, as many as are praisers
and witnesses of his things, and contend a beautiful contest toward one
another, men together and women, youths and virgins, old men with younger,
priests and people, the monastic and the mixed, those of simplicity and of
exactness, as many as =35.1093= of contemplation, and as many as of practice.
Let one praise the as-it-were bodiless and immaterial in fasts and prayers;
another the well-strung and unconquered in vigils and psalmody; another the in
protection of those in need, another the resistance toward the superior, or
the condescension toward the low. The virgins, the bridesman; those under
yoke, the chastener; those of the desert, the one who gives wings; those of
mixture, the lawgiver; those of simplicity, the guide; those of contemplation,
the theologian; those in good spirits, the bridle; those in disasters, the
consolation; the staff, grey hair; the training, youth; poverty, the provider;
plenty, the steward. They seem to me also widows will praise the protector;
and orphans, the father; and beggars, the lover of the poor; and the lover of
strangers, the strangers; and brothers, the lover of brothers; the sick, the
physician, whichever disease and healing you wish; the healthy, the guardian
of health; all, the one becoming all things to all, that he may gain all, or
more. 11. These things then, as I said, let others both marvel at and hymn,
who have leisure to marvel at the small of his things. And when I say small, I
say comparing him with himself, and setting his things beside his things (for
the glorified has not been glorified, even if it is very brilliant, on account
of the exceeding glory, as we have heard); since even few of this man's things
are sufficient for others toward good repute. But for us (for it is not
bearable leaving the word, to serve the lesser), one must turn to the most
sovereign of his things. And it is a work of God, for whom also the word is,
to say something worthy of his magnificence and soul. 12. There was a time
when our things flourished, and were well, when this excess, and the
over-tongued of theology and artful, did not even have entrance into the
divine courts, but it was the same to play with pebbles, stealing the sight by
the speed of the transposition, or to dance down the spectators with all kinds
of androgynous writhings, and to say and to hear something newer and
over-curious about God; and the simple =35.1096= and noble of the word was
thought piety. But from when Sextuses, and Pyrrhos, and the opposing tongue,
as some terrible and malignant disease, crept into our Churches; and babble
seemed education, and, what the book of the Acts says about Athenians, we have
leisure for nothing else, than to say and to hear something newer. O which
Jeremiah will lament our confusion and darkening of mind, the only one who
knows to equal dirges to sufferings! 13. Of this madness Arius began, the one
named from mania, who also paid the penalty of an unbridled tongue, the
dissolution in unhallowed places, having become a work of prayer, not of
disease, and having undergone the bursting of Judas, upon equal betrayal of
the Word. And others having succeeded to the disease, crafted an art of
impiety; who, having bounded the godhead by the unbegotten, exiled the
begotten, and not only this, but also the proceeding from the godhead, having
honored the Trinity with communion of name only, or not even having kept this
for it. But not that blessed man, and truly man of God, and great trumpet of
the truth. But knowing, that to contract the three into one number is of
atheism, and of the innovation of Sabellius, who first invented contraction of
godhead; and that to divide the three by natures is a cutting-up

of godhead unnatural; he both kept the one well, for it is by godhead; and
taught the three piously, for it is by properties; neither having confused by
the one, nor having set apart by the three, but having remained in the bounds
of piety, by fleeing the measureless inclination toward either side and
opposition. 14. And for this first in this holy synod at Nicaea, and in the
number of the three hundred and eighteen chosen men, whom the Holy Spirit led
into one, as far as was upon him, he stopped the disease; not yet ranged among
bishops, but ranged first of those who had come together; for the preference
was of virtue no less than of grades. Then the evil having already been fanned
by the breezes of the wicked one, and taking hold of the more (here for me
also the dramas, of which almost all earth and sea is full), much was the war
about him, as a noble cham =35.1097= pion of the Word; (for the array is
especially toward what resists, and from elsewhere some other of the terrible
things flowing around; for impiety is an inventor of evils, and very bold
toward attempt; and how were they going to spare men, who did not spare
godhead?) and one assault also most harsh. I myself also contribute something
to the drama; but let the dear ground, the fatherland, be excused by me; for
the wickedness is not of her who bore, but of those who chose. For she is both
sacred and known to all for piety; but they are unworthy of the Church that
bore them. You have heard that a thorn also grows in a vine; and Judas, one of
the disciples, the betrayer. 15. There are then some who do not even release
my namesake from the charge; who according to love of education, sojourning
then in the city of the Alexandrians, and having obtained from him every
welcome, equal even of children the most honored, and being one of those
entrusted the greatest things, plots, as they say, an uprising against the
father and protector. And the drama was of others, but the hand of Absalom
with them, as the word. If any of you knows the hand, which the holy man was
falsely accused of, and the living dead man, and the unjust exile, he knows
what I say. Yet this I will willingly forget. For I am thus in the doubtful
things, that one must incline toward the philanthropic, and rather acquit,
than condemn the accused. For the wicked would most quickly condemn even the
good; but the good not even the wicked easily. For what is not ready toward
wickedness, is not even facile toward suspicion. But what is no longer word,
but is deed, nor some unexamined suspicion, but faith proclaimed. 16. Some
Cappadocian monster, setting out from our extremities, wicked in race, more
wicked in thought, not even altogether free, but mixed, such as we know that
of the mules; =35.1100= at first a slave of another's table, and bought of
barley-cake, having learned both to do and to say all things for the belly;
and last also having crept into the state, and having been entrusted the last
of it, so far as to become a receiver of swine's flesh, with which the
soldiery is fed; then having become wicked about the faith, and having
politicized for the belly, when the body alone was left, he thinks of flight,
and changing another land and city from another, such as the things of
fugitives, at last for evil of the common of the Church, as some Egyptian
plague, he takes Alexandria. Here he stands from the wandering, and begins the
knavery. And in the other things he was worth nothing, having shared neither
in liberal words, nor glib in company, nor wearing even some empty scheme and
fiction of piety, but to do knavery, and to confuse affairs the most terrible
of all. 17. You all know and narrate, such things as he plays the youth
against the holy man. For even just men are often handed over into hands of
the impious, not that those may be honored, but that these may be tested; and
the worthless indeed in extraordinary death, according to what is written, are
mocked, yet nevertheless for the present the pious, until the kindness of God
is hidden, and the great treasuries of the things later laid up for both; when

both word, and deed, and thought, is weighed by the just balances of God; when
he rises to judge the earth, gathering the counsel and the works, and
stripping the things sealed with him and saved. Let Job both speaking and
suffering persuade you of these things; who was a true man, blameless, just,
god-fearing, and as many things as have been witnessed; but he is struck by
the one who had demanded him with so many assaults, and so successive and
ambitious, that of many who have often suffered evil from the whole age, and
of those who as is likely have also been wretched, there is no one to compare
with his disasters. For not only money, possessions, good children, many
children, which are much sought by all men; not these alone he is fined, so
that there was not even room for dirges through the continuity of the evils;
but also the body itself last having been struck a blow both incurable and
hard to look at, he had as addition =35.1101= of the disaster the wife
comforting with the worse things (for she was ambitious that the soul also be
struck besides the body); and he had also the most genuine of the friends as
comforters of evils, as he himself says, not healers; who saw the suffering,
but not knowing the mystery of the suffering, took the blow not as a trial of
virtue, but as an exacting of wickedness. And they not only thought, but were
not even ashamed insulting the terrible thing, when even if he suffered
through wickedness, one ought to sophisticate the grieving with words of
comfort. 18. Thus then Job, and the first of the business about him. For it
was a contest of virtue and of envy; of the one, how it may prevail over the
beautiful, being ambitious; of the other, how it may remain unconquered,
bearing all things; and of the one, how it may prosper wickedness, contending,
through the punishment of those succeeding; of the other, how it may hold the
good, even in disasters having the more. And what of the one oracling toward
him through whirlwind and clouds, the slow toward punishment, and swift toward
help, the one not altogether sending the rod of sinners upon the lot of the
just, that the just may not learn wickedness? At the end of the contests he
proclaims the athlete with a brilliant proclamation, and strips the secret of
the blow, Do you think I have oracled to you otherwise, saying, than that you
may be shown just? This is the medicine of the wounds, this the crown of the
agony, this the repayment of the endurance. For the things next are perhaps
even small, even if they seem great to some, and having been managed for the
sake of small things, even if he receives in double of the things taken away.
19. Therefore neither here is it wonderful, if George had more than
Athanasius; but that at least is more wonderful, if the just man was not fired
by the insolences. Nor is this very wonderful, but if the flames sufficed
still more. From here the one was out of the way, and he disposes the exile as
most beautifully. For he gives himself carrying himself to the sacred and
divine houses of thought in Egypt; who separating themselves from the world,
and embracing the desert, live to God, more than all of those being tur
=35.1104= ned in a body; some contesting the altogether monastic and unmixed
life, speaking only to themselves and to God, and knowing this only as world,
as much as they recognize in the desert; others loving a law of love by the
communion, both desert-dwellers together and mixed, dead to the other men and
affairs, as many as are carried in the middle, both whirling and being
whirled, and playing us with the close-turning changes, but being a world to
one another, and sharpening virtue by the setting beside. Having consorted
with these the great Athanasius, as he was of all the others a mediator and
reconciler, imitating the one who made peace of the divided things by the
blood; so also he reconciles the desert life with the communal; showing, that
priesthood also is philosophical, and philosophy needs mystagogy. 20. For thus
he fitted both together, and led them into one, both quiet practice, and
practiced quiet, so as to persuade that to be monastic is in the stability of
the manner

rather, than to be characterized by the withdrawal of the body. According to
which also David the great was both most practical the same and most solitary;
if for anyone the, I am alone, until I pass by, is great toward proof of the
word and trustworthy. For this, prevailing over the others in virtue, they
were more defeated by his mind, than as much as they prevailed over the
others; and contributing few things toward perfection of priesthood, they
received more toward completion of philosophy; and this was law for them,
whatever seemed good to him; and this again sworn off, what did not seem good,
and tablets of Moses for them his doctrines, and the reverence more, than is
owed from men to the saints. Who even when some were present, tracking the
holy man as a beast, when seeking him everywhere they did not find him, they
deemed those sent worthy of no word; but they stretched out the necks to the
swords, as being in danger for Christ, and thinking to suffer something
incurable for him a greatest portion toward philosophy, and much of the long
fasts, and sleepings on the ground, and the other hardship, in which those
always luxuriate, more divine and higher. =35.1105= 21. He then was in these
things, and he praised the of Solomon, who philosophized that there is a time
for every affair. And for this he hid himself a little, as much as fleeing the
time of the war, that he may appear together with the coming of peace; which
then also happens a little later. But the other, already in much desert of the
one who would hinder, overruns Egypt, and plunders Syria by the power of
impiety; and he takes hold also of the east as much as he could, always taking
on the ailing, as the torrents the things flowing together, and fastening on
the lighter, or more cowardly. And he makes his own the simplicity of the
emperor; for thus I call the lightness, respecting the piety. For he was, if
one must say the true, having zeal, but not according to knowledge. And he
buys of those in office the gold-lovers rather than Christ-lovers (for the
things of the poor were plenty for him, being spent badly), and of these
especially the womanish and among men unmanly, and ambiguous in race, but
obvious in impiety; to whom I do not know how and from where the emperors of
the Romans, the things of women being entrusted to them, hand over the things
of men. And these things the servant of the wicked one prevailed, the sower of
the tares, the forerunner of the Antichrist; using as a tongue of the then
eloquent among bishops the first (if it is dear to anyone to call eloquent the
one not so much an impious dogmatist, as an enemy and contentious; for the
name I will willingly pass by); and himself being instead of a hand for the
system, and dragging away the truth with the gold, which being gathered
through piety, the wicked have made an instrument of impiety. 22. A result of
this domination, the synod that first took Seleucia of the holy and
beautifully-virginal Thecla, and after this this great city, which having been
known until then for the most beautiful things, it has made notable for the
most shameful; whether the tower of Chalan, which well divided the tongues (as
I would that those too! for the symphony was for evil), or the council of
Caiaphas, by which Christ is condemned, or some other such thing one must name
that synod, which overturned and confused all things. Having dissolved the
pious doctrine and =35.1108= ancient, and advocate of the Trinity, by throwing
a palisade, and shaking down the consubstantial with engines; and having
opened a door to impiety, through the middle of the things written; pretext
indeed for reverence of Scripture, and of the use of the approved names, but
the true, introducing unwritten Arianism in exchange. For the, Like according
to the Scriptures, was a bait for the simpler, lying around the bronze of
impiety, the image looking toward all who pass, the common buskin of both
feet, the winnowing according to every wind, having taken as authority the
newly-written

knavery, and the invention against the truth. For they became wise to do evil;
but to do the beautiful they did not know. 23. From here the sophistic
condemnation of the heretics, whom they denounced in word, that the attempt
might be persuasive for them, but in deed they led forward into the front;
having charged not measureless impiety, but an insatiable writing. From here
the unhallowed judges of the holy, and the new mixture, public sight, and
mystic problems, and the unlawful examination of the things lived, and the
hired slanderers, and the judgment upon stated things. And some being thrust
out of the thrones unjustly; others being brought in instead, and being
demanded the handwritten of the impiety, as some other of the necessary
things; and the ink ready, and the slanderer near. Which indeed also most of
us the unconquered have suffered, not having fallen in mind, but having been
led aside by a letter, and having come into one with the wicked in both; and
having shared of the smoke at least, even if not of the fire. Which I have
often wept, seeing the then pouring of the impiety, and the now risen
persecution against the right word by the protectors of the Word. 24. For in
truth, Shepherds have played the fool, according to what is written, and many
shepherds have destroyed my vineyard, they have shamed a desirable portion, I
mean the Church of God, gat =35.1109= hered by many sweats and slaughters,
those before Christ and after Christ, and the great sufferings themselves of
God concerning us. For except very few, and of these as many as were thrown
aside through smallness, or stood against through virtue, whom it was
necessary a seed and root to be left for Israel, that it may bloom again, and
live again by the inflows of the Spirit, all have become of the time, having
differed from one another so much, as some earlier, others later to suffer
this; and some to become champions and protectors of the impiety, others to be
ranged the second things, either shaken down by fear, or enslaved by need, or
baited by flattery, or stolen by ignorance, the most moderate; if even this is
sufficient for anyone toward apology of those entrusted to stand over the
people. For as the impulses of lions and of the other animals are not the
same, nor of men or of women, or of older or of younger, but there is even of
