after a little. The unlearned do not distinguish which person of the prophetic
Spirit cries concerning the body of the Lord and which teaches his eternal
generation from the Father, but they show one and the same the impassible Word
and the passible man of Jesus. 41 Of the same. When therefore all creation
shall meet Jesus in clouds and be subjected to him, then the Son himself will
be subjected to the Father, having become on behalf of all creation [the]
faithful apostle and high priest, in order that God may be all in all, that
is, Father and Son and Holy Spirit, the one lordship and Godhead and kingdom.
But if someone should say that he did not say Jesus being subjected, but the
Son, let him hear that Jesus has been named Son of God and is so because of
the God Word hidden in him. 42 From the same discourse. Manifestly Jesus who
came to be from the seed of David was appointed Son of God according to a
spirit of holiness from resurrection of the dead, whom Paul said the Son who
is going to be subjected to God. 43 From the same discourse. The Son himself
therefore will be subjected to the Father. On behalf of all creation, just as
also on behalf of all he delivered himself to suffer, the one born from an
undefiled virgin on our behalf, the one who does not know the day of the
judgment. 44 From the same discourse. For even if he did not say "the Son of
man," but simply "the Son," it is shown that he is not indicating the God Word
as being ignorant, but the man. 45 From the same discourse. The one therefore
who does not know the day of the judgment is the Son born from Mary. For the
God Word from the heart of the Father, who knows all things before their
generation, is ignorant of nothing. 46 And after a little. For the Truth was
going down together with Jesus who died on our behalf into the heart of the
earth, in order that, being Truth, he might set free those deceived by the
lie. 47 From the same discourse. And if Paul the vessel of election said that
there are certain unspeakable words, which it is not lawful either for him or
for another to speak, without doubt the God Word who showed him the
unspeakable words has in Christ Jesus an unspeakable generation from the
Father. 48 And after a little: from the lesser discourse concerning faith. Of
the same. Who at the completion of the ages, having come down from the bosom
of the Father, from the undefiled virgin Mary took up our man, Jesus Christ,
whom on our behalf he delivered to suffer by his own choice. 49 From the same
discourse. And the salvation planted in us unto a planting, new and not old,
for us and not before


us, is Jesus the man understood according to the Savior. 50 Of the same Saint
Athanasius, from the letter to Maximus the philosopher. For the things which
through the body--he may find a pretext. 51 Of the same, from the apology on
behalf of Dionysius. And the things concerning the Godhead--from the Word
himself. 52 From the same letter. The Savior has said--the vine, which is the
Lord's body. 53 From the same apology. For it is necessary of a vine--is of
God. 54 Of the same Saint Athanasius, from the greater discourse concerning
faith. Having said that all things were created in him, the blessed Paul said
him alone before all. For having been educated from the ancient things, being
also learned in the law, and having studied the Proverbs of Solomon, and
having found two kinds concerning the Lord, one concerning the man whom he was
going to take up from Mary, and another concerning the Word begotten eternally
from the Father before all ages, and concerning Jesus from Mary he writes to
Timothy: "Remember Jesus Christ raised from the dead, from the seed of David
according to flesh"; but concerning the God Word always with the Father he
said that he is "before all." Let those therefore who say that the Word of God
was created a beginning of the works be refuted by all the saints, and from
David let them hear that he is not a work, but maker of works, as he says:
"The heavens are works of your hands; they shall perish, but you remain." 55
From the same discourse. "Jesus Christ yesterday and today the same and unto
the ages." And the man understood according to the Savior is said to be the
same, being at one time an infant and at one time advancing in age and wisdom
and grace and beginning to be about thirty years, as the evangelist Luke says.
And the body that admits advance and age, this is the creature and thing made.
56 From the same discourse. Being therefore refuted by the saints, those who
say the Word from the Father is a creature, let them either cease from the
impious blasphemy or be denounced by all as Christ-fighters. For in that in
which they have been defeated they have trusted, in the Proverbs, in the one
who says "The Lord created me a beginning of his ways unto his works," they
slip. For Solomon, prophesying unto the Lord's man who was going to be created
from Mary for the benefaction of the whole world, said from his person the
"The Lord created me a beginning of his ways unto his works." 57 And after a
little from the same discourse. "Let all the house of Israel therefore know
assuredly that God has made him both Lord and Christ, this Jesus whom you
crucified." The apostles therefore, pointing with the finger the crucified man
of the Lord, said: "This Jesus God has made Christ and Lord." He himself
manifestly, the man of the Lord and beginning of ways, was created of the
things written before unto benefaction, ruler of all the saints and of those
named ways, and Lord Jesus Christ the one who was made and crucified,
concerning whom also Jeremiah said according to the edition of the Seventy
interpreters: "The Lord created me a salvation unto a new planting, in which
salvation men shall go about," but according to Aquila concerning the same
saying he says: "The Lord created a new thing in the female." And the new
salvation which he says was created for us and not old and not before us, and
the new thing which he says was created in the female, understand as the man
Jesus according to the Savior. For Jesus is interpreted in one way Savior, in
another salvation (from the Savior the salvation, in the manner that from the
light the illumination), which man Jesus he said a salvation created for us, a
new thing in the female, which is in Mary, or rather from Mary. 58 And after a
little. Whom therefore Solomon said created a beginning of ways, and which
Jeremiah says a new salvation created and a new thing in the female, him whom
on our behalf and through us Wisdom


built for herself a house, Christ Jesus, having shown from the scriptures let
us hold to the things that follow, upon which some, either having been
ignorant or having been preoccupied and remaining in error, wish neither to
teach rightly nor to be taught. 59 From the same discourse. If all were able
to contain the divine oracles spiritually, he would not have refused to impart
to them the spiritual gift, nor would he have given them milk to drink, if all
were able to partake of the solid food. But to those still being infants
concerning the very word of the knowledge of God, after the manner of infants
being nursed, he permits them to receive the word of catechesis, not yet able
to be fed with the bread of the angels, the God Word hidden in a human body.
60 From the same discourse. We must therefore as skilled stewards of divine
mysteries impart the word according to what each is able to contain. For the
old wineskins burst, not containing the new wine of the true vine, that is,
the Word of our Lord Jesus Christ. Since therefore some, or rather even many
who have not yet become disciples, have come from rashness upon teaching, not
knowing either what they say or concerning what they make affirmations, being
blind in the eyes of the mind, not having a mind that sees God as Jacob nor a
pure heart that will see God, joining the sayings written on account of the
man of the Savior whom he wore on our behalf, and the lowly works and poor
voices, to the Godhead of the Word, they wander and cause the unlearned and
unstable to wander, we have had necessity, as Jude writes, to set forth
through this letter which sayings fit the Godhead of the Word and which the
man taken up on our behalf. For as in one purse gold coins and silver are laid
away, both having the image of one king and the inscription of the name, but
the money-changer who keeps them knows the superiority of the gold beside the
lesser value of the silver, so even if in one scripture it has been written
concerning the Godhead of the Son and concerning the body which he wore from
Mary on our behalf, yet we shall recognize each of them as intelligent
disciples of the one and true teacher. As many lowly words and works,
therefore, understand as written unto the body of Jesus; but as many glorious
letters, unto the Godhead of the Word. When he hungers after the fasting, a
defect of the body; but when from five loaves he feeds the five thousand apart
from children and women and has twelve baskets of fragments left over, a work
of the Godhead of the Word. When he sleeps upon the cushion in the boat, a
work of bodily rest; when walking upon the sea he threatens the waves and the
winds "Be silent, be muzzled," at once he hears from those in the boat
confessing with an oath "Truly you are Son of God." When standing at the tomb
of Lazarus he weeps and, when he was about to suffer, says "My soul is very
sorrowful unto death" and is distressed and says that the spirit of the
Godhead is willing unto every economy, but the flesh weak, the being grieved
and being distressed is weakness of the body; when he says commandingly
"Lazarus, come out" and the dead man already four days loosed rises living,
which has not come to be from the beginning of creation, a sign of the
Godhead. When from the journey having toiled on our behalf he sits at the
well, the toiling is of the body; when he says "Come to me all who toil and
are heavy laden, and I will give you rest," a work of the Godhead is also to
heal from bodily diseases and toils and the dangers according to soul. When he
is baptized by John, he who "did not commit sin nor was deceit found in his
mouth," a propitiation of the body on our behalf; when he commands the
apostles to baptize all the nations into the name of Father and Son and


Holy Spirit, it fits his divine power. When he bends the knees and prays as
man, a confession of the body on our behalf; when at the mountain, he being
transfigured, Peter and James and John not being able to bear the glory bend
their faces and knees upon the earth, a strength of the glory of the Godhead
of the Word. When he strengthens the apostles saying "Do not fear those who
kill the body, but are not able to kill the soul," the hidden and strong
consolation of the Godhead; when he declines to die, the cowardice of the
body. For before Adam transgressed there was neither grief nor cowardice nor
toil nor hunger nor death. 61 From the same discourse. "I am," he says, "the
way of those being saved and the life of the dead and the truth of those
fleeing the lie," saying to those in darkness: "Be unveiled, I am the light of
the world." For having put on Adam together with the passions apart from sin,
the one called Jesus, through him he brought the lost sheep to the Father, "as
a sheep led to slaughter and as a lamb voiceless before the one who shears
him," concerning whom Paul writes: "a man, Christ Jesus, who gave himself a
ransom on our behalf and of our sins." And in another place the apostle says:
"Through man death and through man life. For as in Adam we all die, so also in
Christ we all shall be made alive." And again in another place he writes: "The
first man from earth, earthy, the second man from heaven." The first Adam
therefore he indicates, the first-formed of the world, and the second Adam the
man understood according to the Savior, who is called Son of man, sheep, vine,
bread, tree, grain of wheat, and the things like these, and he is called also
Adam, even if much is the difference of the second beside the first. For the
one has come to be from earth, but the other, being Word of the Father, became
flesh, not being emptied of being Word through the wearing of flesh, according
as neither is the gold emptied of the glory when it happens to be within a
foreign case. 62 From the same discourse. If therefore the apostle should say
the saying that says: "He who raised Christ Jesus from the dead will also
raise us with Jesus," do not be ashamed saying: the Lord was not able to raise
his own body, unless the Father raised it. And those who insult the Lord say
this. Understand the man being raised from the dead, whom Peter our apostle
interprets, knowing rather, and says: "Having taken him down from the wood
they placed him in a tomb, but God raised him from the dead." The dead body of
Jesus therefore that was taken down from the wood, which was also placed in a
tomb and buried by Joseph of Arimathea, this the Word raised, saying: "Destroy
this temple, and in three days I will raise it." He who also makes all the
dead alive also made alive the man from Mary, Christ Jesus, whom the Word of
God himself took up. For if while being on the cross he raised the
already-dissolved dead bodies of the saints, much more was the ever-living God
Word able to raise the body which he wore, as Paul says: "For living is the
Word of God and active." 63 Of the same Saint Athanasius, from the same
discourse. And when our teacher says in another place: "Consider the apostle
and high priest of our confession, Jesus being faithful to the one who made
him," do not think the God Word who is from the beginning toward the Father to
be a thing made. For he does not say to consider the invisible Godhead, but
the Lord's man, whom Paul said mediator of God and men, a man Christ Jesus who
gave himself a ransom on our behalf, whom he said to consider, Jesus being
faithful to the one who made him, as the Lord says "Consider the


ravens, how they do not sow" and the rest, and again "Consider the lilies of
the field, how they grow." He says therefore to consider the things that
appear and not angels or other invisible creations. For even if Habakkuk the
prophet should say "I considered your works and I was astonished," he says "I
considered works" and not the maker of the works, and the apostle says to
consider the appearing body of the Lord, not the invisible Godhead of the
Word. For no one has ever seen God nor is able to see, although the Word was
God and is, as the theologian John says. Whom therefore we hear to consider, a
man Christ Jesus, who has given himself a ransom on our behalf, whom he calls
apostle and high priest being faithful, he says to understand the thing made;
all things therefore were created, but the Word of God, who is Son, is not a
creature, but an offspring according to nature. For the Father created all
things through the Word, as Paul says, "in whom all things were created," and
as John says, "all things came to be through him and without him came to be
not even one," but he does not create the Word himself, but begets. For
through him, the Word, all things came to be and were created, but he himself
did not come to be, but the man whom he wore on our behalf, who has come to be
and been created, the scriptures show. 64 From the same discourse. For the
Lord says: "Every branch remaining in me bears fruit." And in the man whom he
wore from earth-born Adam, he will make alive the race from Adam, "having been
put to death in flesh, but made alive in spirit," as Peter writes that he says
him to have been put to death in flesh on our behalf and not in the immortal
life-making Godhead. For the Lord says "I am the life"; the life therefore
does not die, but rather makes the dead alive. For as the light is not harmed
in a dark place, neither can the life suffer anything having visited the
mortal nature. For unchangeable and unalterable is the Godhead of the Word. 65
From the same discourse. "For who," he says, "has known the mind of the Lord,
or who has become his counselor, who will instruct him?" The Son alone is
counselor of the Father and has known his mind, the one who says "No one knows
the Father except the Son, and no one knows the Son except the Father and to
whomsoever the Son should reveal." John therefore also says concerning the
Lord: "When he is manifested, we shall be like him and we shall see him as he
is." See that until now no one has seen the Son of God as he is, until he
reveal himself to us; but the man whom he wore on our behalf, we have seen,
whom Paul said: "Consider the apostle and high priest of our confession, Jesus
being faithful to the one who made him," whom also Stephen says: "I see the
Son of man standing at the right hand of the power of God." He did not say
that he had seen the Word or the Wisdom of the Father, but the Son of man, the
Lord's body from Mary, which also Paul says: "who sat at the right hand of
God"; concerning which body David says from the person of the Father
prophetically: "Sit at my right hand, until I make your enemies a footstool of
your feet," whom he knew beforehand would be enemies, the devil and the
opposing energies cooperating with him, which were at enmity with the Lord's
man, knowing that through his death they themselves were being made void and
the one who has the power of death, the devil, and the Jews who were at enmity
with him, unto whom the thing written is fulfilled that "they hated me without
cause," in return for which, being taught the things toward benefit, they
dared to scourge and crucify, calling him a Samaritan and demon-possessed and
a sinful man, and yet

through him sin having been condemned. Unto whom it has been written: "They
shall look upon him whom they pierced," whom seeing in the judgment judging
living and dead, as John says concerning the Lord, "as he has given him to do
judgment, because he is Son of man,"


repenting tribe by tribe they will say: "This was he whom we once had for
laughter, saying to him: 'He saved others, himself he is not able to save.'"
The thing written through David, therefore, "Sit at my right hand, until I
make your enemies a footstool of your feet," is unto the body of the Lord. For
before this prophecy through David the Word was in the bosom of the Father; he
did not have enemies in heaven. For no one was with the Father before
creation, except the Son and the Holy Spirit; for the Father through the Son
made the boundless ages and beginnings and authorities and all creation. He
therefore who has with no one a common possession or thing, but being Lord of
all, God Word, to whom no one is able to stand against, how did he have
enemies? For those who diminish the Godhead of the Word as the Lord not being
able through himself to ward off the enemies think that the Father has said to
the Son "Sit at my right hand, until I make your enemies a footstool of your
feet"; but let the uninstructed know this even now, that it has been written
unto the man understood according to the Savior. For in the same David it has
been written concerning the Lord's man: "An enemy shall not profit in you,"
and again: "A scourge shall not come near your tent." 66 Of the same, from the
same discourse. "The Lord," he says, "has prepared his throne in heaven."
Other is "Heaven is my throne" and other is a throne being prepared in heaven
as upon some place. Much is the account concerning these things at present for
narration, but the thrones indicated beforehand show the subjection of all
creation. And these things we have set forth concerning the "Sit at my right
hand," that it has been spoken unto the Lord's man. For if "I fill the heaven
and the earth, says the Lord," as Jeremiah says, and God contains all things,
but is contained by nothing, upon what sort of throne does he sit? The body
therefore is that to which he says "Sit at my right hand," of which also the
devil has become an enemy together with the wicked powers and Jews and Greeks,
through which body he has become and been named high priest and apostle,
through which he delivered to us a mystery saying: "This is my body on behalf
of you and the blood of the new covenant, not of the old, which is poured out
on behalf of you." And Godhead has neither body nor blood, but the man whom he
wore from Mary has become cause of these things, concerning whom the apostles
said: "Jesus the one from Nazareth, a man approved from God unto us." Nazareth
he says an earthly city and not a heavenly one, and they call him a man and
not God toward the Jews and those who from then and now think according to
flesh like them, even the apostles. And the Lord himself answered concerning
himself in human fashion saying: "Why do you seek to kill me, a man who has
spoken the truth to you?" He did not say you seek to kill me being God; for
God, being impassible and immortal, makes death void, and makes the dead
alive, saying: "I am the life." But toward Philip, as soon as he heard him
believing, he teaches together with the remaining apostles and those able
likewise to contain spiritually, saying: "He who has seen me has seen the
Father; I and the Father are one and I in the Father and the Father in me." 67
From the same discourse. You have therefore from the very demonstration of the
scriptures that the man of the Savior, whom we are taught to consider, is
himself a thing made; for not the invisible Godhead of the Word hidden in
paternal bosoms, which no one until now has beheld. And this Paul knows rather
than we, saying: "A sufficient light flashed around me and I fell to the
ground and I heard a voice saying to me: Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?
It is hard for you to kick against goads." And I said: "Who are you, Lord?"
And the Lord says to me: "I am Jesus, whom you persecute." And he confesses
that he has been blinded from the glory of the light of the Lord and after


three days from his revelation and command to have looked intently. And if
Paul the vessel of election has been blinded, not being able to see the glory
of the Lord, how does he teach us to consider the Godhead of the Word himself?
But what I have said often, I will say also now: he said we should consider
Jesus the man of the Savior, the one being faithful to the one who made him.
68 From the same discourse. "Become imitators of me," he says, "as I also of
Christ." It is clear that he speaks of the man, the foundation that has been
laid. For of the Godhead of the Word neither Paul nor anyone else is able to
become an imitator except in the passions and meekness of Christ, concerning
which Peter says we follow his steps, as also Paul said to fill up the
remainders of the afflictions of Christ in his own flesh. Unanswerably
therefore the one founded before the age into the hidden things of the
paternal mind was in foreknowledge the Lord's man whom he was going to create
from Mary. 69 Of the same saint, from the same discourse. And concerning the
Lord's body it was manifestly prophesied to Judah: "From you shall come forth
for me a ruler and his beginning is from the beginning from days of an age,"
although at the completion of the ages from the tribe of Judah the God Word
took up the man. 70 From the same discourse. "When," he says, "all things are
subjected to him, then the Son himself also will be subjected to the one who
subjected all things to him." Let us seek therefore who is the one not being
subjected to God unless first all things are subjected to him. For what did
the Word from the Father not have subjected, before the economy through the
man whom he wore on our behalf? For all the things made are subjected to the
one who has made them. But to the man who has been taken up not all things
have been subjected until now; for Paul says: "We do not yet see all things
subjected to him." For to the Jews it is a stumbling-block, and to nations
foolishness, but to us who believe in him Christ the power of God and the
wisdom of God. When therefore all creation shall meet the Son in clouds and be
subjected to him, then the Son himself also will be subjected to the Father,
having become on behalf of all creation a faithful apostle and high priest, in
order that God may be all in all, that is, Father and Son and Holy Spirit, the
one lordship and Godhead and kingdom. But if someone should say that he did
not say Jesus being subjected, but the Son, let him hear that Jesus has been
named the Son of God and is so because of the Word hidden in him. 71 From the
same discourse. As Luke genealogizing said "And Jesus was beginning to be
about thirty years, being son, as was supposed, of Joseph," and having gone
through those genealogized in sequence as far as Adam said "of Adam, of God,"
in order that he might show both the body which he wore from Adam and the
Godhead from the Father. 72 Of the same Saint Athanasius, from the same
discourse. And it is one of all the things which the Father works and shows in
the Son, the knowing the day and hour of the judgment, and he has delivered to
him also all the judgment, in order that as they honor the Father, they may
honor also the Son. And if he has delivered to him all the judgment to do, it
is clear that also the knowledge of it, when and how it comes to be. The one
therefore who does not know the day of the judgment is Jesus born from Mary;
for the God Word from the heart of the Father, who knows all things before
their generation, is ignorant of nothing. 73 From the same discourse. If
therefore Jesus says: "My Father is greater than I," and he also says himself
equal to God, let us learn why he both says the Father greater than himself
and equal. He is greater than the Son because of the passible body which he
wore, but equal inasmuch as the Father wholly begot the whole perfect Son
eternally. 74 From the same discourse. As also Esdras prophesies from the
person of Zerubbabel saying concerning the Son of God: "The truth lives and
conquers and


is strong and remains unto the ages of the ages," indicating that which he was
going to wear on our behalf, a body, and that it would die on our behalf, and
not the Truth hidden in him, that is, the Word of the Father; the Truth was
going down together with Jesus who died on our behalf into the heart of the
