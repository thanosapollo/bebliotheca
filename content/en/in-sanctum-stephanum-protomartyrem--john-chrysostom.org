On Saint Stephen the Protomartyr.
=59.501=
αʹ. Let us crown Stephen with the flowers of encomia, and sprinkle him with
the roses of praises; for he has already crowned himself beforehand with the
contests of the faith. For it was indeed laid down in writing: Do not fear
those who kill the body; but the attachment to the body introduced the
difficulty. But when this young man wished to take up the martyrs' victories,
every difficulty was driven away; and cowardice and fear practiced idleness.
For he feared neither the madness of the high priests, nor did he dread the
boldness of the priests, nor did the words of the elders astound him, nor did
the threat of the scribes disturb him, nor did the words of slanderers cut
short his eagerness, but with a certain noble spirit he hastened into the
contests for Christ. Who then of mortals would bring worthy praises to the
combatant? Who of men would weave an encomium corresponding to his way of
life? What tongue will be able to utter the glories of the champion? What
mouth will be opened toward the praise of Stephen's contests? What lips will
suffice to speak the exploits of the protomartyr? For on earth he wrestled,
and he surveyed the things in heaven; he was present in this life, and he
traveled about the things on high; he conversed with mortals, and was ranked
with the immortals; he fought against men, and danced with angels. Therefore
let every age have firmness; for Stephen wrestled, and the first-evil one
fell. Therefore let also the one who contends in gray hair have firmness; and
the one who succeeds in old age receives the crowns, and the young man who has
contended is crowned, and a lad who has wrestled carries off prizes, and
infants who contend carry off crowns, and women who have drawn up in battle
are crowned with glory, and virgins who have wrestled carry off victories.
Therefore, since Stephen has opened the gates of martyrdom, let every age
henceforth run toward martyrdom. For Stephen first withstood the tyrant, first
received the victories against him, first began those gloriously victorious
contests, first for Christ received the sufferings of Christ; he showed
mortals how death is mocked; he showed men how death will be a laugh; he made
known to those in this life the heavenly ascents. For Stephen, full of grace
and power, did signs and wonders among the people. The grace of the Spirit
advanced Stephen to greater progress; for the measurelessness of his faith
pledged the firmness. For having been appointed a deacon, he was shown forth a
martyr; having been set over the need of widows, he advanced to the grace of
wonders; he was appointed a servant of tables, and was shown forth a worker of
marvels. And Stephen was full of faith and power. Let Stephen, it says, have
in his name the crown of victory; Stephen, who made known to this life an
unpracticed war, who displayed to the world an untaught battle. Nature did not
have this in custom, and Stephen alone drew up against a bodiless army.
For faith accomplished the contests for him; hope after the end urged him to
wrestle. Stephen full of faith and power. Stephen, who showed the first-evil
one a captive =59.502=, who by a noble choice raised trophies against him, who
prepared demons to lament, and men to dance; Stephen the first trophy-bearer
of the martyrs, the general of those who contend for Christ. For whoever
begins to wrestle for Christ finds Stephen a teacher; whoever lays hold of the
martyrs' contests imitates Stephen. Stephen the first-fruits of those who
contend for Christ, the foundation of those who die for him. Stephen full of
faith


and power. The soldier of Christ was fenced about on every side, the pious
war-leader was armored on either side: Full of faith and power. These things,
it says, sufficed for victory, these things were strong enough to furnish the
trophy. For faith and power both conquered the first-evil one and astounded
the Jews. Full of faith and power he did great signs and wonders among the
people. The champion displayed multitudes of wonders, the soldier of Christ
worked a multitude of marvels; He did signs and wonders among the people,
granting courses to the lame, sending forth health to the paralyzed, that he
might stir the high priests toward envy; thus he desired to taste death for
Christ. Then he summons against himself a pentapolis of most impious men. For
there arose, it says, some of those from the synagogue called of the
Libertines, and of the Cyrenians, and of the Alexandrians, and of those from
Cilicia and Asia, disputing with Stephen. O how great a crowd did wickedness
gather! O how great a people did envy collect! Libertines, Cyrenians,
Alexandrians, and those from Cilicia and Asia. An infinite crowd, it says,
drew up against one soldier; measureless multitudes rushed against one
champion; men of five cities ran down upon one young man. O the incurable
wickedness of the Jews! From the very sight they wished to frighten the
champion, that by the excess of the multitude the sinews might be unstrung.
Then the utterly wicked also impose order on the battle, and in the openings
they weave for Stephen words of questions. For there arose, it says, some
from the synagogue called of the Libertines, and of the Cyrenians, and of the
Alexandrians, and of those from Cilicia and Asia, disputing with Stephen.
Why, they say, O young man, do you insult the Divine without consideration?
Why with studied words do you persuade the crowd? Why with the deceit of
wonders do you scatter the nation? Here is the end of the questions: Is he
who was born of Mary God? Is the son of the carpenter Creator? Is not
Bethlehem his village, and Nazareth his nurse? Do you imagine as God him who
was born upon earth? God, he who through cheapness was swaddled in the
manger? he who fled from Herod when he was angry? he who received Jordan as a
bath for purification? he who was enslaved to hunger =59.503= and thirst? he
who felt toil and sleep, he who was seized, and was not strong enough to
escape? he who was struck, and did not avenge himself? he who was hung up,
and was not strong enough to come down? he who found a cursed and insolent
end? Him who was placed in a tomb, do you imagine to be in the heavens? him
who is like mortals, do you proclaim to be the life of the dead? Did God then
accept to suffer these things? Did he not command, and slay those who came
against him? Did he not, by ordering, bring sudden death? Either then cease
from the error, or receive also yourself the same death as his.
βʹ. Saying some such things they were disputing with Stephen. Then Stephen:
Are not the writings of the prophets kept among you? Do you not unroll the
books of the Old Testament? Did you not say to one another, We know that God
has spoken to Moses, but this man we do not know where he is from; we are
Moses' disciples? Did not Moses say: The Lord God will raise up for you a
prophet from your brothers as me; you shall hear him? Did not Micah write
beforehand from afar: And you, Bethlehem, land of Judah, are by no means least
among the rulers of Judah; for from you shall come forth a ruler? Did not
Isaiah announce beforehand the birth of the unwedded: Behold, the virgin shall
have in the womb? Did not Habakkuk foretell the presence of the animals in the
manger: In the midst of two animals you shall be known? Did not Isaiah speak
of the flight into Egypt because of Herod: Behold, the Lord comes upon a light
cloud into Egypt? Did not David signify beforehand the flight of the waters of
the Jordan: The waters saw you, O God, the waters saw you, and they were
afraid? Did not Isaiah write beforehand the outcome of the toils and labors:
He took our sins, and bore our diseases? Did not David the prophet foretell
the violence of the nails: They pierced my hands and my feet? Did not Moses
proclaim beforehand the hanging upon the wood: You shall see your life


hanging upon a tree? Did not Jacob write beforehand the burial in a tomb:
Having lain down he slept as a lion? Did not David cry out beforehand the
things of the resurrection: Arise, O God, judge the earth? Did not the same
one foretell the things of the ascension: God went up with a shout? Either
then show the outcome of the prophecies in some other, or worship the
Crucified as God.
For you, O foolish ones, not knowing the prophecy of Moses, thought you were
crucifying a mere man; but he who is from you, and rather against you, the
vessel of election, Paul cries: For if they had known, they would not have
crucified the Lord of glory. Concerning him the chief of the apostles,
convicting you, said: But you denied the holy and righteous one, and asked
that a man, a murderer, be granted to you, and you killed the author of life.
Isaiah, knowing this voice of yours from afar, said: Woe to those who say that
the bitter is sweet, and the sweet bitter! Therefore, as Stephen was
discoursing thus, They were not able to withstand the wisdom and the Spirit by
which he spoke. And well is it said, To withstand. For since above he said,
Some of those from the synagogue arose, here he says, They were not able to
withstand. Therefore they were thrusting the tongue into silence, they all
shut their own lips, they drew back the arrows of the words, they held back
the bows of the sayings, they hid under the tongue the spear of wickedness.
They were not able to withstand the wisdom and the Spirit by which he spoke. O
the new things! how well does the celebrated Luke write? how does he draw the
promises of Christ to fulfillment? For Christ said, that I will give you a
mouth and wisdom, which all who oppose you will not be able to withstand. Well
then does he say: They were not able to withstand the wisdom, and the Spirit
by which he spoke. What wisdom? Christ speaking in him. And that Christ is
wisdom, let Paul come saying: Christ the power of God, and the wisdom of God.
And that he speaks in those who give thanks, writing in other places he said:
If you seek a proof of Christ speaking in me, who is not weak toward you, but
is powerful in you. And they were not able to withstand the wisdom, that
=59.504= is, Christ. For who will withstand him, and endure? And they were not
able to withstand the wisdom and the Spirit by which he spoke. What spirit?
The Paraclete, concerning whom he said at first: And Stephen was full of faith
and power; and again, And being full of the Holy Spirit, having gazed into
heaven, he saw the glory of God. Then they suborned men saying, that We have
heard him speaking blasphemous words against Moses and God. False witness
succeeded envy. They suborned men. They bought the lie with silver, Come,
saying, O honorable men, serve our things with yours; in our designs let your
tongue minister; to our counsels let your mouth serve. For a certain avenger
of the Galilean has arisen, a champion of that one has been raised up and an
opponent. He honors a God lying in a tomb; he proclaims as Creator him who was
shut up in a sepulcher. Him who was placed in a monument he imagines to be in
the heavens; him who was mocked by soldiers he reports to have come with the
army of heaven; him who was not even strong enough to avenge himself he
promises as judge of creation. Joseph laid his body in a tomb, and this man
imagines him to be at the right hand of God. The disciples denied him as a
deceiver, and this man says that every tongue will confess him; he was not
strong enough to come down from the cross, and this man promises a second
coming of his from the heavens; the malefactors blasphemed him as able neither
to save himself nor them; and this man cries most clearly that he himself is
the Savior of the world. Have you ever seen such daring? Have you even heard
at all words of such madness and blasphemy? Serve then the pretext, and you
will receive a common glory from those now living. Then the descendants of the
slanderers against Naboth used these words: We have heard him speaking
blasphemous words against Moses and God.


Against the lawgiver himself, it says, he dared to speak; he commanded us
saying, Attend, my people, to my law; and this man cries that Jesus will
change the customs which Moses delivered to us. He blasphemed against Moses,
the one venerable among the whole nation, who took the whole creation into
alliance, who freed the nation from harsh slavery, who walled the waters of
the sea with a rod, who by prayer sank the armies of the Egyptians, who at
night by prayer kindled a heavenly pillar, who procured a heavenly untilled
bread, who by the rod drew forth the streams of the barren. Against Moses.
And why do I stretch the word with, Against Moses, and God? He hurled the
arrows against heaven itself; against the Creator himself he stretched out
the tongue; he proclaims a dead man as a rival god to the Maker. Against
Moses, and against God. And they stirred up the people, and the elders, and
the scribes; and having come upon him they seized him, and led him to the
council.
They stirred the people to anger, and they seized him; the beasts seized the
sheep, and led him to the council, filled with hypocrisy and envy; to the
council alien to truth, and bereft of the just; and they led him to the
council, concerning which Jeremiah has cried from afar: I did not sit in the
council of their vanity; And they set up false witnesses saying: This man does
not cease speaking blasphemous words against the holy place and the law. The
temple, it says, this place he was not ashamed to insult, where the divine
words are read, where the writings of the prophets are trumpeted, where the
tablets and the heavenly manna, where the ark and the rod of Aaron, where the
gray hair of high priests, and the dignity of elders, and the honor of
scribes; but he considers the place a plaything, he declares the temple cheap.
And not only did he dare to speak against the place, but also against the law.
He imagines the law to be a shadow; he proclaims without shrinking the Old
Testament to be a type; he says the Galilean is greater than Moses, he
proclaims him who is from Mary stronger than the lawgiver; he was not ashamed
of the dignity of the elders, he did not dread the =59.505= multitudes of the
scribes, but he blasphemed against the place and the law.
γʹ. And learn the trial of his words. For we have heard him saying that Jesus
the Nazarene will destroy this place, and will change the customs which Moses
delivered to us. A dead Master is threatened against us, a deceased Lord is
promised to us. Jesus the Nazarene will rise, and will destroy this place.
This young man, it says, has imagined; he was ignorant of the delight of the
celebrated temple; the labors of the things accomplished in it escaped him.
The wisdom of Solomon designed this house; the wise man had the grace from
above as an ally; he endured many contests in the founding of the work; and
eighty thousand hewed in the mountain; and seventy thousand cut the wood;
thirty thousand conveyed the stone; three thousand six hundred oversaw the
work; he stretched a generous hand toward the temple, and in forty-six years
he was scarcely able to construct the work; and this man says that Jesus the
Nazarene will destroy [this] place; and not only this, but Also he will change
the customs which Moses delivered to us. He says that the all-holy sabbath
will be destroyed by that one; he declared that the lawful circumcision will
be overturned by the Nazarene; he says that our new moons are to be set aside
as cheap; he proclaims that him who is from Mary does not succeed to the
delight of the Tabernacles; he imagines that our sacrifices are unacceptable
with God; he declares that the one who died overturned the sprinklings and the
customary purifications. Who then does not know the end of the Nazarene? And
when can a mortal avenge himself against the living? Isaiah was sawn, and
nowhere is the one who exacts the penalties; Jeremiah was drowned, and


he was not able to avenge; Naboth was killed with stones, and nowhere is his
avenger; Zechariah was slain, and we remain until now unaccused; and when the
Nazarene has died this man stands as a champion. As a judge the dead man is
threatened against us; as a justicer him who is in a sepulcher is announced
by one who is led astray.
Then, as these were thus accusing falsely, Having gazed at him, all who sat in
the council, sending a grim eye against him, hurling brows filled with anger
against him, wishing from the very sight to astound the combatant, fulfilling
in their own wickedness the prophetic saying: The sinner considers the
righteous, and seeks to put him to death; Having gazed at him all saw his face
as the face of an angel. For the joy concerning martyrdom, leaping forth from
the appearance, flashed angelic graces around the young man. And why the face
of an angel? That the word of Christ might be fulfilled, which he said: In the
resurrection they neither marry nor are given in marriage; but they are as
angels who are in the heavens. Thus Moses and Elijah shone on the mountain,
when they were speaking with Jesus, so that the disciples seeing them fell on
their face; thus all the righteous are about to shine in the kingdom of the
Father. They saw his face as the face of an angel. And the high priest said,
Are these things then so? O words filled with hypocrisy! O sayings dressed
with invention and envy! Are these things then so? Did you dare, he says, O
man, to insult the lawgiver Moses? Did you then campaign against God as a
rival god? Did you speak without shame against the all-holy place? Did you
imagine that the law given from God is void? Did you think that the Nazarene
changes the things of Moses? You have become the procurer of great evils for
yourself. Then to these things the protomartyr: Men, brothers and fathers,
hear. Let every age, he says, have for the present the honor belonging to
each. Men, brothers and fathers, hear. Hear, I do not say, Be persuaded; for I
know the unbelieving of your mind; but Hear. The God of glory appeared to our
father Abraham in Mesopotamia. Not in Jerusalem, not in the celebrated temple,
but In Mesopotamia, where the place was given over to idols, where the
fatherland was drowning with the godless; in Mesopo =59.506= tamia, where
reverence was sent up to carved images, where they placed the all-holy name
upon woods and stones, there he appeared to him. For he knew that a rose comes
to be among thorns, and in the midst of tares noble wheat will spring up; just
as the much-contending Job in the prodigal land of Ausitis appeared as a rose
among thorns, and as a lily among thistles, and as a luminary in the darkness
of ignorance he shone with the virtues, and as a tower in overthrow he was
evident to all the powers above, increasing the achievements from earth to
heaven, sending forth the fruits of righteousness from depth to height; whence
God, having smelled their fruit, as also of Noah after the flood (For the Lord
smelled a scent of fragrance), bore witness to his own servant: Behold a man
righteous, true, God-fearing, abstaining from every evil, fulfilling the
things of the law before the law; for having the innate law, he did not need
the written; for known to God from the age are all his works. Then he says to
Abraham: Go out from your land, and from your kindred, and come into a land
which I will show you. Go out from your land. Why, he says, does a polytheist
country contain the father of the nations? Why does an idolatrous fatherland
contain the forefather of the nation? Go out from your land, and from your
kindred. Hard, as among men, are the things commanded to the patriarch. Leave,
he says, fatherland, abandon the whole race, renounce the land in which you
were born, be separated from father and mother. But none of these things shook
the patriarch; he did not reckon with himself, as a mortal would, Unclear
things, he says, does the one who appeared promise me; unstable things does
this one announce. Shall I then leave the things in hand, and pursue the
invisible? shall I let go the things at my feet, and long for the unclear? But


none of these things did the father of the nations reckon; but he was
commanded, and he agreed to the separation; he received commands, and he
longed to seek the unclear. For faith kindled this desire in him. For having
perfectly understood the things promised, he was more eager toward the going
out from the fatherland. For go out, he says, from your land. From your
thighs I am about to be born, from you according to the flesh. Therefore I
promise you nothing; for it is enough for you only that you should be called
my father. The book of the generation of Jesus Christ, son of David, son of
Abraham. Then having narrated the things concerning Abraham, that by one
voice of God he obeyed when he called, that he foretold the slavery of his
seed, and that they would serve the four-hundredth year in Egypt, and that by
the right hand of God they would be redeemed from the necessity, and that he
gave him a covenant of circumcision, he then speaks of the paradoxical birth
of Isaac, and that he circumcised him on the eighth day, as he was commanded.
He still speaks also of the birth of Jacob from Isaac, and that Jacob was
shown forth father of twelve patriarchs. He speaks also of the envy of the
kinsmen toward Joseph, and of his sale by the brothers in Egypt, and that he
found grace and wisdom with the king of Egypt, and that after the slavery he
was shown forth lord and wise. He speaks also, that a famine was shown forth
throughout, so to speak, the whole world, and that Canaan by the lack of
foods was giving up its own dearest, and that Joseph appeared in glory to his
own kinsmen, and that Joseph summoned Jacob the patriarch in Egypt, and that
Jacob went down there in seventy-five souls, and that Israel was multiplied
without measure in Egypt, and that another king arose, not knowing Joseph,
and that having dealt craftily he maltreated the nation of Israel, and that
he sent the male infants out as prey to the river Nile. And he speaks also of
the birth of Moses the lawgiver, and that he was thrown by the parents in the
ark, and that the daughter of Pharaoh the king took him up, and that he was
educated in all the wisdom of the Egyptians, and that he was shown powerful
in words and deeds, and that after the fortieth year he went up to visit the
nation, and that he saw an Israelite being wronged, and he avenged him
against the Egyptian, and that he advised Israelites who were fighting to be
friends, and that he was insulted by a fellow-countryman, as having worked a
murder, and that he fled to the land of Midian for this reason, and that
there he was shown forth father of two sons, and that an angel appeared to
him on the mountain Sinai, and that he saw an unconquerable friendship of
fire and bush, =59.507= and that he heard a divine voice from heaven, and
that he became trembling, not daring to look upon the wonder, and that he was
commanded to loose the sandal of his own feet, and that he heard that the
place in which he stood was holy ground, and that with signs he led the
people out of Egypt, and that through the Red Sea, as through the dry, he led
Israel as general.
And he speaks also of the idolatry of the lawless on the mountain, that the
wayfarers of the sea likened the Divine to a calf, that they attributed to the
idol the victories against the Egyptians. He speaks to them also of the
captivity of the nation in Babylon, and of David's well-pleasing with God,
that he found grace before God, and was called father of Christ. And he speaks
also of the house built by Solomon, and that God does not dwell in places made
with hands. Therefore having thus gone through the histories of the fathers,
having taken the Holy Spirit into cooperation, he then begins the insults
against them, and says: Stiff-necked, unbending and untamed; for what pain
bent your neck? what necessity bowed your throat? Did you not serve Pharaoh
four hundred years in Egypt? Did not Chousarsath the king of Syria acquire you
an eighth year? Did you not come under the kingdom of the Moabites an
eighteenth year? Were you not again enslaved twenty years under the
Canaanites? Did you not again bow to the Midianites a seventh year? Were you
not again mastered an eighteenth year under the Ammonites? Did you not yield
to the Philistines


forty years? Did you not serve the Babylonians seventy? Did not Shalmaneser
remove you to the mountains of the Chaldeans? and nowhere a necessity
changing your minds? Stiff-necked, whom the basket of Egypt did not tame,
whom the burden of the chaff did not soften, whom the force of brickmaking
did not bend. Then, =59.508= having heard these voices, Having cast him out
of the city they stoned him. Where they crucified the king, there they also
cast stones at the soldier. And having cast him out of the city they stoned
him. But even after the casts of stones the combatant received victories, and
with such a voice he used toward the Creator: Lord Jesus, receive my spirit.
Lord Jesus, who by the will of the Father took up passion, who by the counsel
of the Begetter endured death, receive my spirit. Separate then the soul from
the body, transfer the bodiless, receive my spirit crowned with all kinds of
virtues, receive my spirit; For I have finished the course, I have kept the
faith; therefore I ask, receive my spirit. For as long as I am clothed with
the corruptible body, I am afraid; as long as I wear the earthly flesh, I
fear, lest after labors I lose the labors; I grow dizzy, lest after wrestling
I remain uncrowned, lest after the contests I lose the prizes. Receive then my
spirit. Let the devil henceforth mourn, as having lost the victory; let the
first-evil one lament, as not having been strong enough against a mortal; let
the bodiless ones wail, because those in the body laugh at the passions.
Receive my spirit. And having placed his knees, he cried with a great voice:
Lord, do not set this sin against them. And that you may learn that he prays
with eagerness, he does not simply pray, standing and nodding, but Having
placed his knees, he said with compunction, with much compassion: Lord, do not
set this sin against them. And having said these things he fell asleep. For
death is sleep for the righteous, death is rest for the wise. For this reason
death is sleep, that as if awakened from some stupor in the resurrection, they
may enjoy the eternal goods; of which may it be that we all partake, by the
grace and mediation of Christ, through whom glory to the Father in the Holy
Spirit the Paraclete, as from the beginning, now and always, and unto the ages
of ages. Amen.
