* On the Holy Pascha, Sermon 1
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

1 The Jews celebrate an earthly Pascha, having denied the heavenly; but we,
celebrating a heavenly Pascha, have passed beyond the earthly; and that which
was accomplished among them was a symbol of the salvation of the firstborn
among the Jews, when the things of the Jews were not destroyed together with
the firstborn of the Egyptians, having been preserved symbolically by the
blood of the victim of the Pascha, but the Pascha accomplished among us is
the cause of the salvation of all men, beginning from the first-formed man,
who in all is being saved and 2 being given life.
The parts of the perfect things, and the temporal of the eternal, were
rehearsed beforehand as images and types, being sketched in shadow toward the
truth that has now arisen; and when the truth is present the type is
untimely, just as when a king has arrived <no one>, having left the living
king himself, thinks it right 3 to do homage to the image. The inferiority of
the type beside the truth is clear from this itself, seeing that the type
celebrates a short-lived life, that of the firstborn of the Jews, but the
truth the perpetual life of all men; for it is no great thing to escape death
for a little while for one who will die a little later, but it is a great
thing to escape death altogether, which 4 comes to us, for whom "Christ our
Pascha has been sacrificed." And the very name of the feast has the greatest
superiority when interpreted of the truth; for Pascha is, according to
interpretation, a passing-over, when the destroyer who struck the firstborn
passed over the houses of the Hebrews, but among us there is a true
passing-over of the destroyer, when once for all he passes over us who are
being raised by Christ unto eternal life.
5 In this way, then, the whole subject of the Pascha must be contemplated
spiritually and believed according to the apostolic interpretations; but the
faithful man also longs to understand the whole outline, how it stands toward
the truth, and as it were to contemplate the spiritual things that belong to
himself through the bodily things of that time, so that for him all the
things of the law may also be understood fittingly according to Christ, and
through the spiritual the bodily may become more manifest, and as if being
painted in the visible things the invisible may appear.
6 When therefore God was about to bring the tenth plague upon the Egyptians
(and this was the death of the firstborn), he said to Moses: "This month is
for you the beginning of months; it is first for you among the months of the
year," and he next ordained the sacrifice of the Pascha and the anointing of
the blood upon the doors; and by the anointing of this he promised salvation
of the firstborn; but what is this, contemplated at once upon the truth, that
that season is defined as the beginning of the year, in which are the Pascha
and the salvation of the firstborn? That for us too the sacrifice of the true
Pascha is the beginning of eternal life (for the year is a symbol of an age,
because going round in a circle it always turns upon itself and never comes
to a stop in an end), and Christ, offered as a sacrifice for us, is "Father
of the age to come," and making our whole former life untimely he gives a
beginning of another through the washing of regeneration, after the likeness
of 7 his own death and resurrection.
So that everyone who knows the Pascha sacrificed for him must set this down
as a beginning of life for himself, from the time when Christ has been
sacrificed for him; and he has been sacrificed for him then, whenever he
comes to know the grace and understands the life that is through that
sacrifice; and knowing this let him long to take the beginning of the new
life and no longer run back to the old, of which he has come to the end; for
"We who died," he says, "to sin, how shall we still live in it?"
8 Such then is the symbol of the beginning of the year; and "on the tenth of
the month" he commands each to take a sheep according to the house, and that
those eating in the house be so many as to suffice for the eating and that
nothing be left over, and that the sheep be sacrificed "on the fourteenth
toward evening." Therefore for five days the victim is present with those who
are about to be saved through it; and when the fifth is being completed, the
victim is sacrificed, and death passes over, and the one saved enjoys
perpetual light, both through the moon shining the whole night and through
the sun succeeding the moon, for this 9 happens on the fifteenth and at the
full moon. And these five intervals show the whole time of the world divided
into five: that from Adam until Noah, and that from Noah until Abraham, and
that from Abraham to Moses, and that from Moses to the coming of Christ, and
a fifth, <that> of the coming itself; in all which time salvation through the
blessed victim was set before man, but was not yet accomplished, but in the
fifth interval of time the true Pascha was being sacrificed, and the
first-begotten man being saved through it was going out into the perpetual
light; and that the Pascha was sacrificed not upon the evening itself but
"toward evening" showed that Christ suffered not in the very end of the
present age but toward 10 it [the].
And the parable of <Christ> showed the time thus divided, cutting the day
into five and those called into the vineyard, that is, into the working of
righteousness, saying that some are called about the first hour, some about
the third, some about the sixth, some the ninth, some the eleventh; for these
were different callings and different ordinances: one that upon Adam, another
that upon Noah, another that upon Abraham, another that upon Moses, and last
and most perfect that upon the coming of Christ, when also the wage of the
works is given first to the last according to the saving parable, since we
first receive the regeneration in baptism, we for whom "Christ has been
sacrificed," upon whom he rose and breathed the Holy Spirit for our renewal.
11 This then is the fourteenth understood mystically, and such is the symbol
of the victim and the luminous night and day that succeed the sacrifice; and
that the victim is eaten wholly at each house and that none of the flesh is
carried "outside" shows that one house alone has the salvation in Christ, and
this was the Church throughout the whole inhabited world, which of old was
alien to God, but now alone stands in kinship toward God through receiving
those sent by the Lord Jesus, just as the house of Rahab, the house of the
former harlot, having received the spies from Joshua, alone also 12 was being
saved, while Jericho was being sacked; so that even if the houses of the
Hebrews are many they have the force of one, just as the churches in cities
and countries, being many in number, are one Church; for in them Christ is
one everywhere, the perfect and undivided: therefore the victim was "perfect"
at each house and was not divided into different parts; Paul at any rate also
says that we are all one in Christ, because the Lord also is one and the
faith one.
13 The law therefore necessarily prefigured the undivided unity of the victim
unto Christ, in this also prefiguring beforehand the unity of the Church; in
Christ then we have the salvation, and in the sacrifice of Christ, and we
know that all things before the coming were prepared beforehand for the
coming, and that this alone was set forth from the beginning for the
salvation of all humanity, as if before the eyes, that Christ be sacrificed
for all, and we know that this salvation belongs to the one Church, and that
no one from outside the catholic Church and faith can have a share of Christ
14 nor be saved; and knowing these things we know that the salvation of the
whole world is being accomplished not in the things according to the law but
in Christ, nor do we assign anything toward the hope to the godless heresies,
but we set them altogether outside the hope, inasmuch as they do not even
have the slightest participation of Christ, but vainly attach to themselves
the saving name toward the deceit and harm of those who are able to attend to
name and figure 15 rather than to truth. Therefore let no one tear the
ancient things away from Christ, nor suppose that any of those before was
saved without Christ, nor those now who teach otherwise and counterfeit the
truth and prepare vain figures of churches both outside the truth and alien
to Christ: let no one name such people Christians, nor choose communion
toward them; for it is not permitted, since the victim is not carried out of
the sacred house nor brought forth into communion for those "outside."
16 And we see as prefigured unto the glory of the Lord that the sheep is
"perfect" and "male" and "of a year." "Perfect," because Christ alone is
without lack in all virtue and in every way unblemished, and from beginning
even unto end lacking no righteousness, as he himself says: "For thus it is
fitting for us to fulfill all righteousness"; therefore also all the victims
were brought perfect and unblemished, because all were sacrificed as a type
of Christ, and God commanded the priests to be sound and perfect in their
bodies, because they were all a type of the true priest.
17 And the sheep is "male," because it is also governing, having the rule by
nature, just as the male has bodily rule toward the female; for by nature and
in truth Christ is ruler and king, inasmuch as he is a heavenly man, and
having been ranked with us as a brother according to the fleshly nature, but
standing over us as master according to the 18 spiritual Godhead. Therefore
he himself also, and he alone, is bridegroom as of a bride, of all humanity;
and neither is John a bridegroom, the greatest of the prophets, who says
concerning Christ and himself, concerning Christ: "He who has the bride is
the bridegroom," and concerning himself: "But the friend of the bridegroom,
who stands and hears his voice, rejoices with joy because of the voice of the
bridegroom; this my joy therefore has been fulfilled"; nor indeed are the
apostles bridegrooms of the Church, even though they have received the
likeness toward Christ according to grace and have become sons of Christ
through the Spirit of Christ; but what does the blessed Paul say? "For I
betrothed you to one husband, to present a pure virgin to Christ."
19 The Lord then is truly ruler and master and king, not only because he was
God among men, but also according to the pre-existing Godhead, because he is
by nature king of all creation, not having received the reigning by grace,
but having it in truth and in the generation from the Father; and further the
sheep is also "of a year," which showed the Lord being new upon earth and
having nothing of the oldness that is among men.
20 If then anyone says the Lord is a mere man and sets Christ down as of our
nature, for this man the sheep is not "perfect" nor "unblemished," for no man
is unblemished; for this man the sheep is not "male," for none of men has a
natural and complete rule toward men of the same nature; and whoever numbers
the Lord in creation and says that he has a granted Godhead, not a true one,
this man too does not have the sheep sacrificed for him as "male," for he has
not known the king by nature but turns him aside into another who is not so
by nature nor in truth; but also if anyone refers <something> from human
oldness unto Christ, or dares to say he is capable of sin, or belonging to
the slavery of the law, or subject to death by necessity, this man does not
have the sheep "of a year," nor has he perceived the newness that is in
Christ.
21 It remains to understand the symbol of the sheep and of the kid. The sheep
then according to Isaiah is a symbol of the gentleness of Christ; for "as a
sheep he was led to slaughter, and as a lamb before its shearer is
voiceless," but a kid according to the law is a victim for sin. For he says
"a he-goat from the goats 22 for sin." Having been brought then gentle as a
sheep, being as a he-goat he has been sacrificed for sin, giving himself
through gentleness unto the salvation of men, which we shall pray to obtain
through faith and love of the Lord Jesus Christ who suffered for us, through
whom and with whom to the Father be the glory together with the Holy Spirit
unto the ages of ages. Amen.
