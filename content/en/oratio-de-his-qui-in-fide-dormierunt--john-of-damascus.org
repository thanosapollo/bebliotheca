* Oration on Those Who Have Fallen Asleep in the Faith
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

OF BLESSED JOHN CONCERNING THOSE WHO HAVE FALLEN ASLEEP IN THE FAITH, HOW THE
LITURGIES AND ALMS DONE ON THEIR BEHALF BENEFIT THEM.
=95.248=
αʹ. The pleasant and precious things of foods, when they are set forth often,
not only draw the hungry to eating, but sometimes also provoke those already
sated; just as also the drinks that are very sweet and fragrant charm not only
the thirsty, but even those in the contrary state, toward partaking. Likewise
again also those who love wealth, whenever they have taken hold of great
abundance, desire always constrains them to seek occasions, as many as day by
day extend the wealth still further for them. Therefore you too, O most
honored chosen ones of the Church, priests and Fathers, brothers and mothers
and dearest children, it is not famine of the divine oracles, nor any thirst
for the sacred teachings, nor again poverty of godly knowledge, that would
bend you toward the discourse now set forth, but a longing truly most
God-graced, leading you from power to power, and from graces to graces, and
from memory to remembrance. For what has often been left remaining for the
perfect, this a child has found; and what has escaped the wise has fallen upon
the unlearned; and what has eluded teachers has come upon disciples. But we
must not dare even to say this, but as having heaped together a gleaning left
after the vintage, and an ear overlooked after the harvest because of the
abundance, and simply all fruit on the trees forgotten after the gathering, so
we shall receive those who are willing, Christ the true God working with us,
and confirming the word through the works and the proofs.
βʹ. The serpent then who hollows out all things good and God-beloved, both
deeds and thoughts, and the murderous enemy being slaughtered, struck by
brotherly love, split by faith, put to death by hope, shaken by compassion,
the lawless one has fallen upon some with a certain alien and foreign =95.249=
pretext, and altogether contrary to the sacred ordinances; =95.249= that all
the God-beloved works after death do not even benefit those who have gone
before. "For God has shut them out," he says; and, "Each shall receive the
things done through the body, according to what he has done, whether good or
evil;" and that "In Hades who shall confess you?" and again, "For you will
render to each according to his works;" and, "That what each has sown, this he
will also reap. But, O wise ones, let us say to them, search and be taught,
that great indeed is the fear of God the Master of all, yet far greater is the
goodness; and fearful are the threats, but incomparable is the philanthropy of
God; and terrible again are the condemnations, but unspeakable is the sea of
his compassions.
γʹ. For see what the divine Scripture says: how Judas Maccabeus in Zion the
city of the great King, when he saw the people under him slain by the alien
enemies, having searched their bosoms and found idols within them, immediately
offered an atonement for each of them to the Lord who is ready to pity, acting
most piously and very brother-lovingly, whence also in the divine Scripture,
as in all things, so in this he has been marveled at. But the initiates and
eyewitnesses of the Word, the


disciples of the Savior who captured the circle of the world, and divine
apostles, ordained that remembrance of the faithful who have fallen asleep be
made at the fearful and undefiled and life-giving mysteries; which also the
apostolic and catholic Church of Christ and God from the ends even to the ends
holds firmly and very irrefutably, from then until now, and until the end of
the world; they having ordained this not at all unreasonably, nor vainly, nor
at random and as it chanced. For the unerring religion of Christians has
received nothing unprofitable, and has held it unshaken unto the age, but all
things beneficial and God-pleasing and very profitable, and procuring the
greatest salvation.
δʹ. Therefore the one great in divine things and deep, Dionysius, said in the
mystical contemplation concerning those who have fallen asleep, speaking thus:
so that from his divine and venerable words we may take hold of a clear proof:
that "The prayers of the saints, even during this life, and still more after
death, work upon those worthy of sacred prayers, that is, upon the faithful."
And the "still more" here is to be taken as nothing else than "how much more."
And a little after: "For the divine hierarch, the interpreter =95.252= of the
thearchic justification, never sought the things not most dear to God, and
promised to be given by him in a divine manner; wherefore he does not pray
these things for the unholy, that is, the unenlightened, who have fallen
asleep." And again: "Therefore the divine hierarch asks for the things dear to
God, and we shall altogether be granted them." And again: "The prayer of the
thearchic goodness asks that all the faults of those who have fallen asleep
through human weakness be forgiven; and that he be set in the land of the
living in the bosoms of Abraham and Isaac and Jacob, in which place pain and
sorrow and sighing have fled away; the thearchic might overlooking by goodness
the stains that came to be in the one fallen asleep from human weakness,
since, as the oracles say, no one is clean from filth." Do you see, O
contradictor, how he confirms that the supplications on behalf of those who
have departed in divine hope are beneficial?
εʹ. And after this again Gregory, named from theology, the God-speaking one,
in the funeral oration on his brother Caesarius, concerning his own mother: "A
proclamation worthy of every hearing was heard," he says, "and a mother's
passion is emptied through a good and holy promise, to give all things to the
child, his wealth as a funeral gift on his behalf." And after other things:
"Such then are the things from us; and some she has already paid, and others
we shall give, offering the yearly honors and memorials, we who remain in
life." Do you see how he confirms, and calls good and holy, the offerings to
God on behalf of the departed, and receives the yearly memorials?
ϛʹ. Then after him John, truly gold-speaking and gold-named, the guide and
teacher of love of the poor and of repentance, in his God-shining
interpretation of Philippians and Galatians: "For if the Hellenes," he says,
"burn their own things together with those who have departed, how much more
must you the faithful send along with the faithful his own things, not that
they may become ashes, as those things and these, but that you may clothe him
with greater glory; and if the dead is a sinner, that he may loose the sins,
but if righteous, that there may be an addition of reward and recompense." And
again in another discourse, "Let us devise," he says, "benefit for those who
have departed; let us give them the help that is at hand, I mean alms and
offerings; and the matter brings them much profit, and great is the gain and
the benefit. For these things have not been legislated at random and as it
chanced, and handed down to the Church of God by his all-wise disciples, I
mean that the priest make prayer at the fearful mysteries for those who have
fallen asleep in =95.253= faith, unless they knew that there is much gain for
them from this and


much benefit."
ζʹ. Then with these the all-wise Gregory of Nyssa again says thus: "Nothing
has been handed down unreasonably, nor without profit, by the heralds and
disciples of Christ, and has been held throughout the Church of God
everywhere; but the matter is altogether beneficial and God-pleasing, namely
to make remembrance at the divine and all-shining mystagogy of those who have
also fallen asleep in the right faith."
ηʹ. For to say that "You will render to each according to his works," and,
"Each will reap what he has sown," and the things following these, has been
said altogether concerning the coming of the Creator, and the then fearful
sentence, and the conclusion of this world. For then there will be no time at
all for help, but every consolation is ineffectual. For when the festival has
been dissolved, there is no merchandise of trading. For where then are the
poor? where ministers? where psalmody? where good deeds? where well-doings?
Therefore before that hour let us succor one another, and let us offer to the
brother-loving and man-loving and soul-loving God the things of brotherly
love; for he receives them very well, and the fillings-up of deficiencies
offered by those who present them, for those who have departed unprepared and,
so to speak, unready, he reckons to them as work and deed. For thus the
man-loving Lord wishes to be asked, and to grant the things asked of his own
creatures unto salvation, and then he is more wholly bent; not when someone
contends only for his own soul, but when he does this on behalf of his
neighbor. For from this he is formed toward the God-imitating, and he asks the
gifts of others as his own graces, and he encloses the definition of perfect
love, and from this he obtains the beatitude, and he benefits his own soul
together with that of his neighbor as much as possible.
θʹ. And why has the matter been thought burdensome? Did not the first martyr
save Falconilla after death? But perhaps you will say that she according to
worth, since she is first martyr, and her petition ought to be heard. But I
say to this: Well is the first martyr there. But consider again, on behalf of
whom the request: that it was on behalf of a Hellenic woman, an idolatress and
=95.256= altogether unholy and a worker of an alien lord. But here a faithful
one on behalf of a faithful one toward the same Master. Set therefore the one
against the other, so that the matter may be equalized, and the doubting may
not be left remaining.
ιʹ. Pass with me again from these to other things of equal power and like
manner; I mean the historical book of Palladius to Lausus, in which the
miracles concerning the great and wonder-working Macarius have been recorded
most truly; how, having asked the dry skull, he learned all things concerning
those who have fallen asleep. Then to this: Do you then never find any
consolation? (for the saint was accustomed to make prayers for those who have
fallen asleep, and he desired to know if they come to be unto profit;) and the
soul-loving Lord, wishing to show this and to give full assurance to his own
servant, breathed a word of truth into the dried skull. When, it says, you
offer the petitions on behalf of the dead, then we perceive some small
consolations.
ιαʹ. And again another of the God-bearing Fathers having a disciple living in
negligence, and this one having arrived at the end of life carelessly, the
all-pitying and soul-loving Lord, when the elder asked with tears, showed him
as the rich man according to Lazarus burning in the flame up to the neck; so
that from this, the saint having labored much, and having entreated God with
tears, he showed him again up to the waist. Then again the saint having added
toils to toils, he showed him in a vision to the elder completely free, and


redeemed from the fire.
ιβʹ. And who would be able in order to narrate the testimonies of these, those
arranged in the lives of the holy ones, in martyrdoms I mean and in divine
revelations, clearly presenting that even after death the prayers and
liturgies and alms done on their behalf benefit the departed greatly? For
nothing of the things lent to God is destroyed at all, but it is repaid by him
with the greatest addition.
ιγʹ. And concerning the Prophet's saying, "But in Hades who shall confess
you," we have said before, that the threats of the all-seeing One are fearful;
but =95.257= his unspeakable philanthropy conquers these. For even after the
prophet said these things, there came to be altogether a confession in Hades;
I mean of those who believed there at the saving descent of the Master. For
the life-giver did not simply save all, but as has been said, those who
believed even there. Some say then those who had already believed, who are
fathers and prophets, Judges, kings with them, and toparchs, and some others
of the people of the Hebrews, easily numbered, and manifest to all. But we
shall answer these things to those who glorify thus: that this is neither a
gift, nor wonderful, and paradoxical, that Christ save those who have
believed. Since he is the only just judge. And everyone who has believed in
him shall not perish. It was necessary therefore that all these be saved, and
be released from the bonds of Hades, by the descent of God and Master. Which
also came to pass by his providence. But those saved by philanthropy alone
are, as I think, all who had a most pure life, and accomplished every
excellent deed. Living sparely, continently, and soberly, yet they did not
grasp the unmixed and divine faith, not having been trained at all, but
remaining altogether untaught; these the steward and Master of all drew,
captured with divine nets. And he bent them to believe in him, having shone
upon them the divine rays, and having shown them the light of the truth. For
he who is compassionate by nature did not endure that their labors should have
come to be in vain. For they had possessed a most laborious life, toilsome and
narrow beyond word; having become autocrats of the passions, and having spat
upon the pleasures together, and having practiced all possessionlessness, and
continence with vigil again, and simply every excellent work; not piously
indeed, but nevertheless having passed through, worshiping the highest
providence, as they thought, most excellently, yet mistakenly. And there are
some who even grasped the divine glory of the all-powerful Trinity sparely,
dimly; but they glorified it nonetheless. And others spoke the incarnation of
the Word, =95.260= and the venerable sufferings, and his rising; and others
the birth from the Virgin, having also foretold her naming. For Mary, he says,
is the name of the maiden. Again some foretold all the supernatural wonders of
Christ. Of the dead and maimed, and mute, lepers, and deaf, and those with
fever, the paralyzed and the demon-possessed, the hydropic together and the
withered-handed, and the walking on the sea thence, the blessing of loaves and
of fishes, the change of water again to wine, of the woman with the issue of
blood and of her altogether bent together, having prophesied the healings,
with very many others; these the divine power of the Word did not endure to
overlook as perished, nor to destroy the excellent deeds. For the portion of
time lent to him, as we have said before, is not darkened; but having been
kept, it is repaid to all who have lived well, with addition. But those who
have lived a shameful life, and have done nothing at all of good things,
possessing neither seed nor fruit at all, but being altogether gone astray in
life, and in word and in faith,


neither when the divine rain was poured upon them from heaven did they sprout
at all; for, as I said before, they did not cast seed, nor when the sun of
glory shone; they were ripened as altogether fruitless; these Christ does not
benefit at all, he did not raise together, as I think, those who had fallen,
as altogether unworthy of salvation. Nor indeed did they believe in him, it
seems to me. For the lifting-up of darkness, the first dragon, whose servants
they had become from the first, blinded their minds and the noetic eyes of the
heart, so that seeing they may not see, as is right, and understanding they
may not understand at all. But all the others who had seed were ripened when
the sun appeared, and they sprouted when the rain had come to be. See where
the account carrying us has brought us up, to loose with God and to
distinguish a matter all but covered; and this not assertively, because of
unworthiness, but syllogistically, because of brotherly love. These Christ
saved, as I at least think, in Hades.
ιδʹ. It has been shown therefore by the cooperation of Christ that there came
to be a confession in Hades. And we say these things, not doing away with the
prophecy; God forbid; but wishing to show the all-good Lord conquered by his
own philanthropy; just as also, "Nineveh shall be overthrown;" and it has not
been overthrown, but the goodness has conquered the judgment. And in the case
of Hezekiah, "Set in order," he says, "concerning your house. For you die, and
you shall not live;" and he himself did not die. And in the case of Ahab: "I
will bring," he says, "evils;" and he did not bring them, but said: "Have you
seen how Ahab was pricked? therefore I will not bring evils in his days." But
again the goodness more than conquered the sentence, as also in very many
other judgments, and it will always conquer until the last recompense, when
the end of the festival shall come, and there will be no time for help, but a
man, and his burden. But now is a time of trading, a time of exchange, a time
of toil and running and labor; and blessed is he who has not bent, nor been
made slack in hope; but more blessed is he who has contended both for himself
and for his neighbor.
ιεʹ. For this rather delights and gladdens the pity-loving Lord, that each
hasten to the help of his neighbor. This also the merciful One wishes, and
wills, that we all be benefited by one another, both living and after death.
For he would not have given us occasion to make remembrance of those who have
gone before at the unbloody sacrifice, and again third-day, and ninth-day, and
fortieth, and yearly memorials and rites; which without any contradiction his
catholic and apostolic Church, and her God-gathered and all-pious people, hold
unshaken and unslandered, if this were not right in his eyes. For altogether
if the matter were a mockery, and without profit and unbeneficial, many
God-bearing holy patriarchs, Fathers, and teachers having come to be, it would
have fallen upon one of them to stop the error; but none of these has ever
thought to overturn this, but rather has even ratified it; and day by day the
matter is increased and added to, receiving addition upon addition.
ιϛʹ. It is necessary therefore to mention also other histories toward these.
Gregory the Dialogist then, bishop of elder Rome, a man, as all know, renowned
in holiness and knowledge, to whom, they say, while he was liturgizing a
heavenly and divine angel concelebrated. This man once, making his way along
the stone road, and having stood on purpose, made a mighty prayer to the
soul-loving Lord for forgiveness of the sins of Trajan the emperor; who
immediately heard a voice =95.264= borne to him from God saying these things:
"I have heard your prayer," it says, "and I grant pardon to Trajan; but do you
no longer add to offer me prayers on behalf of the impious." And that this is
genuine


and unslandered, all the East and the West is witness. Behold this is above
that of Falconilla. For she became cause of no other evil, but this one
established a bitter death of many martyrs.
ιζʹ. You are wonderful, Master, and wonderful are your works; and we glorify
your unspeakable compassion, because you always incline toward the man-loving,
you provide your servants the occasions of both brotherly love, and of the
firm faith and hope toward you. And you taught us through your servants to do
benefactions on behalf of one another, atonements and whole-burnt offerings,
hymns and psalmody and prayers, not at random and in vain. For you are
unerring and bountiful and giving to all who offer anything unto your glory
the recompense a thousandfold; and nothing at all of the things done in your
name is unprofitable.
ιηʹ. Let no one therefore think this, brothers and Fathers, that as many
things as are offered to God with faith, he does not give the repayment many
times over, both to him by whom, and for those on whose behalf they are
brought. For just as myrrh, or other holy oil, he who wishes to anoint a sick
person, first that one, that is the anointer, partakes of the anointing; then
thus he smears the one who is ill; so everyone contending on behalf of the
salvation of his neighbor first benefits himself, then the neighbor. For God
is not unjust, to forget the work, according to the divine Apostle.
ιθʹ. Athanasius again the great, in his all-harmonious discourse on those who
have fallen =95.265= asleep, says these things: "That even if the one
perfected in piety be laid in the air, do not refuse to kindle oil and
candles, having called upon Christ the God, at the tomb; for these are
acceptable with God, and bringing much recompense from him; for the oil and
the candle are a whole-burnt offering; but the divine and unbloody sacrifice
is an atonement; and the good-doing toward the poor is an addition of every
good recompense. Let then the intention of the one making the offering on
behalf of the one fallen asleep be: in the manner, when someone has an infant
child, and altogether inarticulate and powerless, then this one being sick, he
bring candles and incense with oil into a divine temple with faith on his
behalf, and burn these as a whole-burnt offering for the child; not as the
child himself both holds and offers these, as the renunciations and the
syntheses at the divine regeneration; so also let him think the one perfected
in the Lord both to hold and to offer, both the candles and the oil, and all
the things brought for his ransom; and thus by the grace of God the intention
with faith will by no means be made vain. For all the God-speaking apostles,
and sacrificing teachers, and Spirit-bearing Fathers, having come to be in
possession of the Divine according to what is lawful, and having somewhat
partaken of his ecstatic power, with God-inspired mouth God-lovingly ordained
the liturgies, prayers and psalmody, and yearly memorials of those who have
gone before, which until now by the grace of the man-loving God increase and
are added, from the risings of the sun even to the settings, and north, and
south, unto glory and praise of the Lord of lords and King of those who
reign."
κʹ. Then the adversary says: If these things are so, all will be saved, and no
one will miss. Well this, and would that it were. For the all-good Lord
himself thirsts, and wishes, and seeks, and desires, and in this rejoices and
is gladdened, that no one miss his divine gifts. For did he prepare the prizes
and the crowns for angels? did he come upon the earth to save the heavenly
minds, and was he incarnate from a Virgin without corruption, and was named a
mortal, and tasted sufferings and death? will he say to the angels: "Come, you
blessed of my Father, inherit the kingdom prepared for you?" You cannot say
this, O


contradictor; but all things on account of man, as he has suffered, so also he
has prepared. For who making a banquet, and having called =95.268= the
friends, does not wish all to have come, and to be filled with his good
things? For unto what did he also prepare the feast, if not to receive his own
friends? And if then this is eager among us, what would it be to the
great-giving, and only by nature all-good, and man-loving God; who granting
and giving more rejoices and exults, than the one receiving, and procuring for
himself the greatest salvation.
καʹ. Let everyone then who doubts the things said consider this, that each of
men who has acquired a small leaven of virtues, but has not arrived at making
bread of it, but wished indeed, yet was not able to do this, either because of
slackness, or negligence, or cowardice, or the putting-off from day to day,
and having been overtaken and harvested beyond hope, this one will not be
forgotten by the just Judge and Master, but the Lord will raise up for him
after his death his own and kinsmen and friends, and will direct their minds,
and will draw their hearts, and will bend their souls toward leading and help
of this one; and they will hasten, moved from God, and the Master having
touched their hearts, to fill up the deficiencies of the one who has departed.
But he again who has possessed a wicked life, mixed altogether with thorns,
and full of unclean matters, who never passed through with the conscience, but
fearlessly and indifferently lay upon the stenches of the pleasures, doing all
the desires of the flesh, and taking no thought at all concerning soul, and
having the mind altogether fleshly, and thus having been overtaken to depart
from life, to this one no one at all will stretch a hand; but thus all things
concerning him will be accomplished, so that he be succoured altogether
neither by wife, or children, or brothers, or kinsmen, or friends, because God
does not even have this one in measure.
κβʹ. For me then, and whoever is a friend to me, may it come to be, if
possible, to succor himself, and to leave behind no deficiency; but if I
should be overtaken not having been perfected, may the Lord who is much in
mercy at least bend for me the kinsmen and friends, and warm their souls and
hearts, so that through good and God-pleasing works they may succor me very
eagerly, and if I leave any deficiency as a man, they may fill it up for me
after death. Yes, Lord the God of wonders and provider of things without
resource, may no small or great deficiency be found for me at your coming, nor
indeed for all who have trusted in you; for thus also the God-speaking
Chrysostom mentioned before teaches and cries aloud, so that I may again take
hold of his most useful oracles. "If," he says, "you did not arrive while
still in life at setting in order all the things of your soul =95.269=, at
least at the end of life command your people after death to send along to you
your own things, and to give you help through good works; I mean alms, and
offerings. And from this you may reconcile the Redeemer; for these are
acceptable to him and fitting." And again he himself elsewhere: "Write," he
says, "in your disposition together with the children and kinsmen, the Master
also as co-heir. Let your paper have the name of the Judge, and let it not be
without memorial of the poor; and I am surety of these. We do not therefore
give occasions and pretexts for someone living not to do alms, but to keep
these after death; for this is altogether out of place and profane, and alien
to the divine legislation; but also most beautiful and God-pleasing, and
altogether well-accepted, that every God-fearing and Christ-loving man cleanse
himself through every well-doing, abstaining from all uncleanness, but holding
to the shining commandments of God, so that having come to the end, he may say
with boldness to the Master: My heart is ready, O God, my heart is ready; and
thus he may meet the compelling angels sweetly. But this


indeed few and seldom according to the word of the Master, that Few, he says,
are those being saved. And yet the Wisdom of all did not say this simply, but
as one might say, wonderingly, that Few are those being saved; knowing the
rest, that it is very laborious for someone of the first portion to be found,
of necessity we bring in the second, following apostolic and Patristic
sayings; that both those who have departed are benefited by the goodness of
God, and brotherly love is increased, and the hope of the resurrection is
confirmed, and the prayer toward God is strengthened, and the running-together
around the divine temples is multiplied more warmly, and the good-doing toward
the poor is widened."
κγʹ–ιδʹ. See according to how many manners the matter is profitable and
beneficial, from how many suggestions the benefit of the departed; for it
becomes an occasion of salvation of those left behind. Since if you take away
the cause, altogether also the things following from it. For what necessity
would persuade the small-souled to rise up and to accomplish this, if they do
not have full assurance that they benefit their own? And the portions written
in the dispositions of the poor will also cease, and with these the liturgies
on behalf of the dead, hymnody and psalmody, forties with third-ninths, and
yearly memorials and rites, which were not invented in vain by the teachers.
But =95.272= may it not come to be, either to glorify at all, or to omit any
of these.
κεʹ. But if someone should speak concerning the strangers and the poor, who do
not possess those who will contend on their behalf, nor indeed are they able
to leave behind a portion of liturgies or distributions: What then, he says,
are these? shall they miss salvation because they are without resource and are
strangers and overlooked, and do not have fellow-laborers? Is God then unjust,
that he will give to the one who has, but to the one who does not have he will
provide nothing? Let everyone put this away from himself; for God the Master
is just, or rather to say more truly, righteousness itself and wisdom and
goodness and power hypostatic. As therefore just, he would measure back to the
one without resource the things of abundance; as wise, he will transact the
exchange of deficiencies; as powerful, he will break the strong, and will
strengthen the weak; and as good, he will save the formation of his own hands,
unless someone is of those clearly condemned, and of those spat upon of the
right faith, whose left scale will be weighted much. For the men enlightened
from God say that as on a balance at the last breath the deeds of men are
tested; and if the right scale exceed the other, it is quite clear that the
one who has obtained it would expire among the right-hand angels. But if again
both are equal, the philanthropy of God altogether conquers. But as the
God-speaking ones say, even if the beam on the left incline a little, even
then the mercy of God will fill up what is needed. Behold three divine
judgments of the Master; the first just; the second man-loving; and the third
all-good. Then from these the fourth, whenever the wicked deeds are weighed
overmuch. Woe is me, brothers; and this altogether most just, ordaining just
things for those who have been judged.
κϛʹ. But if some should speak concerning Basil the blessed and ecumenical
luminary, as he having said: "Do not be deceived; God is not mocked. A dead
man is not brought up as a sacrifice. Bring," he says, "your sacrifice living.
You cannot from the leftovers of your table receive the illustrious ones of
the king; for he who offers from the surplus is unacceptable. And you, what

heart." And: "That =95.273= the time you lived, taking pleasure in life, and
overflowing with luxury, you could not even endure to look upon the poor. But
when you have died, what wage of work is owed?" And again, that "The house of
the neighbor overshadows me," and that "The greedy man does not respect time,
nor does he know boundaries, but after the manner of fire, he seizes and feeds
upon all things, and as a river having rushed, he sweeps away by the current
what stands in the way," and other things equal to these, which all know, who
have attentively bent over his all-sacred book, that the discourse is hung
toward those who have given no command at all to the poor, but even seize
their things.
κζʹ. And let no one say: And where, he says, is such a man to be found,
flourishing in wealth, and not pitying the poor? Yes, truly he is found, as
also has been said before in many other times also, among which also in those
of the ever-memorable and all-holy John the Merciful, the one who passed from
implacability to complete almsgiving and holiness, Peter once the publican.
For it is reported in the all-blessed tablet of his life, that, it says, he
having come to be in ecstasy, and having seen his own deeds being weighed, he
is said to have beheld a loaf of fine wheat on the right scale of the balance,
which also he himself in anger had cast down like a stone upon the face of the
poor man; through which vision this blessed Peter has been shown to have
arrived at the very God-fearing.
κηʹ. Behold therefore it has been made known without doubt, toward what sort
the great Basil prepared the discourse. For there came to be also a famine
then strong and great at that time; and those who were rich, conquered by
stinginess, held the wealth together; but the poor, pressed by lack, were
destroyed by the famine. This divine Shepherd then opened, by his cutting and
bending, and at the same time most striking words, the storehouses of those
who had, without fear. For if he had not declared thus, the poor would not
have been fed, nor indeed would those who were rich have been bent toward
pity. And he these things.
κθʹ. But let us hasten, as much as is in our power, on =95.276= the fearful
and terrible day, I mean the second coming of the Lord, not to be accused by
our own, as having neglected the things on their behalf; and especially those
who have received commissions and deposits from them. For let no one think
that there will not be a recognition of each toward each at that fearful
assembly. Yes, truly each will recognize his neighbor, not by the form of the
body, but by the discerning eye of the soul. From where, you demand, shall we
present this? Hear the Master Christ in the parable of the poor Lazarus
teaching clearly, that, he says, "The rich man having lifted up his eyes,
recognized the poor Lazarus sitting in the bosom of the patriarch Abraham;"
and likewise also Abraham. And let no one say, because the matter is a
parable, that the intent is unproven. For the divine parables of our Savior
are of things standing, possible, and proven.
λʹ. And Chrysostom John says these things: that, "Not only shall we recognize
those known here, but we shall also see those who have never come into our
sight. For you have not seen Abraham, nor Isaac, nor Jacob, nor the
forefathers, nor the prophets, and apostles, and martyrs; but having seen
these at that catholic and fearful festival, having recognized them you will
say: Behold Abraham, and Isaac, and Jacob, and the remaining patriarchs.
Behold Peter, and Paul, and all the apostles. Behold David the Forefather of
God, and the swarm of prophets. Behold John the Forerunner, and Stephen the
first martyr, and the multitude of the saints." Then again Basil the great in
divine things, conversing toward the greedy, "Will you not take before your
eyes," he says, "the judgment-seat of Christ?


whenever those wronged by you stand around you in a circle and cry out against
you. For wherever you turn your eyes, you will see clear images of the evils.
From here the orphans, from there the widows; from another side the poor
struck down by you; the household servants, whom you tore; the neighbors whom
you provoked," and the things following these.
λαʹ. And Ephraim the blessed, the most accurate initiate of the second coming
of Christ, teaches thus. "Then," he says, "children will condemn their own
parents, as not having accomplished the good works. And that the wretched see
their acquaintances in that hour; and whenever they see some of them assigned
to the right-hand parts, being separated, they lament the farewell." And again
the one named from theology, "Then," he says, "I shall see Caesarius, bright,
glorious, rejoicing, such as you appeared to me also in a dream, dearest of
brothers." Then Athanasius, illustrious in life and word, and foundation of
the Church =95.277= of God, says these things concerning the dead in the Lord:
that "This also God has granted to those being saved, that until the common
resurrection they be together with one another in soul and rejoice together,
having expectation of the divine gifts about to be allotted to them; but the
sinners are deprived even of this consolation. For they do not have the
discerning among one another. But in that public theater, as the deeds of all
have been uncovered, so also the faces are known by all, until the last
separation of all come to be; and each will be sent away into the place he
prepared for himself; the righteous with God, and with one another; but the
sinners, in the out-of-place things, or even with one another; but
nevertheless unknowingly. For they have been deprived, as has been said
before, even of such consolation."
λβʹ. And what shame then will come to be for those who have been put to shame,
if they are not manifest to all? For then the shame is hard and very great,
whenever one both recognizes, and is recognized again; for everyone who is
ashamed is ashamed before those who have known him; since for the unknown
among unknowns, almost no shame will come to be for one being put to shame.
This then is undoubted and irrefutable, that we all shall clearly recognize
one another; and then the refutation of all who have lived unseemly will come
to be before the eyes of each.
λγʹ. Alas then for all who are according to me; and woe, according to the
divine Ephraim, to those who have obtained the left. But blessed and
all-blessed are those whom the Master will set on the right; and he will cause
a blessed voice to be heard, of which may we all be counted worthy to hear,
who have kept the orthodox faith, and to be filled with all the good things,
whose beauty the eye of mortals has not seen, nor indeed have ears heard at
all, nor has a heart reckoned altogether. Amen; may it come to be, Master
life-giver, by the intercessions of your all-pure Mother, and of your
immaterial and venerable minds, and of all together, O Word, of your saints,
who from the age have been well-pleasing to you. Amen.
