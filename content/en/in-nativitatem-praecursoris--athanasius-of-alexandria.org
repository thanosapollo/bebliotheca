OF OUR FATHER AMONG THE SAINTS ATHANASIUS
On the birthday of the Forerunner, and on Elizabeth, and on the Theotokos.
A field that has been left waste, and abandoned to become a trampling-place of
wild asses, the ploughing ox, cutting furrows with toil, raises the ridges;
for as long as the field has remained unworked by time, so much the more is
the ox that is set to the ploughing bent together with heavier toil, wearing
out the yoke-bearing neck. But a woman who has been made barren and grown old
through long years, and has fallen into two evils, both childlessness and
feebleness, childlessness of nature and feebleness of old age: by a word, and
not by toil, the Fashioner of nature, =28.908= God the Word, both renews the
old age of the body and sets right the barrenness of nature. And my word is
witnessed by the wife of the priest Zechariah, Elizabeth, who did not laugh
after the manner of Sarah, but believed after the manner of Anna, who was
renewed in her loosened members as an eagle; for she reared a child well
known, venerable, a companion of the Jordan streams. Truly great among women
is Elizabeth, as kinswoman of the glorious Virgin, as you heard just now
Gabriel saying to the Virgin: "And behold, Elizabeth your kinswoman." O
nobility of race! O practice of piety! O purity of life! O chastity of body! O
love of God in the soul! O vessel that receives myrrh! The serpent struck Eve
in the heel; to Elizabeth the angel announced the better things. Truly great
among women is Elizabeth, who both carried the Baptist John and reproached the
unbelieving Zechariah. And that Zechariah was unbelieving, reckoning the
weakness of the body more sure than the promise of God, you heard in the
former reading of the evangelist Luke, saying: "And an angel of the Lord
appeared to him, standing on the right of the altar of incense." The vision is
a counselor of the sight. The angel stood on the right, because a right-hand
birth was about to be born, proclaiming the right-hand way of the kingdom of
the heavens, according to the saying: "God knows the ways that are on the
right; but those on the left are perverse"; not changing the road, but hinting
at the manner. "And an angel of the Lord appeared to him, standing on the
right of the altar of incense." Let those present hear, and let them
understand, that angels stand by the altar; and where there is a standing of
angels, there is the tabernacling of God. Zechariah the priest was performing
his course of days, standing at the liturgy from days to days; not remaining
throughout, but serving in his course. But the angels of God who have been
allotted to the altar do not keep a course of days, but night and by day they
stand by the altar without interruption. They know both him who despises and
him who does not despise; they recognize both those who approach piously and
those who do not run up in this way; they know both those who keep their oaths
and those who swear falsely; they seal both those who bear fruit and those who
are numb. Do not look to this, that the altar has been beautified with stones;
for it is borne up by angels, it is encircled by archangels. For if every
national boundary is marked out according to the number of angels, according
to the saying: "He set the bounds of the nations according to the number of
the angels of God"; how much more is the altar filled with angels, where the
mystical coal is laid? which the seraphim, passing through with the tongs of
the Spirit through the liturgy of the priest, grant cleansing of sins to him
who asks? And that the altar is among these things, hear Isaiah the prophet
crying out: "I was looking, and behold, one of the seraphim was sent to me,
and he had a coal in his hand, which he took with the tongs from the altar;
and he touched my =28.909= lips, and said: Behold, it has touched your lips,
and it takes away your iniquities, and it will thoroughly cleanse your sins."
Did you see who stand by the altar, and with what eyes one must gaze within:
clean, without guile, ready to give, lovers of the poor?
What then does the evangelist Luke say? For it is good that we take up the
whole matter: "And an angel of the Lord appeared to him," he says, "standing
on the right of the altar of incense. And when Zechariah saw, he was troubled,
and fear fell upon him. And the angel said to him: Do not fear, Zechariah,
because your petition has been heard, and behold, Elizabeth your kinswoman
will bear you a son, and you shall call his name John. And there will be joy
to you and gladness, and to all your people." When the angel was declaring
these things, Zechariah, in this, seized with cowardice and fear of God,
answered back to the angel, saying: You command me to minister outside of
fear, and you say to me: Do not fear, Zechariah; and how is this possible?
What I have never seen, I have now for the first time beheld: a speaking fire,
an incorporeal man, a painted face, an uncompounded appearance, a form
composed of cloud, an air-walking winged runner; you send forth a word that
sits beside the sacrifice, and you display fear; your words are fiery, and
your promises hard to believe. Will Elizabeth conceive, and shall I be shown
forth as a father? When there is neighborhood of death, then a planting of a
child? When a grandfather in age, then a father in expectation? There is no
word toward offspring; the generative members have been quenched, nature has
gone to sleep at last, it remains unawakened toward desire, it has wrought
forgetfulness of itself. And I in old age have bent toward the earth, and
Elizabeth has hired a staff for her preparation. Once the dry rod of Aaron
blossomed, but not many times. By what shall I know this? And the angel to
him: Why do you chatter, Zechariah? Both do you serve as priest, and do you
doubt? Was not Abraham a hundred years old, and Sarah occupied with the death
of the womb? Did the fruit of these fail to be sent? If you did not have
examples, you doubted well; but if you are descended from those, why do you
not consider their things? The Gentile Abraham believed, and you, the priestly
one, hesitate? He was perfumed apart from incense, and you were smoked at the
altar? He, receiving the heir, rejoiced, and you reckon the forerunner John as
nothing? He himself will deliver you from the condemnation; he himself has
redeemed you from the curse; he himself bears the key of your tongue; he
himself will turn many to the Lord; he himself will go before in the power of
Elijah the prophet, not chariot-driving a fiery vessel, but preparing the
Jordan bath because of the great filth of sins. Therefore I was also commanded
to say to you: "Your petition has been heard"; because the time has arrived
that was of old prophesied and is now fulfilled: "A voice of one crying in the
wilderness: Prepare the way of the Lord, make straight his paths." Not an
angel, not an ambassador, but the Lord himself will come and save us. For this
reason, Zechariah, =28.912= be you silenced, receiving in the time of
contradiction the penalty of the punishment; but I pass from place to place,
and I am about to go to the Virgin, departing from military converse to
apostolic ministry. These things, O friend, have been set forth by me in brief
as a way before, concerning the unbelief of Zechariah that has been taken up.
But what the things just now read also are, it is time henceforth to inquire.
What things are these? The things announced by Gabriel, and believed by the
Virgin, and advanced by the Spirit, and stored up by the Lord, and sealed by
the Father, and guarded by the pious.
You heard just now the evangelist Luke saying: "In the sixth month the angel
Gabriel was sent into a city of Galilee, whose name was Nazareth, to a virgin
betrothed to a man whose name was Joseph." O betrothal-tokens betrothed to
one, and pledged to another! O betrothal-tokens that make joy manifest, and
that divide disturbance! You heard just now the evangelist Luke saying: And
the angel, having entered into the house, greeted the Virgin and said: "Hail,
full of grace, the Lord is with you; blessed are you among women. But she,
seeing, was greatly troubled at his word, and was reasoning in herself what
manner of greeting this might be." Did you see the joy of the angel raising up
trouble? And not only trouble, but also reasonings, but also doubt, but also
things akin to these. For the suddenness of the angelic entrance, and the
strangeness of the appearance, and the unwontedness of the meeting, and the
vastness of the promise, and the unexpectedness of the hearing, cast the
Virgin into cowardice, she who by the device of chastity had put every base
device out of the way. Therefore she was also troubled, and having been
troubled she remained untroubled. What is, And having been troubled she
remained untroubled? Hear with understanding. When Gabriel the angel,
fulfilling an apostolic ministry, was going with noiseless foot toward the
virgin's chamber; for the Virgin alone was present, dwelling in the house of
him who had betrothed her; you all know clearly also how a virgin at the
beginnings of the contract blushes, bearing her glances down, speaking
quietly, not lifting her head, taking thought for all chastity. Seeing her in
these things the angel says to her: "Do not fear, Mary," even if you were in
agony, and as a virgin took thought for chastity, lest your betrothed, rushing
down, should condemn your piety. Know this, O Virgin, that you will have no
communion with your betrothed Joseph. Another matter awaits you, divine, of
the Master, unspeakable, uninterpretable, not able to be set forth in word.
You will be found a shrine of the Master, you will be shown forth a mother
untried in marriage, you are about to receive the Master of heaven and earth,
conceiving an uncorrupted birth. "Do not fear, Mary," establish your
reasoning, sinew your soul, settle your heart. Do not imitate Zechariah, do
not doubt, as he did, you the boast of women; you the gladness of the
Israelite people; you the ornament of the nations; you the root =28.913= of
joy; you the planting of the blessing. You have found grace with God beyond
all the virgins. And, "behold, you will conceive a Son, and you shall call his
name Jesus. This one will be great, and will be called Son of the Most High."
I have come as messenger of these good things to you, even if the things said
are outside nature; but nevertheless with the nature-fashioner of all
creation there is nothing crooked or tangled, but all things are straight and
looking toward ease. Caught in these promises, and released from every base
device, the Virgin no longer kept silence as one without freedom of speech,
saying to the angel: "How shall this be to me, since I do not know a man?" Why
do you both alarm my heart and make my hearing foreign in sound? Why do you
announce what has not been heard from the age? Shall I be found pregnant, and
shall I not take experience of a man? "How shall this be to me?" Shall I be
named mother, and shall I be outside of maternal birth-pangs? "How shall this
be to me?" Shall I give the breast, and bind swaddling-clothes, and nurse a
child with milk, and shall I not know what is in me? "How shall this be to
me?" Tell me the reasonable cause, that I may know the doubtful conception.
The generation of Eve was strange, but nevertheless she was taken from her
husband; and she had the cause from which the mixture was known, that she who
was taken for him was from him, and the own place was filled up for the own.
More strange than Eve was the generation of Adam; but nevertheless this too,
having been made manifest, was handed down in writing, and how it came to be
at once was clearly known, that the Fashioner, taking dust from the earth,
constructed the first-formed as a God-shaped living being. As those things are
known, make this also clear; since what was said beforehand to me by you has
overshot the things that have been said: "How shall this be to me, since I do
not know a man?" And Gabriel to the Virgin: How shall this be to you? Do you
seek to learn the manner from me, O Virgin? Can a slave know the purpose of a
master? How shall this be to you? I was sent as a messenger, not as an
interpreter. "Who has known the mind of the Lord? Or who has become his
counselor?" What I hear I speak, I fashion nothing of my own. How shall this
be to you? Do not seek the how in the case of God. How shall this be to you?
As Isaiah declared beforehand, saying: "Behold, the Virgin shall have in the
womb"; he declared beforehand that you shall have, but I say to you that "you
shall bear a son and you shall call his name Jesus"; to him be the glory unto
the ages of ages. Amen.
