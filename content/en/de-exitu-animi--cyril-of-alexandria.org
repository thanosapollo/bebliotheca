HOMILY 14
Concerning the departure of the soul, and concerning the second coming.
=77.1072=
I fear death, because it is bitter to me. I fear Gehenna, because it is
endless. I fear Tartarus, because it has no share of warmth. I fear the
darkness, because it has no share of light. I fear the venomous worm, because
it is endless. I fear the angels over the judgment, because they are
unmerciful. I fear as I consider the fearful and impartial tribunal of that
day, the dreadful judgment-seat, the impartial judge. I fear the river of fire
that is dragged before that judgment-seat, and that crashes with a most
violent flame, the sharpened swords. I fear the severe punishments. I fear the
chastisement that has no end. I fear the lightless gloom. I fear the outer
darkness. I fear the unlooseable bonds, the gnashing of teeth, the
inconsolable weeping. I fear the inescapable refutations; for that judge needs
no accusers, nor witnesses, nor proofs, nor examinations; but whatever we have
done, and spoken, and planned, he brings into the midst before the eyes of
those who have sinned.
Then there is no one standing by and snatching from the punishment, not
father, not mother, not son, not daughter, not any other of the kinsmen, not
neighbor, not friend, not advocate, not a gift of money, not abundance of
wealth, not the bulk of power; but all these things have been driven like dust
into ashes, and he alone, being judged from the things done by him, endures
the vote that frees, or the vote that condemns. Woe is me! woe is me! as
conscience convicts me, and the writing cries out and teaches me: O soul, of
the pollutions, and of the works abominated even with you! Woe is me, that I
corrupted the temple of the body, and I grieved your Holy Spirit! O God, true
are your works, and just is your judgment, and straight are your ways
=77.1073= and unsearchable are your judgments. Through a temporary enjoyment
of sin I am tortured with immortal things; through pleasure of flesh I am
handed over to the fire. Just is the judgment of God; I was called, and I did
not obey; I was taught, and I did not attend; they bore witness to me, but I
laughed; reading and recognizing, not disbelieving, but in negligence, and
sluggishness, and acedia, and in distractions, and tumults, and storms, living
in luxury and wantonness, and leaping, rejoicing, and making merry, I spent my
years, and the months, and the days on the temporary, and corruptible, and
earthly things, toiling, and laboring, and struggling; not taking into mind,
or reckoning, what fear and trembling, and contest, and necessity the soul has
to see when it is separated from the body. For there come upon us armies and
heavenly powers; and of the opposing powers, the rulers of darkness, the
world-rulers of wickedness, the tax-chiefs, and accountants, and tax-reckoners
of the air; and with them the man-slaying devil, the potentate in evil, whose
tongue is as a sharpened razor; concerning whom the prophet says: The arrows
of the mighty one are sharpened with the desolate coals; and he lies in wait
as a lion in his fold, the great dragon, the apostate, Hades, who widens his
mouth, the ruler of the authority of darkness, who has the power of death and
in a certain manner as a lawsuit, holding the soul, bringing forward and
reckoning all the things done by me in deed and in word, the lawless acts in
knowledge and in ignorance, and


sins, from youth until the day of the end at which I was overtaken, searching
them out thereafter.
What fear and trembling do you suppose the soul has on that day, seeing the
fearful, and wild, and harsh, and unpitying and untamed demons, standing by
like gloomy Ethiopians, whose very appearance alone is more grievous than all
punishment, seeing whom the soul is thrown into tumult, is terrified, is
pained, is troubled, and shrinks, fleeing to the angels of God? The soul then
is held by the holy angels, passing through the air and being raised, and it
finds toll-houses guarding the ascent, and holding, and hindering the souls
that are going up. Each toll-house brings forward their own sins: that of
slander, as many things as through mouth and tongue from falsehood, and oath,
and perjury, idle talk and babbling, and vain speech and gluttonous misuses,
prodigal drinkings of wine, and immoderate and unseemly laughters, and of
unseemly and indecent kisses, and of fornicating songs; and the holy angels
who guide the soul also themselves bring forward as many good things as we
spoke through mouth and tongue, prayers, thanksgivings, psalms, odes, hymns,
and spiritual songs, readings of the Scriptures, and as many good things as
through mouth and tongue we sent forth to God. Second toll-house, seeing of
the eyes, and as many things as from unseemly spectacle, and curious, and
unbridled seeing, and deceitful nods. Third toll-house, of hearing, and as
many things as through such a sense the unclean spirits receive. Fourth
toll-house, the =77.1076= smelling of fragrant odor of unguents, and of
pleasurable smelling, which befit theatrical women and courtesans. Fifth
toll-house, as many wicked and grievous things as were done through the touch
of hands; and the remaining toll-houses of wickedness, of envy and jealousy,
of vainglory and pride, of bitterness and anger, of irascibility and wrath, of
fornication, and adultery, and softness, of murder and sorcery, and of the
remaining God-hated and polluted deeds; of which in the present hour it is not
possible to narrate in detail; but let it be stored up for another time; and
simply thus in order each passion of the soul, and every sin, has its own
tax-collectors and tribute-gatherers.
A soul then that sees these things and things greater and more than these,
what fear, and trembling, and shaking do you suppose it has, until the
sentence comes, and its freedom comes to be? That is the hour full of pain,
and dangerous, and much-groaning, and inconsolable, until it sees what will
result. For the divine powers stand, and the face of the unclean spirits; and
these bring forward its good deeds, through words and works, and reasonings,
and thoughts; the soul considers, standing in the midst in fear and trembling,
until from its practices, and works, and words, the one condemned is bound, or
the one justified is set free (for each is bound with the cords of his own
sins); and if it is worthy, having lived piously, and having lived in a manner
pleasing to God, angels receive it; and thereafter being without care, it goes
having as fellow-travelers the holy powers, according to what is written: How
the dwelling of all who rejoice is in you. Then is fulfilled what was said:
Pain, grief, and groaning have fled away. Then, having been delivered from
those wicked and rotten and fearful spirits, it goes into that unspeakable
joy. But if it is found to have lived in negligence and prodigality, it hears
that most terrible voice: Let the ungodly be taken away, that he may not see
the glory of the Lord.
Then days of wrath, and affliction, and necessity, and distress overtake it, a
day of darkness and gloom; then the holy angels of God leaving it, those
Ethiopian demons receive it, and beating it unmercifully they bring it down to
the earth; and having split it, they cast it bound


with unlooseable bonds, into a dark and gloomy land, into the lower parts, in
the subterranean prisons, and the jails of Hades; where the souls of the
sinners who have fallen asleep from the age happen to be shut up, as Jacob
says: into a dark and gloomy land, into a land of eternal darkness, without
light, nor life of mortals, but eternal pain, and endless grief, and unceasing
weeping, and unsilenced gnashing of teeth, and sleepless groanings; there woe
continually, there woe is me, woe is me! there they cry out, and there is none
who helps; they shout, and there is none who delivers; it is not possible to
narrate that necessity; it is not possible to say through the tongue the pains
of the souls lying and =77.1077= shut up there; every mouth of man is unable
to make manifest that fear and that trembling; there are not lips of man able
to say their distress, and their weeping; they groan continually and
unceasingly, but there is none who pities; they cry from the depth, but there
is none who hearkens; they lament, but there is none who delivers; they call
out and beat themselves, but there is none who is moved with compassion. Then
where is the boasting of this world? where is vainglory? where luxury? where
enjoyment? where wantonness? where fantasy? where rest? where the world? where
the money? where nobility? where then is delight? where the manliness of the
flesh? where the false and useless beauty of women? where then the shameless
and unashamed boldness? where then the adorning of garments? where the
pleasure of sin, the unclean and putrid? where the one who counted the
abominable bed of the unholy as pleasure? where those who anoint themselves
and are smoked with myrrhs and unguents? where those who drink wine with drums
and a lyre? where then the contempt of those living in fearlessness? where
love of money, and love of possessions, and the lack of compassion from them?
where then the inhuman pride that abominates all things, and reckons itself to
be something? where then the empty and vain glory of men? where lust and
incontinence? where power, and tyranny? where then a king? where a ruler?
where a leader? where those in authorities? where those who pride themselves
on abundance of wealth, and do not pity the poor, and despise God? where the
theatres, and the hunts? where then those who are lifted up, and those living
moderately, and those who live without care? where the soft garments, and the
soft and delicate beds? where the high buildings, and the breadth of the
colonnades? where those who have lived in fearlessness?
Then having seen, they will wonder and shudder, and being struck they will
wail, being troubled they will be shaken; let trembling seize them, and pangs
as of a woman in labor; they will be crushed, vanishing, in a violent wind.
Where then is the wisdom of the wise? where the eloquence of the rhetors, and
their vain cunning devices? Woe, woe! they were troubled, they were shaken as
the drunken man, and all their wisdom was swallowed up. Where is a wise man?
where a scribe? where a disputer of this age? O brothers, consider what manner
of people we must be, giving an account for each one of the things we have
done, whether great or small; for even as far as an idle word we shall give a
defense to the just judge. What manner of people must we be in that hour? And
if we find grace before God, what joy receives us as we are set apart at the
right of the King! What manner of people must we be for that unspeakable joy,
when the King of those who reign says to those on his right with gladness:
Come, you blessed of my Father, inherit the kingdom prepared for you from the
foundation of the world.
Then we shall inherit those good things, Which eye has not seen, and ear has
not heard, and have not risen upon the heart of man, which God has prepared
for those who love him. Then thereafter we are without care, no longer fearing
any fear. But let us consider =77.1080= these things, and the endless


punishment of the sinners, when they are led into the fearful judgment-seat,
what shame will take them before the just judge, having no word of defense!
What disgrace will take them as they are set apart at the left of the king!
What darkness will fall upon them, when he speaks to them in his anger, and
troubles them in his wrath; when he says to them: Depart from me, you cursed,
into the eternal fire prepared for the devil and his angels. Woe is me! woe is
me! what affliction, and pain, and distress, and fear, and trembling their
spirits will receive, when there comes to be a cry of all the heavenly powers,
saying: Let the sinners be turned back into Hades! woe, woe! what a strain
they will raise as they lament, and mourn, and wail, beating themselves, being
led away to be punished bitterly unto endless ages! Woe is me, woe is me! of
what sort is the place, where there is the weeping and the gnashing of teeth,
called Tartarus, at which even the devil himself shudders! woe, woe! of what
sort is the Gehenna of the unquenchable fire, that burns and does not give
light! woe is me, woe is me! of what sort is the sleepless and venomous worm!
woe, woe! how terrible is that outer darkness, that also remains forever! woe
is me, woe is me! of what sort are those angels, the ones over the
punishments, without compassion and unmerciful! for they reproach, and they
rebuke terribly. Then those being punished cry out earnestly, and there is no
one who saves; for they will cry to the Lord, and he will not hearken to them;
then they will know that all the things of life have gone away as vain for
them; and the things they thought here to be useful, and full of gladness,
will be found more bitter than gall and bitter venom. Woe to the sinners! when
the righteous stand on the right, and those are afflicted; when the sinners
mourn, and the righteous rejoice; when the righteous keep festival, and the
sinners lament; when the righteous are in calm, and the sinners in storm and
disaster. Woe to the sinners! when the righteous are glorified, and those are
condemned; woe to the sinners! when the righteous are filled with every good
thing, and the sinners being in want groan. Woe to the sinners! when the
righteous are glorified, and those are reproached.
The righteous in sanctification, but the sinners in burning; the righteous are
praised, but the sinners are pronounced wretched; the righteous in the
dwellings of saints, but the sinners in eternal exile; the righteous will
hear, Come, you blessed of my Father, inherit the kingdom prepared for you
from the foundation of the world. But the sinners will hear, Depart from me,
you cursed, into the eternal fire prepared for the devil and his angels. The
righteous into paradise, the sinners into the unquenchable fire; the righteous
live in luxury, the sinners are worn out. The righteous dance, but the sinners
are bound. The righteous sing, the sinners lament. The righteous the
trisagion, the sinners the thrice-wretched. The righteous the song, the
sinners the chasm. =77.1081= The righteous into the bosoms of Abraham, the
sinners into the boilings of Beliar. The righteous into rest, the sinners into
condemnation. The righteous are cooled with dew, the sinners are set aflame.
The righteous rejoice, the sinners are dried up with grief. The righteous are
magnified, but the sinners waste away. The righteous are exalted, but the
sinners are humbled. The righteous are warmed, but the sinners are blackened.
The righteous are filled with good things, but the sinners are shaken. The
vision of God will feed the righteous, the vision of fire will grieve the
sinners. The righteous a vessel of election, the sinners a vessel of Gehenna.
The righteous gold tried by fire, and approved silver and precious stones; the
sinners wood, stubble, grass, fuel of fire. The righteous wheat of the
kingdom, the sinners, chaff of destruction. The righteous chosen seed, the
sinners tares of fire. The righteous divine salt, the sinners stench and foul
smell. The righteous undefiled temples of God; the sinners, defiled temples of
demons. The righteous in the


bride-chamber, the sinners into boundless chaos. The righteous in
light-appearances, the sinners in the gloom of a storm. The righteous with
angels, the sinners with demons. The righteous dance with angels, the sinners
lament with demons. The righteous in the midst of the light, the sinners in
the midst of the darkness. The righteous are comforted by the Paraclete, the
sinners are punished with the demons. The righteous stand by the Master's
throne, the sinners stand by the punishing gloom. The righteous continually
see the face of Christ, the sinners continually stand facing the devil. The
righteous are initiated with angels, the sinners are initiated with demons.
The righteous offer supplication, the sinners unceasing lamentation. The
righteous above, but the sinners below. The righteous in heaven, but the
sinners in the abyss. The righteous into eternal life, the sinners into a
death of destruction. The righteous in the hand of God, the sinners in the
place of the devil. The righteous with God, the sinners with Satan. Woe to the
sinners! when they are separated from the righteous. Woe to the sinners! when
their deeds are stripped bare, and the counsels of the hearts are made
manifest. Woe to the sinners! when the couplings of the mind are convicted,
and the consents of the wicked thoughts are weighed, and the understanding is
balanced. Woe to the sinners! when they are hated by the holy angels, and are
abominated by the holy martyrs. Woe to the sinners! when they are cast out of
the bride-chamber; woe the repentance then! woe the affliction then! woe the
necessity then! woe the tempest then! Terrible, to be separated from the
saints; more grievous, to be separated from God; dishonorable, to be bound
hand and foot, and to be cast into the fire; distressing, to be sent out into
the outer darkness; gloomy, to grind the teeth and waste away; heavy, to be
punished unceasingly.
Wicked, to have the tongue set aflame; without sympathy, to ask for a drop of
water, and not to receive it. Bitter, to be in fire, and to cry out, and not
to be helped. Impassable is the =77.1084= gullet, and immeasurable the chasm,
inescapable the one shut out, unfleeable the one held, unsurmountable the wall
of the prison, without compassion the keepers, dark the jail, unlooseable the
bonds, unpullable the chains, wild, and untamed the servants of that flame,
heavy those punishing helmets, firm and unbreakable the claws, hard the
ox-sinews, turbid and bubbling the pitches, foul-smelling the brimstone,
coal-like those beds there, unquenchable the pyre, reeking and stinking the
worm; unforgiving the tribunal, impartial the judge, without excuse the
defense; branded the faces of the potentates, poor the potentates, beggars the
kings; laymen the wise, foolish and unaccepted the rhetors; senseless the
rich, unheard the flatteries of the forgers of writings; manifest the windings
of the knaves, clear the tracks of the greedy; stinking the smell of the
money-lovers, manifest the duplicity of the hypocrites; linen-struck, the
impostors; for all things are naked and laid bare before them. Woe to the
sinners! they are profane, and accursed, and unclean before God. How their
souls have been defiled! How their bodies stink from lust and incontinence!
How they polluted the bodies, and defiled the soul, not keeping the robe of
holy baptism! How shamelessly and without blushing, in surfeit and
drunkenness, the stench-storehouse of the belly of prodigality, the wineskin
of the stomach, not having aligned themselves with sufficiency and
self-control, but having entrusted their wealth to it, luxuriously rolling in
the pleasures as swine in the mire, they spent their days and years; being
lifted up in filthy reasonings, and base thoughts, and idle talkings, and
theatrical songs!


How their heart was altered by hardness, not taking into mind the enlistment
of Christ, and the renunciation of the devil!
How they were turned aside from the straight way, walking in the darkness of
ignorance, and giving themselves over to the sleep of sluggishness, plunging
themselves into the springboard of Gehenna! How they were alienated from the
light of the virtues, having loved the darkness of sin, because they walked
the broad and spacious way of well-trodden wickedness! How they forgot the
sojourn of our Lord, and God, and Savior Jesus Christ, and his many and
unsearchable benefactions, having been cleansed through water of the divine
baptism and Holy Spirit, and having been arrayed with the myrrh of gladness!
but through a small pleasure, hated and loathsome, having set aside such and
so great gifts, they were enslaved to a spirit of fornication and adultery.
Woe to those who have left the adoption, and have followed the pleasures of
the world! Woe to those who follow the encounters of the world! Woe to those
who love the darkness of sin! Woe to those who have left the light of the
truth! Woe to those who walk in a night of sin! Woe to those who abandon the
day of the knowledge of God! Woe to those filled with the evil custom of
laughter! =77.1085= Woe to those who adorn themselves so as to wound souls
toward intercourse and lust of unclean incontinence! truly it is a hook of the
devil, not an adornment; therefore to those who long, and seek, and wish to be
saved, it is hated. Woe to those who slander one another! Woe to the
eavesdroppers, and the quarrel-joiners, and the troublemakers! Woe to those
who swear through love of pleasure. Woe to the perjurers! woe to the gluttons,
whose god is the belly! Woe to those who are drunk!
But blessed is the one who here makes himself cheap, and humbles himself for
the sake of God, and sets himself at nought and condemns himself! such a one
is exalted by God Most High, and is praised by angels, and in the judgment
does not stand on the left. Blessed is a man who perseveres in prayers, and
endures in fasts, and rejoices in vigils, and wrestles against, and drives off
sleep, and bending the knees to the doxology of God, beating the breast,
striking the face, lifting the hands into the air, raising the eye to heaven
toward the Lord, considering the one seated upon a throne of glory, and
searching hearts, and entering into kidneys! For such a one enjoys the eternal
good things, he becomes a son, and a brother, and a friend, and an heir of
God. This one's face will shine as the sun on the day of judgment, in which is
the kingdom of the heavens. And the one who loves the truth is found a friend
of God; but the one who persists in lies becomes a friend of demons. The one
who hates deceit is redeemed from the curse. The one who endures the trials is
crowned as a confessor before the judgment-seat of Christ. The one who
murmurs, and is impatient in the disasters that fall, and in the afflictions
that occur, both growing slack and blaspheming, this one has gone astray, and
has deaf wits. The meek, and gentle, and humble-minded, is praised by God, is
blessed by the angels, and is praised by men. But the bitter, and
quick-tempered, and irascible, has been cursed from God; this one's food is a
cluster of bitterness of demons; and the wine, wrath of dragons, and the drink
incurable venom of an asp. The pure in heart see the glory of God; but those
whose mind is soiled mirror the devil.
Those who have done base things, and who think unfitting things, and who
reckon evils against the neighbor, hinder them from the divine communion.
Those who moisten the face with crimson, and rub and whiten the cheeks with
white lead, and who array themselves through mirrors and looking-glasses, so
as to catch and net souls into incontinence and unfitting desires, and satanic
loves, on the day of judgment are not found godly; but as despisers of the
commandments of God


they are punished. Those who curiously examine another's beauty are deprived
of the beauty of paradise. Those who rejoice at the fall of others throw
themselves down. Those who desire the things of others betray their own and
are destroyed. The vainglorious, and proud and man-pleasers, are condemned
with the devil. The hypocrites, =77.1088= are punished with Satan. Those who
feed the body beyond what is due starve the soul; those who sin in knowledge
and without necessity, and do not repent, are punished with unbelievers; those
who say, Let us sin in youth, and let us repent in old age, are mocked by
demons, and are jeered at, and as sinning willingly, they are not counted
worthy of repentance, but in youth they are reaped by the sickle of death; as
Ammon the king of Israel, who also provoked God through his wicked reasonings,
and his profane thoughts. For those who say, Today let us sin, and tomorrow
let us repent, such have become vain in their reasonings; and their senseless
heart was darkened, and they lost today, having corrupted and defiled the
body, and having stained the soul, and having darkened the mind, and having
muddied the understanding, and having mired the conscience; and tomorrow they
were stolen; For those who do not weep because of the fall of fornication, and
do not mourn because of the mire of adultery, and do not lament because of the
springboard of lying with males, and do not wail because of softness, cannot
repent with their whole soul concerning the sins that have gone by, nor
correct the things to come. For those who do not seek what they have lost have
not acquired even the things that are being saved. Those who do not reckon the
mixing of the deposit are also damaged in the capitals. Those who do not labor
mindfully, and keep sober in prayers, are taken captive by shameful thoughts;
and those who have been taken captive serve the evil custom, unwilling and not
wishing. Those who do not keep vigil soberly in the psalmody are stolen. Those
who do not watch in the hearing of the divine Scriptures, but have given
themselves over to the sleep of sluggishness, will be shut out with the five
foolish virgins. Those who throw away the weapons of fasting are tripped by
gluttony, and are slain by the fornicating-adulterous sin; those who have not
kept the commandments of God are wounded by demons, and are condemned in a
Gehenna of fire.
Those who distance themselves from the Church and from communion become
enemies of God, and friends of demons. Let every heresy of the godless
heretics be put to shame, let the race of the unbelievers be covered, let the
synagogue of the Jews perish, let the unclean mouths of the God-denying
Hebrews be stopped, when the one who tests the hearts, and searches the
kidneys, and sharper than every two-edged sword, reaching as far as the
division of flesh, and spirit, of joints and marrows, and a discerner of
thoughts, and of notions, sits judging. Then indeed, then you will see not
some few out of many, but all being uncovered, and neither can the hide of the
sheep cover the wolf, nor a whitewashing of the works hide the thought within;
for there is no creature unmanifest to the one who judges, but all things are
naked, and laid bare to his eyes. Therefore let us not array ourselves against
the commandments of God with fleshly passions; let us humble vainglorious
reasonings, let us rise against the devil for battle, let us give eyes to the
understanding toward sobriety, let us put to sleep reasonings of sin; let us
acquire undistracted prayer, a sober mind, an awakened understanding, an
unmuddied conscience, endless self-control, unhypocritical fasting, impartial
love, unadulterated chastity, undefiled prudence, undissembling humility,
unceasing psalmody, unvainglorious reading, unproud kneeling, continual
petition, a clean life, a true word, unmurmuring hospitality, thankful
endurance, unexamined almsgiving; let us ostracize irascibility, let us drive
out anger, let us expel acedia, let us


kill wrath, let us wither grief, let us dry up love of money. Let us not fear
the common death, the reaper of the race of men, but the destroyer of men; for
death in the proper sense is not the one that separates the soul from the
body, but the one that separates a soul =77.1089= from God.
God is life; and the one who is separated from life has died, having cast away
the former boldness as life; and since death, the devil the father of death,
stands as a strong opponent being armed, that he may wrestle us and throw us
in the holy days, and say: I have conquered the soldiers of Christ, having
shown beauty of women, and I hung them from the hearing; having pitted them
with vainglory and gluttony, I seized the hair of their virtues, and having
tripped them with the desires, and having tickled them with wine-drinking,
having shoved I threw them into a pit of fornication; let us not then gladden
the unclean demons, because our God is salvation of all and destroyer of
demons. Since therefore we too have been bound together with bodies, and are
liable to death, let us contend nobly, that we may conquer the unclean demons;
for if we have the fear in the heart, and we carry about the memory of death
in the soul, all the demons are armed against us; but as battering-rams, they
will find us as a wall; because our Lord is with us; for to him belongs glory,
honor and power, unto the ages of ages. Amen.
