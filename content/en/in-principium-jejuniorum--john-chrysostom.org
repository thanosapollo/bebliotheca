On the beginning of the fasts, and concerning fasting.
=62.745=
The spiritual spring of the fast has shone upon us today, beloved, and the
meadows of
self-control have been opened to the merchants of piety. The sailors of the
Church call us to the heavenly commerce, and they make ready for us the voyage
of righteousness, unfolding the prophetic parchments for us, and they take the
tillers of the law in hand. They hoist the mast of the precious Cross, and
they make fast the anchor of the faith. The Holy Spirit makes this
merchant-ship known, and the Maker of all watches over it. Come then, we too
today, beloved, let us receive with joy the purity of the fast standing before
the doors, and with the soul's greetings let us cry out to her: Come, O mother
of good things and of the virtues, fill us with the heavenly fragrance, open
for us the paradise of the evangelical way of life, let us enjoy the spiritual
fruits of self-control, purify us with the diadem of temperance, grant us an
unclouded life, heal the wounds of the souls that come from luxury, quench the
kindling that comes from drunkenness, the fevered and diseased thoughts that
come from despondency; shut the theaters of the devil, take away the frenzy,
hobble the beast-feeding workshop of inhumanity, rebuke the winds of the
wicked spirits, reshape those who fast into the way of life of the bodiless,
show the venerable gray hair of an old man, draw with the plow of
self-control, brighten men who spend their days in dissipation, adorn women
who run the course of dignity, reshape youth toward the angelic life; let
those worn out by sins breathe again from vain labors, let them cast off with
the Forty Days of the fast the filth of the whole year; for you fill not only
the earth, but even paradise itself already.

Now the powers beyond the world exult, the ranks of the angels keep festival
spiritually. Thrones, Dominions, Principalities and Powers rejoice at the
salvation of men. Only the devil who fell from the heavens, looking grim,
says: Where now is my boasting against those thrown headlong by gluttony?
Where is my soul-destroying engine against men through eating? Where now is
the greedy tasting of the tree, by which, deceiving Adam together with Eve, I
stripped them? My devices of wickedness are gone now that the fast has
appeared. From where has this so-called fast shown itself against me? Who has
now levied it as an army against me? No longer is Adam, deceived with Eve,
stripped of God; no longer does he obey a soul-destroying counsel; no longer
does he gape with greed; no longer is he held in the snare of gluttony. Again
through fasting Adam is in paradise, again he boasts, again he takes pride in
the divine image, again he is led back to the archetypal beauty; and what is
more terrible than this, that having lost paradise through transgression, he
has leaped up into the heavens on the chariot of the fast. I was thrown from
those places, and the one who was cast out again rides upon the heavenly
powers. I have become a laughingstock, beaten by men, thrown down by fasters,
driven out by human nature. But let the evil one lament these his own failures
as he is struck; as for us, snatching from the apostolic quiver a well-aimed
arrow for the present, let us wound the pleasures of greed in him, crying with
Paul the things of Paul: We brought nothing into the world; it is clear that
we can carry nothing out either. Would that our entrance might imitate our
exit; would that a man might go away naked and free of punishment; would that
he were poor by nature, and his soul uncondemned; would that he were a beggar
and without charge, would that he were without means and without pain. But
even the most terrible thing: leaving here what he gathered, a man goes away
bearing the sin from those things; and he has been deprived of the gain, yet
has not been set free from the condemnation for them; but another succeeds to
the enjoyment of what was his, while he alone endures the punishment; and the
one who did not acquire, having it, lives in luxury, while the one who
acquired, having nothing, is tormented, having gained one thing only from
being rich: to pay the penalty. Wake from the sea of this =62.746= life, O
man, and divide your wealth willingly among the poor; do not leave it to
enemies; anticipate the mouths that gape at you; do not become prey of those
who hunt out other men's substance.

Are you a father of children? Count the soul as their sister, and set apart a
portion of the substance for her too. Are you a lover of children? Become also
a lover of God, through whom you gained the love of children. But have you
fallen ill with childlessness? Do not fall among sons whom nature has not
given you; doing well by yourself, make yourself the heir; do not appear
before the Judge naked of almsgiving; flee the fire that is threatened; make
ready for the common departure of the race; look round at that last day of
your life. When the angels who come by suddenly stand over you to take your
soul, what will you do, being dragged by the thief death? When you are about
to despond from the danger that holds you, when you speak faintly, when you
utter things indistinct, when you breathe weakly and thickly, when a flame is
kindled within, overpowering the soul, when no ally stands by as the terror
presses, when a brother does not ransom, shall a man ransom? when money runs
away, when the soul is driven together into that fearful judgment-seat, when
you will no longer be master of your own, when you repent of what you have
done, repentance no longer having a place, when the door of repentance is
shut, and the entrance of paradise is walled off, when you groan from the
depth of the heart, and will not find the one who delivers, will you not then
meet such perplexities in your own reasoning? By no means; but you will groan
bitterly in the soul and say without voice, Woe is me, woe is me, the wretched
one; I the miserable am pushed toward the exit, I am dragged by force to break
up my life, I am led bound where there is no returning. Henceforth servants do
not help, friends do not appear, the flatterers have withdrawn, the bodyguards
do not stand by; a purse does not help me, gold does not contribute, an army
does not deliver me, money does not ransom me, the illustrious do not save; in
vain I built houses, I spent so much money out of season, I piled up talents.
The things of glory fall away from me; I no longer command and have servants
stand by; I no longer give orders and have an army encircle me; I no longer
issue commands and have all cities fulfill what is ordered. What shall I, the
miserable one, do? What shall I do when the inescapable things press upon me?

I will importune, I will again entreat the death that drags me, even if he has
no mind of mercy. I beg you, O death, grant me a little time of life as a
gift. Do you not fear a royal dignity? Did you not see my chariot made ready
before the doors? Respect nobility that entreats, be shamed by gray hair that
importunes, spare weeping youth. I do not need, O death, a complete release,
but a small postponement. I have still left my house unfinished; I have not
yet managed, as I ought, to divide the inheritance among the heirs.
Forgetfulness until now has made me fail to sober up, occupied with the cares
of life; I left my mind without provision for what is more timely. Suddenly
you drag me to judgment; what words shall I use as I am brought in? What good
deed shall I call as fellow-worker? What achievement will plead for me as I am
judged? Having done what shall I defend myself before the Judge? What shall I
say, or what shall I utter, being forced to look down under the weight of my
faults? Be long-suffering toward me a little, I beg. Where do you drag me
unprepared? Why are you eager to present me naked to the Judge? Let me, I beg,
say something; I will reckon the offense, I will let out a voice of
confession, I will lament a little, I will groan a short while, I will weep
for a moment, I will greet friends, I will embrace my wife; if only with one
wrapping of repentance I may cover my soul. I will hire the poor from here as
advocates, I will contrive healing for my wounds before the departure; all the
substance I have I give for a little postponement. And even if you say things
wiser than these, O man, you will not hinder the violence of the death that
stands by. For death is an unflattered executioner, death is an unfeigned
decree, death is an impartial collector of the race, death is an unfeigned
murderer, death is the traveler of nature, death stands entering upon kings
without announcement, death is a thief working the same craft of theft both by
day and by night, death seizing, a robber of righteousness, death a possession
longed for by the righteous alone. =62.747=

Hearing these things, beloved, let us rise, having sobered up; let us not
sleep again in the sleep of sin; let us propitiate the things that have gone
before with the things that follow. Did you gather unjustly? Spend justly;
from the very occasions of the passion set medicines against the passion. Have
you ever done injustice? First spare those you have wronged; first be just,
and then a lover of mankind; first give back, and then in this way share;
first turn away from evil, and then in this way do good. Do not appear
generous from things in which you were not a lover of the brethren. When you
have shown mercy to those whom you made poor, then give to other poor from
what you have not seized; when you have stopped the tear of those wronged by
you, then have compassion on other poor who weep. Honor the Lord, said the
Scripture, from your just labors. For do not be deceived, God is not mocked.
He does not receive a gift while another groans; the Fashioner does not endure
to share the plunder with you. There it is not possible for justice to be
overlooked, it is not possible =62.748= for the one being convicted to escape;
but if you know in yourself, O man, that you have done something unseemly,
repent. Have you ever been rolled toward irrational desires, serving the
unlawful necessity? Have you become a debtor of many lawless deeds? Do not
fear the Judge and despair of your own salvation; he knows how to be bought
with mercy, he is by nature importuned by good deeds to the poor, he is bribed
by the consolations of beggars.

Hear him crying in the Gospels: Give alms, and all things are fulfilled. Give
to me, cries the one who enriches all; give to me, cries the one who has given
all things for enjoyment. He even endures receiving two obols from a widow; he
does not blush even to come down to a cup of cold water; he knows how to
accept even the purpose of a poor merciful man; for he himself said, He who
has mercy on a poor man lends to God; to whom belongs glory and might always,
now and ever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
