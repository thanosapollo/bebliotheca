* On Those Who Delay Baptism

OF THE SAME GREGORY, TO THOSE WHO DELAY BAPTISM. The kings of this inhabited
world, whenever they compose laws well regulating the lives of men, putting
them into the hands of the rulers, command through those that they be
published to the subjects, that thus the things ordered may be kept
inviolable. And the great God has given laws to the leaders of the Churches,
which, bringing them forward each year at the fitting times, we read out to
you, and we command you to keep the things written so far as lies in your
power. Behold then the good steward of all things, who cycles the years and
governs the period of the times, has brought a saving day, on which it is our
custom to call the strangers into adoption, the poor into a sharing of grace,
those soiled with offenses into a cleansing of sins. And this is that ancient
proclamation, which a little anticipated the appearing of the Savior: "A voice
of one crying =46.417= in the wilderness: Prepare the way of the Lord, make
his paths straight." And if I am neither John nor David, yet let not the
lowliness of the servant harm the power of the Master's law. For it is not
because we reverence the scribes of the laws that we obey the things decreed;
but fearing the authority of the Lawgiver, we bow to the commands. A royal
grace has come, granting relief to two afflicted orders: release to the
prisoners, remission to the debtors. And for this reason I too open the
fitting remedies to both orders; and with much confidence I promise the help
that comes from the care. And lest anyone think the treatment costly, I
declare beforehand the very medicines with which I heal those who are sick.
For to some I guarantee health through water and washing, and of others I wipe
out the disease through a few tears. For the handling is simple, and the gift
God-sent, and truly great the success, to be delivered without burning or
cutting from chronic wounds, which we suffered badly from the bites of the
serpent. Come then, you who have been harmed, to the care of yourselves, and
do not give the matter over to slackness. For a disease grown old and chronic
becomes stronger than the art of healing. You poor and needy, hasten to the
distribution of the royal gifts; you sheep, to the seal and the sign of the
cross, the defender against evils. Give me the names, that I may engrave them
in the perceptible books and write them with ink; but let God mark them on the
incorruptible tablets, having written with his own finger, as once the law for
the Hebrews. "Draw near to him and be enlightened, and your faces shall not be
ashamed. Wash yourselves, take away your sins." These things were written long
ago; yet the power of the letters has not grown old, but is in its prime and
is strong each day. Come out of the prison, I beg you. Hate the dark dwellings
of sin. Flee the devil, as a bitter jailer, who is fed and profits from the
misfortune of sinners. For as God is gladdened by our righteous deeds, so the
cause of sin takes pleasure in the falls. You are outside paradise, O
catechumen, sharing the exile with Adam the forefather. But now, the door
being opened a little for you, enter whence you went out, and do not delay,
lest death, falling in between, block the entrance. The head is already
growing white, the harvest of life is near, perhaps the sickle is being
sharpened against us, and I fear lest, while we sleep and are occupied with
vain vanities, the terrible cutter should suddenly stand over us. But you, the
young man, will say: I have not yet grown old. Do not then be deceived; for
death is not bounded by appointed terms of ages, nor does it fear those in
their prime, as if it had power only against the old. And of this learn the
daily experience as teacher. For you see the bier of the funeral, how =46.420=
unevenly, and as it happens, it leads out every age, today the old man,
tomorrow the blooming and graceful youth, after a little the one whose down is
growing, again the robust and one in his prime, then the old woman, together
with the girl. Seeing these things, let us make the uncertainty secure; for it
is no labor that a wineskin be emptied of breath, the mouth being opened a
little, nor is it hard for a man in an instant to let go the soul. I see
whenever an earthquake happens upon life, or a famine, or a raid of enemies,
all hastening urgently to the baptistery, lest they depart from among men
empty of the grace. What then? Do not the other manifold and unexpected
sufferings have equal power with those evils? Apoplexies, derangements,
unexpected chokings, attacks of spirits, and the things that follow these? Do
we not see those who fall while running, who while eating cease from food
altogether, who while sleeping are led unawares to death, the brother of
sleep? Make secure the unstable and unclear thing of life. Do not traffic in
the grace, lest you fall out of the gift. Owe so much as it is possible also
to have remitted, so much as will not shut out even the philanthropy of the
king. For the rulers of the earth also, whenever they catch someone who has
extravagantly spent the public funds, and has luxuriated beyond satiety in the
royal moneys, despair of the repayment; but they punish bitterly, being
content with the penalties instead of the moneys. You have granted many times
to pleasure; give leisure also to philosophy. Strip off the old man, as a
filthy garment, the shameful thing, that which is full of shame, patched
together from the multitude of sins, stitched round with the pitiful rags of
lawlessness. But receive the garment of incorruption, which Christ, having
unfolded it, holds out to you, and do not thrust away the gift, lest you
insult the one who gave it. For a long time you have rolled in the mire;
hasten, O man, to my Jordan, not John calling, but Christ urging. For the
river of grace flows everywhere, not having its springs in Palestine and being
hidden in the neighboring sea, but circling the whole inhabited world, and
emptying into paradise; flowing opposite the four that flow out from there,
and bringing into paradise things far more precious than those carried out
from there. For those carry spices, tillage, and the sprout of the earth. But
this brings in men, offspring of the Spirit. And wherever you draw it, there
it flows over, it is channeled toward the whole inhabited world, and it does
not spend the stream in the things that are divided. For it has a rich spring,
Christ, and flowing from him it makes the whole world a sea. This one is sweet
and drinkable, dragging along nothing of the unpleasant brine. For it is
sweetened by the coming-upon of the Spirit as the spring of Marah by the
touching of the wood, fordable for the pious, but deep close in and
unapproachable for the profane. Imitate Jesus the son of Nun. Take =46.421= up
the Gospel, as he the ark; leave the desert, sin; cross the Jordan; hasten
into the way of life according to Christ, into the land fertile of gladdening
fruits, that flows, according to the promise, with honey and milk; overthrow
Jericho, the old custom, do not leave its fortress; from the foundations
dissolve the mother of sinful reasonings; make the deceitful Gibeonites
household-servants of Israel, that is, make the wicked reasonings slaves of
the life according to the Gospel; let Achar the sophist and greedy man be
stoned, who stole the golden tongue. And this is that one who made the keeping
of the precious commandment disappear. All those things are a type of ours,
all fore-signs of the things now appearing. Release then from your life the
gluttonous raven; give the dove a time to fly upon you; which Jesus first
typically brought down from heaven, the guileless, the most gentle, the
many-bearing; which, whenever it finds a man purified like wheat well worked,
settles in and, sitting, fires the soul, and bears many and well-favored
children of its own. And these are good deeds and solemn words, faith, piety,
righteousness, temperance, chastity, purity. These are children of the Spirit,
but our possessions. Until when a pupil of the first elements? Open your soul
as a writing-tablet, and allow us to engrave a perfect lesson; do not always
lisp with the children; do not be childish in mind. I blush for you, that
having already grown old you are sent out with the catechumens, as a mindless
and not close-mouthed little child, when a mystery is about to be spoken. Be
joined to the mystic people and learn unspeakable words. Utter with us those
things which even the six-winged Seraphim, hymning with the perfect
Christians, say. Desire food that strengthens the soul; taste a drink that
gladdens the heart; fall in love with a mystery that invisibly changes the old
into youth. Imitate the Ethiopian, the eunuch of boiling desire. For that man
too, when he received Philip, brought near by the Spirit, as a fellow-sitter
upon the chariot, and as a by-work of the road learned not only to read but
also to know the wise Isaiah; and through the interpretation he tasted, as a
good puppy, of the blood and of the slain lamb; he barked vehemently at
Philip, until he led him to the perfect hunt of the prophecy in his hands; and
seeking a makeshift baptism he received it, neither waiting for a lodging, nor
a city or village, nor awaiting the place of a sanctuary, but reasoning
soberly that every place is of the common Master, and every water is fit for
the need of baptism, only if it find faith of the one receiving, and blessing
of the sanctifying priest. Do not provoke the baptizer by delaying, nor anger
him by putting it off day after day, and spending in promises =46.424= the
flowing time, lest ever he strike you with voices like John's, naming you
offspring of a viper, or a deaf asp, growing savage toward the voice of the
charmer, or show you the axe sharpened and gleaming, lying beside the tree,
and threatening the cut. It is more choiceworthy, in a selection of evils,
that someone, having been counted worthy of the saving washing, become again
in sin, than to finish life without a share in the grace. For the offense
perhaps will even be counted worthy of pardon or philanthropy, of which there
is much hope from the good; but the other has salvation altogether forbidden
by a defined sentence; for when I hear the unlying voice saying, "Amen, amen I
say to you: unless one is born from above, he cannot see the kingdom of God,"
I can expect nothing good for the uninitiated. For of what pardon is it truly
just to give a share to such men, who insulted the royal grace, not receiving
the cuttings-off of the debts, nor worshipping the freedom and greeting it,
when it came of its own accord to them from the heavens; and through contempt
of the gift, dishonoring the one who gave it? A soul unenlightened and not
adorned with the grace of regeneration, I do not know even whether angels
receive it after the separation from the body. For how the unsealed one, that
bears nothing marked of the mastership? Or it is likely that it is borne about
in the air, wandering and roaming, and unsought, as having no master; desiring
rest and an abode, and not finding it, wailing without profit, and repenting
without effect; like the rich man, whom purple and fine linen clothed, and
every kind of luxury and delicate enjoyment fed, matter of the unquenchable
fire. And I wish to tell you also a certain account of a wretched man, who
missed the great hope, and sought water in a waterless time. For recently,
when the raid of the nomad Scythians destroyed much country together with
those dwelling in it, the barbarians plundering the suburbs at this
neighboring city of the Comanians, a certain young man of the well-born,
Archias by name, whom I myself also knew accurately, being in pain at both the
private and the common evils of the fatherland, went out of the city and the
fortress, to reconnoiter accurately how many the plundering barbarians were,
and what sufferings were occurring; and falling among the enemies he was shot
down with arrows. And having fallen and being driven toward death, as he had
power, he cried out (for he was of the uninitiated): Mountains and glens,
baptize; trees, and rocks, and springs, give the grace. And with these pitiful
voices he died. Which the city also, having learned them, lamented more than
the misfortune of the war. And like this man are also those suddenly pressed
by diseases. For when he who yesterday was healthy and was mapping out for
himself long times of =46.425= life suffers the thing of the old vain-minded
man, and it is said to him from the despair, that this night your soul is
loosed from the body, then tumult and cry; and all things in a turn of a
moment are sharply sought, the vessels, the water, the priest, the word that
prepares beforehand toward the grace, which the need demands, but the disease
overshadows, working the panting violent and unyielding. And it makes the
tumult not less than the distress in war; and all those throughout the house,
meeting one another, burst out; and there is a shoving, and confusion, and a
wailing and inarticulate noise, as in a night-battle of people entangled with
one another, slaves, kinsmen, friends, the children, the wife. While then it
is still a time of peace, let us arrange the things concerning ourselves
soberly. In leisure and calm of life receive the gift of Christ, when also the
one receiving the high and incomparable benefaction is numbered with pleasure
in the roll of those adopted as sons, and all the acquaintances make his
change toward the better an exceptional joy and gladness. But I fear, he says,
the easiness of our nature toward sin, and for this reason I delay toward the
grace of regeneration. Fair-faced indeed is the pretext of your cowardice, and
you well disguise yourself with the fiction of reverence; but a short question
brought to you will suffice to strip the scene easily beforehand. For since
you who say you fear sin are a catechumen, what unaccused life then do you
live, either the sinless one, or the one in sins? For if the sinless one, why
do you fear baptism, even before it keeping the legislated exactness? But if
your life is unclean and unguarded, delaying in lawlessness, and by the long
custom acquiring a habit of the evil, it is plain that you wait for the last
breath; so that life may be completed together with the baptism taken along at
the last hour. And this is a new and strange merchandise, not of gold and
clothing, but a meddlesome huckstering of a multitude of lawlessnesses of the
cleansing according to the soul. And just as the swine go from mire to mire,
and have the filth as a dwelling for themselves; so those who live with
uncleannesses join the lawlessnesses one after another. For either they are
defiled by fornication, or they contrive the ill-named adultery, or they
practice drunkenness, the kindling of wicked desires, or they pursue the
sister and kindred sins of these. And they fear baptism, as a hindrance of
pleasures, and an abstinence from the defiled enjoyments. Whenever therefore
he who pretends pretenses in sins says, I fear, it is plain that he is not
being cautious of the sin, but wishing the manner of life in the sins to
become long for him. And those who have this wicked judgment in the depth,
first are not even counted worthy of the grace; but with empty hopes beguiling
themselves =46.428= they are led away unexpectedly, death overtaking them
silently and suddenly as a plotting thief. But even if God, multiplying his
own long-suffering and goodness, has counted the sin-lover worthy of the
mysteries on the last day, there is not for them so much profit of the
business as they think. For they think that the kingdom will at once be opened
a little for them; and that they will receive a certain place full of wondrous
good things, and be counted worthy of equal honors with the righteous. But
this is an empty hope, bewitching the soul with a false opinion. For as it
appears to me, the things of men will be divided in three ways upon the
expected life, to go through it more generally for now without sub-divisions.
And the first is the order of the praiseworthy and righteous; second, of those
neither honored nor punished; third, of those paying the penalty for the
things they offended. Where then shall we rank those who received the
benefaction of the washing as a shroud? Or it is plain, among the second, and
this with pardon, that we may assign the inheritance according to God's own
philanthropy. For he who by hesitation betrayed the successes of virtue is not
free of loss and condemnation, if indeed willing-evil is brought under
accusation, and hating the beautiful things has a plain proof of wickedness.
What great thing then will you gain, falling outside the kingdom, and those
promises which an eye has not seen and an ear has not heard? I am not
punished, he says, nor do I fear the threats; but I am content with not
suffering anything terrible, nor being shown as one who has accomplished
anything noble. The choice of a wicked slave; one worthy of the mill and of
bond and of whips, eager only to strip off the punishments; but of a free
man's glory and of the honors from splendor, not even in a small way laying
claim. This voice is an enemy of all the saints, contrary to the holy ones
from the age. For all those loved the dangers for boldness of speech, and they
offered the body to those who wished to cut and to kill in various ways, and
they were not made soft toward any contest of those in labor, expecting as the
prize of the blood and of the brave deeds the honor in the kingdom of the
heavens. For the sake of those things Abraham, having been commanded,
sacrificed the son; and Moses toiled in the terrible things of the desert; and
Elijah lived the desert and untended life; and all the prophets went about in
sheepskins, in goatskins, being afflicted, being maltreated. For the sake of
that honor evangelists suffered the marks for the Gospel, and martyrs
contended in the punishments from the tyrants, and everyone, if anyone is
truly rational and an image of God, and having kinship toward the high and
heavenly things, does not even wish to be nor to be raised together with the
men who will come back to life; unless he is going to be praised by God as a
good slave, and to be counted worthy of certain prizes. Upon this choice David
also makes the thirst of the deer a familiar example of his own desire toward
God; and he prays to see the face of God, that he may be gladdened in the
=46.429= intelligible welcomings. And Paul prays, having stripped off the
flesh as even a heavy and burdensome garment, to depart and to be with Christ;
both altogether imagining the blessed and unchangeable gladness. And if there
is no love of that, all the other things truly, according to the Ecclesiast,
are vanity of vanities. Flee then, Christian men, partakers of a heavenly
calling, every such thought worthy of robbers and evildoers, and do not
consider even this a thing to be counted blessed, the fleeing of punishment,
but fall in love with gifts and crowns, which God has made ready for the
athletes of righteousness; desire baptism without guile; take the talent, and
add the working; for thus according to the parable you will become governors
of ten cities. But he who has been buried together with the baptism has hidden
the talent in the earth, and will altogether hear those things said to the
idle and slack slave. The newly-enlightened, if he join the commandment to the
faith, has nothing more than the unaccused. He was a prisoner liable to
countless charges, fearing the court, trembling at the time of the accounts.
Suddenly a king's philanthropy came, opened the prison, released the wicked.
Let the one who gave the grace be hymned and worshipped, as having saved by
abundance of goodness those who were not hoping for life; but let the other
know himself, let him live in humility; let him not be disposed as one who has
accomplished something, because he was loosed from the bonds. For remission of
charges bears witness of the mercy to the one who granted it, but not that the
one released will be well thought of. He who has received the washing of
regeneration is like a young soldier enrolled just now in the hoplite rolls,
but not yet having displayed anything soldierly or noble. As therefore that
man, having taken up the belt, and having pinned on the cloak, does not at
once think himself a champion, nor coming forward with boldness talks with the
king as an acquaintance, nor asks the favors of those who have toiled and
labored; so neither you, having obtained the grace, think that you will live
with the righteous, and be led to their lot, unless you endure many dangers
for piety, and war with the flesh, and with it with the devil, and nobly hold
out against all the arrows of the wicked spirits. But rather, if it seems
good, let us take the very voice of the Lord that is going to be brought out
upon the common judgment as teacher of the matter at hand. For what does he
say? "Come, you blessed of my Father, inherit the kingdom prepared for you."
Why? Not because you put on the garment of incorruption, nor because you
washed off your sins, but because you accomplished love. And immediately the
roll is subjoined of those fed, of those given drink, of those clothed. And
reasonably the unerring judge judges thus. For the grace is a gift of the
Master; but the way of life is an accomplishment of the one honored. And of
grace =46.432= which he received no one demands a wage, but the contrary, he
owes. And for this reason, when we are enlightened by baptism, we owe goodwill
to the benefactor. And our goodwill toward God is the kindliness concerning
our fellow-slaves, the salvation of ourselves, and the care of virtue. Put
away then the vain judgment, you who keep baptism as a shroud, knowing that
faith seeks its own sister, the good way of life; of which may we be counted
worthy by a good choice and by the help of God, to whom worship is due now and
unto the ages. Amen.
