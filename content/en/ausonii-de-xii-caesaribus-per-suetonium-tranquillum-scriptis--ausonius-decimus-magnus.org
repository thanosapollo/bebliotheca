**
Monostichs

** AUSONIUS TO HIS SON HESPERIUS SENDS GREETING
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1-h1
:END:

Receive the Caesarean chiefs, into whose reigns from the paired
consuls Roman power long since passed,
the twice-six; their own monostichs mark each,
whose names, deeds, life and death Suetonius once
carried through a full senem.

** MONOSTICHS ON THE ORDER OF THE EMPERORS
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1-h2
:END:

Julius Caesar first opened the royal hall
and transferred both the name and the citadel to Augustus;
after him his stepson Nero Claudius reigns, from whom
the Caesar to whom the camps gave the surname of the boot.
Claudius from here takes possession of the realm, after whom savage
Nero,

last of the sons of Aeneas; after him three, and not in three years:

old Galba, trusting in vain an idle ally;
soft Otho, degenerate through luxury in an infamous age,
and Vitellius worthy neither of the realm nor of death as a man.
Tenth after these and summoned by fate is Vespasian
and Titus happy in the shortness of empire; there followed
the brother whom his own Rome called the bald Nero.

** MONOSTICHS ON THE LENGTH OF THEIR EMPIRE
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1-h3
:END:

Julius, as they tell, the divine, reigns for a triennium.
Augustus after ten lustra prolongs six years,
and Nero Claudius adds twins to thrice seven,
a third winter ends the raging times of Gaius.
Claudius draws a double seven and dread Ne?-o
as much, but of the total one consul was wanting.
Old Galba, wanton Otho, infamous Vitellius,
a third summer did not know you reigning in Latium,

[omitted]

Vespasian fills a fated decade for himself.
While Titus rules thrice, a new laurel girds Janus:
fifteen times, while the brother holds the savage reins.

** MONOSTICHS ON THE DEATH OF EACH
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1-h4
:END:

Julius Caesar perished with the senate raging,
ripe old age added Augustus to the di vis,
old Nero, an exile on Capri, completed a late fate,
soft Chaerea exacted penalties from the Caesar.

Claudius closed his fate with doubtful poison.
Matricide Nero bore the force of his own sword.
Old Galba perished, laid low by savage Otho;
soon infamous Otho, but possessed of a famous death,
the prodigal scepters of Vitellius who must be slain succeed.
Praised empire, a gentle death for Vespasian.
But Titus, love of the world, is snatched in flourishing years;
late but just atonements destroy the heavy brother.

:PROPERTIES:

Now both those already told and those following by lot of reign
I will set forth, whom the series of empire holds;
I will begin from the divine and run through all in order,
whom I, mindful of Roman history, know.

** JULIUS CAESAR
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h3-h1
:END:

The empire, which had once been solemn for paired
consuls, Julius Caesar obtained.
But the right of reign was brief, borne for a single triennium:
the savage faction of the armed toga struck him down.

* Section 2
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h4
:END:

** OCTAVIUS AUGUSTUS
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h4-h1
:END:

Avenger and successor next, Octavius, the same
Caesar and more noble by the name of Augustus.

Long-lived power never violated by doubts
showed that he, placed on earth, was a god.

* Section 3
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h5
:END:

** TIBERIUS NERO
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h5-h1
:END:

Nero, having got the forename Tiberius, passed the first
times of youth in a praised empire,
thereafter shut in vain in the lone cave of Capri,
which he betrays by vices, he believes hidden by places.

* Section 4
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h6
:END:

** CAESAR CALIGULA
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h6-h1
:END:

After him the Caesar with the camp surname of the boot
succeeded, fiercer than a fierce nature,
thereafter stained with slaughters and incests, and who
in every crime surpassed his polluted grandfather.

* Section 5
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h7
:END:

** CLAUDIUS CAESAR
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h7-h1
:END:

Claudius, of a mocked life in private time,
in the reign showed a sample of his talent;
yet having suffered a freedwoman of wives and crimes
he was guilty not by doing, but by enduring.

* Section 6
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h8
:END:

** NERO
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h8-h1
:END:

He who was sixth and last heir of the race of Aeneas,
Nero polluted and closed the Julian rites.
As many names as piety has, so many crimes his life has also.
Learn from Tranquillus: but it irks to remember.

* Section 7
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h9
:END:

** GALBA
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h9-h1
:END:

Old man cheated of hope, as a private citizen seeming
to deserve the scepters, and shown inferior in empire,

your fame was better as a youth; but the juster order is
to have pleased thereafter, to have displeased first.

* Section 8
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h10
:END:

** OTHO
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h10-h1
:END:

About to bear scepters rival to polluted Nero,
Otho is overwhelmed, snatched by a swift end;
yet he will be praiseworthy in his close, who by a seemly death
did this alone as noble, that he perished.

* Section 9
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h11
:END:

** VITELLIUS
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h11-h1
:END:

A fierce life, a foul death for you, nor worthy, Vitellius,
to become Caesar: so the fates please themselves.
Yet a brief shadow of empire; because the prizes of reign
the unworthy often approaches, only the worthy holds.

* Section 10
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h12
:END:

** VESPASIAN
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h12-h1
:END:

Attentive in seeking, convenient in moderate use,

Vespasian increases and does not check his wealth,
who once, as princeps, transferred a doubtful fame in private time,
a thing rare for others, into better.

* Section 11
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h13
:END:

** TITUS
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h13-h1
:END:

Happy in empire, happy in shortness of ruling,
free of civil blood, love of the world.

Dying you said you had one crime;

but we believed none about you, nor you.

* Section 12
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h14
:END:

** DOMITIAN
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h14-h1
:END:

Thus far, Flavian house, you had brought forth just lords.
Why did the third snatch what the two had given?
Scarcely is it worth so much to have had them, because the gifts of
the good are brief; those things that harmed, hurt forever.

* Section 13
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h15
:END:

** NERVA (ON THE CAESARS AFTER TRANQUILLUS TE TETRASTICHS)
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h15-h1
:END:

Next after the tyrant is extinguished, old Nerva
governs the scepters, a princeps in name, a parent in mind.
The man has no offspring; adoption imitates a child,
whom it delights to have chosen, whom he would wish to have begotten.

* Section 14
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h16
:END:

** TRAJAN
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h16-h1
:END:

Trajan enters the rule in a green age,
first in praise of war, having the rest of his father.
He too, lacking offspring, joins to himself by lot of choosing
one whom you would confess good, deny as equal.

* Section 15
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h17

** HADRIAN
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h17-h1
:END:

Aelius next came on, distinguished in the middle of his acts:
a graver fame marks the beginnings and the end,
childless he too: he who will be joined to him will give proofs

how much the adopted stand out above the begotten.

* Section 16
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h18

** ANTONINUS PIUS
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h18-h1
:END:

Antoninus from here takes the rule, that one by calling
and the consuls Pius, having a name of merit,
no son to him by fate; but by the law of his own
he took from the fatherland one who should rule the fatherland.

* Section 17
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h19
:END:

** M. ANTONINUS
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h19-h1
:END:

Thereafter the guardianship is given to Marcus, who bent
the decrees of Plato to empire, better than father Pius.
Dying with his own successor, but a crooked princeps,
he harmed the fatherland in this alone, that he begot him.

* Section 18
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h20
:END:

** COMMODUS
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h20-h1
:END:

Commodus follows, stained with the fights of the arena,
a princeps waging wars with a Thracian gladiator's sword,
at last paying penalties with a crushed throat,
by his crimes confessing his mother's adultery.

* Section 19
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h21
:END:

** HELVIUS PERTINAX
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h21-h1
:END:

Helvius, chosen by the judgment and decree of the senate,
a princeps brought forth by decrees, not by partisanship.
Which the ill-trusted cohort grieved, the error having been proved,
that the curia had yielded the empire to the camps.

* Section 20
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h22
:END:

** DIDIUS JULIANUS
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h22-h1
:END:

The gods well, that Didius does not rejoice in the rich
scepter, and the prizes are quickly taken from the perjured old man.
And you, father Severus, do not shudder at the title of a renewer:
your force does not snatch the empire, but takes it back.

* Section 21
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h23
:END:

** SEVERUS PERTINAX
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h23-h1
:END:

Unwearied Severus moves arms from the chill Hister,
that he may take the realms from parricide Didius.
Punic origin was his; but one who by virtue would prove
that place does not stand in the way, when talent is strong.

* Section 22
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h24
:END:

** BASSIANUS ANTONINUS OR CARACALLA
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h24-h1
:END:

Unlike the father in virtue and much more unlike him
whose adoptive name you claim,
guilty by a brother's death, punished by a bloody end,
you are more Caracalla by the mockery of the people.

:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h25

** OPILIUS MACIUNUS
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h25-h1
:END:

Then the princeps's guard turns the steel taken for Caesar
against the author by slaughter, sluggish Macrinus.
Soon he falls with his offspring. Let him cease to beat
perfidy with heavy complaints: what he suffers, he deserved.
