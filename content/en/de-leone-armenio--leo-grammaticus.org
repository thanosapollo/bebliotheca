COMPOSITION OF A CHRONICLE CONTAINING THE AFFAIRS CONCERNING LEO SON OF BARDAS
THE ARMENIAN.

And thereafter Michael the emperor, in the first year of his reign, crowned
his son Theophylaktos at the feasts, his wife Prokopia also coming forth with
much glory and standing before the altar and seeing the crowning of her son
and making great joy and distributing much money. For she was the one
disposing all the affairs of the empire; for the husband was gentle, as has
been said. And they also did many alms, scattering the money which Nikephoros
had piled up with care, sending what was needed to the churches, to the
monasteries, and to the ascetics in the deserts, and to the poorhouses 336 and
to the xenones and to the captives and to the widows and to the orphans and to
those who had fallen from their substance, and having made all glad they were
glorified by all. And the factions composed poems in each mode toward their
praise, furnishing every pleasure of flattery. And thereafter he recalled all
those toward whom Nikephoros had been hostile, and he cast out of the palace
the brothers of Theodote who were patricians, and Leo surnamed of Skleros, and
he made him strategos in the Peloponnese.

And Leo son of Bardas the Armenian, who had offended Nikephoros, who had been
beaten and tonsured by him and banished, these men recalled, and they made him
patrician and strategos of the Anatolics; who also tyrannized over them, being
wicked, as we shall say later. So then when these things had come to pass, the
Bulgars, lifted up at the victory against Nikephoros, wished to go out to war.
Michael, learning this, taking counsel, gathered all the themes, having levied
also many others in place of those left behind in the war, and assembling
those who guarded the kleisourai of Syria, Lykaonians and Cilicians and
Isaurians and Cappadocians and Galatians, and taking all he went out in the
second year of his reign, of the indiction . and of the 337 city accompanying

there she herself returned again into the city. And thereafter setting out
from there both the emperor and all the host drew near as far as the
kleisourai. And the Bulgars drew up opposite them, and seeing the multitude of
the host they did not dare to attempt it, but they stood drawn up. And
likewise also the host of the emperor stood themselves drawn up also for
fifteen days, not daring to lay hand upon one another. And thereafter they
were worn out standing in the heat of summer day and night, both the men and
the beasts, and they came into great weakness, expecting that the Bulgars were
going to lay hand, and again the Bulgars likewise; and one might see them in
much helplessness. And Aplakes was upon the one part of the height, having the
Macedonians and the Thrakesians, and he makes known to the emperor: "How long
do we stand and perish? I first lay hand in the name of God, and you
yourselves come on with eagerness. And we shall have the victory; for we are

Bulgars were poured upon him, and those of Thrace began to cut them down. And

And the Bulgars, having pursued as far as a little place, thereafter came with

gathering the arms, as has been said, and sitting down against the kastra
where the pursued had taken refuge. And the emperor entering with grief and
weeping went to the patriarch, and there entered with him both the magistros
and the great domestikos and the rest of the patricians, and the emperor

counsel what they should do, and waiting that all the themes should be piled
together and that the emperor should give a command, the theme of the
Anatolics, having first fled from the war, made a wicked counsel, having as
strategos the son of Bardas the Armenian, and mutinying they acclaimed this
same Leo as emperor, and no one opposing, opening the gates they brought him
in with the whole multitude into the city. And Michael the emperor, hearing
these things, took refuge in the church, and receiving a pledge he was
tonsured, becoming a monk.
