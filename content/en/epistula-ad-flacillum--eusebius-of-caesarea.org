2 To the most honored and beloved fellow-minister Flacillus, Eusebius greeting
in the Lord
If toward the garrulous and many-worded writing of Marcellus the things from
us in brief have sufficed, one must not wonder; for there was no need of many
words toward the overturning of things condemned from the very spot even by
chance people. Therefore in the first two, using only the setting-beside of
the man's sayings without any contradiction, I made the proof against them
manifest from themselves. And now besides that detection I have also brought
the overturning of the same through three that are at hand. For he, having
composed one long and much-wandering treatise, says he has done this because
he knows one God, lest somehow, giving room of subsistence to the Son of God,
he divide the writing. And we, honoring the all-holy and thrice-blessed
Trinity, have taken the whole hypothesis in so many, having spared
much-speaking, and in epitomes and brief things having presented the true
theology. If therefore the things written are sufficient, you yourself would
judge as you meet them; and if something is lacking, you would also fill these
up, clearly as a brother the shortcomings of a brother, if anywhere it should
also need therapy, healing through correction. I pray that you be well and
remember me to the Lord through all.
