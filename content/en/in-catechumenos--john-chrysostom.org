On the Catechumens.
=60.739=
In the stadiums of those outside, many endure the toil of the race,
but one carries off the crown of victory; and the labors of the contest and
the sweats belong to many, but the prize of joy belongs to one. But in this
spiritual place of the church there is one race of piety from all, and the
crowns are equal in number to those who run. And this is nothing strange: for
a rich president of the games, who lacks nothing, stands in the midst of those
competing, Christ, receiving the eagerness of each as a race, and preparing
the honors equally. For he does not prefer the ruler and overlook the one who
is poor; he does not receive the glorious and send away the weak; he does not
crown grey hair and despise youth; he does not smile on the righteous and look
askance at the sinner who runs toward him; but he spreads equally for all the
net of compassions, and he draws all alike with the net of piety.
For he has set forth a common invitation for all; and as we have just now
heard, he cries aloud a decree of love for mankind: "Come to me, all who labor
and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest." Come, all who are sailing
across this salt sea of life; entrust to me the tillers of salvation; I have a
skill that contends against the devil's reefs; I procure for you a trade of
salvation unshaken by the waves. Come, all; a great storm of sin is upon you;
I have a harbor of the font that saves men and drowns the devil. O calling
that strips sin bare! O voice that seizes all the substance of the demon! O
medicine that goes to each of those who are sick! O gift that flows unhindered
to those who will! O fountain that hastens to give drink to all who thirst for
life! For if anyone, he says, thirsts, let him come to me and drink; if anyone
thirsts for the streams of salvation, if anyone desires the living water, if
anyone holds converse with the true knowledge, if anyone thirsts the thirst of
piety. For Judas also thirsted the thirst of betrayal; for Herod also thirsted
for the destruction of the infants; the Synagogue thirsted for the murders of
the prophets; Jezebel thirsted for the vineyard of Naboth; Delilah thirsted
for the weakness of Samson; Herodias thirsted for the blood of the Forerunner;
the Jews thirsted for the Savior; but they thirsted a thirst that procures
destruction, they thirsted a thirst that produces fever, they thirsted a
thirst that is the cause of punishment. But if anyone thirsts, as Zacchaeus
thirsted for the sight of me, as the woman with the issue of blood thirsted
for healing, as the harlot thirsted to have her sins washed, as Matthew
thirsted for the dignity of the evangelist, as the thief thirsted for
paradise, as Thomas thirsted for the life-giving handling of the wounds, as
the eunuch of Ethiopia thirsted for baptism, for which, being set on fire, he
cried out to the Apostle: "See, here is water; what hinders me from being
baptized?" Hear, catechumen: a fountain of life not only calls, but even
entreats; for "Come," he says, "all who labor and are heavy laden, and I will
give you rest." I have prepared myself for the whole benefaction of my race; I
wish to let loose a fountain with a rain of gifts. Come, all; even if myriads
are sick, let them approach.
Let no one think that I, the physician who heals the inhabited world, grow
weary; I do not grow tired in healing, since I did not shrink from creating;
the world, dividing my goods, does not convict my abundance; I do not become
poor by giving, I rejoice when I am plundered, I take delight when I am
carried off; I am not diminished when I am emptied, and I am glad when I am
stolen from; and the woman with the issue of blood is a witness, who stole,
and for this was crowned in soul and body together. "Come, all who labor." Did
you see, lover of Christ, how great is the Creator's munificence toward the
race? Why then are you negligent, O catechumen? Why do you delay? Approach the
Master who is calling you. Do you not hear the wise man exhorting and saying:
"Do not delay to turn to the Lord, and do not put it off from day to day; for
suddenly the wrath of the Lord will come"? And he cuts the time short, having
seen that the nature of men is easily and in a brief space turned. For many,
giving themselves to smaller delays, thought they had stored up wisely the
hope from both sides, expecting neither to be without a share of the things
hoped for, and counting it gain for the present to make full use of life, who
were snatched away before the enjoyment of the necessary things, given over to
the ever =60.740= lasting torments and punishments; and as they thought to
steal a short time, they did not perceive that they were being robbed of the
whole of salvation, suddenly snatched away even before old age. For this
reason the Master Christ named death a thief of man; "For as a thief," he
says, "in the night, so it comes"; that we, being troubled at the uncertainty
of the plot, may not hope for an appointed term of longer time. For we do not
have authority over the postponement of time, we are not masters of living as
long a season as we wish. For this reason we were not entrusted with the
knowledge of our own departure, that we might lessen the excess of sins, being
trained to be temperate by the fear of death.
Let us number those who each day are sent forth to the tombs; let us observe
how among the aged a great multitude of the young has been mixed. Why then, O
catechumen, do you overlook the safety of your own soul, and having dismissed
the fear of death, do you promise me time as a strong champion? How many men,
delighting in the pleasures of life, and boasting in the vigor of the flesh as
in an unchangeable dominion, like dust before a whirlwind, were suddenly
snatched away to the grave! Therefore, since we are not able to mark out for
ourselves the time of life, let us not put off the fear of the end. How many
who were careless, and expected a long life, did a fire suddenly reduce to
ashes? How many were buried by an earthquake? How many sailing did the sea
cover? How many did a sudden chasm of the earth receive? How many traveling
were suddenly swept away by rivers in flood? How many, falling among beasts
and robbers, perished? How many did the sword of wars reap? How many did
sudden onsets of diseases consume? How many, separated from their own
fatherland, received the signal of death, and were not even counted worthy of
a common burial? Do you not hear the Master continually crying out, and making
known the nearness of the end: "The axe is laid to the root of the trees"?
What is the axe? Death. What is the root? The life of man. Who is the one who
cuts? The sentence of the Master, which in cutting cries: "You are earth, and
to earth you shall go." Therefore, standing away from this, let us approach
Christ who calls; let us run, being transferred from darkness to light; let us
run to the Lord who gives rest, having subjected the flesh to the spirit; let
us run, let us hasten more swiftly than death, let us anticipate the angels
who come for our presentation. The present life is as a word, and the span of
life runs past as a shadow.
Time is spent, the manner of life is not improved; the deeds run ahead, the
Judge waits, the command presses, the angels present us, the conscience
accuses, and what shall we do when we are called to judgment? Therefore let us
love a good way of life, let us hate the habit of sinning, let us take thought
for the defense that is to come, let us acquire purity of life; for the pure
in heart, they shall see God. Let us love the Master's crowns; with much
eagerness let us run the stadium of life, having as fellow-worker toward the
contest the president of the games, Christ. For "to those who love God," he
says, "all things work together for good."
And hear what is more wonderful: In the time of the contest the Master
contends together with those who wrestle, and in the hour of victory he sits
as president of the games; he anoints for the wrestling, and after a little he
himself sets on the crowns; he wrestles with them, he grants power to the one
who is weary, he furnishes the means of victory, and after a little he pays
the wages as to a victor. From where is this clear? Hear David crying out: "I
am with him in affliction; I will deliver him and I will glorify him." O
unspeakable love for mankind! O goodness beyond every word! For see what the
Master says: "In the time," he says, "of affliction I am with him; I share the
necessity, I snatch him out of the danger. All these things I do as I contend
together. Then afterward as president of the games and Master I set forth the
munificence, saying: I will glorify him, I will fill him with length of days."
This the Master is accustomed to do also toward the one who is generous; he
hol =60.741= lows the hand for the poor man who receives the good deed. When
you hear of the hand of Christ, understand the palm that bears the ends of the
inhabited world. He stretches out the right hand then in an unspeakable way,
he receives the obols secretly, he marks the face of the giver, he takes the
benefaction on earth, and in heaven he prepares the recompenses. And how, and
when does he make the repayment? "When the Son of man comes," he says, "in the
glory of his Father, and all the holy angels with him, then he will sit upon
the throne of his glory; and all the nations will be gathered before him, and
he will separate them from one another, as the shepherd separates the sheep
from the goats; and he sets the sheep on his right, and the goats on the left;
and he will say to those on his right: Come, you blessed of my Father"; for
you have found the Judge a debtor; do not fear the throne, but run toward the
one who sits as toward one who has been benefited; I bring forward the written
bond which I set forth to you; I do not hide the benefaction, lest you be
deprived of the love for mankind; I do not deny the gift; for I hasten toward
the repayment. Come, you: you displayed the seed of compassions, I have
prepared beforehand for you the harvest of joy. Tell, Master, the cause. "I
was hungry, and you gave me to eat." Let no one then send away the poor man as
destitute; for he overlooks the hungry Judge of the coming =60.742= ages. Let
us then acquire as a debtor the one who is going to judge there. For "he who
has mercy on a poor man," he says, "lends to God." Let us be eager to become
worthy of the portion on the right; let us hate the abominable kinship of the
goats, let us pursue the fellowship of the lambs; for we have authority for
the choice of the better.
For this reason the Judge has proclaimed beforehand to us that fearful
tribunal of the Master, and the severity of the sentence, that by learning the
threat we may turn aside from suffering evil. Let us not run past the command
of Paul that says: "Godliness is great gain." It is not subject to an audit
(and by "in due time" he means the severity of the Judge); it does not fear an
accuser's cry at the throne; it does not await condemnation, but it waits for
consolation; for it is an enjoyment of goods that lacks nothing, wealth that
cannot be taken away, honor without a successor, gain that is not locked in a
treasury, a help that goes before, a grace that travels with us, a healing of
the heart, a gladness of the soul, a boast of conscience, a defense easy of
access, a starting-point of boldness. Godliness is profitable for all things,
having promise of the life that now is and of that which is to come; of which
may we also be counted worthy to obtain, by the grace and love for mankind of
our Lord Jesus Christ, to whom be the glory and the might, now and always, and
unto the ages of ages. Amen.
