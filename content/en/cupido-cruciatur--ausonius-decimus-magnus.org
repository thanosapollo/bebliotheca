Have you ever seen a picture 1 painted on a wall? You have seen it
surely and you remember. For at Trier in the dining-room of Zoilus
this painting is colored: women lovers nail Cupid to a cross, not
those of our age, who sin of their own will, but those heroic ones,
who pardon themselves and punish the god. Of whom our Maro enumerates
a part in the mourning fields. This image I have marveled at for its
appearance and its argument. Then I transferred the stupor of
marveling to the ineptitude of making poetry. Nothing pleases me
besides the lemma; but I commend my error to you: we love our moles
and scars, and not content to have sinned by our own vice alone, we
strive that they be loved. But why do I earnestly advocate for this
eclogue? I am sure that whatever you know as mine, you will love;
which I hope more than that you praise it, farewell and love your
parent.

** Cupid Crucified
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h2-h1
:END:

In the fields of air, which the muse of Maro recounts, 1
where a myrtle grove shades the love-maddened lovers, 2
the heroines were leading their rites and each her own,
as they had once fallen, they were bearing the tokens of death,
wandering in a great wood and under a niggard light 3
among both reedy tresses and the pregnant poppy
and silent lakes without a stain, streams without a murmur:
along whose banks in misty light droop
the wept flowers, once names of kings and of boys, 4
Narcissus the admirer and Oebalian Hyacinthus
and golden-haired Crocus and Adonis painted with murex
and Salaminian Aeas written with tragic groan;
all which anxious with tears and sad loves
work remembering griefs, death already having been met:
they call the heroines back again into the lost age.
Semele the deceived child-bearer bewails the thunderbolt
birth and tearing the burnt cradles through emptiness
fans the idle fire of a feigned thunderbolt,
complaining of vain gifts, having rejoiced in the male sex,
Caenis mourns, called back into her ancient form.
Procris still dries her wounds and loves the skilled
hand of Cephalus even though struck, she carries the smoky of a potsherd
lights, the Sestian girl headlong from the tower,
and from cloudy Leucate she threatens a leap

manly Sappho about to perish by Lesbian arrows. 1

sad Eriphyle refuses the ornaments of Harmonia,
unhappy in her son nor fortunate in her husband,
the whole Minoan tale of airy Crete also
quivers in the likeness of pictures under a thin image:
Pasiphae follows the tracks of the snow-white bull,
deserted Ariadne carries gathered threads in her hand,
despairing Phaedra looks back at the thrown-away tablets.
this one bears a noose, this one empty images of a crown:
this one is ashamed to have gone under the hiding-places of the Daedalian heifer.
Laudamia complains of two nights snatched through empty joys,
of a living and a dead husband,
in another part fierce with drawn points all
both Thisbe and Canace and Sidonian Elissa shudder:
this one bears the sword of a husband, this of a father and this of a guest,
and she herself wanders, such as once through Latmian rocks
wont to seek Endymion's slumbers,
two-horned Luna with torch and star-bearing diadem.
a hundred others revisiting the wounds of old loves
rekindle torments with sweet and sad complaints.
Amid the middle of whom the shade of dark gloom
rash Love scattered with hissing wings.
all recognized the boy and by a remembering return
felt him the common accused, although the moist clouds around
and the belts gleaming with gilded bosses

and the quiver and the fire of the ruddy torch were darkening him.
they recognize him nevertheless and begin to brandish empty vigor
and the one enemy who has found places not his own,
when he was driving sluggish flights under dense night,
they press him, a cloud having been made: trembling and preparing empty
refuges they dragged him into the gathering of the middle troop.
a myrtle most known in the sad grove is chosen,
invidious by the punishments of the gods, there had tortured
once-spurned Proserpina Adonis remembering of Venus.
Love hung on the high stem of this
hands bound behind his back and bonds drawn tight at the soles
grieving they afflict with no moderation of punishment.
he is accused without a charge, with no judge
Love is accused. each is eager to absolve herself,
that she may transfer her own faults onto another's crimes.
all reproaching bring forth the tokens of the death endured:
these they think arms, this sweet vengeance,
that each may strive to punish the pain by that by which she perishes,
this one holds a noose, this one the empty appearance of a point
thrusts, that one hollow rivers and a crashing rock
and the fear of a raging sea and a sea without waves.
some shake flames and trembling threaten
hissing torches with no fire, Myrrha tears open her grown
womb with shining tears and at the fearing one
hurls gemmy ambers of the tear-bearing trunk,
certain ones under the appearance of those pardoning only mockeries
alone want, that a thin stylus under the point of a prick
may draw forth the tender blood from which the rose was born
or that they may bring the wanton lights of a lamp to the groin.

the nourishing mother herself also liable to a like fault
Venus enters so great tumults without care.
and not hastening votes for her circumvented son
she doubles the terror and with bitter goads kindles
the two-edged furies and herself transfers onto the crimes of her son
her own disgrace, that she bore the unseen bonds of her husband
when Mars was caught, that for a shameful groin
the form of Hellespontine Priapus is laughed at,
that cruel Eryx, that the half-man Hermaphroditns.
nor enough in words: golden Venus with a rosy garland
beats the grieving boy even fearing heavier things,
from him the sewn rose pressed purple dew
from the bruised body by frequent blow, which already
dyed before drew a more ruddy more fiery dye.
then the fierce threats fell and vengeance greater
than its own crime seemed, about to make Venus guilty,
the heroines themselves intercede and each her own
deaths they prefer to ascribe to cruel fate.
then the pious mother gives thanks that the grieving have yielded
and to dismiss the pardoned faults from the boy.
Such images once with nocturnal figures
work a trembling rest with empty terror.
which after Cupid having suffered through much night
has fled, the gloom of sleep at last driven off
he flies out to those above and escapes by the ivory gate.
