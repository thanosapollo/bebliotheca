* On the Second Coming of the Lord
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

On the second coming of our Lord Jesus Christ, and on the [text], "We shall
all stand before the judgment seat of Christ, and each one will give an
account of himself to God." =59.619= αʹ. I thought I had said some great and
extraordinary things to you on the former day; for they were indeed great by
nature, and exceeding all speech; but nevertheless the things said today have
as great an excess toward those, as those have toward our own. And yet I did
not think they had an excess; but nevertheless, when the things read today
came on, they appeared far more splendid than those. And what are the things
read today? That We shall all stand before the judgment seat of Christ; for it
is written, As I live, says the Lord, that to me every knee shall bow, and
every tongue shall confess to God. So then each of us shall give an account of
himself =59.620=.40 to God. See, he did not say simply, Each shall worship,
but also Each shall confess, that is, shall give account of the things that
have been done. Be then, O man, always in the contest, seeing the common
Master seated upon the judgment seat, and do not split and divide the Church,
tearing yourself away from the grace; for Christ will require an account both
of you and of all human nature. And lest he should seem to say these things as
if deliberately frightening us, from the sequence set before us he again holds
to the =59.621= same subject, saying: Let us therefore no longer judge one
another, but rather judge this, not to put a stumbling-block or a scandal for
his brother. And this is not more that one's than this one's. Therefore it can
fit both, both when the perfect man is scandalized by the observation about
foods, and when the imperfect stumbles at the harsh rebuke. But consider for
me, beloved, how great a punishment we who scandalize simply shall give. For
if, where the thing being done was unlawful, he forbade this to happen because
of untimely rebuking, so that the brother might not be scandalized and
stumble, when we scandalize someone without even correcting, of what are we
worthy? For if not to save is an accusation (and the one who buried the talent
shows it), what does scandalizing not work? What then, he says, if he is
scandalized from himself, being weak? For this very reason then it would be
just to endure all things. For if he were strong, he would not even need so
much care; but now, since he is more imperfect, for this reason he needs much
earnestness. Let us therefore furnish this to him, and from every side bear
him up. For we shall not give an account of our own evils only, but also for
those we scandalize others. And if those accountings are grievous even by
themselves, when the scandals also are added, when shall we be saved? For let
us not think, if we find partners of the sins, that we shall have a defense;
this will even become an addition of punishment for us; since the serpent also
was punished more than the woman; just as the woman also more than the man,
and Jezebel too gave a harsher penalty than Ahab who seized the vineyard. And
you therefore, when you become a cause of destruction to others, will suffer
more grievous things than those tripped up by you. For it is not so much the
sinning that destroys, as also leading others into this; therefore he says,
They not only do it, but also take pleasure with those who practice it. So
that when we see some sinning, let us not only not push them, but also draw
them up from the pit of wickedness, lest we ourselves undergo the penalty of
others' destruction. But let us return to the subject set before us. For We
shall all, he says, stand before the judgment seat of Christ, and we shall
give an account to


God. What then shall we do who in sins willingly betray ourselves? what
comfort shall we have? what consolation? how shall we propitiate the fearful
judge? with how much shame shall we be filled, seeing our impieties before our
eyes? Nevertheless if we are willing now to be roused toward the things
pleasing to God, we shall be able both to wash off all the things we have
sinned, and to escape those fearful tribunals. And this will be, if we
remember continually our body's departure from here; For remember, he says,
your last things, and you will never sin; then let us reckon up the fearful
judgment seat of Christ, at which all things are naked and laid open to his
eyes, those in word, those in deed, those in thought; then the river of fire,
the unbreakable bond, the lightless darkness, the gnashing of teeth, the
venomous worm, that day, painful to sinners, but bright and sweet to those who
have accomplished works of righteousness. βʹ. But there are some of men so
filled with folly as to hold the God-inspired Scriptures false, and
allegorizing the justice of God. For I have heard some who love sin, and are
held by much love of pleasure, saying such things, that, they say, God
threatened these things toward the fear of men; for far be it that, being
merciful, he should punish anyone, and especially those who have come to know
him. Hear then, you men who have destroyed your hearts, who are persuaded by
the suggestion of the hater of the good, who rejoices in your destruction. For
in order =59.622= that, having been frightened, we may not change ourselves to
a good life, and flee the tortures of punishment, for this reason he has led
us into this fearlessness; so that, having been negligent here, we may throw
ourselves into the very pit of Hades. Tell me then, you who make God a liar,
you who reckon the holy Scriptures bare words only, and not truth, what do you
think, O foolish ones? is not that rich man punished, who overlooked Lazarus?
are not the foolish virgins cast out of the bridechamber? do not those who did
not feed Christ go away into the fire prepared for the devil and his angels?
is not the one clothed in filthy garments, hands and feet bound, cast out into
the fire? is not the one who demanded the hundred denarii delivered to the
tormentors? is not what was said about the adulterers true, that Their worm
shall not die, and their fire shall not be quenched? or are these threats
only? Yes, he says. And from where, tell me, you wretched ones, do you dare to
pronounce such a thing, and this bringing the unjust vote from yourselves? But
I, both from what Christ has said and from what he has done, will try to prove
the contrary. For if we do not believe concerning the punishments to come, let
us at least believe from those that have already happened; for surely the
things that have happened and come forth into deed are not threats and words
only. Who then flooded the whole inhabited world in the time of Noah, and
worked that grievous shipwreck, and made the total destruction of our whole
race? who again after these things sent those thunderbolts and those
fire-storms upon the land of Sodom? who drowned all Egypt? who laid low the
six hundred thousand in the wilderness? who burned the synagogue of Abiram?
who commanded the earth to open its mouth and swallow those about Korah and
Dathan? who brought down the seventy thousand in one moment of time in the
days of David? who killed the one hundred eighty-five thousand in one night in
the prophecy of Isaiah? Shall I speak also of those punished privately, Cain
delivered to perpetual punishment, Charmes stoned with all his family, the one
who suffered the same for gathering wood on the sabbath, the forty-two boys in
the time of Elisha who were consumed by the beasts, and did not even obtain
pardon from their age? And if you should wish to see such things even after
the grace, consider how many things the Jews suffered, how their own children
were eaten by their


wives, some roasting them, and others consuming them otherwise; how they were
delivered to an incurable famine, and to various and grievous wars, so as to
hide all the former tragedies by the excess of their own calamities. For that
Christ worked these things upon them, hear him foretelling these things, both
through parables, and clearly and uncoveredly; through parables, as when he
says: But those who did not want me to reign over them, bring them and
slaughter them; and through the vineyard, and through the marriage; but
clearly and expressly, as when he says threatening, that They shall fall by
the mouth of the sword, and there shall be distress of nations upon the earth,
in perplexity of the roaring of the sea and surge, men fainting from fear, and
There shall be such tribulation as has never been, nor shall be. And Ananias
and Sapphira, what a penalty they gave for the theft of a few pieces of
silver, you all know. And the daily calamities, do you not see how many we
undergo when we sin? or have these things not happened either? Do you not see
even now those wasting away with famine, those held by elephantiasis and
bodily mutilation, those living with perpetual poverty, those suffering
countless incurable and grievous things? How then would it have reason that
some are punished, and others not punished? For if God is not unjust, just as
indeed he is not unjust, you too will altogether give a penalty when you sin;
but if, because God is a lover of mankind, he does not punish, these too ought
not to have been punished. But now, because of these cold words of yours, God
punishes many even here; so that, when you do not believe the words of the
threat, you may at least believe the deeds of the punishment; and since the
ancient things do not =59.623= frighten us thus, by the things that happen in
each generation he corrects those who are slothful at the time. γʹ. And for
what reason does he not punish all here, he says? In order to give the others
a season for repentance. For what reason then does he not punish all there? In
order that many may not disbelieve his providence. How many robbers, some have
been caught, and others have gone away without giving a penalty? where then is
the love of mankind of God and his just judgment? for it is mine to ask you.
For if no one at all were punished, I could have fled to this; but when some
give a penalty, and others do not, although sinning worse, how would this have
reason, that the same faults should not have the same penalties? and how would
those who were punished not seem to be wronged? For what reason then, he says,
are not all punished here? Hear him making a defense to you about these
things. For when some died, a tower having been brought down, he said to those
in doubt about these things: Do you think that these alone are sinners beyond
all? No, I say to you; but unless you also repent, you will all perish as
they; exhorting us and teaching us not to be confident, when others have given
a penalty, and we, having committed many faults, do not give one; for if we do
not repent, we shall give it altogether. And how, he says, are we punished
immortally, having sinned a short time here? Just as indeed here too a man,
committing one murder in a brief moment of time, is condemned to the perpetual
misery of the mines. But God is not thus, he says. How then did he hold the
paralytic thirty-eight years in such a punishment? For that he punished him
because of sins, hear what he says: See, you have become healthy; sin no
longer, lest something worse happen to you. But nevertheless he received a
release, he says. But the things there are not such. For that those things
will never have a release, hear him saying, Their worm shall not die, and
their fire shall not be quenched; and, These shall go into eternal life, and
these into eternal punishment. If then the life is eternal, the punishment
also is eternal. Do you not see how many things he threatened the Jews? Did
the things threatened then come to pass, or was what was said a word only? And
again, There shall not remain a stone upon a stone. Did it remain? And what of
this, that he also said, There shall be tribulation such as has never been?
Did it then not happen? Read the history of Josephus, and you will not even be
able to breathe as you hear what those men suffered in the facts. For he says
that those terrible things surpassed every


tragedy, and that no war ever such overtook a nation. I say these things, not
in order to grieve you, but in order to make you secure, and lest, having
entertained you with superfluous things, I prepare you to undergo more
grievous things. For why, tell me, do you think it fit that you should be
punished when you sin? did he not foretell you all things? did he not
threaten? did he not frighten? did he not work countless things for your
salvation? did he not grant the bath of regeneration, and forgive all the
former things? did he not, after this forgiveness and the bath, also give you
again, when you sin, the help from repentance? did he not make the way easy
for you even after these things for the forgiveness of sins? Hear then also
what sort of things he commanded. If you forgive the neighbor, I also forgive
you, he says. What difficulty does this have? Judge for the orphan, and do
justice for the widow, and come, and let us reason, he says, and if your sins
are as scarlet, I will whiten them as snow. What toil does this have? Speak
you, he says, your sins first, in order that you may be justified. What
difficulty does this have? Redeem your sins in almsgivings. What sweat is
this? The publican said, Be merciful to me the sinner, and he went down
justified. What labor is it to imitate the publican? But you do not wish to be
persuaded even after so many de =59.624= monstrations, that there is
punishment and penalty? Then you would not even say that the devil is
punished. For Go, he says, into the eternal fire prepared for the devil and
his angels. For if there is no Gehenna, neither is that one punished; it is
clear that we too who accomplish his works shall [not] be punished; for we too
disobeyed, even if not in the same things. And how are you not afraid, O man,
uttering daring things, when you say that God is a lover of mankind, and does
not punish? And if he does punish, is he found according to you to be no
longer a lover of mankind? Do you see into what sort of words the devil leads
you? And what? shall the monks who have occupied the mountains, and displayed
a myriad of ascetic practice in mountains and caves and the holes of the
earth, go away uncrowned? For if the wicked are not punished, nor is there a
recompense for anyone, another perhaps will say again that neither are the
good crowned. No, he says; but this is fitting to God, that there should be a
kingdom only with him, and not Gehenna. Then shall the fornicator and the
adulterer and the one who has worked countless evils enjoy the same things as
the one who has displayed temperance and holiness and self-control and every
angelic way of life? And if there is no Gehenna, but there will altogether be
a resurrection, the wicked too will obtain the same good things as the
righteous. And who would say this even of men utterly out of their minds?
rather, what demon would say this? But even the demons themselves confess that
there is a Gehenna; therefore they also cried out saying, Have you come here
before the time to torment us? How then are you not afraid and do you not
shudder, O man, yourself denying while the demons confess? how do you not
perceive the teacher of these wicked doctrines? For the one who from the
beginning deceived man, and by a proposal of greater hopes cast him out of the
good things in his hands, this one is the one who now also suggests these
things to say and to think; and for this reason he persuades some to suspect
that there is no Gehenna, in order to throw them into Gehenna. For God on the
contrary threatens Gehenna, and has prepared Gehenna, in order that we, having
learned, may live piously thus, so as not to fall into Gehenna. And yet if,
when Gehenna exists, the devil persuades you of these things, how, if it did
not exist, would the demons have confessed, to whom it is much desired that we
suspect nothing of the sort, in order that, becoming more slothful by the
license, we may fall with them into that fire? And how, he says, did the
demons then confess? Not bearing the necessity laid upon them. Thinking all
these things then, let those who say these things cease deceiving both
themselves and others. For God, he says, will come manifestly, our God, and
will not keep silence. Fire shall burn before him, and round about him a
mighty tempest. Fire shall go before him, and shall flame round about his


enemies. And who are the enemies of God, but the fornicators, the adulterers,
the sodomites, the effeminate, the idolaters, the greedy, the drunkards, the
perjurers, the murmurers, the wrathful, the mindful of injuries, the proud,
the boastful, those disobedient to parents, the unmerciful? To all these on
the fearful day of the common resurrection the judge commands saying, Go from
me, you cursed, into the eternal fire prepared for the devil and his angels.
δʹ. And if we shall give a penalty concerning these things that have been
said, much more shall those give an account at that fearful judgment seat for
these words who ridicule the fearful and shuddering commandments of Christ,
and who relax the tension of many who wish to be in earnest, and who do not
even imitate the barbarian Ninevites. For those men, although inexperienced in
all these things, having heard that the city would be overthrown, not only did
not disbelieve, but also groaned, and put on sackcloth and were confounded;
and they did not sooner leave off doing all things, until they had dissolved
the wrath and the indignation of God. But you the Christian, who have received
so great experience of deeds and of words, do you make nothing of the things
said by Christ? Then the contrary things shall be =59.625= for you. For just
as the Ninevites, having feared the words, did not undergo the trial from the
deeds; so you too, despising the threat from the words, will sustain the
punishment from the deeds. Do you not see what Christ has done here? how,
having taken two robbers, he did not think them worthy of the same things, but
the one he brought into the kingdom, and the other he sent into Gehenna? And
why do I speak of a robber and a man-slayer? For he did not spare even his own
disciple, when he became a traitor, but even seeing him rushing upon a noose,
and being strangled and bursting in the middle (for He burst in the middle,
and all his bowels were poured out); nevertheless he allowed him to suffer all
these things, persuading you from the present things also concerning all the
things there. Do not then cheat yourselves, O men, being persuaded by the
devil; for these thoughts are his. For if judges and masters and teachers,
both civilized and barbarian, honor the good and punish the wicked, how would
it have reason that the contrary things should happen with God, and that both
the good and the wicked should be thought worthy of the same things? And when
will the wicked also be delivered from wickedness? For when they now expect
punishment, and in the meantime have so great a fear from the judges and the
laws, and they do not depart from wickedness; when they have gone away there
and put off this fear, and not only do not fall into Gehenna, but also obtain
a kingdom, when will they stop doing wickedness? Is this then of love of
mankind, tell me, to intensify wickedness? to set a prize upon evil? to think
the temperate and the unbridled, the faithful and the impious, the merciful
and the inhuman, worthy of the same things? For if nothing is a care to God,
neither when we sin, nor when we do right, perhaps the saying that there is no
punishment has some reason; but if God makes so great earnestness, so that we
may not even sin, and transacts so many things, so that we may accomplish the
commandments, it is quite clear that he both punishes those who sin, and
crowns those who do right. But consider for me the inconsistency of the many.
For here they accuse God, because he is often long-suffering, and overlooks
many who are polluted, licentious, greedy, not giving a penalty; there again
if he threatens to punish them, they are vehement and heavy in blaming; and
yet if this grieves, that other thing ought to have been accepted and wondered
at. But O the folly! O the irrational and shameless mind! O soul that loves
sin and loves pleasure, and inclines toward evil! For from much love of
pleasure all these doctrines are born; so that if those who say such things
should wish to lay hold of virtue, they will quickly also be persuaded
concerning Gehenna, and will not doubt. And where, he says, and in what region
will this Gehenna be? For what


does this matter to you? For the thing sought is to show that it exists; but
where it is stored up, and in what region, do not seek. For some mythologizing
say that Gehenna is in the valley of Jehoshaphat, which has been said
concerning a certain war that has passed. For in the valley of Jehoshaphat
too, punishing the adversaries with a just judgment, he utterly destroyed
them, whose weapons sufficed the inhabitants of Jerusalem for burning for a
seven-year time. And now they drag this to Gehenna; but the Scripture does not
say this. And in what place, he says, will it be? Somewhere outside, as I at
least think, of this whole world. For just as the prisons and the mines of
kings stand far off, so also Gehenna is somewhere outside this inhabited
world. Let us not then seek where it is, but how we might flee it, nor,
because God does not punish all here, for this reason disbelieve the things to
come; for he is a lover of mankind, and is long-suffering; and for this reason
he threatens for the time being, and does not punish immediately; For I do not
wish, he says, the death of the sinner, as that he should turn and live. And
if there is no death of a sinner, what was said is superfluous. εʹ. And I know
indeed that nothing is more unpleasant to you than these words; but to me
nothing is sweeter than these. Would that =59.626= it were possible always and
through all for us to converse about Gehenna, both while breakfasting, and
while dining, and while bathing! for we would not have been pained by the
terrible things here, we would not have been pleased by the good ones. For
what terrible thing do you even have to name to me? poverty, disease,
captivity, maiming of the body? But all these things are a laugh compared to
the punishment there. Even if you speak of those tortured throughout by
famine; even if of those maimed from first age and begging, these things are
luxury compared to those evils. Continually then, beloved, let us turn the
words about these things, both in thought and on the tongue; for the
remembering of Gehenna does not allow one to fall into Gehenna. Do you not
hear Paul saying, Who shall pay eternal penalty from the face of the Lord? Do
you not hear what sort of man Nero became, whom Paul also calls a mystery of
the Antichrist? For the mystery, he says, of lawlessness is already at work.
What then? shall Nero who punished Peter and Paul suffer nothing? shall the
Antichrist suffer nothing, shall the devil nothing? Then the Antichrist and
the devil will never depart from wickedness, if they are not punished. Yes, he
says; but that there is punishment and Gehenna is clear to everyone, I
suppose; but the unbelievers only will fall into it. For what reason, tell me?
Because, he says, the faithful have come to know their own Master. And what is
this? for when the life is unclean, they will for this reason also give a
greater penalty than the unbelievers; For as many as, he says, sinned without
law, shall also perish without law; and as many as sinned in law, shall be
judged through law; and, The servant who knew the will of his lord, and did
not do it, shall be beaten with many blows. And if it is not [required] to
give a penalty of life, but these things have been said simply, neither shall
the devil be punished. For he also knows God, and far more than men; and all
the demons both know him, and shudder, and confess him a judge. If then there
is no giving an account of life, nor of wicked deeds, these too will escape.
These things are not so, they are not; do not deceive yourselves, beloved. For
if there is no Gehenna, how do the apostles judge the twelve tribes of Israel?
how does Paul say: Do you not know that we shall judge angels? how much more
things of this life? and how again did Christ say: Men of Nineveh shall rise
in the judgment, and shall condemn this generation? and, It shall be more
tolerable for the land of Sodom and Gomorrah in the day of judgment? Why then
do you play in things that are not for play? why do you deceive yourself, and
cheat your soul, O man? and why do you even fight against the love of mankind
of God? For for this reason he both prepared Gehenna, and threatened, in order
that we may not fall into it, having become better by the fear. So that the
one who takes away the word about these things is secretly doing nothing else
than thrusting himself into Gehenna itself, and throwing himself in through
this deceit. Do not then relax the hands of those who for virtue


are toiling, nor intensify the sloth of those who sleep. For if the many
should be persuaded that there is no Gehenna, when will they depart from
wickedness? and where will the just appear? I do not speak upon sinners and
righteous, but upon sinners and sinners. For this reason one was punished
here, and the other was not punished, sinning the same things, or even far
more grievous. For if there is no Gehenna, he will be able to make no defense
about these things to those who accuse. Therefore I exhort you all, having
ceased from this satanic laughter, to muzzle those who contradict you about
these things. For there will be an exact examination even of the smallest
things in the sins and in the right actions. For we shall give a penalty even
of unbridled eyes, and of an idle word, and of disorderly laughter, and of
simple reviling, and of a wicked thought, and we shall undergo accountings of
drunkenness; as again in the good things we shall receive a wage even of a cup
of cold [water], and of a good word, and of a groan alone. For give, he says,
the sign upon the face of those who groan and are in pain. How then do you
dare to say that God, examining our things with such exactness, threatened
Gehenna simply and in vain? Do not, I exhort, do not, I beg you, destroy
yourselves by these empty hopes, and those who are persuaded by you. For if
you disbelieve our words, examine Jews, =59.627= Greeks, all heretics, and all
as from one mouth will answer, that there will be a judgment and a recompense.
But are the men not enough? Ask even the demons themselves, and you will hear
them saying: Why have you come here before the time to torment us? and
gathering all these things, persuade yourselves not to speak in vain; =59.628=
in order that you may not learn Gehenna through the trial, but, having been
chastened from here, you may be able to escape those tortures, and to obtain
the good things to come, by the grace and love of mankind of our Lord Jesus
Christ, to whom be the glory and the power, with the Father and the Holy
Spirit, unto the ages of ages. Amen.
