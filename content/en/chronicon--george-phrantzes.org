17.1 And there was a secret counsel, being only to me from my lord and despot,
that we should go against Patras; and if we take it, behold we shall be found
in the Morea and his territory, that of the Black Sea namely, shall be given
to his brother the emperor. And if we do not take Patras, we shall go back to
the City and he shall have here in the Morea the castles of his dowry and
there the territory of it, namely the Black Sea; and the castles given by the
despot shall again be given, where the emperor discerns. This also followed,
which was from one side a confirmation of the secret study. 17.2 Passing from
Vostitza by the road of Patras, in order that we might go to Glarentza and
Chlomoutzin, where also the empress was, Andronikos Laskaris the Padiates was
sent to the archons in Patras for certain services; and while he remained
there, both a priest and laymen spoke with him privately about this, that if
his lord wishes, they have a way to make him take Patras. And when Laskaris
came and said among the rest also the secrets of the Patrans, it was dismissed
as impossible and superfluous, of him also having heard and speaking. 17.3
Then the same Laskaris being sent as head to Androusa, and further also the
other archons as heads to the castles there, and indeed also the first of the
archons of his household, Laskaris Alexios, was to have taken Vostitza as his
captaincy and remained there. 17.4 Passing through, as I have shown, my despot
and lord with me and me alone working the matter of Patras, being found in
Glarentza, we wrote often to those with whom Padiates had spoken about this
and they wrote back to us also often, but they judged that from us impossible,
as time also showed. 17.5 At last we set it that we should go and being night
be found at a place near the end of the vineyards of the said Patrans called
Three Churches, since they were of old <there>; and there the men should also
be found and should declare at length the things through writing; and if they
are possible, let them be worked, and if not, let us go openly and shut up the
castle and as that which bears may bear. 17.6 And behold we wrote orders to
all those in the district of Androusa, that on the 15th of the month of March
of the same year they should come

Seizing me then, with many wounds leading me away, they put me in the keep

18.1 And here it is fitting to narrate also concerning the most holy Thomais.
She being of a useful race in the East, being orphaned, her kinsmen bringing
her to Constantinople gave her to the sister of the three hierarchs and
brothers, the Kabasilai, and mother of the most wise Nicholas Kabasilas. 18.2
And she having her and educating her after some time went to Thessalonike,
Neilos Kabasilas her brother being hierarch there, and they dwelt in the
monastery of Saint Theodora with Palaiologina, a virtuous and learned woman,
concerning whom I have heard many praises and often from the late emperor kyr
Manuel; and many canons both to Saint Demetrios and to Saint Theodora and to
other saints I have read, poems of that woman, they having with them also the
holy Thomais and educating her both in virtue and in speech. 18.3 And when
their end came, they left her all their things, and indeed also the wife of
Kyprianos, who being indignant at the harshness of her own husband went off to
the said nuns and became a nun; and since it came about their end,


she was subjected again to the heiress of those women both of their life
somehow and of their virtue; and she ran well the course of subjection until
death, I mean the wife of Kyprianos, of him who came to Constantinople and
founded the monastery in the name of the Holy Theodores, nicknamed that of
Kyprianos. 18.4 And when the impious took Thessalonike, the holy Thomais going
out with also her subject the Kypriane came to Lemnos, so that from there
again to Constantinople. In which Lemnos my grandfather being found with his
own wife and children, the first of his three daughters seeing her and her
virtue and speech, the principally first daughter of his remaining in
Thessalonike with also her husband and children, leaving parents and sisters
and brothers and him whom she had betrothed as husband, going off became a nun
and in subjection of this same holy woman. And going out from there and coming
the three only arrived at the beginning in the city of Constantine in the
monastery nicknamed that of Kleraina. 18.5 And the virtue of this same holy
woman and her experience in the divine scripture being spread both to the
emperor and the patriarch and all those of the city, the emperor sought that
she also take a monastery, whichever is greater and of more revenues, and the
patriarch that he give her a mandate of spiritual authority over the women of
the city who wish it, and those of the city that they also come and see and
serve her for their needs; for there was among those women neither gold, nor
silver, nor anything else, except the handiwork and the providence of God
through the archons. 18.6 And taking a small cell, there ran together into
subjection so many as would not have fitted even into the monastery of Lips
and that of Kyramartha, but she received no more than twelve. And they were
such both in poverty and obedience and chastity and everything whatsoever
acceptable to God, that those of the city left off saying the holy woman, but
the holy women. and they had this same name for them, holy women. And the
common of the nuns as to food and garments and the not having anything of
one's own and the not doing the least thing, whether for common or for private
need, except with permission, and the unshod and little-fed and the lying on
the ground and in cheapness and the other things, as many as are testimonies
of virtue, it is of the superfluous to narrate. Likewise again also of their
holy and spiritual mother, the running together to her and the viewing of
emperor and ladies and archons and archontesses, some for confession, some for
prayer and viewing, who could narrate these things also accurately and her
virtue accurately? 18.7 And narrating one thing only we shall show all true.
On the said Great Saturday, on which there happened to me the things which I
have narrated, she sitting after the completion of the liturgy in the church
and with her all her nuns and remaining, so that they might taste a little
also because of the partaking of the antidoron, then that a reading be set in
the Acts of the Apostles, the holy woman says: Nuns, rise; And they: What need
have you, our lady holy one? And she: A trial has come upon kyr George; and
let us entreat the Theotokos on his behalf. And all hearing this, and
especially the sisters of my mother, for another and later sister also became
a nun and a good subject of hers, they ran with tears: What is this, our lady
holy one? And she to them: Do not be alarmed; God who permitted the trial will
also grant the help and dissolution of this same trial. And standing they sang
the paraklesis to the Theotokos with tears. What better testimony than this of
her virtue toward God and intimacy? 18.8 Living then thus well and God showing
many testimonies of her virtue, at last her death also came honorable before
the Lord and men. For being ill some days a little and all in common

closed, and her mouth always moving toward hymns and thanksgivings to God,
neither taking anything of food or drink, nor speaking the chance thing,
except of the

19.1 And I lying in prison forty days, when there came the memory of the
liberator of captives, the great martyr namely George, I entreated him, as I
too am George and from childhood his servant; and waking it seemed to me that
I was found in the most beautiful temple named in his name of the Mangana,
standing at the phiale and wearing also the irons. As the emperor passed, as
if to go to the office of the church, I entreated him that they free me from
the irons; and the emperor passed within the church, and a certain young
archon turning back to me says to me: Our lord the emperor has ordered that
they take you out of the irons; for our lady the empress also asked it. 19.2
And about the dawn itself of the night at an unaccustomed hour, behold the
archons of the keep and others with iron-tools and they take off me all the
irons; and making excuses they say, not that they have enmity, that they feed
me not well, but that they have not. And after two days coming again they seek
me and I entreat my lord through writing, that he yield that archons come out
to speak toward a settlement, which also happened. 19.3 And being settled they
gave him Saravale thus, that he should rise and go to his house Glarentza; and
until the whole of May if the metropolitan and their lord comes, he shall do
as he wishes, and if not, they shall give the castle. And oaths being made and
he receiving also Saravale, on the 5th of May he rode and went as far as the
bounds of Sklavitzas and Riolou. 19.4 And for me he left John Rosatas, so that
he might take me; for if my being freed is kept they will have the
confirmation of the things done. And being freed half-dead I scarcely arrived,
where also my lord was, who seeing me with joy so great, and grief, the one
that I was thus half-dead, the other that I was freed, and ordering many words
toward my praise and consolation, as I went to my tent, they brought me as his
grant a double tabarion of green kamouchas from Lucca worthy of note, clothed
with also green cloth and good, a Thessalonian skouphia clothed with gold-red
hasdi, a kavadion of crimson kamouchas clothed with heavy lining, a
kurtsouvakin of gold-green kamouchas and a photan of green and a decorated
sword. 19.5 On the morrow going to Glarentza, behold also an archon of the
emir after some few days, saying that Patras gives me harac and it passes as
my own; rise therefore from it and do not besiege it; and if not, we shall
send an army against you. And my lord again said to him: We have heard that
they wish to give it to the Catalans; it did not therefore appear fitting that
enemies both of my brother the great emir and of us we


should let them take such a castle in the midst of our territory. For this we
went there; and examining the matter, we set that it should not happen. And
behold, as you see, we rose and came to our house; in a few days then I have a
purpose to send this same archon to my brother the great emir, showing me, and
he will declare also the greater part of our words. And the Turk hearing these
words, and further being well treated courteously, went off rejoicing. 19.6
And when he had gone, he ordered me: Behold prepare the things needed for the
road, I not knowing beforehand the chance thing. And I reported to him that
only may God grant that the metropolitan not come and that they give us the
castle, and I to fall ill anything more; but I will have all things ready for
the fulfillment of your acceptance. 19.7 And the term coming and the
metropolitan not coming, on the 1st of the month of June of the same year,
riding we go to Patras. In those days then also the lord's son kyr Thomas was
besieging the castle of the prince centurion, Chalantritza, of him who later
also became his father-in-law. And as my lord and his brothers passed the road
leading to Patras, this same brother of his also coming down they remained
together at Kamenitza. 19.8 And when the archon found in Chalantritza of the
prince, Ioannoutzos Balotas, also learned this, asking leave, so that he might
come and do homage to both the lords; he came; and as he came out at once
taking out the keys of the castle he gave them to my lord the despot, saying:
This is a castle privately of my lord and kinsman-in-law of your majesty; for
the son of the prince previously took as lawful wife the sister of the empress
kyra Theodora; and he ordered me that I deliver this to her. And he answered
him: Thus it is, that the place is of a kinsman of mine, but not of one more
genuine than my brother; therefore if my brother had done this earlier, as
toward mine, he would have wished to attempt nothing, and I would have had it
peacefully and your lord otherwise; but now if I take it, it forces that we
differ with my brother; and it does not happen for much, much less for so
much; but go in it and, as you are able, do. 19.9 And when he heard these
things, he asked that he enter well into his own castle. And I was ordered and
I took him with soldiers and went. And about the castle Thomas Raoul being
found thought it easy that he take him from me. And it came to danger that
there become between the brothers some great fighting and grievous thing, yet
then God approving, I settled him well into his castle and I turning back well
found my lord in the temple and tomb of Saint Andrew the first-called, arrived
on the 4th of June late. =19.10= And on the 5th early of the same, day of the
week the 1st, the chosen of the castle and all the people coming out and
coming as far as even the said temple of the saint, they did homage to the
despot and my lord and gave the keys of the castle. And riding with most joy
both of the one part and of the other, we entered into the castle and went as
far as the houses at the temple of Saint Nicholas, the whole road being spread
with all flowers and good order and from the houses on right and left all
being sprinkled with rosewater and roses and thirty-leaved roses, and from
above the keep with engines and crossbows receiving us badly, even if they
harmed nothing; for those of the metropolitan holding the keep, and further
also the lordly houses near it and provisioning and fortifying, held on hoping
[that] when the metropolitan comes they would have again through this what
they also had before. =19.11= And we on the morrow being gathered in the
temple of Saint Nicholas and all the people of the country, they gave oath,
that


they be faithful servants of our lord the despot. And they asked me as head of
themselves; and they heard that for this also and more than this we owe him
and for your request he will be head of you.
20.1 And on the 8th of the month of June I crossing to Naupaktos, so that I
might first go to the emperor, so that he too might learn the things that
followed at Patras, then from there with also an archon of his to the emir.
For according to the 4th of the same month, on which arriving we remained in
the temple of Saint Andrew, behold also from Naupaktos two Turks crossed, the
one of the emir, the other of Turahan, saying an order, that Patras we should
not take it. And they again returned with me, taking answer that since the
present archon of me goes to my brother the great emir, as he orders, we shall
do. 20.2 As then crossing we remained in Naupaktos, early while it was still
dark, behold there also the metropolitan of Old Patras Pandolfo Malatesta came
with a Catalan galley. For passing as he learned about the islets in the midst
of the road, that the castle was given to the despot, he took the shore of the
part of Naupaktos, so that he might first learn this well, as it happened. It
was needful that I also remain there also the morrow, so that I might first
send word of this to my lord, which also happened; and from me he first
learned this; and second, so that I might also learn, as far as possible, what
the purpose of this same metropolitan was. 20.3 And Bernard Marcello that
captain of Naupaktos also becoming a middle, we saw also one another; whom
seeing I wondered, that he differed from all men in being ill-formed. He then
examining what I wish to do to the emir, and I him, what against Patras, I
took from him the so-called koukouzela, and he from me moss. 20.4 Yet the
metropolitan having given to the slaves papers both to the emir and to
Turahan, put much thought into me, lest perhaps he promises to give certain
castles of Patras, if he help him, so that he take it, or many certain moneys.
And my thought did not cease, until having toiled much and having drunk often
and unwillingly, I made those men also so drunk that I took those with the
papers and read and copied them; and those again I sealed and left. 20.5 And
when I arrived at the City, there was given me as fellow-envoy Markos
Palaiologos the Iagros, later protostrator, but then protobestiarites, being
more set against my service than over it; and I know no other cause, but that
envy does not know to prefer the advantageous. And going together to the emir,
we took answer, that we give it to those who had it; I then answered the then
first vizier and archon, Ibrahim pasha, that this I do not dare to say to my
lord, but since that man somehow sent an archon of his to the great lord,
behold let the lord also order a slave of his and let him come with me and let
him say the orders of the great lord. And he consented to this and said: You
speak wisely and well. Iagros privately mocking this to me as idle and only
harmful. And I God approving did this and took a lordly slave; and this was
the first cause of getting correction concerning Patras. 20.6 [And in the same
month of July of the same year there died also the despot Karoulos at
Ioannina.] 20.7 And in the 38th year in the month of September at the Kratikoi
the brothers the despots made the marriage-alliance of the lord's son kyr
Thomas with the daughter of the prince Asanes Zacharias the Centurion. 20.8 In
which month I also again returned to Trikala to Turahan and completely
corrected the service concerning Patras. 20.9 And in the same year in the
month of November, the empress kyra Theodora being found at Stamiron died,
leaving much grief both to her own husband and to us his household because of
her being


most beautiful. And she was buried until a certain point in one of the
churches of Glarentza; and after these things they took her to the monastery
of the Life-giver at Myzithras. =20.10= And in the month of January of the
same year the lord's son kyr Thomas was also blessed at Myzithras to kyra
Aikaterina, the daughter of the said prince.
21.1 And on the 20th of March of the same year on the day and hour at which
the Patrans seized me, as I was going to the despotate as envoy, at their
request for the sake of peace of the nephew despot of Karoulos and
brother-in-law of my lord and of the natural sons of this same Karoulos,
Herkoulis and Menonos, for they made sworn agreements, that whatsoever the
archon of the despot kyr Constantine sent at our request discerns in the
things disputed in the midst of us, we shall consent and both parts, at the
nearby islets of Hagia Mavra the Catalans took me with many both men and
precious vessels being with me. And holding me until a certain point and
leading me as far as even Kephallonia, as if about to cross to the parts about
Naples, at last we turned to Glarentza and they sold both me and those with
me. 21.2 In which month of March also the emir Muratbeis took Thessalonike
from the Venetians by war. 21.3 And the despot and my lord kyr Constantine, in
May of the same year, took the keep of Patras from famine and the other
suffering of those found within it. 21.4 And on the 17th of July of the same
year the Catalans took Glarentza, which also holding until a certain point
they again sold it. 21.5 And in the month of August of the same year the
envoys from the emperor kyr John to pope Martin returning, both Markos the
Iagros and grand stratopedarches, and the grand protosynkellos and abbot of
the august imperial monastery of the Pantokrator, the hieromonk and spiritual
father Makarios named Makrys, an excellent man both in speech and virtue and
understanding, by order of the emperor they made the lord's son kyr Thomas a
despot. 21.6 And in the 39th year in the month of September I was granted the
captaincy of Patras. 21.7 And in the same year in the month of October the
beylerbey of the Turks Sinan took Ioannina and their district. 21.8 And on the
20th of March of the same year, on the same day and hour, in which also the
things previously happened to me, I received also the message unhoped-for by
me through an order of my lord from Vostitza, that on the 7th of the past
January there died of pestilential disease the excellent and my friend
Makarios both being and called Makrys, who before a proud patriarchal eye and
an insatiable Agarene heart was a heretic, but before an unsleeping
pantokratoric eye and truth judging was orthodox. Who also returned to
Constantinople from the Holy Mountain and took the monastery of the
Pantokrator by my own urging and cooperation, as all knew. and by the
concurrence first of God the pantokrator, then by the zeal and care of both me
and him, everything whatsoever good and toward constitution and good order in
that monastery proceeded. 21.9 And at the end of the spring of this same year
Turahan came and destroyed yet also the Hexamilion. And a mortality so great
happened at Patras. =21.10= And on the 31st of January of the 40th year being
ordered I went as envoy both to the emperor, from whom I was then also granted
the office of protobestiarites, and to the emir. =21.11= In which year
according to the month of March there happened also the exchange of the places
of both the brothers and despots kyr Constantine and kyr Thomas; and the one
took Kalavryta and all the things there of kyr Thomas, and the other again
Glarentza and all the things about Androusa. =21.12= And in the 41st year
there died the daughter of Malatesta, and wife of the despot kyr Theodore the
porphyrogennetos, kyra Kleope; and she was buried in the


monastery of the Life-giver. =21.13= And in the 42nd year on the 7th of
January I again went as envoy both to Antony and lord of Athens, and to the
emir and to the emperor. =21.14= In which January on the 29th of it at the 3rd
hour of the night it burned the most beautiful and divine temple at Blachernai
in the name of the Mother of God at Constantinople, which we heard as we were
going there at the river named Mestos on the 1st of February, at an hour after
vespers of the lordly feast of the Hypapante. And we reckoned it a falsehood,
since he who said it was not known; and passing five stations forward and
hearing nothing about this, at our -th station at Raidestos we learned this
accurately, how and when it happened, which is paradoxical.
22.1 And at the beginnings of the summer of the 43rd year there died also 22.1
the lord of Athens and Thebes kyr Antony Delanzoles and at the request of that
man's wife I was sent with a sworn silver-bull and many soldiers, so that I
might receive Athens and give her another place in the Morea, as much and of
what sort appears to me; but Turahan preceding and shutting up Thebes, which
he also took after some days, I turned back idle from the Hexamilion, having
this as a charge. 22.2 And at Stylaria my despot and lord being found and
awaiting the Venetian merchant galleys, so that embarking he might go to the
City, behold I also arrived idle. And I also embarking we go; and arriving at
Euripos, it appeared good and I was sent to Turahan, being found at Thebes,
and I made known to him the service concerning Athens. 22.3 And he assured me
with an oath, that for the acquaintance and love toward the despot and you,
well and readily I would have conceded that this be done, if I had known
something before I went out from my house and came here, since by order of the
great lord I did not do this; and being in my house, I had many coverings, but
now I have no covering any longer. And being treated courteously honorably by
that man and he bringing his sons to homage to me, and handing them over to me
and my lord, of whom one was also the now great and great Amaris, I returned
from there also idle. 22.4 And since those in Euripos preceding raised the
bridge, we remained unwillingly on the rocks outside the bridge; we then

23.1 And on the 27th of November of the same year the emperor kyr John passed
with the patriarch and the despot kyr Demetrios and many archons of the senate

anyone of that part that ours is bad, but good and ancient, and that of those
men not bad, but good. 23.3 And let me say, as in an example, that the Middle
road of the city, the broad and spacious, I pass many years with certain men,
through which we used to arrive at Hagia Sophia. Then after some times there
was found by certain men also another road arriving, as they say, this too
there; and let them urge me, that come also through this road which we found;
for even if this, by which you go, is good and ancient and known to us from
the beginning with you and being passed, yet also this, which we have now
found, is good. And I hear from some that it is good, and from others that it
is not good, why should I not say: With peace and love go well to Hagia
Sophia, from wherever you wish; and I again will pass through the road, which
also with you I used to pass for some long time and which is witnessed good
both by you and by my ancestors and being passed. 23.4 Not for this cause then
I said the as it ought not, for I would have wished that a union of the
churches had happened well even if one of my eyes were lacking to me, but
because this service of the synod also was one cause and first and great for
there to happen the assault of the impious against the City and from this
again the siege and the captivity and such and so great a misfortune of ours.
23.5 And hear true words, I putting forward the self-truth as witness. The
late emperor said to his own son, the emperor kyr John, alone to alone, I also
alone standing


before them, a word having fallen about the synod: My son, we know firmly and
truly from the midst of the heart of the impious, that it much frightens them,
lest we agree and be united with the Franks; for they have it, that if this
happens, some great evil will happen to them from the Christians of the West
through us. 23.6 Therefore concerning the synod, study it and stir it, and
especially when you have some need to frighten the impious; but to do it, do
not attempt this, because I do not see our people, that they are fit to find
some way of union and peace and concord, but that they turn them to our being

such as the times of the prosperity of our ancestors needed; but today, as the

47.1 And about the spring of the same year Zouchasanes also went out against
the place of the emir Mehemet and overran places of his in the East; and thus
the emir went out from Constantinople and went against that man with all his
own power. And drawing near at the beginning that man defeated an army of the
emir, which went out in front against that man; in which he also killed the
beylerbey of the West, son of the sometime Palaiologos Thomas that Gidos and
many others of the archons and heads being in the West. Then going also face
to face of the emir he was defeated and turning he went to his own place;
likewise also the emir turning came to the City. 47.2 And about the end of
July of the same year the lordship of the Venetians also took the castle near
Koryphoi named Strophylin. 47.3 And on the 7th of the month of November of the
82nd year the empress kyra the Palaiologina also died at Hagia Mavra, having
also become a nun and being renamed Hypomone. 47.4 And on the 9th of the month
of December there died also the hieromonk found with us and spiritual father
kyr Hierotheos, who through the great schema was also renamed Joseph, who was
from youth with us and schoolfellow and friend until his own end. 47.5 And the
summer of the 83rd year the archon of the impious making a great armament and
sending it to the upper parts of the Black Sea took the great castle, Kaffa
happening. 47.6 And the summer of the 84th year he went against Vlachia with
all his own power and in September of the 85th year he returned more defeated
than defeating. And the beylerbey he left at Sophia, and he himself came to
Adrianople; and making some days going out he came to Constantinople.
48.1 And of the same year on the 1st of the month of October a rheumatism came
upon me both in the head and the knees and there went out from my mouth and
the nose and the ears so much moisture, that they despaired of me; and three
times they communicated me the unworthy of the undefiled mysteries, which also
would that death had come upon me, the healing of all the hard things in me,
both of old age I mean and of the weakness and of the want not of the many
good and accustomed from youth, but also almost of the necessary, except that
the patience of such things and thanksgiving toward God, by grace of God, is
about to be a cause toward his forgiving many of those in which I sinned
toward him. for there has not happened in life a sin, nor an action, nor an
evil, which I the wretched did not commit according to mind and word and
choice, and by position and



opinion and action having sinned as no other ever yet. 48.2 Yet being
delivered from death I remained deaf until much, so as not even to hear the
semantra near me, and further also bedridden from the pains of the feet; and
it remained to the upper providence, either that I be delivered from such
things, days having passed, or death coming upon me and the rest of men, as
the divine Job previously said. 48.3 But I entreat God thus: But if, savior, I
must suffer, for who knows the depth of your judgments? your philanthropy as
it were loathing my passions, and justice again doing its own, therefore for
the time awaiting an end grant me to give justice, but not for the coming the
endless, where there is not a changing of the affairs. Here then having
cleansed me, using all things that lead to this, thus may you remove me from
the things here worthy of not falling from your mercies in your second and
frightful presence. 48.4 And the summer of the same 85th year <the archon of
the impious> sent a great army against Naupaktos, so that he take it, then
come to Hagia Mavra, which by help of God neither took Naupaktos, nor came
against Hagia Mavra, but returned idle.
