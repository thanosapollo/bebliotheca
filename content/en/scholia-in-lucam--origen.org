ORIGEN ON THE GOSPEL ACCORDING TO LUKE. =17.312= CHAP. I. Verse 1. "Since many
have taken in hand to set in order a narrative concerning the things that have
been fully assured among us." Since the undertaking was overwhelming, for a
human being to compose the teaching and words of God, he reasonably makes a
defense in the preface. And just as among the people of old many professed
prophecy, but of these some were false prophets, and some truly prophets; and
there was a gift of discernment of spirits, from which gift the true prophet
and the false prophet were judged; so also now in the new covenant many have
wished to write the Gospels. But the approved money-changers did not judge
all, but selected some of them. And perhaps even the phrase "have taken in
hand" has a hidden accusation of those who came rashly and without a gift to
the writing of the Gospels. For Matthew did not take it in hand, but wrote
moved by the Holy Spirit; likewise also Mark, and John similarly, and Luke.
Those however who composed the Gospel inscribed according to the Egyptians,
and the Gospel inscribed of the Twelve, took it in hand. And the Gospel
according to Thomas is also in circulation. And already Basilides too dared to
compose a Gospel according to Basilides. Many then have taken in hand also
that according to Matthias, and several others. But the Church of God prefers
the four alone. And he did not simply say "believed," but "fully assured,"
bearing witness to the inviolable character of the things said. And he said
"of things," doing away with the heresy of those who say that the things done
through the Savior, and his incarnation, came about according to appearance.
And by saying "concerning the things that have been fully assured," he shows
his own disposition. For he had been fully assured, and doubted nothing,
whether it is so or not. And that he received from those who had seen with
their own eyes and were hearers, he clearly confessed, saying: "Even as they
delivered to us, who from the beginning were eyewitnesses and ministers of the
Word." And he affirms that he followed from above, not some of the things
said, but all. And it is likely that some suppose that he wrote the Gospel to
a certain Theophilus, =17.313= who was one of those who had believed, thus
fervent in spirit, and insatiable concerning the deeds and words of the Lord;
whom the things now being written also made more secure. But another says:
That if we all are such as to be loved by God and cherished, we are Theophili.
And no one would be a weak Theophilus; for every Theophilus is most excellent,
having the might and the power that is from God and from his Word, and thus he
will come to know the security concerning the words in which he was
catechized. He promises then the firmness of knowledge, so that what that man
received by hearing, he may, encountering it also in letters, keep the faith
more securely. For in Exodus it is written, That the people saw the voice of
the Lord, although a voice is not seen, but heard; that it may be shown
paradoxically that the voice of God is seen by those by whom it is seen. But
in the Gospel it is not the voice that is seen, but the Word who is better
than the voice. Therefore, "Even as they delivered to us, who from the
beginning were eyewitnesses and ministers of the Word." For if to have seen
the Lord according to the body was to become an eyewitness of him, Pilate also
was an eyewitness of the Word, condemning him, and Judas the betrayer, and all
who said: "Crucify, crucify him." Knowing the Word, then, was understood there
where the Savior said: "He who has seen me has seen the Father who sent me."
And Luke also secretly teaches us a fine lesson here, that of some
contemplations the end is contemplation


is; of some contemplations the end is action. For through "eyewitnesses" he
indicates the contemplative, and through "ministers" the practical. And you
can also take otherwise "who became ministers of the Word": either of the
teaching word which the Savior delivered, or even of the Word of God himself,
whom the disciples also served, being present. Luke shows his own disposition.
For he had been fully assured, and doubted in nothing, whether it is so or
not. And this comes about in those who believe firmly. For the Apostle also
says concerning the firm: "That you may be rooted and founded in the faith."
For nothing so fully assures as mind and word. For sight does not fully
assure; since the things are not judged from visible signs and wonders, but by
word it is judged which are true and which are false. And he says "of things,"
because Jesus did not dramatize his incarnate presence according to
appearance, as the children of the heretics have it, but being truth he worked
the things toward truth. Verse 3. "To write to you in order, most excellent
Theophilus." Verse 4. "That you may know the security concerning the words in
which you were catechized." Verse 5. "There was in the days of Herod king of
Judea a certain priest named Zacharias, of the course of Abia; and his wife
was of the daughters of Aaron, and her name was Elisabeth." =17.316= Verse ʹ.
"And they were both righteous before God." It is likely to suppose that he
wrote the Gospel to a certain Theophilus, who was one of those who had
believed, thus fervent in spirit, and insatiable concerning the deeds and
words of the Lord; whom the things now being written also made more secure. If
we all are such as to be loved by God and cherished, we are Theophili. And no
one would be a weak Theophilus. For just as it is written of the people going
out of Egypt: "There was not one feeble among their tribes"; so I would say
that every Theophilus is most excellent, having the might and the power that
is from God and from his Word. And thus one will come to know the security
concerning the words in which he was catechized, understanding the word of the
Gospel. He promises then the firmness of knowledge, so that what that man
received by hearing, he may, encountering it also in letters, hold more
securely, and keep the faith. To be righteous before God is a perfect praise.
For he alone knows the hearts of human beings. For it is possible that
according to what appears someone is righteous before human beings, but
according to the hidden things of the heart is not such, possessing evil
reasonings lurking within. Such also is that in the Apostle: "Whose praise is
not from human beings, but from God." For human beings do not praise worthily,
seeing only what appears; but God worthily makes both the judgment of what is
blameworthy and the praise of what is praiseworthy. Verse ʹ. "Walking in all
the commandments and ordinances of the Lord, blameless." Verse 7. "And they
had no child, because Elisabeth was barren, and they were both advanced in
their days." Verse 8. "And it came to pass, while he was serving as priest in
the order of his course, before God," Verse 9. "According to the custom of the
priesthood, he was allotted to burn incense, having entered into the temple of
the Lord." Verse 10. "And all the multitude of the people was praying outside
at the hour of incense." Perhaps someone will say: Why is "blameless" set
before these? For it would have sufficed for them to walk in all the ways of
God and in ordinances; unless indeed it is possible to walk in all the
commandments, but not blamelessly. I say then to him that, if this were not
so, it would not have been said: "Justly you shall pursue the just." Whenever
then we do the commandments so as not even in the conscience to have a stain
of vainglory, or of people-pleasing, or of something else of that kind, we do
the commandments blamelessly. Verse 11. "And there appeared to him an angel of
the Lord standing on the right of the altar of incense." Verse 12. "And
Zacharias was troubled when he saw, and fear fell upon him." Verse 13. "And
the angel said to him: Fear not, Zacharias, because your petition has been
heard, and your wife Elisabeth will bear you a son, and


you shall call his name John." =17.317= Verse 14. "And there will be joy and
gladness for you; and many will rejoice at his birth." Through condescension
an angel appeared to Zacharias. For no one can see an incorruptible body with
the eyes of a corruptible body. And the angel appeared standing on the right
of the altar, because he was about to announce favorable things to him. And
Zacharias was troubled when he saw. For a strange sight appearing to a human
being is not endurable. But nevertheless the angel turns Zacharias from the
trouble and restores him, saying: "Fear not, Zacharias." Know that God or the
angels do not appear to everyone, but to those who have a pure heart and are
worthy of seeing. For he is found by those who do not tempt him, and he is
manifested to those who do not disbelieve him. Of the same. Since all who saw
angels were afraid, lest they had come as about to take their souls, as Manoah
also says; therefore the angel, resolving his agony, said: "Fear not." For not
only do you not die, where there is grief and tears, but you both live and
beget a child, and there will be great joy for you; for Elisabeth will bear
you a son, than whom none greater has arisen among those born of women. Then
he says: "And many will rejoice at his birth." For even if not all Jews, yet
many rejoiced at the presence of the forerunner announcing the sojourn of the
Savior. According to the contemplation, it is the silence of the Prophets in
the people of God of old. For God no longer speaks to them, but the Word who
was in the beginning has passed over to us. And among us he is not silent, but
among them he has fallen silent. Therefore the prophet Zacharias is silent.
And the nodding while remaining mute. Verse 22. "And coming out he was not
able to speak to them, and they perceived that he had seen a vision in the
temple. And he was nodding to them, and he remained mute." Verse 23. "And it
came to pass, when the days of his ministry were fulfilled, he went away to
his house." Verse 24. "And after these days Elisabeth his wife conceived, and
hid herself five months, saying:" Verse 25. "That thus the Lord has done in
the days in which he looked on me to take away my reproach among human
beings." Verse κ ʹ. "And in the sixth month the angel Gabriel was sent by God
to a city of Galilee named Nazareth," Verse 27. "To a virgin betrothed to a
man named Joseph, of the house of David, and the name of the virgin was
Mariam." And what was he nodding to them? Having heard beforehand that a
delightful thing was about to be for him, he did not hasten to run before the
time, =17.320= but remained to fulfill the ministry; teaching that one must
make all things second to the honor toward God. And most economically
Elisabeth hid herself, until her womb was known, and the matter was beyond
doubt; so that those hearing these things who were not willing to be persuaded
from hearing might not be suspected of the same charge of which Zacharias also
was. The hyperbaton is thus: The angel was sent to a virgin of the house of
David, and the name of the virgin was Mariam, betrothed to a man named Joseph.
And the angel was not sent to Joseph, because there was nothing common to
Joseph with the birth of the Lord. As then sin began from a woman, so also the
good things had to begin from a woman. Verse 34. "And Mariam said to the
angel: How shall this be, since I do not know a man?" Verse 35. "And the angel
answered, and what follows." The "you have found favor" was common. For others
also before her found favor. But to say "you shall conceive" was no longer
common, but peculiar, a strange thing as a promise to a virgin. For you have
found favor with God. Therefore, fear not. For the sojourn of the Savior is a
taking away of every fear. Verse λ ʹ. "And behold Elisabeth your kinswoman,
and what follows." The Forerunner heard, moved by the working of the Holy
Spirit, the greeting of Mary, which the Word of God, the Christ, took up, and
he leaped in gladness in the womb of his mother, as a showing of joy and a
declaration of prophecy. For from that time, as it were, Jesus began to make
him a prophet, furnishing to him within the declaration of the mystery. But
since he did not


give speech to embryos because of the untimely season, while the Baptist was
eager to proclaim the Redeemer, the mother of the body served as an
instrument; for she was not first filled with Holy Spirit before she who was
carrying Christ in the womb arrived. And first the infant was filled with
Spirit and leaped; and then in this way he imparted to the mother, and
Elisabeth cried out prophetic things because of the infant being carried in
her. Of the same. The people does not know the Savior doing signs and wonders;
but John, still in the womb, leaps when the mother of Jesus came. "For
behold," she says, "as the sound of your greeting came into my ears, the
infant leaped in gladness in my womb." It was necessary that this blessed
Mariam, after the sojourn of Gabriel, going up, go up to the hill country. And
she went with haste, led by the Spirit in her, which had come upon her, and by
the power of the Most High, which had overshadowed her. Just as sin began from
a woman, and =17.321= after this reached the man, so also the good things
began from the women, that the women may turn away from the weakness, and
putting off the womanish things may emulate the lives of these blessed
ones. Verse 43. "And whence is this clear to me, that the mother should come,"
and what follows. Of the same. Elisabeth speaks in accord with the son, saying
herself unworthy of the presence of the Theotokos, just as John also of
standing beside Christ. "For what," she says, "so great a good has been done
by me, that the mother of my Lord should come to me?" She calls mother her who
is still a virgin, anticipating the outcome by a prophetic word. A divine
economy then brought the testimony to Elisabeth, so that both the testimony of
John from the womb toward the Lord might be fully assured through his own
mother Elisabeth, and so that, according to the voice of the angel, seeing
Elisabeth and the paradoxical conception in her, she might through this also
believe the greater, that is, I mean the seedless childbirth from herself the
virgin. And there also runs together toward this faith the word of Elisabeth,
saying: "And blessed is she who believed that there will be a fulfillment of
the things spoken to her from the Lord." For the virgin became firmer toward
the faith from these words, being called blessed because she believed, both an
angel signifying beforehand and the kinswoman likewise prophesying. Of the
same. Let us see then a virgin's prophecy. Verse μ ʹ. "My soul magnifies the
Lord." How my soul magnifies the Lord must be considered. If we live worthily
according to the image of him who created us, and are likened to the
archetype, as far as possible, through good works, we magnify the Lord in
words, works, thoughts. And one diminishes him analogously, according to the
wickedness that is in him. The soul of Mary then magnified the Lord, and her
spirit rejoiced in God the Savior. First her soul magnified, then her spirit
rejoiced. One must observe in the whole Old and New Scripture, whether
anywhere there is to be found, upon the birth of sinners, the saying: "The
time was fulfilled for her to give birth." For no one would find it, as I
think; but where it has been named, that the one being born is righteous. And
this also and the rest. Elisabeth, being a prophetess, and not having heard
the revelation that came to the husband, by the Holy Spirit also said the
appellation spoken by the angel, and that the child was to be named John. And
when they also thought it right to learn from the father, and they nodded to
him, as not hearing or able to speak; taking a tablet he wrote in accord with
his own wife the appellation of the child, so that all were astonished at
these things. Then it says: "And his mouth was opened." After the writing,
clearly, on a tablet, that John =17.324= will be his name, the bound voice was
unbound. And he received back his own tongue, no longer as of old bound by
unbelief, but blessing God and prophesying the things that have been written.
It is superfluous to say, and the rest. As I think, our fathers also, Abraham
and Isaac and Jacob, and the whole choir of his holy prophets and righteous,
enjoyed the


presence of Christ. For if he made peace through the blood of his cross, both
the things on the earth and the things in heaven, according to the divine
Apostle, why do you hesitate to accept that the visitation was toward the
fathers also, and that his arrival into Hades accomplished this through his
unsearchable mercy? And this, even if it has come about now, was yet
proclaimed beforehand of old; and it was impossible that those words not come
out into work. For by an oath he confirmed the promises because of their
inviolable character, that it will altogether be. And now the faithful are
delivered fearlessly from the hand of the intelligible enemies. Often some are
delivered, and the rest. The angel does not go away into the synagogue of the
Jews to Jerusalem toward the priests and scribes and Pharisees; but he finds
shepherds living in the fields and keeping watches of the night over their
flock; to these he brings the good news of the birth of the Savior. For these
were unformed, emulating the ancient way of life of the patriarchs. For they
too were shepherds, who also running announced to the others. For those, being
corrupted by envy, were going to hide the mystery of the salvation of the
world. For this one alone opened a womb, being born of a virgin. For nothing
touched that womb before Christ. And all the firstborn, even if they are
firstborn, yet they themselves do not first open the womb, but the husband.
Lest perhaps he had left them. Being in pain then they were seeking the Son of
God, and seeking him they did not find him among the kinsfolk; for he was
above their kinship. CHAP. II. Verse 49. "Did you not know that I must be in
the things of the Father?" As toward the letter let us arm ourselves against
the godless heresies, which say that the creator is not the Father of Jesus
Christ, nor the God of the law, nor the God of the temple, this one is the
Father of Christ. For let those from Valentinus be ashamed, hearing his voice
saying: "I must be in the things of my Father," if they accept the saying.
Were they then so without understanding as not to understand what he said: "In
the things of the Father," i.e.: In the temple? Or does he perhaps hint at
some mystery? =17.325= "If anyone of you is of the Father, does he have Christ
in himself?" For rather, seeing an ensouled temple of the Father, the one both
beautiful and good, than that temple; for he went out from that temple saying:
"Your house is left to you," and he went away upon the temple of the Father,
the Churches everywhere. Verse 51. "And he was subject to them," and the rest.
Upon the prophetic word proclaimed among Jews alone, only a kingdom of Jews is
written; "For a vision," it says, "which Isaiah saw against Judea and against
Jerusalem in the kingdom of Uzziah and Jotham and Ahaz and Hezekiah"; and I
know no other king written in the time of the prophecy than him of Judea; and
upon some prophecies also the king of Israel. But when John was about to
proclaim the mystery of the Gospel, there has been written not Herod king of
Judea alone, nor Tiberius of Romans alone. CHAP. III. If those from the
nations alone were going to be saved, and the salvation of the sons of Israel
has been shut off entirely, the "In the fifteenth year of the rule of Tiberius
Caesar" would have sufficed; but since those from Galilee also must believe,
and those from Ituraea and Trachonitis, and Abilene, therefore these kingdoms
also are written. And he mentioned also the high priests Annas and Caiaphas,
that he might show that from the time at which John came proclaiming, these
were high priests in that time, having begun, and remaining until the passion
of the Lord in the high-priesthood. Of the same. For never did a word of God
come upon any of the prophets in the wilderness. But since there were going to
be many children of the wilderness rather than of her who has the husband,
therefore a word of God came upon John in the wilderness. For if this were not
so, he was doing it superfluously, proclaiming in the wilderness. "And he came
into all the region around the Jordan"; for where should the Baptist go forth,
if not into the region around the Jordan, so that, if anyone should wish to
repent, he may at once have supply of the


river? For truly descending is the river of God, the true water, the saving
water. And he proclaims, it says, a baptism unto remission of sins. And unto
remission of sins, for whom? or for the one no longer sinning. Then does he
set Isaiah presenting this very thing crying out? For what? it says "A voice
of one crying in the wilderness." And he says this as if Isaiah himself were
hearing the words of John. For John was chosen for a sending, and perfect
among prophets. And since John was forerunner of the Word, he is reasonably
named voice, not word. For the voice precedes the word. And "Prepare the way
=17.328= of the Lord," what is it, if not i.e., Be made ready for reception of
whatever he may wish to legislate? "Make straight his paths," i.e., Cease
doing the twisted things. A valley deep and hollow is every unbeliever, who,
having believed in Christ, is filled with the fruits of the Spirit, that is,
the virtues. And a mountain and hill made low is the high-minded people of the
Jews ...... Or mountains and hills are the opposing powers, which through the
care of Christ were made low; and valleys are the human beings, who have been
filled with good works. Rough was our life, and uneven the word. But our Lord
having come has made all things smooth. And these things have come about upon
the nations; but upon the people that has been taken down, every mountain and
hill shall be made low. For it was once a mountain and a hill; but they were
taken down and have been made low. Reasonably he calls all the unbelieving
offspring of vipers. For he who does not leave behind the customs in himself
does not receive baptism well; but is still offspring of a viper, having in
himself the poison of wickedness. Of the same. He names stones the Gentiles,
who have a stony heart, and from much insensibility serve the creation beside
the one who created, and worship stones. He prophesies then the taking down of
that people and the calling of the nations. For to those who boast Abraham he
says: "Do not begin to say in yourselves: We have Abraham as father"; and that
God is able to make the nations children of Abraham. Of what stones? He was
not showing lifeless stones, but human beings who are insensible stones,
because they were worshipping stones. And there came about upon them what is
written: "May those who make them become like them, and all who trust in
them." And further also concerning the stones it has been written in the song
in Exodus: "Let the nations be turned to stone, until this people pass by,
whom you acquired"; as it is clear that after the people has passed they would
no longer be turned to stone. Verse 10. "And the crowds asked him, saying:
What then shall we do?" Verse 11. "And answering he says to them: He who has
two tunics, let him share with him who has none." What they say is of this
sort: The customs given to us from Moses and the fathers concerning
sacrifices, which things shall we do concerning sprinklings, or do you
introduce to us a way of another manner of life? For if the Jewish things
profit us nothing, tell us what one must do. For we do not know a way of
another manner of life. What then does the Baptist? Not daring as a household
servant to move aside the shadow, and to introduce the worship in spirit and
truth, he leads them upon the fullness of the law, that is love, whose fruit
is to be sharing and ready to give, and a lover of the poor. For the Jews had
a boast upon love of brothers, and the distinction of love of the poor;
=17.329= not having the whole of the Gospel, but introducing a beginning of
the New Covenant. He introduces three things, and the rest. CHAP. I. Verse 22.
See the type in Zacharias. For having cast out the word upon themselves, and
not being able to give an account concerning any legal or prophetic word, they
remain mute and without word. Verse 63. "And having asked for a tablet he
wrote, John is his name." For since Zacharias is interpreted memory of God,
and John the one who shows; and one remembers the one who is absent, but shows
the things present; therefore the begetters of this child did not endure to
call him Zacharias, but rather wished to name him John. Because he was going
not merely to remember God as absent, but that one himself with the


finger point him out present and say: "See the Lamb of God." The all-holy
Virgin knew that he was not a child of Joseph, but she reckons her own
betrothed into the persons of these because of the suspicion of the Jews, who
thought he had been born of fornication. More simply then it would be said
that the Holy Spirit honored him with the appellation of father, and the rest
..., lest perhaps he had left them. But the Lord, leaving aside giving a human
answer, gives a divine one, indicating that he would be God incarnate. Or then
you will understand thus: that mountains and hills are the opposing powers,
which through the sojourn of Christ were made low; and the valleys, the human
beings, have been filled upon good works. And all crooked things have become
into a straight. For each of us was crooked. In the sojourn of Christ then
that has come to be into the soul, the crooked things become into a straight.
And again rough was our life, and the word uneven; but the Lord having come
has made all things smooth. CHAP. III. Verse 1. "In the fifteenth year of the
rule of Tiberius Caesar." Upon the prophetic word then proclaimed among Jews
alone, only a kingdom of Judea is written. "For a vision," it says, "which
Isaiah saw in the days of Uzziah and Jotham and Ahaz, who reigned over Judah."
But upon the mystery of the =17.332= Gospel about to be proclaimed in the
whole world, the rule of Tiberius Caesar is written, who seemed to be king of
the whole world. And if those from nations alone were going to be saved, and
the salvation of the sons of Israel had been shut off entirely, it would have
sufficed to mention Tiberius alone. But since those from Galilee and Ituraea
and Trachonitis must also believe, therefore these kingdoms, or tetrarchies,
are written. And never did a word of God come upon any of the prophets in the
wilderness, except now alone through a certain mystical contemplation. For
since there were going to be many the children of the wilderness rather, that
is of the Church from the nations, than of her who has the husband, I mean the
law of the synagogue of the Jews. And to say that "A word of God came to John"
is a command. Verse 3. "And he came into all the region around the Jordan."
Where should the Baptist go about, if not into the region around the Jordan,
so that, if anyone should wish to repent, he may at once have supply of the
river? And Jordan is interpreted descending. For truly descending is the river
of God, the true water, the saving water. Verse 7. And they say that the viper
in conceiving kills the male from whom she conceives; whose seed increasing
kills the one who conceived, and thus it comes out into light, having torn
through the belly of the mother, as a penalty of the destroyed father ........
Such also are the Jews, destroying their spiritual fathers and teachers with
hands. It has been said concerning these more fully in Matthew. CHAP. IV.
Verse 9. "And he led him into Jerusalem, and stood him upon the pinnacle of
the temple." And see how knavishly through the use of the Scriptures he wishes
to take down the glory of the Lord, as needing angelic help, as about to
strike against, if angels should not help him. For the use of the psalm has
not been said concerning Christ. For the master of the angels does not need
angels. And I say the reverse to you, O devil: if Jesus does not help the
angels, they strike against. And you have fallen from heaven for this reason,
because you thought yourself sufficient, and not to need the help of Jesus.
And that you may know that the things written have not been said concerning
Jesus, but are referred to a righteous man, hear: For it has been written: "He
who dwells in the help of the Most High will lodge in the shelter of the God
of heaven." This fits a righteous man rather than the Son of God; and again:
"A thousand will fall from your side, and a myriad," and what follows, which
also themselves are referred upon the righteous man. Verse 40. "And when the
sun was setting, all who had those weak with various diseases brought =17.333=
them to him. And he, having laid hands on each one of them, healed them." And
they were bringing these about sunset, either after the


day, being ashamed, or fearing the Pharisees, and occupied about other things,
or perhaps thinking it is permitted to heal on Sabbath. For the evangelist
noted that he was teaching them on Sabbath. Therefore around the setting of
the sun those belonging to them were bringing those who were in a bad way; and
he healed them. CHAP. VII. Verse 22. And historically: After the sending away
of the little bird, first the one cleansed washes the garments, then he shaves
every hair, and upon these he is washed with water, after which there is
brought in, "He shall be clean." And the garments would be a manner of life
not altogether badly woven, since he would have put these off. And hair the
things that have blossomed dead to the soul, to which are opposed those of
Samuel, and of the Nazirites and of the disciples themselves numbered. For of
the Nazirites iron did not go up upon the head. All things whatsoever they do
of those acting with good speed, so that not even the leaves of them when
bearing fruit flow away. And already from the body he puts away the defilement
with water, the baptism being shown in this. And after this he becomes worthy
of the camp, but does not enter into his own house until seven days, and until
