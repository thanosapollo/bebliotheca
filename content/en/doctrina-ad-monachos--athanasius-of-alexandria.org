OF OUR FATHER AMONG THE SAINTS ATHANASIUS THE GREAT A gentler, spiritual
teaching to monks.
The one who believes in the Lord, and comes to himself, and at last into
discipleship, must first learn to stand away from every sin; and then also,
being drawn by the things that are owed, to fare well toward our Lord in many
respects, even if I should seem to you somewhat unreasonable. For it is
impossible for the one who commits sin, or is entangled in the affairs or
cares of life, to give himself to the service of the necessities of living,
still less to be made a disciple of the Lord; who did not first say to the
young man, "Come, follow me," until he commanded him to sell his possessions
and give to a poor man; nor did he even order this before the young man
confessed that "All these things I have kept from my youth." For he who has
never received the forgiveness of sins and been loosed from them in the blood
of our Lord Jesus Christ, but serves the devil and is held by the sin in him,
is unable to serve the Lord who has given the inviolable sentence in saying:
"Everyone who commits sin is a slave of sin. And the slave does not remain in
the house forever." Paul also, who speaks in Christ, bears witness that "the
slave of sin is free from righteousness." And again the Lord: "No one can
serve two masters," and what follows; through which he taught, and showed,
defining and saying that neither can those who are held by the truly necessary
care of living serve, still less become disciples, which the Apostle learned
when he considered it more broadly; for he said: "What participation has
righteousness with lawlessness, and what fellowship has light with darkness,
and what agreement has Christ with Beliad? or what portion has a believer with
an unbeliever, and what consent has a temple with an idol?" And again he
defines: "The flesh desires against the spirit, and the spirit against the
flesh; and these are opposed to one another, so that you may not do the things
you would." One must first be delivered from the tyrant, the devil, who leads
into sin the one held by it. We said, then, that he who wishes, and when he
has denied all present things and himself, and has withdrawn from attachment
to living, should be made a disciple of the Lord, according as he himself has
said: "If anyone will come to me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross,
and follow me," which is to be my disciple here. And we are commanded not only
to despise possessions and the necessities of living, =28.1424= but we are
also trained to think little of the duties customarily held toward one
another, both those just according to nature and those according to law, the
Lord saying: "He who loves father or mother above me is not worthy of me."
Likewise also the rest that dwells nearest, and it is clear that much farther
also of those foreign to the faith. Therefore I greatly exhort those who are
without anger, in endurance, to the labors of ascesis, fearing in nothing the
methods of the enemy against us. This form of this world is passing away. And
these things, the deceit and the affliction of this life, are temporary and
short-lived, but to serve God is eternal life, everlasting and immortal, where
we who serve Christ wait; for which we also have renounced the world and all
its desires. And we ought not to remember those things which we left behind
because of this commandment, lest, desiring the temporary things again, we
throw away the eternal life. For he is without falsehood who said that
"everyone who has left house," and the rest, "shall receive a hundredfold, and
shall inherit eternal life." For it is not, as some seem to interpret these
things, that the Lord spoke concerning those who in the time of persecutions
bore witness for his name. For he did not say that only those who in the time
of persecutions will give over their own bodies for my name shall inherit
eternal life; but all who for my name have renounced both the world and all
its desires, and have renounced all his possessions, shall inherit eternal
life, together with all those, that is, who have borne witness for my name.
The Savior said that all the poor in spirit, and the pure in heart, and the
meek, and those who fear his name are blessed, "For yours is the kingdom of
the heavens." For the martyrs often, even having contended in one moment of
time, were perfected; but the way of life of the monks bears witness by
contests each day for him. We do not array ourselves against blood and flesh,
but against the principalities, against the powers, the first world-rulers of
the darkness of the age, against the spiritual things of wickedness, having
the struggle until the last breath, and it contends, and is shown forth in the
full armor ... ... of God, being fenced about, and it can do all things
through Christ who strengthens and has mercy. For wishing to grant us his own
wealth in a parable, the Lord said: "The kingdom of the heavens is like a
merchant seeking beautiful pearls," and what follows. Confirming this
commandment the evangelist spoke thus: "Do not love the world nor the things
in the world. For he who loves the world does not love the Lord who loved
him." But he who has hated this world and the scandals in it, and has loved
the will of God, remains well-disposed unto the age. For the Master, wishing
to separate us from the sojourn in the world, said: "No one who has put his
hand to the plow and turned back is fit =28.1425= for the kingdom of the
heavens." And the Apostle says: "One serving as a soldier is entangled in the
affairs of life." And again the Lord: "Let him who is in the field not turn
back," that is, not again to remember or to desire to return to the manner of
life which, having hated it for God and his commandment, he left. Do not
therefore fear the temptations brought upon us from the devil, even if they
are varied, for it is written: "My son, if you attend to serving the Lord,
prepare your soul for temptations." And concerning the heart of endurance,
waiting for the help of God; for he is greater than the devil who wars against
you, as it is often said: Greater is he who is in us than he who is in the
world; and the Psalmist: "I shall not fear evils, for you are with me." And:
"The Lord is my illumination and my Savior, whom shall I fear?" And, "If a
camp is arrayed against me, my heart shall not fear." And, "The Lord is my
helper, and I shall look upon my enemies." Therefore the one serving the Lord
must not disbelieve, as though the Lord does not always shield him; for he
himself has said: "I will not leave you, nor will I forsake you." And at any
rate you have set him as your refuge, "evils shall not come near you, and a
scourge shall not approach your dwelling;" and what follows. Therefore I
exhort you, brothers, let us not yield at all to the evil one, nor kill
ourselves through any pleasure whatsoever, but remembering the good things
laid up for us in the heavens, let us look above all these things. Let us not
then appear a plaything of those who boasted before us. For the Apostle
commands each to remain in Christ in the calling in which he was called, so
that we may receive the crown of endurance from Christ the rewarder, which he
promised to the one who loves him. For it is written: "Conquer your desire
through endurance, and you shall be perfect in Christ;" and: "To the one who
conquers I will give to eat of the tree of life which is in the midst of
paradise." The Lord says again also: Whoever through the wood of the cross
shall conquer all the passions of the flesh, to this one I will give a white
robe. For if we fear the Lord, the unspeakable good things and the crowns of
glory have been prepared. For not by a command of the saints is the war
against the methods of the enemy, and the victory over him, given through the
help of the Lord, but to three, as in the parable of the seed, the Lord
himself said; for he gave, he says, one thirty, another sixty, another a
hundred. And the grounds of these orders, if you search the Scriptures, you
will find in many ways; martyrs then are, as my word has it, those who make
the hundredfold fruit, virgins those who make the sixty; and the rest who
according to the commandments of the Lord are eager to keep themselves
blameless in every manner of life well-pleasing to the Lord, thirty. May it be
that we be enrolled among your disciples, Christ Jesus, and enjoy your holy
good things, now and unto the unending ages of ages. Amen.
