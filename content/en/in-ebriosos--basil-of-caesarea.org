HOMILY XIV.
Against the drunkards.
The evening spectacles move me to speak; but again the fruitlessness of the
former labors holds back my impulse and dashes away my eagerness, since the
loss of all that toil has proved unfruitful. For a farmer also, when his first
seeds have not sprouted for him, is more sluggish the second time to sow again
the same fields. For if, after so many exhortations, which we did not cease
addressing to you in the time past, and after we proclaimed to you the gospel
of the grace of God continually, in witness both by night and by day
=31.445= throughout these seven weeks of the fast, no benefit has come
about, upon what hopes shall we discourse today? Oh, how many nights you have
watched in vain! How many days you have assembled in vain! That is, if indeed
in vain. For one who has made progress in good works and then has fallen back
to his former habit is not only deprived of the reward for what he has done
well, but is held worthy of a heavier condemnation: because, having tasted the
good word of God and been thought worthy of the mysteries of knowledge, he has
betrayed all, beguiled by a brief pleasure. For the least is pardonable
through mercy; but the powerful, he says, shall be powerfully tested. One
evening, and one onset of the enemy, dissolved and made to vanish all that
labor.

What eagerness then of the present discourse? So that even if I kept silence,
know well that I would, unless I feared the example of Jeremiah, who, not
wishing to speak to a disobedient people, suffered those things which he
himself related: that there was made in him as it were a burning fire shut up
in his bones, and he was wearied on every side and could not bear it. Women
unchaste, forgetful of the fear of God, despisers of the eternal fire, on such
a day, when they ought because of the remembrance of the resurrection to be
sitting quietly at home and receiving the thought of that day on which the
heavens shall be opened, and the Judge shall appear to us from the heavens,
and the trumpets of God, and the resurrection of the dead, and the just
judgment, and the recompense to each according to his work; instead of holding
these things in their thoughts, and cleansing their hearts from evil
imaginings, and anointing with tears their past sins, and making themselves
ready for the meeting with Christ at the great day of his appearing, they have
shaken off the yoke of the slavery of Christ, casting from their heads the
coverings of modesty, despising God, despising his angels, putting to shame
every reverent countenance, disheveling their hair, dragging their tunics,
and playing with their feet at the same time, wanton in glance, made mad by
laughter poured out into dancing, summoning upon themselves every license of
the young, having formed choruses in the places before the city, they have
made the hallowed places a workshop of their own shamelessness.

They defiled the air with their lascivious songs, they defiled the earth with
their unclean feet =31.448=, which they struck in their dances, making a
theater for themselves, having ringed about a crowd of young men, truly
frantic and utterly beside themselves, leaving no bound of madness. How shall
I keep silence of these things? How shall I lament as they deserve? Wine has
wrought for us the ruin of these souls: wine, the gift from God given for the
consolation of weakness to those who are sober-minded, but now made a weapon
of licentiousness for the wanton. Drunkenness, the self-chosen demon, cast
into souls through pleasure; drunkenness, the mother of evil, the opposite of
virtue: it makes the brave man a coward, the temperate man licentious; it
knows not justice, it does away with prudence. For as water is hostile to
fire, so intemperance in wine extinguishes reason.

Therefore I hesitated to say anything against drunkenness, not as about some
trifling evil or one worthy to be passed over in silence, but as though the
discourse were providing no benefit. For if the drunkard is out of his senses
and darkened, the one who rebukes chants idly to one who does not hear. With
whom then shall we discourse? Since the one who has need of the exhortation
does not hear the things said, and the one who is sober and awake has no need
of the aid of the discourse, being clear of the passion. What use then shall I
make of those present, if both the discourse is useless and silence is without
resource? Shall we abandon the care? But the negligence is perilous. But shall
I say something against the drunken? But we sound into dead ears. Perhaps
then, as in pestilential diseases the physicians of bodies secure the healthy
with preservative aids, and do not attempt those mastered by the disease, so
also the discourse has for you its usefulness by halves: pointing out the
guard to those free of the passion, but not deliverance and healing to those
mastered by it. In what have you differed from the irrational, O man? Is it
not in the gift of reason, which, having received from your Creator, you
became lord and master of all creation? The one then who has stripped himself
of prudence through drunkenness was reckoned among the cattle, the senseless,
and was likened to them.

But rather I would say that those in drunkenness are more irrational than the
beasts; for the four-footed creatures and the wild beasts have their impulses
toward intercourse ordered, while those held fast in soul by drunkenness,
having filled their body with the heat contrary to nature, drive it at every
season and every hour into unclean and shameful unions and pleasures. And not
only does this work unreasonableness in them, but also the perversion of their
senses =31.449= makes the drunkard worse than any beast. For what beast so
mis-sees and so mis-hears as the drunkard? Do they not fail to recognize their
own dearest, yet often run to strangers as to their familiars? Do they not
often leap over shadows as over ditches or ravines? With sounds and noises, as
it were of a sea tossing, their ears are filled; and the earth seems to stand
up to rise on high, and the mountains to run round in a circle. At one time
they laugh without ceasing, at another they suffer pain and weep
inconsolably. Now they are bold and fearless, now terrified and cowardly.
Heavy are their sleepings, hard to rouse and suffocating, and truly neighbors
of death; and their wakings from sleep are more senseless still. For life is
to them a dream, who, having neither cloak nor aught to eat on the morrow,
reign and command armies in their drunkenness, and build cities and distribute
moneys. Such fancies and so great a deception does wine pour round about,
filling their hearts. But others are turned about into opposite passions:
despondent, dejected, pained, tearful, timorous, faint-hearted. The same wine,
working through differing conditions of bodies differing passions upon souls:
for those in whom, causing a diffusion of the blood toward the surface, it
shall pour it out, these it makes bright and pleasant and flushed; but those
in whose constitution it shall press down through its weight, contracting and
compressing their blood, it turns about to the opposite condition. And what
need to tell the crowd of the other passions? The difficulty of temper? The
quickness to anger? The complaining? The lightness of the soul? The shouting?
The tumult? The easy beguilement by every deceit? The unmeasured haste unto
wrath?

But the incontinence toward pleasures is carried openly, as it were, from a
fountain of wine, and the disease falls in with the unchaste licentiousness,
showing forth upon womankind a second madness of bestiality more than the
drunkenness of men; for the irrational creatures know the boundaries of
nature, while the drunken seek the female in the male and the male in the
female. Nor is it easy to go through in discourse all the terrible things from
drunkenness. The harms from the plague come upon men in time, the air
depositing little by little its own corruption in bodies; but those from wine
fall upon them at once. For having so lost their soul as to be marked, as it
were, by every stain, they corrupt besides even the very constitution of the
body: not only =31.452= stretched out and flowing through unto lustfulness
by the excess of pleasures inflaming them, but also bearing a body swollen and
puffed up and loosed from its vital tone. Dark are their eyes; sallow the
surface; breath panting; tongue hanging out, and cry unmeaning; feet
tottering, like those of children; and the discharges of the refuse flow of
themselves, as from things without soul. Pitiable in their luxury, more
pitiable than those storm-tossed at sea, whom waves succeeding one another and
washing over do not suffer to lift up the head from the flood. Thus also do
these souls drift submerged, baptized in wine. Therefore, as storm-tossed
ships, when the hold is filled beyond the pumps, are of necessity lightened by
the casting out of the cargo, so for these men the casting away of the
things weighing them down is of necessity. For vomiting and discharging with
difficulty they
are freed from the burden; so much more pitiable than those who sail in
peril, as the latter blame the winds and the sea and the necessities from
without, while the former draw upon themselves of choice the tempest from
drunkenness. Wretched is the demoniac: but the drunkard, suffering the same
things, is not even worthy of pity, wrestling with a demon of his own
choosing.

They even compound remedies of drunkenness, contriving not to suffer any
terrible thing from the wine, but not to cease being drunk. For brief to them
is the day, and brief the night and the storm, in comparison with the time of
drinking. And there is no end of the evil: for the wine itself leads itself on
further and further. For it does not console the need, but works an unavoidable
need of another draught, kindling the drunkards, and ever calling forth a
desire toward the greater amount. And devising to have an unfilled appetite
for drinking, they suffer the opposite of what they chose. For by the
continuance of their luxury they wither the senses. For the exceeding
brightness blinds the eyes, and as those struck by great noises are turned by
the excess of the blow into not hearing at all, so these also have lost the
pleasure through the excess of their pleasure-loving, without knowing it. For
the wine is tasteless and watery to them, even if unmixed; and the perception
of the young wine is lukewarm, even if it be the purest, even if snow itself,
unable to quench the flame burning within them from the excess of the wine.
For whom the woe? For whom the tumult? For whom the judgments? For whom the
disgusts and the revels? For whom the shatterings in emptiness? Whose eyes are
darkened? Not of those who linger over wine, and spy out where drinkings are
held. The woe is a lamenting byword. And worthy of lament are the drunkards,
because they shall not inherit the kingdom of God. And the tumult is because
of the disturbance arising in their reasonings from the wine =31.453=. And
the disgusts are because of the bitter reactions from the pleasure of
drinking. For of these the feet are bound and the hands are bound by the
streams sent up upon them from drunkenness.

And yet even before these passions, at the very moment of the drinking, the
sufferings of the frenzied come upon them. For when the membranes become full
of the vapor which the wine gives off as it is exhaled, the head is cast into
unbearable pains, and not being able to remain upright upon the shoulders, it
falls now upon one side, now upon another, slipping upon the vertebrae. And a
revel means the immoderate and quarrelsome talk in the drinking parties. And
shatterings in emptiness come to the wine-bibbers, who are not able to stand
upright from drunkenness: for they are upset into all manner of corpse-like
postures, so that they must needs have shatterings in emptiness upon their
body. But who shall say these things to the wine-smitten? For their heads are
heavy from the headache; they grow drowsy; they gape; they see a mist; they
are seasick. Therefore they hear not the teachers sounding into their ears
from every side: Be not drunk with wine, wherein is excess; and again: The
wine is licentious, drunkenness insolent. Willfully deaf to these, they have
straightway the ripe fruits of drunkenness: the stomach is bloated, the eyes
rheumy, the mouth dry and parched. For as the ravines, so long as the torrents
flow into them, seem full, but, the flood past, are left dry: so too with the
wine-bibbers the mouth, while the wine lies pooled in it, is somehow full and
moist, but, a little running by, is found dry and unwatered. And being ever
swept along and flooded by the intemperance of wine, it loses besides even the
vital moisture. For what constitution of man is so strong as to withstand the
evils from drunkenness? For what contrivance could keep the body ever warmed
and ever soaked with wine from becoming washed out and worn away and running
to ruin? Thence the tremblings and the weaknesses: for the spirit being beaten
down in them by the intemperance of wine, and the nerves loosed from their
tone, a shaking comes upon the whole mass of the body. Why draw upon yourself
the curse of Cain, =31.456= trembling and wandering through the whole of
life? For the body not having its stability from nature of necessity totters
and is shaken. How long, O wine? How long, O drunkenness? You are in danger of
being, instead of a man, mire: so wholly have you been mixed with wine and
rotted together with it, ceasing to live from the daily headache of wine, and
corrupted with it, like the most useless of vessels.

For these Isaiah laments: Woe to those who rise early and follow strong drink,
who abide till evening; for the wine shall burn them; for they drink wine with
harp and pipe, and behold not the works of the Lord, and consider not the
works of his hands. Strong drink is the name customary among the Hebrews for
every drink able to work drunkenness. Those then who at the day's beginning
look about where drinkings are held, and gather round taverns and wine-shops,
and take one another up to drink, and spend every care of the soul upon such
provision: these the prophet bewails, as leaving themselves no time for the
consideration of the wonders of God. For the eyes of such have no leisure to
lift themselves to heaven, and to behold its beauties, and to visit all the
adornment in the things that are, so as to perceive from the good order of
these the Maker: but straightway at the day's beginning they adorn their
feasts with many-colored carpets and flowery hangings, and show speed and
diligence in the preparing of the cups, setting in order cooling-vessels and
bowls and saucers, as at some procession and festival, that the variety of the
vessels may steal in upon them surfeit, and the change and interchange of cups
beget for them sufficient occupation for drinking.

And over these are certain masters of feasts and chief cupbearers and marshals
of couches: and an order has been devised in disorder, and a rank in a thing
most unseemly, that, as the dignity of magistrates from without is increased
by their attendants, so also they, having environed drunkenness as it were
some queen with courtly attendance, may veil away its shameful reproach by the
excess of their zeal. Crowns upon these =31.457=, and flowers and unguents
and incense, and ten thousand other outward ministrations, prepare a greater
preoccupation for the perishing. Then, the drinking going on further, contests
about the greater amount, quarrels and trials, they vying to outdo one another
in drunkenness; and the devil is the presider over their games, and sin the
prize of the victory. For he who pours out the greater draught of unmixed wine
bears off the victory-trophies over the others. Truly, their glory is in their
shame. For they quarrel with one another, and defend themselves. What speech
can attain to the shamefulness of what is done? All is full of unreason, all
of confusion: the conquered are drunk, the conquerors are drunk, the servants
laugh to scorn. The hand is withdrawn, the mouth receives not, the belly
bursts, and the evil does not cease.

The wretched body, its natural tone paralyzed, is driven out on every side,
not enduring the violence of intemperance. A pitiable spectacle in the eyes of
Christians: a man in the flower of his age, vigorous in body, held worthy of
the military rolls, carried home upon shoulders, not able to stand upright,
nor to go upon his own feet. A man who ought to be terrible to the enemies is
a source of laughter to the children in the market: without iron he is
overthrown, without enemies he is slain. A man in armor, bearing the very
flower of his age, has become the vine's drink-offering, ready to suffer from
his enemies whatever they wish. Drunkenness, ruin of reasonings, corruption of
strength, untimely old age, speedily-coming death. For what else are the
drunkards than the idols of the nations? They have eyes and see not; ears have
they and hear not; the hands are unstrung, the feet are deadened.

Who is the one that plotted these things? Who the cause of these evils? Who
mingled into us this drug of madness? Man, you have made =31.460= the
banquet an array of battle. You cast out the young men, led by the hand as
wounded from war; you drowned the flower of youth with wine; and you call him
as a friend to the dinner, and cast him out dead, having quenched his life
with wine. And when they are thought to be sated with wine, then they begin to
drink, and drink in the manner of beasts, as from a chance-found spring letting
flow gushes equal in number to those set before them. For as the drinking goes
on further, there enters among them a youth of brave shoulders, not yet drunk,
bearing a very large bowl of chilled wine; who, thrusting aside their
cupbearer, taking his stand in the midst, distributes the drunkenness equally
to the fellow-drinkers through crooked tubes. This is a new measure of
intemperance: that they may be wanton together in equal measure, none
outdoing the other in drinking. For dividing the water-pipes among
themselves, and each receiving the one turned toward himself, they drink
without drawing breath, as the oxen, as it were from some cistern, hastening
so to draw in their throats as much as the cooling vessel lets down to them
from above through the silver channels. Bend down to your wretched belly:
consider the size of the vessel receiving: that it has the hollow of one cup.
Look not to the cupbearer, when you empty it, but to your own belly, that it
has long been filled. Therefore: Woe to those who rise early and follow strong
drink, who stay till evening and spend the day in drunkenness: so as to leave
themselves no season for beholding the works of the Lord and considering the
works of his hands. For the wine shall burn them: because the heat from the
wine arising in the flesh becomes fuel for the enemy's kindled darts. For the
wine baptizes the reason and the mind, and raises up the passions and
pleasures like some swarm of bees. For what chariot of colts is borne so
lawlessly, having cast off the charioteer? What ship, without a pilot, borne
along by the waves at random, is safer than the drunkard?

From such evils, men and women together, having formed common dances, giving
over their souls to a wine-inspired demon, struck one another with the barbs
of the passions. Laughter from both, shameful songs, lascivious postures,
provoking unto wantonness. You laugh, tell me, and delight in unchaste
delight, when it were right to weep and sigh over those who have gone before?
You sing the songs of a harlot, having cast out the psalms and hymns which you
were taught. You move the feet and leap madly and dance what must not be
danced, when it were right to bend the knees =31.461= for adoration? Over
whom do I lament? The maidens unmarried? Or those held in the yoke of
marriage? For the former returned without their virginity; the latter brought
not back to their husbands their modesty. For even if some escaped the sin in
body, yet surely they received in soul the corruption.

Let these things be said also concerning the men. He has seen ill, he has been
seen ill. Whoever looks upon a woman to desire her has already committed
adultery. If the meetings from chance encounter hold so much danger for those
who curiously pry, what shall the meetings by design say for themselves, so as
to see women in shameful plight from drunkenness, and disordered unto
looseness, singing broken songs, things which, merely heard, can implant every
goad of pleasure in the unchaste, who have gathered from such spectacles a
whole swarm of evils? Is it not that, looking for this very reason, they stir
up their desires? Then they are liable to judgment, by the unavoidable
sentence of the Lord, to the verdict of adultery. How shall Pentecost receive
you, Easter having been so insulted? Pentecost had the clear and manifest
indwelling of the Holy Spirit, known to all: but you, going before, made
yourself a habitation of the contrary Spirit, and became a temple of idols,
instead of becoming a temple of God through the indwelling of the Holy
Spirit. You have drawn down upon yourself the curse of the prophet, who spoke
in the person of God: I will turn their feasts into mourning.

How shall you rule servants, yourselves enslaved to foolish and hurtful
desires as bondmen? How shall you admonish your children, living a life
without admonition and without order? What then? Do I leave you in these
things? But I fear lest the unsubdued become more insolent, and the one
stricken down be swallowed in the greater grief. For, he says, a remedy shall
put to rest great sins. Let fasting heal drunkenness; the psalm, the shameful
melody; let a tear become a medicine for laughter; instead of the dance let
the knee be bent; instead of the clapping of hands let the breast be struck;
instead of the adornment of raiment, lowliness. Above all let almsgiving
ransom you from sin: for a man's ransom is his own wealth. Make many of the
afflicted your fellow-workers unto the communion of prayer, that haply the
imagining of evil may be forgiven you. When the people sat down to eat and
drink, and rose up to play (and their play was idolatry): then the Levites,
arraying themselves against their brothers, completed =31.464= their hands
unto the priesthood. To you then who fear the Lord, as many as now have been
grieved at the shamelessness of the condemned, we make this distinction: if
you see them repenting of the strange things done, pity them as your own
members diseased; but if they persist in boldness and despise your grief for
them: Come out from the midst of them, and be separated, and touch not the
unclean: that so those, being shamed, may come to the knowledge of their own
evil, and you may receive the reward of Phinehas, through the righteous
judgment of God and our Savior Jesus Christ, to whom is the glory and the
might unto the ages of ages. Amen.
