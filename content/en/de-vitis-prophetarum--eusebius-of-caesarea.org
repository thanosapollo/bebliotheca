=22.1261= From the writings of Eusebius of Pamphilus concerning the naming of
the book of the prophets and, in part, what each contains.

HOSEA, 1.

There were prophets even before this one. In the time =22.1264= of David,
Samuel and Nathan and Gad; in the time of Rehoboam and Abijah and Asa, Ahijah
the Shilonite and Shemaiah and Iddo and Azariah and Hanani; in the time of
Jehoshaphat and of Ahab, Elijah and Elisha, and the one who prophesied to Ahab
concerning Syria, and the one who struck himself with a wound and rebuked
Ahab, and Jehu the son of Amei [nota in margine, Ἀνανὶ], and Oziel, and
Eliada; but no writings of their own are transmitted, except only their words
in Kings and in Chronicles. Among those who wrote, whose writings also are
transmitted, these were first: Hosea and Isaiah. For they prophesied under the
same kings, Uzziah and Jotham and Ahaz and Hezekiah, kings of Judah, and in
the days of Jeroboam son of Joash, king of Israel. And the word came to him,
and this one prophesies against the priests, and against Israel, and against
the house of the king, and concerning the destruction of Samaria; and having
rebuked them and shut them up in disobedience, he signifies the Church from
the nations, through the harlot wife whom he took, whom having taken he
brought to sobriety, drawing her to himself. He also prophesies concerning the
coming of the Savior.

AMOS, 3.

This one also was contemporary at the beginning with Hosea and with Isaiah, in
the time of Uzziah king of Judah, and in the days of Jeroboam son of Joash
king of Israel; he arose two years before the earthquake. He saw words, and he
prophesies against Jerusalem, and against Israel, and Samaria, and against the
nations, Damascus, Gaza, Ashkelon, Ashdod, Ekron, Tyre, Idumea, Thamaan [nota
in margine, ἴς. Θαιμὰν], the sons of Ammon, and Moab; and thus he rebukes and
shuts all up in disobedience. But even when Amaziah the priest of Bethel
slandered him to king Jeroboam as speaking against Samaria and against the
people, he none the less did not cease speaking freely, and saying the oracles
also concerning the coming of the Savior.

MICAH, 6.

He was second to those mentioned before, because he prophesied after Uzziah.
But he was contemporary with them, since he too began to prophesy in the time
of Jotham and Ahaz and Hezekiah. And the word of the Lord came to him, and he
prophesies against Jerusalem, and concerning Samaria, and against the prophets
who lead the people astray, and against Ahab. He also signifies beforehand the
coming of the Savior, saying: In the last days the mountain of the Lord shall
be made manifest.

JOEL, 2.

The word of the Lord came to him and he prophesies to the elders and to all
who dwell in the land, concerning the capture of the land and of those in it;
he also prophesies concerning the grace of the Holy Spirit that will come to
all [lege γενησομένης] from the Savior. He also proclaims concerning fasting
and repentance, =22.1265= and concerning the coming of the Savior, and the
signs that took place at the cross.

OBADIAH, 4.

He reports a vision against Idumea and its end; and against the nations that
committed outrage against Israel. For he signifies beforehand that the house
of Esau will be consumed by Jacob.

JONAH, 5.

The word of the Lord came to him to go away to Nineveh and to preach in it,
that yet three days and Nineveh will be overthrown. But Jonah, fleeing the
preaching, went up into the ship. And when a storm arose he was thrown out and
swallowed by a sea monster. Then after three days the sea monster cast Jonah
out alive. And again a second time the word of the Lord came to him. And he
went and preached in Nineveh, and those people, having heard, repented, and
the Lord turned his wrath away from them. He himself became a sign of the
coming of the Savior, both being swallowed by the sea monster and being cast
out unharmed.

NAHUM THE ELKOSHITE, 7.

A burden concerning the destruction of Nineveh, a book of vision. He
prophesies and also proclaims concerning a feast. And in it he signifies
beforehand concerning the Savior, and he announces all the rest against
Nineveh and its end, and against the enemies of Israel.

HABAKKUK, 8.

He too saw a burden; he prophesies, praying concerning the capture of
Jerusalem, and signifying beforehand concerning the coming of the Savior. He
also speaks and prophesies against the despisers. Then in the midst there is a
prayer of the prophet with a song. And there are three diapsalmata in the
so-called song, as he sings concerning the coming of the Savior and the
victory over death.

ZEPHANIAH THE SON OF CUSHI, 9.

This one and Jeremiah were contemporaries of one another. For both prophesied
in the time of Josiah king of Judah. And the word of the Lord came to him, and
he prophesies in Jerusalem and to all Judah; and he foretells concerning a
great day that will come, so that all the idols and the groves will be taken
away, and much lamentation and beating of the breast will take place. He also
prophesies concerning the Savior and the resurrection which he works for us.
And against Gaza and Ashkelon and Ashdod and Ekron, and against Moab and
Ammon, and against Damascus, and against Nineveh, and against the Ethiopians.

HAGGAI, 10.

This one arose after the captivity, at the return, in Jerusalem, in the time
of Darius king of the Persians, in his second year, in the sixth month. The
word of the Lord came by his hand, and he prophesies to Zerubbabel and to
Joshua the son of Josedek and to the people =22.1268= concerning the building
of Jerusalem; he also prophesies concerning the passing away of the shadows,
and concerning the calling of the nations.

ZECHARIAH SON OF SADDO, 11.

This one also prophesies in the second year of Darius. But in the eighth month
the word of the Lord came to him, and an angel of the Lord spoke in him, and
he prophesies to the fathers and to Jerusalem. And he sees many and various
visions, and he prophesies concerning the Savior through many things, also
signifying that he comes upon an ass and a colt the foal of an ass.

MALACHI, 12.

This one also arose after the captivity, in the time of Darius. And a burden
of the word of the Lord by the hand of his angel upon Israel, he prophesies.
He reproaches Israel for the transgressions, and blames them for neglect of
the tithes and of sharing with the poor; he also prophesies concerning the day
of judgment and the shining forth of the Savior, and he signifies that Elijah
is sent before the coming of the Savior.

ISAIAH SON OF AMOZ.

This one and Hosea, as I said before, were contemporaries of one another; for
this one also arose in the time of Uzziah and Jotham and Ahaz and Hezekiah,
kings of Judah; and having seen a vision he prophesies against Judea and
against Jerusalem. And the word of the Lord also came to him; and the greater
part of his prophecy is gospel concerning the incarnate coming of the Word of
God, and of the things he received by suffering for us. He also prophesies
against these nations: against Babylon, against the Philistines, against
Damascus, a vision of Egypt, a vision of Idumea, a vision of the valley of
Zion, a vision of Tyre, a vision of the four-footed beasts in the wilderness.
He also recounts the invasion of Sennacherib, and the arrogance of Rabshakeh,
and their fall and destruction; and that Hezekiah, having fallen sick and
being about to die, prayed and received an addition of fifteen years. He also
prophesies to eunuchs, and to the nations at the end, concerning the day of
judgment.

JEREMIAH THE SON OF HILKIAH,

Of the priests. This one, as I said before, and Zephaniah were contemporaries
of one another; and this one remained until Jehoiakim, and until the eleventh
year of Zedekiah king of Judah, until the captivity of the people. And a word
of God came to him, as the word of the Lord was born to him; and he rebukes
the injustices of Jerusalem through many things; he prophesies against
Jerusalem, and concerning the drought; and against the nations of Elam, and of
the coming of the king of Babylon to cut off the land of Egypt; and against
Babylon; and a word which the Lord commanded Jeremiah to speak to Seraiah son
of Neriah, and to Idumea, and to the sons of Ammon, and to Kedar queen of the
court, which Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon struck, and to Damascus, and to
Moab, and against Jerusalem, and against Judah, and to the house of Rechab,
and of the sons of Jonadab, and to the Jews =22.1269= dwelling in the land of
Egypt; and to those seated in Magdol and at Taphnas, and in the land of
Pathoures. And to Baruch, when he wrote these words in the book from the mouth
of Jeremiah. And the book contains at the end that the Jews, plotting against
the prophet, threw him into a pit of mire, and Abdemelech the Ethiopian
brought him up. Why is the book inscribed Baruch? Because this one, having
written the words in the book from the mouth of Jeremiah, read them to those
taken captive in Babylon, comforting them and promising them the hope from
God. Why is the book inscribed Lamentations? Because Jeremiah lamented these
words concerning Jerusalem, when it was taken and the people were led captive.
Why is it called the Epistle of Jeremiah? Because Jeremiah wrote this to those
in the captivity, securing them against the idols, so that they might not,
seeing them, be carried away with those who worship them.

EZEKIEL SON OF BUZI THE PRIEST.

This one arose in the captivity after Jeremiah, and in the fifth year of the
captivity of Jehoiakim he began to prophesy. And the word of the Lord came to
him; and at the outset he sees a marvelous and most astonishing and hard to
interpret vision of a four-faced living creature. He prophesies against the
prophets of Israel, and concerning the wood of the vine, and a narrative
concerning the eagle, and he testifies to Jerusalem her lawless deeds and her
sins; and concerning the parable which they spoke, the fathers ate the unripe
grape, and the children's teeth were set on edge. And to the sons of Ammon,
and concerning Ohola and Oliba. And a lament over Sur, and over Sidon, and
concerning the day of the Lord, and to Pharaoh king of Egypt, and a lament
over Pharaoh and Egypt, and over mount Seir, and concerning the bones that
came to life, and against Gog and the land of Magog; and to the prince of Tyre
and a lament against him, and to the sons of his people, and against the
shepherds of Israel; and last of all he prophesies, describing the building of
the temple and of Jerusalem. Why is it called Daniel? Because he too, having
arisen in the captivity, until king Cyrus the Persian, himself judges the
matters concerning Susanna, and condemns the elders as false accusers; and he
himself both reports and interprets the dreams of the king; and he also sees
complete visions: two concerning the kingdoms, and two concerning the coming
of the Savior and the cessation of Jerusalem and the coming of the Antichrist.
He recounts the martyrdom of Shadrach, Meshach, Abednego, and their hymns; and
that they themselves were saved, but those who kindled the fire underneath
were burned, and the king changed his mind; and that he himself was thrown
into the den of lions: first, because when the Babylonians were praying to
Darius the king, he alone =22.1272= prayed to God; and second because he
overthrew Bel and slew the dragon in the time of king Cyrus, and he himself
was saved. And he himself came up from the lions out of the den. But those who
plotted against him were thrown into the den, and were immediately eaten by
them.
