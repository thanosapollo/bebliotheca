An apologetic oration of our father among the saints John of Damascus against
those who slander the holy images.

We ought always, being conscious of our own unworthiness, to keep silence and
to offer to God the confession of the things we have sinned, but since all
things are good in their season, and I see the church, which God built upon
the foundation of the apostles and prophets, Christ his son being the
cornerstone, being struck as by a sea-surge heaped up with successive waves,
being churned and troubled from a most grievous onset of the wicked spirits,
and the tunic of Christ, the one woven from above, being divided, which sons
of the impious have dared to rend, and his body being cut into different
opinions, which is the people of God and the tradition of the church that has
prevailed from above, I did not think it reasonable to be silent and to put a
bond upon the tongue, looking with fear at the threatened sentence that says:
"If you draw back, my soul has no pleasure in you," and "if you see the sword
coming and do not announce it to your brother, I will require his blood from
you." Struck therefore with unbearable fear I have come to speaking, not
setting the height of kings before the truth; "for I spoke," I heard David the
ancestor of God saying, "before kings and I was not ashamed," but rather and
rather being pricked by this toward speaking. For a king's word is terrible
toward the subjection of subjects; for few are those who from of old have made
light of the royal decrees, those who know that the king on earth is himself
ruled from above, and that the laws hold the kings.

First of all therefore, having fixed as a keel or foundation in my reasoning
the keeping of the ecclesiastical ordinance, through which salvation is
accustomed to come about, I have opened the barrier of the discourse and have
urged it on from the starting-post as a well-bridled horse. For I thought it
truly terrible and beyond terrors that the church, gleaming with so many
advantages and adorned with the traditions of the most pious men from above,
should go back to the poor elements, fearing a fear where there is no fear,
and as if not having known the true God should suspect the slip into idolatry
and should fall short even in the smallest of perfection, as bearing a
blameworthy spot in the midst of a face very much adorned, marring the whole
by the unextended of the par ascratch of the beauty; for the small is not
small, when it is carried out into a great thing, since neither is the scratch
a small thing, that a tradition of the church that has prevailed from above
should be overturned, as if those who led us beforehand were condemned, whose
manner of life we ought, considering, to imitate the faith. I therefore
beseech first the almighty Lord, to whom all things are naked and laid bare,
to whom our word is, who knows the unmixed character of my humble mind in this
and the sincerity of the purpose, to give me a word in the opening of my mouth
and to take up the reins of the mind with his own hands and to draw it to
himself, making the course toward a straight and right path, not inclining
toward the things that seem on the right or that are openly recognized as on
the left, after whom all the people of God, the holy nation, the royal
priesthood, together with the good shepherd of the rational flock of Christ,
who inscribes in himself the hierarchy of Christ, to receive my word with
goodwill, not


attending to the least of the dignity or seeking turns of words, since of
these I, the poor man, am not altogether skilled, but to take thought for the
power of the thoughts ("for the kingdom of the heavens is not in word, but in
power"); for the purpose is not to conquer, but to stretch out a hand to the
truth that is being warred against, since choice stretches out a hand of
power. Having therefore called upon the hypostatic truth as helper, from this
point I will make the beginnings of the discourse.

2 Second oration of John the humble monk of Damascus toward those who decry
the images.

Give pardon to one who asks, my masters, and receive a word of assurance from
me, the useless and least slave of the church of God. For I did not rush to
speaking for the sake of glory or of display (God is witness), but by zeal of
truth; for him alone I have as hope of salvation, and I hope and pray to meet
the master Christ together with him, offering this to him, that it may become
an atonement for the things I have unfittingly committed. For the one who
received the five talents from the master brought other five, having gained
them, and the one who received the two brought two equal in number; but the
one who received the one and buried it and brought it fruitless, having been
called a wicked slave, has been condemned into the outer darkness. Which thing
I, fearing lest I suffer it, yield to the master's command and set before you,
the wise bankers, the talent of the word given to me from him, so that when my
Lord comes he may find it multiplied and bearing interest of souls, and having
found a faithful slave may lead me into his sweetest joy that is longed for by
me. But give me an ear of hearing and, having spread open the tables of the
hearts, receive my word and discern purely the power of the things said. And I
composed this second discourse concerning images; for some of the children of
the church suggested to me to do this, because the first is not very easy for
the many to discern. But in this too pardon me who have fulfilled obedience.

3 Oration of the same holy John of Damascus toward those who reject the august
and holy images.

2,2͂3,1 It is a custom of the wicked and first-evil serpent, I mean the devil,
to war in many ways against the man who was fashioned according to the image
of God and through opposites to work his death. For immediately at the
beginning he sowed in him a hope and desire of deification and through it
brought him down to the death of the irrational things, and moreover he often
baited him also with shameful and irrational pleasures. And how great is the
diameter of deification and of irrational desire. At one time he led him into
atheism, as David the ancestor of God says: "The fool said in his heart: There
is no God," and at another time into polytheism; and at one time he persuaded
him not even to worship the God who is so by nature, and at another time he
prepared him to worship demons, and still heaven and earth, sun and moon and
stars and the rest of creation even as far as beasts and reptiles. For it is
equally hard both not to bring the due honor to the honorable and to attach
the unfitting glory to the dishonorable. Again he taught some to say that evil
is co-unoriginate with God, and he deceived others to confess that the God who
is good by nature is the cause of evil. And some he led astray to say
foolishly one nature and one hypostasis of the godhead, and others he
adulterated to worship unlawfully three natures and three


hypostases. And to some he suggested to glorify one hypostasis and one nature
of our Lord Jesus Christ, the one of the holy Trinity, and to others two
natures and two hypostases of the same. But the truth, walking a middle path,
denies all these absurd things and teaches to confess one God, one nature in
three hypostases, Father and Son and Holy Spirit. And it says that evil is not
a substance, but an accident, a certain thought and word and act contrary to
the law of God, having existence in being thought and said and done, and being
made to vanish at the same time as it ceases. And still it proclaims the one
of the holy Trinity, the Christ, two natures and one hypostasis. 3,1 But the
truth, walking a middle path, denies all the absurd things. 2,4͂3,1 But the
enemy of the truth and adversary of the salvation of men, who once, having
often deceived not only the nations but even the sons of Israel themselves to
make images of demons and of impious men and of birds and of beasts and of
reptiles and to worship these as gods, now strives to throw into confusion the
church of Christ that has peace, seasoning the wickedness with divine words
through unjust lips and a deceitful tongue and trying to cover the unseemly
and dark form of this and to shake the hearts of the unstable from the true
and father-delivered custom; for some have risen saying that one must not
image and set forth for contemplation and glory and wonder and zeal the saving
wonders and passions of Christ and the exploits of the saints against the
devil.

And who, having divine knowledge and spiritual understanding, does not
recognize that this is a suggestion of the devil? For he does not want his
defeat and his shame to be published, nor the glory of God and of his saints
to become a written record. For if we were making an image of the invisible
God, we would truly be sinning; for it is impossible that the bodiless and
unshap en and invisible and uncircumscribed be imaged. And again: if we were
making images of men and were considering these gods and were serving them as
gods, we would truly be impious. But we do none of these things. For God
having been incarnate and having been seen upon the earth in flesh and having
kept company with men through unspeakable goodness and having taken up nature
and thickness and shape and color of flesh, we do not err in making the image;
for we long to see his character; for as the divine apostle says: "We now see
in a mirror and in a riddle." And the image too is a mirror and a riddle
fitted to the thickness of our body; for the mind, laboring much, cannot go
out of the bodily things, says the divine Gregory. Away from you, envious
devil, you envy us to see the likeness of our master and through it to be
sanctified and to see his saving passions and to wonder at his condescension
and to contemplate his wonders and to recognize and glorify the power of his
godhead. You envy the saints the honor given them from God. You do not want us
to see their glory written down and to become zealots of their courage and
faith. You cannot bear the bodily and psychic benefit that has come to us from
the faith toward them. We are not persuaded by you, envious and man-hating
demon. Hear, peoples, tribes, tongues, men, women, children, elders, youths
and infants, the holy nation of the Christians: If anyone evangelizes you
besides what the catholic church received from the holy apostles and fathers
and synods and has guarded until now, do not hear him nor receive the counsel
of the serpent, as Eve received it and harvested death. Even if an angel, even
if a king evangelizes you besides what you received, shut the ears; for I
hesitate for the present to say, as the divine apostle said: "Let him be
anathema," awaiting the


correction. But those who do not search the mind of the scripture say that God

boast in their idols," and many other such things. For whatever they may bring
forward from the divine scripture and from the holy fathers, they are of the
same meaning. What then do we say toward these things? What else than what was
said by the Lord to the Jews, "search the scriptures"? For the search of the

beloved, for God to lie; for there is one God, one lawgiver of old and new
covenant, who of old spoke in many parts and in many ways to the fathers in

attention therefore with accuracy. The word is not mine. The Holy Spirit

physician does not always give the same form to all, but provides to each the

perfect according to age another; another to the sick and another to the
healthy, and to each of those who are sick not the same, but toward the habit

gods and setting aside the worship of God and bringing his glory to the
creation, forbade the making of images. For of God the bodiless and invisible

concerning image and worship, come let us distinguish accurately the discourse
concerning these. An image then is a likeness characterizing the prototype,
together with also having a certain difference toward it; for the image is not
likened according to all things toward the archetype. The living, natural and
unvarying image of the invisible God is therefore the Son, bearing the whole
Father in himself, having the identity toward him according to all things, and
differing only by the being caused. For the Father is a natural cause, and the
Son is caused; for a father is not from a son, but a son from a father. For

the same way. For the divine is unturned according to all things, and there is
not in him change or shadow of turning. These images and paradigms the holy
Dionysius, great in the divine things and having considered with God the
things concerning God, calls predefinitions. For in his will all the things
predefined by him and going to be without transgression were characterized and
imaged before their generation, just as, if someone should wish to build a
house, he first forms and images the shape according to thought. Then again
there are images of the invisible and unformed things, being formed bodily
toward a dim understanding. For the divine scripture also puts types upon God
and angels, and teaching the cause the same divine man says, that reasonably
the types of the unformed and the shapes of the unshaped have been put for
ward, one would say the cause is not only the analogy according to us, which
is unable to be stretched up immediately upon the intelligible contemplations
and needs proper and connatural anagogies. If then the divine Word, taking
thought for our analogy, providing from every side the upward-drawing for us,
even puts certain types upon the simple and unformed things, how will he not
image the things that have been formed with shapes according to their own
nature and that are longed for, but through not being present cannot be seen?
For through sense a certain imagination is constituted in the front cavity of
the brain and thus is sent on to the critical faculty and is stored in the
memory. The God-speaking Gregory at any rate also says that the mind, laboring
much, is altogether unable to go out of the bodily things; but also "the
invisible things of God from the creation of a cosmos are seen, being
understood by the things made." For we see images in the creatures indicating
to us dimly the divine manifestations; as when we say that the holy Trinity,
the one above beginning, is imaged through sun and light and ray; or a
fountain bubbling and a stream being fountained and an outflow; or mind and
word and spirit according to us; or a rose plant and flower and fragrance.
Again an image is said of the things that will be, sketching riddlingly the
future things, as the ark the holy virgin and Theotokos and the rod and the
jar, and as the serpent the one who through a cross abolished the bite of the
first-evil serpent, and the sea, the water and the cloud the spirit of
baptism. Again an image is said of the things that have happened, either a
memory of some wonder or of honor or of shame or of virtue or of vice toward
the later benefit of those who contemplate, so that we may flee the evil
things, and may zealously imitate the virtues. And this is double, both
through word written in the books, as God engraved the law on the tablets and
commanded the lives of the God-loving men to become a written record, and
through sensible contemplation, as he commanded the jar and the rod to be
placed in the ark for a


memorial. So also now we draw the images of the things that have happened and

How many manners of worship? A first manner of worship is that according to
service, which we bring to God alone who is worshipful by nature, and this
according to different manners. First according to that of slavery; for all
the creatures worship him as slaves a master, "because all things together,"
he says, "are your slaves," and some willingly, and others unwillingly. Some
worship willingly with knowledge as the pious, and others knowing and not
wishing worship unwillingly as the demons; others not knowing the God who is
so by nature worship unwillingly him whom they do not know. A second manner is
that according to wonder and longing, according to which we worship God
through his natural glory; for he alone is


glorified, not having the glory from someone, but himself being cause of all

powerful power, alone worthy through himself to be wondered at and to be
worshipped and to be glorified and to be longed for. A third manner is that of
thanksgiving over the good things that have come about unto us; for all the
things that are owe to give thanks to God and to offer an ever-flowing
worship, both because all things have the being from him and consist in him
and he imparts ungrudgingly to all of his own gifts even without asking and he
wishes all to be saved and to partake of his own goodness and he is
long-suffering upon us sinning and he makes the sun rise upon righteous and
unrighteous and he rains upon wicked and good and because the Son of God
became according to us through us and made us partakers of divine nature,
because "we shall be like him," as John the theologian says in the catholic
letter. A fourth manner is that according to need and hope of benefactions,
according to which recognizing that we are able to do nothing or to have a
good thing apart from him, we worship asking from him each, of which he senses
he has need and which he longs for, both to be delivered from evil things and
to obtain good things.

A fifth manner is that of repentance and of confession; for sinning we worship

as not to miss the benefactions of God or fearing the punishments. And the
first is from well-mindedness and longing of God himself and a filial
disposition, the second of a hired one, and the third of a slavish one. How

co-working of God, who are also truly called gods, not by nature, but by
position, as the iron that has been fired is called fire, not by nature, but

midst he will discern gods." Just as therefore they are truly gods not by
nature, but as partakers of the God who is so by nature, so they are
