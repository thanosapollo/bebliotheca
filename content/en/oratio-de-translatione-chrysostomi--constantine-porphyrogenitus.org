* Oration on the Translation of Chrysostom
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Oration of Constantine, emperor in Christ himself, the eternal King, son of
Leo the most wise and ever-memorable emperor, when the sacred and holy body of
the wise Chrysostom, brought back from exile, was laid up as some much-blessed
and much-beloved treasure in this queen and most splendid of cities. Bless,
Father. What is more delightful than the spectacle now seen, or what more
splendid than the present day? Behold a chosen people, dedicated to God alone
and to virtue, and displaying such abundance of their own good order and
seemliness, that no one may pass them by without acceptance and wonder and
praise. For what flowering meadow, luxuriant with plants of every kind and
adorned on every side with varied flowers, can so feast the eye and not permit
it to wander elsewhere, but hold the whole of sight bound, as it were, upon
itself through the beauty in it, as this sacred company, this holy nation,
this royal priesthood? And what is the manner of the gathering, and who has
thus brought us together to the same place? Or is it not plain that the people
of the Lord is assembling a triumph for the father Chrysostom as he returns,
and is preparing the procession beforehand, and for this reason there is much
rejoicing in each soul, and the trumpets of the words are piercing, as a
company worthy of the present day, as such a gathering fitting for this
splendid and beloved festival? Therefore come, let us cry out joyfully with a
great and high proclamation: blessed is God, who has counted us worthy to
arrive at this sacred and venerable festival, in which the people of God is
both sacred and holy, whom the Lord chose for himself, well painted upon the
immaterial hands of the Lord. For it is fitting that from the prophetic
foretellings we should be thus and fittingly made splendid toward those
present. Who gathered you as from a sacred appointed day, and as one might say
from a command? From where, taking the occasions of the gathering here, did
you accomplish so eager and ready an arrival? Who assembled this all-people
festival, and as from some hive of this sacred house compelled so great a
swarm of sacred bees to pour forth, and this in so fierce a sharpness of
winter? Who, as in a movement of a camp, showed the roads leading here
straitened from the multitude of those streaming in, and this in winter gloom,
when even Scythian and barbarian and as many other most warlike and combative
of the nations know to be still, being cut 307 short from untimely travel by
the sharpness from the winter, when also the sea is left empty of sailors and
of seafaring, and the farmer, having left the plough and the things fitted for
farming, richly feeds and tends the ploughing oxen at the manger: or is it not
plain that this divine and venerable festival summons all the God-loving and
Chrysostom-loving as father-loving with a splendid and rejoicing proclamation,
and that the gloom of the night, being very fittingly changed toward the
brightness of day by abundant light, is in this likened and compared, to speak
poetically, to the starry heaven? Therefore come, sacred people, chosen
portion, precious and holy nation, strongest measuring-line of the Lord, the
fair and thrice-desired sheep, who, hearing well and savingly the voice of the
teacher, are customarily fed upon life-bearing and saving pastures: weaving
the praise with me, cleanse hearing and mind of the marketplace tumults, that
we may take delight in the remembrance of his fair teachings, and may behold
the athlete's return, as from the double course, toward the turning-post of
victory. For if we have not dared to strip for the whole of the subject
because of the unattainable,


yet even the narration of the part will lead by the hand toward the
remembrance of the discourse, and from the last things we shall contemplate in
the mind the things of the beginning, as guessing the lion from the claws and
the web from the woof, or also, what is clear to all, the tree from the fruit.
The other things, then, it is possible to hear many speaking and writing, as
many as are not wholly uninitiated in the education of letters and its
mysteries and have tasted of the musical mixing-bowl. For so many marvel at
his things as almost also have a share in speech. But as many things as tend
toward the present day, and which going through I shall perhaps not seem
superfluous and troublesome, these I go through. There was a time when the
affairs of the people of the Lord were well, and freedom from faction was
citizen in the churches of God, and the love toward one another was kept and
guarded as some ancestral lot, and the field of Christ was multiplied, and all
were eager to seek out God and to walk in the paths of his divine
commandments, when this great canon of orthodoxy, the most drinkable and
ever-flowing sea of words, the acropolis of wisdom, the truly heavenly voice,
the lodging of the divine Spirit, the trumpet of the saving proclamation, the
unsetting sun of the inhabited world, the fitting glory of the church, was
illuminating the whole earthly inhabited world with a sacred firmament, and
the world was made bright with the lightning-bearing rays of his words, and
all were plucking from his well-watered and fragrant meadow the manifold and
varied flowers 308 of the virtues and were filling the folds of the mind, and
he had all well taught and fed upon life-bearing pastures and upon water of
spiritual rest. But since envy was not going to bear so great a crop of goods
meekly, from the imperial chambers the intrigues and the unjust exile were
stitched together and made ready. For the emperor's little woman, being
possessed by gold-madness and casting an eye upon the money of others and what
did not belong to her, hated him who was reproving in the gates. May the
desire of more perish from men, and the greed and prodigality that is its
companion, and the devil, the man-slayer, who from the beginning badly
exhibited it. For how many and of what kind were the soul-profiting
exhortations that this just man and truly man of God was chanting each day to
this greedy and polluted empress from divine laws and teachings, it is
possible for you who have come upon his life to learn without fail. But she,
having thrust away all understanding and shame, was ill-disposed toward the
divine and saving charms, and was rather heavily and burdensomely disposed
toward the saint, having badly deliberated in herself beforehand by folly, and
she badly let loose her mind against that blessed and holy soul. Using then
such all-shameful fellow-workers and helpers, that madwoman, being also
possessed and brought forward from evil begetters and teachings, moves against
that unassailable and venerable soul the sorceries and marvel-mongerings which
she had already gathered. And indeed the Solomonic leech and filthy one,
having as fellow-worker the partner of her bed and of her folly, you know the
things dared, the exile and the things celebrated even until now. But the
discourse must be brought back toward the turning-post of the subject. And
first she wins over the emperor's simplicity, or to speak the truth rather his
lightness, for the emperor was in truth gentle and womanish; then, having made
partners of so great a daring those who were ignobly playing the priestly
stage and badly trafficking the matter, and not only these, but also those who
had been stopped from priesthood by the judgment of John because of the
vileness of their own manners, she ratifies the unjust exile against him, and
she strips away the seemliness of the church, and subjects it to a mourning
tonsure, the new Jezebel and Delilah, having ruined and torn her own
adornment, as a shameless sow wallowing in the mire and gasping. What is the
exile? Cucusus, some desolate little town and in want of divine faith and of
fear of God, and after this Arabissus, and


after that another barbarian place, dry and graceless, besieged each day by
the neighboring Isaurians 309, named Pityous. These are the unlovely and
deadly limits of our farthest parts, held by unbelief toward God and wandering
about in certain vain opinions, assigning worship in ignorance to sun and
fire, and serving certain other counterfeit objects of worship as gods. This
great and truly precious man of God, full of knowledge of God and of faith,
shortly showed all the sons there to be sons of light through the regeneration
from water and spirit, and by the nourishing and most drinkable teaching of
his words he made those who were before useless and impious into good and
God-fearing. These are the achievements of the truly great and wonderful
sojourn and unjust exile; these are the wrestlings of the second Job, and the
outcome of the business concerning him. For it was a contest of virtue, of him
how he might pluck out this overweening and raging second idolatry, that is
love of money, and of her how she might repay the saint with a wicked
recompense. For she was not even able to see this, being weighed down at the
time of the madness as by some yoke by the evil one, and having the correction
hard to call back. The impious then was prevailing, the pious was being driven
out, and the affairs of the churches were in a terrible night-battle; envy
seemed to be setting up the trophies of its own wickedness and to be shouting
the victory-songs. But when, to speak apostolically, having finished the
course and kept the faith, after those many sweats and toils and the great
hardship of the successive exiles, the King and crown-giver Jesus, my Christ
and God, was calling him to himself, his own athlete and victory-bearer, to
receive the prizes of the many labors: here what happens, and what just
wonder-working is to be related to God-loving ears. He sends forth to him
again Peter and John, the foremost and precious of the apostles, announcing as
good news the rest from the many and long toils and contests, strengthening
the champion, sealing the proclamation, applauding the victory-rites, the
rewards of victory, the prizes, the unfading crowns, the glory which he would
inherit with them, signifying beforehand the bitter death of the accursed
little woman through the eating of worms, as most hateful, as to be spat upon,
as worthy of dogs and of their embracing, in return for the things in which
she sinned against him, feeding him with ambrosia. They keep him without food
until the time of the going-forth toward God and of the departure. And again
after these things another wonder and worthy of history: for when some three
months had passed, the fair-victorious martyrs Basiliscus and Lucian announce
as good news 310 the royal glory of the victory and the going-forth toward the
crown-giver Christ and God and his being ranked with them in his own tomb. The
athlete then departs from the things below, having committed the spirit to the
hand of God, and his enduring holy body, wasted by many sweats, is buried
together in the tomb of the martyrs, and having shown his own blessed one,
through endurance of the toils that came upon him, not far from martyr's
honor. And that the one praised is also worthy of the choir above, and that
that blessed and holy soul, that world-wide and most precious good, the
guidance of the whole world toward the fair, and a kind of light-leading and
illumination, was allotted the way of life as a counterpart of his own toils
and contests, and obtained the nearest standing and rank, I mean that with
angels and where the dwelling of those purified from virtue is, it is possible
not unfittingly from certain still remaining in the flesh and having imagined
the truth, that the lamp of virtue not be hidden under the bushel of the
counterfeit and, as one might say, untrue supposition of some and escape
notice, and to show more brightly than the sun to those who limp and even if
they are disputable the truth that truly concerns the one unlearnedly
supposed. One of the friends, and especially of those who had loved him,


Adelphius by name the man, who also received him when he had been carried into
exile in a manner of compassion, and the man was notable by distinction among
those honored with the episcopate. And a certain reasoning was wearing him and
consuming him, not outside what is fitting, the biting reasoning suggesting
this, pricking and shaking, and seeking with all eagerness to learn of what
glory then and honor my dearest and precious John, and of what rank he
obtained, for whom there are many toils through words and writings, and a
whole world enlightened and turned toward virtue and divine zeal. Thus, when
the said Adelphius was being pressed and worn by the reasoning, that he might
also perceive some more divine assistance, and more intensely both applying
himself to the supplication and persevering in the prayers and begging, he
seemed somehow to have gone out of himself, and, a certain youth in white
taking him by the hand, to go forward to the heavenly things, to lead him to a
place full of most splendid light, where the whole choir of the true high
priests who had been made at home with God was dwelling. And again the same
perplexity and confusion, the desired one not being seen together. And when he
had also mentioned him by name and he was not there, and thereafter being
gloomy he was being sent away full of distress and dejection, not seeing
together the thrice-desired thing, in which he had hoped, the one truly golden
both in words and in deeds, another youth of the same appearance, serving in
this aforementioned many-lighted 311 house that had been seen before, having
seen the dejection of the face, was asking to learn the manner. But he was not
counting him worthy of an answer, being held by grief. And when the other was
more vehemently pressing and seeking to learn what was causing so great
distress, and at the same time that it was not even just to go away grieving,
this only he said, still wandering and somehow groaning, that, he says, I have
not seen John the bishop of Constantinople dwelling together with those
present. And the other immediately taking it up said: And do you think to see
this one of those in the body wherever he is, and of what rank he has
partaken? And that most blessed nature has not stood by, and it is bold to
speak beyond man. Having taken this most true answer of the reasoning that
until then was doubting and tyrannizing, this sacred Adelphius was glorifying
God, who multiplies the recompenses of the things faithfully hoped and honors
with the highest graces and those of the mind's grasping. But when the divine
justice justly judged that madwoman and persecutress and condemned her to end
her life pitiably by the eating of worms, so that there was not even room for
lamentations because of the continuousness of her own evils, but also the body
last is struck by God with a blow both incurable and hard to look upon, she
paying back what she had seized and being triumphed over for what she had done
to the saint, and Theophilus having fallen into a stony disease and such as
are the sufferings from the kidneys and having violently broken off living,
and the remaining accusers, who had prepared the exile of the saint, in turn
exchanging life for death by various blows and God-sent diseases, and the
emperor Arcadius himself having drunk the common cup of death owed to all, and
Theodosius, his child, having inherited the ancestral scepters, the time
between was putting forward by divine vote as high priest of God a man both
sacred and holy, reared on the teaching-milk of the great John. And it was
Proclus, who of old, while he was still living, had served him in the most
mystical things. In the fourth year then of his high-priesthood, the remaining
heart of the church, as some ember of memory, having been moved by the divine
Spirit and boiled up, when it saw the world as it were taking hold of and
shining with clear weather, and the clouds of envy scattered, which is
accustomed to be raised against what stands against it, but toward what is out
of the way to slacken and wither, when the praiseworthy deed, being
contemplated in itself, makes the worker to be marveled at. Reckoning then
this time suitable for frankness for themselves and for release from
difficulties, they were making an all-people embassy to the one presiding over
the sacred affairs, begging to persuade the emperor's


mind to soften this man and, even if late, to dissolve the exile for the
common father and teacher 312 of all who had died, and to free them from
orphanhood by granting them his sacred dust and the remnant of the virtuous
form as some gold-bearing treasure, that in place of the milk of teaching that
had gushed for them from him they might let loose upon him the rivers of
tears, and that there might come to be for them from this also some
sanctification and fulfillment of the fair desire. The priest then was at the
very imperial palaces, and the things of the embassy were being accomplished,
and the all-people entreaty of the children was being made clear, the high
priest saying to the emperor and suggesting those things which know even to
break the hard-cut rocks and to urge on and to stir up and fan toward a divine
love the precious and venerable return of the saint's body. Hearing these
things then the emperor is warmed in heart and does not stand against the
request, but reckoning this a time of good repute for himself and of divine
acceptance and of praise from men and of most genuine affection he was making
ready toward the taking-up, and all things were being accomplished for the
pious purpose. When then, having been furnished with the emperor's letters,
they stood at the city that had the much-valued treasure, as had been
commanded, and nothing was thought able to stand against, for the heirs of the
possession were yielding to an imperial hand and were being pained and
disheartened and distressed, but they were not able to stand against and not
yield, here the power of the great one was being recognized: for both honoring
those who had obtained and wrapped him, and showing to those trying to
transfer him by authority that they do not have authority over all, not as in
being persecuted he was obedient, imitating the Christ who does not strive nor
cry out at all, so also toward the recall was he becoming able to be forced
against his will, but he was as it were grown onto the unmoved coffin and
wholly hard to tear away or hard to bring up toward the transfer; for the body
of the saint is said to have been turned to stone, and by difficulty of
movement it seemed to have been changed into the weight of a stone. The
emperor's servants then were at a loss, the ministers of the command were
disheartened, the heirs were considering, having the rejoicing of the soul
evident. These things are reported to the emperor, they are made known to the
high priest, the cause of the delay is recognized by the counsel of those in
office from the ruler himself. It was giving a suspicion that he was standing
against the recall from command, not remembering evil against those who had
grieved him. The form of the mission that had seemed contrary is changed, the
letters are exchanged toward supplication. That emperor himself with his own
hand writes out the petition. To the ecumenical teacher and spiritual father
John, the holy and golden-mouthed father and patriarch. We beseech, father, in
a slavish 313 manner as well-disposed, we do not command as emperors as also
being lords of you; we call you as master, we do not force you as a subject.
We are moved by the affection of genuine children; show a father's compassion.
Give back your dead to those to whom you also formerly gave yourself. You will
do injustice if you delay still more. If you were giving yourself to those who
were going to be ungrateful toward you, for the most clear-sighted mind was
not ignorant of the things that would overtake, will you deprive of your
protection those who are eager to render you the honors that are due? And when
with the emperor's petition and the all-night hymns and prayers they brought
the former attempt to the coffin, it was to be seen, O the wonder, that holy
body again receiving its natural composition and lightness, the transfer easy
and the moving very ready, as of someone changing house in a movement of
choice. And this also is said as the wonder, that the saint lightened himself
for those conveying him toward the imperial coffin. As then those who have
taken a city of ten thousand men and are leading down a great triumph have
many streaming together toward the spectacle, so also those bringing down that
divine body had a countless crowd escorting. One being far off, having seen
the


multitude of the lamps, might have guessed that stars, having changed the
position lying above in a cloudless state and becoming near the earth, truly
enclose a moon and keep the form of a chorus moving in good order, having the
just man in the midst as some chorus-leader. And when, as one came nearer, the
psalmody fell upon the hearing, he would have seemed to behold an image of the
incarnation of the Word of God upon earth, according to which the choir of the
angels was crying Glory in the highest. So great was the escort and procession
about the great John, so great a crowd was streaming together from everywhere.
What return then of an emperor who had subdued countless nations and taken
walled cities by force was escorted with such honor? Or who had so many
running together toward the spectacle? When then they arrived at the acropolis
opposite this imperial of the cities, it was to be seen that the strait in the
midst of these was being thickened as by some fleet with the small
merchant-ships and boats and skiffs hastening toward the meeting. Nor was
there any other trade for the ferrymen, nor was the ship seen being carried
elsewhere. For with the emperor the senate-council also was crossing, and with
the high-priestly polity the whole allotted people was going out together. Nor
did the continual staying at home hold the women at that time, but many,
having then first dared a male sight, were going out to the meeting, believing
they would partake of sanctification even from the sight alone. 314 A trireme
of the emperors was ready, which would receive the one already reigning with
God. And again even here he magnified the entrance with the wonders of God:
for suddenly a violent squall having been raised, and the hollow of the sea
having been split in a violent south wind, and a great swell having been
raised, and the waves colliding disorderly and having much tossing and tumult,
the other ships were being thrown this way and that as each happened, having
run aground in certain hollows or also having been saved toward harbors. But
the one bearing the just man, the cables having been broken, being steered as
by a hand by the power of the one being borne, was moored at the field of the
widow, which the tyrant empress had badly seized from the poor little woman,
as I think God showing even to those who have less of shrewdness and of mind
the cause of this exile. Or rather not even having died did the just man cease
from the advocacy and from vindicating the little woman who had been abused,
but as some unlying witness called from afar for a showing of boundaries he
set his own vote and was witnessing with a voice better than tongue, whose the
plot of land was. And when the will concerning this itself had been
accomplished, that the field be given back at once to the widow, the sea was
immediately calmed, and it was spread for the swell, and as it is wont to
happen in these things, when a calm is purpled and plays upon the shores
something sweet and gentle. And as saved from some shipwreck the little boats
were being gathered from everywhere and were escorting as a flagship the one
bearing that precious coffin. And thus her seemly adornment and the truly
imperial diadem was given back to the queen city. And these things in these.
But the things now are another splendor and unapproachable light and joy
pouring through the hearts of the faithful. For day just now smiling, and the
Chrysostomic and sun-shining body sending forth splendid rays upon our city,
the temple of the ever-memorable apostle Thomas, lying toward the sea, which
the city knows to name of Amantius, receives so great a good. And being
honored through the whole night with all-night hymnodies and praises, and
having received many greetings with tears, as one truly having been treated
ungratefully and abused contrary to likelihood, he receives those who seemed
to have done injustice and are being reconciled, approaching with reverence
and a certain shame; so also the high priest of God first was receiving the
one himself steering the power, this both as honoring and magnifying a father
and sponsor who had come to be from the divine washing, and this as begging
off the just wrath of the mother according to the flesh


and bringing many-formed petitions that the exaction of the things 315 in
which she had failed toward him be loosed for her, and that the shaking of her
coffin be stopped, which for thirty-five years unyieldingly was being shaken
as in an earthquake, which as shaking the church together she had already for
so many years been shaken according to the likeness of Cain and having
changed. And indeed he also obtains the petitions, as from the manifest it is
possible to infer the unmanifest. See here the most warm purpose of the
Christ-loving emperor: for he also converses with that holy and thrice-desired
relic as with one living. For it was to be seen a wonder truly surpassing
every thought. For as if the senses were just now at rest he was reckoning the
saint to be sleeping sweetly. And indeed he also spreads the imperial cloak
above that sacred body, as making the truly autocratic rule of the Romans
subject to him, hoping at once both to be sanctified through it and the power
of the Romans. Then after him also the chosen priesthood. There was much
pushing both of those in office and of the people toward coming near and only
touching the sacred-bearing coffin. Then with abundant light and ungrudging
fragrances of incense and spices and with the songs of the hymns lifted up and
with piercing voices, triumphing as it were through the midst of the city, he
is led away to the great and catholic church of Peace. And having been set
upon his own throne by the sacred Proclus, and the whole city having cried out
with much gladness and rejoicing, Enjoy your throne also and the people, whom
you enlightened with your teachings, high priest proposed by God: then thus by
the imperial command of the autocrat that sacred body was laid reverently and
at the same time auspiciously upon an imperial chariot, as a sun driven in the
fiery chariot. And if also Proclus was a fellow-rider with the wise
Chrysostom, laboring about his holy body and serving, thanks to the fair
teacher who both chose him for discipleship and showed him a lawful successor
of both throne and virtue. The ever-memorable Proclus then, having set before
himself that much-drawing chest of the saint's relic, is settled toward the
famous and most beautiful temple of the divine apostles. And thus having been
lifted upon the sacred seat and having been established even here upon the
divine throne, and having mystically moved the closed lips and having given
peace to the flock, as was both said and believed by those counted worthy to
see such things more clearly, he was laid in the coffin of rest, and here this
inviolable good has been stored up as treasure for us, and now it is for
emperors and rulers, high priests and clergy and the people a much-valued
wealth, an unceasing heirloom, an inalienable treasure, a fountain of
healings, an ever-flowing river of teachings, a foundation of orthodoxy, a
pillar of churches, a fortification 316 and adornment and crown of boasting of
the great city that received him. For it was not right that she who is
directed by so many fair things and is made splendid by so many statues of
virtue should as it were be maimed about the more vital and be stripped of her
own adornment, and put on a squalid and mourning robe, seeing him who had
arrayed her as a bride with the beauty of words dwelling overlooked according
to the exile. And when the divine and unbloody sacrifice had been completed
according to custom, Proclus the all-memorable, with the priestly catalogue
and the emperor Theodosius being present at the things being performed and the
whole senate-council assembled to the same, turn toward the burial of that
God-bearing and truly undefiled relic. And again a wonder in a wonder and
going beyond reasonings and as it were extraordinary: for a certain man held
by arthritic disease and for many years paralyzed in hands and feet from it
and as some other paralytic laid upon a bed and having despaired of all
salvation and all but expiring, they brought and were wiping him upon this
precious relic of the blessed one. And the paralyzed man himself with tears
beseeching the saint, the fitting things came to be, that he not miss the
healing, so that all who had seen sent up glory to God who had granted such
grace


to this venerable and holy father Chrysostom. And with his own hands the
patriarch lays his own father in spirit and teacher in the memorial of rest,
in the innermost parts of the sacred and apostolic precinct, the disciple and
apostle of Christ to be altogether with the disciples and apostles of Christ.
For this reason the desired is given back to those who desire, the
child-loving father to the most faithful children, the all-knowing teacher to
the subjects, the protector to the orphans, the one advocating freely to those
being wronged, the great helper to those being abused, the one winging desire
toward God to those often enslaved to pleasures, the guide of repentance to
those who have slipped into the pit of vice, the pattern of self-control to
those overcome by satiety and luxury, the one prevailing everywhere over all,
the one carrying off from everyone what is conceded in all things, the one
falling short of none of those before him in every kind of virtue and
education, the one who has conceded to none of those after him to come near
himself, unless also they fall very far short by the excess of virtue, the one
who has left no depth of the hidden mysteries of God unsearched, the one who
has wonderfully opened and unfolded the whole depth of Scripture and as it
were brought it into light and truly written it out threefold. My heart blooms
and the mind wells 317 and longs to keep festival and desires rather to speak.
Come then, complete for me the funeral song of hymns for him, as many as have
partaken of his fair things and marvel at his things. You emperors, him who
makes your souls more splendid with the all-golden streams of his tongue than
the body with the flower of the purple and the beauty of pearls and the diadem
of precious stones. You priests, the straight canon of the high-priestly
constitution; those about words, him who has hidden away all the pleasant of
words in all things. You rich, the best steward of the stored-up and
superfluous moneys for you. You poor, the provider of the necessary and the
one loosing the want. Those in mourning the consolation, those in misfortunes
the comfort, those who have been dashed down by despair toward the depth of
destruction the recall. Him who became all things to all, to speak according
to Paul, the great apostle, that he might gain all or the more. Therefore from
this well-watered meadow of the goods of the soul let us pluck the roses of
virtue and love as most sweet, furnishing the praises from soul and from
ardent affection, and saying at the same time and thus naming a mighty shelter
and a fortified palace, a living heirloom of friendship, a garden enclosed, a
fountain sealed, and a paradise of delight, opened and shut in due season, a
harbor of calm and of refreshment for those tossed by storms, the ever-flowing

see the rewards of his toils. Remember all his sweetest and most pleasant
teachings. I know this and am conscious that you will always remember, and of
what sort is your affection about the common father and benefactor, and how
you have hung upon this affection and love, and this only you breathe and are
refreshed. See of what sort I am, sharing both the zeal and the love of this
wonderful and praiseworthy father and teacher; would that we might also become
obedient to the sacred toils which he proposes to us through his God-inspired
oracles and to the more temperate way of life. And may it be, Master of mercy
and Lord of all comfort, that both the whole world and before all we possess
this only as joy, the common hope, the inalienable exultation, the unfailing
memory, the soul-nourishing; whom if we do not honor and love and hold to this
saving teaching, what other shall we cherish, or whom of those better and
leading up toward zeal of virtue shall we follow, or through whom shall we
find the hoped-for goods? Yes, children of a noble and honor-loving polity of
the blessed city, most fortunate


and nurslings of her who honors the precious things with words and with
all-people feasts and festivals, Christ-loving, 318 apostle-loving,
martyr-loving, Chrysostom-loving, the sum of all the most precious, breathing
their honors and festivals, disposing these auspiciously, turning about in
these. For I know your all-night standings and vigils, the psalmodies and the
sacred hymns, the hearing of the oracles of the Spirit and besides these also
of our divine and wise Chrysostom, and the ascent toward God or going-forth
and change of place, the divine both offspring and problems of that great
mind. For this reason one must be persuaded by his suggestions: for this is
truly the luminary of the inhabited world, holding the word of life in the
world, the pillar of the churches, the bulwark of the faithful, the unerring
guide, the fair farmer of our souls, the one who even only being remembered is
pleasure and sweetness, whom we so especially admire and upon whom we have so
hung and have so bound our souls with the memory concerning him, as hanging
from some golden chain of unalloyed and faithful disposition. Most well, so
great a fair thing having been granted us for enjoyment. He who has remembered
Chrysostom has held off from all the base; for that blameless and God-pleasing
life of his has sanctified and honored, wherefore also by us he is always
loved and honored and counted blessed. If anything fair is citizen in the life
of men, if anything worthy of solemnity, if anything holding to a most
God-beloved way of life, from this it has been found and achieved. The world
looking toward God would have been empty of every good, if we had not obtained
the fair root of the wise herald, the unerring guide, the unset and most
splendid light. The spirit is eager and rejoices and as it were the affection
boils around and stands up, and the discourse does not wish to be drawn away
from the memory concerning him. Would that it were somehow possible even only
to pass through by virtue toward him. Would that it were possible to be with
the desired one and to count this only joy and enjoyment; for what is sweeter
even than only to remember him? And what more delightful than to have him
through the tongue? Thus managing ourselves wisely we shall more graciously
meet with the expected salvation. But O all-golden mouth of Christ, O heavenly
trumpet of the Spirit, O beholder and herald of the beginningless Father, the
interpreter of the Holy Trinity and true evangelist, the one immediately
interceding and ambassadoring for us to the holy and thrice-sunned Godhead,
ask from God peace for the churches which you enlightened with your teachings,
that these festivals may be kept more generously for us. Let not greed, which
is a second idolatry, lift a head against us. Let not a tare of gold-madness
having been raised choke the wheat of your communicative teaching toward those
in need. Let not some rock come to be against 319 us, showing the hardness of
vice, lacking the richness of your humility, and let the fair and rich field
of your seeds be shown rootless. We have need of the benefit from you and we
seek the ungrudging abundance of your many and great benefactions. Be
ambassador on behalf of your holy flock toward the common God and King, O
precious and man equal in weight to the whole world, that our failings be
loosed by your prayers. You have many ambassadoring together with your
greatness; take these as fellow-ambassadors. Gather the choir of the wise
apostles and with these beseech, as being of like manner with these. Remind
them of the things they suffered ill on behalf of the churches, on behalf of
which they bore the dangers and wore the chains, on behalf of which they were
also maimed of members and were violently taken from living, on behalf of
which they passed through fire and water, to speak prophetically. We look with
suspicion at the snares brought in upon us from the evil one. We expect
something of the troublesome, this of the wicked thought giving birth, lest
perhaps an idol of sin having been formed in us thrust us far from your
teaching. For even if you have passed beyond life and as some immaterial and
heavenly one you minister and glorify God with angels, yet you know that
slippery and


straight-running toward the contrary the human nature is. Do not forget your
children, whom you reared lovingly with the milk of your teachings. For we,
even if we pursue something of the better and God-beloved virtues, reckon the
benefaction to you. And we ask also toward the future that you well direct us
and lead us through upon life-bearing and saving pastures of the divine
commandments. And ask from God for emperors a calm power in the subjection of
the hostile, for high priests a peaceful constitution in the overturning of
scandals, for the subject the abundance of the goods within and of those
without, that the lamps of our soul, which the love of wisdom has kindled, may
make the churches bright, that these most beautiful temples may partake of the
fitting adornment by the swarm of those streaming together, and that all,
dwelling in the meadows of virtue and flowered about with the unfading crowns
plaited from there, having been made fair beyond stone and sapphire, having
been made to shine beyond lychnite and emerald, and by the whiteness of the
virtues having been made milk-white as pearls and having gleamed, may appear
bright brightly to the fair bridegroom of the pure souls, Christ our God,
because through him salvation has flowered for us all, now and unto the ages
of the ages. Amen.
