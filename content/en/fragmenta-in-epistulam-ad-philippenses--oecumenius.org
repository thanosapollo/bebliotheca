* Fragments on the Epistle to the Philippians
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Phil 1:19 And he says salvation is the martyrdom as good as already. Phil 1:24
And according to another you will understand the more necessary; that to lead
more to Christ through the faith more made him himself at home with Christ.
Phil 2:2 Or he also speaks concerning the doctrine. Phil 3:8 You can also
according to another understand the law as dung; for dung is a beast-like food,
and the law then after the faith is a keeping for more irrational men. And why
altogether have you thought it a loss and dung, O Paul? because for a long time,
he says, it harmed me sitting beside the letter of Christ, not that he was now
harmed from it, for he knew to use it spiritually, but he speaks concerning that
loss 453 of before his faith; for even after the loss that harmed us has passed,
still remembering it we call it a loss. For see how safely he said: I think it a
loss because of the exceeding of the knowledge of Christ, as if he said: by
nature it is not a loss, but since supposedly keeping it I did not recognize
Christ, it became a loss to me. For if, he says, someone should set beside the
bodily keeping of the law toward the knowledge of Christ, he will find the law a
loss; so great is the difference, he says, of the knowledge of Christ. Phil 3:14
And we read in certain copies: unto the prize of the unblamedness of God in
Christ Jesus. And the mind of the word there would be such: the prize is a prize
of unblamedness, so that the one who has taken hold of the thing pursued and
obtained the aim and taken the prize in no way falls into a charge from God.
Phil 3:19 He well said the end destruction; for if, he says, they remain such
until the end; for it was likely they would also be changed. Phil 3:21 And see
how much like-mindedness of the holy Trinity he knows, that having set above
that the Son makes the change of our body, now he says the Father does this. And
then, if we understand thus, is it not daring that the Son will do this
according to his power, that in which he subjected all things to his own body?
but if we understand thus, there is fear lest we seem to divide after the union
the one incarnate nature of the God Word.
