Of Saint Athanasius, Bishop of Alexandria, Letter to Amun the monk.
A. Concerning one who has a seminal emission.
All the works of God are good and clean; for the Word of God has made
nothing useless or unclean: "For we are a fragrance of Christ among
those who are being saved," according to the Apostle. But since the
darts of the devil are varied and of many kinds, and he prepares those
who are simpler in mind to be troubled, and he also hinders the
brethren from their accustomed exercise, sowing among them thoughts of
uncleanness and defilement, come, 64 in a few words, by the grace of
our Saviour, let us also drive away the deceit of the evil one, and
strengthen the mind of the simpler. "For all things are clean to the
clean, but to the unclean both the conscience and all things have been
defiled." I admire the sophism of the devil, that being himself
corruption and ruin, he suggests thoughts that seem to be of purity,
yet what is happening is an ambush rather than a trial. For so that,
as I said before, he may occupy the ascetics away from their customary
and saving study, and may seem to prevail in this, he stirs up certain
buzzings of this sort, which bring nothing useful to life, but empty
questions and idle talk, which one must refuse. For what sin or
uncleanness, tell me, beloved and most devout man, does a natural
discharge have? As if someone should wish to make a charge also of the
mucus sent out through the nostrils and the spittle through the mouth;
and we have still more than these to name, the discharges through the
belly, which have been established as necessary to the living creature
for life. 65 And further, "if we believe that man is a work of the
hands of God," according to the divine Scriptures, how could some
defiled work have come to be from a clean power? And if "we are the
offspring of God," according to the divine Acts of the Apostles, we
have nothing unclean in ourselves. For then only are we defiled, when
we work the most foul-smelling sin; but when some natural discharge
happens involuntarily, then by the necessity of nature, together with
the rest, as we said before, we undergo this also. But since those who
wish only to contradict what is rightly said, or rather what has been
made by God, also bring forward a Gospel saying, that "not the things
that enter defile the man, but the things that go out," we must of
necessity also refute this unreason of theirs, for we would not call
it a question. For first they force the Scriptures according to their
own ignorance, 66 being unstable; but the divine oracle stands thus:
for when some again, like these, were in doubt about foods, he
himself, resolving their ignorance, or rather publishing their deceit,
says that "the things that enter do not defile the man, but the things
that go out"; then he adds also from where they go out, "the things
from the heart." For he knows that there are the evil treasures of the
profane thoughts and of the other sins. And more briefly the Apostle,
having been taught this, says: "Food will not commend us to God"; and
one might now reasonably say also, a natural discharge will not
present us for punishment. And perhaps even the disciples of
physicians, so that they may be put to shame even from things outside,
will speak in defense of this, that certain necessary outlets have
been given to the living creature to send away 67 the surplus
according to the parts that are nourished in each of us: for example,
the superfluities of the head, hair, and the watery things sent from
the head, and the voidings of the belly, and likewise that superfluity
of the seminal passages. What sin then is there, before God, O most
God-beloved elder, when the Master himself who formed the living
creature willed and made these members to have such outlets? But since
we must anticipate the objections of the wicked, for they might say:
then neither is the true use a sin, if the organs have been fashioned
by the Creator; let us stop them on this by a question, saying: What
use do you mean? the lawful one, which God also permitted, saying,
"Increase and multiply and fill the earth"; which the Apostle also
accepted, saying, "Marriage is honorable and the bed undefiled," or
the common one, 68 yet performed secretly and in adultery? For in the
other things that happen in life we shall also find the differences
coming about according to circumstance: for example, it is not
permitted to murder, but in wars to slay the adversaries is both
lawful and worthy of praise; thus those who have excelled in war are
also counted worthy of great honors, and monuments of them are raised
proclaiming their achievements. So that the same thing in one respect
and at one time is not permitted, but in another respect and in due
season is allowed and granted. The same account, then, holds also
concerning union. "Blessed is he who in youth, being freely yoked,
uses nature for the begetting of children"; but if toward
wantonness, the punishment written by the Apostle will receive
"fornicators and adulterers." For there being two ways in life
concerning these things, one more moderate and of this life, I mean
69 marriage, and the other angelic and unsurpassed, that of virginity,
if someone should choose the worldly one, that is marriage, he has no
blame, yet he will not receive so great gifts; for he will receive,
since he too bears "the fruit of thirtyfold"; but if someone should
embrace the pure and supra-worldly way, even if the road is rough
compared with the first and hard to accomplish, yet it has more
wonderful gifts, for it has sprouted the perfect fruit, the
hundredfold. So that their unclean and wicked questions have had their
own solutions, already long ago resolved also from the divine
Scriptures. Strengthen, then, O father, the flocks under you,
exhorting from the apostolic words, guiding the soul from the Gospels,
counseling from the Psalms, saying, "Give me life according to your
word," and his word is to worship him from a clean heart. For knowing
this, 70 the same prophet, as if paraphrasing himself, says: "Create
in me a clean heart, O God," so that filthy thoughts may not trouble
me; again David: "And establish me with a governing spirit," so that
even if thoughts should ever throw me into confusion, some strong
power from you may establish me, being as it were a foundation. Do you
yourself, then, counseling these and such things, say to those who are
slower to be persuaded by the truth: "I will teach the lawless your
ways"; and trusting in the Lord that you will persuade them to abstain
from such wickedness, sing: "And the ungodly will turn to you." And
may those who inquire with evil intent cease from such vain labor, and
those who through simplicity are in doubt be strengthened "with a
governing spirit." But as many of you as know the truth firmly, hold
it unbroken and unshaken, in Christ Jesus our Lord, with whom to the
Father be glory and might, with the Holy Spirit, unto the ages. Amen.
