* On the Fast of David
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

On fasting, and on David, and on elders, and on Joseph, and against
Naucratius.
=62.759=
The blessed David, suggesting to us a member that is catholic and not partial,
came forward to sing: I will confess you, Lord, with my whole heart; I will
declare all your wonders. O the piety of the blessed David, who stumbled and
did not despise! For even if he fell as a man slipping, yet as a man he made
confession. I will confess you, Lord, with my whole heart. For if I do not
confess you, I am not delivered from my sins. I will confess you, Lord, with
my whole heart. For if I do not confess you, I shall not be made white as
snow. I will confess you, Lord. For if I do not confess you, the devil,
playing at murder and adultery, does not depart from me. I will confess you,
Lord. For if I do not confess you, I do not return to my former honor. I will
confess you, Lord. For if I do not confess you, I cannot hear "your father
according to the flesh." I will confess you, Lord. For if I do not confess
you, the Holy Spirit suggests nothing to me, and no one endures to learn the
fiftieth psalm. It is fitting then for each one who comes to holy baptism to
say the words of the blessed David: I will confess you, Lord, with my whole
heart. For if I do not confess you, I do not strip off the old man. I will
confess you, Lord. For if I do not confess you, I do not put on the new man. I
will confess you, Lord. For if I do not confess you, I shall not be numbered
with the flock of the faithful. I will confess you, Lord, with my whole heart.
For I was persuaded by your saying, Be not ashamed to confess your sins. I
will confess you, Lord, with my whole heart. For I heard you saying, I came
not to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance. And timely too are the
words of the blessed Paul. What are they? Now is the acceptable time. What
time? The adornment of the fast. What time? The illumination of love for the
poor. What time? The acquisition of repentance. Now is the acceptable time,
now the day of salvation. The same things the Ecclesiastes too, writing, says:
A time to weep over sin, and a time to laugh in salvation. To laugh, not to
mock; for one laughter is reckless, and another laughter profitable. And that
I am not lying in saying these things, take the proof from the divine
Scriptures themselves. Concerning the one who laughs recklessly, hear Wisdom
saying: A fool does evil in laughter; and concerning the one who laughs with
understanding and profitably, hear Job saying: Every gift of the impious he
shall not receive, but a truthful mouth will astonish laughter. Well then did
the Ecclesiastes say: A time to weep, and a time to laugh. A time to weep over
sin, and a time to rejoice in righteousness; =62.760= for righteous is the
Lord, and he has loved righteousnesses. A time to break up enmity, and a time
to join love; obeying the blessed Paul who says, Let not the sun go down upon
your wrath. A time to root out impiety, and a time to plant piety. All these
things too the Lord our God, showing them to Jeremiah the prophet concerning
the thorn-bearing land of the Jews, said to him: Behold, I set you today to
root up and to plant, to dig up and to build: to root up polytheism, and to
plant monarchy; to dig up and to build; to dig up the Greek shrines, and to
build the martyrs' oratories. But the "Today I set you" do not take as a fixed
day, but as for ever and always, as the blessed Paul says: Today if you hear
his voice, harden not your hearts, as in the provocation, that is, "on
whatever day you hear the Lord saying, Repent, for the kingdom of heaven is at
hand," hear it as: Harden not your hearts, as those called Cathari did. These
things hold not only before baptism and under the law, but also after baptism
and under grace. For these very things the Master Christ, delivering them to
his own disciples in other words, said: Amen, amen, I say to you, whatsoever
you shall bind on earth shall be bound in heaven; and he made his own
disciples accountable not simply for certain words, but for deeds, to loose
and to bind. Look first at the blessed Peter, how he bound to death those who
agreed in the lie, Ananias and Sapphira; and how he loosed the affliction of
the man lame from his birth all at once, granting him his feet. What will the
Cathari say to these things, they who are purer than the pure, and more
defiled than all defilement? For no one is clean from filth, not even if his
life on earth were one day. What then will the many say to these things, those
holding the vainglorious thought, daring to say with a mouth made foolish: I
have leave to approach this elder and to confess to him my sins, he who has
often stumbled more than I; how can this man, being a man and a sinner, wipe
out my sins? Such excuses do those who are in sins make, who lift themselves
up in contempt. But, O man, you who plot against yourself with your boastful
and deceitful words, who embitter God by your folly and haughtiness: do you
not approach a man, as if a man forgave you your sins? You approach the Master
and King of all, God, who through the priest grants you pardon for your
transgressions. For the priest is a mediator, not an authority. Why then do
you judge and abhor him whom God does not condemn? He divides the precious
body and performs the priestly rite over the unspotted blood. So long as he is
a priest, =62.761= and God permits him to perform the priestly rite, do not
refuse to receive from him the gift; for it is not he himself who provides you
the forgiveness of sins, but Christ the great high priest, who entrusted to
him the priesthood. And that not only under grace, but also before grace, God
wrought salvation for men through a man (for this reason our Master became
man, the mediator of God and men): you know accurately the history of the
blessed David, who just now said, I will confess you, Lord, with my whole
heart; how the same man, having fallen into murder and adultery, the extreme
of evils, remained unconfessed. What then does God do, that he may bring even
this man to repentance, and make those thereafter to embrace repentance? He
sends to him Nathan the prophet, announcing the things concerning him in a
stranger's person. For the prophet remembered, it says, came to David and
said: King, I have something to say to you. And David said: Say. And the
prophet Nathan: A certain man was rich, having many flocks and herds; and a
certain man was poor, having one ewe lamb, which ate from his table, and drank
from his cup, and slept in his bosom: calling David the rich man, and Uriah
the poor. What then? There came, he says, a stranger to the rich man. Who is
the stranger? The thought of desire, which casts us into punishment. There
came a stranger to the rich man; and the rich man, sparing his own sheep, sent
and took the poor man's lamb, and slew it for the stranger. The king David,
hearing this, even if he had fallen into sin, yet still put on the just thing;
for the sin was of the body, but the good deed of the soul. Whence answering
in anger he said: As the Lord lives, worthy of death is the man who has done
this, and the lamb he shall repay sevenfold. David exceeds the law; for the
law commands fourfold, but he commands sevenfold. And Nathan said to him: You
are the man, king, who has done these things. Then David, being prudent, and
having lived in piety, did not abuse his royal dignity, did not tyrannize over
him who reproved him, though he was able; he did not say, Who are you, who
dared to reproach me before all? For he knew clearly that even if he is an
earthly king, yet he is ruled by the King of the powers above. For as soon as
he heard the voice of the prophet, that You are the man, king, immediately he
made confession: I have sinned against the Lord. And Nathan: And the Lord has
taken away your sin; you shall not die. O Nathan, what did you dare to
pronounce? For you are about to be accused by the haughty Naucratius. David
sinned against God, and confessed to him; and whence do you know that he
received forgiveness, that you have taken away the sin? Learn first, and then
impart the loosing of the sin. And Nathan says: God entrusted to me to make
complete the visitation of David. For if he did not wish to forgive him
through me, he would not have sent me to him. Who accuses a physician visiting
a man who is sick, and does not thank him and repay rewards? Clearly then it
is demonstrated that not only under grace, but also before grace, God forgave
men their sins through men. But let us also use a modest illustration for the
clearness of the things said: Set before me, O man, you opponent of
repentance, some man who has stumbled in some charge, and has been cast into
condemnation by a ruler, and is about to be sent away either into exile or
into some other punishment: does this man, held under the condemnation, go
forth by himself and entreat the ruler to be freed from that affliction? Does
he not, tracking down and seeking the mediator, the one called, that I may say
it more clearly, the household officer, through him accomplish his salvation,
and strive to annul the vote of the condemnation, and transform the ruler's
anger into clemency? If upon bodily punishments there is such order and
entreaty, shall it not rather, upon spiritual ones, be a far higher mediation
and need of help? Wherefore, O man, if for this reason you are not willing to
come to repentance, because the priest is a man and a sinner, then be not
baptized; for the one baptizing is a man; and partake not; for the one
dividing the Lamb of God is a man. But if you have faith in these things,
receive that one also with faith. Take heed lest, keeping other things and
losing others, you fall into judgment. For thus the evil beast, the devil,
trips us up, and wishes to cast us into Gehenna through his many-scheming
devices. It is written in the law: You shall not speak evil of the ruler of
your people. If therefore the King of those who reign has given you the priest
as ruler, why do you reject him and torment him? Can you not, standing before
the earthly king who gives honor to a ruler, hinder him and say to him: This
man is not worthy to become of this rule? If then you cannot hinder that one,
whom he wills and wishes to honor, how do you dare that the masterly command
be hindered in the case of the priest, because he is a sinner? Did not David
the prophet fall into adultery and murder, as I said before? Did not Peter,
denying the master three times, fall into sin and denial? Did not Paul
persecute the Church of God, as no other? But the loving Master chose and
glorified these, so that you should not despise nor judge any of the rulers of
the Church. For the priest baptizing and offering the unbloody sacrifice
raises his hands toward the things set forth; but the Holy Spirit sanctifies
and magnifies these. Wherefore also here, when you repent and confess, you
raise nothing else than your tongue, and God provides you the salvation and
the forgiveness. And if these things are of this sort, and it is possible for
you after baptism to be saved through repentance, why did the apostle Peter
put Ananias and Sapphira to death at once, and not recall them through
repentance? Attend accurately. Did the blessed Peter hide the plaster of
repentance? No, but they themselves, having fallen into such a fall, did not
seek the plaster suitable for repentance; but by the counsel of the devil they
perished together with their money. For then is repentance, not when it is
honored with the voice, but when it is known by the deed. For there is
repentance proceeding from the soul, and there is repentance made
superficially; whence some are saved, and some are condemned. The blessed
Peter repented, and the wretched Judas; but Peter, repenting genuinely and
weeping, and washing away the denial with his tears, returned again to his
former honor; but Judas made repentance a by-work. For even if he threw the
pieces of silver to the Jews and said, I have sinned, betraying innocent
blood, yet he did not sigh, he did not weep, he did not do things worthy of
repentance, but only, having plaited a noose, he satisfied the devil. What
then of Simon the magician? Having been baptized and tripped up, and condemned
by Peter because he offered money, wishing to traffic in the gift that is not
for sale, did he not ask for repentance? Did not the blessed Peter answer
saying: Repent of this your wickedness, and pray to the Lord? Why, he says,
did he not say to Ananias and Sapphira these words, Repent of your wickedness?
Attend accurately. The grace of the Spirit is not trifled with. For Ananias
and Sapphira, being convicted by Peter, and hearing from him, Why is it that
you agreed together to tempt the Spirit of the Lord? did not confess their
sin, did not fall down, did not entreat, did not say, As men we erred; but
what? Being convicted by him they opposed his words, trying to conceal from
him, not understanding that the Holy Spirit is not trifled with. Whence at
once the blessed Peter, provoked, brought forth the sentence fitting for them.
But Simon, hearing from the apostle Peter, Your silver be with you into
perdition, because you thought the gift to be acquired through money, did not
rage, did not run wild, did not leap away, though he was evil; but receiving
the insults and the impositions as an exchange for the charges, falling down
not only to Peter but also to all the apostles, he said: Pray you for me to
the Lord, that none of the evils you have said come upon me. Greatly, he says,
I have sinned; I dare not myself appease God; you the mediators entreat for
me. Well then did the Ecclesiastes say, A time to weep, and a time to laugh: a
time to weep for those sick with incurable diseases, and a time to laugh for
those loving repentance. For thus the great Joseph wept, as it were adhering
by prophetic vision to the blessed David who says: Those who sow in tears
shall reap in joy. Joseph then sowed also the tears of brotherly love,
tears of brotherly love.
=62.763=
and reaped the crown of the kingdom. For you all know clearly that, after that
strange recognition, Joseph, seeing his brothers, was not willing to reproach
them. For what brothers are those who broke asunder, as far as in them lay,
the twelve-linked chain of brotherhood? Yet Joseph, having turned his
afflictions into relief with a kind of laughter, wept upon the necks of his
brothers, wept over them. Why upon the neck, and not upon another part?
Necessarily he wept upon their necks, they who had lost the yoke of brotherly
love. Do you see the gentleness and piety of the man? But some say of Joseph,
that being constrained by necessity and dwelling in foreign places, and not
having human help or a protector, for this reason he showed the gentleness and
the humility. Attend therefore, you who say these things; for Joseph himself,
from his own disposition, put on the paternal piety: the memory of Joseph, and
the presence of light. For the Master of light, Christ, freed this man from
the darkness of prison. Wherefore you talk idly, man, you do not plead justly,
who say that Joseph was pious and guileless by necessity. For thus, before the
law, Joseph was a lover of God and a keeper of the law, in that even when
flattered by his own mistress he abhorred the drama of adultery. Was not the
law then given, which commands, You shall not commit adultery, you shall not
steal, you shall not bear false witness? Did he not, becoming a law to
himself, and engaging with the natural criterion, flee naked out of her, so
that he might not defile his mistress's bed? Did not this man's mistress call
him shamelessly, and promise to give him tablets of freedom, and to make him
master of all her possessions, if he should agree to what she was striving
for? Did not, as much as she ran forward, so much he bend away, according to
the voice of the Lord, having made himself a eunuch, not with a sword, but
with faith? Did not the just man say these words to her: If my master, lady,
knows nothing of the things in his house, but has entrusted all things into my
hand, and takes nothing away from me except you, because you are his wife, and
how shall I do this evil sin in the sight of God? For is not the God of my
fathers present here? For is there another god in Canaan, and another in
Egypt, even if you are ignorant? For is there not some supervising power
beholding the one who wrongs his neighbor? For, your husband being absent, has
not my God also gone abroad with him? Your husband, woman, being my master,
entrusted all his subsistence into my hand, and shall I ravish his side? He
joined one to one; for not one to many, or many to one? Be still, lady; for I
know the one who plots against you through me. It is not you who war against
me, =62.764= but the devil who armed my brothers against me. For once having
made me a stranger to my father, he hastens also to make me depart from God;
he circles above and below, so that he may make me a stranger to the dreams.
Be still therefore; for it is better for me to bear the name and not the deed
of an adulterer. If I am not persuaded by you, my body is scourged; but if I
disobey my God, I shall be punished for both together. For he knew the one who
said: Fear him who is able to destroy both soul and body. These things are
said for the proof of the gentleness and guilelessness of the prudent Joseph.
But hear also his other affair. For you all know clearly that, having dwelt in
prison through the calumny of the Egyptian woman, but having been made king of
Egypt by the decree of divine providence, and now at last his dreams, or
rather his revelations, showing forth his outcome, and his brothers having
come down to Egypt because of the scarcity of necessities, Joseph, seeing
them, even being king and master of all Egypt, did not unsheathe a sword, did
not shut them in prison, did not melt them with hunger, did not lay on blows,
did not reckon those who had not pitied him as beasts, was not mindful of the
tearing of the coat, nor of the pit's hollow, nor of the donkey's price of
slavery, nor of the smoke of envy, nor of the smoke of hatred of brothers, nor
of the burden of the paternal loss, nor of the alienation of his same-mother
brother Benjamin; but having put away all the grievous things, and having
joined threat with gentleness, he sought an occasion how he might be
recognized by his brothers. For he only beheld them, and his same-mother
brother Benjamin, the guileless, the sincere, the last cluster of the paternal
vine, the untested by hatred of brothers, the brother both by nature and by
judgment, and immediately, having wet his cheeks with joyful tears, he cried
out saying: I am Joseph your brother; now let me not seem hard to you. O the
strange things! The unaccountable makes defense as accountable. I am Joseph, O
brothers. If you did not bow down willingly, my dreams are false; if my sheaf
was not raised upright, well did you sharpen the sickle of hatred of brothers.
Learn then even thus, O brothers, that everyone hoping in the Lord shall not
be put to shame. Of a mean coat you deprived me; behold, I am clothed in
purple; in a pit you dug me; behold, in a gilded chariot I ride; as a slave
you sold me; behold, I have been appointed king; I dwelt in prison; behold, I
dwell in palaces; I wore a noose; behold, I am girt about with a diadem of
precious stones. Timely then truly to say the words of the blessed Paul: To
those who love God all things work together for good, in Christ Jesus our
Lord, to whom be the glory and the power for ever. Amen.
