1 Matthew indeed among the Hebrews put forth a writing of the gospel in their
own dialect, while Peter and Paul were evangelizing in Rome and founding the
church. And after their departure, Mark, the disciple and interpreter of
Peter, himself also handed down to us in writing the things preached by Peter.
And Luke also, the follower of Paul, set down in a book the gospel preached by
that one. Then John, the disciple of the Lord, who also leaned upon his
breast, himself also published the gospel, sojourning in Ephesus of Asia. 2 It
is not easy to persuade a soul held under error. 3 The blessed apostles, then,
having founded and built up the church, entrusted the ministry of the
episcopate to Linus; Paul makes mention of this Linus in the letters to
Timothy. And Anencletus succeeds him. And after this one, in the third place
from the apostles, Clement is allotted the episcopate, who also had seen the
blessed apostles and had associated with them, and still had the preaching of
the apostles ringing in his ears and the tradition before his eyes, not he
alone; for many were still then remaining who had been taught by the apostles.
In the time of this Clement, then, no small dissension having come about among
the brothers in Corinth, the church in Rome sent a most sufficient writing to
the Corinthians, bringing them together into peace and renewing their faith
and the tradition which it had newly received from the apostles. 4 And this
Clement is succeeded by Evarestus, and Evarestus by Alexander, and then in
this way Xystus is appointed sixth from the apostles, and after this one
Telesphorus, who also gloriously bore witness; then Hyginus, then Pius, after
whom Anicetus; Soter having succeeded Anicetus, now in the twelfth place
Eleutherus holds the lot of the episcopate from the apostles. In the same
order and in the same teaching both the tradition from the apostles in the
church and the preaching of the truth have come down to us. 5 And Polycarp
also, not only having been discipled by apostles and having associated with
many who had seen the Lord, but also having been appointed by apostles bishop
in Asia in the church in Smyrna, whom we also have seen in our first age (for
he remained a long time and, very aged, having borne witness gloriously and
most conspicuously, departed from life), having always taught these things
which he also learned from the apostles, which also the church hands down,
which also alone are true. All the churches throughout Asia bear witness to
these things, and those who have succeeded Polycarp until now, being a far
more trustworthy and more firm witness of truth than Valentinus and Marcion
and the rest of those of evil mind. Who also, having sojourned in Rome in the
time of Anicetus, turned many from the aforementioned heretics into the church
of God, having preached this one and only truth which he had received from the
apostles, the one handed down by the church. And there are those who have
heard from him that John, the disciple of the Lord, having gone in Ephesus to
bathe and having seen Cerinthus inside, leaped out of the bath without
bathing, but saying besides: "Let us flee, lest even the bath fall in,
Cerinthus the enemy of the truth being within." And Polycarp himself also,
when Marcion once came into his sight and said: "Recognize us," answered: "I
recognize, I recognize the firstborn of Satan." Such reverence the apostles
and their disciples had toward not even sharing so far as a word with any of
those who counterfeit the truth, as also Paul said: "A heretical man after a
first and second admonition refuse, knowing that such a one is perverted and
sins, being self-condemned." And there is also a letter of Polycarp written to
the Philippians, most sufficient, from which also the character of his faith

and the preaching of the truth those who wish and who take thought for their
own salvation are able to learn. 6 But also the church in Ephesus, founded by
Paul, and John remaining with them until the times of Trajan, is a true
witness of the tradition of the apostles. 7 For Valentinus came to Rome in the
time of Hyginus; and he flourished in the time of Pius and remained until
Anicetus. And Cerdo, the one before Marcion, himself also in the time of
Hyginus, who was the ninth bishop, having come into the church and making
confession, continued thus, sometimes teaching in secret, sometimes again
making confession, sometimes being convicted concerning the things he taught
badly and standing away from the assembly of the brothers. 8 Whom the Lord
will slay with the spirit of his mouth and will bring to nothing by the
appearing of his presence, whose presence is according to the working of
Satan. For he does not say that the presence of Christ comes about according
to the working of Satan, but the presence of the lawless one, whom we call
Antichrist. 9 Further Matthew says at the baptism: "The heavens were opened to
him, and he saw the Spirit of God descending as a dove and coming upon him.
And behold a voice from heaven saying: You are my beloved Son in whom I have
been well pleased." For Christ did not then come down upon Jesus, nor is
Christ one and Jesus another, but the Word of God, the Savior of all and
ruling heaven and earth, this one is Jesus, as we have taught before; having
taken on flesh and having been anointed by the Father with the Spirit, he
became Christ Jesus, as Isaiah says. 10 And for what did they also add "in the
city of David," if not that they might announce as gospel the promise made by
God to David, that from the fruit of his belly there would be an eternal
King, as having been fulfilled? Which promise the Maker of this universe
had made. 11
Why in the world are the gospels neither more nor less in number? For since
there are four climates of the world in which we are and four catholic winds,
and the church has been sown upon all the earth, and the pillar and support of
the church is the gospel and the Spirit of life, it is reasonable that she
have four pillars breathing incorruption from every side and rekindling men.
From which it is manifest that the Artificer of all things, the Word, who sits
upon the Cherubim and holds all things together, having been manifested to
men, gave us the fourfold gospel, held together by one Spirit. As David,
asking for his presence, says: "You who sit upon the Cherubim, appear." For
the Cherubim also are four-faced, and their faces are images of the economy of
the Son of God. "For the first living creature," he says, "like a lion,"
characterizing his practical and ruling and kingly character; "and the second
like a calf," showing the sacrificial and priestly order; "and the third
having the face of a man," most clearly describing his presence according to
man; "and the fourth like a flying eagle," making clear the gift of the Spirit
flying upon the church. And the gospels then are in agreement with these, in
which Christ is seated. For that according to John sets forth his ruling and
glorious generation from the Father, saying: "In the beginning was the Word,
and the Word was with God, and the Word was God." And that according to Luke,
being of a priestly character, began from Zechariah the priest offering
incense to God; for already the fatted calf was being prepared, about to be
sacrificed for the finding of the younger son. And that according to Matthew
proclaims his generation according to man, saying: "Book of the generation of
Jesus Christ, son of David, son of Abraham." And that according to Mark made
the beginning from the prophetic Spirit, the one coming upon men from on high,
saying: "Beginning of the gospel, as it is written in Isaiah the prophet." And
the Word of God himself also was conversing with the patriarchs before Moses
according to the divine and the glorious, and to those in the law he assigned
a priestly order; and after these things, having become man, the

gift of the Holy Spirit he sent forth into all the earth, covering us with his
own wings. Of what sort then the economy of the Son of God is, of such a sort
also is the form of the living creatures; and of what sort the form of the
living creatures is, of such a sort also is the character of the gospel; for
the living creatures are fourfold, and fourfold also is the gospel and the
economy of the Lord. And for this reason four catholic covenants were given to
humanity: one after the flood to Noah, upon the bow; and a second to Abraham,
upon the sign of circumcision; and a third that of the legislation through
Moses; and a fourth that of the gospel through our Lord Jesus Christ. And
these things being so, vain are all and ignorant and besides even daring,
those who set aside the form of the gospel and introduce faces of gospels
either more or fewer than those spoken of, some that they may seem to have
discovered more than the truth, and others that they may set aside the
economies of Christ. 12 Making the filling up of the apostles from the things
spoken by David. 13 The God then who promised through the prophet that he
would send his Spirit upon humanity, this one also sent it, and God is
announced by Peter as having fulfilled his own promise. 14 The apostles were
not announcing another God, nor one Christ who suffered and was raised, and
another who raised him and remained impassible, but one and the same God
Savior and Christ Jesus who was raised from the dead; and they were preaching
the faith toward him to those who did not believe in the Son of God, and from
the prophets they were bringing them together that the one whom God promised
to send as Christ he sent, Jesus, whom they crucified and God raised. 15
Manifest is the preaching that Peter together with John was preaching to them,
announcing as gospel that the promise which God made to the fathers has been
fulfilled through Jesus, but not announcing another God, but leading Israel
into knowledge of the Son of God who also became man and suffered, and
preaching in Jesus the resurrection of the dead, and signifying that as many
things as the prophets announced concerning the passion of Christ, these God
fulfilled. 16 Thus the apostles were not changing God, but were announcing to
the people that the Christ is Jesus the crucified, whom the God who sent the
prophets, being himself God, raised and gave salvation in him to men. 17 These
are the voices of the church from which every church has had its beginning;
these are the voices of the metropolis of the citizens of the new covenant;
these are the voices of the apostles, these the voices of the disciples of the
Lord, those truly perfect, who after the ascension of the Lord were perfected
through the Spirit, and who call upon the God who made the heaven and the
earth and the sea, the one preached through the prophets, and his Child, whom
God anointed, and who know no other. For Valentinus was not there then, nor
Marcion, nor the rest, destroyers of themselves and of those persuaded by
them. Wherefore also the God who is Maker of all things heard them; for "the
place," it says, "in which they were gathered was shaken, and they were all
filled with the Holy Spirit, and they were speaking the word of God with
boldness to everyone wishing to believe." 18 Peter having seen the revelation
in which the heavenly voice said to him: "What God has cleansed, do not you
make common," this, that God, having distinguished through the law between
clean and unclean, this one has cleansed the nations through the blood of his
Child, whom also Cornelius was worshipping. 19 Clearly signifying that the one
whom Cornelius formerly feared as God, whom he was being instructed in through
the law and the prophets, for whose sake he also was doing the alms, this one
is in truth God. But the knowledge of the Son was lacking to him. 20 That this
one is Jesus and that the scripture has been fulfilled in him, as the eunuch
himself, having been persuaded and immediately asking to be baptized, was
saying: "I believe that Jesus is the Son of God." Who also was sent into the
regions of Ethiopia, preaching this which he himself believed, one God, the
one through the

prophets preached, and that the Son of this one has already made his presence
according to man, and has been led as a sheep to slaughter, and the rest of as
many things as the prophets say concerning him. 21 Paul, after the Lord spoke
to him from heaven and showed that he was persecuting his own Master in
persecuting his disciples, and sent Ananias to him, and he recovered sight and
was baptized, "in the synagogues," it says, "in Damascus he was preaching with
all boldness Jesus, that this one is the Son of God, the Christ." This is the
mystery which he says has been made known to him according to revelation, that
the one who suffered under Pontius Pilate, this one is Lord of all and King
and God and Judge. 22 Signifying through these words that he would not have
gone to them, if he had not been commanded. For perhaps he would not even have
given them baptism so readily, if he had not heard them prophesying after the
Holy Spirit had rested upon them. And for this reason he was saying: "Can
anyone forbid the water, that these should not be baptized, who have received
the Holy Spirit as also we?" persuading at the same time those present with
him and signifying that, if the Holy Spirit had not rested upon them, there
was one who would forbid them the baptism. 23 Wherefore again in the letter he
says: "Everyone who believes that Jesus Christ has been begotten of God,"
knowing one and the same Jesus Christ, to whom the gates of heaven were opened
because of his incarnate ascension; who also will come in the same flesh in
which he also suffered, revealing the glory of the Father. 24 The Spirit then
having come down because of the preordained economy, and the Son of God the
only-begotten, who is also Word of the Father, having come in the fullness of
the time, having been incarnate in man and having fulfilled all the economy
according to man, Jesus Christ our Lord, being one and the same, as the Lord
himself bears witness and the apostles confess and the prophets preach, all
the teachings of those who have invented ogdoads and tetrads and seemings have
been shown false. 25 It is manifest then that Paul knows no other Christ, but
this one who both suffered and was buried and rose and was born, whom he also
calls man. For having said: "But if Christ is preached that he has been raised
from the dead," he adds, giving the cause of his incarnation: "For since
through man death, also through man resurrection of the dead." And everywhere
concerning the passion of our Lord and his humanity and his dying he has used
the name of Christ, as in: "Do not destroy with your food that one for whom
Christ died," and again: "But now in Christ you who once were far off have
been made near by the blood of Christ," and again: "Christ has redeemed us
from the curse of the law, having become a curse for us; for it is written:
Cursed is everyone who hangs upon a tree." 26 He united then, as we said
before, man to God. For if man had not conquered the adversary of man, the
enemy would not have been conquered justly. And again if God had not granted
the salvation, we would not have had it securely. And if man had not been
united to God, he would not have been able to partake of incorruption. For it
was necessary that the mediator of God and of men, through his own kinship
toward each, gather both together into friendship and concord, and present man
to God, and make God known to men. 27 "For as through the disobedience of the
one man" who was first fashioned from untilled earth "the many were appointed
sinners" and they cast away the life, "so" it was necessary "also through the
obedience of one man" who was first born from a virgin that many be justified
and receive back the salvation. 28 "I said: You are gods and sons of the Most
High, all of you; but you die as men." These things he says toward those who
have not received the gift of adoption, but who dishonor the incarnation of
the pure generation of the Word of God and deprive man of the ascent toward
God and are ungrateful to the one who was incarnate for them

Word of God. For unto this the Word became man and Son of man, the Son of God,
that man, having contained the Word and having received the adoption, might
become a son of God. 29 For as he was man that he might be tempted, so also
Word that he might be glorified, the Word being quiet in being tempted and
being crucified and dying, but the man being present with him in the
conquering and enduring and being kind and rising and being taken up. 30 God
then became man and the Lord himself saved us, giving the sign of the virgin.
But not as some of those now daring to retranslate the scripture say: "Behold
the young woman shall have in the womb and shall bear a son," as Theodotion
the Ephesian interpreted and Aquila the Pontic, both Jewish proselytes; whom
the Ebionites having followed claim that he was born from Joseph. 31 For
before the Romans strengthened their rule, while the Macedonians were still
holding Asia, Ptolemy the son of Lagus, being ambitious to adorn the library
constructed by him in Alexandria with the writings of all men, as many at
least as were of worth, asked from those of Jerusalem to have their scriptures
transferred into the Greek dialect. And they, for they were still then subject
to the Macedonians, sent to Ptolemy the seventy elders among them most
experienced in the scriptures and in both dialects, God having done what he
willed. And he, wishing to take a trial of them privately and being cautious
lest perhaps having agreed together they hide the truth in the scriptures
through the interpretation, having separated them from one another commanded
them all to write the same interpretation, and he did this upon all the books.
And when they had come together to the same place with Ptolemy and each had
compared his own interpretation, God was glorified, and the scriptures were
known as truly divine, all of them having declared the same things in the same
words and the same names from beginning to end, so that even the nations
present might know that the scriptures have been interpreted according to the
inspiration of God. And it is no wonder that God has worked this, who also in
the captivity of the people under Nebuchadnezzar, the scriptures having been
destroyed, and after seventy years the Jews having gone up into their own
land, then in the times of Artaxerxes the king of the Persians inspired Ezra
the priest from the tribe of Levi to set in order again all the words of the
prophets who had gone before and to restore to the people the legislation
through Moses. 32 And in saying: "Hear then, house of David," it was
signifying that the one whom God promised to David that he would raise up an
eternal King from the fruit of his belly, this one is the one being born from
the Davidic virgin. 33 If then the first Adam had a human father and was born
from seed, it would have been reasonable for them to say that the second Adam
also was born from Joseph. But if that one was taken from earth, and God is
his Fashioner, it was necessary that the one being recapitulated into him, the
man fashioned by God, have the same likeness of generation as that one. Why
then again did God not take dust, but worked that the fashioning come about
from Mary? That another fashioning might not come about nor that which is
saved be another, but that very one be recapitulated, the likeness being
preserved. Greatly then do they fall who say that he has taken up nothing from
the virgin, that they may cast out the inheritance of the flesh and throw away
the likeness. 34 Since even his descent into Mary is superfluous. For why also
was he coming down into her, if he was going to take nothing from her? And
further if he had taken nothing from Mary, he would not have accepted the
foods taken from the earth, through which the body taken from the earth is
nourished; nor would he, having fasted for forty days likewise to Moses and
Elijah, have hungered, his body seeking its own food; nor would John his
disciple, writing concerning him, have said: "And Jesus, having grown weary
from the journey, was sitting"; nor would

drops of blood; nor would he have said that "My soul is very sorrowful"; nor,
