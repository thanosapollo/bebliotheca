* On that saying, All things have been delivered to me
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

On the saying, All things have been delivered to me by my Father, and no one
knows who the Son is except the Father, and who the Father is except the Son,
and he to whom the Son may will to reveal him.

And not understanding this, those of the heresy of Arius, both Eusebius and
those with him, act impiously toward the Lord. For they say: If all things
were delivered (saying "all things" of the lordship of creation), there was
once when he did not have them. And if he did not have them, he is not from
the Father; for if he were, he would have had them always, being from him, and
he would have had no need to receive them. But from this also their folly will
rather be exposed. For the saying does not signify ruling the creation, or
overseeing the things made, but it wishes to indicate a certain sense of the
economy. For if, when he was speaking, they were delivered to him, it is clear
that before he received them the creation was empty of the Word. And where
then is, All things consist in him? And if, at the same time as the creation
came to be, the whole was delivered to him, there was no need of a delivering;
for all things came to be through him, and it is superfluous that those things
of which he himself is maker be delivered to the Lord. For he was Lord of the
things coming to be in the making of them. But if also after they came to be
they were delivered to him, consider the absurdity. For if they were
delivered, the Father also withdrew when this one received them; and we risk
falling into the mythologies of certain men, that having delivered them to
this one, he himself departed. Or if, the Son having them, the Father also has
them, he ought not to have said, they were delivered, but that he took a
partner, as Paul did Silvanus. But this too is rather absurd; for God is not
in want, nor did he take the Son as a help because of need; but, being Father
of the Word, he makes all things through him, and does not deliver creation to
him, but through him and in him he provides for the creation, so that not even
a sparrow falls to the earth without the Father, nor is the grass clothed
without God, but also, while the Father works, the Son also works until now.
Therefore the mind of the impious is vain. For it is not as they understand,
but the saying is indicative of the economy according to the flesh.

For since man sinned and fell, and when he fell all things were thrown into
confusion, death had power from Adam until Moses, the earth has been cursed,
Hades was opened, paradise was shut, heaven was angered, and at last man was
corrupted and made like the beasts, and the =25.212= devil leapt against us.
Then God, being a lover of man and not wishing the man according to the image
to perish, said: Whom shall I send, and who will go? And all being silent, the
Son said: Behold, I, send me; and then indeed saying, Go, he delivered man to
him, that the Word himself might become flesh, and taking the flesh might set
all things right. For man was delivered to him, as to a physician, to heal the
bite of the serpent; as to life, to raise the dead; as to light, to illumine
the darkness; and being Word, to renew the rational. When therefore all things
were delivered to him, and he became man, immediately the whole was set right
and perfected: the earth instead of a curse has been blessed, paradise was
opened to the thief, Hades cowered, and the tombs were opened, the dead being
raised, the gates of heaven were lifted up, that he who comes from Edom might
arrive. And wishing himself to signify how all things were delivered to him,
the Saviour added at once, as Matthew says: Come to me, all who labour and are
heavy laden, and I will give you rest; for you have been delivered to me, that
I may give rest to those who have laboured, and give life to those who have
been made dead. And in accord with this is also what is set down in John: The
Father loves the Son, and has given all things into his hand. For he has
given, that just as all things came to be through him, so in him all things
might be able to be renewed. For they were not delivered to him so that from
being poor he might become rich, nor did he receive all things so that, as not
having authority, he might receive authority, God forbid! but rather that, as
Saviour, he might set all things right. For it was fitting that the beginning
of the creation come to be through him, that they might exist, but the setting
right in him, the words having a difference. For in the beginning they came to
be through him unto being; but later, when all had fallen, the Word became
flesh, and put it on, that in him the whole might be set right. For suffering
himself he gave us rest, and hungering himself he fed us, and going down into
Hades he brought us up. Then, when all things were coming to be, the
coming-to-be was a command, such as, Let it bring forth, and, Let there be;
but for the setting right it was fitting that all things be delivered, that he
himself might become man, and in him all things be renewed. For being in him,
man was made alive; for this reason the Word was entwined with man, that the
curse might no longer have power against man. For this reason also in the
seventy-first Psalm those =25.213= who petition God concerning the human race
say, O God, give your judgment to the king, that the judgment of death against
us, which was against us, might also be delivered to the Son, and thereafter
he himself in himself might abolish it, having died for us. For signifying
this he himself also said in the eighty-seventh Psalm: Your wrath has been
established upon me. For he himself bore the wrath against us, as also in the
one hundred thirty-seventh he says: The Lord will recompense on my behalf.
Thus then it is possible to understand that all things have been delivered to
the Saviour, and if one must, having understood, say it again, those things
have been delivered to him which he did not have. For he was not man before
this, but he became so in order to save this one. And the Word was not in the
beginning flesh, but later he became flesh, in which he also reconciled the
enmity toward you according to the Apostle, and abolished the law of the
commandments in ordinances, that he might create the two into one new man,
making peace, and reconcile both in one body to the Father.

Yet what the Father has, these things are also the Son's, as he says in John:
All things whatsoever the Father has are mine, the words being very well put.
For when that which he was not was coming to be, all things are delivered to
him; but when he wishes to signify the unity toward the Father, he does not
hide it, but teaches, saying: All things whatsoever the Father has are mine.
And one must marvel at the precision of the saying. For he has not said, All
things whatsoever the Father has, he has given to me, lest he be shown as once
not having them; but that they are mine. For these things being in the
authority of the Father, they are likewise in that of the Son. And what the
Father has must be examined again. For if he means the creation, then he had
nothing before the creation, and he appears as taking on from the creation.
But God forbid that we think this! For as he himself is before the creation,
so also before the creation he has what he has, which we also believe to be
the Son's. For if the Son is in the Father, then all things whatsoever the
Father has are the Son's. And this saying overturns the wickedness of the
heterodox who say: "If all things have been delivered to the Son, then the
Father has ceased from the authority of the things delivered, since he has
appointed the Son in his own place; For the Father judges no one, but has
given all judgment to the Son." But let the mouths of those who speak unjust
things be stopped; for it is not that, because he has given all judgment to
the Son, he himself has been stripped of lordship; nor that, because it has
been said that all things have been delivered to the Son by the Father, he
himself is not over all; but they clearly separate the Only-begotten of God,
who is inseparable by nature, even if with words these, being mad, separate
him, the impious not =25.216= understanding that the light would never be
separated from the sun, but is in it by nature. For using from things at hand
and customary a poor image, one must set forth the meaning in speech, since it
is rash to enter upon the incomprehensible nature.

As therefore the light from the sun that illumines the inhabited world could
never be thought by those of sound mind to shine around apart from it, since
the light from it is united to the nature of the sun, and as if the light
should say: I have received from the sun to illumine all things, and to
increase and strengthen all things through the heat in me; no one, being mad,
will think to separate the naming of the sun from the nature from it, which is
the light; so it is pious also to think the divine essence of the Word united
by nature to his own Father. For the saying at hand will furnish the clearest
explanation of what is sought, the Saviour having said: All things whatsoever
the Father has are mine, which shows that he is always with the Father; for
the "whatsoever he has" shows that the Father holds the lordship; and the "are
mine," the inseparable union. We must therefore understand that in the Father
there is the everlasting, the eternal, the immortal; and they are in him not
as alien to him, but as in a fountain they rest in him and in the Son.
Whenever therefore you wish to think concerning the Son, having learned what
things are in the Father, believe that these also are in the Son. If then the
Father is a creature or a thing made, these things are also in the Son; and if
it is lawful to say of the Father, there was once when he was not, or from
things that are not: let this be said of the Son also. But if it is impious to
say that these things are in the Father, let it be impious also to think these
things in the Son. For the things of the Father, these are the Son's. For he
who honors the Son honors the Father who sent him, and he who receives the Son
receives with him the Father; for he who has seen the Son has seen the Father.
As therefore the Father is not a creature, so neither is the Son; and as it is
not possible to say of him, there was once when he was not, or from things
that are not; so it is not fitting to say the same of the Son either. But
rather, as in the Father there is the everlasting, the immortal, the always,
the not-creature; so also it is consistent to think concerning the Son. For
according to what is written, As the Father has life in himself, so he has
given also to the Son to have life in himself. And he has said "has given,"
that he might show the Father who gives. But as in the Father, so also in the
Son is the life, that he might teach the undivided and everlasting. For this
reason he has also said precisely, Whatsoever the Father has, that, even here
naming the Father, he himself might not be thought the Father. =25.217= For he
has not said, I am the Father, but, Whatsoever the Father has. For the
Only-begotten Son of his, O Arians, may be styled Father of the age to come
from the Father, not that he is the Father, as you, being led astray, might
perhaps suppose, but Son of the Father who begot him, and father of the age to
come. For one must take away all your suspicions. He says therefore through
the prophet: A Son has been born to us, and has been given to us, whose
government is upon his shoulder, and his name is called Messenger of Great
Counsel, Mighty God, Ruler, Father of the age to come. Therefore the
Only-begotten is also father of the age to come, and Mighty God, and the Son
of God exists as Ruler; and it has been clearly shown that all things
whatsoever the Father has are his, and as the Father gives life, so likewise
it is present to the Son to give life to whom he wills. For the dead, he says,
will hear the voice of the Son, and will live. And there is one will of Father
and Son, and one counsel, since also the nature is one and undivided. And the
Arians beat themselves to excess, not understanding what was said by our
Saviour, All things whatsoever the Father has are mine. For from this both the
madness of Sabellius is overturned, and it will expose the folly of the Jews
of now. For this reason the Only-begotten, having life in himself as the
Father has, also alone knows who the Father is, being in the Father, and
having the Father in himself. For he is image, and accordingly, as in an
image, all the things of the Father are in him. For he is an equally stamped
seal showing the Father in himself, living Word, true, power, wisdom,
sanctification, and our redemption.

For in him we live and move and are; and, No one knows who the Father is
except the Son, and who the Son is except the Father. And how do the impious
dare to babble things that are not lawful, being men, and not finding how to
recount the things upon the earth? And why do I say the things upon the earth?
Let them tell us their own things, if indeed they will be able to find out how
to trace their own nature. They are truly rash and arrogant, not trembling at
glories, where angels desire to look in, who are so much higher both in nature
and in rank. For what is nearer than the cherubim or the seraphim? and yet
they offer the doxology not even seeing, or standing with feet, nor with
uncovered faces, but covered, as also with their faces, with unceasing lips
glorifying nothing else than the divine and unspeakable nature with the
thrice-holiness. And nowhere has any of the inspired prophets, of those
especially counted worthy of such a vision, reported to us that in saying once
"holy," with a great voice, but in repeating it, less, and in saying it a
third time, in a lower way; and after this that they ascribe lordship to the
first sanctification, and subordinate the second, and =25.220= set the third
lower. But away with the folly of the God-hated and mindless! For the
all-hymned and reverend and worshipful Trinity is one and undivided and
without figure. And it is joined without confusion, just as also the monad is
divided without cutting. For that these honoured living creatures offer the
doxology a third time, saying Holy, holy, holy, is showing the three perfect
hypostases, as also in saying Lord they indicate the one essence. Those
therefore who lessen the Only-begotten Son of God blaspheme against God,
holding a wrong opinion of the perfection, and slandering it as imperfect; and
they make themselves liable to the greatest punishment. For he who blasphemes
against whichever of the hypostases will not have forgiveness either in this
age or in the one to come. But God is able to open the eyes of their heart
toward understanding of the sun of righteousness; that, having come to know
him whom they formerly rejected, with an intense reasoning of piety they may
glorify him with us, for his is the kingdom, of the Father, and of the Son,
and of the Holy Spirit, now and unto the ages. Amen.
