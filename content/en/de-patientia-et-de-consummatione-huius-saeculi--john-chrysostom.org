* On Patience and on the Consummation of This Age
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

On patience, and on the consummation of this age, and the second coming, both
the kingdom of the righteous that has no successor, and the unending
punishment of sinners; and also a theme of confession, and an exhortation
toward the study of the divine Scriptures, and what the devices of the Enemy
are, and what the benefit of stillness is.

=63.937=
The life of the righteous is bright; and how is it made bright, if not through
patience? Having acquired this, love it, O brother, as the mother of courage.
For the psalm exhorts, saying: Wait upon the Lord, and keep his way. And Paul,
how you may acquire the virtue, says: Tribulation works patience. Pursuing
this, you will find the fountain of good things, hope: And hope does not put
to shame. Submit therefore to the Lord, and make supplication to him, and from
this you will find the word, And may he give you the requests =63.938= of your
heart. What is more blessed than this, than to possess so favorable a hearing
from such a king? Who will not wish to have the judge's ear open and
hearkening? You are a laborer of virtue, brother; Christ has hired you into
his vineyard; while you have time, work the good.

Hear Paul saying: Whatever a man sows, that he will also reap. Sow into the
Spirit, that you may reap eternal life. For he who sows into the body, he
says, will from the body reap corruption. Hear the good exhorter saying
=63.939=: Sow for yourselves righteousness, gather the fruit of life. Do not
grow faint in laboring, seeing the hope before you. For where there are
contests, there also are prizes; where wars, there also honors; where the
wrestling, there also the crown. Looking toward these things, anoint yourself
through patience; always call out to yourself, crying with the saints: Be
manly, and let your heart be strengthened, and wait upon the Lord. Prepare
your works for the going out, and make ready for the field; for this life is a
field. Take the Old Testament. Hedge your possession about with thorns. With
what kind? With fasting, with prayer, with teaching. If you have the fence,
the beast will not enter, I mean the devil. Till your soul as a good vine. And
just as those who guard vineyards clap with the hand, cry out with the voice,
and through the noises stop those who plot against them: so you also cry out
with prayer, shout with psalmody, and you will drive away the treacherous
beast, the fox, which is the devil; concerning whom the Scripture said: Catch
for us the little foxes, and the rest. Keep watch always on the Enemy. If he
comes to shoot your heart toward an unseemly desire, and if he comes to sling
at your soul, attacking with filthy thoughts, set against him the shield of
faith, put on the helmet of hope, draw the sword of the Spirit, which is the
word of God. And being thus armed against the Enemy, endure, and do not grow
faint in the war, but be sober in all things. The Enemy brings on at one time
broad places, at another afflictions. For toward whatever he tests the
disposition that lies in the man, toward that he himself also campaigns,
drawing up through cunning.

Therefore, my brother, be sober securely, and always be eager to cling to
reading, that it may teach you how one must flee the snares of the Enemy, and
lay hold of eternal life. For the reading of the divine Scriptures gathers in
the wandering mind, and grants knowledge toward God. For it is written: Be
still, and


know that I am God. You hear, brother, that he who is at leisure upon the
divine Scriptures in a true heart receives knowledge of God. Therefore, my
brother, do not neglect your soul, but be at leisure for reading and for
prayers, that your understanding may be enlightened, and that you may become
perfect and complete, lacking in nothing. Others boast in conversation with
great men, both rulers and kings; but you boast before the angels of God,
conversing with the Holy Spirit through the divine Scriptures; for it is the
Holy Spirit that speaks through them. Be eager therefore to meet with the
divine Scriptures, and to persevere in the holy prayers. For as often as you
meet with God through them, so often is your body sanctified, and the soul and
the spirit. Knowing this then, my brother, that whenever you meet with them
you are sanctified, be eager to meet with them more frequently; and even if
the hands are not at leisure, pray with the mind. For even the blessed Anna,
the mother of the prophet Samuel, prayed, and her lips moved; but her prayer
had entered into the ears of the Lord of Sabaoth. For her request was also
given to her. Therefore, my brother, even if the hands are at leisure, still
pray with the mind; for God hears even those who are silent. But if you do not
know how to read, cling where there is hearing and being benefited. For it is
written: If you see a man of understanding, go early to him, and let your foot
wear out the steps of his doors. For this contributes, my brother, not only to
those who do not know how to read, but also to those who do know. For there
are many who read, and do not understand what they read.

See therefore, brother, that you do not neglect the gift that is in you, which
was given to you by the gift of Christ; but take thought and seek out how you
will be able to please God, that you may obtain the beatitude. For it is
written: Blessed are they who search out his testimonies; they will seek him
with the whole heart. See that the Enemy not cut you off, when you wish to
read, bringing injustice before you, and casting you into temptations, and
saying: Do this thing first, inasmuch as it is little, and thus you may read
without care. For when he suggests these things, and furnishes eagerness
toward handiwork, he throws all these in, that he may occupy you away from
reading =63.940= and being benefited. For when the devil sees a man reading
with labor and being benefited, with these occasions and many others he draws
up against him, seeking to hinder him. Do not then be persuaded by him, but
become as a thirsty hart, and longing to come upon the fountains of waters,
that is, upon the divine Scriptures, that he may drink from them, and they may
cool your thirst which burns you through the passions. Drink in also the
benefit that comes to you from them. I mean something of this sort: that when
the Lord grants you to understand a word from them, you may not pass it by,
but may meditate on it in your mind, and write it in your heart, and keep it
by memory inerasable. For it is written: In your justifications I will
meditate; I will not forget your words. And again, In my heart I hid your
oracles, that I might not sin against you. And again, In what will a young man
set his way right? In the keeping of your words. You see, brother, that in
remembering the words of God a man sets his way right. For who is it that
remembers the words of God and does not set them right, unless perhaps that
man is unapproved and wretched, who remembers nothing at all, but even forgets
the things he seems to remember?

For to such a man God says: Why do you declare my justifications, and take up
my covenant through your mouth? He therefore commands that even what he seems
to have be taken from him; because this man seems to have faith, for he also
calls himself a Christian, but by works he denies it, and is worse than an
unbeliever. Therefore he commands the Holy Spirit to be taken from him, which
he received in the day of redemption, which he seems to have. And


such a man becomes like a jar of wine that has made a crack, and through it
has lost the wine; which all who see it, not having seen what has happened,
think to be full; but when it is examined, then it is made plain to all that
it lies empty. So then also such a man, being examined on that day of
judgment, is found out; and then the things concerning him become plain to
all. Such they are even if they will say to the king on that day: Lord, did we
not do many mighty works in your name? And answering the king will say to
them: Amen I say to you, I do not know you. You see, brother, that such a one
has no mercy. You then remember the words that you hear, and set your way
right. See that you do not let forgetfulness come down and snatch it from your
heart. See that you do not let the birds come down and eat up the seed of God;
for he himself said that the seed is the word which you heard. Hide the seed
in the flanks of the earth; that is, hide the word in the midst of your heart,
that it may bear fruit to the Lord in fear. And when you read, read carefully
and with labor, going through the verse in much composure, and do not be eager
only to go through the leaves, but if there is need, do not shrink from going
through the verse twice and thrice and very many times, that you may
understand its power. And when you are about to sit down and read, or again to
hear another reading, first entreat God, saying: Lord Jesus Christ, open the
ears and the eyes of my heart, that I may hear your word, and understand, and
do your will, Lord, for I am a sojourner in the earth. Do not hide your
commandments from me, but unveil my eyes, and I will consider the wonders from
your law; for I have hoped in you, my God, that you may enlighten my heart.
Yes, my brother, I exhort you, always pray thus to God, that he may enlighten
your mind, and make plain to you the power of his words. For many have gone
astray, having trusted in their understanding, and claiming to be wise they
became foolish, not understanding the things written, and they fell into
blasphemy, and perished.

If then in your reading you find a word hard to understand, see that the Evil
One not teach you to say in yourself, that It is not so, as this word says;
for how can it be so? and such things. But if you believe God, believe also
his words, and say to the Evil One, Go behind me, Satan. For I know, The
oracles of God are pure oracles, =63.941= silver tried sevenfold; and there is
nothing crooked in them, nor twisted, but all the good things are plain before
those who understand; but I am without understanding, and I do not know. I
know therefore that they have been written spiritually; for the Apostle also
says that The law is spiritual. Then thus look up to heaven, and say: Lord, I
believe your words; I do not contradict, but I trust in the words of the Holy
Spirit. You then, Lord, save me, that I may find grace before you. For I seek
nothing else, except only to be saved, that I may obtain your mercy, O
compassionate one; for yours is the kingdom and the power unto the ages, amen.
Acquire also stillness, brother, as a strong wall. For stillness makes you
higher than the passions; for you fight from above, and they from below.
Acquire therefore the stillness in the fear of God, and all the darts of the
Enemy will not harm you; for stillness yoked with the fear of God is a fiery
chariot.

And let Elijah the prophet persuade you, who loved stillness and the fear of
God, having been taken up as into heaven. O stillness, progress of monks; O
stillness, heavenly ladder; O stillness, way of the kingdom of heaven; O
stillness, mother of compunction; O stillness, mirror of sins, that shows a
man his trespasses; O stillness, that does not hinder tears; O stillness,
begetter of meekness; O stillness, tent-companion of humility; O stillness,
yoked with the fear of God, light-bringer of the mind;


O stillness, spy of thoughts, and fellow-laborer of discernment; O
stillness, begetter of every good, foundation of fasting, and hindrance of
gluttony; O stillness, leisure of prayer and reading; O stillness, calm of
thoughts, and fair harbor; O stillness, freedom from care of the soul; O
stillness, kind yoke and light burden, giving rest and bearing the one who
bears you; O stillness, gladness of soul and heart; O stillness, bridle of
eyes and hearing and tongue; O stillness, destruction of vainglory, and enemy
of shamelessness; O stillness, prison of the passions; O stillness,
fellow-worker of every virtue, patron of poverty, a place of God bearing good
fruits; O stillness, wall and stronghold of those who wish to contend for the
kingdom of heaven. Yes, brother, acquire this good portion, which Mary chose.

For this Mary became a pattern of stillness, sitting beside the feet of the
Lord, and clinging to him alone. Therefore the Lord also praised her, saying:
Mary has chosen the good portion, which will not be taken from her. You see,
brother, of what sort stillness is: that he himself is, and our Master God
praises the one who acquires it. Acquire this, my brother, and you will
delight in your Lord, sitting beside his feet and clinging to him alone, that
you also may say =63.942= with boldness: My soul has clung behind you; your
right hand has upheld me. Therefore my soul would be filled as with fat and
richness. Yes, my brother, acquire this that is sweeter than honey. For better
is a morsel in salt of stillness and freedom from care, than a setting-out of
costly foods in temptations and cares. Hear the Master God who says: Come to
me, all who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.

For the Lord wishes to give you rest from cares, from anger, from temptation
and affliction of this age; he wishes you to be free from care of the
brickmaking of Egypt; he wishes to lead you into the wilderness, that is, into
stillness, that he may light your ways with the pillar of cloud, that he may
feed you the manna, I mean the bread of stillness and freedom from care; that
you may inherit the good land, I mean the Jerusalem above. Yes, beloved, love
this, acquire this, that you may delight in the way of the testimonies of God,
as in all wealth. Yes, brother, acquire stillness with the fear of God, and
the God of peace will be with you, to whom glory is due unto the ages. Amen. I
exhort you therefore, brothers beloved by the Lord, that we be eager each day
to make remembrance of these things, I mean of hope, of faith, of love, of
humility, of sealing ourselves always in the prayers of God, and in the
meditations of the divine Scriptures, and in stillness. For these things,
being present to you and abounding, do not make you idle, nor unfruitful,
toward the knowledge of our Lord Jesus Christ. For a Christian who has not
acquired these things is blind, short-sighted, having taken forgetfulness of
his former sins; and the thing of the true proverb has happened to him, A dog
that has turned back to his own vomit; and, A sow that has washed, to
wallowing in mire. Because having fled the pollutions of the world, as it is
written, and having denied these things in the holy bath, and having come into
the knowledge of our Lord Jesus Christ, then again entangled in these: the
last things have become worse for them than the first.

It would have been better for them not to have known the way of truth, than,
knowing it, to turn back again. Therefore, beloved, faithful servants and
elect soldiers of God, Christians, let us take up the full armor of the things
aforesaid in our heart, and each day making remembrance of them without delay;
that we may be able to contend the good contest, and to trample down all the
power of the Enemy; that we may be delivered from the wrath that is coming
upon the sons of


disobedience, and may find mercy and grace on the fearful day before the
righteous judge, who renders to each according to his works; to whom glory is
due, with the almighty Father, and the all-holy Spirit, now and always, and
unto the ages of ages. Amen.
