PROVERBS COLLECTED FROM VARIOUS BOOKS AND COMPOSED HERE ACCORDING TO THE

BEGINNING OF A.
1.1 This cord has drawn nothing: it is said from the fishermen. 1.2 An
Aphrodisian oath is not punishable: the gods, being forsworn in erotic
matters, are not angry. 1.3 Marketplace of Cercopes: of the wicked and
ill-natured. For certain Cercopes were deceivers in Ephesus. 1.4 An eagle's
old age, a lark's youth: inasmuch as even an aging eagle is better than a
young bird. 1.5 Goods of Cilicians: of those growing rich not well. For a
certain Cilician betrayer, having betrayed Miletus, became prosperous. 1.6 Do
not despise a rustic orator: that not even of the incomplete ** 1.7 Garden of
Adonis: of things short-lived and untimely. 1.8 At another time to become of
another sort and to follow the land: that it is fitting for one to make
himself like the customs of the cities and places in which he may come to be;
and it is said from the octopuses. 1.9 Shake another oak: of those refusing to
give still to those asking; they are accustomed to take. =1.10= You sing as
one sailing to Delos: of those living without care and fond of pleasure.
=1.11= Salt and a bean: of those not knowing and pretending; for the seers set
out salt and a bean. =1.12= To lift the armpit: instead of to feast. It is
said from those dancing in a rustic way. =1.13= The goat the knife. =1.14= A
tearless war; and: A trophy of one sleeping: of those succeeding most easily
and contrary to hope. =1.15= Always jackdaw to jackdaw; and: Always god leads
like to like. =1.16= Horn of Amaltheia: because Zeus gave to Amaltheia his
nurse, from a goat that was feeding him, a goat's horn, through which there
gushed forth for her everything she might ask. =1.17= You teach an eagle to
fly. =1.18= For the dice of Zeus always fall well: of those worthily honored.
=1.19= A contest admits no excuse, nor does friendship: =1.19= of those
destroying. =1.20= An eagle in the clouds. =1.21= A milled life: of the ready
and without toil. =1.22= You stir Anagyrus: of those bringing evils upon
themselves. =1.23= May such enemies always pursue me: of the despised and
worth nothing. =1.24= An eagle sees woodworms: of those despising certain
people. =1.25= There is no brine in him: of the unpleasant. =1.26= A fox is
not bribed: of those not easily caught. =1.27= Libya always brings some evil:
of those always discovering some new evil. =1.28= The springs of rivers go
upward: of things said contrariwise, as if the fornicator calls the temperate
man a fornicator. =1.29= Fountains of silver speak: of those made conspicuous

guard yourself; in the Ionic. =1.31= An Arabian piper: who piped for a

play the fox toward another fox: of those wishing to deceive their likes.
=1.38= To some a tongue, to others grinders: of the talkative and the
gluttons. =1.39= An old man's skull is a raisin: of those useful for nothing.
=1.40= Leucon says one thing, but Leucon's ass another: of those whose deeds

flows in: of those toiling in vain. =1.42= I asked for water-buckets, but

2.1 Enough of the oak: of those changing to some better way of life; for the
