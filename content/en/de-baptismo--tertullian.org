* Chapter 1
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Happy is our sacrament of water, for when the sins of our former blindness
have been washed away, we are set free into eternal life! This treatise will
not be idle labor; it is meant to instruct both those who are just now being
formed in the faith and those who, content with having believed in a simple
way, carry a faith that is probable but unexamined, with no inquiry into the
reasons behind the traditions handed on to them. And indeed, not long ago a
certain viper of the Cainian heresy, which had lately been active in this
region, snatched away a great many people with its most venomous teaching,
and its first aim was to destroy baptism. That is plainly in character with
its nature. For vipers and asps and basilisks, those regal snakes, generally
haunt dry and waterless places. But we, little fish, after the manner of our
ICHTHYS, Jesus Christ, are born in water, and we are saved in no other way
than by continuing in the water. So that monstrous creature, which did not
even have the right of sound teaching, knew best of all how to kill the
little fish by taking them out of the water.

* Chapter 2
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h2
:END:

But how great is the force of perversity, for shaking the faith or refusing
it outright, that it attacks it on the very grounds on which it stands!
Nothing so hardens men's minds as the simplicity of God's works, which are
visible in the act, set against the magnificence promised in the effect; and
so, precisely because a man is lowered into water with such simplicity,
without pomp, without any new apparatus, and, in a word, without expense, and
is dipped amid a few words, and rises again not much, or not at all, the
cleaner, the attainment of eternity that follows is thought the more
incredible. I am lying, if it is not out of their ritual setting and
apparatus, and out of their expense, that the solemnities of the idols, or
their mysteries, build up faith and authority for themselves. O wretched
unbelief, which denies to God what belongs to him, simplicity and power! What
then? Is it not also wonderful that death should be washed away in the bath?
Yet it must be believed all the more, since it is not believed because it is
wonderful. For what sort ought the works of God to be, if not above all
wonder? We ourselves wonder too, but we wonder because we believe. Unbelief,
on the other hand, wonders and does not believe. It wonders at the simple
acts as if they were empty, and at the magnificent results as if they were
impossible. And let it be quite as you think: the divine pronouncement has
run ahead, sufficient for both sides. God has chosen the foolish things of
the world, to confound its wisdom; and things very hard for men, he makes
easy for God. For if God is both wise and powerful (which even those who pass
him by do not deny), he has rightly taken the materials of his working from
the contraries of wisdom and power, that is, from foolishness and
impossibility; since every power takes its cause from the things by which it
is provoked.

* Chapter 3
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h3
:END:

Mindful of that pronouncement as of a prescript, we nevertheless treat how
foolish and impossible it is for a man to be re-formed by water. What has
this material, after all, done to deserve a service of such high regard? The
authority of the liquid element, I suppose, must be sought out. And it is
abundantly supplied, and that from the beginning. For water is one of those
things that, before all the furnishing of the world, still rested with God,
unformed in appearance. In the beginning, it says, God made heaven and earth.
But the earth was invisible and unformed, and darkness was over the abyss,
and the Spirit of the Lord was borne over the waters. Here, man, is what you
must first venerate in the waters: their age, because they are an ancient
substance; and then their dignity, because they were the seat of the divine
Spirit, more pleasing, no doubt, than the other elements were at that time.
For the darkness was still wholly shapeless, without the ornament of stars,
and the abyss was dreary, and the earth unprepared, and the heaven rough;
water alone, ever a perfect material, glad, simple, pure of its own nature,
offered a worthy vehicle to God. And what of this, that from then on the
arrangement of the world was established by God through the waters, in a way,
as its moderators? For that the firmament of heaven might be hung in the
midst, he made it between divided waters; that the dry land might be hung up,
he drew it out by separating the waters. After the world had been ordered
through the elements, and inhabitants were to be given it, the waters were
first commanded to bring forth souls. The first liquid produced what had
life, so that it is no wonder if in baptism the waters know how to give
life. For was not the work of fashioning man himself also completed with the
waters lending their aid? Material is found in the earth, but it is not fit
unless it is moist and juicy; and the waters, separated before the fourth day
into their own station, temper the earth with their remaining moisture into
clay. If I were to pursue all, or even more, of what I might recall
concerning the authority of this element, how great its power or grace is,
how many natures, how many services, what an instrument it supplies to the
world, I fear I may seem to have gathered the praises of water rather than
the reasons of baptism; though I would by that much more fully teach that it
is not to be doubted that God, who disposed this material in all his things
and works, made it also serve in his own sacraments, if it is he who governs
life on earth and provides for it in heaven.

* Chapter 4
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h4
:END:

But it will be enough to have touched on those things in which the first
principle of baptism is recognized, the one even then being marked out by the
very bearing of the Spirit of God, who from the beginning was borne above,
lingering over the waters of the baptized, as if over a figure of baptism. A
holy thing, of course, was borne over a holy one; or else the thing borne
over borrowed holiness from the one bearing it, since every underlying
material must take on the quality of what overhangs it from above, and most
of all the corporeal from the spiritual, which by the subtlety of its
substance is easy to penetrate and to indwell. Thus the nature of the waters,
sanctified by the Holy One, itself conceived the power of sanctifying. Let no
one say: Are we then washed in the very waters that were in the beginning?
Not in those waters, certainly, except insofar as the genus is one, though
the species are many. What belongs to the genus, moreover, flows down into
the species as well. And so there is no difference whether a man is washed in
the sea or a pool, in a river or a fountain, in a lake or a trough; nor does
it matter between those whom John dipped in the Jordan and those whom Peter
dipped in the Tiber; unless, too, the eunuch whom Philip dipped amid his
journeys with water that came to hand had more or less salvation conferred on
him. All waters, then, in virtue of the pristine privilege of their origin,
attain the sacrament of sanctification once God is invoked. For the Spirit
comes at once from the heavens and rests over the waters, sanctifying them
from himself; and so, once sanctified, they drink in the power of
sanctifying. Although the similitude suits the simple act itself, that since
we are defiled with sins as with dirt, we should be washed in waters. But
sins do not show themselves in the flesh, since no one carries the stain of
idolatry or uncleanness or fraud on his skin; and so things of that kind are
foul in the spirit, which is the author of sin. For the spirit rules, the
flesh serves. Yet the two share guilt with each other, the spirit by its
command, the flesh by its service. So the waters, in a way medicated through
the angel's intervention, wash the spirit corporeally and cleanse the flesh
in them spiritually.

* Chapter 5
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h5
:END:

But the nations, strangers to all understanding of spiritual powers, supply
the same efficacy to their idols; and with waters bereft, they deceive
themselves. For they are initiated through a bath into certain rites, of some
Isis or Mithras; they even carry out their own gods with washings. Moreover,
they everywhere purify villas, houses, temples, and whole cities with a
sprinkling of carried water. Certainly at the Apollinarian and Eleusinian
games they are dipped, and they presume that they are doing this for their
regeneration and the remission of their perjuries. Likewise among the
ancients, whoever had defiled himself with murder sought purifying waters.
If, then, on the mere ground that water is the proper material for washing,
they flatter themselves with omens of cleansing, how much more truly will
waters render that service through the authority of God, by whom all their
nature was established? If they think that water is medicated by religion,
what religion is more potent than that of the living God? Once that is known,
we recognize here too the effort of the devil, rivaling the things of God,
since he also practices a baptism in his own subjects. What likeness is
there? The unclean one cleanses; the one who ruins sets free; the condemned
one absolves. He will destroy his own work, presumably, by washing away the
sins which he himself inspires. These things have been set down as testimony
against those who reject the faith, so that they may at least believe the
things of God in the counterfeit attempts made by God's rival. Are there not
also, without any sacrament, unclean spirits brooding on waters, aping that
gestation of the divine Spirit in the beginning? All shady springs know this,
and all unfrequented streams, and the pools in bathhouses, and the channels
in houses, and also cisterns and wells which are said to snatch men away,
that is, by the force of a harming spirit. They call nymph-caught, and
distraught, and hydrophobic those whom the waters have destroyed, or whom
frenzy or dread has driven out of their minds. What was the point of all
this? That no one should think it too hard that a holy angel of God attends
the waters, tempering them for man's salvation, when an evil angel frequents
profane commerce with this same element to man's ruin. If the angel's
presence in the waters seems a new thing, an example of what was to come ran
ahead: an angel intervening used to stir the pool of Bethsaida. Those who
were complaining of sickness kept watch for it; for whoever had gotten ahead
of the others in descending into it, after the bath, ceased complaining. That
figure of bodily healing sang of spiritual medicine, in the form in which
things carnal always precede the spiritual as their figure. And so, as the
grace of God advanced among men, more than to the waters it came to the angel
as well. Those who used to remedy defects of the body now heal the spirit;
those who used to work temporal salvation now renew eternal; those who used
to free one man once a year now keep whole peoples safe every day, death
being done away through the ablution of sins. With guilt removed, of course
the penalty is removed too. So man is restored to God in his likeness, who
before had been after his image. The image is reckoned in the form, the
likeness in eternity. For he receives again that Spirit of God which he had
then first received from his breath, but afterward had lost through sin.

* Chapter 6
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h6
:END:

Not that in the waters we obtain the Holy Spirit, but that, cleansed in the
water under the angel, we are prepared for the Holy Spirit. Here too a figure
went first; for so John was beforehand the Lord's forerunner, preparing his
ways. So too the angel, the steward of baptism, makes the paths straight for
the Holy Spirit who is about to come, by the ablution of sins, which faith
obtains, sealed in the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit. For if by the
mouth of three witnesses every word shall stand, how much more, when through
the benediction we have the same arbiters of faith as we have sureties of
salvation, does the very number of the divine names suffice for the
confidence of our hope! And when under three the attestation of faith and the
pledge of salvation are both pawned, mention of the church is necessarily
added, because where the three are, that is, the Father and the Son and the
Holy Spirit, there is the church, which is the body of the three.

* Chapter 7
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h7
:END:

Then, coming out of the bath, we are thoroughly anointed with a blessed
unction, from the old discipline by which men were accustomed to be anointed
with oil out of a horn for the priesthood; from which Aaron was anointed by
Moses, whence Christ takes his name from chrism, which is unction, a name
which lent itself to the Lord, being made spiritual, because he was anointed
with Spirit by God the Father, as in the Acts: For they were gathered
together truly in this city against your holy son, whom you have anointed. So
also in us the unction runs carnally, but profits spiritually; just as the
carnal act of baptism itself, that we are plunged in water, has a spiritual
effect, that we are freed from sins.

* Chapter 8
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h8
:END:

Then the hand is laid on, through the benediction calling and inviting the
Holy Spirit. Shall it be granted to human ingenuity to summon a spirit into
water, and, applying suitable hands from above, to animate their union into
one body with another spirit of such brightness, while God shall not be
permitted, in his own organ, through holy hands, to sound out the spiritual
sublimity? But this too is from the old sacrament, in which Jacob blessed his
grandsons from Joseph, Ephrem and Manasseh, with his head laid on them and
his hands interchanged, and indeed crossed and slanted toward himself, so
that, sketching the figure of Christ, they even then portended the benediction
that was to be in Christ. Then that most holy Spirit, over bodies cleansed
and blessed, willingly descends from the Father; over the waters of baptism,
recognizing, as it were, his primal seat, he reposes, gliding down upon the
Lord in the figure of a dove, so that the nature of the Holy Spirit might be
declared through a creature of simplicity and innocence, a creature which
even bodily lacks the gall of the dove. And therefore he says, Be simple as
doves; nor even is this without the argument of a preceding figure. For just
as, after the waters of the flood, by which the old iniquity was purged,
after the baptism, so to speak, of the world, the dove, herald of heavenly
wrath assuaged, announced peace to the earth, sent out of the ark and
returning with the olive, which sign is also held out among the nations as a
token of peace; so by the same disposition of spiritual effect upon the
earth, that is, upon our flesh, as it emerges from the font after the old
sins, the dove of the Holy Spirit flies down, bringing the peace of God, sent
out from the heavens, where the church is figured in the ark. But the world
fell again; whereby baptism is ill compared with the flood. And so it is
destined to fire, as is also the man who after baptism renews his sins, so
that this too ought to be accepted as a sign for our admonition.

* Chapter 9
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h9
:END:

How many, then, are the patrocinies of nature, how many the privileges of
grace, how many the solemnities of discipline, figures, prefigurations,
prayers, which have ordained the religion of water! First, indeed, when the
people, set free from Egypt, escaped the violence of the king of Egypt by
crossing through the water, and the water extinguished the king himself with
all his forces, what figure is more manifest in the sacrament of baptism? The
nations are set free from the world by means of the water, to wit, and the
devil, their former master, they leave behind, overwhelmed in the water.
Again, water is remedied from its fault of bitterness to its own sweetness,
by the wood of Moses. That wood was Christ, remedying out of himself the
veins of a nature once poisoned and bitter into the all-wholesome waters of
baptism. This is the water which flowed for the people from the rock that
accompanied them. For if the rock was Christ, without doubt we see baptism
blessed by the water in Christ. How great is the grace of water in the sight
of God and his Christ, for the confirming of baptism! Christ is never without
water: if, that is, he himself is dipped in water; he inaugurates in water
the first rudiments of his power, when called to the wedding; when he makes a
discourse, he invites the thirsty to his own everlasting water; when he
teaches about agape, he approves among works of love the cup of water offered
to the poor; at the well he recruits his strength; he walks upon the water;
he gladly crosses it; he ministers water to those learning of him. The
witness of baptism lasts even to the passion: when he is given up to the
cross, water intervenes; Pilate's hands know it; when he is wounded, water
bursts out of his side; the soldier's lance knows it.

* Chapter 10
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h10
:END:

We have spoken, so far as our moderation allowed, of the whole range of
things that build up the religion of baptism; now, equally as well as I can,
I shall go forward to the rest of its state, on certain little questions. The
baptism announced by John had a question raised about it even then, proposed
by the Lord himself to the Pharisees, whether that baptism was heavenly, or
indeed earthly. About which they were unable to answer with firmness, as
being unable to understand, because not believing. We, for our part, with as
much understanding as faith, can judge that it was a divine baptism indeed,
yet of mandate, not of power; for we read that John was sent by the Lord for
this duty, and human in condition. For it furnished nothing heavenly, but
fore-ministered to things heavenly, being appointed over repentance, which is
in man's power. Finally, the doctors of the law and the Pharisees, who were
unwilling to believe, did not repent either. But if repentance is human, then
his baptism too must be of the same condition, or else it would give both the
Holy Spirit and remission of sins, if it had been heavenly. But none forgives
sins and grants the Spirit except God alone. Even the Lord himself said that
the Spirit would not otherwise come down unless he first ascended to the
Father. What the Lord was not yet conferring, the servant, of course, could
not furnish. So later in the Acts of the Apostles we find that those who had
John's baptism had not received the Holy Spirit, whom they did not know even
by hearing. It was not, then, heavenly, what did not furnish heavenly things;
since the very thing that was heavenly in John, the spirit of prophecy, so
completely failed after the transfer of the whole Spirit to the Lord, that he
sent to inquire whether he himself was the one he had preached, whom he had
marked out as coming. The baptism of repentance was dealt with, then, as a
candidate, as it were, for the remission and sanctification soon to follow in
Christ. For when he preached the baptism of repentance for the remission of
sins, the declaration was made for a future remission: since repentance goes
first, remission follows, and this is preparing the way; but he who prepares
does not himself complete, but procures for another to complete. He himself
professes that the heavenly things are not his, but Christ's, by saying, He
who is of the earth speaks of the earth; he who comes from above is above
all: and again, that he alone washed in repentance, and one would soon come
who would wash in Spirit and fire. Evidently because true and stable faith is
washed with water unto salvation, while the false and weak faith is washed
with fire, unto judgment.

* Chapter 11
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h11
:END:

But behold, they say, the Lord came and did not wash. For we read: And yet
he did not wash, but his disciples. As if John had really preached that he
himself would wash with his own hands. That is not at all how it is to be
understood, but was said in the common way of speaking; just as, for
instance, one says, The emperor has posted an edict, or, The prefect cudgelled
him: does the emperor himself post, or the prefect himself cudgel? He is
always said to do it whose service is done. So what is written, He himself
will wash you, is to be taken for, You will be washed through him, or into
him. But let it not trouble some that he himself did not wash. Into whom
would he have washed? Into repentance? What use, then, would he have made of
his forerunner? Into remission of sins, which he gave by the word? Into
himself, whom he was concealing in humility? Into the Holy Spirit, who had not
yet descended from the Father? Into the church, which the apostles had not
yet founded? And so his disciples washed as ministers, as John before as
forerunner, with the same baptism of John, lest anyone think it another,
since no other exists except that of Christ afterward, which at that time
could not be given by his learners, inasmuch as the glory of the Lord was not
yet fulfilled, nor the efficacy of the laver established through the passion
and resurrection; because neither could our death be dissolved except by the
Lord's passion, nor our life restored without his resurrection.

* Chapter 12
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h12
:END:

But when it is laid down that salvation belongs to none without baptism,
especially from that declaration of the Lord who said, Unless one be born of
water, he has not life, there arise from it the scrupulous, nay rather the
rash, retreats of certain people, how, under that rule, salvation belongs to
the apostles, whom we do not find washed in the Lord, except Paul. Indeed,
since Paul alone of them put on the baptism of Christ, either the peril of
all the others who lack the water of Christ is prejudged, so that the rule
may stand, or the rule is rescinded, if salvation is ordained even for those
not washed. I have heard such things, the Lord being witness, lest anyone
think me so abandoned as to invent out of the license of my pen things that
would strike others with scruple. And now, as well as I can, I will answer
those who deny that the apostles were washed. For if they had entered the
human baptism of John and desired the Lord's, insofar as the Lord himself had
defined one baptism, saying to Peter, who was unwilling to be washed, He who
has once bathed has no need to bathe again, which he certainly would not have
said at all to one not washed, and this is a plain proof against those who
deprive the apostles even of John's baptism, so as to destroy the sacrament
of water. Can it seem credible that in these persons the way of the Lord was
then not prepared, that is, the baptism of John, who were destined to open
the way of the Lord through the whole world? The Lord himself owed no
repentance: was it not necessary for sinners? So then, the others were not
washed, but they were not the companions of Christ, but his rivals in the
faith, the doctors of the law and the Pharisees. Whence it is also suggested,
when those who opposed the Lord refused, that those who followed the Lord
were washed, and were not of one mind with their rivals, especially since the
Lord, if they were cleaving to anyone, would have exalted John in testimony,
saying, Among those born of women there is no one greater than John the
Baptist. Others, plainly, put forward the suggestion well enough that the
apostles then filled the place of baptism, when in the little boat, sprinkled
by the waves, they were covered; and that Peter too, walking on the sea, was
well dipped. As I think, however, it is one thing to be sprinkled or
overwhelmed by the violence of the sea, and another to be washed in the
discipline of religion. Besides, that little boat bore the figure of the
church, in that it is disquieted on the sea, that is, in the world, by the
waves, that is, by persecutions and temptations, the Lord sleeping, as it
were, through patience, until, roused at last by the prayers of the saints,
he checks the world and restores tranquillity to his own. Now whether they
were washed in any way at all, or persevered unwashed; so that that saying of
the Lord about the one washing, spoken under the person of Peter, may look to
us only; still it is rash enough to pronounce about the salvation of the
apostles, because to them the prerogative of the first calling, and hence of
an intimacy never afterward divided, could confer the advantage of baptism,
since they, I suppose, followed him who promised salvation to every
believer. Your faith has saved you, he said, and, Your sins shall be
remitted you, to one believing, of course, yet not washed. If that was
lacking to the apostles, I do not know on whose word of the Lord it was that
one, roused, left the toll-booth behind, and deserted the father and the
craft by which he sustained his life, and, looking after his father,
fulfilled before he heard it that highest precept of the Lord, He who prefers
father or mother to me is not worthy of me.

* Chapter 13
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h13
:END:

Here, then, those most wicked men provoke questions. And so they say,
Baptism is not necessary for those to whom faith is enough; for Abraham, by
no sacrament of any water, but of faith, pleased God. But in all things the
ends conclude, and the things that follow prevail over the things that went
before. There was salvation in the past through bare faith, before the Lord's
passion and resurrection. But when faith was enlarged, to believe in his
nativity, passion, and resurrection, an amplification was added to the
sacrament, the sealing of baptism, the clothing, so to speak, of a faith that
before was bare, and which now cannot be without its own law. For the law of
being washed was imposed, and the form prescribed: Go, he says, teach the
nations, washing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the
Holy Spirit. That definition, joined to this law, Unless one be born again of
water and Spirit, he will not enter into the kingdom of the heavens, bound
faith to the necessity of baptism. So all who believed from then on were
washed. Then Paul too, when he believed, was washed. And this is what the
Lord had commanded him in that plague of blindness, saying, Arise, and enter
Damascus, and there it will be shown you what you ought to do, that is, to be
washed, which was the only thing he lacked. Otherwise he had sufficiently
learned and believed that the Nazarene was the Lord, the Son of God.

* Chapter 14
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h14
:END:

But they turn over from the apostle himself what he said, For Christ sent me
not to wash. As if by this argument baptism were taken away! For why did he
wash Gaius and Crispus and the house of Stephanas? Though even if Christ had
not sent him to wash, yet he had commanded other apostles to wash. But these
words were written to the Corinthians on account of the condition of that
time, since schisms and dissensions were being stirred among them, while one
attributed himself to Paul and another to Apollos. Wherefore the
peace-making apostle, lest he seem to defend all things to himself, said that
he was sent not to wash, but to preach. For preaching is first, washing
second. So preaching went first. But I think washing was also lawful for him
to whom preaching was lawful.

* Chapter 15
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h15
:END:

I do not know whether anything more is raised in the controversy about
baptism. Certainly I will unroll what I omitted above, lest I seem to cut off
the approaching senses in the middle. There is for us altogether one
baptism, both from the Lord's gospel and from the apostle's letters, since
there is one God and one baptism and one church in the heavens. But about the
heretics, indeed, what is to be kept, let anyone inquire worthily. For it was
published for us. But the heretics have no fellowship with our discipline,
whom the very taking away of communion itself testifies to be outsiders. I
ought not to acknowledge in them what is commanded me, because they do not
have the same God as we, nor one Christ, that is, the same; and therefore not
one baptism either, because not the same; which, since they do not have it
duly, without doubt they do not have it, and what is not had cannot be
counted; and so they cannot receive it either, because they do not have it.
But of this point we have already treated more fully in Greek. We enter,
then, the laver once; once sins are washed away, because they ought not to be
repeated. Besides, the Jew of Israel washes every day, because he is defiled
every day. Lest this be practised among us too, therefore the one laver was
defined. Happy the water which washes once, which is not a sport to sinners,
which, not being infected afresh by the assiduity of filth, does not again
defile those whom it washed.

* Chapter 16
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h16
:END:

There is indeed to us also a second laver, one and the same, namely of
blood, of which the Lord said, I have a baptism to be washed with, when he
had already been washed. For he had come by water and blood, as John wrote,
so that he might be washed by the water and glorified by the blood; and so to
make us, in like manner, called by water and chosen by blood. These two
baptisms he sent out from the wound of his pierced side, so that those who
believed in his blood might be washed with water, and those who had been
washed with water might also drink the blood. This is the baptism which
represents the laver both where it was not received and restores it where it
was lost.

* Chapter 17
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h17
:END:

To finish this small matter, it remains to remind you of the observance also
of giving and receiving baptism. Of giving it, the high priest, who is the
bishop, has the right; then the presbyters and deacons, yet not without the
bishop's authority, for the honor of the church, in which being preserved,
peace is preserved. Otherwise even laymen have the right. For what is
received equally can be given equally. Unless bishops, or presbyters, or
deacons, are present, those who are learning are called. The Lord's word
ought not be hidden from anyone. So then baptism, equally God's property, can
be exercised by all. But how much more does the discipline of shame and
modesty bear upon laymen, since those things belong to the greater, lest they
take to themselves the office of the bishop as one consecrated to it! The
emulation of the episcopate is the mother of schisms. All things are lawful,
said the most holy apostle, but not all things are expedient. Let it suffice,
namely in necessities, that you use it, if anywhere the condition of place or
time or person compels. For then the steadfastness of the one succoring is
taken up, when the circumstance of the one in danger presses, since he will
be guilty of the loss of a lost man if he shall delay to give what he could
freely. But the petulant woman, who has usurped the right of teaching, will
by no means also breed for herself the right of washing, unless some new
beast has arisen like the former, so that, as the former took baptism away,
so some other should confer it of her own right. But if any defend the
writings falsely written under Paul's name, the example of Thecla, for the
license of women to teach and to wash, let them know that in Asia the
presbyter who framed that writing, as if adding to Paul's title from his own,
was convicted, and confessed that he did it out of love for Paul, and left
his place. For how near to faith would it seem that he should give to a woman
the power of teaching and washing who did not even permit a woman to learn
with firmness? Let them be silent, he says, and consult their husbands at
home.

Besides, they whose office it is know that baptism is not rashly to be
believed in. Give to every one that asks you, has its own title, belonging as
it does to almsgiving. Nay, this rather is to be looked into: Give not the
holy thing to the dogs, nor throw your pearls before the swine; and, Lay not
on hands readily, lest you share in the sins of others. If Philip so readily
washed the eunuch, let us consider that a manifest and open regard of the
Lord had intervened. The Spirit had commanded Philip to press on that road;
the eunuch himself was also found not idle, nor one who would suddenly desire
to be washed, but gone to the temple for the sake of prayer, and, having
marked the divine scripture, was to be caught in this way, to whom God of his
own accord had sent an apostle, whom in turn the Spirit commanded to join
himself to the course of the eunuch: the scripture of the faith itself met
him in season; he is taken up when exhorted; the Lord is pointed out; the
faith does not delay; the water is not waited for; the apostle, the work
finished, is snatched away. But Paul, too, was in fact quickly washed. For
Simon his host had quickly known him to be a vessel constituted for election.
God's regard sends his prerogatives before it; every petition can both
deceive and be deceived. And so, according to the condition and disposition
of each person, and even age, the delay of baptism is more profitable,
principally, however, in the case of the little ones. For what need is there,
if it is not so necessary, that sponsors too be thrust into danger, who, by
their mortality, may themselves fail their promises, and by the outcome of a
bad disposition be disappointed? The Lord did say, Forbid them not to come to
me. Let them come, then, while they are growing; let them come while they are
learning, while they are taught whither they come; let them become Christians
when they have been able to know Christ. Why does the age of innocence hasten
to the remission of sins? One will act more cautiously in worldly things, so
that he who is not trusted with an earthly substance is believed for the
divine. Let them learn to ask for salvation, that you may seem to have given
to him who asks. For no less cause are the unmarried also to be deferred, in
whom the trial is prepared, both for virgins through maturity and for widows
through their freedom, until they either marry or are strengthened in
continence. If any understand the weight of baptism, they will fear the
attainment more than the delay; an unbroken faith is secure of salvation.

The Passover gives a more solemn day to baptism, when also the Lord's
passion, in which we are washed, was fulfilled. Nor will it be unfitting to
interpret for the figure, that when the Lord was about to celebrate the last
Passover, having sent his disciples to prepare, he said, You will meet a man
carrying water: by the sign of the water he showed the place for celebrating
the Passover. After that, Pentecost is a most joyful season for ordering the
lavers, in which also the Lord's resurrection was frequent among the
disciples, and the grace of the Holy Spirit was dedicated, and the hope of
the Lord's advent was hinted at, for then, when he had been received back
into the heavens, the angels told the apostles that he would come in the way
he had ascended into the heavens, namely at Pentecost. But moreover, when
Jeremiah says, And I will gather them from the ends of the earth on the feast
day, he signifies the day of Passover and of Pentecost, which is properly the
feast day. Besides, every day is the Lord's; every hour, every time is fit
for baptism: if there is a difference in the solemnity, there is none in the
grace.

For those about to enter baptism, one ought to pray with frequent prayers,
fastings, and bendings of the knee, and vigils, with the confession of all
past sins, as they set forth even the baptism of John. They were washed, it
says, confessing their own sins. It is matter of congratulation for us, if
now we confess our iniquities or our disgraces publicly. For at the same time
we both make satisfaction for the former in the struggle of flesh and
spirit, and we build ramparts against the temptations to come. Watch and
pray, he says, lest you fall into temptation. And so, I believe, they were
tempted, because they had fallen asleep, so that they deserted the Lord when
he was seized, and he who had stood by him and used the sword even denied him
three times; for it had gone before as a saying that no one untried should
attain the celestial kingdoms. The Lord himself, straight after the laver,
was beset by temptations, having spent forty days in fasts. Therefore,
someone will say, we too ought rather to fast after the laver. And who
forbids it, unless the necessity of joy and the congratulation of salvation?
But the Lord, I think, in what a little thing, retorted upon himself the
reproach from the figure of Israel. For the people, having crossed the sea
and been carried into the wilderness for forty years, there when it was fed
with divine stores, nevertheless remembered its belly and its gullet more
than God. Then the Lord, after the water, withdrawn into the wilderness,
having measured out fasts of forty days, showed that the man of God lives not
by bread, but by the word of God, and that the temptations laid against the
fullness and immoderacy of the belly are ground down by abstinence. Blessed,
then, are they whom the grace of God awaits, when, ascending from that most
holy laver of the new nativity, they open their hands for the first time in
the mother with the brothers, ask of the Father, ask of the Lord, the
specials of grace, the distributions of charisms, to lie open. Ask, and you
shall receive, he says. For you have sought, and you have found; you have
knocked, and it has been opened to you. Only this I beg, that, when you ask,
you remember Tertullian the sinner too.
