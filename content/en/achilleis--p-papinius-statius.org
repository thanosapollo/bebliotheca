Tell, goddess, of the great-souled son of Aeacus, the offspring feared by the
Thunderer and forbidden to succeed to his father's heaven, though the hero's
deeds are much renowned in Maeonian song, yet more remains: be willing that we
go through the whole hero (such is our love), and bring him forth hiding on
Scyros with the Dulichian trumpet, and not halt at Hector dragged, but lead
the young man through all of Troy.
Only, if we have drained the old spring with a worthy draught, give me new
fountains, Phoebus, and bind my hair with a second spray: for I do not strike
the Aonian grove as a stranger, nor do my temples now swell with their first
fillets. The Dircaean field knows it, and Thebes numbers me among the ancient
names of her fathers, together with her own Amphion.
But you, whom Italian valor and Greek alike marvel at as far first, for whom
the twin laurels of poets and of captains flourish in rivalry (long has the
one grieved to be outdone), grant pardon and suffer me to sweat a little while
in this anxious dust. We undertake you with long preparation not yet
confident, and great Achilles is a prelude for you.

The Dardan shepherd had loosed his fleet from the Oebalian shore, having
blandly ravaged unsuspecting Amyclae, and bearing the full forewarnings of his
mother's dream he was tracing again the guilty path, where Helle, now a Nereid
hidden in the sea, commands the hated waves: when Thetis beneath the glassy
flood started in fear at the oars of Ida (alas, never vain the omens of
parents!).
Without delay, a crowd of her sisters accompanying her, she sprang from her
watery chambers. The meeting shores of Phrixus seethe, and the narrow sea does
not unfold room for the ladies.

When first she rose into the air, the sea shaken apart: 'This seeks me,' she
says, 'this fleet threatens me with ruin. I recognize the warnings and Proteus
who spoke true. See, Bellona brings Priam a new daughter-in-law, with torches
raised from the stern. Already I see the Ionian and the Aegean pressed by a
thousand keels; nor is it enough that the whole region of the Greek-born
conspires with the swollen sons of Atreus: already by sea and land my Achilles
will be sought, and he himself will wish to follow. Why else did we commit his
cradle, when he was small, to Pelion and the cave of a grim master? There,
unless I am wrong, the reckless boy plays at the battles of the Lapiths and
already measures himself by his father's spear.'
'O grief, O fears late in a mother's heart! Could I not, unhappy, when first
the Rhoetean timbers fell upon our flood, raise the great sea and pursue the
incestuous pirate's sails with a deep storm, and bring all my sisters against
him? Even now, but it is late; already the injury of the stolen woman is
complete. Still I will go, and embracing the gods of the sea and the right
hand of favoring Jove, which is left, I will, a pitiable suppliant, beg
through the years of Tethys and of my aged father for one winter.'
She spoke, and in that moment she sees the great king. He was coming from
Ocean as his guest, glad from the feast and his face steeped in sea nectar:
hence storms and winds are silent, and in a quiet song the armor-bearing
Tritons go, and the craggy sea-beasts and Tyrrhene herds wheel round him and
within, having saluted their king. He himself stands high upon the calm waves
and with his triple spear bids his yoked team go. They gather a foaming course
from their breasts; behind they swim and blot the tracks of their feet with
their tails.

Then Thetis: 'O father and ruler of the great deep, do you see to what
wretched uses you have thrown the sea open? The crimes of the lands go with
safe sails, from the time the Pagasaean ship of Jason broke the laws of the
strait and its withdrawn majesty with plunder. See, another crime by theft,
carrying a guest's spoils, the rash arbiter of unjust Ida sails. Alas, what
groans he will give to earth and heaven, what groans to me! So we pay for the
joys of the Phrygian palm. These are the ways of Venus; this is the gift of a
grateful foster-child?'
'These ships at least (do they bring back demigods and our Theseus?) if any
honor still belongs to the waves, overwhelm, or give me the sea! There is no
cruelty in it: let it be right for me to have feared for my son. Grant me to
drive away grief, and do not let it please you that of so many waters I
inhabit one shore and the rocks of an Ilian tomb.'

She was pleading, her cheeks torn and her breast bare, and she stood in the
way of the sea-green horses; but the ruler of the waters invites her into his
chariot and soothes her with friendly words: 'Do not seek, Thetis, in vain to
sink the Dardan fleet. The fates forbid it. A fixed order has pleased the
gods, that Europe and Asia mingle bloody hands, and Jupiter has proclaimed the
decrees of war and years grim with slaughter. What a son you will see there in
the Sigean dust, what funerals of Phrygian mothers you will look upon as
victor, when your Aeacides now floods the Teucrian fields with warm blood, now
forbids the thickened rivers to go forth, and slows his chariot with Hector's
corpse, and with his hand will drive against our walls, works in vain! Cease
now to complain of Peleus and a lesser marriage: you will believe you have
borne a son to Jove; nor will you grieve unavenged, and you shall use the
kindred straits. I will grant you to raise the waves when the Danaans return
and Caphereus puts forth his night signals, and we alike will hunt dreaded
Ulysses.'

He had spoken. She, her face cast down at the heavy refusal, who was already
preparing to stir the strait and war against the Ilian ships, devising other
plans in her mind, sadly turns her arms toward the Haemonian lands. Three
times she tried with her hand, three times she thrust back the liquid sea with
her steps, and the Thessalian shallows strike her snow-white soles.
The mountains rejoice and open the folds of their bridal caves, and Sperchios
runs wide to meet the goddess and circles her steps with sweet water. She
takes no joy in the places, but works over the plans begun in her breast, and,
cunning, with piety as her teacher, she seeks aged Chiron.
The steep house bores through the mountain and hangs Pelion in a long arch.
Part was hollowed by hand, part its own age had broken; yet the signs of the
gods' couches are shown, and the place each consecrated by reclining and by
his genius. But within, the Centaur's deep stalls lie open, not like those of
his unspeakable brothers. Here are spears that have known no human blood, no
ashes lopped in wedding wars, no mixing-bowls smashed against kindred enemies,
but innocent quivers and the empty hides of wild beasts. These too while he
was green; for then his one labor, unarmed, was to know the health-giving
herbs for doubtful living things, or to show the old heroes to his pupil on
the lyre.

And then, waiting on the first threshold for him who would return from the
hunt, he hastens the feast and brightens the house with a broad fire: when the
Nereid was seen rising far off from the shore. He bursts from the woods (joy
gives strength) and the old one's familiar hoof rang on the unaccustomed
plain. Then, blandishing with his right hand and stooping to his lowest
shoulders, he leads her into his poor roof and points out the cave.
Already Thetis surveys everything with silent gaze, and not enduring delay:
'Where is my pledge, Chiron, tell,' she says, 'or why does the boy now spend
any time without you? Not without cause is a mother's restless sleep and the
black signs of the gods, and great fears: would that they were false! For now
I see swords hostile to my womb, now my hands livid with beating, now savage
beasts going at my breasts; often I myself (unspeakable!) carry my son down to
empty Tartarus and again to plunge him in the Stygian springs.'
'The Carpathian seer bids me abolish these fears by the order of a magic rite,
and to purify the boy in secret waters under a completed axis, where the
farthest shores of Ocean are and father Pontus grows warm with the sliding
stars. There, dreadful expiations to unknown gods, and gifts, but it is long
to count all, and I am forbidden; rather, hand him over!' So the mother
invented: for he would not have given him, if she had dared to confess to the
old man the soft dress and the shameful coverings.

Then he himself replies: 'Lead him, best of mothers, I pray, lead him, and
bend the gods with humble prayer. For your vows surpass measure, and much envy
must be appeased. I do not add fear, but I will confess the truth: some great
thing (nor do my father's omens fail me) his hastening force prepares, and it
overtakes his slender years. Once he used even to bear threats and eagerly
hear commands, and not go far from our cave: now Ossa does not hold him, nor
huge Pelion, nor the snows of Thessaly. The Centaurs themselves often complain
to me of houses plundered and herds dragged off before their eyes, and that
they themselves are put to flight from fields and rivers: they prepare ambush
and war, and they threaten, swollen with pride. Once indeed, when the
Thessalian pine carried the Argive kings this way, I saw the young Alcides and
Theseus, but I am silent.'
A cold pallor transfixes the Nereid. He was at hand, greater with much sweat
and dust, and yet among arms and hurried labors still sweet to see: a purple
fire swims on his snow-white face, and his hair shines more pleasing than
tawny gold. Not yet is his first age changed by new down, and quiet torches
are in his eyes, and much of his mother is in his face: such as hunter Apollo
when he returns from Lycia and exchanges the plectrum for savage quivers.
By chance he is at hand and glad (O how much joy adds to beauty!): he had
struck with iron a pregnant lioness under Pholoe's crag and had left her in
the empty cave, but he brings the cubs and incites their claws. Yet when his
mother was seen on the faithful threshold he throws them aside and binds her,
caught up, with greedy arms, already heavy in the embrace and already equal to
his mother in height.

Patroclus follows, already then bound by great love, and stretches himself as
a rival to such deeds, equal in pursuits and in the measure of age, but far
behind in strength, and yet destined to see Pergamum with equal fate.
At once he goes down with a swift leap to the nearest river and renews his
smoking cheeks and hair in the springs: as Castor enters the shallows of
Eurotas on a panting horse and wakes the weary radiance of his own star.
The old man marvels and attends him, now stroking his breast, now his strong
shoulders. Her own joys choke the mother. Then Chiron asks them to pour the
feast and the gifts of Bacchus, and weaving various delights for the
astonished woman he brings out at last the lyre and moves the strings that
soothe cares, and lightly gives the boy the chords tried by his thumb. He
sings gladly the huge seeds of praise: how many commands of his swollen
stepmother the son of Amphitryon overcame, with what raw caestus Pollux
overwhelmed the Bebrycian, with how great a grip the son of Aegeus broke the
Minoan arms of the bull, and last the mother's marriage-bed and Pelion
burdened with gods. Here anxious Thetis smiled with a conquered face.
Night draws them into sleep. The huge Centaur sinks on the rock, and
blandishing Achilles twines himself on his shoulders, though his faithful
parent is there, and prefers the accustomed breast.

But Thetis, standing through the night on the wave-sounding rocks, turns over
this way and that with divided mind what hiding-place she wants for her son,
in what lands she means to conceal him. Thrace is nearest, but too much given
to the pursuits of Mars. Nor does the hard race of the Macedonians please her,
nor the sons of Cecrops, who would give goads of glory, nor Sestos and the
bays of Abydos, too convenient for keels. She is pleased to go through the
high Cyclades. Here are despised Myconos and low Seriphos, and Lemnos unjust
to men, and Delos, host to the nations. Lately, sent to follow Aegaeon
loosening his hard bonds and to count the hundred chains of the god, she had
heard from the unwarlike hall of Lycomedes the gatherings of maidens and the
shores ringing with play. This pleases her; this land is safest for a fearful
mother.
As a bird, already busy with near birth and already afraid, considers on what
leaf she may hang her empty home, here she looks out for winds, here in
anxiety she thinks of snakes, here of men: at last the shade pleases her in
her doubt, and she has scarcely stood on the new branches and at once the tree
is loved.
Another care remains to the plan and wearies the sad goddess: whether she
herself, having embraced her son in her bosom, should carry him through the
waves, or on great Triton, or call the flying winds, or Thaumantis's daughter,
who is accustomed to feed on the sea. Then she draws from the straits and
bridles with sharp murex a yoked pair of dolphins, whom greatest Tethys had
nursed for her in the Atlantic flood under a sounding valley of the sea (to
none through Neptune's shallows is there so great honor of sea-green beauty
and power of swimming, nor more of a human heart). She bids them halt at the
full shore, lest the contagion of the naked earth harm them.
She herself then carries Achilles, relaxed in all his breast with the sleep
that belongs to boys, from the rocks of the Haemonian cave to the calm waters
and the shores bidden to be silent. Cynthia shows the way and flashes with her
whole orb. Chiron follows the goddess and, secure of the sea, asks for a swift
return, and hides his wet eyes, and from his horse reared up looks after them,
snatched away suddenly and now already hiding, where the white signs of flight
foam a little and the track dies in the liquid sea. Already sad Pholoe,
already cloudy Othrys groan for him who will not otherwise return to
Thessalian Tempe, and Spercheos thinner in his waters, and the mute cave of
the learned old man. The Fauns seek the boyish songs, and the Nymphs weep the
marriage long hoped for.

Already day presses the stars, and from the low sea Titan unrolls his dewy
horses, and the sea lifted by the chariot from the great aether falls. But the
mother, having long measured the waves, was holding the Scyrian shallows, and
the dolphins, weary, had come out from their mistress's yoke: when the boy's
shaken rest and opening eyes felt the day poured in. He is stunned in the
first air: what places, what waters, where is Pelion? He sees all things
turned and unknown, and he hesitates to recognize his mother.
She seizes him by the hand and addresses the frightened boy blandly: 'If a
just lot had brought me the marriage it was giving, dear boy, I would hold
you, a great star, embraced in the ethereal regions, and as one who had borne
for the great heaven I would fear nothing from the lowly Fates and earthly
destinies. Now, son, your birth is unequal, and the way of death is closed
only from your mother's side. Nay, even the times to be feared draw near, and
dangers brought to the last turning-posts. Let us yield; submit a little your
manly spirit, and deign to wear my dress.'
'If the Tirynthian bore hard Lydian wool in his hand and soft spears, if it
befits Bacchus to sweep his steps with a gilded robe, if Jupiter put on a
maiden's limbs, and doubtful sexes did not break great Caeneus: allow me, I
pray, by this means to go out from the threats and the malignant cloud. Soon I
will give you back again the fields, again the Centaur's haunts. By this glory
and the coming joys of youth, if for your sake I have known the lands and a
lowly husband, if I armed you, born, in the stern stream of Styx (and would it
had been all of you!), take for a little while these safe coverings that will
harm the spirit not at all. Why do you draw back your face, or what are your
eyes preparing? Are you ashamed to grow mild in this dress? By you, dear boy,
by the kindred seas I swear: Chiron will not know this.'
So she handles his shaggy breast, soothing in vain. His father stands against
her as she asks, and his huge nurse, and the raw beginnings of a great nature:
as if one should try to submit to first reins a horse swollen with the fire of
unbridled youth. He, long rejoicing in fields and rivers and proud honor, does
not offer his neck to the yoke nor his rough mouth to the bits, and chafes, a
captive, to enter a master's commands, and marvels to learn other courses.

What god conferred frauds and cunning on the astonished parent? What mind drew
down unteachable Achilles? By chance Scyros was celebrating a day of honors
for Pallas of the shore. And the sisters born of gentle Lycomedes, on the
sacred morning, had gone out from their father's walls (a rare license) to
give the riches of spring and to bind the goddess's stern hair with leaf and
sprinkle her spear with flowers. All had outstanding grace of form, all the
same dress, and, the tender bound of shame already fulfilled, a virginity ripe
for the bed and swelling years.
But as much as the beauty of the sea, added, overwhelms the green Nymphs, or
as much as Diana leaves the Naiads behind in her shoulders, so much does
Deidamia shine, queen of the fair chorus, and stands in her sisters' way. In
her the purple is kindled on a rosy face, and there is greater light in her
gems and gold more winning; and her form is equal even to the goddess herself,
if she should lay aside the snakes of her breast and her face be calmed with
the helmet removed.
When he saw her leading the allied ranks far off, the fierce boy, his breast
profaned by no motion, stiffened, and drank a new fire into his bones from
without. Nor is the love he has drunk hidden, but the torch shaken in his
marrow returns to his face and features, and tints the light of his cheeks,
and wanders over him, struck with a thin sweat. As when the Massagetae darken
milky cups with purple blood, or ivory is stained with dye: so the sudden
flame is plain by various marks (he pales and blushes). He would go and of his
own will, wild, scatter the stranger rites, careless of the crowd and
forgetful of his age, did not shame and reverence for his mother at his side
hold him.
As a father of the herd, once, and a leader to be, whose horns are not yet
completed in a full circle, when he sees a heifer, partner of the pasture, of
snowy brightness, his spirits catch fire, and first love foams through his
mouth; the herdsmen look on, glad, and stand in the way.
The mother, already aware, seizes the moment: 'Is it a burden, son, to feign
the dances among these and to weave your arms in play? What of this kind was
there under cold Ossa and the ridges of Pelion? O if it should befall me to
join my cares and carry another Achilles in my bosom!' He is soothed and
blushes with pleasure, and casts wanton glances aside, and with a lighter hand
pushes back the clothes. The mother sees him doubtful and willing to be
compelled, and throws the folds upon him.
Then she softens his stiff neck and lowers his heavy shoulders and loosens his
strong arms, and tames his uncombed hair in a sure order, and transfers her
own necklace to the beloved neck; and checking his steps with a pictured hem
she teaches him gait and motion and shame in speech. As wax that will live
under an artist's thumb takes forms and follows fire and hand: such was the
image of the goddess changing her son. Nor did she struggle long; for much
grace remains to him, though valor is unwilling, and his doubtful sex, hiding
by a thin difference, deceives those who look.

They go forward, and bland Thetis, warning again and wearying him again: 'So
then you will bear your step, so your face and hands, son, and you will
imitate your companions with feigned ways, lest the ruler not admit you,
suspect, to his soft court, and the lies of the theft begun perish.' She
speaks, and with touch brought near she does not stand off from arranging him.
So when Hecate, wearied in maiden Therapnae, returns to her father and
brother, her mother clings as companion to her as she goes, and herself veils
her shoulders and bared arms; herself sets the bow and quivers and draws down
the hiding dress, and is eager to compose the scattered hair.

At once she approaches the king, and there, the altars witnessing: 'This
sister of our Achilles, ruler,' she says, 'we hand to you (do you not see how
grim she is in the cheeks, and to be matched with her brother?).
High-spirited, she was seeking to bear arms and a bow on her shoulders and to
drive off marriage in Amazon fashion. But I have cares enough for the male
stock. Let this one bear baskets and the rites; you break her, unteachable, by
ruling, and hold her by her sex until the marriageable age and shame to be
loosed. And do not grant her to practice wanton games of the palaestra or to
wander in the haunts of the woods; rear her within and shut her among girls
like herself. Remember especially to keep her from shore and harbor. You have
just seen the sails of the Phrygians: already keels sent across the sea have
learned to cheat mutual rights.'
The father assents to her words, and by a parent's nature receives the hidden
son of Aeacus (who would stand against the frauds of the gods?). Of his own
will he even venerates her with a suppliant right hand and, chosen, gives
thanks. Nor does the crowd of pious Scyrian girls cease to fix with too much
gaze the face of the new maiden, how much she stands out in neck and hair, how
much she spreads in shoulders and breast. Then they urge her to join the
dances and come to the chaste rites; they yield place and rejoice to touch
her.
As Idalian birds, when they break the soft clouds, long gathered in sky and
home, if a bird has joined wings and come as a guest from a different tract,
all at first marvel and shrink: soon they fly nearer and nearer, and in the
air itself they have gradually made her their own, and glad they circle her
with favoring beat and lead her to the high nests.
The mother departs, having delayed much on the threshold, while she repeats
her warnings and plants secret murmurs in his ears and gives last words with a
silent face. Then, received by the strait, her neck bent far back, she swims
away and addresses the shores with winning vows: 'Land dear to me, to whom we
have committed the pledges of a great care and a huge deposit by timid
cunning, be happy and be silent, I pray, in the manner Crete was silent for
Rhea. Long honor and eternal temples will gird you, nor will you be outdone in
fame by unstable Delos; sacred to winds and straits, and among the shoal
Cyclades, where Aegean storms break the rocks, a quiet house of Nereids and an
island for sailors to swear by: only do not admit Danaan keels, do not, I
pray! Here let there be only revel-bands and nothing useful for wars (teach
fame to tell this), and while Dorian arms are prepared and Mars rages between
the two worlds, I yield indeed: let Achilles be a maiden of pious Lycomedes.'

Meanwhile avenging Europe, kindled by the sweet furies of arms and the
suppliant complaint of kings, stirs her deserved griefs. For that son of
Atreus canvasses the more, whose wife is at home, and he sharpens the Ilian
crime in the telling: the offspring of heaven captured without Mars, without
arms, the foster-child of powerful Sparta; laws, faith, the gods above
trampled by one rape. This the Phrygian treaty? These the dealings of the twin
lands? What awaits the peoples, when so great an injury has come down upon the
first leaders?
Every race and age comes together: not only those are roused whom the Isthmian
barriers shut with a wall of two seas, or whom the boss of wave-sounding Malea
circles, but far off those whom the path of Phrixus forbids Europe and Asia to
be joined, and the peoples whom in order the wave of the upper sea binds on
the Abydenian shore.
Love of war seethes and raises the shaken cities. Temese tames bronze, the
Euboean shore is shaken with dockyards, Mycenae rings with countless anvils,
Pisa renews chariots, Nemea gives the hides of beasts, Cirrha strives to pack
arrow-bearing quivers, Lerna to clothe heavy shields with slaughtered
bullocks. The Aetolian and the rough Acarnanian give infantry for war, Argos
drives squadrons, the pastures of rich Arcadia are emptied, Epirus bridles her
swift nurslings, Phocis and the Aonian shades grow thin of javelins, Pylos and
Messene stretch the engines of walls. No ground is exempt. Arms long laid
aside by fathers are torn from high doorposts, and the gifts of the gods melt
in the flame: Mars carries off gold snatched from the gods above.
Nowhere are the old shades: Othrys is lesser and steep Taygetus sinks; the
mountains, stripped, have seen the bronze. Already every grove swims: oaks are
felled for the fleet, the lesser wood for oars. Iron is wearied for countless
uses, to bind beaks, to guard arms, to bridle war-horses, to join scaly tunics
with a thousand chains, to smoke with blood and drink deep wounds, to drive
deaths with conspiring poison. They thin the wet stones by rubbing and add
anger to sluggish points. There is no measure in bending bows or pouring
slings or hardening stakes or raising helmets with crests.
Among these motions Thessaly alone groans at her dull quiet and accuses the
fates with twin complaints, that Peleus is too old and Achilles not yet ripe.
Already the Lord of War, hurling men and horses madly onto the benches, had
drained the lands of Pelops and the Greek world. The harbors seethe and the
pools covered with keels, and the fleet, moved out, raises its own storms and
its own waves; already the sea itself fails the sterns, and the canvas
consumes all the winds.

Hecatean Aulis first gathers the Danaan ships, Aulis climbing the Euboean sea
from exposed rocks and the ledge of a long ridge, shores much loved by the
mountain-wandering goddess, and beside her Caphereus lifting his head barked
at by the sea. When he saw the Pelasgian ships crossing, three times from the
mountain, three times from the waves he thundered and gave forewarnings of a
savage night.
There the gathering of arms fatal to Troy, there huge war is sworn, until the
yearly sun should finish all his turning-posts. Then first Greece looked upon
her own strength; then the scattered and dissonant mass comes together into a
body and a face and is arranged under one king. So a curved hunting-net shuts
hidden beasts and gradually narrows them with the toils brought near. They
fear fire and sound and leave the spread wilderness and marvel that their
mountain shrinks, until they have fallen from every side into a narrow valley;
and in turn the herds are stunned and grow tame with shared fear: at once the
shaggy boar, at once the bear and the wolf are penned, and the hind has
scorned the captured lions.
But although the twin sons of Atreus alike take up their wars, and Tydides and
Sthenelus press with greedy valor their father's fame, and Antilochus does not
think of his years, and Ajax flashes with a shield of seven ox-kings and a
circle equal to walls, and watchful Ulysses contends in counsels and in arms:
the whole host of the war burns for absent Achilles. They love the name of
Achilles, and against Hector Achilles alone is demanded. Him alone they speak
as fatal to the Teucrians and to Priam. For who else has grown in the
Haemonian valleys, crawling over the dug snow? Whose raw beginnings and tender
years has the Centaur undertaken and shaped? Who is nearer by the line of his
father's heaven, or what other son has a Nereid borne secretly through the
Stygian streams and fenced his fair limbs with iron?
These things the Greek cohorts repeat and hand on in the camps; the crowd of
leaders yields and not sadly confesses to be outdone. So when the pale
heaven-dwellers were gathering into the Phlegraean camp, and already Gradivus
was rising into his Odrysian spear, and Tritonia was lifting her Libyan
snakes, and the Delian was bending his huge bow with his hand, panting Nature
stood in fear, looking only to the Thunderer: when would he call storms and
thunder from the clouds, how many bolts would he demand of fire-bearing Aetna?

And there, while the kings, walled by the mixed crowd of their own, consult
the seasons of sea and of war, Protesilaus, chiding the seer Calchas with a
great uproar (for in him the desire to fight is outstanding, and already then
the glory of first death was given), says: 'O too forgetful of Phoebus and of
your own tripods, son of Thestor, when will you more justly move a mouth
possessed by the god, or why do you not open the secrets of the Fates 3? Do
you see how all are stunned and roar at the unknown son of Aeacus? The
Calydonian hero is cheap in the crowd, and Ajax born of great Telamon, and
Ajax the second, and we too: but Mars and Troy seized will prove it.'
'Him, the leaders neglected (shame, alas!), all love as a warlike divinity.
Speak quickly, or why are garlands on your hair and much honor? In what shores
is he hidden, or in what land do you bid him be sought? For rumor says he
dwells neither in Chiron's cave nor in the hall of his father Peleus. Come,
burst in upon the gods and harass the hiding fates, and drain, if ever, most
greedily the laurel-bearing fires! We have remitted to you the dreadful arms
and savage swords; never shall a helmet violate these unwarlike fillets: but
you will be happy and more excellent than every number of leaders, if through
you you secure great Achilles for the Danaans.'

Already the son of Thestor carries his eyes around with a trembling motion,
and by first pallor confesses the entering god; soon twisting his cheeks with
fire and blood he sees neither comrades nor camp, but blind and absent now he
catches in the aether the great gatherings of those above, now he addresses
prophetic birds, now the hard threads of the sisters, now in anxiety he
consults the incense-bearing altars and snatches the peak of the flames and
feeds on the sacred murk. His hair starts out and the fillet labors on stiff
locks, neither is his neck in place nor his step in order. At last, trembling,
he looses his mouth, weary with long bellowings, and a voice has struggled out
against the opposing fury:
'Whither do you snatch the huge pupil of great Chiron with womanish tricks,
Nereid? Send him here: why do you take him away? I will not suffer it: he is
mine, mine. You are a goddess of the deep, and Phoebus drives me. In what
hiding-places do you try to conceal the overthrower of Asia? I see you through
the high Cyclades, astonished, seeking shores in a shameful theft. We are
undone: the knowing land of Lycomedes has pleased her. O crime! See, flowing
clothes come upon his breast. Tear them, boy, tear them, and do not yield to a
fearful parent. Woe is me, he is snatched and goes! What shameless maiden is
this far off?'
Here, his step wavering, he stood, and with the strength of the fury lost he
fell trembling before the very altars. Then the Calydonian hero seizes the
hesitating Ithacan: 'That labor calls us: nor would I refuse to go as
companion, if your care should draw me, though he be pressed in the sounding
caves of averted Tethys and in the lap of watery Nereus. You will find him;
only, far-seeing, stretch a watchful mind with cunning and raise a fertile
breast. In doubtful cases no seer would dare to see the fates before me.'
Glad Ulysses adds: 'So may the almighty god confirm it, so may that paternal
virgin nod to you! But slippery hope delays me: it is a great thing indeed to
lead armed Achilles into the camps; but if the fates deny it, how foul and sad
to return! Still I will not leave the vows of the Danaans untried. And even
now either the Peleian hero will be here with me, or the truth lies hidden
deep and Calchas is without Apollo.'
The Danaans shout, and Agamemnon goads the willing. The gatherings loosen and
the ranks, released with glad murmur, depart: as when night is already near
birds are brought back from pasture, or mild Hybla sees her troops return into
the caves, heavy with new honey. Without delay the Ithacan canvas already asks
the right-hand breezes, and the youth sat glad at the oars.

But far off Deidamia alone had already known the son of Aeacus, hidden under
the image of a false sex, as a man by a hiding theft; yet conscious of a
covered fault she fears all things and thinks her silent sisters perceive. For
when hard Achilles stood in the maiden flock and his mother, departing, had
loosed his raw shame, at once he chose a companion, though the whole crowd
comes together upon him, and blandly he moves new snares against her fearing
no such thing. Her he follows and presses, shameless; her he takes up again
and again with his eyes. Now he clings too much to the side of her who does
not avoid him; now with light garlands, now with baskets slipped of their own
will, now he strikes her with a sparing thyrsus; now he shows the sweet
strings of the known lyre and the thin measures and the songs of Chiron, and
leads her hand and breaks her fingers on the sounding cithara; now he seizes
the mouth of her as she sings and binds her in embraces and praises her
through a thousand kisses.
She learns gladly on what peak Pelion is, and who the son of Aeacus is, and
she is stunned at the boy's name and deeds heard without cease, and she sings
of Achilles present. She herself also shows him how to put forth his strong
limbs more modestly and to thin the raw wools with a worn thumb, and she
remakes the distaffs and the lost tasks with a hard hand; and she marvels at
the sound of his voice and the weight of him holding, and that he flees his
companions, that he carries himself with too much light, and that he is
untimely breathless in his words. Already now preparing to open the tricks he
flees with maiden lightness and forbids him to confess. So under mother Rhea
the young ruler of Olympus gave insidious kisses to his unsuspecting sister,
still a brother, until reverence for the blood between them yielded and the
sister started in fear at loves turned.
At last the timid Nereid's cunning was uncovered. A grove stood lofty for the
rites of Agenor's Bacchus, and a wood admitted to the sky. In its shade the
pious mothers had been accustomed to renew the alternate trieteric feast, and
to carry a torn flock and beams torn from the earth, and to furnish the god
with welcome furies. The law is that males go far off. The revered leader
repeats the precepts, and the cave is proclaimed inaccessible to men; nor is
it enough: a fearful priestess stands at the given bound and explores the
approaches, lest any violator wander in the womanish troop. Silent Achilles
laughed to himself.
Him, leading the standards of the maiden band and loosing his great arms with
a difficult motion (and the sex equally becomes him, and his mother's lies),
the companions marvel at, and no longer is Deidamia the fairest of her crowd,
and, brought so near the proud son of Aeacus, she is outdone as much as she
herself presses her sisters.
But when he sent down the fawnskin from his smooth neck and gathered the
wandering folds with ivy and bound high his yellow temples with purple fillets
and shook the wreathed missile with a heavy right hand, the crowd stands in
astonished fear, and, the rites left, they are pleased to go round him and to
lift their downcast faces. Such is Euhius when he has relaxed face and spirit
at Thebes and has sated his breast with ancestral luxury: he lifts the
garlands from his hair and the mitre and arms the green thyrsus and, stronger,
visits the hostile Indians.

The Moon was climbing the summit of mid-heaven on her rosy yoke, when sleep
more inert on all its wings flows down upon the lands and embraces the mute
world. The choruses have sat, and the bronze, a little exercised by the beat,
is silent, when Achilles, alone from the tender troop, says this to himself:
'How long will you bear the inventions of a fearful parent, and first lose the
unwarlike prison's flower of spirit? Are Mars's weapons not allowed to your
right hand, nor to drive frightened beasts, where are the Haemonian plain and
rivers? Do you seek my swims, Sperchios, and the hair I promised? Or is there
no honor for a deserter pupil? Am I already said to be snatched far to the
Stygian shades, and does bereft Chiron beat my funeral?'
'You now, Patroclus, direct weapons in your hand, you my bow, and you mount
the yoked team reared for me: but I already know how to spread my arms with
vine-wreathed thyrsi and to thin the distaff (it shames me and wearies me to
confess this!). Nay even, captured, you dissemble night and day the fire of a
beloved maiden and a torch of equal age. How long will you press the wounds
burning the breast, and not even by love (shame, alas!) prove yourself a
male?'

So he speaks; and glad that in the dense shade of night the silences lie
timely numb for his thefts, he takes his vows by force and with his whole
breast he brings true embraces. The whole chorus of the stars saw from on
high, and the horns of the tender Moon blushed. She indeed filled grove and
mountain with a cry: but the companions of Bacchus, the cloud of slumber
shaken off, think the signals have been given for the dances. A known crash is
raised from every side, and Achilles was again shaking the thyrsi. Yet first
he soothed the doubtful girl with friendly words:
'I am he (why do you tremble?), whom the sea-blue mother, born almost to Jove,
sent to be reared in Thessalian woods and snows. Nor would I have undergone
these clothes or the shameful coverings, had you not been seen first on the
shore. I yielded for your sake; for you I bear the wool in my hand, for you
the soft drums. Why do you weep, added as a daughter-in-law to the great sea?
Why do you groan, you who will bear huge grandsons for heaven? But your
father: Scyros will lie cut out by fire and iron, and these walls, turned,
will go into swollen storms, before you pay for my marriage with a savage
death. We will not so obey a mother in all things.'

The princess was stunned, terrified by so great prodigies, though the faith
had long been suspected, and she shuddered at him close and at the
much-changed face of him confessing. What should she do? Should she herself
bear her own mishap to her parent and press at once herself and the young man,
who might perhaps drink bitter punishments? And still there remained in her
heart that love long deceived. Sick, she is silent and already presses a
common crime. She is pleased to add one nurse as partner to the thefts, who,
conquered by the prayers of both, nodded. She by silent cunning hid the stolen
shame and the rising womb and the months sick under the weight, until Lucina,
the revealer, brought the appointed times with full turning-posts and loosed
the birth.

And already the Laertian ship was going through the Aegean bends, and the
breezes 4 were changing countless Cyclades: already Paros and Olearos lie
hidden; already high Lemnos is grazed and Bacchic Naxos shrinks from the
stern, Samos growing before the eyes; already Delos darkens the sea. There
from the high stern they pour cups and pray for the faith of the response and
for Calchas true.
The Archer heard and drove Zephyr from the peak of Cynthus and gave omens to
the doubtful with a full sail. The ship goes secure on the sea: for the high
commands of the Thunderer were keeping Thetis from turning the sure laws of
the fates, sick with tears and fearing many things, because she might not root
up the sea and follow with all winds and straits Ulysses, already then hated.
Phoebus was already breaking his rays, prone on low Olympus, and was promising
the penetrable shore of Ocean to his panting horses, when rocky Scyros raised
herself. Upon her the Laertian leader sent out all the sheets from the stern
and bids his comrades take up the sea again and supply the falling Zephyrs
with oars. They draw near, and more and more Scyros was undoubted, and
Tritonia guardian above the calm shore. Having gone out, the Aetolian and the
Ithacan venerate the power of the friendly goddess.
Then the far-seeing hero, lest they terrify the host walls by a sudden
gathering, bids his men remain on the ship; he himself with faithful Diomedes
seeks the heights. But already Abas, watcher of the shore citadel, had gone
before and had reported to the king that unknown sails, yet Greek, were coming
up to the lands. They go forward, like twin wolves joined by a pact under a
winter night: though their own hunger and their cubs' strike them, they wholly
dissemble rage and threats and go low, lest the care of dogs announce enemies
and warn the trembling masters to watch.

So the heroes go slow and wear with alternating talk the open field that lies
between the harbor and the high city. First keen Tydides seizes the word: 'By
what method do we now prepare to search out the truth? For I have long turned
it under a doubtful breast, why you have bought unwarlike thyrsi and bronze in
the midst of cities, and Bacchic hides and mitres, and have brought here
fawnskins sprinkled with various gold. With these will you arm Achilles, heavy
to Priam and the Phrygians?'
The Ithacan, smiling at him, his face a little relaxed: 'These, if only he is
hiding by fraud in the maiden hall of Lycomedes, will of their own will lead
the son of Peleus into battle, confessed. You, remember to bring all quickly
from the ship when the time shall be, and to join to these gifts a shield that
is fair with signs and roughest with gold; this spear will be enough. Let good
Agyrtes be with you with the trumpet, and let him bring a hidden horn for
silent uses.'

He had spoken, and on the very threshold of the gates he sees the king and,
having first spoken peace with the olive shown: 'Great rumor, I think, of the
fierce war that now shakes Europe and Asia has long since come to your ears,
gentlest of kings. If by chance the names of the leaders have been borne
hither, I am here, among those with whom avenging Atreides goes: this man,
whom great-hearted Tydeus begot better than so great a stock, is for you; I am
Ulysses, captain of the Ithacans. The cause of the journey (for why should I
fear to confess all to you, since I am a Greek and the one most renowned for
known faith 3) is to explore the approaches and the hated shores of Troy, and
what they prepare.'
He cuts him off in mid-speech: 'May Fortune have nodded, I pray, and may the
gods on the right favor these things! Now honor my roof with hospitality and
my pious hearth.' At once he leads them within the threshold. Without delay
the serving crowd already furnishes tables and couches. Meanwhile Ulysses
surveys with his gaze and searches the house, if there be any traces of a
great maiden or a doubtful face suspected by its figure; and he wanders in the
spreading porticoes and approaches all the household gods, as if he marveled:
as that hunter, holding the undoubted lair of his prey, reads the fields with
a mute Molossian, until he sees the enemy stretched in sleep under the leaves
and the teeth laid on the turf.
Rumor has long been roaring in the secret hall, the house faithful to the
maidens, that Pelasgian leaders have come and a Greek ship and comrades
received. The others fear with reason; but the son of Peleus scarcely hides
new joys and, greedy, desires to have seen the new heroes and arms even such
as he is. And already the halls seethe with royal noise and they recline on
pictured gold, when the father bids his daughters go and the chaste companions
of his daughters. They come in, such as Amazons on the Maeotian bank, when
they have plundered Scythian homes and captured Getic walls, feast with arms
laid aside.
Then indeed intent Ulysses balances faces and breasts with his gaze, but night
and the lights brought in deceive him, and at once the measure of those lying
hid. Yet still he fixes the one with face raised and wandering eyes, keeping
no signs of maiden shame, and with a sidelong light he points him out to his
companion. And unless Deidamia, embracing him, had warned him, headlong, with
a winning bosom, and had always held his bared breast and put-forth hands and
shoulders in the dress, and had forbidden him too often to come forward from
the couches and demand wine, and had replaced the golden hairband on his brow,
Achilles would even then have been open to the Argive leaders.
When hunger was appeased by the feast twice and three times replaced, the king
first addresses them and urges the Achaeans with the bowls: 'I envy your
undertakings, I confess, renowned glories of the Argive race. Would that a
stronger age were mine as well, which I had when I thoroughly tamed the
Dolopes when they attacked the Scyrian shores and broke them on the shallows,
which signs of triumph, the keels, you have seen on the high front of the
walls! At least if there were offspring fit to send to war: now you yourselves
see my strength and my dear pledges. When will this crowd give me new
grandsons?'
He had spoken, and cunning Ulysses, the moment seized: 'You desire what is not
to be scorned; for who would not burn to see countless peoples and various
leaders and the ranks of kings? All the strength and glory of powerful Europe
at once has of its own will sworn to deserved swords. Fields and cities are
empty, we have spoiled the high mountains, every strait is covered with a long
shade of sails; fathers hand over arms, youth snatches them beyond recall.
Never otherwise has so great a store of fame been given to the brave, or valor
been exercised on a greater field.'
He looks at him intent and drinking these things with a watchful ear, while
the others fear and turn lowered eyes, and he repeats: 'Whoever is proud in
ancestors and race, whoever is powerful with horse and javelin, who prevails
with the bow, all honor is there, there great names contend. Scarcely do
fearful mothers or maiden ranks hang back. Ah, much damned to barren years and
hated by the gods is he, if this new glory passes him idle!' He would have
gone from the coverlets, had not far-seeing Deidamia, a sign given, urged all
her sisters and left the tables, embracing him; but he clings, looking back at
the Ithacan, and goes out last from the gathering.
He indeed relaxes a little from the speech begun, yet adding a few things:
'But you remain tranquil in deep peace and prepare marriages for your dear
daughters, whom chance has given you equal to the starry faces of goddesses,
as reverence sometimes silently touches me! That grace and that look of beauty
mixed with the manly.' The father meets him: 'What if you should see them
bearing Bacchic rites, or around Pallas's altars? And we will give it, if by
chance a new south wind delays.' They take it up greedily, and hope is added
to silent vows. The rest of Lycomedes's palace, cares laid down, is silent
under tranquil peace, but the night is long for the sagacious Ithacan, and he
desires the light and finds sleep heavy.

Scarcely was the day risen, and already Tydides, accompanied by Agyrtes, was
at hand and was bringing the promised gifts. No less the Scyrian girls, gone
out from the chamber, were going to show the dances and the promised rites to
the revered guests. The princess shines before the others, and the son of
Peleus as companion: as under the crags of Sicilian Aetna, among the Naiads of
Enna, Diana and fierce Pallas and the betrothed of the Elysian tyrant were
shining.
And already they move their steps, and the Ismenian boxwood has given signals
to the revel-bands; four times they beat Rhea's bronze, four times the
inspired hides with the hand, and four times they have gathered various
returns. Then they alike lift the thyrsi and alike put them down and multiply
the step, now in the act in which the Curetes go, and in which the pious
Samothracians, now turned opposite in Amazon comb, now in the circle in which
the Delian rouses the Laconian women and whirls them, beating, in her own
Amyclae.
Then indeed, then especially Achilles is plain, and he does not care to keep
the turns or to join arms; then he scorns the soft steps, then the dress, more
than usual, and he breaks the choruses and throws many things into disorder.
So Thebes, already sad, was watching Pentheus refusing the thyrsi and the
drums received from his mother.
The praised troop is loosed and they seek again their father's threshold,
where in the seats of the mid hall Tydides had long since placed gifts that
would break maiden gazes, a sign of hospitality and a price of labor, and he
urges them to choose, nor does the gentlest king forbid it. Alas, too simple
and raw, who does not know the cunning gifts and the tricks of the Greeks and
shifting Ulysses!
Here others, whom idle sex and nature lead, try either the smooth thyrsi or
the answering drums, or bind their temples with double fillets. They see the
arms and think them given to their great parent. But the fierce son of Aeacus,
when close at hand he catches sight of the shining orb, engraved with battles
(and by chance it was red with savage stains of wars), leaning on the spear,
he roared and twisted his cheeks, and the hair rose from the abandoned brow.
Nowhere the parent's commands, nowhere the hidden love, and in his whole
breast is Troy.
As a lion, when snatched from his mother's udder he has taken manners and has
learned to have his mane combed and to fear man, and to be seized into no
angers except when bidden, if once iron has flashed with opposing light, the
faith is forsworn and the tamer is an enemy: upon him is his first hunger, and
he is ashamed to have served a timid master. But when he came nearer and a
rival light gave back his face and he saw himself such in the like gold, he
shuddered and blushed at once.
Then keen Ulysses, brought to his side, in a lowered voice: 'Why do you
hesitate?' he says. 'We know. You are the pupil of half-beast Chiron, you the
grandson of heaven and of the sea; you the Dorian fleet, you your Greece
awaits with standards raised, and Pergamum itself already nods to you with
doubtful walls. Come, break delays: let faithless Ida grow pale, and let it
please your father to hear these things, and let cunning Thetis be ashamed to
have so feared for you.'
Already he was loosening his breast from the dress, when Agyrtes, so bidden,
sounded loud on the trumpet. They flee, the gifts scattered from every side,
and they implore their father and believe battles have been stirred. His
clothes fell untouched from his breast; already the shield and the shorter
spear are consumed by his hand (wondrous faith!), and he seemed to overtop the
Ithacan and the Aetolian leader in the shoulders. So much do the sudden arms
and the Martian heat confound the household gods with a dreadful light. Huge
in his step, as if at once demanding Hector, he stands in the middle of the
trembling house, and the Peleian maiden is sought.
But in another part Deidamia was beating the uncovered tricks, whose great
laments and known voices when first he received in his breast, he stuck, and
his valor was broken by a hidden heat. He lowers the shield and, turned to the
king's eyes, astonished at the deeds and fearing the unexpected prodigies,
just as he was, he addresses Lycomedes in naked arms:
'Me to you, dear father (dismiss doubtful fear!), kindly Thetis gave. So great
a glory was awaiting you long since; you send to the Danaans Achilles, sought,
more welcome to me even than a great parent, if it is right to have said it,
and than sweet Chiron. But turn your heart here a little, willing, and as a
good man receive these words: Peleus as father-in-law and guest Thetis join
you to their son, and they allege their own gods from either blood. They
demand one from the maiden troop of your daughters. Do you give her? Or do we
seem to you a low race and degenerate? You do not refuse; therefore join hands
and conceive a compact, and pardon your own. Deidamia is already known to me
by a silent theft; for what could stand against these arms, by what could she,
possessed, drive back our strength 1? Bid me atone for those things; I lay
down the arms and give them back to the Pelasgians and I remain. Why do you
roar sad? Why do you change your eyes? Already you are a father-in-law.'
He laid the son before his feet and adds: 'And already a grandfather. How
often will the ruthless sword be repeated! We are a crowd.' Then the Danaans
also through the rites and the faith of hospitality, and bland Ulysses, press
him with prayers. He, although the discovered injury of a dear daughter and
Thetis's commands move him, and so great a deposit of the goddess seems to be
betrayed, yet fears to go against so many fates and to delay the Argive wars.
Suppose he should wish: Achilles would have scorned even his mother there. Nor
yet would he have refused to join himself to such a son-in-law. He is
conquered. Deidamia, ashamed, brings her step from secret darkness, nor does
she, out of her mind, at once believe in pardon, and she appeases her father
with Achilles set against him.

One is sent to Haemonia, to fill Peleus with the great deeds and to demand a
fleet and comrades for battle. The Scyrian ruler also draws down twin alders
for his son-in-law and excuses his strength to the Achaeans. Then the day was
consumed in feasts, and at last the compact was uncovered, and knowing night
joins the undaunted lovers.
Before her eyes she thinks of new wars and Xanthus and Ida and the Argive
ships and the very waves, and she fears the dawn. Poured on the dear neck of
her new husband she now looses tears and seizes his limbs. 'Shall I see you
again and lay myself on this breast, son of Aeacus? And will you deign again
to own your offspring? Or, swollen, bringing back Teucrian homes and captured
Pergamum, will you be unwilling to remember a maiden hiding-place? What should
I pray, alas, or fear first? Or what should I, anxious, command, I who
scarcely have room to weep? A single night has just given you to me and envied
you! These the times for our chamber? Is this a free wedding?'
'O sweet thefts and tricks, O fear! Permitted Achilles is snatched from me,
wretched. Go (for I would not dare to call back so great preparations), go
cautious, and remember that Thetis did not fear in vain. Go happy, and return
ours! I ask too shamelessly. Already the fair Trojan women will hope for you
with tears and beating, and will wish to give their necks to your chains and
to pay for their country with the bed, or Tyndaris herself will please, too
much praised by an unchaste rape. But I myself will be told to the
serving-women as the tale of a first boyish fault, or I shall lie hidden,
dissembled.'
'Nay come, lead me as companion; why should I not bear the Martian standards
with you? You have borne thyrsi in your hand and Bacchic rites with me, which
unhappy Troy will not believe. Yet this one, whom you leave me as a sad
comfort, this one at least hold under your heart, and grant to me as I pray
this only, that a barbarian wife bear nothing to you, that no captive give
Thetis unworthy grandsons.'
Achilles himself, not unmoved, soothes her speaking such things and swears
faith and pledges what he has sworn with weeping, and, returned, he promises
huge serving-women and captured Ilium and gifts of Phrygian treasure. The
windy storms were snatching the words, void.

Day, brought forth from Ocean, has stripped the world wrapped in dripping
darkness, and the father of flashing light was still lifting a torch dull from
neighboring night and not yet shaken dry of the sea. And already all look out
on the son of Aeacus, his breast bared of the purple cloak and distinguished
by the very arms which he had first seized (for the breeze calls and the
kindred seas persuade), and they fear to remember nothing of the young man and
the leader. So he has returned, changed in all his look, as if he had never
suffered the Scyrian shores and were boarding the ships from the cave of
Pelion.
Then according to custom (for so Ulysses was advising) he makes offerings to
the gods and to the seas and to the south winds, and under the wave itself he
venerates with a bull the sea-green king and grandfather Nereus; his mother is
appeased with a filleted heifer. Here, casting the swollen entrails on the
foaming salt, he speaks: 'We have obeyed, mother, though you were bidding 1
things hardly to be borne, we have obeyed too much. Sought, I go to the Trojan
wars and the Argive ships.' So he began, and he leaped into the alder and is
snatched far from the neighboring lands, the south wind whistling deep; and
already steep Scyros begins to draw clouds and to recede on the long sea.

Far off from the highest tower his wife was hanging, accompanied by her
sisters' tears, holding the entrusted child who has the name Pyrrhus, and with
her eyes fixed on the canvas she herself was going on the strait, and now she
alone was seeing the stern. He too turns his sidelong face toward the beloved
walls and thinks of the widowed house and the groans of her left: a hidden
heat is born again under his heart and valor gives place.
The Laertian hero feels him grieving and, having approached to turn him with
calm words: 'Do you,' he says, 'destined destroyer of great Troy, whom the
Danaan fleets, whom the oracles of the gods demand, and War awaits erect on a
gate unbarred, did a cunning mother violate you with a womanish dress and
commit so great thefts to those hiding-places and hope for faith? O too
anxious, too much a mother! Or would this valor have lain numb in silent
shade, which at the mere heard clang of the trumpet has fled Thetis and
companions and the fires it had suppressed? Nor is it our doing that you come
to arms and follow those who pray: you would have come.'
He spoke, and the Aeacian hero seizes him with such words: 'It is long to set
out the idle causes and a mother's crime; this sword will excuse Scyros and
the unseemly dress, crimes of the fates. You rather, while the strait is
gentle and the canvas enjoys the zephyr, tell what first beginnings of so
great a war there are for the Danaans. It pleases me from this to take just
angers at once.'
Here the Ithacan, the beginning sought a little farther back: 'It is told that
on Hector's Ida, if we believe such things, a shepherd chosen to loose a
contest of beauty held the anxious goddesses, and saw with a friendly light
neither the grim face of Minerva nor the partner of the ethereal ruler, but
too much Dione alone. And indeed that quarrel arose under your caves in a
council of those above, while Pelion sweetly marries Peleus, and already then
promising our arms. Anger shakes the conquered; the judge seeks deadly prizes:
easy Amyclae are shown to the ravisher.'
'He cuts the Phrygian groves, the inner shrine of the turret-bearing mother,
and bids the pine, forbidden to fall to the ground, come down, and, carried
down to the Achaean lands by the strait, he spoils the chambers of his host,
the son of Atreus (shame, alas, and pity of powerful Europe!), and proud with
Helen he sails and carries captured Argos down to Pergamum. Then, the rumor
given through the cities everywhere, from every side, unsummoned, each for
himself and of our own will we gather as avengers. For who would endure that
marriage pacts be broken by unlawful tricks and wedlock be dragged by an easy
rape, as a flock or a herd or cheap heaps of harvest? This loss would move
even the strong.'
'Imperious Agenor did not bear the snares of the gods and the sacred lowings,
and he sought Europa, carried by a great divinity, and scorned the Thunderer

His hand returned to the hilt, and at once a huge blush drove his face:
Ulysses was silent, content.
The son of Oeneus takes it up: 'Nay, O most worthy offspring of heaven, tell
your companions, much favoring, your rites and the elements of your first
nature, and, strong youth soon being added, what seeds of praise Chiron was
accustomed to open to you, and the approaches of valor, by what arts to
increase the limbs, by what the spirit. Let it be a prize to have sought
Scyros deep through long waves and to have shown arms first to these arms.'

Whom would it irk to speak his own deeds? Yet he begins modestly, a little
doubtful and nearer to one compelled: 'I am said even in tender years and
still crawling, when the old Thessalian received me on the hard mountain, to
have taken in no foods according to custom nor to have sated hunger at kind
breasts, but to have torn the dense entrails of lions and the marrow of a
half-living she-wolf. This was my first Ceres, these the gifts of glad
Bacchus; so that father used to give.'
'Soon he was teaching me to go with him through pathless haunts, drawing me
with a greater step, and to smile at beasts when seen, and not to tremble at
rocks broken by rushing waves nor at the silences of a vast wood. Already then
arms in the hand, already then quivers on the neck, and a hastened love of
iron, and skin hardened by much sun and frost; nor limbs slack on a tender
bed, but a rock common with the huge master.'
'Raw life had scarcely turned for me twice six circles of years, when already
he was compelling me to outrun flying stags and Lapith horses and to follow
weapons sent ahead in the course. Often Chiron himself, while age was swift,
would drive me on the fields at a headlong step, let out at full speed on all
of them, and when I was exhausted with wandering pace through the grass he
would praise me, rejoicing, and lift me onto his own back. Often too at the
first torpor of the river he would bid me go above and not break the ice with
a light sole.'
'This was a boyish glory. Why now should I tell you the battles of the woods
and the glades already empty of savage murmur? Never did he suffer me to hunt
unwarlike does through the pathless places of Ossa, or to lay low timid lynxes
with the spear, but to trouble grim bears in their lairs and lightning boars,
and wherever there was a greatest tigress or a cave of a pregnant lioness
withdrawn on the ridges. He himself, sitting in the vast cave, would wait for
the deeds, if I should return sprinkled with black blood; nor did he admit me
to kisses before, unless the weapons had been inspected.'
'And already, with neighboring youth, I was fitting myself for sword-bearing
tumults, nor did any image of wild Mars pass me by. I learned with what
rotation the Paeonians whirl arms, with what the Macedonians brandish their
own pikes, with what whirl the Sarmatian his pole, and the Getan his sickle,
and the Gelonian stretch his bow, and the Balearic wielder of the bent thong
with what hanging twist he would poise the wounds he drew, and mark the
enclosed air with his own circle.'
'I could scarcely remember all the deeds, even if we did them well. Now he
teaches me to join huge ditches with a leap, now to seize the head of an airy
mountain as I climb, with what step one flees over the flats, and in images of
battle to catch millstones sent on a shielded boss, and to wander through
burning huts, and as a footsoldier to halt flying four-horse teams.'
'I remember Sperchios was going most swift, fed by constant rains and melted
snows, and was carrying living beams and rocks: when he bids me, sent in where
the onset of the wave was more savage, stand against it and drive back the
swollen floods, which he himself could scarcely have borne, his step so often
opposing. I was standing indeed, but the stirred river was carrying me back,
and the fog of a broad flight. Fierce he, leaning from above, would threaten
and urge shame with words. I did not go off except when bidden. So lofty glory
was driving me, nor were the hard labors so under such a witness.'
'For to hide Oebalian discs far into the clouds, and to knot a liquid
wrestling, and to scatter the caestus, was play and rest to me; nor was there
greater sweat in those things than when I would shake sounding strings with
Apollo's plectrum and marvel at the old honors of men. Nay he even taught
thoroughly the juices and the grasses that help diseases, by what medicine too
much blood may stand, what makes sleeps, what closes gaping wounds, what
plague must be checked with iron, what would yield to herbs, and he planted
under my breast the warnings of sacred justice, by which he was accustomed to
give reverend laws to the peoples of Pelion and to pacify his own
double-formed kind.'
'Thus far, comrades, the elements of my years I both remember and it pleases
me to remember. My mother knows the rest.'
