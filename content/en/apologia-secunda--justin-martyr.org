Of the same holy Justin, Philosopher and Martyr
Apology on behalf of Christians
to the Senate of the Romans.

And the things that happened both yesterday and the day before in your city
under Urbicus, O Romans, and the things similarly done everywhere unreasonably
by the governors, have compelled me, on behalf of you who are of like passions
and brothers, even if you are ignorant and do not wish it because of the glory
of the supposed dignities, to make the composition of these words.

For everywhere, whoever is being brought to sobriety by a father or neighbor
or child or friend or brother or husband or wife on account of a deficiency,
except those who have been persuaded that the unjust and intemperate will be
punished in eternal fire, and that the virtuous and those who have lived
like Christ will dwell with God in passionlessness (and we mean of
those who have become Christians), because of the difficulty of changing and
the love of pleasure and the difficulty of being moved to rush toward the
good, the wicked demons also, being hostile to us and having such judges as
subjects and serving, as being therefore rulers possessed by demons, prepare
them to kill us. And that the cause of the whole thing that happened under
Urbicus may also become clear to you, I will report the things that were done.

A certain woman was living with an intemperate husband, she herself also being
intemperate formerly. But when she came to know the teachings of Christ, she
herself was brought to sobriety and tried to persuade the husband likewise to
be sober, bringing forward the teachings and reporting the punishment that
will be in eternal fire for those who do not live soberly and with right
reason. But he, remaining in the same licentiousness, made the wife alien by
his deeds. For the woman, considering it impious thereafter to lie with a
husband who, contrary to the law of nature and contrary to what is just, was
trying to make ways of pleasure from every source, wished to be separated from
the union. And since she was put to shame by her own people, who still advised
her to remain, as if the husband would sometime come to a hope of change,
constraining herself she remained. But when her husband, having gone to
Alexandria, was reported to be doing worse things, in order that she might not
become a partner of the injustices and impieties, remaining in the union and
becoming a sharer of table and of bed, having given what is called among you a
repudium, she was separated.

But this noble and good husband of hers, whereas he ought to have rejoiced
that she who formerly readily practiced, delighting in drunkenness and every
vice, the things he used to do with the servants and the hirelings, had ceased
from these deeds and wished him also to cease from doing the same things, he
not being willing, she having departed, has made an accusation, saying that
she is a Christian. And she presented a little book to you the emperor, first
asking that she be permitted to arrange her own affairs, then to make defense
concerning the accusation after the arrangement of her affairs; and you
granted this. But her former husband, not being able still now to say things
against her, turned against a certain Ptolemaeus, whom Urbicus punished, who
had become her teacher of the Christian lessons, in this manner. He persuaded
a centurion who had cast Ptolemaeus into bonds, being a friend to him, to take
hold of Ptolemaeus and ask him if, this very thing only, he is a Christian.
And Ptolemaeus, being a lover of truth but not deceitful nor false-speaking in
his mind, having confessed himself to be a Christian, the centurion caused him
to be in bonds, and for a long time punished him in the prison.

And at last, when the man was brought before Urbicus, likewise this very thing
only was examined, whether he were a Christian. And again, being conscious of
the good things for himself through the teaching from Christ, he confessed the
school of divine virtue. For he who denies anything either, having condemned
the matter, becomes a denier, or knowing himself unworthy and alien to the
matter, flees the confession; of which things none belongs to the true
Christian. And when Urbicus commanded him to be led away, a certain Lucius,
himself also being a Christian, seeing the judgment that had thus unreasonably
taken place, said to Urbicus: "What is the cause? that you have punished this
man who has been convicted neither of being an adulterer nor a fornicator nor
a murderer nor a clothes-stealer nor a robber nor of having done any injustice
simply, but of confessing the appellation of the name Christian? You do not
judge things befitting a Pious emperor nor a son of a philosopher Caesar nor
the sacred senate, O Urbicus." And he, answering nothing else, said also to
Lucius: "You too seem to me to be such." And when Lucius said: "Certainly," he
commanded him also to be led away. And he confessed that he knew gratitude,
knowing that he was being delivered from such wicked masters and was going to
the Father and King of the heavens. And another third also, coming forward,
was condemned to be punished.

I too therefore expect to be plotted against by one of those named and to be
fixed on a wood, or even by Crescens the lover of noise and lover of boast.
For it is not worthy to call the man a philosopher, who at least publicly
bears witness against us of things he does not know, as if Christians were
atheists and impious, doing these things for the favor and pleasure of the
many who have been led astray. For if, not having met with the teachings of
Christ, he runs us down, he is thoroughly wicked and much worse than the
unlearned, who often keep themselves from discoursing and bearing false
witness about things they do not know; or if, having met with them, he has not
understood the greatness in them, or having understood, he does these things
so as not to be suspected of being such, he is much more ignoble and
thoroughly wicked, being inferior to a vulgar and irrational opinion and fear.
And I wish you to know that I put forward and asked him certain questions of
this sort both to learn and to prove that he truly knows nothing. And that I
speak truly, if the communications of the words were not reported to us, I am
ready also before you to share the questions again; and this too would be a
royal work. But if my questions and his answers have also become known to you,
it is clear to you that he knows nothing of our things; or if he does know,
but on account of those who hear he does not dare to speak, like Socrates as I
said before, he is shown not a philosopher but a lover of glory, who at least
does not even honor the Socratic thing that is worthy of love: "But a man is
not to be honored before the truth." And it is impossible for a Cynic, who has
set indifference as the end, to know the good except indifference.

And that no one may say: "All of you then, having killed yourselves, go
already to God and do not cause us trouble," I will say for what cause we do
not do this, and for what cause, when examined, we confess without fear. We
have been taught that God has not made the world in vain, but for the human
race; and we said before that he rejoices in those who imitate the things that
belong to him, and is displeased with those who embrace the base things either
in word or in deed. If therefore we all kill ourselves, we shall be
responsible for no one being born and being discipled into the divine
teachings, or even for the human race not existing, as far as is in us,
ourselves also doing what is contrary to the will of God, if we do this. But
when examined we do not deny, because we are conscious in ourselves of nothing
base, and considering it impious not to speak truth in all things, which we
also know to be dear to God, and now being eager to release you also from the
unjust prejudice.

But if this thought also should steal upon someone, that if we confess God as
helper, we would not, as we say, be overpowered and punished by the unjust, I
will dissolve this also. God, having made the whole world and having subjected
the earthly things to men and having arranged the heavenly elements for the
growth of fruits and the changes of seasons and having appointed a divine law
for these, which things also he appears to have made for men, handed over the
providence of men and of the things under heaven to angels, whom he appointed
over these things. But the angels, having transgressed this order, were
overcome by mixings with women and begot children, who are the so-called
demons. And furthermore they enslaved the human race to themselves; some
things through magical writings, some through fears and punishments which they
brought, some through teaching of sacrifices and incense and libations, of
which they have become in need after being enslaved to the passions of
desires; and they sowed among men murders, wars, adulteries, intemperance, and
every wickedness. Whence also the poets and mythologists, not knowing that the
angels and the demons begotten from them did these things to males and females
and cities and nations, which things they wrote, referred them to God himself
and to the sons as born from him by seed and of those spoken of as his
brothers and children likewise of those from them, Poseidon and Pluto. For
they called each by the name which each of the angels set for himself and for
the children.

And there is no given name for the Father of all, he being unbegotten; for by
whatever name anyone is called, he has the one who set the name older than
himself. But Father and God and Creator and Lord and Master are not names, but
appellations from the good deeds and the works. And his Son, who alone is
called Son in the proper sense, the Word who was both with him before the
things made and being begotten, when in the beginning through him he created
and arranged all things, is called Christ according to his being anointed and
God's arranging all things through him, a name itself also containing an
unknown signification, in the same way that the address "God" is not a name,
but an innate opinion of a matter hard to explain in the nature of men. And
Jesus has both the name and signification of a man and of a savior. For he
also became a man, as we said before, having been born according to the will
of God and Father on behalf of the believing men and for the destruction of
the demons; and now you can learn from the things that take place before the
eyes. For many who were possessed by demons throughout the whole world and in
your city, many of our people, the Christians, exorcising by the name of Jesus
Christ, who was crucified under Pontius Pilate, have healed those not healed
by all the other exorcists and enchanters and sorcerers, and still now they
heal, putting out of action and driving out the demons that hold the men.

Whence also God continues not to make the confusion and dissolution of the
whole world, in order that the wicked angels and demons and men may no longer
exist, on account of the seed of the Christians, which he knows in the nature
that it is a cause. Since if this were not, it would not even still be
possible for you to do these things and to be worked upon by the wicked
demons, but the fire of the judgment, having come down, would have
distinguished all things without restraint, as also formerly the flood,
leaving no one except the one alone with his own, called among us Noah, but
among you Deucalion, from whom again so many have come to be, of whom some are
wicked, some earnest. For thus we say the conflagration will come to be, but
not, as the Stoics, according to the reason of the change of all things into
one another, which has appeared most shameful; nor yet that men do or suffer
the things that happen according to fate, but that each according to choice
succeeds or sins, and that according to the working of the wicked demons the
earnest, such as Socrates and those like him, are persecuted and are in bonds,
while Sardanapalus and Epicurus and those like them seem to be happy in
abundance and glory. Which the Stoics, not having understood, declared that
all things come to be according to the necessity of fate. But because God made
both the race of angels and of men in the beginning self-governed, they will
justly receive the punishment in eternal fire for whatever they may
transgress. And this is the nature of everything generated, to be capable of
vice and virtue; for none of them would be praiseworthy, if it were not
possible to turn both ways and it had the power. And this is shown also by the
men everywhere who have legislated and philosophized according to right
reason, from their prescribing to do these things and to abstain from those.
And the Stoic philosophers in the discourse concerning morals honor the same
things stoutly, so that it is made clear that in the discourse concerning
principles and incorporeals they do not succeed. For if they will say that the
things that come to be by men come to be according to fate, or that there is
no God besides things that are changed and altered and always dissolved into
the same, they will appear to have had a grasp of perishable things only, and
God himself through the parts and through the whole coming to be in every
wickedness, or that there is neither wickedness nor virtue; which is contrary
to every sober thought and reason and mind.

And those from the Stoic doctrines, since they have become orderly at least in
the ethical discourse, as also in some things the poets, because of the seed
of the Word innate in every race of men, we know to have been hated and
murdered; Heraclitus, as we said before, and Musonius in our times, and others
we know. For as we indicated, the demons have always worked that all who even
in any way at all are eager to live according to reason and to flee wickedness
be hated. And it is no wonder if the demons, being convicted, work that those
who live not according to a part of the seminal word, but according to the
knowledge and contemplation of the whole Word, which is Christ, be hated much
more; who will receive the worthy punishment and vengeance, having been shut
up in eternal fire. For if they are already defeated by men through the name
of Jesus Christ, it is a teaching of the punishment in eternal fire that is
going to be both for them and for those who serve them. For thus also all the
prophets proclaimed that it would come to be, and Jesus our teacher taught.

And that no one may say what is said by those supposed to be philosophers,
that the things said by us, that the unjust are punished in eternal fire, are
boasts and terrors, and that we require men to live virtuously through fear
and not because it is good and pleasing, I will answer this briefly, that if
this is not so, neither is there a God, or, if there is, he does not care for
men, and there is neither virtue nor vice, and, as we said before, the
lawgivers punish unjustly those who transgress the things appointed as good.
But since those are not unjust, and their Father, teaching through the Word to
do the same things as he, those who agree with these are not unjust. But if
someone should put forward the different laws of men, saying that among some
men these things have been held good and those shameful, but among others the
things shameful among those are held good, and the good shameful, let him hear
also the things said toward this, and we know that the wicked angels have
appointed laws like to their own wickedness, in which men who have become like
them rejoice, and right reason, having come in, shows that not all opinions
nor all doctrines are good, but some base, some good; so that to such people
also the same and like things will be said by me, and will be said at more
length, if there is need. But now I go back up to the matter at hand.

Greater then than every human teaching our things appear, because of this,
that the whole rational, Christ who appeared for us, has become both body and
word and soul. For as many things as those who have philosophized or
legislated have always spoken well and found, have been worked out by them
according to a part of the Word through discovery and contemplation. But since
they did not know all the things of the Word, who is Christ, they also often
said things contrary to themselves. And those who came before Christ, having
tried according to human reason to contemplate and refute the things, were
brought as impious and busybodies into courts. And Socrates, who became more
vigorous than all of them toward this, was accused of the same things as we;
for they said that he was bringing in new divinities, and that he did not hold
those whom the city thinks gods. But he taught men to reject the wicked demons
and those who did the things the poets said, having cast out of the state both
Homer and the other poets, and he was urging them toward the knowledge of God
who was unknown to them through a search of reason, saying: "But the Father
and Craftsman of all it is neither easy to find, nor, having found him, safe
to speak to all." Which things our Christ has done through his own power. For
no one was persuaded by Socrates to die for this doctrine; but by Christ, who
was known even by Socrates in part (for he was and is the Word who is in every
one, and through the prophets having foretold the things that were going to be
and through himself having become of like passions and having taught these
things), not philosophers nor philologians only were persuaded, but also
craftsmen and altogether unlearned people, having despised both glory and fear
and death; since he is a power of the unspeakable Father and not a
construction of human reason.

Nor would we even be murdered, nor would the unjust men and demons be more
powerful than we, if it were not altogether owed to every man who is born also
to die; whence also paying the debt we give thanks. And yet that Xenophontic
thing also we now consider it good and timely to say both to Crescens and to
those who are foolish like him. Xenophon said that Heracles, walking upon a
certain meeting of three ways, found both Virtue and Vice, appearing in the
forms of women. And Vice, being in delicate clothing and with a face made
amorous and blooming from such things, and being immediately enticing to the
sights, said to Heracles that if he follows her, she will cause him always to
live taking pleasure and adorned with the most brilliant and like ornament
that is about her. And Virtue, being in a squalid face and garment, said: "But
if you obey me, you will not adorn yourself with ornament nor with the beauty
that flows and perishes, but with the eternal and beautiful ornaments." And we
are persuaded that everyone, fleeing the things that seem beautiful, and
pursuing the things thought hard and unreasonable, receives happiness. For
Vice, wrapping around herself as a screen of her deeds the things that belong
to Virtue and are truly beautiful, through imitation of immortal things (for
she has nothing immortal nor can she make it), enslaves the men who grovel on
the ground, having put around Virtue the base things that belong to herself.
But those who have understood the things that belong in truth as beautiful are
also immortal in virtue; which also concerning Christians and those from the
contest and the men who have done such things as the poets said concerning the
supposed gods, every man of sense ought to suppose, drawing the reasoning from
our despising even death, which is to be fled.

For I myself also, delighting in the teachings of Plato, hearing Christians
being slandered, but seeing them fearless toward death and all the other
things thought fearful, considered that it was impossible for them to exist in
wickedness and love of pleasure. For what lover of pleasure or incontinent
man, and one who thinks the feeding on human flesh a good, could embrace
death, so that he might be deprived of his own goods, and would not rather try
from every source always to live the life here and to escape the notice of the
rulers, not to mention denounce himself to be murdered? Already the wicked
demons have also worked this to be done through certain wicked men. For they
themselves, murdering some upon a slander against us, have also dragged to
tortures household slaves of our people or children or women, and through
fearful outrages they compel them to accuse these mythologized things, which
they themselves openly do; of which, since none belongs to us, we do not care,
having God the unbegotten and unspeakable as witness of both the reasonings
and the deeds.

For for what grace would we not also confess these things publicly as goods
and demonstrate them to be a divine philosophy, saying that we celebrate the
mysteries of Kronos in murdering men, and in filling ourselves with blood, as
it is said, things equal to the idol honored among you, on which not only
bloods of irrational animals are sprinkled but also human, making through the
most notable and noblest man among you the pouring of the blood of those
murdered, and having become imitators of Zeus and the other gods in going into
men and mixing with women without fear, bringing as a defense both Epicurus
and the writings of the poets? But since we persuade men to flee these lessons
and those who have done and imitate these things, as even now we have
contended through these words, we are warred against in various ways; but we
do not care, since we know God as a just overseer of all things. Would that
even now someone would cry out with a tragic voice, having gone up upon some
high platform: "Be ashamed, be ashamed, referring the things you openly do to
those without blame, and wrapping around these, of whom there is no share even
in the least, the things that belong both to yourselves and to your gods.
Change, be brought to sobriety."

For I also, having learned that a wicked wrapping had been put around the
divine teachings of the Christians by the wicked demons for the turning away
of the other men, and these things being falsely spoken, both laughed at the
wrapping and at the opinion among the many. I confess that I found myself a
Christian both praying and contending with all my might, not because the
teachings of Plato are alien to Christ, but because they are not altogether
like, just as neither are those of the others, both Stoics and poets and
writers. For each one, seeing from a part of the seminal divine Word that
which was akin, spoke well; but those who have said things contrary to
themselves in the more important matters appear not to have possessed the
knowledge that cannot be looked at and the knowledge that cannot be refuted.
As many things then as have been well said among all, are of us the
Christians; for we worship and love the Word from the unbegotten and
unspeakable God after God, since he also became man for us, in order that,
having become a sharer also of our sufferings, he might also make a healing.
For all the writers were able dimly to see the things that are through the
innate implanted seed of the Word. For it is one thing for a seed of something
and an imitation to have been given according to capacity, and another thing
that itself of which according to the grace from that one the sharing and
imitation comes to be.

We therefore ask you, having subscribed what seems good to you, to set forth
this little book, in order that our things also may become known to the others
and they may be able to be delivered from the false opinion and ignorance of
the good things, who through their own fault become liable to the punishments,
for these things to become known to men, because of there being in the nature
of men the faculty of recognizing good and shameful, and because of condemning
us, whom they do not know to do such shameful things as they say, and because
of rejoicing in gods who have done such things and still now requiring the
like from men, so that from even assigning to us, as doing such things, death
or bonds or some other such thing they condemn themselves, as not needing
other judges.

And I despised also the impious and deceiving Simonian teaching in my nation.
But if you publish this, we would make it clear to all, in order that if they
could they might change; for this cause alone we have composed these words.
And our teachings are not shameful according to a sober judgment, but superior
to every human philosophy; and if not, at least they are not like the Sotadean
and Philaenidean and Archestratean and Epicurean and the other such poetic
teachings, which it has been permitted for all to meet with, both spoken and
written. And we will cease at last, having done as far as was in us, and
having prayed in addition that all men altogether be counted worthy of the
truth. May it be then that you also, worthily of piety and philosophy, judge
the just things on behalf of yourselves.
