Therefore if as through the Word, so also through the Son all things came to

says, "Have I been so long a time with you, and you have not known me, Philip?
He who has seen me has seen the Father. And how do you say, Show us the
Father? Do you not believe that I am in the Father and the Father is in me?
The words that I speak to you I do not speak from myself; but the Father who
remains in me, he does the works. Believe me that I am in the Father and the
Father in me; but if not, believe me because of the works themselves. Amen,
amen I say to you: he who believes in me, the works that I do, he also will
do, and greater than these he will do, because I go to the Father. And
whatever you ask in my name, this I will do, that the Father may be glorified
in the Son." Therefore if the Father is glorified in the Son, the Son would be
the one who says, "I am in the Father and the Father in me," and himself also
"He who has seen me has seen the Father." For he who says these things shows
himself to be Son by adding, "that the Father may be glorified in the Son." 20
If then they say that the man whom the Word wore is himself the Only-begotten
Son of God, and that the Word is not Son, the man would consequently be the
one in the Father, in whom also the Father is. And the man would be the one
who is "one," himself and the Father, and "he who is in the bosom of the
Father," and "the true light." And they will be compelled to say that through
the man himself the world has come to be, and that the man is the one who came
"not to judge the world, but to save it"; and that he himself also is "before
Abraham came to be." For "Jesus," it says, "said to them: Amen, amen I say to
you, before Abraham came to be, I am." And how is it not absurd, according to
them, to say that the one who came from the seed of Abraham after two and
forty generations exists before Abraham came to be? And how is it not absurd,
if the flesh which the Word wore, this is the Son, to say that this flesh from
Mary is that through which the world came to be? And how will they save also
"He was in the world"? For the evangelist, signifying the time before the
birth of the Son according to the flesh, added saying, "He was in the world."
And how, if the Word is not Son, but the man is Son, can he save the world,
being himself also one of the world? And if they are shameless at these
things, where will the Word be, the man being in the Father? And of what rank
will the Word be toward the Father, the man and the Father being one? And if
the man is Only-begotten, of what rank will the Word be? For either one would
say that he is second, or if he is above the Only-begotten, he himself would
be the Father. For as the Father is one, so also the Only-begotten from him is
one. And what more does the Word have than the man, if the Word is not Son?
For it is written that the world has come to be through the Son and through
the Word, and it is common to the Word and to the Son to craft the world; but
the seeing of the Father is no longer written in the Word, but in the Son, and
the saving of the world is no longer through the Word

was it said, but through the Only-begotten Son. For "Jesus said," it says,
"Have I been so long a time with you, and you have not known me, Philip? He
who has seen me has seen the Father." And it is not written that the Word
knows the Father, but the Son; and to see the Father the Word has not been
said, but the Only-begotten Son who is in the bosom of the Father. 21 And what
more does the Word contribute toward our salvation rather than the Son, if
according to them the Son is one, and the Word another? For the command is
that we believe not in the Word, but in the Son. For John says: "He who
believes in the Son has eternal life; but he who disobeys the Son will not see
life." And holy baptism, in which the constitution of all our faith is moored,
is given not into Word, but into Father and Son and Holy Spirit. If then
according to them the Word is one and the Son another, and the Word is not
Son, baptism has nothing toward the Word. How then according to them is the
Word with the Father, not being with him in the giving of baptism? But perhaps
they would say that in the name of the Father the Word also is contained. Why
then not also the Spirit? Or is the Spirit outside the Father? And a man, if
the Word is not Son, is named after the Father, but the Spirit after the man;
and no longer according to them is the monad dilated into a triad, but into a
tetrad: Father and Word and Son and Holy Spirit. Being ashamed in these
things, they take refuge in another and say that not the man by himself, whom
the Lord wore, but both together, both the Word and the man, are Son; for both
joined together are named Son, as they themselves say. What then is cause of
what, and which of the two made which a Son? Or, that I may speak more
plainly, is the Word Son because of the flesh, or is the flesh called Son
because of the Word? Or neither of these, but the concurrence of both? If then
the Word is Son because of the flesh, of necessity the flesh is Son, and there
follow the absurdities, as many as have been said before from saying that the
man is Son. But if the flesh is called Son because of the Word, then even
before the flesh altogether the Word, being Son, was Son. For how is it
possible for someone to make others sons, not being himself Son, especially
when there is a Father? If then he makes sons for himself, he himself would be
Father; but if for the Father, of necessity he is Son, or rather he himself is
the Son, through whom the others are made sons. 22 Since if, he not being Son,
we are sons, God would be our Father and not his. How then does he appropriate
it rather, saying "My Father" and "I from the Father"? For if he is common
Father of all, he is not Father of him alone, nor did he alone come out from
the Father. But he says that he is sometimes called our Father also, because
he himself has partaken of our flesh. For this is why the Word became flesh,
in order that, since the Word is Son, because of the Son dwelling in us he may
be called our Father also. "For he sent," it says, "the Spirit of his Son into
our hearts, crying: Abba, the Father." Therefore the Son in us, calling upon
his own Father, makes him to be called Father of us also. Indeed of those in
whose hearts the Son is not, of these neither would God be called Father. If
then because of the Word the man is called Son, of all necessity, since the
ancients also are said to be sons even before the becoming-man, the Word also
is Son before the sojourn. For "Sons," <it says>, "I have begotten," and at
the time of Noah: "The sons of God seeing"; and in the Song: "Is not this
himself your Father?" Therefore there was also the true Son, through whom
those too were sons. But if according to them again neither of these is Son,
but it is because of the concurrence of both, of necessity neither is Son, I
mean neither the Word nor the man, but some cause, through which they were
also joined; and thus the cause of the joining, which makes a Son, will
precede. Therefore according to this also the Son was before the flesh. When
these things are said they will take refuge in another, saying that neither
the man is Son, nor both together, but

that the Word in the beginning was Word simply; but when he became man, then
he was named Son; for before the appearing he was not Son, but Word only; and
as the Word became flesh, not being flesh before, so the Word became Son, not
being Son before. These then are their idle talkings; but they have the
refutation evident. 23 For if, having become man at all, he became Son, the
becoming-man is cause. And if the man is cause of his being Son, or both
together, the same absurdities will meet. Next, if first Word and later Son,
he will appear to have known the Father later, and not before. For not as Word
does he know, but as Son. For "No one knows the Father except the Son." And
this too will meet, that later he came to be in the bosom of the Father, and
later he and the Father became one, and later is "He who has seen me has seen
the Father." For all these things were said of Son. From these things then
they will be compelled to say: the Word was nothing, but a name only. For
neither is he in us with the Father, nor did he who has seen the Word see the
Father, nor was the Father known to anyone at all; for through the Son the
Father is known; for so it is also written: "And to whom the Son may reveal
him." And the Word not yet being Son, not yet did anyone know the Father. How
then was he seen by Moses, how by the fathers? For he himself says in the
Kingdoms: "Being revealed I was revealed toward all your fathers." But if God
was revealed, it is clear that the one revealing was the Son, as he himself
says: "And to whom the Son may reveal." It is impious then and senseless to
say that the Word is one, and the Son is another. And whence then they have
got such a suspicion, it is good to ask. They say then that because it has not
been said in the Old concerning Son, but concerning Word, and for this reason
they confirm that they suspect the Son to be later than the Word, because not
in the Old, but in the New only, it was said concerning him. These things they
speak impiously. For first, to divide the covenants, and not to have the one
holding to the other, is the practice of Manichees and Jews, the one party
contradicting the Old, the other the New. Next according to them, if the
things lying in the Old precede in time, and the things in the New are later,
and the times are judged from the writings, of necessity again later are "I
and the Father are one," and "Only-begotten," and "He who has seen me has seen
the Father"; for not from the Old, but from the New these things are
witnessed. 24 Not so, however, but truly in the Old also many things are said
concerning Son; as in the second Psalm, "You are my Son, I today have begotten
you"; and in the ninth the title, "Unto the end, over the hidden things of the
Son, a psalm to David"; and in the forty-fourth, "Unto the end, over those who
shall be changed, to the sons of Korah for understanding, a song over the
Beloved"; and in Isaiah: "I will sing now to the beloved a song of the beloved
to my vineyard. A vineyard came to be for the beloved." And who would the
"beloved" be if not an Only-begotten Son? as also in the hundred and ninth,
"From the womb before the morning star I begot you," concerning which it will
be said later; and in Proverbs: "And before all the hills he begets me"; and
in Daniel: "And the vision of the fourth is like a son of God"; and many
others. If then from the Old is the ancientness, ancient would be also the Son
who is made clear in the Old in many places. "But yes," they say, "it lies
there, but let it be prophetically." Therefore also concerning the Word it
would be said to have been spoken prophetically. For not the one, and the
other <not>. For if "You are my Son" is of a future, it is clear that also "By
the Word of the Lord the heavens were established." For he did not say "they
came to be," nor "he made." And that "he established" is of a future, it says:
"The Lord reigned," then: "for he also established the inhabited world, which
will not be shaken." And if that in the forty-fourth, "over the Beloved," is
of a future, it is clear that also the thing brought on, "My heart uttered a
good Word." And if "from the womb"

is of a man, then also "from the heart." For if the womb is human, the heart
also is bodily. But if "from the heart" is eternal, "from the womb" also is
eternal; and if "the Only-begotten in the bosom," the Beloved also is in the
bosom. For the only-begotten and the beloved are the same, as "This is my Son
the beloved." For he did not say "beloved" wanting to signify the love toward
him, lest he seem to hate the others, but he was declaring the only-begotten,
in order to show that he is the only one from him. And to Abraham at any rate,
wanting to signify the only-begotten, the Word says: "Offer your son the
beloved." And it is clear to everyone that Isaac is from Sarah alone. The Word
is then Son, not lately come to be or named Son, but always Son. For if not
Son, neither Word; and if not Word, neither Son. For that which is from the
Father is Son. And what is from the Father, if not Word who came forth from
the heart and was begotten from the womb? For the Father is not Word, nor is
the Word Father, but the one is Father, the other Son; and the one begets, the
other is begotten. 25 Arius then raves in saying that the Son is from things
that are not, and "there was once when he was not." And Sabellius also raves
in saying that the Father is Son, and again the Son is Father, in hypostasis
one, but in name two. And he raves also in using as an example the grace of
the Spirit. For he says: "As there are divisions of gifts, but the same
Spirit," so also the Father is the same, but is dilated into Son and Spirit.
And this is full of absurdity. For if as in the case of the Spirit, so also in
the case of God it is, the Father will be Word and Holy Spirit, becoming to
one Father, to another Word, to another Spirit, fitting himself to the need of
each, and in name Son and Spirit, but in being Father only, having a beginning
of becoming Son, and ceasing to be called Father, and having become man in
name, but in truth not even having sojourned, and lying in saying "I and the
Father," but in being himself Father? and as many other absurdities as meet in
the case of Sabellius. And of necessity the name of the Son and of the Spirit
will also cease when the need has been fulfilled; and the things that come to
be will then be as far as child's play, because they were displayed not in
truth, but in name. And when the name of the Son ceases according to them, the
grace of baptism will cease also; for it was given into Son. And what will
follow but disappearance of the creation? For if, in order that we might be
created, the Word came forth, and we are when he has come forth, it is clear
that when he withdraws into the Father, as they say, we will no longer be. For
he will be as he was; thus we will no longer be, as then we were not. For the
Word no longer having come forth, the creation will no longer be. 26 These
things then are absurd. But that the Son has no beginning of being, but always
and before the becoming-man is with the Father, John makes clear in the first
epistle, saying thus: "That which was from the beginning, which we have heard,
which we have seen with our eyes, which we have looked upon, and our hands
have handled, concerning the Word of life; and the life was manifested, and we
have seen and we bear witness and we announce to you the eternal life, which
was toward the Father, and was manifested to us." Here saying that the life
did not come to be, but was toward the Father, at the end of the epistle he
says that the Son is the life. He writes then: "And we are in the true one, in
his Son Jesus Christ. This is the true God and eternal life." And if the Son
is the life and this was toward the Father, and if the Son was toward the
Father, and the same says: "and the Word was toward God," the Son would be the
Word who always is toward the Father. And as the Son is Word, so God himself
would be the Father. The Son however according to John is God not simply, but
is true God. For according to this same one "and the Word was God"; and the
Son was saying: "I am the life." Therefore the Son is Word and the life that
is with the Father. And again that which is said in this same John, "the
Only-begotten Son, who is in the bosom of the Father"

shows that the Son always is. For whom John calls Son, this David psalms as
hand, saying: "Why do you turn away your hand and your right hand from the
midst of your bosom?" Therefore if the hand is in the bosom, and the Son in
the bosom, the Son would be the hand, and the hand would be the Son, through
whom the Father made all things. For "Your hand," it says, "made all these
things" and "with the hand he led out the people"; therefore through the Son.
And if also "this is the changing of the right hand of the Most High," and
again "Unto the end, over those who shall be changed, a song over the
Beloved," the Beloved then is the hand that was changed, concerning whom also
the divine voice says: "This is my Son the beloved." Equal then is "This is my
hand" to "This is my Son." 27 But since some of the uninstructed, taking away
the being of a Son, cheapen "From the womb before the morning star I begot
you," as if this fitted in the case of Mary, saying that he was begotten from
Mary before the morning star (for it would not fit, they say, to speak of a
womb in the case of God), it is necessary to say a few things. If then,
because the womb is human, for this reason it is foreign to God, it is clear
that the heart also has a human meaning. For it follows that the one who has a
heart has also a womb. Both then being human, either it is necessary to take
away each, or one must seek the mind of both. For as from a heart a word, so
from a womb an offspring. And as when a heart of God is spoken we do not
understand it as human, so if the writing says "from the womb," one must not
receive this as bodily. For it is a custom of the divine writing to speak and
signify the things above man in a human way. Indeed, narrating concerning the
creation it says: "Your hands made me and fashioned me," and: "My hand made
all these things," and: "He commanded, and they were created." Fittingly then
concerning each it signifies, of the Son the proper and the genuine, and of
the creation the beginning of being. For the ones he makes and creates, but
him he begets from himself, Word, Wisdom. For womb and heart declare the
proper and genuine. For we too have the genuine from a womb, but the works we
make through a hand. 28 What then, they say, also is "before the morning
star"? And I would say: if "before the morning star" of this one shows the
birth from Mary as wonderful, many others also were begotten before the rising
of the star. What then wonderful has been said in his case, that he mentions
it as of some exceptional thing of his, it being common also in the case of
many? Next, to beget differs from to lead out. For to beget has a beginning of
foundation; but to lead out is nothing other than to bring forward what
exists. If then the voice fits in the case of the body, one must know that he
did not then receive a beginning of coming to be, when it was evangelized to
the shepherds by night, but when the angel spoke toward the virgin. And it was
not then night; for it has not been said; but it was night when he came out
from the womb. Scripture sets this difference, and says on the one hand that
he has been begotten "before the morning star," and on the other names "from
the womb" a going-forth, as in the twenty-first Psalm: "You are the one who
drew me out from the womb." Besides he did not say "before the rising of the
morning star," but simply "before the morning star." If then the saying is to
be taken of the body, of necessity either the body is before Adam (for the
stars are before Adam) or one must seek the mind of the letter, which it is
possible to take from John. For in the Apocalypse he says: "I am the alpha and
the omega, and the first and the last, and the beginning and the end. Blessed
are those who make broad their robes, in order that their authority shall be
over the tree of life, and they may enter by the gates into the city. Outside
the dogs and the sorcerers, and the fornicators, and the murderers and the
idolaters and everyone who does and loves a lie. I Jesus sent my angel, to
bear witness to you these things in the churches. I am the root and the race
of David, the bright star, the morning one. And the Spirit and the bride say:
Come; and let him who hears say: Come;

and let him who thirsts come; let him who wishes take water of life as a
gift." If then the race of David is the bright star, the morning one, it is
clear that the according-to-flesh of the Savior has been called morning star,
which the offspring from God preceded, so that such is that in the Psalm:
"From myself I have begotten you before the appearing according to flesh." For
"before the morning star" is equal to "before the enfleshment of the Word." 29
There are then also in the Old things lying clearly concerning Son, even if it
is superfluous to dispute concerning these. For if the things not lying in the
Old are later, let those who contend thus say, where in the Old has it been
said concerning the Paraclete Spirit? For concerning Holy Spirit it was said,
but concerning Paraclete nowhere. Is then the Holy Spirit one and the
Paraclete another, and the Paraclete later, since he does not lie in the Old?
But may it not be either to say that the Spirit is later, or to divide and say
that the Holy Spirit is one, and the Paraclete another. For the Spirit is one
and the same both then and now, hallowing and comforting those receptive of
him, as also the Word himself is one, Son, leading into adoption of sons even
then those who were worthy. For there were sons also in the Old, not through
another, but through the Son being made children. For if the Son of God was
not also before Mary, how is he before all, there being sons before him? And
how also first-born, being found second after many? But neither is the
Paraclete second (for he was before all) nor is the Son later ("for in the
beginning was the Word") and as the Spirit and Paraclete are the same, so the
Son and Word are the same; and as the Savior says concerning the Spirit: "But
the Paraclete, the Holy Spirit, whom the Father will send in my name," saying
the same and not dividing, so John, narrating the like, says: "And the Word
became flesh, and tabernacled among us, and we looked upon his glory, glory as
of an Only-begotten from a Father." For here too he did not divide, but
announced the identity. And not as another Paraclete, and another the Holy
Spirit, but one and the same, so not another Word, and another Son, but the
Word is Only-begotten. For he did not speak of a glory of the flesh itself,
but of the Word himself. Let him then who dares to divide Word and Son divide
also Spirit and Paraclete. But if the Spirit is undivided, undivided also is
the Word, being himself Son and Wisdom and Power. And the beloved even the
Greeks know, those clever concerning phrases, that it is equal to saying
"Only-begotten." For Homer says upon Telemachus, the son of Odysseus being
only-begotten, these things in the second of the Odyssey: "Why then, dear
child, has this thought come to be in your mind? And where do you wish to go
over the wide earth, being only, beloved? And he has perished far from his
fatherland, Zeus-born Odysseus, among a people of strangers." He then who is
only to the father is called beloved. 30 Some of those from the Samosatene,
dividing the Word from the Son, say that the Son is the Christ, but the Word
is another. And they take a pretext of this from the Acts; which Peter said
well, but they receive badly. And it is this: "He sent the Word to the sons of
Israel, evangelizing peace through Jesus Christ. This one is Lord of all." For
they say that as the Word having spoken through Christ, as also in the case of
the prophets, "Thus says the Lord," the prophet was one, and the Lord another.
But to this it is similar to oppose that in the first to the Corinthians:
"Awaiting the revelation of our Lord Jesus Christ; who will also confirm you
unto the end unblameable in the day of our Lord Jesus Christ." For as not
another Christ confirms the day of another Christ, but he himself confirms in
his own day those who await, so the Father sent the Word having become flesh,
in order that through himself, having become man, he might preach. Straightway
at any rate he adds: "This one is Lord of all." And Lord of all is the

Word. 31 <From Leviticus.> "And Moses said toward Aaron: Come toward the
altar, and do that concerning your sin and your whole-burnt-offering, and make
atonement concerning yourself and your house, and do the gifts of the people
and make atonement concerning them, just as the Lord commanded Moses." See
then here, although Moses is one, Moses himself is speaking as concerning
another Moses: "just as the Lord commanded Moses." Thus then also concerning
the divine Word, if the blessed Peter says he was sent to the sons of Israel
through Jesus Christ, one must not understand the Word as one, and Christ as
another, but one and the same because of the union that is toward his divine
and man-loving condescension and becoming-man. And even if he should be
understood in two ways, yet not as of the Word having been separated,
according to the inspired John, who has said: "And the Word became flesh,"
"and tabernacled among us." What then was said well and rightly by the blessed
Peter, those of the Samosatene, understanding badly and crookedly, do not
stand in the truth. For Christ is understood as both together in the divine
writing, as when it says: "Christ, God's power and God's wisdom." If then
Peter says "the Word has been sent through Jesus Christ to the sons of
Israel," let this be understood as signifying that the Word having been
enfleshed has been manifested to the sons of Israel, in order that it may be
in agreement with "And the Word became flesh." But if they understand that
otherwise and, while confessing the Word, as he also is, to be divine, they
separate from him the man taken toward him, with whom also he is believed to
have been united, saying that he has been sent "through Jesus Christ," they do
not understand that they are speaking things contrary to themselves. For those
who here, separating from the divine enfleshment, understand him as divine,
therefore make him small when they hear that he has become flesh, and they
think the things of the Greeks, as indeed they also think, taking the divine
enfleshment as an alteration of the Word. 32 But it is not this; may it not
be! For in the manner that John here preaches the inexpressible union, "the
mortal having been swallowed up by the life" and he being life itself,

according as he himself, toward Martha, saying "I am the life," so also when
the blessed Peter says that the Word has been sent "through Jesus Christ," he
signifies the divine union. For as one hearing "The Word became flesh" would
not think that he is therefore no longer Word, which is absurd, as has been
said before, so also hearing Word who has been joined to the flesh let him
understand the divine mystery as one and simple. And more clearly and
undisputed of every reasoning, that which was said toward the Theotokos
herself by the archangel would show the oneness of the divine Word and man.
For he says: "Holy Spirit will come upon you, and power of the Highest will
overshadow you; wherefore also the holy thing that is begotten will be called
Son of God." Senselessly then those of the Samosatene separate the Word who
has been clearly shown to have been united to the man from Mary. He was not
then sent through him; but he was sending in him, saying: "Go, make disciples
of all the nations." 33 And this is a custom of the writing, to express the
phrases without over-curiosity and simply; for so also in Numbers one will
find: "Moses said," it says, "to Raguel the Midianite, father-in-law of
Moses." For not another Moses is the one speaking, and another of whom Raguel
was father-in-law, but Moses was one. For if the Word of God and Wisdom
likewise is named also Power and right hand and arm and as many such things
(and he has been united to us in a man-loving way, having put around himself
our first-fruits and having been blended with this), then the Word himself has
reasonably received also the remaining names. For that John has said "in the
beginning the Word was, and this one toward God, and himself God, and all
things through him, and without him nothing has come to be," shows clearly
that even man exists as a fashioning of God the Word. If then, having taken
this one when he had become unsound into himself, he again renews him through
his sure renewal unto

endless permanence, and for this reason is united, leading him up into a more
divine lot, how is it possible to say that the Word has been sent through the
man from Mary, and to number with the remaining apostles, I mean prophets, who
have been sent by him, the Lord of the apostles? And how also would a mere man
be called Christ? But united to the Word he would reasonably be named Christ

34 Let us then understand both together as Christ, the divine Word united to
him from Mary in Mary. For in her womb the Word fashioned for himself the
house, in the manner that from the beginning he did Adam from the earth, or
rather more divinely, concerning whom also Solomon says, knowing the Word and
that Wisdom is named, clearly: "Wisdom built herself a house"; which also the
apostle interpreting says: "Whose house we are," and elsewhere he addresses it
as "temple," inasmuch as it is fitting for God to dwell in a temple, of which
also as an image he commanded the ancients to build that from stones through
Solomon; whence when the truth appeared the image has ceased. For when the
ungrateful wished to show the image as truth, and the true dwelling, which we
also clearly believe to be union, he did not threaten to take down, but
knowing that they themselves dare against themselves, he says to them:
"Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it," our Savior clearly
showing that the things eagerly pursued by men have the dissolution from
themselves. "For if the Lord does not build a house and keep a city, in vain
have the builders toiled, and the keepers have kept watch." The things of the
Jews then have been loosed; for they were a shadow; but the things of the
church have been settled, "for it has been founded upon the rock, and gates of
hades will not overpower it." It was theirs to say: "How do you, being a man,
make yourself God?" Of these the Samosatene is a disciple; whence reasonably
he announces the things from him to his own. "But we have not so learned the
Christ, if indeed we have heard him and have been taught from him, having put
off the old man who is being corrupted according to the desires of deceit, and
having taken up the new who has been created according to God in righteousness
and holiness of the truth." Let Christ therefore be piously understood as both
together. 35 And if also the writing in many places calls the body "Christ,"
as when the blessed Peter says toward Cornelius teaching "Jesus the one from
Nazareth, whom God anointed with Holy Spirit," and again toward Jews "Jesus
the one from Nazareth, a man appointed from God unto you," and again the
blessed Paul toward Athenians: "in a man whom he ordained, having furnished
faith to all, having raised him from the dead." And we find in many places the
appointment the same as the anointing and the sending, from which it is
possible for the one who wishes to learn that there is not some disagreement
in the words of the saints, but that they name differently the union of the
Word of God that came to be toward the man from Mary, sometimes "anointing,"
sometimes "sending," at another time "appointment." What then is said by the
blessed Peter proclaims the Godhead of the Only-begotten as right and sincere,
not separating the hypostasis of the Word of God from the man from Mary. May
it not be! For how would he who has heard in many ways "I and the Father are
one" and "He who has seen me has seen the Father"? through whom also after the
resurrection the same, the doors having been shut, coming in toward the whole
company of the apostles, and if anything from this was hard to persuade,
having dissolved it by saying: "Handle me and see, that a spirit does not have
flesh and bones, as you behold me having." And he did not say "this one" or
"the man of mine whom I have taken up," but "me." Wherefore not one pardon to

And if you would also wish to see the wounds, learn from Thomas: for "bring,"
he says, "your hand and put it into my side, and bring your finger, and see my
hands." Naming his own side and hands, God the Word, and himself whole man and
God together, now of the Word even furnishing the perception to the saints
through a body, as it is possible to understand, by entering when the doors
had been shut, and immediately standing present with the body and furnishing
the full assurance. Let these things have been said in due measure to the
faithful for confirmation and to the unbelieving for correction. 36 Let Paul
of Samosata then also be corrected, having become a hearer of the divine voice
of him who says "my body," and not the Christ another besides me the Word, but
him with me and me with him. For the chrism I am, the Word, and that which has
been anointed by me is the man; not then without me would he be called Christ,
but being with me and I in him. The sending of the Word then being signified
makes clear the union toward the Jesus from Mary, who is interpreted Savior,
not through some other thing, but through having been united to the Word of
God. This saying is signified as the same as "the Father who sent me," and "I
have not come from myself, but the Father sent me." For the union toward the
man, with whom it was possible for the invisible nature to be made known to
men through the seen, he named "sending." For God does not change places
