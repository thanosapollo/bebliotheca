That wickedness is from sloth, and virtue from diligence, and that neither
wicked men, nor the devil himself, can harm the one who is sober; and the
proof is from elsewhere in many ways, and from the things concerning Adam and
Job.
Homily 3.
αʹ. We, before yesterday, were moving the words concerning the devil toward
your love; but others, before yesterday, when such words were being moved
here,




were sitting in the theaters, looking at the devil's procession; they had a
share in fornicatory =49.264= songs, you were partaking of spiritual
hearings; they were eating the devil's uncleanness, you were being fed the
spiritual myrrh. Who then led them astray? Who then split them from the sacred
flock? Did =49.265= the devil deceive those men too? How then did he not
deceive you? You and they are the same humans, I mean according to nature; you
have the same soul, you and they, you have the same desires that are from
nature; how then are you and they not in the same things? Because the choices
are not the same for you and for them; therefore they are in deception, but
you are outside the deception. I do not say these things again to free the
devil from charges, but striving to free you from sins. The devil is wicked, I
too confess, but wicked to himself, not to us, if we are sober; for such is
the nature of wickedness; it is destructive only to those who possess it, but
virtue is the opposite; it is able to benefit not only those who possess it,
but also the neighbors. And that you may learn that the wicked man is wicked
to himself, but the good man is good also to others, I provide you a
proverbial testimony. Child, it says, if you become wicked, you alone will
draw the evils; but if you are wise, for yourself and for the neighbor. They
were deceived in the theaters, but you were not deceived. This is a very great
proof of the facts, a clear refutation and an undisputed reckoning that choice
is master everywhere. Use this manner of proof yourself too; and if you see a
man living in wickedness and displaying every evil, then blaming the
providence of God and saying that he has given over our nature to the
necessity of chance and fate and to the tyranny of demons, and drawing the
cause away from himself from every side, but transferring it onto the creator
and the one who provides for this universe, stop up his mouth, not with a
word, but with a deed, having shown another fellow-servant living in virtue
and equity. There is no need of long words, no need of a woven construction,
nor of syllogisms; the proof comes through the deeds; say to him: You too are
a slave, and he is a slave; you too are a man, and he is a man; you inhabit
the same world, under the same heaven you are fed the same foods; how then are
you in wickedness, but he in virtue? For this reason God also left the wicked
mixed with the good, and did not give one earth to the wicked, and settle the
good in another inhabited world, but mixed these with those, working much
usefulness. For the good appear more approved, being in the midst of those who
hinder them from living rightly and drag them toward wickedness, and yet
laying hold of virtue. For there must, he says, also be heresies among you,
that those who are approved may become manifest among you. Therefore he also
left the wicked to be in the midst, so that the good may shine more. Do you
see how great the gain is? But the gain is not from the wicked, but from the
courage of the good. For this reason we also admire Noah, not because he was
righteous, nor because he was perfect only, but because in that generation,
the twisted and wicked one, he preserved virtue, when he had no example of
virtue, when all were calling him toward wickedness; and he went entirely
opposite to them, like some traveler going the opposite road against a great
crowd being carried with a rush. For this reason it did not simply say, Noah
was righteous, perfect, but it added, in his generation, in the twisted one,
in the desperate one, when there was no merchandise of virtue. This then was
the gain for the good from the wicked; thus at any rate even trees, being
blown by contrary winds, become stronger. There is =49.266= also gain for the
wicked from the mixing of the good; they feel shame, they are ashamed, they
blush before those, and even if they do not stand away from wickedness,
nevertheless they dare what they dare with concealment. And it is




no small thing, not to have lawlessness made bold; for the life of the others
becomes an accuser of these men's wickedness. Hear at any rate what they say
about the righteous man: He is grievous to us even when he is seen. And it is
no small beginning of correction to be stung by his presence; for if the sight
of the righteous man did not sting them, they would not have uttered this
word; and to have the conscience stung and contracted from that man's presence
would be no small hindrance against using wickedness with license. Did you see
how much gain there is both for the good from the wicked, and for the wicked
from the good? For this reason God did not divide them, but left them mixed.
βʹ. The same word then for us also concerning the devil. For this reason he
also left this one to be here, that he may make you stronger, that he may make
the athlete more splendid, that the contests may be greater. When then someone
says, Why did God leave the devil? say these words to that man: Because those
who are sober and pay attention he not only did not harm at all, but even
benefited, not from his own choice, for it is wicked, but from the courage of
those who used the wickedness as needed. Since he also grappled with Job, yet
not for this, that he might make him more splendid, but that he might trip
him; for this he is wicked, because of such a mind and choice; but
nevertheless he harmed the righteous man in nothing, but that man even gained
more from the battle, which then we also showed; and the demon displayed the
wickedness, and the righteous man the courage. But he also trips many, someone
says; from their weakness, not from his own strength; for this too has already
been shown through many things. Set right then your own mind, and you will
never receive harm from anyone, but you will even gain the greatest things,
not only from the good, but also from the wicked. For this reason, as I said
before, God left men to be with one another, and especially the good with the
wicked, so that they may also lead those over to their own virtue. Hear at any
rate what Christ says to the disciples: The kingdom of the heavens was likened
to a woman, who taking leaven hid it in three measures of meal. So the
righteous have the power of leaven, that they may shift the wicked toward
their own quality. But the righteous are few, and indeed the leaven too is
small; but the smallness does no harm to the dough, but that smallness shifts
all the meal toward itself through the power that is in it. Thus then also the
power of the righteous has its strength not in the quantity of the number, but
in the grace of the Spirit. The apostles were twelve; did you see how small
the leaven? the whole inhabited world was in unbelief; did you see how much
the dough? but those twelve turned the whole inhabited world toward
themselves. The leaven and the dough are of the same nature, but not of the
same quality; for this reason he left the wicked among the righteous, so that
just as they are of the same nature as the righteous, they may also become of
the same choice. Remember these things, with these stop the mouths of the
slothful, the dissolute, those more hesitant, those who shrink back from the
labors of virtue, those who accuse the common Master. You have sinned, he
says, =49.267= be still, do not add a second sin more grievous; it is not so
grievous to sin, as after the sin to accuse the Master. Recognize the cause of
the sin, and you will find no one else, except you who have sinned. Everywhere
there is need of a good choice; I showed you this not from bare reasonings,
but from fellow-servants living in this very world. Use this proof yourself
too; thus also the common Master will judge us; learn this manner of proof,
and no one will be able to syllogize you. Does someone commit fornication?
show him another who is chaste; does someone grasp




and seize? show him the one who gives alms; does he live in envy and

equal? Because choice did not allow it; for this alone made the division. For

says, ten virgins. Again here the choices that succeeded, again those that

those women's achievements; for the comparison makes these things clearer.
Both these were virgins and those, and these five and those; they had lamps
both these and those; they were all waiting for the bridegroom.




How then did some enter, but the others did not enter? Because some were
inhuman, but the others gentle and loving toward man. Do you see again that
choice becomes the cause of the end, not the devil? did you see the judgments

Fellow-servants will judge fellow-servants. Do you want me to show you also a

the Jews rather; but the opposite happened. And these disbelieved the Master

men's ingratitude may appear. What, a demon? what, a devil? what, chance?
what, fate? was not each becoming cause to himself both of wickedness and of
virtue? For if they were not going to be liable themselves, he would not have
said that They will condemn this generation; he would not have said that the
queen of the south also condemns the Jews. For not only will peoples condemn
peoples then, but also one man often a whole people, when those who it was
more possible would be deceived appear remaining undeceived, but those who
ought to have prevailed in every way happen to have been defeated. For this
reason we also remembered Adam and Job; for it is necessary to return again to
that subject, so as to give the remnants to the word. He attacked Adam then
through bare words, but Job also through deeds; for he stripped Job of all
wealth and deprived him of the children; but from Adam he took away nothing
small, nothing great of the things that were. Rather let us also examine the
words themselves, and the manner of the =49.269= attack.
The serpent approached, it says, and said to the woman: Why is it that God
said, You shall not eat from every tree in the garden? Here a serpent, there a
woman in the case of Job; for the present the interval of the counselors is
great. For the one was a slave, but she a partner of the life; she a helper,
but he was subjected. Did you see how great the inexcusableness is? for the
subjected and serving one deceived her, but that man not even the partner and
helper was able to trip. But let us see also what he says: Why is it that God
said, You shall not eat from every tree? And yet God did not say this, but the
opposite. See then the devil's villainy; he said what had not been said, so
that he might learn what had been said. What then the woman? She ought to have
stopped his mouth, she ought not to have given a share of speech, from
mindlessness she spoke out the Master's mind; for this reason she furnished
him a strong hold.
δʹ. See how great an evil it is to hand ourselves over simply to enemies and
plotters. For this reason Christ was saying: Do not give the holy things to
the dogs, nor cast your pearls before the swine, lest turning they tear you.
And this happened in the case of Eve; she gave the holy things to the dog, to
the swine; that one trampled the words, turning he tore the woman. And see how

speaking; after these things, when he spoke opposite, why were you following?
God said: You shall die by death; that one spoke against




these things, and said: You shall not die by death. What is clearer than this
war? from where else ought one to learn the enemy and adversary, except from
here, from having spoken against God? One ought then to have fled the bait
immediately, one ought to have leaped away from the snare. You shall not die
by death, he says; for God knew that on whatever day you eat, your eyes will
be opened, and you will be as gods. By a hope of a greater promise he even
threw out the good things in hand; he promised to make them gods, and he cast

possible to see through the deception, but she did not wish it? And yet God

granted a helper, and planted a garden, and having permitted the use of the

thus he deceived. But nevertheless she thought the devil more trustworthy than
God, although through the deeds =49.270= he had displayed the goodwill; to him
who brought bare words only, and nothing else, the woman believed. Do you see
how from mindlessness alone and sloth, not from force, the deception happened?
And that you may learn it more clearly, hear how the Scripture also accuses
the woman. For it did not say, Having been deceived, but, Seeing the tree,
that it is beautiful, she ate. So the charge is of the uncontrolled sight, not
of the deception from the devil alone; for she was defeated by her own desire,
not by the wickedness of the demon. For this reason she did not even enjoy
pardon, but having said that The serpent deceived me, even so she paid the
last penalty; for it was in her not to fall. And that you may learn this more
clearly, come let us lead the word to Job, from those who were defeated to the
one who prevailed, from the conquered to the conqueror; for this man will also

did not cast away children, he did not lose money, he was not sitting on a
dunghill, but he was inhabiting a garden of luxury, and enjoying trees of
every kind and a spring and rivers and all the other license. Nowhere toil nor

fell and was tripped. Is it not quite clear, that through sloth? Just as then
that man, when all these things were lying on him and weighing him down, stood
nobly, and did not fall, is it not quite clear that this man too through
soberness of soul?
εʹ. From both sides then, beloved, reap a very great gain, and flee the zeal

that blessed and noble man, standing in the common theater of the inhabited

torn =49.271= garment, but that man was sitting naked, and the only garment he

have many things to blame themselves; and this is no small thing toward

seen him come even to extreme poverty, and

laughter; and to be pulled to pieces by all was something unendurable. For not
so much the nature of the misfortunes, as those who reproach us beside the
misfortunes, are accustomed to sting our soul. Not only was there no one
encouraging, but many from many sides were pressing upon him reproaching. And
you see him lamenting this, and saying: But you too have come upon me; and he
calls them merciless, and says: That my neighbors have renounced me, and my
household slaves were speaking against me, and they were calling sons of my
concubines, but they themselves were leaping away. And others, he says, were
spitting on me, and I became a byword of all, and even, he says, my very
garment abhorred me. These things are not even endurable to hear, much less to
endure through the deeds themselves. Extreme poverty, and unendurable and new
and paradoxical disease; casting away of so many and such children and in such
a manner; reproaches and scoffs and insults from humans; some were mocking,
some were reproaching, some were despising, not enemies only, but also
friends; not friends only, but also household slaves; and they were not only
mocking nor only reproaching, but also abhorring, and these things not for two
and three and ten days, but for many months; and what happened in his case
alone, he did not even have the consolation from the night, but of the
terrible things by day a greater addition was the imagination of the night
fears. For that he was enduring more grievous things in sleeps, hear what he
says: Why do you frighten me in sleeps, and terrify me in visions? What iron
man, what adamant would have endured so many terrible things? For if even each
of these by itself is unendurable, think coming together at once how great a

should say that, That man was Job and for this reason he bore it, but I am not
according to that man, you speak a greater accusation of yourself to me, and
again praise of the righteous man; for it is more likely that you would bear
it, than that man. Why 49.275276 then? Because that man was before the grace
and before the law, when there was not much exactness of the way of life, when
the grace of the Spirit was not so great, when sin was hard to wrestle down,
when the curse was prevailing, when death was fearful; but now the wrestlings
have become easier, after the presence of Christ all these things having been
taken away. So there is no defense for us, after so much time and so great an
advance and so many gifts given us by God, if we are not able to arrive at the
same measure as that man. Thinking then all these things, that that man's
terrible things were greater, and when the contest was more grievous, then he
stripped and wrestled, let us bear all the things that come upon us nobly, and
with much thanksgiving, so that we may be able to obtain the same crowns as
that man, by the grace and love of man of our Lord Jesus Christ, with whom to
the Father be the glory together with the Holy Spirit, now and always, and
unto the ages of ages. Amen.
