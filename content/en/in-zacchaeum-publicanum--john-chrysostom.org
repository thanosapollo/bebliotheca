On Zacchaeus the publican.
=61.767=
Those who love what is good, beloved, are just like those who thirst. The
more they fail to find what they seek, the more they burn with thirst for
the things they long for. By night they picture the springs they desire, as
thirsty men do; by day they go from place to place, looking about with
restless eyes, hunting what the heart wants. Just as travelers in the
season of burning heat run across a waterless land, pressed by thirst, and
scan about for springs, so that they often even climb the hills where a
spring may be: and if they see it from far off they rejoice, and they press
their journey, hurrying toward it; then, coming to the spring, they quiet
the thirst with water: such are the men who love Christ. For by day they
seek out the Christ they long for by good works, and by night they speak
with him in prayers, and in sleep they imagine themselves walking with him.
If in visions they see him from afar, they rejoice and exult, as the
thirsty do when they find the springs they longed for; and waking again
they want to sleep, that they may meet the same vision in their rest.
Such also was Zacchaeus, who has just now been read aloud to you from the
Gospel. Look at him for me, running, burning with divine longing, climbing
the tree, looking about for Jesus, that he might see the life-bearing
spring.
When Zacchaeus beheld the Lord, he gave rest to his sight, but he kindled
his heart still more toward longing. And Jesus, it says, entered Jericho
and was passing through. And behold, a man named Zacchaeus; and he was a
chief publican, and he was rich; and he desired to see Jesus, because he
was about to pass that way. Look at a man's longing, beloved. And he could
not see because of the crowd, for he was small in stature. And running on
ahead, he climbed a sycamore, that he might see Jesus, because he was about
to pass that way.
Zacchaeus, small in the stature of the body but great in the understanding
of the spirit, sought to see Jesus. He desired to see God among men,
bestowing the things of heaven. He sought to see the maker of the angels,
and the kindler of the heavenly light and of the light above the earth,
walking with human steps. He sought to see how the sun of righteousness,
seated in the cloud of the body, had lit the eyes of the heart of the
faithful. He sought to see God, Jesus: the fair form, the longed-for
reality, the sweet name Jesus, who by the name itself signifies the thing.
He desired to see the sheep with the purple fleece, whose blood redeemed
the world, and whose wool has covered those who were stripped, from Adam
until the end. The captive soldier desired to see his own king; the sheep,
the shepherd; the wanderer, the way; the darkened, the light. He who had
not the sweetest seasoning of the knowledge of God desired to see the
herald of piety. The sick man sought to see health; the hungry, the
heavenly bread; the thirsty, the life-bearing spring. He desired to see
the life-giver of the priests, and the awakener of Lazarus. O divine love,
O good desire! O golden-winged love, or rather Christ, who lifts to heaven
the soul that has him! Already the divine love that had taken him up from
the earth had made him a climber of trees. It no longer let him look at
earthly things, nor any longer allowed him to converse with men; but
looking toward divine love, he awaited the things of heaven. From those
things he ran toward =61.768= those toward which he was hastening; and
having climbed the tree he looked about for Christ, and in his mind he was
mounting the cloud.
When Zacchaeus saw Christ, he said, as was fitting: I have lifted my eyes
to you who dwell in heaven. Zacchaeus saw the Lord, and he burned still
more with longing. It touched his heart, and he became another man from
what he had been: instead of a publican, a zealot; instead of an
unbeliever, a believer; and instead of a wolf, a sheep that bears the seal.
Who longs so for father or mother, who ever loved wife or children as
Zacchaeus loved the Lord, as the very deed itself is enough to prove? For
he gave all his goods to the poor for Christ's sake, and to those he had
defrauded he restored fourfold. O good manner of a disciple! O the
teacher's gentleness and divine power, leading into action by the mere
appearing of Jesus! For the Lord had not yet spoken a word of teaching to
Zacchaeus, but he appeared only to the one who longed, and the power of
faith was drawn up over the heart of the one who longed.
Something of this kind happened also with the woman with the issue of
blood. For she came to the Lord and asked him to heal her, yet she did not
wait for the touch of his hand, but coming forward herself she touched the
hem of his garment in secret; and the power of healing was drawn by her
who touched, as by a sponge. And Zacchaeus, for his part, was acting
without knowing, carried by divine zeal, and burning with spiritual love
he ran to the sycamore. But the Lord, seeing something mystical, said,
Come down. I know your soul; I know your holy love. Come down. Remember
that Adam, when he was stripped, hid under a fig tree; but you, if you
wish to be saved, do not run up a sycamore. I must dry up this sycamore
and plant another, the Cross. That is a good tree: run up that one with
the step of the soul. From there you are ready to be launched into the
heavens. About this tree the serpent also twines himself in the leaves; in
this he hides; in this he made his nest. Hurry, come down before he
whispers to your soul, as he also persuaded Eve to taste the sweet
pleasure. Hurry, come down. While I stand here, come down; for while I am
looking at him, he is muzzled. Hurry, come down. I will not leave you on
the sycamore; I do not want you to be lost. For you are my sheep; you have
run to me. Hurry, come down, and go on ahead into your house: I must rest
there.
For where faith is, there I rest; where love is, there I go. I know what
you are about to do. I know that you are about to give all your goods to
the poor, and first to restore fourfold to those you defrauded. With such
people I gladly lodge. And Zacchaeus hurried down, and went to his house,
and received Jesus. And filled with joy he stood and said: not walking,
not sitting, but standing, that he might show the unchangeable purpose of
his soul. He stood and said, when, fervent in the spirit, he was striving,
having thought things he would not repent of. For he knew where he sows,
and where he is about to reap. And he said: Behold, half of my goods I
give to the poor; and to those I have defrauded I restore fourfold. O pure
confession, coming from a pure heart! A confession without shame, standing
before the unashamed glory of God, breathing faith and flowering with
righteousness! Of which righteousness may the God of all count us worthy,
by the grace and love for mankind of our Lord Jesus Christ, to whom be
glory and dominion unto the ages of ages. Amen.
