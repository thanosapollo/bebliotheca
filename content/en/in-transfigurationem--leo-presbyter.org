Of our father among the saints John, archbishop of Constantinople,
Chrysostom, a discourse on the transfiguration of our Lord Jesus Christ

Christ, the abundant host, has today also set before us a table honored
not by custom but made known by the knowledge of God; a table not
partaking of earthly things but holding the things of heaven; a table
not brightened with the dishes of Solomon but crowned with the divine
laws; a table not blessed for the multitude of foods but made venerable
by the divine thoughts. For what is richer than the table of Solomon,
which spent on each day, as the third book of Kings contains, thirty
measures of fine flour and sixty measures of beaten meal and ten fat
oxen and choice ones and twenty oxen from the pastures and a hundred
sheep, besides harts and gazelles and choice birds? But so great a
multitude of dishes benefited the great Solomon not at all nor guided
him toward perfect virtue; on the contrary it made him to be destroyed
at the last beyond measure, having made him revel without limit. But
the table of the Lord, set before us today in abundance, the
unembodied, the simple, the incorruptible, the immortal, the
illimitable, the not established by reason, awards not only the things
on earth but also the things in heaven; for it offers not thirty
measures of fine flour but bestows the kingdom of the heavens leavened
as three measures of meal; nor again does it set forth sixty measures
of meal, but the very bread of heaven, I mean Christ himself the
Master, it awards to all the faithful each day. But I think I am
talking at large, comparing the sensible breakfast of Solomon with the
spiritual table of the Lord; for there ten chosen calves, here the
lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world; there twenty oxen from
the pastures, here innumerable martyrs benefiting souls and bodies;
there harts decked with the excellence of their horns, here prophets
comparing spiritual things with spiritual; there gazelles struck in
the liver by the arrow, here apostles illumining the inhabited world
with divine letters; there birds flying without reason, here a devout
people leaping spiritually; there a hundred unreasoning sheep rejoicing
only the house of Solomon, here the rational sheep of us, Christ,
divided everywhere over all the earth and receiving no diminution at
all.

You heard just now the rational sheep himself, Christ, saying to the
apostles the time of the passion of the Lord, so that in the season of
the passion they might not endure a passion. For what does the
evangelist Matthew say? As you heard just now: Then began Jesus to show
to his disciples that he must go to Jerusalem and suffer many things
from the elders and scribes and chief priests of the people and be
killed and on the third day rise. Do you see a Master's table
possessing a death-raising dish? What such thing had the table of
Solomon? There all things, slaughtered against their will, passed into
nonexistence; here the rational sheep of us, Christ, even if he was
slaughtered, yet he was slaughtered by his own will, was buried by his
own will, rose by his own will, ascended into the heavens by his own
will, and will come in the glory of his Father by his own will,
rendering to each according to his action. Then began; well did the
evangelist set it first, Then began Jesus; for he began to make the
appearance of another economy. No longer does he speak with them as
with infant disciples, but he addresses the apostles as perfect; no
longer does he give them to drink the milk of consolation, but he
displays the solid bread of temptations; no longer does he soothe them
as weak, but he binds them up as strong. And most fittingly.

As an excellent teacher in the prologues flatters the children handed
over to him, holding the head, smiling at the face, sweetly lisping
with them, forgiving those who err, but when they have advanced a
little in their instruction he shows a stern face, threatening great
things, inflicting blows, not that he may harm but that he may improve;
in the same manner Christ, the all-wise teacher, in the prologues of
the evangelical preaching set forth certain simple and smooth sayings
to his own disciples, saying to them: Come after me, and I will make
you fishers of men; and: No one shall injure you; but also often, when
they disbelieved and stumbled, the Master Christ forgave them; but only
when he beheld them having advanced in piety, having been initiated in
the knowledge of God, having been fully assured by the display of the
signs, strengthened by the testimony of the Father, fortified by the
illumination of the Holy Spirit, no longer does he forgive them when
they stumble, but he begins with a sharper teaching, saying to them:
Whoever wishes to save his soul shall lose it; and whoever loses it for
my sake shall find it; and again: Whoever wishes to come after me, let
him deny himself and take up his cross and follow me. For the excellent
disciple must journey behind the teacher; hence also the blessed Paul,
pleased with such teaching, writing said: I am crucified with Christ;
I live no longer I, but Christ lives in me. Hearing these words also,
the blessed Peter, boiling with measureless heat, was beside himself at
the words of the Lord. For when the Lord had said that he must go to
Jerusalem and suffer many things from the elders and scribes and chief
priests of the people and be killed and on the third day rise, Peter,
hearing these wise words, thinking the Immovable to utter strange
things, seizing the Lord, as you heard just now, began to rebuke him,
saying: Mercy to you, Lord; this shall not be to you. You do not know
what you utter, Master; you lay contrary things before yourself. He who
put all the demons to flight together; does he fear the elders of the
Jews? Mercy to you, Lord; this shall not be to you. He who spread the
sea beneath his own feet and lulled the troubling winds and calmed the
raging deep; does he fear the chief priests of Jerusalem? He who dried
up the fig tree by a word and stretched out the withered hand into a
living one; does he anguish over men? Mercy to you, Lord; this shall
not be to you. He for whom Enoch, making no sign, was taken up together
with his own body; and you who have wrought so many things are about to
be covered by a tomb? If death did not shudder at you, how did it
render Lazarus to you? If the legion of demons did not fear you, how,
coming out of the man, did it beseech you to go away into the herd of
swine? If the water did not know you who you are, how at your nod in
Cana of Galilee was it changed into wine? If Siloam did not recognize
you that you are the maker of the whole inhabited world, how, washing
away the clay of the one blind from birth, did it grant the seeing at
your nod? Did not the Father of lights cry concerning you: This is my
beloved Son; hear him? When therefore the Son of God is dead, in what
things is our hope? Mercy to you, Lord; this shall not be to you. What
do you promise me, a resurrection? No one ever raised himself being
dead. But if he is raised by another, show me that one, that leaving
you I may become his disciple. Mercy to you, Lord; this shall not be to
you. Did you not counsel us, Master, saying: I am the light and the
resurrection and the life? When does light change into darkness? When
is resurrection covered by a tomb? When is life hemmed in by graves?
Depart, Master, depart. Mercy to you, Lord; this shall not be to you.

What then the Master Christ, the all-wise teacher? No longer does he
forgive Peter stumbling, but he is angry and troubled at the devil who
scandalizes him, saying, as you heard just now: Go behind me, Satan,
who secretly contrived that my Peter disbelieve. You are a scandal to
me, Peter, because you mind not the things of God but the things of
men. Unless I am killed, no one of you tastes immortality. You are a
scandal to me, Peter. Unless I am murdered, no one of you inherits the
vineyard. You are a scandal to me, Peter, because you mind not the
things of God but the things of men. I am a grain of wheat according to
the word of the incarnation: Unless the grain of wheat falling into the
ground dies, itself alone remains; but if it dies, it bears much fruit.
You are a scandal to me, Peter, because you mind not the things of God
but the things of men. Unless I go down into Hades, no one frees Adam
from slavery. You are a scandal to me, Peter, because you mind not the
things of God but the things of men. Unless I am slaughtered, no one of
you partakes of the mystical dish. Unless I am crucified, Peter, the
thief does not open paradise. You are a scandal to me, Peter, because
you mind not the things of God but the things of men. I came not only
to go round the living but also to benefit those who have fallen
asleep. I did not lie then, Peter, when I said: I am the light and the
resurrection and the life. Since therefore I am light, Peter, I must
shine for those in darkness. For what light is it that does not put
darkness to flight? I am resurrection, Peter; I must raise those held
beforehand. For what resurrection is it that does not raise those who
sleep? I am life, Peter; I must put death to death; for I suffer as
man, and I save as lover of mankind. For now I suffer economically,
Peter; but after a little I shall come despotically, appearing not in
the form of a servant but in the paternal glory attended by angels. Now
I am set at naught; I permit it on account of the lowliness of the
appearance; but after a little I shall judge with authority on account
of the power of the Godhead. And that the Master Christ had these
things in mind, you heard him just now saying: The Son of man is about
to come in the glory of his Father with his angels, and then he will
render to each according to his works.

Let also the now-present Arius be stopped up, who did not fear the
impartial criterion of the Lord. If the Master Christ is subordinate as
God the Word and Son, unlike and sharing nothing in substance with the
one who begot him, as that one ravingly utters, how does he come in the
glory of the Father? Behold, being consubstantial he shall come in the
paternal glory; for those whose substance differs, of these also the
glory differs; but those whose substance is the same, of these also the
glory is the same. But immediately the children of the Arians will say,
for nothing is unbold to them, falling as they do into a mass of evils
and despising them: This glory, he says, the Only-begotten received from
the Father, having besought and prayed and entreated. For we have often
heard him praying and saying: Father, glorify me with the glory which I
had before the world was with you. If therefore he received the glory
from the Father, how is he consubstantial with the Father? These are
the words of Arius. But, O Arius, did the Master Christ pray these
things as God the Word or as man? If as God the Word he prayed, did he
then, after the world, according to you, lose the glory innate in him?
For he says: Glorify me, Father, with the glory which I had before the
world was with you. From which it is shown that here the Master Christ
prayed not in the person of God the Word but in the person of the
humanity. For God the Word has the glory innate, consubstantial, unmade
by hands, unsuccessive, inalienable; for he possesses nothing second or
later in order of birth. For if he lost the innate glory, according to
you, how does the writing speak true that says: Glory to God in the
highest, and on earth peace, among men good will; and again: My glory
and I will lift up my head; and elsewhere: The nations shall see your
glory, and kings your righteousness. From which it is clearly shown
that the Master Christ spoke not in the person of the Godhead but in
the person of Adam. Hear intelligently. Since the Master Christ became
a second Adam and took up the old man, and the old Adam lost the glory
in paradise by the thieving deceit of the devil, for this cause the
Master Christ prayed in the person of Adam, saying: Father, glorify me
with the glory which I had before the world was with you, according to
the foreknowing word. Hence also again the Father cried with a loud
voice, saying: And I have glorified and again I will glorify. What is,
And I have glorified and again I will glorify? I glorified you, he
says, in paradise; but since the devil changed the glory into dishonor,
again I will glorify, he says, changing the dishonor in the cross. Here
therefore he prayed in the person of the incarnation; but in the words
set before, in the person of the Godhead.

But hearing these things, do not imagine two persons of the Son, but
know one God the Word together with his own flesh. Since therefore the
discourse of his incarnation was being brought to its end, no longer in
the person of the incarnation but in the person of the Godhead he made
the teaching, saying: For the Son of man is about to come in the glory
of his Father with his angels, and then he will render to each
according to his action. What paternal glory does he say? The
unsuccessive kingdom, the illimitable, the timeless, the unmade by
hands. For the glory of the Father is the kingdom of the Son, the
heavenly, not the earthly. And that the paternal glory is the kingdom
of the Son, you heard the Master Christ himself just now saying: Amen I
say to you that there are some of those standing here who shall not
taste of death until they see the Son of man coming in his kingdom. Do
you see one glory of Father and Son? A little before he said: The Son
of man is about to come in the glory of his Father; here: until they
see the Son of man coming in his kingdom, so that the kingdom of Christ
is paternal glory, and the paternal glory the kingdom of the Son. Who
then divides the undivided? Who weighs out the unweighable? Who pries
into the paternal bosom? Who, being mad, divides mind and word and
spirit? Amen I say to you: there are some of those standing here who
shall not taste of death until they see the Son of man coming in his
kingdom. But certain of the ancients who interpreted, I do not know
considering what, took up these words of the Lord for his second
glorious coming. Hence also they confirmed that the evangelist John did
not partake of death but waits for the glorious coming of the Lord, on
account of the Lord saying: There are some of those standing here who
shall not taste of death until they see the Son of man coming in his
kingdom. It has, however, this meaning; for one must not imagine but
speak true, and especially now when he sharpened even infants for
discourse. Here therefore he speaks not of his second glorious coming
but of the transfiguration on the mountain. For transfigured on the
mountain, in some small degree the Master Christ showed to his own
disciples the glory of the unseeable divine kingdom of him.

But immediately those going about with a hunting tongue will say: And
if the divine glory of God the Word is unseeable, how did he display it
to the apostles? For if visible, it is not unseeable; but if unseeable,
not visible. Therefore hear intelligently. Here the Master Christ
showed to his own disciples the glory of his unseeable kingdom and did
not show it, in the one case fully assuring, in the other sparing. For
fully assuring, he showed them the divine glory of the unseeable
kingdom, not such as it was, but as much as the bodily eyes going round
could bear; but sparing them and not envying them, he did not show them
the whole glory of his unseeable divine kingdom, that they might not
lose with the seeing also the life. And the witness of this is the God
of all, saying to Moses desiring to behold him, as the divine oracle
says: Then Moses answered before God and said: If I have found grace
before you, manifest to me yourself, that I may see you knowingly, face
to face. What then God to him? You err, Moses, seeking these things; I
do not envy you the seeing, but I spare you your salvation. No man
having seen God shall live. Here therefore also he both showed and did
not show, in the one case fully assuring, in the other sparing. And
that it is not concerning his second glorious coming but concerning the
transfiguration on the mountain, and not concerning John alone but also
Peter and James, for not singly but plurally he cried out: There are
some of those standing here; he said not one, but some; hear what
follows: for it is necessary to remind you, not to teach. And after six
days Jesus takes Peter and James and John his brother, and brings them
up into a high mountain apart, and was transfigured before them, and
his face shone as the sun, and his garments became white as the light;
and behold a voice saying: This is my beloved Son in whom I am well
pleased; hear him.

Do you see that he said it not concerning his second glorious coming
but concerning the transfiguration on the mountain? What then the
Lord? Let us proceed in order, that by your prayers we may not fall
from the things set before us. And after six days he takes Peter and
James and John. But someone may say: And why did the Master Christ not
take the twelve disciples with him and bring them up into the mountain,
but Peter and James and John? Why? Because Judas was not worthy with
the eyes of his choice to behold so great a glory of the Lord. But
immediately some will say: And if this was the cause, why did he not
leave Judas alone below, and bring up the eleven with him? But we are
not wiser than the Master Christ. For if it happened that Judas be left
alone below and the remaining eleven, having gone up into the mountain,
enjoy the same sight, it is likely that some would say that for this
cause Judas, as if embittered, came to the betrayal and the selling of
the Master. That therefore nothing such may be said either by us or
excused by him, for this cause he leaves him alone below, that the
three, fully assured by the sight, may be glorified, and the nine,
having believed by hearing, may be called blessed. Hence also the
Lord, blessing those believing by hearing, said: Blessed are those who
have not seen and have believed. But otherwise, he brought up only the
three, Peter and James and John, that the spoken word might be
fulfilled: By the mouth of two and three witnesses every word shall
stand. Three, Peter and James and John, of the unseparated threefold
cord of piety; two, Moses and Elijah, of the unbending pillars of the
law. For you heard just now the evangelist saying: And there appeared
to them on the mountain Moses and Elijah talking with him. For the sake
of what then did he transfer Moses and Elijah on the mountain, and so
great a transfiguration came to pass, a cloud overshadowing, bright,
and the paternal voice thundering? For the sake of what? Because the
apostles supposed the Master Christ to be a mere man, not God in the
flesh. Hence also, thinking him to be a simple man on account of the
economic tears and the occasional cowardice and the prayers and the
entreating, the apostles often both contradicted and disbelieved and
were astonished. That therefore he might root out such suspicion of
them, bringing them up into the mountain and opening a little the door
of the incarnation, he showed clearly how great a glory was hidden in
him. But otherwise also he fully assured the apostles that he himself
is the Lord of the heavenly and the earthly and the subterranean. From
above he fished Elijah, from below he angled Moses, and from the things
on earth he presented Peter and James and John; for from the extremes
the whole is made known.

Hence Peter, beholding so great a glory, and Moses and Elijah standing
as servants and conversing quietly, no longer rebukes, but worships and
entreats, saying, as you heard just now: Lord, it is good for us to be
here and not to go up to Jerusalem. There Pilate and Caiaphas breathing
death, here Moses and Elijah announcing the resurrection. Lord, it is
good for us to be here; and if you will, let us make three tabernacles,
one for you and one for Moses and one for Elijah. But, O blessed Peter,
why, held by fear and shaken by cowardice, do you hasten to weave
strange tabernacles of twisted twigs, ranking the Master with the
servants? Him whom a cloud attends a hut cannot contain. For the heaven
and the earth are full of his glory, of the Father and of the Son and
of the Holy Spirit, now and always and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
