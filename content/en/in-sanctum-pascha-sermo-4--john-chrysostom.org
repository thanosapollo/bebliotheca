On the same treatise, 4.
=59.731=
The Jews celebrate Pascha as a memorial of the salvation from Egypt, an
escape from the death of the firstborn. It is named Pascha for this
reason, that it is a passing-over of death; for this the word signifies
according to the language of the Hebrews. But we celebrate Pascha, not a
partial escape from death, as they did, nor a liberation from a
temporary slavery, but a perfect redemption from death, and a perfect
release from the slavery of the devil. The death of the firstborn passed
over them, when they sacrificed the typical sheep, and anointed the
parts around the doors with the blood; but the death that came from the
first-formed man passes over us, being unable to seize those who have
received the sacrifice of the divine sheep, and have been fenced about
by the faith of the saving blood.
For them the sheep was from the earth; for us from heaven, according to
the type that came to be with Abraham, when he had a God-sent sheep to
offer in place of the son; but now in truth the sheep is God-sent from
heaven, offered for us, that we may no longer be held by death, because
of Christ who cannot be held by death. For God, he says, sent the Son as
Savior of the world; and, I have come down, he says, from heaven. And
the Baptist bears witness concerning him, saying, Behold, the lamb of
God, who takes away the sin of the world. For an irrational sheep did
not take away sin; therefore neither the death that came from sin; but
the most rational sheep loosed sin, having also destroyed death together
with it. And he leads us out not from the Egyptian land into Judea, but
into an eternal possession from a world that is perishing. He is at once
a sheep, and a shepherd leading the sheep.
For I will go, he says, before, leading them unto the eternal life. The
sacrifice of the sheep, and the exodus from Egypt, is the beginning of
months for the Jews; but for us the beginning of life is the passion of
the Lord, and the translation with Christ from the world. For then we
live, when we die to the world; then we are born again unto God, when we
pass away from the birth upon earth. The sheep is undivided, eaten in
one house, the spiritual Church, and not found elsewhere; just as it was
also commanded to the bodily Jews not to carry out any of the flesh of
the sheep from the house. A sheep, because of meekness, Christ; but
according to the way that raises up, a kid, a likeness of him, was then
sacrificed. Unleavened bread was for those eating the Pascha, but for us
communion toward Christ with a new and fresh and most simple mind; just
as the unleavened =59.732= is a new and simple dough. The bitter herbs
eaten with the Pascha then signified our afflictions, through which we
share in the sufferings of Christ. For affliction is bitter, even if the
fruit from it is sweet. A bone of the sheep was not broken, and this the
law commanded, prefiguring Christ, as the Gospel interprets, that no one
broke the holy legs of the Lord; that the Scripture might be fulfilled,
A bone shall not be broken of him. And the flesh was eaten in the night,
and was not left over into the day, because also the death of Christ is
remembered in the present age, but in the one to come the life appears.
And the figure of those eating the Pascha: the loin girt, sandals on the
feet, staffs in the hands. The whole figure is of travel, of those
prepared for a journey, hastening to depart; but for us the purpose of
the soul is of travel, being sent toward a departure from the world.
Therefore the souls, well-girt and compressed from every side, flee the
cares of life, and are ready for the divine journey, so as to run into
heaven. For our commonwealth is in the heavens, he says. And we have
taken up a staff, by which we are supported, the faith and hope that is
toward Christ. And a lamp has also been kindled for us in the souls, as
the bodily one for them. And the lamp of souls for us is the law of
Christ; For your law is a lamp to my feet, and a light to my paths. And
the Lord himself also says concerning these things: Let your loins be
girded, and the lamps burning, and you like men awaiting their lord. It
remains to see who they are who eat the Pascha. And no one uncircumcised
eats the Pascha, but even if he is a foreigner, since he is also bought
with silver, he is circumcised. For no one can become a partaker of
Christ, unless he is circumcised of the human superfluities, nor unless
he is cleansed from the fleshly pollutions, which we inherited according
to birth from the fathers.
For of this the bodily circumcision was a type. Wherefore it was also
said by the prophet Jeremiah that your hardness of heart be circumcised.
Let us therefore hasten unto the blessed communion of Christ; for this
reason let us become clean of worldly evils, that we may be sanctified
by the spiritual goods; let no one turning back toward the world, but
all hastening into that we may not remain under the slavery of the ruler
of this age, but may be translated into the freedom that is with Christ,
to whom be glory unto the ages of ages. Amen.
