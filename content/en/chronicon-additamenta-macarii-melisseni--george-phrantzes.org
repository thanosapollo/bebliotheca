=272.20= But even so he yielded to him whatever is at the feet toward the
setting of the sun of the over-great mountain of Taygetos as far as even Pylos
called Hyitylos. And from the outer yoke the castle of Zarnada and Gastitza,
the unshaken Mele, Dirachion, Polianoi, Gardikia and the parts about them, and
further granting also the whole trusteeship and administration of the great
protostrator Nikephoros Melissenos, who was also surnamed Melissourgos. This
great protostrator Nikephoros was descended from the tribe of the in all
things most excellent Caesar Sallustius, grandson of the patrician Eugenios
Vespos of Lutatius, one of the twelve magnates who settled from Rome in this
queen of cities by command and summons of Constantine the Great of pious
memory, who founded this great city and transferred the empire hither. And
that patrician Eugenios, according to the chronography of Eunapius of Sardis
and as the verses say of him, which I too have often read, traced his race to
Androklos son of Kodros son of Melanthos son of Neleus. And being genealogized
unto the said Nikephoros and great protostrator, this man is descended from
the said Sallustius. And of Sallustius the son was Rufinus the magistros, and
of him Nikephoros the caesar, and of him Prokopios and Kyros the most wise;
and of Prokopios Olybrios and Pelagios the patrician, whose work they say is
also the so-called Homerocentras; and of Olybrios Secundus the consul and
Tzitas the count of Armenia; and of Secundus Rufinus and Narses the
cubicularius, and of Rufinus John the consul, and of him Priskos the general
of Europe and Germanos the patrician, and of Priskos Bonossos and Theodore;
and of Bonossos Theodosios and Andronikos, and of Theodosios Michael the
magistros, and of Michael Theophylaktos and Theodotos. And Theophylaktos
begets Michael Rangabel, and Theodotos Michael Kasideres. And Rangabel, having
been driven from the empire by Leo the Armenian and exiled on the island of
Prote, became a monk and left life, and they deprived his sons of the
generative members, and thus the affairs of Rangabel came to an end.

And the patrician =274= Kasideres had two sons: Demetrios the magistros and
Theodotos. And the one steered the Church of the Lord, as those who have
written histories about him record, and Demetrios begets Theodotos; and he
Niketas also called Oryphas, and he Romanos the patrician, and he John the
domestikos called Kourkouas, Theophanes the patrician and Theophilos, the
grandfather of John Tzimiskes. And of John the domestikos is born Romanos, and
of him John and Christopher and Andronikos; and of Christopher Romanos and
Theophilos, and of Romanos Nikephoros, and of him Theoleptos and Leo the
magistros; and of Leo Theodosios and Nikephoros, and of Nikephoros Theodore,
and of him Leosthenes, and of him Nikephoros the caesar, and of him from Maria
Komnene daughter of John, John Komnenos, and of him Alexios and Nikephoros;
and of Alexios from Eudokia of Alexios son of Michael Aspietes, Michael, and
of him Theophylaktos and Alexios, and of Theophylaktos Theodosios, and of him
Michael and the caesar Alexios, who freed this great city from the Italians
and the Franks. And of Caesar Alexios is born Gregory the despot, who made the
great judgment concerning the property of the said Michael his father's
brother, with kyr Andronikos of kyr Michael of pious memory, in the 801st
year. And of Gregory is born Michael and Nikephoros and Theophilos, and of
Nikephoros is born Leo, and of him the patriarch kyr Gregory. And of Michael
is born Nikephoros, and of him Andronikos, and of him Theodosios, and of him
Leo.

Of this man is the aforesaid great protostrator Nikephoros. This man from
Maria, granddaughter of kyr Constantine Dragas, of John Raoul, begets
Nicholas. And the cause of such trusteeship was of this sort: the despot kyr
Theodore received the said Nicholas of the great protostrator Nikephoros from
holy baptism. And in the past spring this great protostrator being about to
depart from among men, and his son Nicholas being a three-year-old infant, and
having great assurance and disposition toward the despot kyr Theodore, having
made a will, left the despot kyr Theodore trustee and governor of his
spiritual son Nicholas and of all his lands and possessions. And if the son
and heir Nicholas should die at once, the places and lands of all his lordship
and dominion, those without and within the Peloponnese, shall be the despot
kyr Theodore's. For without were the whole Chersonese of the Hellespont, great
Ainos as far as even Peritheorion; and those within the Peloponnese, in the
Messenian gulf, where that river Pamisos also flows through the midst:
Androusa, I mean, and Kalamata, Mantineia, Iannitza, Pedema, Mani, Nesi,
Spitalin, Grebeni and Karantza and Aetos, Loi, Neokastron, Archangelos,
Saulaouros, Ioannina, Ithome, Ligoudista, Philatria, Pylos and the parts at
the feet of the mountain and peak called of Nike of the Demos, as far as the
plain of Steniklaros and the stream of Bellira and Vostitza and the parts
about it, which I also, being sent, received, as many as from those without
were very few not found under the power of the Hagarenes.

For the most part they also named certain of this race of the Melissenoi
Strategopouloi, and in the chronicle histories they wrote thus for such a
cause. For the emperor kyr John Doukas appointed the begetter of the said
caesar Alexios and of Michael strategos of Paphlagonia, being still an ephebe
and quite a youth. And he displayed martial works and set up trophies against
the adversaries, for which cause they surnamed him Strategopoulos and through
him they named his sons. =276= And the protostrator Leo Phrangopoulos also
went through the said trusteeship and administration at a certain time by
command of the despot kyr Theodore, then he granted this in due form to his
brother the despot kyr Constantine.

A. alii codices praebent: ... whom they also surnamed Melissourgos for his
dexterity in all things; he was descended from the tribe of that ancient
Nikephoros caesar Melissenos. And this Nikephoros was of the offspring of the
patrician Eugenios. For the emperor Michael who had also the name Rangabel and
the patrician Michael Melissenos were offspring of two siblings and
descendants of the said caesar; and the one, having been driven from the
empire and exiled on the island of Prote, became a monk and left life; and
they deprived his son Theophylaktos of the generative members, and thus the
affairs of Rangabel came to an end. And the patrician Michael had two sons,
Demetrios and Theodotos; and the one, having become patriarch, steered the
Church of the Lord, as those who have written histories about him record, and
Demetrios begets Nikephoros, and Nikephoros begets Theodotos, and Theodotos
begets the patrician Niketas Oryphas. And Niketas begets Theophanes the
patrician and protovestiarios and Nasar, and Theophanes Christopher, and
Christofos the magistros Leo and Theoleptos. And the magistros Leo begets
Theodosios, and Theodosios Theophylaktos, and Theophylaktos Theodore, and
Theodore Nikephoros the caesar, the brother-in-law of the sovereign Alexios
Komnenos. And the caesar Nikephoros begets John Komnenos, and Komnenos John
begets Alexios, and Alexios begets Theodosios, and Theodosios begets Alexios
the caesar and Michael, whom they surnamed Strategopouloi. And the caesar
Alexios begets Gregory, and Gregory Theophilos and Michael, and Theophilos
begets Leo, and Leo the great protostrator Nikephoros and George. And the
great protostrator Nikephoros begets Nicholas.

And the cause of such trusteeship was of this sort. The despot kyr Theodore
received the said Nicholas through holy baptism. And in the past spring this
great protostrator being about to die, and his son Nicholas being three years
old, having made a will, left the despot kyr Theodore trustee and governor of
his spiritual son Nicholas and of all his possessions. And if the son and heir
should die at once, the places and lands of all his lordship and power,
without and within the Peloponnese, shall be the despot kyr Theodore's. For
without were: great Ainos, Peritheorion and Xanthe and the parts about it and
the whole =278= Chersonese of the Hellespont. And those within the Peloponnese
in the Melissenian gulf, where that river Pamisos also flows through the
midst: Androusa I mean and Kalamata, Mantineia, Ianitza, Pedema and Mani and
Nesi and Spitalin and Grebeni and Karantza and Aetos and Eloi and Neokastron
and Ithome now called Messene and Archangelos and Sablaouros and Ioannina and
Ligoudista and Philatria and Pylos and the parts about them and the parts at
the feet of the mountain peak called of Nike of Simos as far as the plain of
Steniklaros and the stream of Belira, and being sent I received them, as many
as were not found under the power of the Hagarenes, very few. For the most
part they also named certain of this race of the Melissenoi Strategopouloi,
and in the chronicle histories they wrote thus for such a cause: for they
surnamed Alexios caesar Melissenos Strategopoulos, and from then this surname
prevailed upon certain of this race. And the protostrator Leo Phrangopoulos
also went through the said trusteeship and administration at a certain time,
by command of the despot kyr Theodore, then he granted it to his brother kyr
Constantine.

=448= and he inquired many things concerning the faith of Christ, which we
shall relate in detail, how the patriarch in summary made clear to him the
confession of the faith in chapters; that is, the inscription has thus: Homily
spoken by the most wise and most learned kyr Gennadios Scholarios, patriarch
of Constantinople New Rome, concerning the right and blameless faith of the
Christians. For being asked by the said emir and sultan these things: The
emir: What do you Christians believe? the patriarch answered and said to him
these things:

Ch. 1. We believe that there is a God, creator of all things that are from not
being; that he is neither a body, nor has a body, but lives intellectually.
And he is mind most excellent and most perfect and most wise, incomposite,
without beginning, without end; he is in the world and above the world, he is
in no place and he is in every place. These are properties of God, by which he
is separated from his creatures. And there are others like these. All such
chapters were translated into the Turco-Arabic tongue by Achmat, kadi of
Beroea, the father of Mahmout Celebi the recorder, and thus the chapters
follow in order.

Ch. 2. Text: He is wise and good and true and truth itself. As many
excellences as his works have in a divided way, he alone has in a higher
manner; and these perfections his creatures also have, because he gives these
also to his creatures; they are good, because he is wise, wise; because he is
true, true =450= and the rest likewise, except that God has them properly, and
the creatures have them by participation.

Interpretation: Ch. 3. Text: We believe that there are in God three other
properties, which are as beginnings and springs of all his other properties;
and through these three properties God lives eternally in himself even before
the world was created by him; and through these he created the world and
through these he governs it. And these three properties we name three
hypostases, that is three persons; and because these properties do not divide
the one and most simple essence of God, for this reason God even with these
properties is one God and they are not three gods, as some daringly say.

Interpretation: Ch. 4. Text: We believe that from the nature of God there
arise word and spirit just as from fire light and heat. And as fire, even if
there is nothing being lighted and heated by it, nevertheless the fire itself
always has light and heat and sends forth light and heat to us, so also before
the world was created the Word and the Spirit were natural energies of God,
since God is mind, as has been said before, and these three, mind, word and
spirit, are one God, just as in the one soul of man there is mind and
intelligible word and intelligible will, and yet these three are one soul
according to essence. Again we name the Word of God wisdom of God and power
and his Son, because he is an offspring of his nature, just as the offspring
of the nature of man we call son of man, and just as the thought of the same
is an offspring of his soul. And again we name the will of God Spirit of God
and love. And the mind itself we call Father, because he is unbegotten and
uncaused, but cause of the Son and of the Spirit. Since then God does not only
think his creatures, but rather thinks and knows himself, and for this reason
he has word and wisdom, through which he thinks himself properly; likewise God
does not only will and love his own works, but he wills and loves himself far
more. For this reason eternally there proceed from God his Word and his Spirit
and they are eternally in him. And these properties with God are one God.

Interpretation: Ch. 5. Text: We believe that God through the word of his
wisdom and power created the world; and through the Spirit, his good will and
love, he provides and governs and moves every nature of his in the world
toward the good according to the order of that nature. And for this reason we
believe that, when God willed, through his mercy alone he turned men from the
error of the demons and from idolatry, because except for the small place of
the Jews, in which one God was worshipped and believed from the law of Moses,
all the rest of the inhabited world reverenced the creatures of God and
worshipped many gods that are not, in place of the one and only true God; and
they lived each according to his own desires, not according to any law of God.

Interpretation: Ch. 6. Text: Then God made such a refashioning of men through
the Word and through his Holy Spirit; and for this reason the Word of God put
on human nature, that as man he might speak with men, and as Word of God and
wisdom of God he might teach men to believe in the one and true God and to
live according to the law which he himself gave; and again as man, that he
might give his way of life as an example of his teaching. For he first himself
kept the law which he gave to men. And as Word of God and power he might be
able to accomplish the worldwide good which he willed, because it was
impossible through one man by power to turn the inhabited world toward God.
And thus through his Word the almighty and invisible God sowed the truth in
Jerusalem, and through his Spirit he enlightened and strengthened his apostles
=452=, that they themselves might sow the truth also into all the world and
that they might despise the world for the love of the God who sent him and for
the love of the salvation of the world according to the example of Jesus, who
willingly died according to the human for the world to be saved. Thus we
believe the one God in three, Father, Son and Holy Spirit, as our Lord Jesus
Christ taught; and because he is true, we believe that this also is true. And
his disciples taught us thus more fully and thus we understand also from the
power of wisdom.

Interpretation: Ch. 7. Text: We believe that the Word of God and the man whom
the Word of God put on is the Christ. And the life of Christ in his flesh was
the life of a man most holy, but the power of his wisdom and of his works was
power of God.

Interpretation: Ch. 8. Text: We believe that just as the soul and the body of
the one man become one man, so the Word of God from one part and from the
other part the soul and the body are always two natures perfectly divided in
one man. Thus also the humanity and the divinity are in the one Christ
perfectly divided by nature, united only according to hypostasis and
personally. And neither was the Word of God changed into the flesh or the soul
of Christ, nor was the flesh of Christ or his soul turned into Word of God,
but there was and is in Christ after that most wonderful economy of his the
Word of God as Word, and the humanity as humanity. And that the humanity of
Christ did not assume the divinity, but the divinity of the Word of God
assumed the human nature in the very constitution of being assumed. Everything
that is in God and from God is by nature God, because there is no accident in
God. And for this reason we name and believe the intelligible Word of God to
be God. And because this Word of God was in Christ, for this reason we confess
Christ God and man, man through the soul and the body, and God through the
Word of God in him.

Interpretation: Ch. 9. Text: We believe that the Word of God was in Christ and
in the world and in heaven and in God and the Father, because the Word of God
is infinite just as God who begets him, that is who thinks him, is infinite;
and he has infinite power; except that in God he was in one manner and in
Christ in another and in the world in another.

Interpretation: Ch. 10. Text: We believe that when God shares his goodness and
grace with some creature, he is in no way diminished, but rather is magnified,
because from the virtue of the creatures the height of God is made manifest.
And the more the creature is improved by the greater sharing of his goodness,
so much more the goodness of God and the power and the love is shown. For this
reason the goodness of God and the power and the love toward men was more
magnified from the Word of God and God coming with all his power into Jesus
than that he sent into the prophets one grace of his or two, into one prophet
a lesser, into another a greater.

Interpretation: Ch. 11. Text: We believe that Christ was crucified and died by
his own will for many and great benefits needing many words, and all according
to the human; for the Word of God is neither crucified, nor dies, nor rises,
but rather he raised the dead themselves, just as he also raised the flesh
which he wore. We believe that Christ having risen was taken up into the
heavens and will come again with glory, that he may judge the world.

Interpretation: Ch. 12. Text: We believe that the souls of men are immortal,
that the bodies of the saints will rise incorruptible, luminous, light,
needing neither food, nor drink, nor clothing, nor any other bodily pleasure,
and that the souls and the bodies of those who believe well and live well will
go away into paradise, and of the unrepentant wicked and unbelieving into
punishment. And the paradise of the saints and the enjoyment is in heaven, and
the punishment =454= of the wicked on the earth. And the enjoyment of the
saints is nothing other than when such souls will be perfected into knowledge
and then they will behold the mysteries of God, which now they do not know
except through faith of Jesus Christ.

Interpretation: Ch. 13. Text: And why was it necessary for the Word of God and
God to be incarnate? There are necessary and many reasons; and when there is
need, we are ready to give them also. But beyond those reasons these seven
inform us of the truth of our faith:

Interpretation: Ch. 14. Text: That the prophets of the Jews, whom we also
love, prophesied Jesus and as many things as he did and as many as came to be
upon him and as many as his disciples did afterward by his power. Likewise the
oracles of the Greeks also prophesied by permission of God and the astronomers
of the Persians and of the Greeks, and all these with great praise of Jesus.
And that all these prophecies are inerasable we have to show.

Interpretation: Ch. 15. Text: That all the writings of our faith agree in all
things, because those who wrote them had one teacher, the grace of God, for
otherwise they would have disagreed in some things.

Interpretation: Ch. 16. Text: That men everywhere received this faith, being
new and strange, with eagerness and with many dangers, and not only simple and
unlearned, but also the prudent and the wise. And for this reason the error of
the demons was completely dissolved.

Interpretation: Ch. 17. Text: That this faith contains nothing impossible,
nothing inconsistent, nothing bodily, but all things in spirit; and it is a
way carrying the souls of men into the love of God and of the coming eternal
life.

Interpretation: Ch. 18. Text: That as many as received this faith and lived
virtuously according to the law of Jesus received great gifts from God and
wrought many powers in the name of Jesus, which would not have been, if this
faith were contrary to the truth.

Interpretation: Ch. 19. Text: That as many things as some say against this
faith, we are able to dissolve easily and reasonably.

Interpretation: Ch. 20. Text: That the kings then and their governors in the
inhabited world warred against this faith through many punishments and murders
for three hundred eighteen years, being polytheists and idolaters, and they
prevailed nothing, but the faith conquered and remains until now; and the Lord
when he comes will find it. And if this faith were not from the will of God,
it would have been dissolved then easily. To our Lord Jesus himself glory;
amen.
