112 It is not accurate to say that a union of hypostases has taken place in
Christ, but the hypostasis of God the Word, having taken human nature,
hypostasized it in itself, according to which we also dogmatize that the Lord
Jesus Christ has one hypostasis. For if we shall hold a hypostasis, that is a
definite person, which is what hypostasis means to signify, what argument will
still check the madness of Nestorius from advocating two hypostases in Christ?
For by what reason would the two complete persons come together into one
person [συστακῆναι Y]? For if we grant this, we shall also give occasion to
those who babble of one composite nature in Christ to dogmatize this as
according to rightness.
113 SCHOLIA ARETHAE™ 9 this is a clumsy device: if it is against Nestorius, it
has reason; but if against Theodoret, it is unmixed slander and such as to
procure for the speaker envy of glory.
114 SCHOLIA ARETHAE™10 sq. to speak without respect of persons, they charge
one another faultily, saying the same thing about the same thing and insisting
that they disagree, in the manner also of those who quarrel about number if
one says eleven of so many units, and the other ten and one. 12 this is a like
fault to the madness of Arius, who insisted concerning the word homoousios
that it was not found among the ancient fathers, but was newly coined. 14 and
what of you, Theodoret? have you not heard the theologian's voice: "O the new
mixing, O the paradoxical blending"? are you not ashamed to babble on these
things?
115 SCHOLIA ARETHAE™18 of hypostases: again he badly says a union of
hypostases. 23 of blending] of blending, but a paradoxical one [cf.p. 114,
14].
116 SCHOLIA ARETHAE™1f2 he revolves about himself; he turns about in the same
things 10 O what hypostases again? 17sq. the pre-eternal God the Word,
hypostasis of the Son, coming forth from the paternal divine hypostasis, by
assumption of human nature, not in a separated individual, completed himself
as the Lord Jesus Christ, composite from a divine hypostasis, yet not also
from a human hypostasis. For he did not also take some man, but a soul and
body of human nature, and he individualized them in himself, declaring the
God-man. Since therefore he did not assume an individualized man, but human
nature, and individualized in himself one hypostasis, the God-man, according
to this we say we are cautious to say that two hypostases were composed in
Christ, shrinking from the mad doctrine of Nestorius; for the assumption of
the human nature also is one of the things that individualize according to the
Lord Jesus. Except perhaps that the two hypostases have been said of him
economically because of the wickedness of Arius and Apollinaris and the
phantasiasts after them.
117 SCHOLIA ARETHAE™ 1sq. it is shameful and sufficiently irrational to take
the likeness of the things above from the things below and to weigh the
superessential from material substances [σταθμᾶσθαι?] and to balance the
uncreated from his own creatures and to reckon the necessity in these things
to the one who brought them forth from non-being (and, to speak concisely,
that he too must by necessity have brought forth such things) and to contend
about the unknown as if they were altogether known. 3 if it is not natural
because it does not fall under necessity, how is the contrary to nature not
rather more compulsive, and so as even to be made to bow down to the unwilling
and unwilled, to be forced? 19 nature, but not hypostasis
118 SCHOLIA ARETHAE™ 8f9 he declares one to us; but if it is not one, but
different, it is possible to refute, =118.30= what is the cause of neglecting,
preening oneself in bare promises?
119 SCHOLIA ARETHAE™ 9 and he brought it in, doing well; for a natural and
irreproachable passion is not the same, through which also the timely [uoluit
ἐπίκηρον] of the nature of which it is, is refuted, compared with that which
alone belongs according to the better and forever and unchangeably for the
presentation of the nature of what is indicated. 14 but these are constitutive
of the subject and belonging forever; but the things toward a showing of
corruption are neither always co-present in act and according to the authority
of that to which they belong, being allowed to be seen as inactive. Elijah the
Tishbite and those according to him in virtue will furnish the proof. 19f20 a
coming-together of hypostases: until when, saying a union of hypostases, do
you open a door of blasphemy to Nestorius? ibid. see, even now without
distinction you bring coming-together upon conjunction, as if forgetful of the
promise to provide that the meaning of each is different. 23f24 relative ...
conjunction: and where is there a word of relative union in Theodoret?
120 SCHOLIA ARETHAE™ 2f3 you have been caught, O father, in the things for
which you blame Theodoret; for where has this man been caught dreaming against
the things now cobbled together by you? 14sq. and where is it possible to hear
Theodoret babbling these things? if nowhere, it is sure for him to say that
psalmic word: "against me all their thoughts are for evil" [Ps. 55, 6]. 123
SCHOLIA ARETHAE™ 16 sq. and what is this differing from Theodoret's exact

and ten differs from mia and ten.
124 SCHOLIA ARETHAE™ 14f15 you divide God and man together] a faulty slander.
23sq. as it seems, these things were feigned and the God-man did not bring
them forth in truth; but if in truth, the strange blame toward Theodoret is in
vain, that man also going through the same things, even if more clearly.
125 SCHOLIA ARETHAE™ 14 vulgarity: if vulgarity, I do not know; at any rate
you yourself also are carried about with the same words.
127 SCHOLIA ARETHAE™ 10 what then? is this not also the opinion of the divine
Basil [de spirs. S.. 12] and of Gregory the Theologian in the oration against
the Eunomians? 19sq. this too is slander: for where did Theodoret insist on
advocating two persons in the Lord Jesus Christ?
128 SCHOLIA ARETHAE™ 13 sq. what the difference is here from the anathema, I
am not able to understand [sic], so that the fathers set forth are caught in
vain wrangling with one another, always turning about the same things and
affirming the same things about the same things and agreeing, so that I cannot
say on what account they are set at difference with one another.
130 SCHOLIA ARETHAE™ 16 if you grant an instrumental service to the human of
the Lord, and no instrument would accomplish its own without the craftsman,
what is the blame for the one who insists on the same things as you? you will
perhaps say that inasmuch as nothing more than the apostles is given to the
Lord proceeding thus? but the argument is of one who does not know what is the
instrument always coexisting and not substitutable to the craftsman, and what
is that which fills the need through another and acquired thing. For neither
would wooden stilts taken into a dance [sic] be supposed something identical
with inborn feet.
131 SCHOLIA ARETHAE™ 2f3 and who of the pious has taken this even in thought,
where not even Theodoret has? wherefore you pursue the unattainable,
attempting to destroy things not even in a dream conjectured by the one
pursued.
132 SCHOLIA ARETHAE™ 1®7 these words do not strike according to the mark. For
the one says he declines a duality of persons, but [leg. ὁ δὲ] hears the
declining upon a union of natures, blaming that of which no distinction has
been made. 12 and this too is more overcurious and sophistic and according to
that in Job [11, 12] "but a mortal otherwise floats upon words." For where is
there a word of relative union in Theodoret, upon which to swagger? in no way
did it belong [sic Y, καθῆκέ σοι?]
135 SCHOLIA ARETHAE™ 10 not by the Lord of glory making it a work to accuse,
unless also truly reaching the words set forth. 10sq. and this too is slander;
for where and from where is it possible to show this? 15 if you blame
"according to the flesh," Peter the apostle is also blameworthy to you, who
first introduced this in the things in which, speaking to the Jews, he says
concerning David speaking about Christ: "being therefore a prophet and knowing
that God swore with an oath" [sic] "to him from the fruit of his loins" to
raise up the Christ according to the flesh [Act. 2, 30]. 18sq. to speak
without passion and as a lover of truth, these men, mastered by vainglory and
by [leg. τῶι] who might be crowned with its destructive power, did not know
nor understand that going through the same things about the same things with
one tongue [sic] they have been so bold by malice of empty glory as to show
them discordant.

140 SCHOLIA ARETHAE™ 16 then what do you say? that God the Word has not
assumed human nature, but some man? O the stunned agreement with those who
divide the one Christ into two hypostases, and how we have already set this
beside. 26 but what do you say? that Christ was called high priest not
according to his human, but according to the divine? and that his divine has
had the priestly anointing and the priesthood according to the order of
Melchizedek? O the insensible violence and of one who does not even as Cain
know, in addition to not judging rightly, not to divide rightly [Gen. 4, 7].
141 SCHOLIA ARETHAE™ 10 sq. if these are Theodoret's words, but not the divine
Paul's, your charges are well; but if they are the divine Paul's, leaving off
stretching the blames toward the begetter of the words [τὸν Y] you have chosen
to insult Theodoret. And since you cannot refute Paul (for you know, I think,
how that man thinking these things puts them forth, as indeed Theodoret also),
have you then neglected [ὀλιγώρησας Y] the interpretation upon these sayings
so as to attach to Theodoret the reproaches equal [legas τῶι ἴσα] to those who
take these things crookedly? O the unjust opinion. 15 accusing Paul of these
things, whenever you willingly go blind not to understand them as that man has
spoken, you will be a great bulwark and champion of those who from the crooked
thoughts about them have slipped into destructive heresies.
142 SCHOLIA ARETHAE™ 3 sq. we both believe the union is true and more strongly
than you; for we do not say this from two hypostases, as you are often caught
thinking, but from two natures, divinity and humanity, but one hypostasis of
the divine Word, who having taken human nature, that is soul and body,
individualized [sic] them in himself, according to which also the hypostasis
of the Lord is advocated as composite by the church of the faithful. And two
natures of his unemptied [sic], with which two natures he worked the divine
and the human things, yet not in parts because of the extreme union; but how,
it is reserved to him alone to know exactly. But if you, assigning this
exactness to yourself, hunt to be someone, there is no envy for you provided
you refrain from blasphemously attacking those who simply pursue the simple
and guileless mystery, and yet, to speak as a lover of truth, you yourself
are first held by these [legas ἐκείνοις] with which you strike others
[βαλλεῖν Y] [τούτους Y].
143 SCHOLIA ARETHAE™ 1 sq. this is slander; for if not this, John the
Theologian also is found held by the same charge through the things in which
he says also "the Word became flesh" [Ioh. i, 14]. 9 thus I think the things
set forth of the discourse would be rightly brought forward: those who have
slipped from the rightness and truth of the thoughts. For as it now stands, it
