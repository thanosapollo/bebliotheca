On the Ascension of our Lord Jesus Christ. Sermon 2.
=52.793=
Blessed be God. It is timely today for us all to cry out that prophetic
saying, and together to hymn the Master of us all, and to say: Who shall speak
the mighty deeds of the Lord, shall make all his praises to be heard? For
today our firstfruits has gone up into heaven, and he who took up the flesh
from us has occupied the Father's throne, that he may work reconciliation
toward the servants, and do away with the ancient enmity, and grant the peace
of the powers above to men upon the earth. For common to us today are the
trophies against the devil, common the prizes, common the awards, common also
the crowns, common also the glory. Therefore let us all leap for joy, seeing
our firstfruits seated above, and our nature taking the throne at the right
hand of God. For if the wondrous prophet blesses those who have seed from
Zion, and kinsmen in Jerusalem, much more must we rejoice and exult, seeing
our firstfruits gone up to the very summit of heaven, and having laid hold of
the royal throne.
For consider, beloved, how great the goodness of our God has been, and what an
unspeakable economy has taken place concerning our race, which had fallen from
paradise through the deceit of the devil, and been condemned to so great a
curse: to how great a height he has all at once raised it, and how we who had
shown ourselves unworthy of the earth have today been led up into heaven, and
our nature which formerly was thought unworthy even of paradise. For this has
taken the first seat of heaven, and that which had become the plaything of the
demons is today worshipped by angels and the powers above. O blessed envy! O
good plotting! O envy of good things, procurer =52.794= of countless goods for
us, you have given birth to an ordinance of immortality. For that wicked
demon, having envied our nature while it had its sojourn upon earth, now sees
it worshipped in heaven; and having stolen away, as he thought, things great
and incomparable, he now sees us enjoying greater and more splendid things.
Therefore I leap and exult today, and I exhort you to join me in the dance.
For we who were not even worthy of the rule below have gone up to the kingdom
that is highest of all, we have passed beyond the heavens, we have laid hold
of the royal throne, and the nature for whose sake the Cherubim were guarding
paradise is borne aloft and worshipped. Be astonished, then, beloved, at the
resourcefulness of your Master, and glorify him who has granted you so many
things.
For the munificence of the gift has surpassed the magnitude of the loss. For
see: we had fallen from paradise, and we have been led up into heaven itself;
we were condemned to death, and immortality has been granted us; we had given
offense and been cast away, and he deigned to call us sons, and not sons only,
but also heirs, and not heirs only, but also fellow-heirs with Christ. See how
the things granted us by the Lord are greater than those that were taken away.
But when you hear that he went up and was taken up, do not suppose a local
descent in the case of God; for the Godhead fills all things, and is present
everywhere, and is nowhere left behind, as overseer of all and maker of all.
But the body of like race with us, which having assumed he deigned to appear
upon the earth, like us in all things, this is our body being taken up. And
for this reason let us all together today cry out that prophetic saying: God
has gone up with a shout, the Lord with the voice of a trumpet, =52.795= and
let us keep this conspicuous and splendid festival, since we have all together
also enjoyed the gift from him.
And just as a kind master, having loved his own household servant, because of
his affection toward him does not refuse to put on his garment; so also our
Master Christ, having loved our nature, and having seen it brought down into a
depth of wickedness, having no hope of salvation, and needing much
condescension, deigned to assume our body. And for this reason, having
appeared to the world, he freed all men from error, and brought them back to
the truth; and he cries out in the Gospel, saying: I came not to call the
righteous, but sinners, to repentance. What he wished, then, he accomplished,
and he raised up human nature lying somewhere below, and he displayed such
loving-kindness that human reasoning cannot contain the account of what has
taken place.
For how, tell me, have we who had given offense, who had shown ourselves
unworthy of the earth, and had fallen from the rule below, been led up all at
once to so great a height? How was the war dissolved? How was the wrath taken
away? For this is the wondrous thing and full of much astonishment: that we
did not run to him; HE who was justly angry toward us appealed for us, and
thus the peace came to be. And Paul cries out, saying: We are ambassadors on
behalf of Christ, as though God were appealing through us. Did you see
resourceful wisdom? Did you see unspeakable loving-kindness of the Master? Did
you see exceeding care? Now the sting of death has been dissolved; now the
victory of Hades has been torn apart; now the dominion of death has been
abolished; now the enmity has been dissolved; now the long-standing war has
been quenched; then men were made like angels, men were mingled with the
angels, and a great bond of the shared name has been established on both
sides. For the common Master went up, having reconciled his own Father to the
race of men. And that he went up for this reason, hear him saying: It is
expedient for you that I go away; for if I do not go away, the Comforter will
not come to you. And as a sign of the reconciliation he promises to give us
the coming of the Comforter. For see also how exactly he added what follows:
And he, he says, will guide you into all the truth; that is, us who were
wandering and being carried this way and that, and the Holy Spirit having been
sent down will guide us all to the truth. For since he was angry, and we even
so were turning away from the loving Master, our Master Christ, throwing
himself into the midst, worked the reconciliation. For he truly loosed the
middle wall of the partition. Therefore we have good hopes henceforth, looking
toward the Lord of our firstfruits.
For whatever you may say hereafter, I no longer fear, even if you speak of a
worm, even if dust, even if fire, even if punishment, even if gnashing of
teeth, even if ashes, even if you say anything at all to me, all things are
easy for me to despise when I look upon my firstfruits seated above, and all
but conversing with all men and crying out: Depart from sloth henceforth, and
recognize your own nobility, having considered the measure of the gifts, of
which you are shortly going to receive the participation. For dwelling with
them he charged them not to depart from Jerusalem, but to wait for the promise
of the Father. See in this also the care of the Master; for having seen the
weakness of human nature, and that it needs much allied help, he granted the
help of the Holy Spirit, that man might become unconquerable against the
devices of the devil. And the same thing has come to pass as if some excellent
general, seeing the soldiers arrayed under him not bearing the threats of the
adversaries, but struck with panic by the contest, should strengthen them all,
fencing them round on every side with breastplate and shield and helmet, and
the rest of the full armor; =52.796= so also our Lord Jesus Christ, having
seen that the dominion of the opposing powers was great, and that human nature
therefore needed much help, granted the grace of the Holy Spirit, that he who
formerly feared the phalanxes of the demons might now appear fearful to them,
and strike them with terror, and merely by being seen drive away the deceitful
serpent. For such is the grace of the Holy Spirit; for wherever it may appear,
it makes the phalanxes of the demons take to flight, and all the opposing
powers.
For you shall receive, he says, power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you,
and you shall be my witnesses both in Jerusalem, and in all Judea, and
Samaria, and unto the end of the earth. And what was this power? That which he
was saying to them also formerly: Heal the sick, cleanse lepers, cast out
demons, and as many such things; for there will be no disease, he says, so
strong that it will not yield to the power of the Holy Spirit. This present
day has become the cause of so many good things for us. For today angels have
received what they long desired of old, seeing our nature flashing from the
royal throne. And that you may learn the difference between servant and
master, when Elijah was being taken up, he underwent this in a fiery chariot;
but when our Master with flesh, a cloud ministered to the ascent. For as they
were looking, it says, he was lifted up, and a cloud received him from their
eyes. And that one was going up as into heaven, for he was a servant; but this
one into heaven itself, for he was Master. Behold two men in white clothing,
and they say to them: This Jesus who was taken up from you into heaven, this
one shall come in the manner you beheld him.
And when the servant had to be summoned, a chariot was sent down; but when the
Son, a royal throne, and not simply a throne, but the Father's own. For hear
Isaiah the loud-voiced saying concerning the Father: Behold, the Lord sits
upon a light cloud. For this reason he sent down the cloud also to the Son.
And Elijah, having gone up, left a mantle upon Elisha, but Jesus, having gone
up, left gifts upon the disciples, making not one prophet, but countless
Elishas, or rather far greater and more splendid than that one. Considering
all these things, beloved, let us too repay our Master through the excellent
way of life, that as he glorified us through his own gifts, so we too through
right deeds and the excellent way of life may make God gracious. Let us
therefore glorify our Master, and hold the pride of present things in
contempt. For this is the most excellent teaching, able to lead the
unbelieving by the hand to the truth. For they do not attend so much to what
is said by us as to what is done by us. For this reason Christ also was
saying: Blessed is he who has done and taught; showing that the teaching
through works is more exact than that through words, and more able to lead in
the ungrateful. Let us therefore become well-pleasing to God, and with
exactness order our own life, that we may both draw to ourselves the grace of
the Spirit, and have more ease toward the achievement of virtue, being helped
by the power of the Spirit.
For wherever grace sees a sober soul, there it alights with abundance. Let us
therefore prepare ourselves, I exhort you, that being empowered by the
operation of the Spirit we may both complete the present life without grief,
and attain the goods to come, and be deemed worthy of the kingdom of heaven;
of which may it be that we all attain by the grace and loving-kindness of him
who redeemed us with the precious blood of his life-giving side, and delivered
us from error and from slavery, Jesus Christ our Lord; with whom befits honor
and might, all glory and magnificence to the Father and to the holy and
life-giving Spirit, now and always, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
