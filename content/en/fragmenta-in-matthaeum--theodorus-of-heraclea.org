* Fragments on Matthew

Mt 1:1 The blessed Matthew, straightway and from the prologues, shows that he
had the whole aim, that Christ appeared according to the prophetic voices,
rendering the unlying to the promises made of old, and leaving not even one
pretext of unreasonableness to those disbelieving: "The book of the origin of
Jesus Christ, son of David, son of Abraham," i.e. "him the voices that came
before were proclaiming, him the promises made to the righteous were
breathing forth." Mt 1:1 Superfluous it is to seek this: for what difference
does it make to say "the first" or "the other"? For through either he was
shown to be a descendant of both likewise. But the evangelist does not say
even thus; for he did not say the Christ son of David and son of Abraham, but
the Christ of David, and of Abraham the David, signifying the same thing both
ways, but not saying this before in the ready use of the phrase; for by "son"
clearly he says not the one born, but the descendant. But one might say to
him: Matthew did not wish to trace up the lineage from David unto Abraham;
wherefore indeed, if he wished this, did he not genealogize him according to
Luke? But he says this peculiarly for the causes we mentioned. It was among
these twelve to make mention of the things through which the lineage happened
to be led down unto Christ. Mt 1:16 Since therefore he seemed to show
something new by the change of phrase in saying "Jacob begat Joseph the
husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus, the one called Christ," he promised
to set forth for us also the manner in which he was born, and says: "Now the
origin of Jesus Christ was thus," i.e. not as the rest was he begotten, but
otherwise. Mt 1 It is possible to perceive the power of God; for "spirit"
also he is called. Mt 2:7 Herod secretly desired to inquire of the magi,
fearing lest somehow the Jews save him as their own king. Mt 2:23 Others say
these things disagree with the things lying in Luke: for if from Bethlehem
according to Luke he came up unto Jerusalem and from there unto Nazareth, how
according to Matthew, from Bethlehem, Joseph, instructed in a dream, departed
into Egypt? For Luke, narrating the season of the birth of our savior,
makes mention of the kingdom of "Augustus" and of the "enrollment" under him,
and of the things coming to pass at the birth of the savior, but also "when,"
he says, "the days of his circumcising were fulfilled, they carried up" the
child "unto Jerusalem," and, having completed upon him the things ordained by
law, they go away into "Nazareth." But Matthew makes mention not at all of
the things with Luke, but sets down the things passed over in silence by him,
I mean the things concerning the magi, who departed perhaps from their own
land at the very birth of Jesus, but had not yet completed so long a journey
in a brief season. How great then was this interval of time between the
appearing star and the arrival of the magi, the evangelist himself taught,
saying "from two years old" and "according to the time which he had carefully
learned of the magi"; therefore a time of two years had already passed from
the birth of the Lord unto the arrival of the aforesaid. Therefore the things
with the holy evangelists do not disagree, if Luke on the eighth day from the
birth brings him up together with his parents unto Jerusalem, and from there
leads him away unto Nazareth, but Matthew records him as again being in
Bethlehem after two years' time, and thence to have gone into Egypt because
of the plot of the king. And it was likely that they visited the place not
only a second time, but very often, for the sake of the memory of the
strange thing. That however there was not one time with both, it is possible
to reason out also otherwise. Luke says they were not able to find lodging in
Bethlehem because of the multitude of those lodging there under pretext of
the enrollment, but Matthew, that "coming into the house they saw the child
with Mary his mother, and falling down they worshiped him." But they do not
take the child lying in a "manger," like the shepherds, but within in "the
house with his mother" they behold him; for there, because there was "no
place in the lodging," he is set in a "manger," but here, after two years'
time, he is found in a "house" by the magi "with his mother," leisure being
in Bethlehem, "and falling down they worshiped him." Mt 3:2 The kingdom of
the heavens is the presence of Christ; for this grants to us the partaking of
the Father and the ascent into the heavens and the immutable toward the good
for the saints in the age to come. Mt 3:5 Josephus, in the fifteenth book of
the Jewish history, bears witness to John the baptist, who promised a
purification to those being baptized. Mt 3:7-9 Neither boasting in
forefathers: neglect not the virtue of the soul. Mt 4:12 It seems John,
having fulfilled in Jesus the ministry of the baptism, which ended in him,
thereafter was handed over and to be shut up in prison; for after baptizing
Jesus we find nothing recorded concerning John, or that, after the
temptation by the devil, straightway and together with the baptism, having
been led out by the Spirit into the wilderness, as Mark wrote, Jesus heard
that John was delivered up and withdrew into Galilee. But Luke also joins the
things concerning John's being shut up by Herod to the narrative of John's
baptizing. It is clear however that also according to the evangelist John
this was for him the season of being shut up in prison; for there is said by
him: "Now John also was baptizing in Aenon near to Salim; for John was not
yet cast into prison." According to the four evangelists then neither before
Jesus was baptized did any impediment meet John baptizing, nor after these
things did he still baptize, but it was necessary that the things of John
conclude at the beginning of the gospel being administered by Jesus. Perhaps
then the power guarding John, until in Jesus the economy of the baptism
should be brought to completion, left him over both to the temptation in
prison and to the beheading. Mt 5:5 This beatitude some of the copies
contain third, but the one set next after these second. Mt 5:6 Another says
that of such a desire it promises him the enjoyment of the things to come as
reward. Mt 5:12 From here there is afforded to them the consolation from the
communion of those who suffered evils before them, so that from those too it
is fitting for us to know that it is not possible for those reaching toward
virtue to be established outside of the grievous things. Mt 5:16 He does not
say: "Let your light shine before God"; for the light of lamps does not shine
before the sun. Mt 5:15-16 A light is the virtuous citizenship. One must not
speak the evangelic word in secret, but with boldness of speech, in order
that the men upon earth may be enlightened. Mt 5:22 "Raka" he calls the one
spat upon; for so also among the Syrians the spittle is called. Mt 5:35 The
earth a footstool, because under the authority, even if not now receptive of
his holiness because of the sins. But Jerusalem is a city of the great king.
This however he says toward the supposition of the Jews. Mt 5:40 He said not
to the one wishing to take from you the cloak: give also the other garment,
but to the one wishing you to be judged; showing that it is not fitting to
flee the contentiousness toward the adversaries, whenever on account of
piety they bring this upon us, and to endure gladly both sufferings and loss,
so as not to overlook piety. This however must be guarded, that, when we
choose to look toward virtue. Mt 6:11 As man is nourished unto life, so some
say the "epiousion" is beside "to come on" and "to come upon," in order that
the thing said may be such: the bread not proper to the present age, but of
the coming one and after these things to be rendered by you to the worthy to
receive: give us this day already, and "daily." Mt 6:22-23 Some said that
"The lamp of the body is the eye" was said concerning the teacher of the
church, but they seem to me not to have examined the whole sequence of the
things said from the beginning. For he began "Lay not up treasures," then,
inserting "The lamp of the body is the eye" and "No one can serve two
masters," he added: "You cannot serve God and mammon," showing throughout
that the aim was for him to turn away those hearing from the zeal for the
things present. Mt 7:21 For he enjoys the kingdom of the heavens whoever
shows in the works the affinity, both teaching and doing these things which
he himself, being present, handed to the men. Mt 8:11-12 Just as then, for
comparison with those perishing, few were those being saved, so understand
with me those being saved to be many for comparison with the few perishing
now. Mt 9:12-13 For if upon the bodies we do not loathe those suffering, how
much rather is it fitting to do all things for those ill disposed in their
souls, in order that they may be able to have these in the best state. "It
was necessary for you," he says, "to learn from the divine writing that the
pity for the neighbor is preferable to every sacrifice." Whom is it just to
pity if not these, who, not having their souls in the best state, in order
that they may have them better, need correction; so that for me too, he says,
the aim of the presence was not that, being righteous, I should benefit men
(for nor would the righteous, being such, need any such thing), but that
lying ill with many sins they shall be translated by his own grace toward the
better; for they have no need, he says, "of a physician, but they that are
ill." Mt 9:37-38 "See," he says, "how great is the progress of those
believing, and how there are not those who would teach them the fitting
things, so that I am compelled from this, those following after me, to
endure so much toil." Mt 9:37-38 But he calls them harvesters, showing that
also the things that came to be through the prophets came to be through his
own power and supplying, and that of prophets and apostles he is Lord and of
all men as maker, and upon his own seed, not upon another's, he sends and
dispatches the disciples. Mt 10:8-10 "Freely you received, freely give."
"Those devouring the houses of the widows," the Pharisees, he turns away from
imitating, introducing a new manner of teaching, the pure and
unbought, and showing in the very deed the imitation toward him; wherefore he
commands those receiving freely from him the gifts to share them freely also.
"Neither gold nor silver nor brass," for the sake of the need on the way, he
says, "shrink from taking," but I wish you not even to have a purse, so as to
show to all from your very manner of life how far you have stood aloof from
desire of money. Mt 10:34 Having armed them sufficiently against the
revilings, but also having trained them to bear the sufferings nobly, since
the condition of kinship was left behind, being able to soften resolve
greatly (for who would easily choose to be on hostile terms with those most
akin?), he says well "Think not"; for so greatly do I wish you to stand aloof
from respecting the bodily kinship, that it is fitting for you to know rather
this: that my preaching will make a great division of your kindred; for
those who have once chosen to see the things toward piety, I wish every
kinship, even the nearest, to be placed second. Mt 12:30 "How is it
possible," he says, "for me to be gathered together and the demons so
plainly, I on the one hand working the things contrary to those, and those on
the other practicing to do all things to the contrary? For I and those with
me attempt to lead up all men toward God, and this the collection of my own
wishes for me, but they have striven to drag away all." Mt 12:33 Since
Beelzeboul is wicked, but the things coming to be from me hold of goodness
beyond measure, the things from you are discordant; for neither can the
wicked do any good thing nor the good wicked, just as neither do trees bear
fruits unlike themselves. Mt 12:38 As Moses had persuaded them through
wonders and so given the law, they wish him also to do some such thing, in
order that, as pretending, they shall change their opinion, if he should do
this. Mt 13:24-30 After the progress of the Christianity, and when it begins
already to appear throughout all the world, heresies will be pointed out of
some perverting the truth and bearing the name of Christians and by this
baiting the many; for such also is the tares, coming to be from the wheat by
corruption and bearing something of the truth of the wheat according to the
appearance. "This will grieve," he says, "also the invisible powers in their
goodwill toward me, to have wished and to make away with the finders or
those persuaded by them; but I will bring this to pass through those
strangers to the faith, lest while these suffer nothing, they supposed the
adversaries that Christians are punished by the demons for the daring deed
and that they teach all to depart from the idols, and so become wholly
estranged from piety, so that hereafter the gathering of the many fleeing to
approach the Christianity, through ignorance of the things coming to be,
diminishes for them of the pious. What more, when the servants, coming near,
are at a loss, does he answer them? And to the one he tells the condition,
and to the other the cause of the permission; for also when he shapes
discussions upon the parables, to the parables he gives what is likely, in
order that they may also seem complete, coming to be in the order of a
narrative. It is fitting for us to examine the aim which he wishes to
signify through the parable." Mt 21:12 More severe the deed, not coming to be
in childishness of speech, when he also cast out the sellers and overturned
the tables; and yet according to the prophetic he did nothing violent,
according to "He shall not strive nor cry aloud, neither shall his voice be
heard." And John said he "made a scourge of cords"; and this too is a sign of
rebuke and of severity. In symbols therefore this too must be counted; for he
did not strike anyone with a scourge, but it was a sign of the scourge
brought upon those who dye the things of God with marketable things. Mt 22:16
Herodians, they say, are either the soldiers of Herod or the exactors of the
taxes imposed upon the Jews by the Romans. Mt 23:25 For much fairer than the
water does the righteousness cleanse the vessel, as also it had upon the men
themselves. Pure in the bodies were the righteous beside those cleansed with
waters, as David says: "I will wash my hands in innocency"; so that
accordant to the things of the law are also the things of the Lord. Mt
25:14-18 A sojourn abroad he calls the season from the ascension into the
heavens until his presence, in the midst, in which he seems to be away from
us. But the "straightway" shows the unlifted-up of the zeal, how it is
necessary to work the things of God. But the five and the two talents and
the one, the difference of the ministry, as if to say of bishop, of
presbyter, of deacon; for by the quantity of the talent the more or the less
of the ministry it shows. Whence well he said "to each according to his own
ability," in order that he may say: wherefore it happened to each,
correspondingly to the things entrusted, to be able to fulfill the ministry;
for he wishes to show that both the readiness he requires from all and is
repaid likewise. Mt 25:20-25 The doubling of the things given he calls to
show forth with zeal the fitting things and to become for others a cause of
profits, for which he says he shall obtain great rewards rightfully; for this
is the "over many things I will set you." But the "to hide" he said, to show
forth nothing of the things due, but to work hidden the grace given to him.
Mt 26:29 The kingdom of the Father he named the resurrection, from which both
to him the kingdom was drawing near and to the rest of men the communion of
it. But what is "new," i.e. "newly," and incorruptible and not needing
nourishment. Mt 26:37 Not even that must one marvel, how the Lord, agonizing,
appears according to the human, and yet the disciples rejoicing at the things
they suffered often. Just as, being able to rescue himself from the death to
suffer, he consented for the salvation of the world, so also according to the
human nature he consented to agonize, persuading them not to suppose the
passion some pretense, but truth, and especially, the short of the
resurrection being able easily to lead them down unto this, the suspicion.
