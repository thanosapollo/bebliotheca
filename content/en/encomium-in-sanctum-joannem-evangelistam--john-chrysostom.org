* Encomium on Saint John the Evangelist
:PROPERTIES:

:END:

Encomium of our father among the saints John Chrysostom on Saint John the
Evangelist.
Again the great and only theologian John has sojourned among us from heaven;
again he proclaims God the Word and prepares the whole inhabited world to look
up toward the heavens. John has sojourned among us today from the heavens, the
fisherman who threw aside the net and took up the Gospel, John, who left the
reed and took the Word in hand, John, who taught the inhabited world doctrines
higher than those of Moses. For the one said: In the beginning God made the
heaven and the earth; but this one, leaving the creatures behind, ascends to
the Creator. In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the
Word was God, the Word in hypostasis, the Word manifested in later times and
saving the inhabited world, the Word unchanging from the essence of the
Father.
For just as the word in me, together with the essence, belongs to that of the
soul, and after being uttered through the mouth and taking voice as a garment
is no longer hidden in me alone but is made known to all, so also the
Only-begotten, being in God and being of the essence of the Father, remained
known in the beginning, but after putting on the flesh he thus becomes
manifest to all. For just as the word, having put on the voice, is made known
to all, so God the Word, having put on the flesh, was revealed to all men
through the flesh. We do not take these images on account of the nature of
God; for that nature is untraceable and inconceivable and incomprehensible and
inscrutable; for if the judgments of God are unsearchable, and his ways
untraceable, how does anyone dare to search out that incomprehensible nature?
But we take human images because of our weakness, if we might be able even in
part to understand the image of the heavenly wonders. For this the apostle
also made known to us when he said that we know in part and we prophesy in
part. Therefore from earthly and human things we strive to receive a partial
knowledge of what is above. But just as it is not possible to cut the word
from the soul, but it is of the same essence, remaining in the soul and
conceived together with it without interval and not being cut, so also, being
of the same essence and of the same Godhead of the Father, having come forth
from the paternal treasures, he came down clothed in flesh and granting to all
the wonders of the gift.
This treasure John uncovered for us today, he who was more voiceless than the
fishes and who sewed shut the mouths of orators and philosophers. In the
beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.
Nowhere the "was not," but everywhere the "was"; nowhere the "he made," but
everywhere the "was"; after the "was," then the "he made," not upon the
Creator but upon the creatures. And that you may learn how the "was" stands
and how the "he made," I will speak, you count. In the beginning was the Word,
and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. Nowhere the "was not," but
everywhere the "was." This one was in the beginning with God. See, a fourth
time he mentioned the Word, and not even once did he say "came to be." What
then after these things? All things came to be through him. He did not say
"was" here. Why? Because the discourse was about the creatures. All things
came to be through him. "Came to be": and he did not say the "was" but the
"came to be." All things came to be through him, and without him not even one
thing came to be. Again the "came to be." Where it is about God, the "was";
where about the creatures, the "came to be." In the beginning was the Word.
From this the Father also was made known according to the Old Testament.
For when Moses said to him: If the sons of Israel say to me, Who is the one
who sent you to us, what shall I say to them? And God said: I am the One who
is; the One who is has sent me to you: the Father the One who is, the Son was
in the beginning. But if someone contends, saying that it is not the same in
the case of the Father and of Christ, for in the case of the Father it is "I
am the One who is," but in the case of the Son "in the beginning he was," and
"who is" and "was" are not the same, I show that the Only-begotten also has
the same title. Hear the evangelist saying: the only-begotten Son, who is in
the bosom of the Father. See, the Gospel has one voice for "the One who is."
And again the apostle: whose are the fathers, and from whom is Christ
according to the flesh, who is God over all, blessed. Did you see how John
makes you secure, having set the "was" upon God and the "came to be" upon the
creatures? For neither does "came to be" befit God, nor "was" the creation. In
the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God;
this one was in the beginning with God. All things came to be through him, and
without him not even one thing came to be. When the "came to be" descends upon
the creatures, when the "was" ascends to the Creator. And again, life was:
again he ascends to the Creator. Today he brings a ladder filled with many
mysteries. When he ascends toward God, he proclaims the "was"; when he
descends toward the creatures, he takes the "came to be." When he ascends
toward God, he lays aside the "came to be" and takes the "was." Just as one
coming to a king enters with the robe in which it is fitting to see a king,
but if he enters to a private man he enters with private clothes, so also the
evangelist, when he ascends toward God, ascends with that diction that befits
God: In the beginning was the Word; when he descends toward creation he
descends with the slavish diction: All things came to be through him, and
without him not even one thing came to be that has come to be; in him was
life, and the life was the light of men; and again: He was the true light,
which lights every man coming into the world.
Where the Word was, where life was, where the light was, where the creatures
came to be. There came to be a man sent from God, whose name was John. But
some of the heretics dare to say: There was once when the Father was, when the
Son was not. Tell me, how great is the interval between them? Time was the
interval. But the Son made both seasons and the times and the ages. All things
came to be through him. Hear Paul saying: In many ways and in many portions
God, having spoken of old to our fathers, in the last of the days spoke to us
in Christ Jesus, whom he appointed heir of all things, through whom he also
made the ages. How then an age or a time before the one who made the ages?
Hear then also a prophet saying: He who exists before the ages. The apostle
says that through him he made the ages; the evangelist: all things came to be
through him; the prophet: he who exists before the ages; and again the
apostle: whether principalities, or authorities, or thrones, or lordships; all
things have been created through him and unto him, and he is before all
things. And you dare to say that the Father was when the Son was not? But they
have to give account of this daring on the day of judgment. For on the day of
judgment he calls both John and those who say the "was" and those who say the
"was not." Why? Because the Father judges no one, but has given all judgment
to the Son, that all may honor the Son as they honor the Father. He calls then
on the day of judgment both those who say the "was" and the "was not." Each
one's written bond comes forth. You, what did you say? Read the confession. I
said the "was." This one, what? This one said the "was not."
Who taught you to say the "was not"? John, did you teach him to say the "was
not"? I did not, Master. But what? In the beginning was the Word, and the Word
was with God, and the Word was God; and this one was in the beginning;
everywhere "was," and nowhere the "was not." From human reasonings. What else
did you confess? I, he says, that he is equal. That one, what? That he is
unlike. And you, where did you learn that he is equal? Who taught you? The
evangelist, saying: For this reason, he says, they persecuted the Savior,
because he not only broke the Sabbath but also called God his own Father,
making himself like God. This the apostle also taught. Saying what? He did not
count it robbery to be equal with God, but emptied himself. From where then
did that one learn the "unlike"? From human reasonings. And you again, what
did you confess? I, that the Son works even as the Father. Who taught you?
The evangelist. Saying what? As the Father raises the dead and gives life, so
also the Son gives life to whom he wills. What then did you confess again?
That he is like the Father in all things. Who said it? The evangelist: He who
has seen me has seen the Father. That one, what did he say? That the Son is a
creature. Who taught you? No one; but from human reasonings. See these
blasphemies. The heretics dare, contrary to this, to baptize outside the
command of Christ: Go, make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them in
the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit. Let not heretics
dare to baptize in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the unlike,
but their baptism becomes lame. But we, having one confession, receive one
baptism of the Holy Trinity, hallowed without division.
This foundation of the faith, then, I exhort you all to hold firmly, and
nowhere in the case of the Only-begotten to take either the "came to be" or
the "was not"; for these things are below the ineffable and unutterable
essence of the Only-begotten. Why? Because in the beginning he was, and of
this beginning nothing is prior. For however far you may go up,
however far you may be shot beyond, however far you may stretch, you find
him beyond that beginning, and not beyond the beginning. In the beginning
was the Word; and do not go up beyond it, for you cannot. But consider blessed
John, how, leaving bodily fishing, through these words he netted the whole
inhabited world unto salvation. In the beginning was the Word. How many
tyrants strove to destroy this voice and could not? A wind came, it blew, the
waves dashed against the building, that is, against the faith, and it did not
fall; for it was a tower built upon the rock, struck from every side and not
yet falling.
Upon this rock I will build my church, and the gates of Hades shall not
prevail against it. Kings rose up, tyrants rose up, wars came to be against
Christians; tyrants and fighters against God often burst their tyranny against
the Christians, but they could not blot out one hair of the Gospel. In the
beginning was the Word: not passing away along with the age; a Word conquering
all, teaching all, and stretching out together with the age, making the earth
heaven and offering many sons to the Father according to grace, to whom
belongs glory, honor, might, majesty, and magnificence, together with the
unoriginate Father and his life-giving Spirit, now and always and unto the
ages of ages. Amen.
