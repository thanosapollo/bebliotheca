* Against the Anomoeans
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Homily spoken in Constantinople to the Anomoeans concerning the
incomprehensible, and that the New is in accord with the Old, and to those who
are left behind from the divine assemblies.

Discourse eleventh.

=48.795=

αʹ. I discoursed to you one day, and from that day I so loved you, as having
been reared with you from the beginning and from the first, I was so bound to
you with the bonds of love, as having enjoyed your sweetest company for an
unspeakable time. And this happened, not because I am some friendly and
affectionate man, but because you are longed-for and most lovable of all. For
who would not admire and wonder at your kindled zeal, your unhypocritical
love, =48.796= your goodwill toward the teachers, your concord toward one
another, all of which are sufficient even to draw a soul of stone? For this
reason we also love you no less than that Church in which we were both born
and reared and educated; for this is sister of that one, and through the works
you have shown the kinship. And if that one is older according to time, yet
this one is warmer according to the faith; greater is the assembly there, and
more splendid the theater, but greater here is the endurance, and greater the
display of courage. Wolves from every side encircle the sheep, and the flock
is not =48.797= consumed; storm and winter and surge stand around this sacred
ship continually, and those who sail in it are not drowned; the insolence of
heretical flame circling from every side, and those in the midst of the
furnace enjoy spiritual dew. It is likewise paradoxical to see this Church
planted in this part of the city, just as if one should see in the midst of a
furnace an olive tree flourishing, and thick with leaves, and heavy with
fruit.

Since therefore you are so well-disposed and worthy of countless goods, come,
let us with all grace pay down the promise which we lately promised you, when
we were philosophizing among you concerning the weapons of David and of
Goliath, showing by the word that the one was fenced from every side with some
great and much panoply, while the other, naked of those weapons, was walled by
faith; and the one shone from the breastplate and the shield from without, but
the other from within from the Spirit and the grace. For this reason then the
boy overcame the youth, the naked prevailed over the armed, the shepherd threw
down the soldier; the nature of a shepherd's stone crushed and destroyed the
nature of warlike bronze. Therefore let us also take in hand that stone, I
mean the cornerstone, the intelligible one. For if it is permitted to Paul to
philosophize concerning the rock in the desert, surely no one will be
indignant at us either, taking this stone in the same manner. For just as in
the case of the Jews it was not the nature of the seen rock, but the power of
the thought one, that sent forth those rivers of waters; so also in the case
of David it was not the sensible, but the intelligible stone that brought down
that barbarian head; thus we also then promised you to say nothing from
reasonings. For our weapons are not fleshly, but spiritual, destroying
reasonings and every height lifting itself up against the knowledge of God. We
are commanded then to destroy them, not to lift them up; we were ordered to
dissolve them, not to arm ourselves with them; For the reasonings of mortals
are cowardly, he says. What is cowardly? The coward, even if he walks on a
secure place, does not yet have courage, but fears and trembles; so also that
which has been accepted by reasonings, even if it be true, does not yet
furnish the soul with full assurance and sufficient faith.

Since therefore so great is the weakness of reasonings, come, from the
Scriptures let us take hold of the battle against the adversaries. From where
then must we begin the word? From wherever you wish, whether from the New, or
from the Old; for not only in the evangelical and the apostolic words, but
also in the prophetic and in all the Old, it is possible to see the glory of
the Only-begotten shining with much abundance; wherefore it seems good to me
from here to skirmish against them. For not only making these words from here,
but also many other heretics we shall be able to throw down, Marcion,
Manichaeus, Valentinus, all the companies of the Jews. For just as in the case
of David Goliath fell, but the whole camp fled, and the death was of one body,
and the blow of one head, but the flight and the cowardice were common to the
whole camp; so also now with us, one heresy having been struck and having
fallen, there will be a common flight of all those enumerated. =48.798= For
the Manichaeans and those who are sick with the same things as they seem to
receive the Christ who is proclaimed, but they dishonor the prophets and
patriarchs who proclaim him; but the Jews again on the contrary seem to
receive and serve those who proclaim him, I mean the prophets and their
lawgiver, but they dishonor the one proclaimed by them. Whenever therefore by
the grace of God we show the glory of the Only-begotten much proclaimed
beforehand in the Old, we shall be able to put to shame all these God-fighting
mouths, and to muzzle the blasphemous tongues. For when the Old Testament
appears proclaiming Christ, what defense will there be for the Manichaeans and
those like them who dishonor that which proclaims beforehand the common Master
of all? and what pardon and excuse for the Jews who do not receive the one
announced by the prophets?

βʹ. Since therefore so great is the abundance of the victory, let us lead the
word to the more ancient books, and of the ancient themselves the most ancient
of all, I mean to Genesis, and of Genesis itself let us go up to the summit.
For that Moses says many things concerning Christ? hear Christ himself saying:
If you believed Moses, you would believe me; for concerning me that one wrote.
And where did that one write concerning him? This I shall already try to show.
For when the whole creation had been completed, and the heaven was crowned
with a varied choir of stars, and from below the earth flashed back to it with
flowers of every kind, and the peaks of mountains were full, and plains and
ravines too were full, and simply the whole surface of the earth was of plants
and trees and herbs, and flocks were skipping, and herds were skipping, and
the choir of singing birds, displaying its own nature, filled all the air with
music, and the seas were full of marine animals, and lakes and springs and
rivers were full of all things born in them, and nothing was unfinished, but
all things had been completed, the body was seeking the head, the city the
ruler, the creation the king, I mean the man. Being about then to form him,
God says: Let us make man according to our image and likeness. To whom does he
converse? It is quite clear that it is to his own Only-begotten. And he did
not say, Make, lest you suppose the thing said to be a slavish command, but,
Let us make, so that through the figure of counsel of the words he might
uncover the equal honor. For sometimes God is said to have a counselor, and
sometimes not to have one, not because the Scripture fights with itself, but
uncovering ineffable doctrines to us through both of these. For whenever it
wishes to present his needlessness, it says that he has no counselor; but
whenever the equal honor of the Only-begotten, it calls the Son of God
counselor. And that you may learn both of these, that the prophets call the
Son counselor, not as if the Father needed counsel, but so that we may learn
the honor of the Only-begotten, and that again he does not need a counselor,
hear Paul saying: O the depth of the wealth and wisdom and knowledge of God,
how unsearchable are his judgments, and untraceable his ways! For who has
known the mind of the Lord, or who has become =48.799= his counselor? This one
then presented his needlessness, but Isaiah again speaking concerning the
Only-begotten of God says somehow thus: And they shall wish that they had been
burned with fire, because a child was born to us, a son also was given to us,
and his name is called messenger of great counsel, wonderful counselor. And if
he is wonderful counselor, how does Paul say: For who has known the mind of
the Lord or who has become his counselor? Because Paul, as I said before,
wishes to present the needlessness of the Father, but the prophet the equal
honor of the Only-begotten. For this reason also here he did not say, Make,
but, Let us make; for the Make is of a command being made toward a slave, and
this is clear from here.

A centurion once came to Jesus, and says: Lord, my servant lies in the house
paralyzed, grievously tormented. What then does Christ? I coming will heal
him. The centurion then did not dare to draw the physician into the house, but
the caring and philanthropic one of his own accord promised to go there, so
that he might give him occasion and pretext to show us his own virtue. For
knowing what that one was about to say, he promised to be present, so that you
may learn the reverence of the man. For what does the centurion say? Lord, I
am not sufficient that you should enter under my roof. Not even the necessity
of the disease and the weakness persuaded him to forget his own reverence, but
even in the misfortune he recognized the superiority of the Master. Wherefore
he says, Speak by a word, and my servant will be healed. For I also am a man
having soldiers under myself, and I say to this one, Go, and he goes, and to
another, Come, and he comes, and to my slave, Do this, and he does it. Do you
see that the Make is of a master conversing toward a slave? Therefore the Let
us make is of someone of equal honor. Whenever then a master speaks to a
slave, he says Make; but whenever a Father converses with a Son, Let us make.
What then, he says, if the centurion thus suspected, but the matter was not
thus? For is the centurion an apostle? For is he a disciple, that I should
receive his words? It is likely that he was mistaken, he says. Well; what
then? Let us see then what follows. Did Christ then correct him? Did he rebuke
him as erring and introducing corrupted doctrines? Did he say to him: What is
this you are doing, man? You have a greater opinion concerning me than is
fitting; you grant me more than is owed to me; you suppose me to command from
authority when I do not have authority. Did he say anything of such a kind? By
no means; but he even confirmed his judgment, and said to those following,
Amen I say to you, not even in Israel have I found so great faith. The praise
of the Master then becomes a confirmation of the words of the centurion. For
no longer are the words that have been said the centurion's, but it is a
masterly sentence; for whenever he himself praises the things said, and votes
for them as well said, I receive them as a divine oracle. For the authority
received it from above from the answer of Christ.

γʹ. Do you see how the New is in accord with the Old? how each displays the
authority of Christ? What then, if he was making the man, but as a minister
=48.800= he was making? Therefore this is of untimely contentiousness. For
having said, Let us make the man, he did not add, according to your lesser
image, nor, according to my greater image, but what? According to our image,
and according to likeness, by speaking thus showing that there is one image of
Father and Son; for he did not say images, but Our image; for they are not two
some unequal ones, but one and this equal image of Son and Father. For this
reason also he is said to sit at the right, so that you may learn the equal
honor and the unvarying of the authority; for a minister does not sit
together, but stands by. And that the sitting is of the equal honor and
unvarying of the masterly authority, but the standing by of slavish and
subjected, hear what Daniel says: I was considering until thrones were set,
and the Ancient of days sat. Ten thousand times ten thousand were ministering
to him, and a thousand thousands were standing by him. And again Isaiah: I saw
the Lord sitting upon a throne high and lifted up, and the seraphim were
standing around him. And Micaiah too, I saw the Lord the God of Israel sitting
upon his throne, and all the army of heaven was standing at his right and at
his left. Do you see everywhere the powers above standing by, but him sitting?
Whenever then you see the Son also having the seat at the right, do not
suppose him to be of the liturgical and ministerial dignity, but of the
masterly and having authority. For this reason also Paul, knowing both of
these, that the standing by is of those who minister, but the sitting of those
who command and give orders, see how he divides both of these saying thus:
Toward his angels he says: Who makes his angels spirits, and his ministers a
flame of fire; but toward the Son: Your throne, O God, is unto the age of the
age; through the throne declaring to us the kingly authority. Since then
through all these the word has shown us that the Son is not of ministerial
dignity, but of the masterly, as Master let us worship him and of equal honor
with the Father; for thus he himself also commanded saying, That all may honor
the Son, just as they honor the Father; and let us join the exactness through
the way of life and the works to the orthodoxy of the doctrines, so that the
things of salvation may not be for us from a half.

And nothing is so able to accomplish exactness of way of life and purity of
life, as the continual sojourn here and the hearing with eagerness. For what
food is in the case of the body, this the teaching of the divine oracles is in
the case of the soul. For not upon bread alone shall man live, he says, but
upon every word going out through the mouth of God. For this reason also not
to partake of such a table is known to work a famine. Hear then God
threatening this, and stretching it forth in the rank of chastisement and
punishment: For I will give them, he says, not a famine of bread, nor a thirst
of water, but a famine of hearing the word of the Lord. How then is it not
absurd, while for the sake of driving off the bodily famine we do and busy
ourselves with all things, yet to draw upon ourselves willingly the famine of
the soul, though it is much more grievous, by as much as the damage is also
concerning greater things? Do not, I beg and entreat, do not thus badly
counsel concerning ourselves, but let the sojourn here =48.801= be preferred
to every occupation and care. For what so great thing do you gain, tell me,
being left behind from the assembly, as you damage both yourself and the whole
house? For even if you find a whole treasure full of gold, and because of this
you are left behind, you have been damaged more, and by so much more, as the
spiritual things are better than the sensible. For those things, even if they
are many, even if they flow from every side, yet they do not journey with us
toward the life there, nor do they migrate with us toward heaven, nor do they
stand by at the fearful tribunal, but often even before the end they leave us
and go away; and if they should remain until the end, yet they are altogether
cut off by the end. But the spiritual treasure is an inalienable possession,
and it follows us walking and sojourning everywhere, and it gives us much
boldness at that tribunal.

δʹ. And if from the other assemblies so great is the gain, from the assemblies
here this becomes double. For we do not harvest this only, that we water the
soul with the divine words, but that we scatter much shame upon the enemies,
and furnish much consolation to our brothers. For this is the gain of the
battle-line, to hasten toward the toiling part of the war, that which is full
of danger. Wherefore all must run together here, and beat off the enemies who
are attacking. You are not able to stretch out a long word, nor do you have
teaching? only be present, and you have accomplished the whole. For the
presence of the body becomes an addition of the flock, and it gives much
eagerness to your brothers, and casts shame around your enemies. For if
someone stepping upon these sacred thresholds sees few gathered, he also
quenches the eagerness that is, and grows numb and shrinks back and becomes
more sluggish and withdraws; then thus little by little the whole multitude is
more slack and more careless for us. But if he sees them running together,
being eager, flowing together from every side, the eagerness of the others
becomes a ground of eagerness even for the exceedingly sluggish and relaxed.
For if a stone rubbed against a stone has often caused sparks to leap out, and
yet what is colder than a stone, and what hotter than fire? yet nevertheless
the continuity conquered the nature; and if this happens in the case of a
stone, much more in the case of souls being rubbed together with one another
and being heated through by the fire of the Spirit.

Did you not hear that in the case of our ancestors all the faithful were a
hundred and twenty, or rather before the hundred and twenty twelve only, and
not even these all remained, but one of them was lost, Judas, and they were
eleven in all? Yet nevertheless from those eleven a hundred and twenty came
=48.802= to be, and from the hundred and twenty three thousand, then five
thousand, then they filled the whole inhabited world with the knowledge of
God. And the cause was that they never left behind their gathering together,
but they were always with one another spending the day in the temple, and
attending to prayers and readings; for this reason they kindled a great pyre,
for this reason they never leaked away, but they drew the whole inhabited
world. And let us also imitate these. For how is it not absurd, not even to
display so much care concerning this Church, as the women concerning their own
neighbors? For they too, whenever they see some virgin poor and destitute of
all protection, all contribute the things from themselves becoming in the rank
of those who belong to her, and one might see there much tumult and crowd of
the virgin being given in marriage; and some often brought in money, and
others the presence of the body; and this is not small; for the eagerness of
these becomes a covering of the cheapness, and thus they hide the poverty
through their own willingness. This then do also in the case of this Church.
Let us all run together from every side and cover her poverty, or rather let
us dissolve her poverty by continually being present here.

The head of the woman is the man; the woman is a helper of the man. Let
therefore neither the head without the body endure to step upon these holy
thresholds, nor let the body appear without the head, but let the whole man
enter here, having also the children with themselves. For if it is delightful
to see a tree having from its own root a young plant standing up, much more is
it delightful to see a man, and more delightful than every olive tree, having
from his own root the child standing near like a young plant; and not
delightful only, but also profitable. For, as I said before, there is more
wage here for those who gather; since we also especially admire a farmer, not
when he tends the land that has often been farmed, but when having taken the
unsown and untilled fields, he thinks them worthy of much care. Thus also Paul
was doing, being ambitious to preach the gospel, not where Christ was named,
but where he was not being named. Let us also imitate this one, both for the
revenue of the Church, and for our own benefit; let us then run here to every
assembly. And if desire burns, you will be able easily to quench it, having
seen this house only; and if you are angry, you will with ease put the beast
to sleep; and if some other passion besieges, you will be able to dissolve all
the winter, and to work a calm, and much peace for the soul; of which may it
be that we all enjoy, by the grace and philanthropy of our Lord Jesus Christ,
with whom to the Father together with the Holy Spirit be the glory, now and
always, and unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
