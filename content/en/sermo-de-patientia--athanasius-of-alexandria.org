1 Of the same Athanasius, Archbishop of Alexandria,
On Patience.

Our teacher Paul commands that you present your bodies a living sacrifice,
well-pleasing to God. The command is common, but rather it promises to those
who practice holiness. The virgin is like the other women in nature, but by
purpose she has surpassed nature. And having willed to approach the Holy One
in a holy manner, if she succeeds here, she has carried off a great wonder;
but if having set forth she did not complete, such a fall has become
extraordinary; for it is harder to fall from higher things than to fall down
from those on the ground. And as great as the promise is, so much worse is the
fall. For the promise surpasses nature, and carries her up into heaven, and
prepares her to dance with angels. Hence already by her way of life, as the
Savior also says, bearing witness to this dignity, that after the resurrection
of the dead the life is, he says, the expectation of men. And again he says
that those counted worthy of the resurrection of the coming good things
neither marry nor are given in marriage. What therefore is laid up in the
kingdom of the heavens among the promises, this the purpose of some, having
anticipated, has and possesses. For the saints, who have their reasoning
stretched up toward God, and eating from the heavenly food, considered this
world as a prison, and have given over their bodies unto martyrdom, and have
kept their own soul pure, that God may dwell in them. And they endured, just
as our Lord says, that he who has endured unto the end, this one will be
saved; and as the psalmist says: He toiled unto the age, and he will live unto
the end; for as many righteous as have their hope in God have not had fleshly
rest that separates them from God; but they remained in labors, not shrinking,
nor indeed doubting concerning the divine promises; but receiving the
afflictions lightly, they remember the one who says through the Apostle: And
you accepted the plunder of your goods with joy. And he also bears witness
concerning those who did not accept the deliverance, that they did not wish,
having turned back, to be again among the heavenly things; but we must not be
among the worldly things, but pass over to be with God. For now you also,
beloved brothers, do not grow weary, nor become like the people who in the
time of Moses transgressed in forty days only, when he was going up to receive
the divine law; and from this they became liable, and they fell in the
=26.1300= wilderness; but let us become like Moses who made forty days and
nights, fasting without food and drink. Not only these, but also another
forty, having both his heart and his eyes and his flesh hung toward God. Let
us not desire as those who then became desirers of meats, falling in the
wilderness, While their food was still in their mouth, as the psalm says, and
the wrath of God went up upon them; but let us keep them for the one who gave
the commandments, as the sons of Jonadab, to whom God promised saying: There
shall not fail a man of the sons of Jonadab standing before me all the days of
his life; who did not build a house for themselves, nor indeed plant
vineyards, and they became a reproof to the people. And the people, being a
multitude, were taken captive; but these, being few, were guarded by God. You
too therefore, who keep the way of the fathers, consider yourselves sons of
the sons of Jonadab. And let us keep the inheritance of the fathers, as the
noble Naboth, who was threatened by Ahab to take his vineyard for a garden of
vegetables; and not accepting, he was killed while struggling; but God avenged
him. For he did not want his own vineyard to be a garden, where leeks [πράσα]
and onions and garlic grow, inasmuch as he turned aside; but he wanted a
vineyard of gladness; or rather his own mind gladdening, and not limp like a
garden-mallow in the heat, or like a half-boiled beet, as those who have now
betrayed. For if it had been taken away, perhaps the mind of the ancestral
piety would have been taken away; for he was also named Israelite, because he
was a mind seeing God. But those now, having suffered nothing, have sold their
own vineyard of the true vine to Ahab, and so many blameworthy vegetable-like
things were planted. Let us not become lovers of money as the servant of
Elisha, who for this drew the leprosy of Naaman upon himself; but let us
endure in the afflictions, that we may become partakers of the rest; for no
one will enter into the kingdom without affliction. Let us imitate our earlier
saints. The first righteous man was Abel, who offered a first-fruit to God
from the firstborn of his sheep and from their fat, whom his own brother
envied. For what injustice did Abel do to Cain? But the envy came to be in him
for this reason, because he himself was not accepted by God in not offering
rightly. And he killed the body, thinking to quench the divine love between
him, not knowing that his soul cries out to God. And our Savior makes mention
of him in the Gospel; but the memory of Cain has been taken away. Today again
they kill the guiltless Abel; but the blood cries out to the Lord; and we hope
that it will not be passed over in silence because of the blood of such great
martyrs. As was that time, so also now. =26.1301= And let us remember also the
endurance of Enoch, that he spent three hundred and sixty-five years having
his mind stretched up toward God, until he was translated. Do not therefore
grow weary; for we have not delayed. And Noah also spent five hundred years
being righteous, perfect in the time of the transgression, before God spoke to
him to construct the ark, and another hundred years working it, being mocked
by those who opposed; and those who mocked perished by the flood; but he,
having hoped upon God, was saved with his household, and became a salvation
for the world. And Abraham again, having become obedient to the Lord, left his
own dwellings, until he received the great promises; for being tried even to
the offering of his own beloved son as a sacrifice to God, Abraham also
received so great an oracle as to be called friend of God. And the Philistines
were greedy against Isaac, and they blocked up his wells, and they fought with
him about the water; but when at last he withdrew from them, God enlarged him,
and filled his mouth with blessing. And today again they wish to block up the
wells of Isaac; but do not allow it; take with you the servants of Isaac, and
dig, which are helps of the teaching of the apostles, who are champions of the
truth. Jacob again his own brother Esau persecuted, and he withdrew into
Mesopotamia, and he shepherded the sheep of Laban, watching day and night,
suffering hardship as a hired man, he was longsuffering in toil. And in his
own understanding he placed the rods in the troughs of the watering-places,
and all the many-colored sheep became his own. And to this man God at the last
enlarged; for he himself went away alone, but he has come with a great
multitude, he acquired sons and cattle, and not only, but also multitudes of
angels rejoiced with him. Therefore the true Jacob will come with the heavenly
angelic array, the shepherd of the sheep, and he will render to each according
to his deed. And Joseph his own brothers envied; the sons of his father sold
this one for silver, and they divided his price. See the so great injustice of
these men. But the righteous one was longsuffering in all these things; for
not only the brothers, but also the woman envied him. And God appointed this
one who had been longsuffering king of the land of Egypt. He became heir not
only of the sensible kingdom, but also of the heavenly. And today there are
those who sell their own brothers and divide the silver. Let us remember also
the endurance of Job, who neither when prospering turned aside, nor indeed
being tried with so many things in each matter, but having been longsuffering
steadfastly, until he received double from God. And it has been witnessed
until now, that this one suffered, that he might appear righteous to all. And
Moses did not desire to acquire the wealth of the place; but he fled, becoming
a sojourner in a foreign land, or he left the present things, that he might
partake of the eternal things. But those then perhaps forgot [ἐπελάχοντο] the
kingdom. =26.1304= Time will fail us as we narrate concerning each of the
saints. But that we may speak the word briefly, we make clear that Jezebel
also persecuted Elijah, and having endured in the labor he was taken up. And
Elisha also with those with him ate herbs, but he had the Word living in him;
and his bones, when he had died, raised a dead man, a sign being given that
the hope of the righteous does not perish. Jeremiah they threw living into a
pit of mire, being envied by the false prophets. And Ezekiel was lying upon
his sides one hundred and ninety whole days, eating bread and drinking wine by
measure, he did not turn from side to side, until God said to him. But those
who forsake the Lord turn over onto the side of their own rest, who are
evident as having received their own portion from here. Remain therefore upon
the side until the divinity commands. For the great bonds of Ezekiel are the
most sure words of God. And the satraps accused Daniel, wishing to hinder him
from the prayers; but he did not cease, and he was thrown twice into a pit of
lions; and God blocked up their mouths. And today they accuse us, not wishing
us to pray to God. This is clear, that they persecute both us and our Fathers
the bishops. Blessed is the man who is blameless, and no cause was found in
him, but this only, the praying to the Lord. This is to endure unto the end.
Let no one among you therefore be faint-hearted. For consider the three holy
ones, being small, not submitting to the image of Nebuchadnezzar, and through
the same holiness and way of life quenching so great a furnace of flame, and
not even a smell of fire came to be in their garments. Nor let there come to
be in your garments a smell of the fire of Nebuchadnezzar, which are the
perishable reasonings of the devil that are being quenched. Let not food
deceive you, nor let anyone say: I have grown old, and I cannot fast; but let
him remember the aged Eleazar, who was not deceived by the king to taste or to
eat unclean food. And let the younger among you remember the seven most noble
Maccabees, each encouraging his own brother, until they were perfected in the
martyrdom, and became pillars of light in the temple of the Lord. And again,
as many mothers as are among you, let them be comforters of the children, just
as theirs was unfailing and unerring, exhorting the sons, that she might
receive them in the kingdom with herself. And even if you are persecuted, and
they take you, let it suffice that your body is a temple of God. Remember the
apostles then being persecuted and thrown into prison, and giving thanks with
joy, that they were counted worthy to be dishonored for his name; and the holy
martyrs who bore so many tortures, even to the digging out of eyes, and they
became one-eyed. But the knowledge of God was in their heart, to whom the
=26.1305= word of the Savior reached, saying: Blessed are the pure in heart,
for they shall see God. But some now have betrayed, having suffered no torture
at all, on account of three things, both love of money and enjoyment of flesh,
of foods and drinks, and vainglory, love of rule and human fear. These things
we have written exhorting you; but there is a measure of each according to
each one's own way of life. Let each of you therefore acquire friends for
himself above; wherefore the Scripture says: Let a friend be yours in every
time. And let brothers be useful in necessities; for for this they are born.
As many therefore as are imitators of each of those mentioned before will be
fellow-heirs of these, and will have these as friends in a day of necessity.
If anyone is a presbyter, let him imitate Timothy who presides well, and
obtains a standing for himself and much boldness in Christ. If anyone is a
deacon, let him imitate those around Stephen, the seven entrusted to serve,
who also became faithful, full of the Holy Spirit. If anyone is a reader, let
him become a fellow-partaker of the one who says: My tongue is a pen of a
swiftly writing scribe; and as Peter said: If anyone speaks, as oracles of
God; and, Silver refined is the tongue of a righteous man. As many as are
perfect monks, let them have these holy men as friends, Elijah, Elisha, John;
and let the younger among them imitate the three holy ones then together with
the Maccabees. And the virgins are expected with their companions in the
heavens, Mary together with the sister of Aaron, and Mary and Thecla who
plucked the fleshly desire from herself, and the four prophesying daughters of
Philip. And let the widows in their way of life be as Deborah judging the
people, who was named a mother in Jerusalem; and as Judith in prudence and
sanctification taking off the head of Holofernes; and as Anna daughter of
Phanuel, being eighty-four years, who was in the temple serving in fasts and
prayers, and at the end she gave thanks to the Lord. As many as are elders in
age, let them be imitators of the good old age of Abraham in hospitality, and
as Isaac and Jacob, and Job and Lot. And as many as are elderly women or
widows, let them emulate the widow in Zarephath, and the one who offered the
two mites. But now, my beloved in the Lord, without many words, it is a time
to mourn. And it came to pass after Jerusalem was taken captive, and Israel
was made desolate, Jeremiah sat weeping, and he lamented this lamentation over
Jerusalem, and said: How did the city sit solitary that was full of people?
she became as a widow; for the saints weep over the fall of men. For behold,
today the Church has become as a widow; behold, the ways of Zion mourn because
of there not =26.1308= being those coming at the feast; behold, her priests
groan, and her virgins are led away, our Sabbath has been shut, and the feast,
and the new moon. And in another place the prophet weeps saying: I will weep
for you, vine of Sibmah; why were your grape-gatherers destroyed? the wine is
in the vat, needing empty vessels worthy to contain it. And many, because of
the multitude of the wounded in the present time, the wounded have declined.
As many then as remained unharmed, let them come forward as new empty vessels
that have been kept, to partake. And again in another place he weeps saying:
Who would give my head water, and my eyes a fountain of tears, and let us weep
for this people day and night, the wounded of the daughter of my people, who
are wise to do evil, but have not known to do the good? Doing this they weep
over the fall of men. And the prophet Ezekiel again lamented over each of
those who had fallen away, as it was said to him: Take up a lamentation upon
the prince of Tyre. And he weeps not only over the prince of Tyre, but over
the many; perhaps they will arrive from his tears; for they are watchmen of
the whole, and messengers of mourning. And David wept over the fall of mighty
ones saying: How have the mighty fallen, and the weapons of war perished? Tell
it not in Gath, nor in the streets of Askelon, lest the daughters of the
uncircumcised rejoice; for behold, today also mighty ones have fallen, and
great weapons of war, which one must weep over. And he wept over Absalom, not
because he died, but because he transgressed. We too therefore let us not weep
for those who have died, but for those who have denied their own faith. Let us
also receive the word of the prophet Joel, and let us accomplish it, as he
commands and calls to weep over those in destruction saying: Let the
bridegroom go forth from his chamber, and the bride from her inner room. The
priests who minister at the altar shall weep, and they shall say: Spare, O
Lord, your people, and do not give your inheritance to reproach, that the
nations should rule over them, lest they say among the nations: Where is their
God? And perhaps somehow it might be said also to us: And the Lord was jealous
for his people, and he spared his people. And in the Lamentations again it is
laid down: Let the walls of Zion bring down tears like torrents day and night.
Does he then speak to walls or stones, or altogether to those who are holy
walls of the Church? For behold, it has been fulfilled upon all of them, who
have brought down tears like torrents everywhere. But now let us also take
hold of the word of the Song: Going they went and wept, bearing their seed.
Just as those who preside now and do not have where to cast the seeds. But
nevertheless coming they shall come in exultation, bearing their sheaves. For
blessed are those who mourn, for they shall be comforted; and this: In the
evening weeping shall lodge, and toward the morning exultation. =26.1309= For
the apostles also then weeping in the evening, exulted in the morning. And
Mary Magdalene sat beside the tomb weeping; to this one the Lord, having
appeared, took away the tears from her eyes; the thing said beforehand was
fulfilled: And God took away every tear from every face. Remember our Lord,
that he suffered so many things for us; he was stripped of the clothing, they
clothed him with a scarlet cloak, they put around him a crown of thorns, they
spat on him, they mocked him, and the Master of all was struck by a servant.
He was hung upon a tree, they gave him vinegar with gall to drink, they
divided his garments while he looked on, they pierced the side with a spear,
he committed the spirit into the hands of the Father, his body was placed in a
tomb, he rose on the third day, he was taken up in glory into the heavens,
holy angels proclaimed him beforehand saying: Lift up your gates, you princes.
Consider therefore the one who showed such endurance, that whoever becomes an
imitator of him, this one may also become a fellow-seated and fellow-heir of
him, as he himself promised, in the kingdom of the heavens unto the ages of
the ages. Amen.
