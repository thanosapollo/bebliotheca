* Homilies on Luke

1.3 Just as among the people of old many professed prophecy, but of these some
were false prophets, and some truly prophets, and there was a gift for the
people of discernment of spirits, from which both the true prophet and the
falsely named one were judged; so also now in the new covenant "many" have
wished to write the Gospels, but "the approved money-changers" did not approve
all, but selected some of them. 1.4 And perhaps even the "they have taken in
hand" has a hidden accusation of those who came without a gift to the writing
of the Gospels. For Matthew did not "take it in hand," but wrote from the Holy
Spirit, and likewise Mark and John, and similarly also Luke. Those however who
composed the Gospel inscribed according to the Egyptians and the Gospel
inscribed of the Twelve 1.5 "took it in hand"; and already Basilides too dared
to write a Gospel according to Basilides. "Many then have taken in hand": for
the Gospel according to Thomas is also in circulation and that according to
Matthias and several others. These are of those who took it in hand; but the
church of God prefers the four alone. And Luke at any rate, by saying
"concerning the things that have been fully assured among us 1.6," shows his
own disposition, that doubting nothing and not guessing, but he confirmed all
things with boldness as knowing well. For he had been fully assured and
doubted in nothing, whether it is so or not. And this comes about in those who
believe firmly and pray and obtain and have said: "Confirm me in your words."
For the apostle also says concerning the firm: "that you may be rooted and
founded in the faith." For nothing so fully assures as mind and word; for
sight does not fully assure, since the things are not judged from visible
signs and wonders, but by word it is judged which are true and which are
false. 1.7 "The eyewitnesses," he says, "and ministers of the Word." In Exodus
it is written that "the people saw the voice of the Lord." And yet a voice is
not seen, but heard, that it may be shown paradoxically that the voice of God
is seen by those by whom it is seen. But in the Gospel it is not the voice
that is seen, but the Word who is better than the voice. Therefore: "Even as
they delivered to us, who from the beginning were eyewitnesses and became
ministers of the Word." Therefore the apostles were "eyewitnesses of the Word"
not only having seen Jesus according to the body, but also the Word of God.
For if to have seen Jesus according to the body was to become an "eyewitness
of the Word," Pilate also was an "eyewitness of the Word," condemning him, and
Judas the betrayer, and all who 1.8 said: "Crucify, crucify him." But far be
it to say that those were "eyewitnesses of the Word." To see the Word, then,
is understood there where the Savior said: "He who has seen me has seen the
Father who sent me." And Luke also secretly teaches us a fine lesson here,
that of some contemplations the end is contemplation, and of some
contemplations the end is action. 1.9 In order then that through
"eyewitnesses" he may indicate the contemplative, and through "ministers"
present their practical, he said "eyewitnesses and ministers." "It seemed good
to me also, having followed from above": and I write, he says, not having
received a bare hearing, but having followed accurately from above; he affirms
that he followed not some of the things said, but all. And the apostle also
praises the blessed Luke, saying: "whose praise is in the Gospel." =1.10= And
it is likely that some suppose that he wrote the Gospel to a certain
Theophilus. But also all, if we are such as to be loved by God and cherished,
are Theophili. And if anyone is a "Theophilus," this one is also "most
excellent"; for no "Theophilus" is weak. And just as it is written of the
people going out of Egypt =1.11=, that "there was not among their tribes one
who was weak," so I would say that every "Theophilus" is "most excellent,"
having the might and the power that is from God and from his Word; and thus


someone will come to know "the security concerning the words in which he was
catechized," having understood the word of the Gospel. =2.12= "And they were
righteous," he says, "both." Those who wish to plead for their own sins think
it impossible that anyone be wholly sinless, and they use the words of Job:
"No one is clean from filth." But the being sinless is understood in two ways:
one in never having sinned, and the other in no longer sinning. From never
having sinned, then, no one would be sinless; for all men have sinned at some
time, even <if> later they become "righteous." =2.13= But it is possible from
no longer sinning to be called sinless; for thus also Christ "presented to
himself the church not having spot or wrinkle," not that the churchman was
never stained or came to be in a wrinkle of the "old man," but that he no
longer has a "wrinkle." And an example of a man's being able, by no longer
sinning, to be called sinless is the case of Zechariah and Elizabeth. "They
were," he says, "righteous and walking blameless." =2.14= And it could have
been written: "They were righteous in the commandments," but now the addition
of "before God" is necessary. For it is possible that according to what
appears someone is righteous before men, but according to the hidden of the
mind not be such, having lurking wicked reasonings. For when a man has nothing
base to say about me, but considering the things of mine that are seen he
praises, I am righteous "before" men. But men do not know my hidden part, when
I sinned by looking =2.15= at a woman to desire her, so that the adultery in
the heart was reckoned to me. And Paul also indicates something of this sort,
when he says concerning some: "whose praise is not from men, but from God."
For men do not know how to praise according to worth, but God alone knows both
to praise worthily the praiseworthy and to make worthily the judgment of the
blameworthy. =2.16= Necessarily there is added: "they were righteous before
God." To which Solomon also summons: "take thought for good things," saying,
"before the Lord and men." And let us see the praise of these blessed ones:
"walking in the commandments," he says, "and in the ordinances of the Lord."
When we judge well concerning others, we walk "<in> the ordinances of the
Lord," but when we do this or that, we walk "<in> the commandments of God."
Perhaps then Luke, knowing this difference, as these living according to them,
rendered this praise: "walking in all the commandments and ordinances of the
Lord, blameless." Perhaps someone will say: why is "blameless" added to these?
For it would have been enough for them "to walk in all the ways of God and
ordinances," unless indeed it is possible to walk "in all the commandments and
=2.17= ordinances," but not "blamelessly." How then [he says] is it possible
"to walk in all the commandments," but not "blamelessly"? I say then to him
that, if this were not so, it would not have been said: "You shall pursue the
just justly," since even the good is not good, when it does not come about
well. Whenever then we do the commandments so as to have in the conscience
filth of vainglory or of pleasing men or of some other such thing, =2.18= even
if we seem to do the commandment, we do not do it "blamelessly." It is a work
then to walk "in the commandments and ordinances of the Lord blamelessly"
according to praise that will be rendered by God in the divine court. =3.19=
The sensibles, in order to be seen, do nothing, but the healthy eye having
been set, whether the sensible wishes to be seen or not, sees it. But the
divine things are not such, nor are they seen in the present without their own
activity. And at any rate by grace God "appeared" to Abraham or to some of the
saints, not the eye of Abraham's soul alone having become the cause of God's
being seen by him, but God's having offered himself to manifestation to the
righteous one, who had become worthy of his appearing. =3.20= But indeed an
angel also, so far as he does not wish to be seen by some one of us, though
present is not seen. And do not wonder at the present, if God wishing
"appeared" to Abraham, but not wishing did not "appear" to so-and-so, and "an
angel


appeared" to Zechariah, and not wishing would not have "appeared" to him. But
also after the "present age," if we go out, know that God or the angels do not
appear to everyone, =3.21= but each of those who see, by having a pure heart
and having been prepared toward seeing God, will see God. And in the same
place two, one having a pure heart, and the other an abominable one: the one
will see, since God is not seen in a place, but with a pure heart, and the
other will not see him. Thus it must be understood also in the case of Christ.
For do not suppose that all who were looking at Christ were seeing. They were
looking at the body of Christ, but Christ, according as he is Christ, they
were not seeing. And he was seen only by those worthy to see his greatness.
=3.22= And the Scripture also promised: "that he is found by those who do not
tempt him, and he appears to those who do not disbelieve him." =4.23= "And
Zechariah," he says, "was troubled when he saw"; for a strange sight appearing
to a man is not endurable to him; yet the angel turns him from the trouble,
saying: "Do not fear, Zechariah," and he recovers him by announcing the birth
of John, saying: "Your petition has been heard." =4.24= And to say "he shall
be great" indicates the greatness of John's soul, and a greatness seen by
God. For there is a certain greatness of soul according to virtue, which is
seen by the Lord, and there is a certain smallness of soul. I hear thus the
saying in the Gospel: "Do not despise one of these little ones," those in the
church. For there he calls "little" the opposite of the great, not needing a
commandment concerning the great, in order that he may say this also: for
neither is the =4.25= great despised. And that "the little one" is <not> just
anyone, is clear from: "whoever shall scandalize one of these little ones."
For a great one is not scandalized; but the great one is better than every
scandal. The people then do not know the Savior already doing "signs and
wonders," =4.26= but John still being in the womb of the mother leaps, when
the mother of Jesus came. "For behold," she says, "as the sound of your
greeting came into my ears, the babe leaped in exultation in my womb." John
then had the Holy Spirit still being in the womb of the mother. He himself
then is said to have "turned many." John "turned many of the sons of Israel to
the Lord their God," according to the voice of the angel, but our Lord Jesus
Christ enlightened all =4.27= unto the knowledge of the truth. For this is his
work: "and he will turn many of the sons of Israel to the Lord their God." For
he does not say "in the soul of Elijah" (for there was not transmigration of
souls) but "in the spirit and power of Elijah." For there was upon Elijah
spirit and power, or rather a spiritual gift, just as also upon each of the
prophets. The spirit then that was in Elijah came to be in John. That one was
taken up, and this one became forerunner of the Savior. =4.28= He died before
the Savior, in order that, having become forerunner of the Savior also to
those in Hades, he might preach the descent of Jesus Christ. =5.29= Zechariah
the priest is condemned to be silent, the minister of God who offered the
sacrifices, and he is silent and being silent only made signs, and he remained
dumb until the birth of John. What then are these things? The silence of
Zechariah is <the silence> of the prophets among the people of God of old. For
God no longer speaks to them, but "the Word who was in the beginning, the Word
who was with God, the God Word" has passed over to us and among us is not
silent, but among those he has been silent. Therefore the prophet Zechariah is
silent; for he is also said to have been a prophet. And what of: "and he made
signs to them"? The "making signs" while remaining dumb is of this sort:
actions without word =5.30= differ in nothing from noddings, but actions with
word are not noddings; for they are adorned by the Word. If then you see the
polity of the Jews as without reason, so that they cannot give a reason
concerning the things they do, look at the type that came about in Zechariah
remaining dumb and making signs to them. According to this, circumcision
without word is for them noddings, and Passover and prayers. For now the
people is dumb and speechless; and how was it not going to be dumb and without
reason and


speechless, having cast the Word out from itself =5.31= and being able to give
an account concerning nothing of a legal or prophetic word? =6.32= <Not> every
time is given even to those who are in marriage for marital communion, but
there is a time when it is necessary to stand off from this, already having
grown old and having been shut off by providence toward conceiving <through>
the works of marriage. Since then she according to economy through the word of
the angel "conceived," she was ashamed if [not] <being> an old woman she
sleeps
with a husband, =6.33= and "she hid herself for five months," until Mary also
conceived. For when Mary conceived and came to her, and "when the greeting"
of her "came into" her "ears," "the babe leaped in exultation in the womb" of
Elizabeth, and Elizabeth prophesied and from "the Holy Spirit" spoke the
things written. And "all these words were spoken in the hill country." When
they had been spoken then as concerning a divine conception and as concerning
a prophet about to be born among the people, she no longer "hid herself," but,
if one must speak thus, she also spoke boldly, that her offspring was the
forerunner of the Lord. =6.34= Then after these things it is written that "in
the sixth month," of the conception of Elizabeth, that is, "the angel Gabriel
was sent from God to a virgin betrothed to a man whose name was Joseph." Why
ever, God having judged to generate the Savior from a virgin, did he not
choose a virgin unbetrothed? Perhaps then it was also economy that she having
a betrothed should conceive, so that the conception might not seem to bring
shame upon her body? Wherefore it is well written in one of the letters of a
certain martyr (I mean Ignatius, the second bishop of Antioch after the
blessed Peter, who in the persecution at Rome fought with beasts): =6.35= "and
the virginity of Mary escaped the ruler of this age." As, if the seeming
marriage had not come about, it would not have escaped him, but "the ruler of
this age" would have known that Mary, never having slept with a man, conceived
and that this conception must be divine. And the Savior wished throughout the
whole economy to escape the notice of the devil, and at any rate he commanded
the disciples not to make him manifest. But also when he was tempted by the
devil, nowhere did he declare nakedly that he is Son of God, but he only said:
I must not worship you, I must not make the stones into bread, I must not
throw myself from above downward. =6.36= But the apostle also says that the
economy of the passion came about according to a forgetting of the opposing
energy: "which none of the rulers of this age knew. For if they had known,
they would not have crucified the Lord of glory." But if it did not escape the
demon (for he said: "We know you, who you are, the Son of God"), see that the
lesser in wickedness knew the Savior, but the worse in it, from the greatness
of the wickedness, was hindered from =6.37= contemplating him. And our
wickedness too, if it is lesser, we are helped through the less toward living
well; but if more wickedness is found in us, there is a greater contest to
cast it off. He said to her a certain strange greeting, which I have not found
in any Scripture; come then, let us examine concerning this. "Hail, favored
one" has been said nowhere; but neither has this been said to a man anywhere,
and this greeting was reserved for her. What then of the virgin, knowing as
one learned in the law a greeting strange and spoken to no one before? =6.38=
She was troubled at the word; wherefore he also says to her: "Do not fear."
"This one shall be great," he says. This has been said also concerning John;
but when the one who is truly "great" came, that one was small. For "he was
the lamp that was burning and shining." And his greatness was not then
indicated as much as it now appears. For see <the> greatness of his power:
"that into all the earth the sound of his teaching has gone out, and into the
ends of the inhabited world his words." =6.39= and with those in <Britain
.......> and with those in Mauretania and with those who believe everywhere
and extending wholly. For go up with the Word into heaven and see him filling
the things there; for he "appeared"


"to angels." Go down with the Word into the abyss [and likewise]; for he went
down there also. "For he who went down is himself also the one who went up,
that he might fill all things," "that in his name every knee should bow of
things in heaven and on earth and under the earth." See this power; for it
passed through the whole world; =6.40= for he says that Jesus the Son of God
has gone through the heavens. If then you see these things, you will see that
"he shall be great" is not a little saying, but it is shown in work that our
Lord Jesus Christ is "great" both being absent and being present. =7.41= The
better come to the lesser, in order that they may do them good. Thus also the
Savior came to John, in order that he might sanctify his baptism; thus Mary,
having heard from the angel that she would bear the Savior and having learned
that her kinswoman Elizabeth had conceived, came to her. For Jesus in her was
already about to do good to John already in Elizabeth. And this is clear from
John's not leaping before Mary came and greeted Elizabeth, but together with
the word of Mary's greeting, which the Word of God, the Christ, =7.42=
suggested, the forerunner heard, being moved by the activity of the Holy
Spirit, the greeting of Mary, and "the babe leaped in exultation in the womb,"
and as it were from then Jesus began to make him a prophet. And it was
necessary that this blessed Mary, after the sojourn of Gabriel to her and the
annunciation, going up, go up "into the hill country," and as not sluggish in
zeal go zealously; and she was going with haste, being led by the Holy Spirit
that came upon her and by the power of the Most High that overshadowed her.
=7.43= Therefore in the greeting "Elizabeth was filled" through the son "with
the Holy Spirit"; for the mother was not "filled with the Holy Spirit" first,
but primarily John having been filled, secondarily the mother, as having the
one filled "with the Holy Spirit." And one must not hesitate to say, lest
someone be deceived by those who use plausibilities and teach falsely, that
someone dared to speak against Mary, as though the Savior denied her, since,
he says, she was joined after the birth of the Savior to Joseph. =7.44= If
ever then such words are brought forward by heretics, one must answer thus,
that Elizabeth, filled "with the Holy Spirit," says: "Blessed are you among
women." And if she has been blessed by the Holy Spirit, how did the Savior
deny her? But neither can they prove that she used intercourse after the birth
of the Savior; for the sons of Joseph were not from Mary, nor does anyone have
this to present from the Scripture. And "whence is this to me?" Elizabeth
filled with the Holy Spirit does not say in ignorance, that: I do not know
that it has come about from God that the mother "of my Lord" has come "to me,"
but "whence is this to me" is of this sort: for what so great good, she says,
has been done by me, "that the mother of my Lord should come to me"? from
whence, from what sort of
righteousness =7.45= or of good deeds did I obtain this? "For behold the babe
leaped
in my womb." The soul of John was marvelous, and still being in the mother's
womb and about to come to birth it knew him whom Israel did not know. And he
did not simply "leap," but "in exultation"; for he perceived that the Lord had
come in the mother, in order that, going before him, he might sanctify him.
=7.46= Since you believed "the things that were spoken," you are blessed; why?
For there will be a fulfillment of them, and they have not been spoken in vain
by the angel. =8.47= Before John, Elizabeth prophesies; before the birth of
our Savior, Mary prophesies. And just as sin began "from the woman" and after
this reached the man, so also the good things began from the women, in order
that the women may be urged, putting away the weakness and the womanish
things, to emulate the lives of these blessed ones. Let us see then the
virginal prophecy, which says: "My soul magnifies the Lord," not only the
body, but also "my soul." =8.48= How "the soul magnifies the Lord" must be
considered. For someone will say: the


Lord neither becomes greater nor lesser, but what he is, he is; how then can a
soul magnify the Lord? Because "he is the image of the invisible God," not
having become an image, but "according to an image" preexisting it, if we
conduct ourselves worthily according to the image of the one who created us
and are likened to the prototype according to what is possible through good
works. And each of us, perfecting his own soul and leading it on to
blessedness, as it were sets up an image of Christ, either imitating the
prototype, the Son of God, =8.49= the image of God. When then I make great
the image of the image, and I mean the soul itself, magnifying by words,
works, thoughts, then it is said to magnify. And just as "the soul" of the
righteous "magnifies the Lord" according to the greatness of the life and of
the word, so also someone diminishes him analogously according to the
wickedness that exists in him. =8.50= Mary's "soul" then magnified "the Lord,"
and her "spirit rejoiced in God the Savior." And she well goes up by the
progresses of the works; first her soul magnified, then her spirit rejoiced.
For one rejoices in spirit in God the Savior in a progress of living, coming
from the magnifying to this. =8.51= "Because he looked," she says, "upon the
humility of his servant." "Humility" differs in nothing from want of vanity;
since then it is fitting for God to look upon the virtues, and humility is a
virtue, "he looked upon the humility of his servant." =9.54= [that] to many
the "the time of her bearing was fulfilled and she generated a son" seems
superfluous. For what woman can bear a son, the time of the conception not
having been fulfilled? One must observe in the whole old and new Scripture,
whether anywhere it is possible to find said upon a sinner's birth: "the time
of bearing was fulfilled"; for one would not find it, as I think, but where
such a thing has been named, the one being generated is righteous; the days
then of the birth of the righteous one and of his sojourn are fulfilled. And
the sojourn of the righteous has fullness, and that of the base has emptiness;
so also now =9.55= it has been said that "for Elizabeth the time of bearing
was fulfilled," since she was bearing a holy one. And the kinsfolk wishing, in
honor of the father, to set the name Zechariah for the child, Elizabeth being
a prophetess and being moved by the Holy Spirit, although she had not heard
the revelation of the child's name that came about to the husband, knew by the
spirit the appellation spoken by the angel and said that the little child was
to be named John. And when they thought it right to learn also from the
father and "they made signs" to him as not hearing or being able to speak, and
Zechariah also with the hand and the letters =9.56= spoke things agreeing with
the woman; taking a tablet he wrote the appellation of the child, so that all
were astonished at this and conjectured "what the little child will be." And
John is interpreted "grace of God"; wherefore also after Zechariah wrote on a
tablet that "John is his name," immediately by the grace of God "his mouth was
opened," "the bound voice was loosed" and he received his own tongue, no
longer as of old bound by unbelief; for when the tongue was bound by unbelief,
it was human, but when =9.57= it has been loosed, no longer; for "he speaks
blessing God" and prophesying the things written. =10.58= And Zechariah
prophesies two generic prophecies, the first concerning Christ, and the next
concerning John; and it is possible from the words to observe of the former,
that concerning Christ as already present and having sojourned in the life,
then
later concerning John he speaks as to one present and says: "Blessed is the
Lord the God of Israel, because he has visited and made redemption for his
people." =10.59= And perhaps in the three months in which Mary was with him,
being benefited by an ineffable power little by little, he came to being
filled with the Spirit and to prophesying the things written. For since
"Christ has come according to the flesh from the seed of David" and this one
was truly "a horn of salvation for us in the house of David his servant,"
therefore he prophesies these things. And that Christ is a horn,


the prophecy presents: "For a vineyard was generated in a horn." In what horn?
In this Christ, concerning whom he says: "he raised a horn for us." "He
raised," he says, God and Father "a horn of salvation" for us, the Christ,
according to the prediction of his "holy prophets"; for to all the prophets
the mystery concerning Christ has been announced; =10.60= who delivered "us
from our enemies and from the hand of all who hate us," and enemies not
sensible, but intelligible. For Christ came, "Lord, mighty in war," to abolish
all our enemies. And "to do mercy with our fathers" I think indicates this,
that perhaps in the sojourn of Christ our fathers Abraham and Isaac and Jacob
and the whole choir of his holy prophets and righteous ones also enjoyed the
mercy of God. For I do not say that formerly they were benefited, but in the
sojourn of Christ they were not benefited. And why do I say this concerning
the fathers? For I will go up with the word and I will say, having been
persuaded by the Scriptures, that in the presence of Christ not only the
things on earth were benefited by his sojourn and =10.61= economy, but also
the things in the heavens. The apostle at any rate says: "having made peace
through the blood of his cross, whether the things on earth, or the things in
the heavens"; and if he made peace, why do you hesitate to accept that the
visitation came about also toward the fathers in the sojourn of Christ, and
that his arrival into Hades worked this, in order that it might be fulfilled:
"to do mercy with our fathers; and to remember the covenant that came about
toward Abraham." "To give us," he says, "to be delivered without fear from the
hand of our enemies." Often some are delivered from the hand of enemies, but
with fear. But now the sojourn of Christ has made us =10.62= to be delivered
"without fear from the hand of the enemies"; for we did not perceive the plot
of the enemies, but as it were suddenly it took us not perceiving the plot of
the enemies; and if one must speak thus, being without perception it
transferred us from those "unto his lot and portion." Wherefore we also ought
"to serve him in holiness and righteousness before him," which comes about
through the fulfillment of all his holy commandments, not for a little time
only, but "all the days of our life." "And you, little child," he says; I was
seeking by myself, why =10.63= he did not speak the prophecy concerning John
as concerning him, but as to him; for he says: "And you, little child, shall
be called prophet of the Most High." For it is superfluous to speak to one who
does not hear; why ever then does he say these things as to one hearing?
Perhaps then John, having taken on a paradoxical birth (as an archangel having
proclaimed this and as Mary after having conceived the Lord being present to
Elizabeth and to him already being generated) already had also a paradoxical
=10.64= following from the beginning. And if you disbelieve whether John
follows together with being generated the prophecy and heard the "And you,
little child, shall be called prophet of the Most High," see the more
paradoxical, that "he leaped in exultation in the womb, as the voice of the
greeting came about." And if being in the womb he leaped and rejoiced, why do
you disbelieve if, having been generated, together with being generated he
heard the father prophesying and saying to him: "And you, little child, shall
be called prophet of the Most High; for you shall go before before the face of
the Lord to prepare his ways"? Therefore more quickly the father prophesied to
him being present, since as a prophet he knew =10.65= that after a little he
is not going to have him being nourished with himself, but living "in the
deserts." For "the little child was in the deserts until the day of his
showing to Israel." For John was a paradoxical thing, concerning whom it was
also said: "I send my angel before your face"; as then an angel sent he heard
the father prophesying the things written. =11.66= For "to increase" is
twofold: the bodily, where not even the choice of a man cooperates, and the
spiritual, where choice lies as cause for the increase. He recorded then the
better increasing; for the <wh>ole of what is said is of this sort: "he
increased in spirit"; for it is not


that the spirit remaining in itself increased, of what sort it was from the
beginning, but the spirit increased for itself, and as it increased and became
more day by day, his soul also increased together correspondingly with the
spirit. And if God commands, saying protectively: "Increase and multiply,"
