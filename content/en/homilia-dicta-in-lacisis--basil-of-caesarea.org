HOMILY DELIVERED AT LACIZIS.

The enemy contends, striving by the outward tumult to outsound our discourse;
but we, not being ignorant of his thoughts, let us turn our ears and minds to
the things said here, pitying those left out of the hearing, not going away
together with those outside in mind; but where we are with our bodies, there
let us also be with our hearts. Gather up for me the memories of the spiritual
oracles that were read to you from early morning, teachings profitable to the
soul, healings of souls. Remember the psalmic teachings; collect for me the
proverbial counsels; search out the beauty of the histories; add to these the
apostolic exhortations. Upon all set as a kind of crown the memory of the
evangelical sayings, so that having taken some gain through all you may
return, each toward that to which he happens to be gladly disposed, having
received from this the benefit from the Spirit. Thus altogether in a
many-personed church, as many as are the differences of persons, so many also
of the dispositions; as many as of the ages, so many also of the sins. And the
devices of the devil are of many kinds and many ways; he draws one in one way,
another in another; toward whatever he sees more inclined, toward that he uses
his own baits. =31.1440= Does someone stand here having a soul damaged by
envy? Would that there were no one; and this is a prayer fitting for me. But
since it is hard in human nature that there not be some one of the passions,
small or greater, let each pick out the benefit from the things said. Are you
envious? take the healing. Are you irascible? the cure lies before you. For he
discourses concerning all these things. And if possible, we will say a few
things, plucking from each of the things said. Are you greedy? here is the
cure. Are you proud? here you will tame the pride; here you will chastise the
height of the soul, if you will. Drawing all the goods of the spiritual words
as from a gushing spring, having thus richly poured them upon yourself, go
home. A first saying has been spoken in Proverbs, according to the proverbial
form, obscurity having been laid on for the exercise of our mind, that "He who
looks on smooth things will be shown mercy." Did you snatch the meaning of the
saying from itself, or do you seek, being a labor-loving hearer, how the one
looking on smooth things will be praised? For praiseworthy are the things
according to choice. But we men see not such things as we wish, but such as
the nature of the things seen is. So that if something is rough according to
the surface, such as it is by nature, such it is to be seen; but if we cast
our eyes upon the smooth of the things seen, whence is the praise for the one
seeing the smooth things? Smooth things are those that have a leveled surface;
but rough is that which is composed unevenly at first sight. Since then the
nature of things seen is uneven, how does Scripture praise the one looking on
smooth things above the one looking on the rough? And does it condemn
mountains, and crags, and ravines, and density of wood, and continuity of crop
making the surface rough, or also the sea often roughened by winds, or also
earth being split by the plow, and instead of smoothness being cut into rough
things? Will the one seeing these things be condemned? And where is the just
judgment of God, if we are condemned for things we suffer involuntarily? If I
see the nature of the earth being uneven (for as it was created, such it is
seen), am I condemned for these things? But understand for me loftily, "He who
looks on smooth things." There stand before you differences of brothers: one
poor, one rich; one a stranger, one a household member, either being judged as
you sit in judgment, or needing as you give a share. If you sit judging,

do not look on uneven things, not the rich as high, and the poor as low. If
there stand at your door the one seeking to have his need set right, not even
so look on uneven things. Do not say: This one =31.1441= is a friend, this one
a kinsman, this one a benefactor; that one a stranger, an alien, unknown.
Looking on uneven things, you will not be shown mercy; look on smooth things.
Nature is one, a man both this one and that one. Need is one, the same want in
both. Give to the brother and to the stranger.

Neither turn away the brother, and make the stranger a household brother.

Therefore one must look on smooth things. Do not prefer the friend over the
alien, the one setting right his own need. It wants you to be a comforter of
those in need, not a respecter of persons, not one who gives to the kinsman
but thrusts away the stranger. All are kinsmen, all brothers, all offspring of
one father. If you seek the spiritual father, the heavenly one; if you seek
the earthly things, mother is the earth, and from the same clay we have all
been fitted. So that sister is nature according to flesh, sister is the birth
according to spirit. He bears the same blood as you from the first man; he
received the same grace as you from the Master. Look then on smooth things,
that you may be shown mercy. Do not say: This one is rich, and worthy of
honor; that one is poor, and I make light of the poor; nor by these
distinctions distribute honors unevenly. For there is the rich man a liar, the
rich man a robber, the rich man a fornicator, the poor man righteous. Do not
then attend to the outward appearance, but going into the hidden, there
distribute the differences. Honor the one who has possessed the honorable
things. What is more honorable, virtue or wealth? Why do you judge from the
outward things, not searching out the hidden? You see that Bel outwardly had
bronze, but within clay hidden under the flashing bronze. If you see those
conspicuous in life, know that bronze shines around them, but within they are
clay, the bronze propping up what is rotten. Many have gold within, but a
potsherd lying around outside. This is the treasure covered in an earthen
vessel. This one is temperate, and that one fornicates. Who is richer, the one
having temperance, or the one who has gathered money from injustice? The
memorial of this one is inalienable; the wealth of that one is temporary. This
a thief does not dig through; that blooms today, but tomorrow withers. So look
on smooth things, equally honored according to the same; yet do not obliterate
the discernment of the honorable things, that you may be one who has been
shown mercy. "And with an irascible man do not lodge." Say =31.1444= then that
There is someone among us ambitious of glory, and not irascible. Are you
ambitious of glory? you do not look on smooth things. Are you irascible? "And
with an irascible man do not lodge." Did you see how in brief speech the
correction of sins is given, unless indeed we are struck at the ears by the
voice, but do not send on to the heart the meaning of the things spoken. But
examine: where was what is mine read? Recognize the things concerning the
irascible, you who are irascible. Say: This passion is mine; I recognize my
illness; take the healing. If you stand by the physician, and see the
costliness of the drugs laid up in the many-leaved tablets, look around for
what is stored as proper to your passion. The one who has stubbed his foot
does not seek the healing of the eye; but the one having a damaged eye seeks
the help proper to the passion. Therefore let each take from Scripture the
things akin to his own passion. Are you irascible? restrain the anger. "For an
irascible man," it says, "is not seemly." Learn from Scripture. Let Scripture
become a mirror of your face. Learn there. Since the ungodly reasoning, having
darkened, does not let you reckon how great a sin anger is, Scripture says to
you: "An irascible man is not seemly." How does the irascible man behave
unseemly? He puts off the human form, he takes on the form of a beast.
Consider the one who is angry.

His anger has boiled, and the eyes have been altered; they are not the same
eyes, he sees fire, the blood has rushed up in him, it has boiled around the
heart, it has run around the coats of the eyes, he has become bloodshot
yielding to the passion, it has altered his eyes. "An irascible man is not
seemly." If you see the angry man whetting his teeth, consider that such a one
is like a swine, showing forth the inward anger through the grinding of the
teeth. If you see the angry man foaming; if you see him uttering inarticulate
things in the heart; if you see the angry man forgetting a father and not
knowing a child's body, going unsparingly through all, in order to satisfy his
impulse, you see the unseemliness of the thing. In another's evil heal
yourself, that you may not take his unseemliness also yourself. "An irascible
man is not seemly." And, "With an irascible man do not lodge." It is an evil
thing to be bound with a dog, and to enjoy continual barking. "An irascible
man is not seemly." =31.1445= Flee the sojourns with him; for it is necessary
that you learn something of his ways. He said something insulting, he also
moved the anger in you. As a dog's bark calls forth another dog's noise, so
his voice has awakened the anger in you that was until then sleeping and at
rest, and you have become barking against one another, then standing against
one another, you let fly as from slings the words of unseemliness. He said
something insulting, you gave back with a greater excess, imitating the one
who went first. He, having received the return from the insulting word, does
not remain with the impulse, but turned and brought his own sin to a head. He
wished to become greater by saying something more grievous. And you, receiving
again, were lifted higher; and there has come a rivalry of evils. And the one
who wins in contests of evils, this one is more wretched. "An irascible man is
not seemly;" and, "With an irascible man do not lodge." "Nor dine with an
envious man."

Another passion dwelling in the life of men, and always grown onto our souls,
and consuming the heart more than rust consumes iron, is envy, in which there
are many evils, but one useful thing, that it is an evil to the one who
possesses it. For the envious man harms the one envied little, but consumes
himself with grief and groaning at the prosperity of the neighbor; and he has
not lessened the land of the neighbor, but has consumed himself with envy. "Do
not move eternal boundaries. And do not enter into the possession of orphans."
Do not enter into the possession of the orphans, that you may enter into your
own possession. And your possession is the prepared kingdom of the heavens. Do
not move the boundaries of fathers. Do not desire the neighbor's land. Do not,
adding furrow to furrow, little by little increase your own. For as much as
you widen the land from the addition of another's, by so much you prepare a
longer sin for yourself. And the land remains here, having been added to you
little by little, and having become through greed, no longer yours, but of
those after you, and you leave an unjust thing to the one who succeeds to the
life; "The earth stands unto the age," but the sin will follow you, as a
shadow attending the soul. For as the shadow follows the body, so also sin has
grown together with the soul. Do not be one multiplying sin for yourself. For
nowhere does the disease of greed stand, but it is like the nature of fire.
For fire too, whenever it takes hold of a burning, hastens to spread over all
the matter, and no one can stop it, until the matter fail. And what can hold
back the greedy =31.1448= man? He is more grievous than fire, feeding on all
things by continuity. He took the things of the neighbor; another neighbor
appeared; and he appropriated those things too. He will not attend to the
things behind, that they are many; but to the things lacking, that he has
neighbors. The things acquired do not gladden, but the things remaining
grieve; he does not attend to the enjoyment of the things gathered, but
consumes himself with the desire of the

acquisition of more. Then from this come sleepless nights, the anxieties, the
cares. As much as wealth increases, so much does it bring the anxiousness of
life.

A judge is expected, and the greedy man looks around lest he be dragged into
courts; lest an orphan publish his own tear in court. He deliberates at night,
which of the bitter advocates he will prepare, that he may hire false
testimonies; how he will storm the one in solitude, and will overpower this
one, and having stolen the truth in court; and he will consume both, both
having cheated the judge, and having defrauded the infant. These cares consume
the soul of the greedy man. A dog barks, and the rich man thinks it is a
thief. A mouse's noise, and the rich man's heart leaps; he suspects the
servant, he has all things through suspicion; he sees sons growing as
plotters, because their age presses the succession. The greedy have also
another disease. He sets his life on his fingers; one part he publishes, the
other he has buried, laying it up toward hopes that are unclear; hopes that
are not hopes. For if the laying-up were toward hopes, the preparation would
have been toward the eternal hope. But now he hides under the bed the unclear
wealth, thinking to turn aside the coming loss. But whether the need will ever
come is unclear; a time will come, however, in which you will repent, not
having managed the wealth. This is evident, and I am the one who guarantees
it.

For it is necessary to discourse concerning all the passions, reminding you;
and you, attending, to take the benefit from the words, so that, each having
stripped off sin as a serpent's old age, he might thus return stripped, and
having righteousness blooming upon him, having become another from another.
For this is an assembly. For this our fathers recognized these gatherings, so
that what we cannot learn each day, by lack of those who teach and remind,
these things coming together yearly, we may receive in a common festival; then
keep them as provisions for the time following, and having the several things
ringing in the memory, we may flee sin, and pursue the work of righteousness.
=31.1449= "Do not move eternal boundaries. And do not enter into the
possession of orphans." Do not make the children a pretext, you greedy man.
Are there children? treasure up for them an eternal treasure. And piety is a
treasure of riches. Leave to children a good memory rather than much wealth.
Make all men fathers of your child through well-doing. It is necessary that
you sometime depart from life; then you will leave your child in minority,
needing those who preside. For if you are noble and good, each will rear your
offspring as his own; for he will remember that you too became a father of
orphans; but if having lived in wickedness, and having grieved many, and
having become more grievous than any beast to those who meet you, you then
slip out of life, you have left your offspring a common enemy of the living.
For as one who has seen a scorpion's child fears, lest coming to age it
imitate the father; so also your offspring, being heirs of paternal
wickedness, before coming to age, will be plotted against by all. Why then do
you gather many plotters and enemies for your own children? And yet, even if
you lived the whole time, you ought rather to have the goodwill of the many as
a fellow-worker. But since what concerns you is unclear, leave through
well-doing many who will stand in front; lest after you have slipped out, then
the wealth which you gather not being able to be succeeded to for benefit,
each shake the head and say: How were the things badly acquired not sent on to
offspring and children? These things I say to you from human words; but the
things from the Lord, such as the Gospel will say to you, you know.

Do not make the children a pretext. Why do you put forward a fair-faced
occasion for the sin? The one who made the child also made you. The one who
furnishes you the resources will also furnish the child the courses of life.
Each will give account of his own life to the Lord. What do you know for whom
you treasure? "He treasures, and does not know for whom he will gather them."
Often the child is the occasion of the things being treasured. But there came
either a robber's plunder, or a sycophant's waste, or enemies' captivity of
the things gathered by you, or a plague striking down. For many are the
vanishings of life. Tell me: when you asked children from God, when you wished
to become a father of =31.1452= children, what did you add to the prayer? Give
me children, that, having become greedy, I may by the pretext of the children
be handed over to Gehenna? Give me children, that I may not do the
commandments? Give me children, that I may despise the Gospel? These were not
the things you said then, but you sought children as fellow-workers of life.
You received fellow-workers, partners of life; teach also with good words, and
with useful examples, of what sort it is fitting to be toward God, of what
sort you have often seen. For this is the wealth more honorable than much
money. This is the good inheritance, sent on from father to children. "And do
not enter into the possession of orphans." And, "Do not dine with an envious
man." It has also been said concerning envy. But let it be added for greater
guarding against the evil, that envy is a passion proper to the devil. For the
devil was not created devil at once, but having received an angel's authority,
he was overthrown into the nature of the demon. And he became a wicked demon,
the one characterized by this wickedness, and alienated from kinship toward
God, but turned toward the part of apostasy, since he saw man a small living
being, but more honorable than all creation. Being older than man, he saw that
all the swimming things were created by a word. For "Let the waters bring
forth," it says, "creeping things of living souls." By a word he made the
sea-monsters that dwell in the Atlantic deep.

By a word he made the land animals. By a command of God they were brought into
being. By a command came the magnitudes of the elephants, those of the camels;
horses, and oxen, and as many animals as differ in size and strength, were
created in herds by one command; and what is still more marvelous, by a
command heaven, by a command the sun, by a command herbage, by a command the
nature of the waters, both that spread above the heaven and the earth, and
that poured out in the hollows, all by a command; but man was formed with
hands. He saw the honor concerning man, the devil envied. He saw that he is
more honorable than the sun. For the one came into being by a command; but the
other was formed by the hands of God. And the one was created for man, but man
for God; man, that God might be glorified; the sun, that it might serve men.
He learned thoroughly the honor concerning man. He saw that creation is first,
but man last, because he prepared for him as for a householder a house filled.
For he did not first make man, that he might not be created =31.1453= poor,
but he made heaven, having created a natural shelter, he spread the earth
beneath, preparing a strong floor for the feet of man. He made it bloom with
all kinds of yields of herbs and fruits. All things ready for man; the beasts
of burden were made ready in herds; creation was created. Some were prepared
as sharing pasture; others wild toward exercise of the body. Some to be
acquired by toil, that they might become beloved; others readily present, that
he might be served by creation from the start. He saw that the earth of itself
prepared the fruit for them. All creation awaited those who would enjoy, and
man was brought in to a banquet. He saw that the philanthropic God was not
satisfied with the enjoyment of the earth; but wishing man to be his own
special dwelling, his own adornment, he set him there in paradise. The devil
became wicked seeing much enjoyment flowing around

man, angels as pedagogues attending him; God having become of the same tongue
with men, conversing from his own voice, the infant child being trained from
every side, that he might run up to the likeness of God; since he learned man
thoroughly, since he saw that the Lord was calling this small living being
toward equal honor with the angels; leading him up through virtue, and through
temperance of the things according to life toward the perfection of the soul.
For as long as man was alone, the devil had no hold, but when the woman was
created, a tender living being, the Creator necessarily having implanted the
natural tenderness, that the infants might be easily nourished by the
philanthropic one. For if the woman were austere, she would not have embraced
the weeping infant to her breasts; she would not have neglected her own food,
but held the breast toward the benefit of the nursing child. Now indeed the
compassions of mothers often drive sleep from the eyelids, whenever the infant
is a little troubled.

Therefore that the infant might be reared, the womanly nature was brought in
tender, tender and philanthropic. Attending then to the tender and the
yielding, the devil made the ease toward virtue into ease toward vice. This
then is the proper evil of the devil, envy; envy that cannot be spoken out,
that does not admit of healing. The one whose head aches tells the physician
=31.1456= the headache; the one sick with envy, what should he say? Do the
brother's goods grieve me? This is the truth; but each is ashamed of the
words. Why do you groan? a proper evil, or another's good? For feeding dogs we
tame them; but doing good to the envious man we make him more grievous. For he
does not rejoice at whatever good things he may suffer; but he is grieved at
your plenty, that you have matter from which you will fill his need. Do not
then fall together into the deceit of the devil; he envied you, he envied; he
banished you from paradise. Through him thorns, through him sweat, through him
the place of exile; into which having fallen, do not forget the ancient
fatherland.

Remember your own nobility; remember the fatherland from which you were
banished. Remember the one who damaged you with such great things; do not make
a truce with him, nor ever accept converse with him. For he has wronged in two
ways, both from the beginning he cast out, and now he cuts off the return. He
envied you the first goods through a woman, and again by devices according to
the woman he hinders the restoration. He invented fornication, that you may
not enter into paradise. The first damage was not enough for him, that he
overthrew; but he invents second methods, holding back our course. He steals
through the lie, since the lie is a wall to those traveling into paradise. His
practices are countless. His offshoots, cruelty, roughness, greed, railing;
all things that the word of truth hates, these are of the devil.

A hater of man, since he is also a fighter against God. He hated first, he
became stiff-necked against God almighty, he despised the Master, he was
alienated from God. Since he saw man according to the image and likeness of
God, not being able to turn against God, he emptied his own wickedness into
the image of God; as if some man being angry should stone the image, since he
cannot the king, striking the wood that has the imitation. I have seen the
most man-hating of the beasts in the stadia often, either I saw, or I heard;
for let the word be safe. Except that these very leopards have a certain
natural wrath toward man, and they are by nature especially wont to leap at
the eyes of men. Those then who mock the madness of the beast show forth in
paper an image as a man. And she, not reasoning because of the excessive
impulse, as a man, tears the paper, and there shows the hatred of man. Thus
also the devil showed in the image the hatred of God, since he could not lay
hold of God. Thus the war against us has proof that the wicked one is a
fighter against God, and

first wars against the Master. That one brought man down from Jerusalem to
Jericho, from the high things to the hollows. Jerusalem is upon the
mountainous country =31.1457=; but Jericho below by the salt sea. If any of
you knows the place, he knows the truth of the things said, that Jericho has
the hollows of Palestine, but Jerusalem is fixed upon the peak, holding the
height of the mountain that stands up through all that country.

From the high things then man came to the things below, that he might fall
among the robbers; having gone out from the safety according to Jerusalem,
that he might become easy to catch for the robbers according to the
wilderness, who laid on blows, and stripped. First blows, and then stripping.
A blow of the soul is sin; stripping of the soul is putting off the garment of
incorruption. And sin is destructive of the grace given us through the washing
of regeneration. Fornication, a blow, adultery, another blow, envy a blow,
each of these a blow, a blow striking the very vital parts, a blow thrown in
by robbers, by demons who cooperate with us toward the sins. After the blows
the one struck was stripped. If the saying were bodily, they would first strip
with care for the garment, and then lay on blows. Or having stripped they
would have laid on blows, that both the garment might be preserved, and the
one struck might be beaten. Now however the blows precede the stripping, that
you may learn that sin precedes the putting off of the gift, that given you
through the philanthropy of the Lord. To him be the glory unto the ages of
ages. Amen.
