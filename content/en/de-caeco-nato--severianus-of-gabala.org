* On the Man Born Blind

On zeal and piety, and on the man born blind.
1. The Word of God is a fountain of light; for being full of light, and
flashing forth light, he lights and flashes round the minds of the faithful.
For he shines from himself and in himself, and he illumines those who enjoy
him; and he not only lights the mind of the faithful, but he also grants the
name of light. For divine Scripture justly names those who are companions of
ignorance, and who live with unbelief, darkness, since they do not receive the
lamp of the truth, that they may be sons of light. For this reason the divine
apostle Paul also says: We are not sons of night, nor of darkness, but sons of
light and of day. And we are truly sons of light, we who belong to the gospel
word spoken through the mouth of God; for I am, says Christ, the light of the
world; he who follows me shall not walk in the darkness, but shall have the
light of life. For that is a word of light which, if it is believed by us,
prepares one to become a son of light. Hence the Savior also says: While you
have the light, believe in the light, that you may become sons of light. For
as among bodies this sensible light is, so among souls is the intelligible
Word of God; and what darkness is in the night, this ignorance is in souls
that have gone astray. For this reason the blessed David also writes
concerning such people, saying: They have not known, nor have they understood;
they go about in darkness. So then God also calls the synagogue that has been
baptized in ignorance, and that does not wish to look up toward the knowledge
of the spiritual teaching, night, because of the ignorance that lives with it.
For when God said to the Jews through the prophet: I have likened your mother
to night; and explaining what sort of night he means, like darkness, he added:
My people has been likened to a people that has no knowledge. For this reason
They have not known, nor have they understood; they go about in night. But
those were so; and those who have received the light of the teaching of
Christ, such people are made light. For our God is light; and his Word, who
was begotten from him before the ages without passion, is called light; and
the holy and consubstantial and life-originating Spirit, light. Light, and
light, and light; but one light; and the soul that has received the word is
named light. But God is by nature a light of three hypostases, and we by
participation in him; and again our Lord Jesus Christ himself is light, of
whom the evangelist John says: He was the true light, which lights every man
coming into the world. But being light from unspeakable light, he also sends
forth the law full of light, according to the prophet who says: Your law is a
lamp to my feet, and a light to my paths. When then the soul has received the
luminous word of the truth, it too is made light, and it hears from Paul: For
you were once darkness, but now light in the Lord. And when the mind receives
the gospel light, it is named light also according to the faith; and likewise
also according to the works. Let your light shine, he says, before men, that
they may see your good works, and glorify your Father who is in heaven. For it
is necessary that the one who has received light of faith come also to works
of light. And the soul of the faithful must not only be light, but also shine
according to the works before men; for this reason the Savior also says: Let
your light shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify
your Father who is in the heavens. And see the wonder; for the light from God
is simple, but it has different rays not by nature, but by the operations; and
the shining teaching of God is sent upon every faithful soul, according to
what was said: And let the


brightness of the Lord our God be upon us, that our works may be directed with
him. The brightness is one, but coming to us it produces many brightnesses,
brightness of almsgiving, brightness of temperance, brightness of hospitality,
brightness of affection. With God there is one brightness; but this, being
sent to us, produces by the operations different =59.544= gleams; granting to
one brightness of prophecy, to another brightness of apostleship, to another
brightness of martyrdom. There the brightness is one; but among us many,
according to what was said: With you is the beginning in the day of your power
in the brightnesses of your saints. Why then does Paul enumerate, and say: Are
all apostles? are all prophets? are all teachers? Paul does not divide thus
simply, but as he finds souls that are fit, he does not portion according to
authority, but according to the confession of the faith; not that he gives
contrary to his own will, nor contrary to the wish, but that his will is not
without judgment, but according to the charisms of the gifts. For just as the
flowery and well-dyed parts of the royal garment receive a solar ray, yet send
up different gleams, and of whatever sort the beauty of the dye may be, such a
good color it works, and just as by the dye set before it even the beauty of
the air is re-dyed into gold, or into purple, or into green, or into some
other of the colors, the ray not being divided, but of the garment the
arrangement of the ray being shaped by the light; with such flowers always the
beauty of the Church shines, having the royal garment, and arrayed in a
heavenly and varied robe; as the psalmist also says: Daughters of kings are in
your honor; the queen is present at your right in a vesture of gold, clothed,
embroidered. How has she been embroidered? By the gifts of the Holy Spirit,
according to the voice of Paul, God working with signs and wonders and various
powers, and distributions of the Holy Spirit. But what shall I name the Church
of Christ? A bride arrayed, a queen made beautiful, a paradise in full leaf, a
meadow teeming with good fruit, and if I say anything else, I speak less than
the dignity. For this reason the prophet Isaiah, taking up her voice from the
person of the Church, said: Let my soul rejoice in the Lord; for he has
clothed me with a garment of salvation. And since it was in a figure, and he
could not speak the whole beauty of the Church, he sets a difference upon it,
saying: As a bride he has adorned me with ornament, and as a bridegroom he has
set a mitre upon me, and as earth increasing her produce, and as a garden of
herbs its seeds. For a garden truly closed and a paradise is the Church of the
living God; and I speak of a paradise not according to that ancient one, but
far higher than that. For there a serpent reigned, but here Christ reigns. 2.
Do not attend to the credibility of the name, but to the power of the thing.
For neither is a paradise wonderful, if it does not have a commandment of God
that is kept; nor are the cheap among places insulted, if they have the law of
God who reigns. What is better? what is stronger? a paradise, or a dunghill?
Yet paradise did not help Adam because of the transgression; and a dunghill
did not harm Job because of the endurance; Adam is put to shame in paradise
because of the disobedience, but Job is crowned on the dunghill because of the
endurance. For nowhere does the credibility of the places contribute, unless
the good mind runs with it; and heaven is higher than paradise, but the devil
falls from there. The earth is deep, and heaven is lifted up; the devil falls
from heaven having transgressed, according to the, I beheld Satan as lightning
fallen from heaven; but the poor are raised from earth, according to the, He
who raises the poor from the earth, and lifts the needy from the dunghill. Do
you see that it is not the difference of the places that grants us the
advantages, but everywhere faith, and endurance, and hope weave the crowns for
the worthy? Nothing of a bedchamber


is more august, nothing more naked than a theater; what did the adultery in
the bedchamber profit the unchaste Egyptian woman? what did wrestling naked in
the theater harm the first martyr Thecla? She did not sin in being stripped,
the holy one is not insulted, but she is crowned. And the one is crowned in
the =59.545= theater, and the other is posted up in the bridal chamber. What
did the idols profit, and the worship of things that are not? I mean the sizes
of the temples; and what did the cheapness of the tombs harm the martyrs? was
not the error put to shame in greatness, and the things of the martyrs raised
in humiliation? Thus the Church of the living God is a paradise of delight. In
that paradise the serpent danced, and deceived; in this paradise the Savior
says: Behold I have given you the authority to tread upon serpents and
scorpions, and upon all the power of the enemy, and nothing shall harm you. In
the paradise the way of life was shut to Adam because of the transgression;
and that God may show that the virtuous soul is not hindered, outside paradise
she receives the greater things than those in paradise. Paradise is shut
against Adam, and Peter is entrusted with the keys of heaven; Adam is cast out
of paradise, and Paul is caught up to the third heaven; the tree of life is
walled off from Adam; for God said, he says, Lest Adam touch the tree of life.
There life has been forbidden, and the tree of life was forbidden from the one
who transgressed; here the tree of the cross was planted, and all who wish
gather eternal life. In paradise after the transgression Adam hides from God,
struck by his conscience; he who seeks all things calls him back, and says:
Adam, where are you? For did you not know where he is? Adam, where are you?
Convicting him of his lawlessness he says, Where are you? instead of, In what
things are you? Did the deceit of the serpent bring you equality with God? did
you see how you have been stripped? Where are you? I heard your voice, and I
hid. That one hides in paradise; the apostles say, We all with unveiled face
behold as in a mirror the glory of the Lord, from glory to glory. Did you see
boldness outside paradise? did you see shame inside paradise? In paradise
after the transgression God clothed Adam with coats of skin. Let us see what
he has been clothed with. That one there put on a coat; here, As many as were
baptized into Christ, have put on Christ. There, paradise having been shut,
the Cherubim, and the turning sword forbidding the way of paradise; and it was
as a wall and a barrier to Adam, not permitting him to enter because of the
disobedience in paradise after the transgression. I do not speak in accusation
of paradise, but that that place befitting God did not help at all the one who
did not wish to conduct himself with a good mind. And there was a wall of fire
circling paradise, and not permitting the one who transgressed to enter; that
God may show that it was not the fire that was hostile to him, but the
transgression prevented Adam from entering the wall of fire; and a chariot of
fire snatched Elijah into heaven. The Church of God does not lie when she
says: God clothed me with a garment of salvation, and as a bride he adorned me
with ornament, as earth increasing her produce, and as a great garden of the
truth. This is the great. paradise, the ecumenical Church. Christ reigns, and
in legislating is believed, unless someone is found in the Church sick with
heretical error, under the teaching of the serpents, I mean of the heretical
error, and is cast out from here, as Adam from paradise. For just as there the
one who was persuaded by the teaching of the serpent was banished from
paradise; so also in the Church those who are not persuaded by Paul, who are
not taught by Peter, but who are persuaded by the teachings of the serpents,
are cast out of this paradise. From where is this clear? Paul says: For I am
jealous over you with a jealousy of God; for I betrothed you to one husband,
to present a pure virgin to Christ. But I fear lest somehow, as the serpent
deceived Eve, so your thoughts may be corrupted. But there is no room here for
teaching of a serpent, while the great shepherd with the staff of the faith


crushes the head of the serpents. For as a shepherd our wonderful father
crushes the heads of the dragons with the gospel faith and with the hope in
Christ; and as an excellent planter and gardener, he feeds the plants of the
Church, but he roots out, if there is any bastard seed and shoot; the disease
of impiety, the Hellenic error, he hands over as thorns to fire; and the
heretical error, having plucked it out as couch-grass, he casts out, =59.546=
clothed with zeal of God, which the many do not know. For some are accustomed
to play the sophist against the Church and against the holy shepherds, as also
against this wonderful father; and many in a form of gentleness introduce
idleness, and insult zeal, and try to blunt the word of God that is in its
prime. For one may find someone in a form of gentleness saying: For what have
we in common with the Hellenes? does he wish to be saved? let him be saved.
The one who says these things has not yet been wounded with zeal of piety; the
one who sees a man perishing, and does not mourn that man's destruction, and
set it down as his own loss, does not know zeal of piety, has not been wounded
with darts of truth. 3. The one then who sees a man perishing, and is not
zealous as Paul says somewhere, Who is weak, and I am not weak? this man is
not a disciple of Paul. Let him compare the achievements of the Father, and he
will find him in accord with the apostolic voices: Who is weak, and I am not
weak? who is scandalized, and I do not burn? Those who think thus are brothers
of the apostles. For a righteous man is not downcast only in the things in
which he sins, but also in the things in which he sees others sinning, as
David says: Despondency seized me from sinners who forsake your law. If anyone
is downcast at the sins of others, he has zeal of piety. For the righteous
man, when he sets his own life right, weeps and mourns to wipe out his own
sin; and then, having ceased from his own sin, he mourns thereafter the sins
of others, as also Paul: Lest perhaps when I come I shall mourn many of those
who have sinned before, and have not repented. Paul mourns those who have
sinned, and have not repented; Samuel mourns Saul, so that God says: How long
do you mourn over Saul? Do you see a soul perishing, and you do not mourn? If
someone sees a dead man being carried out, and does not weep, he is convicted
as harsh and hard and beastlike; if you see a soul being baptized in impiety,
and being condemned to eternal death, and remaining in unbelief, do you not
weep, tell me, and mourn? and is this of a devout soul? All the other sins
then the well-minded man must mourn, but especially, when he sees a soul
baptized in the impiety of Hellenism. Over such a soul one must not only
mourn, but also burn and be set on fire with the zeal; that he may become a
disciple of Paul. For while Paul was spending time, he says, in Athens, his
spirit was set on fire, as he saw that the city was full of idols. And why do
the saints think these things, as also this wonderful father was somewhere an
avenger of piety? For just as there is a king who holds on earth the inhabited
world under him, and he has in each court of the palace those who advocate; so
God has the faithful shepherds as avengers of piety. For many of the Hellenes
often, when they mock, say, when they hear Christians saying to them, Why do
you blaspheme? why are you impious? they immediately mock: See the avengers of
God; can God not avenge himself? But he does not avenge himself (for he does
not insult the divinity), but through faithful servants he scourges the
ungrateful slaves. Does God have need of avengers? Yes; not because he himself
is weak, but that he may open for you a door of zeal. For if he did all things
through himself, the way of life would be shut to the zealot. Did Pharaoh
never fight against God, and was he sunk in impiety; and was a scourge nowhere
brought, until the faithful Moses stooped? Did not the polytheist error
flourish together with the false priests and the false prophets, and was there
nowhere a blow from God, until Elijah the zealot arose? The saints are
confessedly avengers of God. Therefore Paul also says: In readiness


we have to avenge every disobedience, when your obedience is fulfilled. And
further the wonderful Deborah among women, who had a woman's weakness, and
prophesied manliness of virtue, of whom Scripture says, and Deborah arose, a
mother in Israel, she became a mother of the people through piety, and she was
in such prime, that even the generals when war had come, those around Barak,
said to the woman: If you do not come into war with us, we do not come. They
did not take courage from the manliness of the woman, but from the power of
the prophecy. Many went out as fellow-workers of the counsel of God, and they
cut off the aliens. That blessed Deborah then, bearing the truth, says: Lord,
those who love you, as a rising of the sun; but your enemies, as the wiping of
a lamp. =59.547= Lord, those who love you, as a rising of the sun; since the
sun is never quenched; but your enemies, as the wiping of a lamp; for a lamp
both has a temporary light, and is quenched again. Then she praises the
general who served the counsel of God, and after these things she commands
that those who dwell in Mazor be cursed, because they did not go out to the
help of the Lord. Did God then need help? Yes. Those who help piety must
appear. So then this wonderful father also does not help God as one who is
failing, but having been appointed to this he avenges piety, showing in the
body toward destruction, but putting souls toward power. For the arrows of the
mighty are sharpened. And do not wonder, if the shepherds of Christ and
teachers of piety avenge the truth; but even if someone is not a teacher, nor
entrusted with a teacher's throne, but has simply received the word of Christ,
and is in the rank of a disciple, he ought to have zeal of God, and to render
returns to the benefactor in speaking freely, and to speak on behalf of the
truth, and to contend against impiety, that he may hear from the holy
Scripture: Child, contend for the truth, And the Lord God will fight for you.
It is no wonder, if saints avenge piety. Often he calls something through
gentleness, as God who is good, and the prophets do not permit piety to be
wounded from the gentleness of God. God says through Moses: Say to Pharaoh,
Send forth my people; and if you do not wish, behold I will send the signs,
and I will strike you. Moses was struck at the word, and a thought is given to
the pious man, and he says: Do not think that he himself has a people from
equal honor, and God a people. And being in agony, lest perhaps the great
goodness and gentleness of God harm him, and he think to be a rival polity to
God, having heard, Your people, and, My people, he raises the proclamation.
Thus says the Lord the God of the Hebrews: Send forth my people; and if you do
not wish, behold I will send forth the signs, and I will strike you and your
attendants; that you may learn, that The earth is the Lord's and the fullness
of it. The earth heard a people and a people, and he did not follow the word,
but he says, All the earth is the Lord's. For even if God used gentleness, yet
this man does not betray the master's authority. For this reason God also
spoke thus, that the zeal of the pious man might be stripped. But the faithful
also always love to go beyond the words of God; for the Savior says to the
disciples: Who do men say that I the Son of man am? One of his disciples says:
You are the Christ the Son of the living God. Let the zeal of the Holy Spirit
teach you also to be zealots, and to suffer for piety, and to contend the good
contest for righteousness. 4. For it is worthy that good disciples be
imitators of a teacher, as Paul also says: Become imitators of me, as I also
of Christ. Do you wish to learn that even the simple disciple and the one who
has the rank of a disciple ought to have zeal of piety? Hear how Christ healed
the man born blind; or rather let us hear the history itself from the
beginning. For after many things said by the evangelist, he began to speak
thus: As Jesus passed by he saw a man blind from birth. And his disciples
asked him saying, and the rest. And having spat,


and having made clay, He anointed his eyes, and said to him: Go, wash in the
pool of Siloam; and having washed he saw again. The Jews knew him, and they
say: Is this not the one who sat for alms being blind? Others said, Yes;
others, No, but he is like him. That man said, that I am he. But the Pharisees
and the rulers of the Jews, seeing that the wonder has many to draw to Christ,
being bitten from the zeal of impiety, and being struck with the sting of the
truth, call in the one who was once blind. And one could see a wicked theater,
piety being avenged, and wicked judges judging, and a great advocate
contending with them, and God awarding and overseeing. The children of the
Jews call the one who was once blind and who saw again, and they set him in
the midst of the council, and they say to him: =59.548= Say how you saw again,
and who opened your eyes, and what he did to you? They said these things, not
that they might learn, but that by the form of the question they might bring
fear. For often the form of the question becomes a teacher to the one asked to
answer, or to be silent; as if one should say to someone about to bear witness
to a matter: Do you know the case? were you present? from where do you know
the matter? and the form teaches him to deny. So also those men: How did you
see again? who opened your eyes? But he, taking less thought of the threat,
held rather to the truth, and says: A certain man, called Jesus, having made
clay anointed my eyes and said to me: Go, wash into Siloam. And having gone I
washed, and I saw again. He did not know the truth, but he recognized the
benefaction; he did not yet know the dignity of the benefactor. A certain man
having made clay anointed my eyes, and I see. The Jews did not believe that he
was blind, and saw again, until they called the parents of him who had seen
again. The exception of wickedness brought the truth into doubt. They call the
parents of the blind man; wickedness gathers the witnesses against itself. The
parents of the blind man were called. Again the same form of the question: Is
this your son, whom you say, that he was born blind? How then does he now see?
Those men, having understood the manner of the wickedness, that they bring
threats, say to them: We know that this is our son; and that he was born
blind, we know; but how he now sees, or who opened his eyes, we do not know;
he has age, ask him; he will speak the things concerning himself. And see the
wisdom of God. That they may not except against the voice of him who saw
again, either as being young, or as not knowing how to speak, as in the common
courts, God arranged that he first be shown of lawful age. He has age himself,
he says; ask him; since those under age need advocates, but those who bear
perfect age become advocates of themselves. His parents answered and said,
that This is our son, and that he was born blind, we know. The parents
confessed, that there might not be a doubt, that he was not blind. He has age,
ask him; he will speak the things concerning him. They call him again, he
says, a second time, and they say to him: What did he do to you? how did he
open your eyes? That man then, having put on zeal of God, spits out the
question, and as unworthy of an answer, he does not think them worthy of a
word, but what does he say to them? I told you already, and you did not hear;
why do you wish to hear me again? do you also wish to become his disciples?
And he imitates the teaching of Christ, he who has not yet heard the teaching
of Christ. For the Jews at the dedication, having surrounded the Savior in the
porch of Solomon, said to him: How long do you take up our soul? If you are
the Christ, tell us openly. He says to them: I told you, and you did not hear;
why do you wish to hear again? And see the piety of an advocate. Since he
attended to the wickedness of the Jews, that they do all things, and they put
on a threat, and shake fear at him so that he may not confess him as teacher,
he says to them: I told you already, and you did not


hear; why do you wish to hear again; do you also wish to become his disciples?
By saying, Do you also? he showed that the I is confessed. Let every Christian
who comes down from Hellenism have this voice. For when a Hellene meets you
who has an ancient custom of error, and says to you, that you have become a
Christian, say to him, Yes; do you also wish to become one? The aim then of
the one who saw again both in soul and in body was such. I told you already,
and you did not hear; why do you wish to hear me again? do you also wish to
become his disciples? Instead of, You are so far from persuading me otherwise,
that I rather expect you, having been changed, to confess Christ himself. Then
what do the Jews say to him? They reviled him, and said: You are a disciple of
that man, but we are disciples of Moses. We know that God spoke to Moses; but
this man we do not know from where he is. Consider the multitude of those who
contradict, and the power of the one who has stood against them. And the
advocate of the truth was not silent again, but =59.549= he stood, For in this
it is wonderful, saying, that you do not know from where he is, and he opened
my eyes. If this man were not from God, he could do nothing. He stood
contending, and God was overseer and awarder; many terrible men against one
strong, an unbelieving cloud against one drop of faith. From the age, he says,
it has not been heard that anyone opened the eyes of one born blind. If this
man were not from God, he could do nothing. Since you pride yourselves, he
says, in the teaching of Moses. He does not mention Moses for the time, that
he may not provoke; but he says, From the age it has not been heard that
anyone opened the eyes of one born blind, that is, What other prophet opened
them? not even seeing the superiority, nor a comparison. And as much as this
man held to the truth, so much those men did not cease from shamelessness.
Since they could not deny, they were eager thereafter to cover, and they say:
Give glory to God; we know that this man is a sinner. The glory of the Son is
the glory of the Father, and the glory is undivided. He contends having the
good contest toward the Jews. They say to him, Give glory to God. To which? Of
our law? And is it just that one man benefit, and another be glorified? Give
glory to God. Another is the benefactor, and am I to give glory to another?
They confessed that Jesus made clay, and anointed his eyes; the one who opened
is confessed, and am I to carry up the glory to another? And why should I give
glory to God? Because this work is of God. See then unwillingly you confess
that Christ is God. Give glory to God. For if the work is of God, and the
glory is owed to him; the maker of the work is himself God. Give glory to God.
We know that this man is a sinner. The one who was once blind answered them:
If he is a sinner, I do not know; one thing I know, that being blind I now
see. 5. Up to this, as he spoke freely, they commanded him to be cast out. And
they cast him out, says the evangelist, outside. Blessed is he who has been
separated from a synagogue of men doing evil. And see what happens. Since he
contended the good contest, and advocated for the glory of God, and confessed
the Savior gloriously with much boldness; and his theater was filled with the
wrestlings, the wrestler was cast out, not having received the crown, and the
awarder meets him outside. For Jesus heard, he says, that they cast him out;
and having found him he says to him. O the speech of the one who found him;
whom error destroys, truth finds. And why did he meet him? The Savior saw a
soul full of good mind; he saw a soul not yet known as speaking for God, but
thinking it was for some faithful prophet; for he is asked about Christ. And
what does he say about him? That he is a prophet. But the Savior said this: If
thinking him a prophet, and supposing him a man, he contends thus, what will
he not do, if he learns the glory of the divinity? If supposing him a man, and
naming him a prophet he brought such advocacy for the truth, and took up so
great a contest not yet having been catechized, but from the benefaction alone


having been enlightened; consider him, that if thinking to advocate for a man
he did not stand aside from the truth, if he learns to say in the gospel way:
In the beginning was the Word; and the Word was with God, and the Word was
God; what will he be? For this reason then he set him right into knowledge,
having received the contest, and having sealed the faith for him. The Savior
meets him; for this man did not know the character. For when he sent him in
the pool of Siloam to wash, he sent him blind; and when he returned seeing, he
did not see the Savior being mixed with the crowd, but immediately, before
seeing the Master, having been called into the contest he contended. And the
Master made clay before he washed, and anointed his eyes, that he might fill
up what was lacking. When then they cast him out, Christ having found him said
to him: Do you believe in the Son of God? For you must not believe as in a
prophet while contending, but as in a Son of God. That man, not =59.550=
having recognized the face, recognized the voice. Do you believe, he says, in
the Son of God? He recognized the voice, and with great joy he cried, And who
is he, Lord, that I may believe in him? Why do you say to the faithful man: Do
you believe in the Son of God? I know that your word is true; yet even if you
tell me the knowledge, I do not doubt to believe you. And who is he, Lord,
that I may believe in him? And the one who knows beforehand answered: And you
