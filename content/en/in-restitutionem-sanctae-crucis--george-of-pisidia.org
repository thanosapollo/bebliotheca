Extempore verses toward the reading that took place of the commands for the
sake of the restoration of the precious woods
1 O Golgotha, leap for joy; all creation again honors you and calls you
God-receiving; for the emperor, having
arrived from Persia, shows the Cross planted in you; applaud him with
song-worthy words; but if the stones have no mouth, prepare new branches of
palms for the meeting of the new victory-bearer; he has taken away from you
the sport of error; for if he himself had not recovered the woods, the
bold-mouthed magi would have had laughter; but now having learned their power
in deed they have come to know, I think, how fire conversely is turned to ash
by woods mystically kindled. Well done to you, most mighty one, for the zeal
and the contests of the battle toward peace; and how shall we bless you with
flowing words, whom grace shows as blessed, how you recovered the stolen
woods, having put to sleep the serpent that snatched them? You have been shown
a hero to God, having snatched the golden fleece from the slain dragon; for
you yourself did not slay the beast with Median drugs, but by fixing it with
the wood. Cease then, bastard Israelites, from the ancestral unbelief among
you. The Cross has come, received in kingly fashion with supplications,
prayers, tears, vigils, with lame, articulate and speaking organs, having
fitted a great trophy to the emperor, enemies longing and fearing still more;
for it did not wish to sojourn with the barbarian, even if, punishing the sin
then, it had gone abroad into a foreign land; but it has come back up and
gathers the children from the formless and bastard sojourn and is worshipped
and glorified still more, as chastening and redeeming still more. O Paul,
initiate of the ineffable words, it is no longer possible to call the Cross
foolishness to the nations; for it has been found effective and has appeared
terrible and still more to the nations. Now, O emperor, you have the authority
of our mystery-rule, and every land and City and the whole world from one
accord blesses the grace given to you. Let great Constantine hymn you as such;
for another blessing you will not suffice. Appear, Constantine, to Rome again;
applaud the child, how having taken your substance mixed he showed it saved.
It is right that you now, leaving the city above, dance together with us in
this one below; for you had a gloomy and afflicted spirit until you knew the
Cross returning and victory-bearing, which you first found hidden in its own
place, but your child brought back not only hidden but having gone into
Persian furnaces, to the places from the beginning; and you have a child from
God's providence; so that Constantine may be found again fortified by the
life-giving woods. How I am struck with amazement, how with reverse swings a
pious autocrat before the emperors reduced the fire to soot with fiery wood,
which Chosroes despised as wood, but found a spear of his own heart. Who,
winged with words or having flown through, would assault so great a mystical
strategy? And now you walk, purified in soul, a laughing road, dancing
together with Angels. The Cross in you has appeared to the adversaries a new
ark, and more than the ark; for the one inflicted a wound on the barbarians as
far as the arrows, but the stretching of the wood sent living arrows against
them; and Parthians burn Persians, and Scythian slays Slav and is slain again,
and stained with their own murders they have much confusion into one battle;
but you yourself are silent, bearing crown and scepter, like an umpire of the
wrestlers in the midst, having wrestled with many, but now having loosed the
fight; and henceforth those in the middle of the contests look toward your nod
from an opposing slant; and where there is a nod, the wrestling has justice;
but if you nod against, even the



power of victory nods along with you, and seeing your nod [...] you delight,
playing at swords without danger, and you smile at the theatre of the
barbarians, beholding those who formerly pursued as subjects. These good
things having been announced among us at an acceptable time, at a
victory-bearing time, when he who refashioned our substance, having approached
the tyrants of the tombs, sent forth life upon the dead man of Lazarus (for it
was necessary, I think, that at the resurrection of the dead there should be
again announcements of the Cross), the whole City came together into itself as
sand, as a stream, as measureless waves making many bodily outpourings; for
they had eagerness, like a gazelle in summer thirsting and throbbing, to reach
quickly the moisture of your syllables, most mighty one.
