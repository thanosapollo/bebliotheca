"The parents brought the child Jesus up to Jerusalem." What then were they
going to do? Surely not to take over the kingdom of Israel? Or to forge
weapons, or to set an army in motion, or to gain together for the child some
other of the earth-fallen and temporal things? No, he says; but "to present
him to the Lord, as it is written in the law of the Lord." What do you say, O
man? And is there a God of the true God; or another Lord of the one who is
Lord in the proper sense? There is, he says, and do not fear; for this is not
according to nature, but according to grace and the highest economy. For just
as God is called Father of those who are not sons in the proper sense; "For I,
he says, have said, You are gods, and sons of the Most High all;" so also of
the Son who is Son in the proper sense according to nature (for the Son is
consubstantial with the Father), he is called God according to grace and
economy; "For I go up, he says, to my Father and your Father, and my God and
your God." For which reason "neither is the Son ashamed to call us brothers;
For I will declare, he says, your name to my brothers, in the midst of the
Church I will hymn you." "As it is written in the law of the Lord." O the
economy! O the unsearchable economy and goodness of the Word concerning us!
The lawgiver under the law; the giver of the Mosaic commands does not refuse
to submit to those who are commanded. He who freed man from the curse of the
law does not delay to serve the law as man. "As it is written in the law of
the Lord." Who is the one who wrote, who? The Lord. Is not this one and the
same, the one glorified with Father and Spirit? Yes, he says; but this is of
the economy, and of not being thought by the Christ-fighters opposed to God.
"Do not think, he says, that I came to destroy the law or the prophets; I came
not to destroy, but to fulfill." And what strange thing if he who accepted
generation and a manger and swaddling-clothes, and an eight-day circumcision,
and to be presented before the Lord according to the law, goes up to
Jerusalem; who so little hides himself with these things, that even to Stephen
being stoned by Jews he shows himself standing at the right of the Father,
although he has the sitting at his right permanent and unfailing. "For the
Lord, he says, said to my Lord, Sit at my right hand, until I put your enemies
a footstool of your feet." But that is above and in a manner befitting God,
and this below and in a manner of economy because of the assumption, and
because this one was called God and man, one hypostasis from both; and through
this one both these things, both being and being known; that by the communion
of the natures and of the properties belonging to them he might procure for us
a most easy and most concise salvation.

"That every male opening the womb shall be called holy to the =28.980= Lord."
What then, is every firstborn holy? It is time for you then also Ishmael, and
Esau, and before these Cain, and many others of the God-hated and accursed
men, because they first opened a womb, to call holy to the Lord. And for what
reason is it said concerning Ishmael, that "Cast out the maidservant and her
son;" and concerning


Esau, that "Jacob I loved, but Esau I hated"? And for what reason, if Cain was
holy to the Lord, were seven vengeances remitted at his murder? If every male
opening the womb shall be called holy to the Lord, what more does Christ have
than Reuben? And what is the surplus of this one toward Amnon? For these too
would seem, by being first brought forth, somehow to have opened a womb. But
see, do not dart hotly about the Scriptures, and do not run far from the
turning-post. For I did not say this, he says, that Every firstborn shall be
called holy to the Lord; for thus holiness would have passed even to the
irrational nature; but that Every male opening the womb; that is, When the
offspring proceeds without marriage; when not of bloods, nor of the will of
flesh, nor of the will of a man, but of the Holy Spirit the conception of the
one being born takes place; when, no one having knocked from without, the
infant itself opens from within; when, the seals and bars of virginity being
kept unmoved within, the one being born leaps out; thus "Every male opening
the womb shall be called holy to the Lord." For there the infants did not open
the wombs, but the coming-together of the man toward the woman; "For Adam, he
says, knew Eve his wife, and conceiving she bore Cain." But here not so, but
the one born himself opened the gates of the womb, having needed not even one
fleshly agreement from those without. "For before, he says, they came
together, she was found having in the womb of the Holy Spirit." And you can
also take the saying more by synecdoche of the Virgin, being heard along from
without, so that the sequence may be thus: "Every male opening a virgin womb
shall be called holy to the Lord." Which has been done only upon Christ, no
other of all having shared in the thing. For this one alone, being born in
flesh, opened a virgin womb; and again kept this one a virgin, as before the
birth.

"And to give a sacrifice according to what has been said in the law of the
Lord; a pair of turtledoves, or two young pigeons." What then? Did he also
offer sacrifices according to the law? And how does Paul say, that "Such a
high priest was fitting for us, who does not have daily necessity, as the high
priests, first to offer sacrifices for his own sins, then those of the people.
For this he did once for all having offered himself," that is having
sacrificed through the cross? But if he himself =28.981= also goes up to
sacrifice, first he will be found confirming and establishing those things
which he himself came to change toward the better; then he himself will also
be one of the many, serving the curse of the law. And for what reason does
Paul say, that "Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law"? Or are these
things not for the infant? No, the evangelist would say, and very nobly, but
for the Mother who bore the infant. And this is clear from the things said
above; "For when, he says, the days of her purification were fulfilled, they
brought the child up to Jerusalem to present him to the Lord, as it is written
in the law of the Lord;" this concerning the infant; "and to give a sacrifice
according to what has been said in the law of the Lord, a pair of turtledoves,
or two young pigeons;" this concerning the Virgin and her betrothed. But if
these things are also concerning the infant, nothing strange. For he who did
not refuse to be circumcised according to the law, how would he have declined
the sacrifices according to the law? For whom so far was it from delaying
these things and putting them off, that he did not even refuse to be baptized
with the baptism of John; although the Baptist had need to be baptized by him.
But nevertheless he condescended to us so much, and was so moved by his own
love of man, as to accept not only the things according to the law, but also a
cross and death, that he might draw us up to himself, lying below in the fall
of the transgression.

"And behold, he says, there was a man in Jerusalem, whose name was Simeon; and
this man was righteous and


devout, waiting for the consolation of Israel." Who would bring to the man a
praise worthier than these very brief words? "And behold there was a man;" a
man what appears, and above man what is understood; a man in substance, but in
dignity exceeding men by a great interval; by nature a man, but by virtue an
angel. A man having as sensible dwelling the Jerusalem below; but as
intelligible the Jerusalem above the mother-city, "whose craftsman and maker
is God;" a man associating with men in the flesh, but living as fellow-citizen
with angels in the spirit; or rather Simeon is even superior to the angels; if
indeed the one unseen to them, this man has been deemed worthy to see; and not
only to see, but what was greater than this and more paradoxical, also to take
in his arms. "And this man was righteous;" for he rendered to each what was
according to worth; to reason, both to think and to reckon the divine things;
to take captive every thought into the obedience of Christ; not to set the
creation up against the Creator, according to the foolish; to suppose nothing
to be outside the divine providence; through the creatures analogously to
think the Fashioner; and to spirit, the austerity against the serpent, the
severity against =28.984= sin; the unsparing and untamed against those who act
impiously. Elijah against the captains of fifty of Ahab; and Phinehas against
the Midianite abomination; and Peter and Paul, the one against Ananias and
Sapphira, the other against Elymas and Alexander, and many others, most
righteously made use of it. And to desire, to meditate in the law of the Lord
continually; to reckon the judgments of the Lord desirable above gold and much
precious stone, and sweeter above honey and honeycomb; to say with David: "How
beloved are your tabernacles, O Lord of hosts; my soul longs and faints for
the courts of the Lord." Thus was Simeon righteous, lacking no form of virtue;
if indeed we know righteousness to be inclusive also of all the other virtues,
as many as are ranked under these generic ones. And "devout" how? being devout
from the face of the Lord and of his commands; for he had read and very
carefully considered the, "Upon whom shall I look but upon the meek and quiet
and trembling at my words?" "Waiting for the consolation of Israel." What
consolation? Which Abraham, having seen in the form of an angel, worshipped in
a manner befitting God. What consolation? Concerning which Jacob foretold; "A
ruler shall not fail, saying, from Judah, nor a leader from his thighs, until
he come for whom it is laid up, and he is the expectation of nations." But
what consolation? That very one concerning which Balaam, even if standing
outside the church's court, prophesied; "A star shall rise, saying, out of
Jacob, and a man shall be raised up out of Israel." That very one concerning
which Moses says, that "The Lord our God will raise up for you a prophet from
your brothers, as me." This consolation, having been oracled through the Holy
Spirit, Simeon was waiting for. What then? Surely he was not somehow cheated
of his hopes, always waiting for this, or did he miss the expectation? Not at
all, by no means; but he both saw and took in his arms. O the virtue of the
man! O the surpassing devoutness and purity! "Into which things," according to
the blessed Paul [read: Peter], "angels desire to look," into these Simeon was
able not only to look, but also to enter purely, and to handle these things,
and to be initiated unmixedly.

"And he came in the Spirit into the temple." O the wonder! He does not even
use his own feet for the ministry of the mystery; but the Holy Spirit becomes
a chariot for him and, as some winged and ether-running other Habakkuk, leads
him into the temple, and presents him to the sanctuary; surely saying and
addressing such things toward him: Come, =28.985= go,


dear old man, that you may see clearly the outcomes of the wonders long ago
oracled to you, and may know of what things you were stored as initiate, of
what glory you have obtained beyond the others. Come, take up the outcomes of
the good virtue; and what formerly the God-seer, having been covered sensibly
by the rock in Sinai, but intelligibly by the unbreakable faith concerning
God, barely as through some hole, of the dim, I think, contemplation and
knowledge, has seen in the form of the hinder parts; these things you now
learn clearly, and pass henceforth from the death that is in the body toward
the life that is in spirit; and the law in letter, which you have ministered
very well and in a manner dear to God, transfer toward that in grace. For you
have already toiled enough with the types and the shadows; you must now be
made young by the truth, that the ends of the legal observances may be for you
a beginning toward the teachings of grace. These things the divine elder
having heard according to the intelligible ear from the Spirit, has been
filled wholly with joy and gladness; and having become as it were inspired by
the amazement, he answered somehow thus to the one conversing: And have those
things come, O Paraclete, which I have been oracled to behold? Have those
things come at all, which I have been promised beforehand by you to see? Give
then a hand to old age, O Paraclete, give. Set under me the grace from you,
set it under. For my body has already toiled, and is too weak to walk. Carry
yourself, O all-working and unwearied nature, carry me the weak, the old man,
and lead me away with speed toward those things. For if I wish to use my own
feet for walking, I delay altogether on the way, and those things pass by, and
I do not behold them. And what of the gloomy things for me, if I shall become
a stranger to these, of which I received the appearances from afar! Not yet
had the elder formed these things in mind, and he is presented at the gates of
the temple quicker than word. "For he came in the Spirit, he says, in the
temple." For such is the power of the Spirit, having the will as work, and the
things accompanying the will.

"And when the parents were bringing in the child Jesus, to do according to the
custom of the law concerning him, he also received him into his arms." Did you
see a meeting strange and above man? Did you see a meeting, not from some
chance, or signal, but from spiritual energy and grace? And, O the wonder!
neither did the one a little precede the other, but together Christ and
Simeon, the one by the mother's arms, the other being carried by the Spirit,
were standing at the doorpost of the temple. He then, having lifted from the
bending toward the earth; for he was until then nodding down, being bent by
old age; a little he supports the two feet, having thrown away the staff, and
having stretched out the hands, he was trying to take from the mother's arms
the God-man infant; but she held back a little; =28.988= for she did not yet
clearly know the arrival of the old man in the Spirit toward the temple; then,
as she saw the divine infant leaping about in her arms and striving to leap
out toward his palms, she both more quickly knows the power of the mystery;
and she further gives this to the elder spreading his hands; Receive, crying,
more honored man, the one now hastening toward you rather than toward me who
bore him; receive the one longing for you more than Joseph; receive the one
reckoning, as it seems, the affection toward me the mother as second to your
friendship; receive, and, as you wish, enjoy the one longed for. These things
the Theotokos said, and she places the son upon the palms of the elder.

And he, having bent his face toward this one as quickly as he could, and
having pressed his eyes upon this one's lips, used somehow such voices toward
him: What is so great a love of man and condescension of yours concerning us,
O Master? What is so great a wealth of your compassion? What is it that moved
your goodness to visit us the humble? How then have you come to us, O Master
who loves man and loves good?


Have you then set in motion the cherubim and the seraphim, or bringing on the
whole array of the heavenly things? Are you then "some earthquake, or a great
and strong wind dissolving mountains and crushing rocks, or a fire easily
terrifying and consuming those who happen by; or a voice of a thin breeze,"
with which you also showed yourself to the Tishbite? No, he says, O most
philosophical old man; I am shown to you by none of these. For the mortal and
material nature does not know how to contain these things; but some
flesh-bearing one, and like you; an infant, as you see, and in bodily age
still infant-appearing and incomplete; an infant still being bound round with
swaddling-clothes, still grasping with both hands the mother's two breasts; an
infant, still uttering something indistinct and cut short because of the flesh
from Mary; an infant being held in your arms, but looking off toward the
mother and the breast. And now these things; but a little later, as much as to
hurry through a time of thirty years, I shall also be baptized with the
baptism of John, and I shall be betrayed, and as a condemned man I shall be
led to Caiaphas, and I shall be spat upon, and I shall be buffeted, I shall be
scourged; finally I shall be crucified, and I shall be pierced with a spear,
and I shall be shut in a tomb appearing as a corpse, and I shall be named a
deceiver, and whatever may be proper to human nature, like you, apart from
sin, I shall take up. And do not wonder, nor stand out of your mind and be
struck; for this is the mystery hidden from the ages and from the generations;
this is the great counsel of the Father and the foreknowledge, of which I have
been foretold as angel to the God-speakers; this is the compassion toward you
=28.989=, and the not wishing to see my image terribly enslaved to the
demons; the not bearing to see the king of the creation badly serving the
creation; the not enduring to behold the apostate and proud one insolently
lifting himself against my creature. These things raised me unchangingly from
the paternal bosom, and made me make the journey through flesh toward you;
these things have made me to be born and to be an infant, and to be presented
to the temple, and to be laid back in your arms. For it was not otherwise for
the tyrant to be bound, and his vessels to be plundered, unless I share in the
humanity like you apart from sin; it was not otherwise for Adam to be released
from the many-plaited nets of Hades, and to receive back the first condition,
and this one to become wholly God, unless I become man, and share with him the
same both substance and nature. Therefore having learned the causes of my
becoming-man, go; and to Adam spending time gloomily in the things toward
Hades appear in a bright manner; and instead of the pangs in griefs carry to
Eve the good news of the joy: The redemption has come, saying; the deliverer
has come; the forgiveness has come; the liberator has come. No longer lament,
O human nature; for he who will take hold of you will come, he will come and
will not delay. The Abraham-like elder then, having heard these divine voices
according to the noetic ear, having become as it were in forgetfulness of old
age, sprang up more quickly, and being filled wholly with eagerness, with a
most loud voice: "Be strong, he says, slackened hands, be strong; paralyzed
knees, be set upright;" crushed shins, be strengthened, that, having
accomplished the journey into Hades with a straight shot, you may be angels of
such great goods to the forefathers. Then as he had speed having turned toward
the divine infant: "Now you dismiss, he said, your servant, O Master,
according to your word in peace; because my eyes have seen your salvation;
which you prepared before the face of all the peoples; a light for revelation
of nations, and glory of your people Israel." O the boiling! O the heat! Since
these things, he says, have seemed good to you, O Master, and you have been
moved by your own love of man to pity your creature, and I have not been
cheated concerning the hopes, and the oracle of the divine Spirit


has remained unfalsified; dismiss me with speed; dismiss, I beg. Do not hold
me for long, O Master; let me not be slow to carry these gladdening good
tidings to the race-chiefs; let not the infants anticipate me, O Master, who
because of you endured the murder from Herod. They are infants and swift and
easily moved; but I am an old man and slow and hard to move; and I fear lest
somehow, having run ahead of me more quickly, they become angels of such
greatest and splendid goods to the forefathers of the race. Thus then the
truly divine man, and clear herald of the presence of Christ, having given
thanks, for this is what to bless God =28.992= means, having prayed was being
dismissed. For the Savior, I think, touched his mind invisibly, and heard his
petition in all things, and as his own worshipper, having toiled very much
with the law and the shadow, but being made young by the divine dogmas of
grace, he received him. And Joseph and his mother, "were wondering, he says,
at the things being spoken concerning him." And they were wondering, not yet,
I think, having received perfect assurance of his Godhead; although the
wonders according to the generation were enough; those, I mean, according to
the magi and the shepherds; and still the various attendance of the angels,
and the wonder beyond all wonders, the guard and preservation of the virginity
toward his perfect assurance and consent. But nevertheless, looking off toward
the bulk of the wonder, and having their own devoutness bridling the
reasonings (for they supposed it a great thing and in truth that God has been
incarnate), they still had the mind being shaken and doubting. Whence the
elder, having given the elements of the theology concerning the infant in
brief, handles the reins of the words toward those henceforth, both blessing
them and calling them blessed for the ministry concerning the economy; and
also foretelling the things concerning the cross both excellently and most
perfectly.

"For Simeon, he says, blessed them, and said to Mary his mother; Behold this
one is set for a fall and a rising of many in Israel, and for a sign spoken
against." For Simon will speak against and doubt concerning this one and the
dogma according to him toward Marcion, and Arius toward Sabellius, and
Eutyches toward Nestorius, and both toward the Church; some assigning the
Godhead alone to him, others the flesh; some introducing him as created, and
alien from the paternal substance, others some unhypostatic Word and empty of
substance, and differing in nothing from that which is poured according to
utterance; some supposing his presence through flesh a fantasy, others
contending that he has not assumed flesh at all; or that he has assumed, but
this soulless and inactive, or having a soul, but this mindless and
irrational; and in a word others will otherwise doubt and speak against, each
concluding the things concerning him according to what seems good to him. "For
concerning this heresy," the Jews say to Paul, "it is known to us, that it is
spoken against everywhere." It is spoken against, but not thrown; it is warred
against, but not conquered; it is shot at, but not wounded; it is besieged,
but not enslaved; the greatest, it falls, but is not crushed; or rather it
even overturns. For this is the exceptional thing of our pure faith, and
beyond every wonder; that not only this, although being fiercely surrounded
from every side by the gatherings and storms from the heresies, continues
unshaken =28.993= and unmoved; but that it also through the opposites wisely
works the opposites, raising bright trophies against those lying in wait. Such
then, he says, O blessed Virgin, is he whom you brought forth from your womb;
easily crushing and troubling those who have torn themselves away from the
truth; but raising up and leading to the Father, as many as may be added to
this one, and keep his justifications. Not indeed, since you have had such a
Son, and


have obtained such glory, as to be known altogether beyond every earth-born
nature, will you be some untempted and impassible one, and as it were
unconsumed toward the afflictions; but "a sword shall also pass through your
own soul;" for let not, he says, the greatness of the wonder lift you toward a
bulk of inflation. For even if you conceived above nature, yet you will have
affliction rivaling those who give birth according to nature; or rather much
greatest and surpassing, by as much as those indeed endured the afflictions
concerning the children without wonders; but you after so great a multitude of
marvels, after those boundless outcomes of the prophetic words, after the
famed guard and preservation of the virginity, O the wonder! "a sword shall
also pass through your own soul." Of whom? "of you yourself, he says," who
were allotted Gabriel as evangelist, and signifier of the birth; "of you," who
had the power of the Most High overshadowing, and the Holy Spirit itself, both
visiting and flying around you, most clearly; "of you yourself," whom magi,
having been guided through a star with the star, worshipped, and shepherds
with angels blessed together the divine infant, and hymned together; "of you
yourself," whom Jacob saw going as a ladder of heavens; and Moses the God-seer
a bush burned with fire, and a lampstand, and a jar, and a tablet, and a
table; and Aaron a rod that sprouted without moisture; "of you yourself," O
all-incorrupt and all-blameless, whom David calls ark of holiness, and Solomon
a golden bed, and a throne, and a valley of lilies; of the divine virtues, I
think, and a God-planted paradise. And "a sword shall pass through your own
soul; of you" the chosen even before generation, the one predestined from the
ages and from the generations; "of you yourself," whom the Father chose, and
the Son tabernacled in, and the Holy Spirit overshadowed in a manner befitting
God; and all the creation proclaims Mother of God and Theotokos most properly
and most truly. "And a sword shall pass through your own soul." Why, she says,
through me, O most wonderful old man? Did I proclaim myself Son of God? Did I
prophesy concerning the temple? Or did I raise Lazarus? Or did I heal the
paralytic? Or did I give eyes to the =28.996= one blind from birth? For what
reason shall the sword run through my soul? Because I loosed the Sabbath?
Because I transgressed the law? Behold through that one I both circumcised the
Son, and arrived at Jerusalem, and I am present at the temple, and I set upon
the divine table the young of the turtledoves, being a sample of a small and
most poor sacrifice. What then is it, either small or greater, which I have
loosed of the law, that a sword should pass through my soul? Speak with speed,
lest somehow we suspect you as having gone beyond, and having grown old with
much of life. No, says that divine elder, and one full of the prophetic grace;

incorrupt birth, the inviolate virginity, the unstained of chastity, the
having suffered nothing in the birth of a natural law and maternal sequence,
through being and remaining a virgin both before birth and after birth
likewise most truly. "That reasonings may be revealed from many hearts." For
of Peter the zeal until then hotter than fire will be revealed, some lukewarm
and as it were under-cold through the denial; and of John and James and the
rest through the flight, which, having left him, they all fled, as if having
forgotten all the greatest wonders. And again from the opposite Pilate will be
revealed coming to =28.997= repentance through the wiping of the hands; and
his yoke-mate how she believed, through the nocturnal vision; the centurion
through the signs; Joseph and Nicodemus through the burial; Judas through the
noose; Jews through giving the silver to the guards. And in a word, there will
be some battle and faction of reasonings and opposite deliberations; some
deliberating the things of the Godhead, others the things of the humanity. For
they will wonder, he says, seeing this one suffering, and agonizing, and
sending out drops of sweats; and still fearing, and praying the declining of
the passion; and thinking of the resurrection of the dead and the giving of
eyes to the blind; and the cleansing of the lepers, and the driving out of the
wicked spirits; and the whole other multitude of the signs accomplished by
him; they will wonder, and will have the mind being shaken here and there,
seeing these things unaccordant with the first, and unsuitable; and again they
will wonder who see the earthquake, and the night in day, and the splitting of
the veil, and the tearing of the rocks; and still the uncovering of the tombs,

be revealed from many hearts, some deliberating these things, others those.
But nevertheless this battle of the reasonings, and the faction, and the
mixing, and the opposition, will be a cause of concord and agreement, and of
the highest peace to the Church; for through both what Christ was will be
proclaimed, and the same will be known as God and man, the same willing and
working with self-authority, some things by the Godhead, others by the
humanity. These things then, I think, that truly divine and Abraham-like elder
would have said, if someone had asked him concerning these things. Then after
him Anna also is presented to the letter, walking with aged steps; for the
mystery was calling her too, about to fulfill a grace of prophecy; that she
too might bear witness prophetically, that the Lord has made known his
salvation, and that he has revealed his righteousness before the nations. But
first let us learn from the Gospel, who and from where this one has been
established, and upon what things and when she has stood at the temple; and
again let us search what sort of things she was saying. For the evangelist
says: "And there was Anna a prophetess daughter of Phanuel of the tribe of
Asher; this one having advanced in many days; having lived with a husband
seven years from her virginity; and she a widow of about eighty-four years;
who did not depart from the temple, serving with fasts and petitions night and
day. And this one, having stood by at that very hour, was giving thanks to the


Lord, and was speaking concerning him to all who were waiting for redemption
in Jerusalem." What is the reason of the =28.1000= evangelist having made such
an exact narration concerning this prophetess; so as to mention also her
father and the tribe, which is found upon almost no other? The exact reason
concerning this only God of the saints himself would know, and those who have
been taught to speak mysteries by his inbreathing of Spirit, according to the
divine Apostle; but she herself, shining with prophetic grace, was generating
a God-given consolation for those waiting for redemption in Jerusalem; that
the Redeemer of the all, saying, has arrived, and the Savior of all has come
and has been manifested, wishing to redeem his own image, and willing to save
the creature which he fashioned. For this reason Christ is named Jesus, since
Jesus is also interpreted Savior. And while all these things were happening in
the deeds, and being said in the words, both the God-mother Mary was being
astonished, and the present Joseph was wondering. Nevertheless having
completed the things decided for the law, since Christ has arrived to fulfill
all of the law, and to give rest to the commands of the law, they return to
Nazareth; where also she who bore the Savior to the world was evangelized by
the angel Gabriel, having conceived from a seedless conception, but remaining
a virgin after generation, and showing to all who see, that the Salvation of
God has been made known, and the light has shone to the nations; and that
righteousness of God and redemption has appeared to all the ends of the earth;
and has clothed those who have received with a glory at once divine and
everlasting, and not knowing flowing and corruption. But we, O most
longed-for, just as those, so let us meet with Christ the God, bringing
temperance, carrying chastity, offering harmlessness, showing forgetfulness of
wrongs, separating ourselves from distractions, and bringing ourselves pure to
God, bearing gentleness of manners, and having the kindly and mutual-loving
toward all. And in addition to all these bringing the sympathy toward all, and
always bubbling the mercy coming forth from a sympathetic soul, thus let us
receive Christ approaching; thus let us house Christ; thus let us take Christ
in our arms in a manner befitting God, and let us give thanks with prophetic
words, hymning his coming-forth toward us, and proclaiming with loud mouths
the sympathy which he has worked toward us; that we may both obtain the
kingdom of heavens, and enjoy everlasting goods, in him Christ our God,
