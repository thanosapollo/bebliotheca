* On Holy Pascha, Sermon 2
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

1 The reasonableness of the legal types appears in the truth, as for instance
that the sheep which the law used for the sacrifice of the Pascha should be
unblemished, that it should be male, that it should be a year old, that being
taken on the tenth of the month it should be sacrificed on the fourteenth
toward evening, that the time when the Pascha was celebrated should be
reckoned as the beginning of months: for all these things stand together in
Christ and in the passion of Christ, both true and necessary, and by
themselves they can have no account.
2 Nor would those who do not know Christ find a defense to make concerning
these things and to teach why God commanded them, nor even at all what cause
there is in appointing a sacrifice for the salvation of the firstborn and in
making an anointing of the blood upon the entrances: for what power toward the
salvation of the firstborn has the slaughter of the victims, and what
necessity has the anointing of the blood as a sign upon the houses? God, at
any rate, even without signs knew the houses of the Hebrews, so that it is
marvelous to say "I will see the blood and I will shelter you": but one who
also raises these things to the truth recognizes their reasonableness, just as
3 we have shown in the other things said before. For when the unblemished
victim suffers for us, the death that came from the first-formed is loosed,
and the first-born man is saved, being in us all, being made alive by the
resurrection of the Lord (for life is justly the dissolver of sin and death),
and the unblemished blood becomes a saving sign to those who share in it, and
God, looking to this, saves those who have been anointed with it through
faith, who are not otherwise able to escape the angel who justly punishes,
except through the blood 4 poured out for us in love of mankind.
You see how great a power the type has when it is considered in the truth, so
that if some Jew should attempt to say: Why then did the Son suffer, that the
world might be saved, as though it would not have been saved even without
this? let it be said to him: Why then was the Pascha sacrificed for the
salvation of the Jewish firstborn, as though they would not have been saved
even without this? And he will not have anything to answer, for God has no
need of sacrifice nor of a sign for the recognition of houses: but the matter
advocates for us and bears witness to the holy faith, our injustice being
loosed in the righteousness of Christ, and otherwise it would not have been
loosed (for darkness is made to vanish by light), and our death also being
abolished through the life of Christ as darkness by light.
5 Sacred, then, was that victim also, because it was a type of the true: but
this is truly sacred, as being true, given over by the ignorance of the
ungodly to death and destruction, yet by the counsel of God taken for a sacred
sacrifice and by his own will offered to the Father as an offering. For "the
Father," it says, "gave over the Son for us," and Christ "offered himself" to
the Father for the church "an offering and a sacrifice for a savor of sweet 6
odor." And for those who expected the destruction of Christ the counsel is
ruinous, but for those who seek salvation through the death of the Lord the
faith is saving: punishment and death pass over these, because they are not
held by death toward destruction, according to what was said by the Savior:
"If anyone keeps my word, he shall never 7 see death unto the age."
For through the blood poured out for us we receive the Holy Spirit, since the
blood and the Spirit came into one, that through the blood of the same kind as
we are we might be able to receive the Holy Spirit which is not of the same
kind as we are, and through this shut the entrance of death into us.
8 And there is a certain double entrance for death, and for this reason the
seal of the blood is made double, "upon the two doorposts," it says, "and upon
the lintel." For "death enters through sin," as the Apostle says, and of sin
there is on the one hand that according to the passion in us (and this is
twofold, either softening the soul toward softness, or hardening it toward
roughness), and on the other that according to the reasoning, when it would
not reckon things sound nor right: and the reasoning is as it were a lintel
(that is, the beam over the door) upon both doorposts, for it is ruling and
higher by nature, while the passion has an analogy to the doorposts, being
subject to the reasoning, 9 just as the doorposts are subject to the lintels.
What are the passions that lead toward softness? Gluttony and drunkenness and
desire of intercourse: what are the hard things of the soul? Angers and
arrogances: of which Paul, making the destruction, said: "Not in revels and
drunkennesses, not in beds and wantonnesses, not in strife and jealousy, but
put on our Lord Jesus Christ."
10 The anointing, then, is like the putting-on, when being formed by the
passion of Christ we put around ourselves the holiness of Christ, neither
dissolving into pleasures nor being made rough into rages: and it is good also
to say that for the reasonings too an anointing and an over-garment is the
wisdom that is not fleshly but that according to Christ, so that we are put to
death as regards the fleshly mind as though having died, and are adorned with
the spiritual: for thus neither through the irrational passions nor through
the foolish reasonings does sin come upon us, nor does death dominate us.
11 Such then is the mystery of the anointing: and after the anointing is the
eating, which houses the divine body in us and mixes it toward union: "They
shall eat," 12 it says, "the flesh this night roasted with fire." Night is
this present age: "The night has advanced," Paul says: the partaking of the
holy body is through fire, that is, through a fiery and boiling zeal. "Boiling
in the Spirit," it says, and "I came to cast fire upon the earth," he says,
"and what do I wish if it is already kindled?"
13 And just as the fire, being indicative of the boiling zeal, is taken for
the enjoyment of the meats, so the unleavened things are of simplicity and the
bitter herbs of the afflictions that follow: for "They shall eat unleavened
bread," it says, "upon bitter herbs." 14 Simple is the manner and not
evil-doing, putting off that old and cunning thing, as it were leaven, and
having taken up the new and without cunning, that it may be unleavened: "For
our Pascha," it says, "Christ has been sacrificed, so that we may feast not in
the old leaven nor in the leaven of malice and wickedness, but in unleavened
things of sincerity and truth": and the afflictions are bitter, "but we also
boast in the afflictions," it says, "knowing that affliction works endurance,
and endurance proof, and proof hope, and hope does not put to shame. Through
many afflictions we must enter into the kingdom," so that we accept the
affliction in the manner of a seasoning because of the hope from it and the
kingdom.
15 And the command forbids eating of the meats raw: and this for those who
hear it bodily would have no account, through which God forbade what would
never happen, for no one would eat it raw: but among us not approaching idly
the partaking of the divine body also furnishes a very great account: for it
is made unpartaken for those who handle it idly and do not claim the mixing
with it through good works: "Faith," it says, "without works is dead," and for
this reason afflictions are brought upon those who are disposed idly "for what
is profitable," as Paul says, that they may partake of his holiness.
16 And the pleasure-loving of the flesh produces idleness concerning the
spiritual works: wherefore the lawgiver, showing this by another symbol, also
forbids eating "what is boiled in water": for what is boiled is prepared
toward the pleasure of eating, and it is not possible to receive the divine
food with pleasure: for a spiritual power is not mixed with a fleshly manner.
17 Do not then boil the holy flesh, he says, mixing it with seasonings, but
let it be eaten only "roasted with fire," which is intense zeal without love
of pleasure. The pleasure-loving is not God-loving, as Paul says, and "whose
god is the belly," for these God is not God: nor are those who turn the
Eucharist into costly feastings acceptable with the Apostle, making the
sanctifying communion a pretext of much and varied eating and of drinking, to
whom Paul says: "It is not to eat a Lord's supper: for each one takes his own
supper beforehand in the eating, and one hungers, and another is drunk. For do
you not have houses for eating and drinking, or do you despise the church of
God, and put to shame those who have not? What shall I say to you? Shall I
praise you? In this I do not praise": and he says that from this unprepared
use concerning the holy food the trials happen to them, because they do not
approach the holy in a holy way: for "For this reason," he says, "many among
you are weak and sickly, and many sleep. If 18 we judged ourselves, we would
not be judged." For those who do not furnish the body as fit for the mixing of
his body are liable for impiety toward the Lord, which body he gave us that
being mixed with it we might be mingled with the Holy Spirit, since for this
reason also the Word of God gave himself wholly into a body and "became flesh"
according to the Gospel voice, that since it is not possible for us to partake
of him as Word, we might partake of him as flesh, having made our flesh akin
to the spiritual flesh and the spirit to the Spirit according to power, that
we might be established as likenesses of Christ, having become temples of the
Spirit, as the Apostle says: "You are temples of God," and again: "Do you not
know that your bodies are a temple of the Holy Spirit in you, which you have
from God?" and through the mixing with the Spirit they become
bodies of Christ, and one must treat them "in sanctification," treating
these then as members of Christ: for "Do you not know," he says, "that
your members are members of Christ? Shall I then make the members of
Christ members of a harlot? May it not be."
19 The preparation of the holy food, then, the legislation thus and in such
symbols ordered, having ordered the then prefiguration "toward what is
profitable" for us: and "the head," it says, "with the feet and the inward
parts," hinting by the head at the beginning of the appearing among men, and
this was the former coming, and the feet the end, and this was the second
coming, without which the former cannot be believed, since it did not fulfill
all the things prophesied: wherefore the Jews do not believe, because the
things of the glorious coming have not been fulfilled, thinking that he who
thus 20 came is not Christ. For this reason, having woven them together, he
said "the head with the feet," that is, the former coming with the second,
that you may both receive the Lord led with dishonor as "a sheep to slaughter"
and see him appearing as "king with glory," both of which Isaiah the prophet
foretold, and that you may not demand the redemption of Jerusalem, but later
expect this: "The deliverer will come from Zion and will turn ungodliness away
from Jacob," he saving the remnant of the people that is going to turn to him,
and in the former coming you may see the contrary fulfilled, the "I have left
my house, I have abandoned my inheritance" and the "He gave over the ungodly
for his burial and the rich for his death."
21 Saying then "the head with the feet" he bears witness beforehand to the
Jews that you should not separate the inglorious coming of the Lord from the
glorious, lest having disbelieved you be separated from the communion with
him: but also "with the inward parts," he says. What is this? Do not be
scandalized about what appears, but consider also what is hidden: do not think
him a man equally with you, because he is "also a man" according to the body,
but know him spiritually 22 and you will know the Father in him. For as long
as you do not see the inward nor partake of the inward part, you have not yet
seen Christ, thinking him a man, not God: and he himself says: "He who has
seen me has seen the Father."
23 And it must also be considered what the "You shall not leave from it until
morning" is, and let Paul interpret this for us, saying that we make a
"remembrance" of the passion of the Lord "until he comes": for this is
accomplished until so much and saves those who accomplish it, until the
present night passes and morning comes and the living Christ shines upon us
from heaven, saving us through his own life, he who now saves through death:
"For if being enemies," he says, "we were reconciled to God through the death
of his Son, much more, having been reconciled, 24 we shall be saved in his
life." If then now we are saved in the death of Christ, but then in life, the
symbol is fitting, that the meats of the victim not be left "until morning":
for we too shall then be living and our present deadness is with us, we now
not being saved otherwise unless we have been put to death, but then having a
living salvation: "Do not mind the earthly things," it says, "for you died and
your life has been hidden with Christ in God. When Christ your life is
manifested, then you also will be manifested with him in glory." 25 Wherefore
also if anything of the meats of the victim is left over it is burned with
fire, that we may know that according to the present age Christ has died "to
sin once for all," and "death no longer lords it over him," but the signs of
death have been taken away by the power of the Spirit as by fire, life having
prevailed.
26 And as for the "A bone shall not be broken from him," the blessed John says
that it was also observed bodily, his legs not having been broken, and it also
has the spiritual indication, that the firmness in him remained unconquered,
and for this reason corruption of the body did not touch him, since he had a
most firm and unbroken foundation, because the body was constituted divinely
and not fleshly and, having a divine generation, was not subject to the
corruption of men. "His flesh," it says, "did not see corruption."
27 Having examined these things concerning the saving anointing and the sacred
eating, let us cease from the discourse, and now leaving the things after this
for another examination, asking from the Lord the request which David asked:
"Uncover my eyes, and I shall perceive the wonders from your law," that the
reading of the legal things may not be easily despised by us as by the Jews
who stand as far as the types, but that perceiving the divine symbols divinely
we may appear toward the most divine truth in Christ Jesus our Lord, through
whom and with whom to the Father is the glory with the Holy Spirit unto the
ages. Amen.
