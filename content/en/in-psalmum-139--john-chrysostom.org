On the text, Deliver me, O Lord, from the evil man.

=55.707=

1. I wonder why the prophet did not pray to be delivered from demons, or
lions, or dragons, but from an evil man. It is clear, then, that wicked men
are worse than lions. For the lions stood in awe of Daniel, but the Jews
crucified the Lord; the ravens fed Elijah on the mountain, but Joseph's
brothers, while selling him, were eating, and they even disregarded him when
he pleaded. For the child was begging them not to be sold to the foreigners;
but they, by the excess of their cruelty, disregarded his entreaty, and they
neither pitied his young age, nor reverenced brotherly bowels. They were of
one sower, and almost one field had brought them forth, yet by the excess of
cruelty they sold the most well-born, even a brother, as a slave.

Deliver me, O Lord, from the evil man. Wild bees fed John in the wilderness,
but Herodias beheaded him at a banquet. Dragons and horned serpents and asps
were ashamed before the man, but a wicked woman did not shudder at the
prophet. Daniel in Babylon slew the dragon, but David could not overcome
Saul's envy. The serpent deceived Eve only; but Cain murdered his brother by
guile. The dog Cain, when scourged, submits to the Master; but Judas, even
while being fed, betrayed the Master. Deliver me, O Lord, from the evil man:
not from beasts; for the beasts possess their wickedness openly, but wicked
men hide their wickedness under the guise of fairness. And just as those who
give deadly drugs often smear the bitterness with honey, with the sweetness;
so also wicked men carry the sweetness of words in the mouth, but keep the
bitterness of their ways in the heart; they bear the honey on the tongue,
=55.708= and they forge the sword in the intent, appearing as one thing
without, and being hidden as another within. Thus then the wicked man is more
grievous than the beast; and how, hear. The beast, not having intelligence,
does not find evils against the man; for it does not reason, nor has it the
intelligence that would dictate deceit to it; but the wicked man, possessing
intelligence, uses it toward wickedness, and whenever he wishes, he invents
snares through his intelligence. Deliver me, O Lord, from the evil man: not
from an evil angel. For there are evil angels too; as when David says: He sent
out upon them the wrath of his anger, wrath and anger and affliction, a
sending through evil angels.

He made a path for his wrath. But the wicked man is worse than the wicked
angel. For the wicked angel keeps what has been commanded; but the wicked man
counterfeits what has been commanded. What is more wicked than the devil? Yet
nevertheless, having envied Job, he did not dare to approach him without a
divine command. But the wicked man, hearing, "Love your enemies," not only
does not love his enemies, but even hates those who do him good, and murders
them, as Saul also did to David. For the one, slinging, killed his adversary
Goliath, and drove away the fear; but the other was breathing treachery
against him. Again he was driving away his demon by his psalmody, but the
other, having sharpened the sword against him, was hurling it. For such is the
wicked man: the more he is benefited, the more he is kindled toward envy. For
strength is fuel of envy to the wicked man who is being benefited. Deliver me,
=55.709= O Lord, from the evil man: not from demons. For the demons are driven
away by the sign of the cross, but the wicked man arrays himself against the
Lord himself. Paul, however, drove out the demons by a word, but he could not
overcome the wickedness of Alexander the coppersmith; for he says, Alexander
the coppersmith showed me many evils; may the Lord repay him. Deliver me, O
Lord, from the evil man: not from a good man. For the good man out of the good
treasure of the heart brings forth good things; but the wicked man out of the
wicked treasure brings forth wicked things; and as from a kind of root, the
heart, each of them brings forth the shoots, the good man goodness, and the
wicked man wickedness. And just as of the bitter root the branches and the
leaves and the flowers and the fruit are bitter; so also of the wicked man the
gait and the look and the bearing and the hands are wicked. Concerning the
feet, as when wisdom says: Turn aside from them and pass by; for their feet
run toward wickedness. Concerning the hands, as when the prophet says in
prayer: Rescue me, O Lord, from the hand of the sinner, from the hand of the
transgressor and the wrongdoer.

Concerning the evil eye, as when the Lord cries: Is your eye evil, because I
am good? Deliver me, O Lord, from the evil man. Such a one weaves deceits,
practices hypocrisies, forges envy in the heart. From the evil man: not from a
righteous man. For a righteous man pities the souls of beasts, as it is
written; but the unjust take away even those of men. A righteous man pities
the souls of beasts, as Moses, having run up to the mountain, watered Jethro's
thirsty sheep; but the unjust Pharaoh drowned the firstborn of the Hebrews in
the river; and the one through water gave sheep to drink; the other through
water killed men. 2. We must therefore flee wickedness and injustice, as far
as our strength allows; and not only flee, but also deliver those who are
wronged from the hand of those who wrong them, as Abraham delivered Lot when
he had been taken captive from the hand of the foreigners; as Lot himself
delivered the angels from the lawlessness of the Sodomites. For even if the
nature of the angels is not susceptible, yet the wounds (forte: the daring of)
men are ready at hand. As Moses delivered the people of the Hebrews from the
hand of the Egyptians; as David delivered Saul from the violence of the demon;
as the angel who came down with them delivered the three children from the
burning furnace; as faith delivered Daniel from the den of lions; as
repentance delivered Jonah from the belly of the whale; as Abimelech the
eunuch delivered Jeremiah from the pit of mire; as Daniel in Babylon delivered
Susanna from the judgment of the lawless; as the Lord delivered all humanity
from the hand of the devil; as the apostles delivered those held by spirits
and diseases. But the word does not only legislate that men do this; it also
teaches women. For many women have delivered many from death and dangers: as
Sarah delivered Abraham from the hand of Abimelech, having contrived that he
was her brother; as Rebecca delivered her son Jacob from the hand of his
brother Esau, advising him to flee; as Rachel delivered her father from
idolatry, having stolen lifeless idols; for she stole them for no other reason
than that she might deliver her father from idolatry; as Rahab the harlot
delivered the spies from the hand of the foreigners, having hidden them in the
stalks of flax; as Michal delivered David from the hand of Saul, having told
him the secret; as Abigail delivered Nabal from death, having fallen down
before David; as the mother of Moses delivered the infant from the hand of
Pharaoh, having put him into a basket; as Martha and Mary delivered their
brother =55.710= Lazarus from Hades, having wept before the Lord; as the
widows delivered Tabitha from death, having prevailed upon Peter; as faith
delivered the woman with the issue of blood, and fasting the Ninevites.

Deliver me, O Lord, from the evil man, and from the unjust man rescue me. Let
us see from whom the prophet prays specifically to be delivered. For Doeg was
an evil man, and Saul an unjust man.

David, then, was fleeing from the face of Saul to Nob, the city of the
priests, and there Abimelech the priest gave him the spear of Goliath the
foreigner and provision. Then Doeg the Edomite, who was over Saul's mules,
being present and seeing what was happening, went and slandered David and
Abimelech the priest, saying: Yesterday I was in Nob the city, and David was
there; and Abimelech the priest gave him provision, and the spear of Goliath
the foreigner. Then Saul, having grown angry and becoming wholly of wrath,
says to the soldiers who ran beside him: Slay the priests in the city of Nob.
But they did not dare to lay hands upon the priests, their hands bridled by
the fear of God, and before the hands, their minds. Then Saul, turning to
Doeg, says: You, put all the priests of the Lord to death, and do not spare,
neither elder, nor younger, nor woman, nor virgin, nor infant, nor nursling;
even to donkey and sheep and calf, kill them all for extinction. Then Doeg,
turning about, and dividing the people under his command, runs, and he made
some surround the city, so that no one should flee and procure safety, and he
ordered others to throw fire into the city; and he himself, like a lion eager
for prey, rushed upon them in the city, as upon sheep shut in a fold, and he
slaughtered on that day three hundred and fifty priests who bore the ephod,
and he spared neither elder, nor younger, nor virgin, nor woman, nor nursing
infant, and from sheep and calf and donkey, and he burned the city with fire.
Then David, having seen this, said as he fled: Deliver me, O Lord, from the
evil man; from the unjust man rescue me. For truly one could see most cruel
things, the blood of the young and of the infants mixed together, and the tips
of infants' mouths still fastened to their mothers' breasts, and an untimely
doom (f. shearing) being cut before its time, and ears of grain reaped before
their time, and grape-clusters not yet darkened, murdered by a hail of wrath.
The solemn mystery of women's locks, which ought to have been hidden, was
making a public show. For when the little linen tunic upon the head was thrown
off, the skull was shown bare of hair, and breasts laid bare, which were
ashamed even to see the face of the sun, and the child-bearing parts, which
nature itself taught to cover.

And who would be able to tell the distress that happened then? Since,
therefore, such things the wicked man and the unjust man accomplish, let us
never cease to entreat God earnestly and to say: Deliver me, O Lord, from the
evil man, from the unjust man rescue me. And let us not only pray these things
with the mouth, but also through good works prepare ourselves as worthy for
our petition to be heard. For thus, calling upon God, we shall be heard, and
he will not only deliver and rescue us from the evil man and from the unjust
man, but will also redeem us from our invisible enemies, and together with the
earthly good things he will also count us worthy of his eternal good things.
And especially let us keep the love toward one another unfeigned and sincere,
and let us reckon the progress that comes to our neighbor as our own gain.

For in this we become imitators of God, as far as is possible for men. For our
God both is love and is so named. To him be the glory and the power, to the
Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and always, and unto the
ages of ages. Amen.
