HOMILY IX.
On the Transfiguration of our Lord and God and Savior
Jesus Christ.
=77.1009= Those who know well how to contend take pleasure in the applause of
the spectators, and by the hopes of the prizes they are whetted toward the
victory that befits them. But those who long to obtain the divine gifts, and
who thirst for a share in the hope prepared for the saints, gladly take upon
themselves the contests for piety toward Christ. They accomplish the approved
life, not preferring unrewarded sluggishness, nor yet loving unmanly cowardice;
rather they prove themselves men against every temptation, and they count the
onsets of persecutions as of little account, and they consider it wealth to
suffer for him. For they remember the blessed Paul writing, that the sufferings
of this present time are not worthy to be compared with the glory that shall be
revealed to us. Behold therefore our Lord Jesus Christ employing even now a
most beautiful economy, for the benefit and edification of the holy apostles.
For he said to them: If anyone wishes to come after me, let him deny himself,
and take up his cross, and follow me. For whoever wishes to save his soul shall
lose it; but whoever loses his soul for my sake shall find it. And the command
is saving, and fitting for saints, and a procurer of the highest glory, and
productive of gladness unto the end. For to choose to suffer for Christ is not
without reward; rather it procures a share in eternal life and glory. Yet since
the disciples had not yet received the power from on high, it was likely that
they might perhaps fall into human weaknesses, and, having thought such a thing
within themselves, say: How shall anyone deny himself? Or how, having lost his
own soul, shall he find it again?
And what equal prize shall there be for those who have =77.1012= suffered this?
Or of what gifts shall he be a partaker? In order therefore to turn them away
from such thoughts and words, and as it were to recast them toward manliness by
implanting in them a desire for the glory that would be given them: I tell you,
he says, there are some of those standing here who shall not taste of death
until they see the kingdom of God. Will their measure of life, he says, be
stretched so far that they should reach even those times after which, at the
consummation of the ages, he will come down from heaven and restore to the
saints the kingdom prepared for them? Yet this too was possible for him. For he
can do all things, and nothing is impossible or unaccomplishable to his most
all-powerful commands. But he calls kingdom the very vision of the glory in
which he himself also will be seen at that time when he shall shine upon those
on earth. For he will come in the glory of God and the Father, and not rather
in the lowliness that is according to us. How then did he make spectators of
the wonder those who had received the promise? He goes up into the mountain,
having three chosen from among them; then he is transformed into a certain
exceptional and God-befitting brightness, so that even his clothing seemed to
shine with the impact of the light.
Then Moses and Elijah, standing about Jesus, were speaking with one another of
his departure, which he was about to fulfill in Jerusalem, that is, the mystery
of the economy with the flesh, and the saving passion, I mean that upon the
precious cross. For it is true that the law through Moses, and the word of the
holy prophets, showed forth beforehand the mystery of Christ; the one all but
depicting it in types and shadows, as upon a tablet, and the others having
foretold in many ways that he would also appear in due seasons in the form that
is according to us, and that for the salvation and life of all he would not
refuse to suffer death upon the wood. Therefore the standing of Moses and
Elijah, and their speaking with one another, was a certain economy showing very
well that our Lord Jesus Christ is escorted by the law and the prophets, as
having been shown beforehand by them to be master of both law and prophets,
through the things they proclaimed in harmony with one another. For the things
of the prophets are not inconsistent with those through the law. And this, I
think, is the speaking with one another of Moses the most holy and of the best
of the prophets; and this was Elijah. And there is also something else to
consider. Since the crowds were saying, some Elijah, some Jeremiah, some one of
the prophets, he brings the chiefs, so that from this too they might see the
difference between the servant and the master. After this one may also speak
another thought. For since they were continually accusing him of transgressing
the law, and they thought him a blasphemer, as appropriating glory not
belonging to him, that of the Father, and they said: This man is not from God,
because he does not keep the Sabbath; and again: For a good work we do not
stone you, but for blasphemy, and because, being a =77.1013= man, you make
yourself God; in order that it might be shown that both charges are of envy,
and that he is guiltless of both of these, and that what is done is neither a
transgression of the law, nor is it an appropriation of glory that does not
belong, to say that he is equal to the Father, he brings into the midst those
who shone in each of these.
For Moses gave the law, and the Jews could reason that he would not have
overlooked the law being trampled, as they thought; nor would he have attended
upon the one who was transgressing it and was an enemy to the one who had
established it. And Elijah also was zealous for the glory of God. And he would
not, if Christ were an adversary of God, and called himself God equal to the
Father, not being what he said, nor doing this fittingly, have stood by himself
also and obeyed. And one may also give another reason besides those already
said. What then is this? That they might learn that he has authority over both
life and death, and rules over things above and things below. For this reason
he brings into the midst both the one who is living and the one who has died.
For appearing they were not silent, but they were speaking of the glory which
he was about to fulfill in Jerusalem; that is, both the passion and the cross,
and in these also the resurrection. But the blessed disciples drowse a little,
as Christ was occupying himself with prayer. For the human things were being
fulfilled economically. Then, having fully awakened, they become spectators of
so venerable and paradoxical a change. But the divine Peter, perhaps thinking
that the time of the kingdom of God had already come, welcomes the sojourning
on the mountain; and he says that three tents should be made, not knowing what
he says. For it was not the time of the consummation of the age, nor yet of the
present time for the saints to receive a share of the hope promised to them.
For Paul says, Who will transform the body of our humiliation, that it may
become conformed to the body of his glory, of Christ, that is. Since therefore
the economy was still in its beginnings, and not yet completed, how was it
likely that Christ should cease from wishing to suffer for the world, having
withdrawn from it, for the sake of his love toward it?
For he saved the world under heaven, having endured even death itself according
to the flesh, and having abolished it through the resurrection from the dead.
Peter therefore did not know what he said; yet besides the paradoxical and
ineffable vision of the glory of Christ, something else also has been done that
is useful and necessary, for confirmation of the faith in him, and not only for
the disciples, but indeed also for us ourselves. For from a cloud a voice was
given down from above from God the Father, saying: This is my Son, the beloved,
in whom I am well pleased; hear him. And when the voice came, he says, Jesus
was found alone.
What does the stiff-necked Jew say to these things, the intractable, and
disobedient, and having a heart that cannot be admonished? Behold, Moses being
present, the Father commands the holy apostles to hear him. But if he had
wished them to follow the commandments of Moses, he would have said: Obey
Moses, keep the =77.1016= law. But now God the Father does not even say this;
but with Moses standing, and his prophet Elijah, he rather commands them to
hear him. And lest the truth be slandered by some who say that the Father
commanded them rather to hear Moses, and not Christ the Savior of us all;
necessarily therefore the evangelist noted, saying that when the voice came,
Jesus was found alone.
Therefore when God the Father was commanding the holy apostles from above as
from the clouds, saying: Hear him; Moses was absent, and Elijah was not
present, but Christ was alone. Therefore he has commanded them to hear him. For
he himself is the end of the law and the prophets. For this reason he also
addressed the crowds of the Jews, saying: If you believed Moses, you would
believe me; for he wrote of me. But since they have remained throughout
dishonoring the commandment through all-wise Moses, and setting aside the word
through the holy prophets, they have justly been alienated, and have been
driven out from the good things promised to their fathers.
For obedience is better than sacrifice, and to hearken than the fat of lambs,
as it is written. And such are the affairs of the Jews; but for us who have
recognized his appearing, all good things will altogether be ours through him
and from him, Christ; through whom and with whom to God the Father be glory and
power with the Holy Spirit unto the ages of ages. Amen.
