according to that very evening they were being performed; since salvations
too, I mean of the living and of the dead, were being accomplished. And the
Jew was binding, sacrificing a lamb for slaughter, but the one from the
nations God in flesh. And the one was gazing at the shadow; but the other was
running toward the Sun, God. And those who had bound Christ were sending him
away, but those from the nations were receiving him eagerly. And the ones were
offering a beast-sacrifice, but the others a God-bodied sacrifice. But the
Jews were remembering the crossing from Egypt, while those from the nations
were proclaiming beforehand the ransom from error.

And these things where? In Zion, the city of the great King; in which he
worked salvation in the midst of the earth, known in the midst of two living
creatures, Jesus the child of God; in the midst of Father and Spirit, the two
living creatures, life from life, he says, being made known as living, and in
the midst of angels and men being born in the manger; and in the midst of two
peoples lying as a cornerstone; and in the midst of law and prophets being
proclaimed together; and in the midst of Moses and Elijah appearing on the
mountain; and in the midst of the two robbers, God being made known to the
well-disposed robber; and in the midst of the present life and the one to come
sitting as eternal judge; and in the midst today of living and dead having
made a double life and salvation. I say again a double life, a double birth
together with regeneration, and hear the facts of Christ's double birth, and
applaud the wonders.

An angel announced to Mary the maternal birth of Christ; but an angel
announced to Mary Magdalene the dread regeneration from the tomb. By night
Christ is born in Bethlehem; by night again in =43.444= Zion he is born again.
He accepts swaddling clothes at the birth; he is wrapped in swaddling clothes
here too. Having been born he received myrrh, and at the burial he accepts
myrrh and aloes. There Joseph is named the unwedded husband of Mary; but here
Joseph of Arimathea is shown as the undertaker of our life. In Bethlehem the
place is also a manger; but in the tomb too the place is as upon a manger.
First of all shepherds announced the birth of Christ; but first of all
shepherds, the disciples of Christ, were evangelized of Christ's regeneration
from the dead. There, "Hail," the angel cried to the Virgin; and here,
"Rejoice," the Angel of great counsel, Christ, cried out to the women. In the
first birth Christ after forty days entered the earthly Jerusalem, into the
temple, and as firstborn offered to God a pair of turtledoves; but also in the
resurrection from the dead Christ after forty days went up into the Jerusalem
above, from which he was not separated, and into the true Holy of Holies as
firstborn incorruptible from the dead, and offered to God and Father as two
unblemished turtledoves our soul and our flesh; whom also he received, as some
Simeon, the Ancient of Days, God and Father, as in his arms in his own bosom
uncircumscribably. But if you hear these things as myths and not faithfully,
the unbroken seals of the Master's tomb of Christ's regeneration accuse you.

For just as, the all-innate womb-opening locks of the virginal nature having
been sealed, Christ was born of a Virgin; so, the seals of the tomb being
unopened, the regeneration of Christ has been accomplished. But how in a tomb
and when, and


by whom Christ the life is laid down, let us hear from the sacred oracles.
When evening had come, it says, there came a rich man named Joseph; this man,
having dared, went in to Pilate, and asked from him the body of Jesus. A
mortal went in to a mortal, asking to receive God; he asks for the God of
mortals; clay toward clay to receive the fashioner of all; the grass from
grass to fetch the heavenly fire; the pitiable drop from a pitiable drop
receives the abyss. Who has seen? Who has ever heard? A man grants to a man
the maker of men; a lawless man of the lawless promises to grant the bound one
even of the law; an unjudged judge as a condemned man lets the judge of judges
go for burial. When evening had come, it says, there came a rich man named
Joseph. Truly rich, having received the whole composite hypostasis of the
Lord; truly rich, because he received the double substance of Christ from
Pilate; and indeed rich, because he was deemed worthy to receive the priceless
pearl. † =43.445= rich; for he carried a purse full of the treasure of the
Godhead. For how is he not rich who has acquired the life and salvation of the
world? And how is Joseph not rich, having received as a gift the one who feeds
all and rules all? When evening had come; for the sun of righteousness had
already set in Hades. Therefore there came a rich man named Joseph from
Arimathea, who was hiding, because of fear of the Jews. And Nicodemus also
came, who had come to Jesus by night. A mystery of mysteries hidden. Two
hidden disciples come to hide Jesus in a tomb, teaching by their own hiding
the hidden mystery in Hades of the hidden God in flesh. And the one surpassing
the other in disposition toward Christ. For Nicodemus is magnanimous in the
myrrh and in the aloes; but Joseph is praiseworthy in the daring and boldness
toward Pilate. For this man, having cast off all fear, having dared, went in
to Pilate, asking for the body of Jesus; and having gone in he used all
wisdom, that he might come within the desired aim. Therefore he did not use
toward Pilate certain boasts and lofty words, lest having kindled him to anger
he fall from the request; nor does he say to him: Give me the body of Jesus,
of the one who a little before darkened the sun, split the rocks, and shook
the earth, and opened the tombs, and tore the veil of the temple. He says
nothing so great to Pilate. But what? A certain pitiable request, and small to
all. O judge, I have come asking from you a request altogether small.
And thus: Give me a dead man for burial; the body of that Jesus of Nazareth
condemned by you, Jesus the poor, Jesus the houseless, Jesus the hanged, the
naked, the cheap, Jesus the carpenter's son, Jesus the prisoner, the one in
the open air, the stranger, and unrecognized in a strange land, the despised,
and hanged upon all. Give me this stranger; for what does the body of this
stranger profit you? Give me this stranger; for he came here from a far
country, that he might save the stranger; for he came down into the dark to
bring up the stranger. Give me this stranger; for he himself even alone is a
stranger. Give me this stranger, whose country we strangers do not know. Give
me this stranger, whose father we strangers do not know. Give me this
stranger, whose place and birth and manner we strangers do not know. Give me
this stranger, who lived a strange life and way of life among strangers. Give
me this Nazarene stranger [whose birth and manner we strangers do not know.
Give me this willing stranger], who has not here where to lay his head. Give
me this stranger, =43.448= who as a stranger in a strange land, houseless, was
born upon a manger. Give me this


stranger, who from the manger itself fled as a stranger from Herod. Give me
this stranger, who from the swaddling clothes themselves was a sojourner in
Egypt, having no city, no village, no house, no dwelling, no kinsman; this
stranger is in a foreign land. Give me, O governor, this one naked upon the
wood; I will cover him who covered the nakedness of my nature. Give me this
one dead and God together; I will cover him who covered my lawlessness. Give
me, O governor, a dead man, who buried my sin in the Jordan. I plead for a
dead man wronged by all, sold by a friend, betrayed by a disciple, persecuted
by brothers, and struck by a slave. I am ambassador for a dead man, condemned
by those freed by him from slavery, given vinegar to drink by him, wounded by
those healed by him, abandoned by the disciples, and deprived even of his
mother. For a dead man, O governor, I entreat, the houseless one hanging upon
the wood.
For there is not present for this one a father upon earth, no friend, no
disciple, no kinsman, no burier; but he himself alone, the only-begotten of
the Only, God in the world, and no other. These things having been said thus
by Joseph to Pilate, Pilate commanded that the all-holy body of Jesus be given
to him. And having come to the place Golgotha, he took down God in flesh from
the wood, and sets him upon the earth; God in flesh naked, and not a mere man.
And he is seen lying below, who drew all above; and for a little he becomes
breathless, the life and breath of all; and he is seen without eyes, who
created the many-eyed; and he lies supine, the resurrection of all; and God is
put to death in flesh, who raises the dead; and for a little the thunder of
the Word of God is silent; and he is lifted by palms, who holds the earth in
his grasp. Do you then, do you, O Joseph, having asked and received, know whom
you have taken? Having come to the cross, having taken down Jesus, do you know
whom you carried? If you truly know whom you hold, now you have become rich.
And how then do you accomplish this God-bodied and most dread funeral of
Jesus? Praiseworthy is your desire; but more praiseworthy is the manner of
your soul. Do you then, do you not shudder, carrying upon your hands him at
whom the cherubim shudder? Or with what fear at all do you strip the linen
from that divine flesh? And how then did you reverently lower your eye? Do you
not shudder gazing, uncovering the nature of the flesh of God, of the one
above nature? Do you then, do you, tell me, O Joseph, also bury toward the
east a dead man, the East of easts? And do you also with your fingers in the
manner of the dead close the eyes of Jesus, of him who with the undefiled
finger opened the eye of the blind? And do you also shut the mouth of him who
opened the mouth of the mute? And do you also compose the hands of him who
stretched out the withered hands? Or also =43.449= do you bind the feet in the
manner of the dead of him who gave walking to unmoving feet? And do you also
lift upon a bed him who commanded the paralytic: Take up your bed, and walk?
And do you also pour myrrhs upon him who emptied his own heavenly myrrh, and
sanctified the world? And do you also dare to wipe that still-bleeding
God-bodied side of Jesus, of the God who healed the woman with the flow of
blood? And do you also wash with water the body of God, of him who washed all,
and gave the purification?

And what lamps then will you kindle for the true light, who enlightened every
man? And what funeral songs will you sing to him who is unceasingly praised by
every heavenly army? And do you also shed tears as for a dead man over him who
wept, and raised Lazarus dead four days? And do you also make dirges for him
who gives joy to all, and who dissolved the grief of Eve? Yet I bless your


hands, O Joseph, that served, and that felt the still-bleeding God-bodied
hands and feet of Jesus; I bless your hands that approached the bleeding side
of God, before Thomas the faithful unbeliever and praiseworthy busybody; I
bless your mouth filled insatiably and united to the mouth of Jesus, and
filled from there with Holy Spirit; I bless your eyes set against the eyes of
Jesus, and that partook from there of the true light; I bless your face that
drew near to the face of God; I bless your shoulders that carried him who
carries all; I bless your head to which Jesus, the head of all, drew near; I
bless your hands, in which you carried him who carries all; I bless Joseph and
Nicodemus. For they have become, before the cherubim, those who lifted God in
themselves and bear him; they have become, before the six-winged, ministers of
God, covering and honoring the Lord not with wings but with linen cloths. Him
at whom the cherubim tremble, this one Joseph and Nicodemus bear upon their
shoulders, and all the ranks of the bodiless are astonished.
For Joseph and Nicodemus came; therefore the whole God-host of the angels also
ran together; and cherubim go before, and seraphim run with them, and thrones
help to carry, and the six-winged cover, and the many-eyed shudder, seeing
Jesus in flesh without eyes, and powers cover together, and the principalities
sing, and the ranks shudder, and all the armies of the lofty orders are
astonished, and being amazed they are at a loss among themselves, and say:
What is this fearful word, and fear, and trembling, and manner? What is this
great, and paradoxical, and incomprehensible spectacle? He who above is to us
the bodiless as God naked, unseeable, below is to mortals naked, well-seeable.
=43.452= Him before whom cherubim stand with reverence, this one Joseph and
Nicodemus bury with license. When did he come down who did not leave the
things above? How did he go out who is within? How did he come upon earth who
fills all things? How did he strip who escaped the notice of all? He who above
with the Father as God is without lack, below with the mother as a mortal
truly without lack. He who never appeared to us, how does he appear to men as
man together and as God who loves mankind? How was the invisible seen? How was
the immaterial made flesh? How did the impassible suffer? How did the judge
stand at the tribunal? How did life taste death? How is the uncontainable
contained in a tomb? How does the tomb house him who did not leave the
paternal bosom? How does he enter a cave's gate who opened the gates of
paradise, and did not tear the gates of the Virgin, but crushed the gates of
Hades; who did not open gates upon Thomas, but opened the gates of the kingdom
to men, yet keeping the gates of the tomb and the seals unopened? And how is
he reckoned among the dead, he who is free among the dead? How does the light
that has no evening come among those in darkness and the shadow of death?
Where does he go? Where does he come, he who cannot be held by death?

What is the word? What is the manner? What is the counsel of his descent into
Hades? Perhaps he comes down to bring up Adam the condemned and our
fellow-slave. Truly he goes to seek the first-formed as a lost sheep. Surely
he also wishes to visit those sitting in darkness and the shadow of death;
surely God goes to loose from the pains the captive Adam, and Eve his
fellow-captive, and her son, whence also he has been shown as his son. But let
us go down together, but let us dance together, but let us hasten, but let us
leap together, but let us escort, but let us hymn, but let us make speed,
seeing God's reconciliations toward men, a release of the condemned coming
from a good Master. For he who is by nature a lover of mankind goes to lead
out those from of


old bound with much valor and authority, those dwelling in tombs, whom bitter
and insatiable death swallowed tyrannically, having tyrannized, and having
plundered from God together and heaped up, to number them with those dwelling
above, having freed them. There is bound Adam the first-formed, and
first-born, lower than all the lowest condemned. There is Abel the first-dead,
and first-just shepherd, a type of Christ the shepherd of the unjust
slaughter. There is Noah the type of Christ, builder of the great ark of God's
Church, which saved all the beastlike nations from the flood of impiety
through a dove, the Holy Spirit, and banished from it the gloomy raven. There
is Abraham the Christ-father, the sacrificer, who sacrificed to God a most
blessed sacrifice at once sword-against-sword and death-against-death. There
below is bound Isaac, who of old was a bound Christ- =43.453= type made by
Abraham above. There is Jacob in Hades, in pain below, who before was in pain
above because of Joseph. There is Joseph the bound, who in Egypt became in the
prison a prisoner and master as a type of Christ. There is Moses in dark
places below, who once was in the ark in dark places above. There is Daniel in
Hades the pit, who once was in the pit of lions above. There is Jeremiah as in
a pit of mire, in the pit of Hades and of the corruption of death. There in
the world-receiving whale of Hades lies as a type Jonah of Christ, the eternal
and pre-eternal Jonah who lives unto the age, and unto the ages of ages and
upon the age. And still there is David the God-father, from whom is Christ
according to the flesh. And why do I speak of David and Jonah and Solomon?
There is even the great John himself, greater than all the prophets, as in the
dark womb proclaiming Christ beforehand to all those in Hades; the double
forerunner, and herald of living and dead; who from Herod's prison was sent to
the public prison of Hades, whence of those from of old, both just and unjust,
who have fallen asleep. And the prophets and all the just were offering from
there unceasing prayers to God in a hidden-mystical way, asking ransom from
that all-painful and downcast, enemy-ruled, gloomy, and all-evening all-night.
And one was saying to God: From the belly of Hades hear my cry, my voice; and
another: Out of the depths I have cried to you, Lord; Lord, hear my voice; and
another: Make your face to shine, and we shall be saved; and another: You who
sit upon the cherubim, appear; and another: Stir up your might, and come to
save us; and another: Let your compassions quickly overtake us, Lord; and
another: Deliver my soul from lowest Hades; and another: Lord, bring up my
soul from Hades; and another: Do not abandon my soul to Hades; and another:
Let my life come up from corruption to you, Lord my God. Having heard all of
whom, the all-compassionate God, Christ, judged it not just to impart his own
love of mankind only to those in his time and after him; but also to those
held in Hades before his sojourn, and sitting in darkness, in the shadow of
death.
Therefore men who were in flesh the God Word visited through ensouled flesh;
but to souls released from bodies he appeared in Hades through his divine and
undefiled soul, the body but not the Godhead having been released. Therefore
let us hasten in mind, and let us walk even to Hades, that we may see how
there once he by force overpowers the one strong in force, the force-tyrant of
force, and with a whole-army people, by his lightning, he subdues those
immortal ranks of the phalanxes without a hand =43.456= ; having taken
doorless doors out of the midst, and having broken woodless gates with the
wood of the cross, Christ the door, and with the divine nails having crushed
and smashed eternal bars; and with God-handed bonds having dissolved the
unbreakable chains as wax; and with the God-sided spear having pierced the


fleshless heart of the tyrant. There he crushed the strengths of the bows,
when with the cross he stretched God-handed bowstrings of archers. Therefore
if you follow Christ with quiet, you will now see where he bound the tyrant;
and where he hung up his head; and how he dug up the prison, and where he led
out the prisoners, and how he trampled the serpent, and where he hung the
head; and how he freed Adam, and how he raised Eve, and how he dissolved the
middle wall, and how he condemned the bitter dragon, and how he set up the
unconquered trophies; and where he put death to death, and how he corrupted
corruption, and where he restored man to the ancient dignity. He therefore who
yesterday in the economy was declining the legions of angels, and saying to
Peter: Can I not now present more than twelve legions of angels? today in a
manner befitting God together and in warlike fashion, and in a manner
befitting a master, comes down below of Hades and death; and through death the
tyrant, having not some twelve legions of the immortal armies of the bodiless
and of invisible orders, but myriads of myriads and thousands of thousands of
angels, authorities, throneless thrones, six-winged, wingless, many-eyed,
eyeless, heavenly orders; inasmuch as inasmuch as escorting and spear-bearing
and honoring Christ as their own Master and king; not as allies; far be it!
For of what alliance does the almighty Christ have need? But owing together
and loving to stand always by their own Master, God, certain trustworthy
spear-bearers, hoplites and bright scepter-bearers of the sharp divine
masterly scepter-bearing, by a nod alone with haste at the divine speed
outstripping one another, together bringing the act to work by the command,
and crowned with the victory toward arrays of enemies and lawless ones.
Therefore also then coming down with courses together and as fellow-runners
with God and Master upon the things in Hades and subterranean, and deeper than
all earth, the under-earth dwellings of those fallen asleep from of old,
leading out with valor those bound from of old. When then the all-doored, and
sunless, and all-evening prisons and dwellings of Hades, descents and caves,
the God-host, light-bearing presence of the Master overtook, Gabriel the
commander-in-chief goes before all; inasmuch as inasmuch as being from custom
one who brings gospels of joy to men and a certain strong speech of most
archangelic and most general =43.457= bright and lionlike voice toward the
opposing powers, and he says: Lift up gates, you rulers of yours; after whom
Michael also cries: And be lifted up, eternal gates.
Then the powers also say: Depart, you lawless gatekeepers; then also

Make a way for him who has mounted upon the settings of Hades. The Lord is his

their bridles saying: Who is this king of glory? Who is this so great one, who
with so many is accomplishing such wonders here? Who is this king of glory,
who in Hades is now doing the things that never happened in Hades? Who is this
who is leading out from here those fallen asleep from of old? Who is this who
has loosed and destroyed our, the unconquered ones', daring and force, and is

strong and unconquered in wars. This is that one who from the heavenly vaults
exiled and cast you out, O wretched and lawless tyrants! That is this one who
in the waters of Jordan crushed the heads of your dragons. This is that one
who through the cross pilloried, and triumphed over, and put you to death.
That is this one who bound, and darkened, and sent you to the abyss. This is
that one who sends you to eternal fire and Gehenna and destroys you.
Henceforth do not delay, nor wait, but hasten, and lead out the prisoners,
whom even until now you have swallowed badly. For your force has henceforth
been destroyed; your tyranny has henceforth ceased; your insolence has been

trampled and has perished. These things the masterly powers of the Master were
saying to the opposing powers, together also urging them on. And some were
digging down the prison from the foundations themselves; and others were
pursuing the opposing authorities fleeing from the outer chambers further in.
And others were searching the descents, and the forts, and the caves, and they
were running. And others, one another from elsewhere, were bringing a prisoner
to the Master, and others were binding the tyrant with unbreakable bonds, and
others were releasing those bound from of old; and others were commanding, and
others were ministering as quickly as possible; and some, as the Master was
entering further in, were running ahead, and others were following as a king
and God, victory-bearing.

These things then thus, but also beyond these things, happening in Hades and
being cried, all being rumored and shaken, as the presence of the Master was
about to overtake


the lowest of the lowest themselves, that Adam, the of all =43.461= men
first-created, and first-formed, and first-dead, further in than all, being
held a prisoner with much security, was hearing the feet of the Master
entering toward the prisoners, and he recognized his voice walking in the
prison, and having turned to all those bound from of old with him he says: I
hear the sound of the feet of someone entering toward us; and if at all that
one has deigned to come here, we have been freed from the bonds; if at all we
shall see that one with us, we are ransomed from Hades.

Master entered toward them, holding the victorious weapon of the cross. Whom
seeing, Adam the first-formed, and in astonishment striking his breast, cried

Therefore arise, let us go from here, from death to life, from corruption to
incorruption, from darkness to the eternal light. Arise, let us go from here,
from pain to gladness, from slavery to freedom, from prison to the Jerusalem
above, from bonds to rest, from detention to the delight of paradise, from
earth to heaven. For for this I died, and rose, that I might be lord of both
dead and living. Arise, let us go from here; for my heavenly Father awaits the
lost sheep. The ninety-nine sheep of the angels await the fellow-slave Adam,
when he shall arise, and when he shall go up and return to God. A cherubic
throne has been prepared; those who bear him up are swift and ready; the
bridal chamber has been prepared; the foods are ready; the eternal tents and
dwellings are ready; the treasuries of good things have been opened, the
kingdom of the heavens has been prepared before the ages; the good things
which eye has not seen, and ear has not heard, and have not arisen upon a
man's heart, await the man.
These things and such things of the Master saying, there arise with him Adam
united in him, and Eve also arises with him; and many other bodies of those
fallen asleep from of old in faith arose proclaiming the Master's three-day
resurrection, which brightly, you faithful, let us receive, and let us see,
and let us embrace, dancing with angels, keeping feast with the bodiless,
together also glorifying Christ who raised you from corruption, and made you
live; to whom be the glory, and the power with his beginningless Father, and
his all-holy and good and life-giving Spirit; now and always and unto the ages
of ages. Amen.
