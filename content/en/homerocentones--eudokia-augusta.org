Hypothesis of the Homeric Centos.

This is the book of god-fearing Patricius the priest, who wrought a great
work, from Homer's book fashioning a precious song of glorious verses,
announcing the deeds of the unconquered God: how he came into the assembly of
men, how he took human form and, while still small, was hidden in the womb of
a blameless maiden, whom the boundless circle could not contain; and how he
drew the breast of the God-bearing virgin, a stream of maiden milk welling
forth; how Herod, a fool, slew children still tender-minded, seeking the fate
of the immortal God; how John washed him in the streams of the river, and how
he obtained twelve blameless men as companions; how many whose limbs God had
fashioned whole, driving out hateful diseases and the blindness of the eyes;
and how he quenched the flowing tracks of blood when a much-weeping woman
touched his robe; and how many, overcome by grievous fates, he led back to
light from the earthly pit; and how he left us memorials of the holy passion,
and how under mortal hands he was stretched in freezing bonds, himself
willing; for none of those upon the earth would war against God who rules on
high, unless he himself should command; how he died, how he broke the iron
gates of Hades, and from there led God-obedient souls into heaven under the
unstained promptings of the Father, rising on the third light-bringing dawn,
primordial offspring of God the Begetter who has no beginning.

These things Bishop Patricius set forth; and this is the apology of Eudocia
Augusta the Athenian, wife of Theodosius Augustus the younger, son of King
Arcadius: This is the history of a song that delights God. And Patricius, who
wisely wrote this book, is worthy of everlasting praise throughout, because he
first devised the glorious work. Yet even so he did not tell all things wholly
true; nor did he keep the harmony of the verses entire, nor did that man, when
he sang, remember only those verses which the bronze heart of blameless Homer
spoke. But I, when I saw Patricius's famous work half-finished, taking the
holy pages in hand, whatever verses in the books were not according to order,
all these together I drew out of that man's wise book; and whatever he left
out, I wrote again in the pages and gave harmony to the holy verses. And if
anyone should blame us and drag us into censure, because there are many
doublets in the illustrious book, and much of Homeric verses that is not
lawful, let him know this, that all are servants of necessity. And if anyone,
hearing the sober song of the hymn-maker Tatian, should delight his own
hearing, because that man never, having mixed doublets from Homeric books, set
them in his own tablet, it is no wonder, because that man from Homeric song,
and that man from his own verses, made a song telling the evil war-cry of
Trojans and Argives, and how the sons of the Achaeans sacked the city of
Priam, Troy itself, and gods themselves and men fighting in grievous tumult,
whom once the bronze-voiced man Homer cried aloud. But Patricius, who wrote
this wise tablet, in place of the Argive host spoke the race of the Hebrews,
and in place of the demonic and god-opposing phalanx he sang the immortals and
the Son and the Begetter. Yet even so the labor was common to both, to
Patricius and to me, though I am a woman; but he alone among men won great
glory, who first planted the famous foundation of the house, bringing forth a
fair report of the mortal race.

1 Concerning the Father's good pleasure and the sending of the Son. Hear, you
countless tribes of neighboring men, both those who dwell toward the dawn and
the sun and those behind toward the misty gloom, that you may know well both
God and man, whose fame shall be as far as the dawn is spread. Hear me,
suitors of the illustrious kingdom, that I may speak what the spirit in my
breast commands. Saffron-robed Dawn was spreading over all the earth,
lamenting piteously, shedding a blooming tear; for there was no man sweet of
heart nor kindly, but they were sacrificing oxen, sheep, and fat goats, and
all were praying to the cloud-wrapped son of Cronus and to those under
Tartarus, who are called Titans, wretches who know neither justice well nor
ordinances, covered with mist and cloud; and never did he let them stand, and
some harsh daemon arose for them to pray to daemons and to promise hecatombs.
But he himself saved them and devised a great benefit, he who rules over all
mortals and immortals, having raised up a son, a lover of the people, a ruler
of the people, an only, well-beloved son among many possessions, who knew what
is and what will be and what was before, who sat near him; and he loved him
most of all, embracing him as if he were his own son. And addressing him he
spoke winged words: "Would you now obey me, dear child, in whatever I say? For
no one will think a mind better than this; and in your own breast your spirit
knows gentle counsels; for you think the things that I think. Therefore I will
speak the word to you readily, and will not hide it. You see that again
mortals upon the boundless earth, insulting us, contrive reckless deeds,
sacrificing oxen, sheep, and fat goats, and they know nothing of death and
black fate. But even so I wish and long all my days to save the race and
offspring of all men, that the race may not perish seedless and unseen. But go
now among the people of the Achaeans, and nowhere else, coming down from
heaven out of the untilled aether; and with your gentle words restrain each
man, with your gentleness and winning speech, that you may win great honor and
glory for me, which may also be among men yet to be. And let it be your own
care, and heed my words. And they, as soon as you go, will plot evils
afterward, eager to kill and to strip away your famous boast. But you,
speaking to them with close-packed words, honoring all, do not exalt yourself
in spirit, that they may not all perish when God is angered; for you yourself
will go long, testing each, and you would know who are righteous and who
lawless, and who are hospitable, and whose mind is god-fearing. And you would
be more honored and dearer to men, if you should die and fill up the fate of
life. And whoever is himself blameless and knows what is blameless, he would
quickly turn his mind after your heart and mine, and all things would be his
that he desires in his mind. But whoever refuses and stubbornly denies us
both, very like one another, always thereafter toil and cares will be his, and
evil famine would drive him over the divine earth, or seizing him I will hurl
him into misty Tartarus, angry at this deceit. These things will not be
unaccomplished."

2 Concerning the obedience of the Son. And standing by him the glorious son
addressed him: "O Father, you who rule over gods and men, I know, I
understand; you command these things to one who perceives, as a father to his
child, and I will never forget them; for I too am God, and my race is from
there, from where yours is. But consider quite at ease whatever you wish; for
the word will not be thrown away, whatever you say. If it is my fate to die
and to follow destiny, I will accomplish all and obey, as you command in your
own spirit, and let all the Achaeans know that my

heart in my breast is never faithless. I would wish, even having suffered many
pains, to save the race and offspring of all men; for my mind too is righteous
and not slack; I would rather the people be safe than perish." Thinking these
things they stood by one another by one another's will, bringing grace to men,
immortals to mortals over the grain-giving earth.

3 Concerning the Annunciation. And then they sent a messenger, who should
announce to a woman the counsel that then pleased them as they planned; and
swiftly he darted to the much-nourishing earth. Coming down from heaven out of
the untilled aether he comes announcing the light of early-born Dawn. He went
to go into the richly wrought chamber, where a maiden sat on a chair (and a
footstool was under her feet), unwed, whom a man had not yet led under the
yoke. Then in the halls her father and lady mother had given her to a dear
husband; and he received her readily, neither using the pretext of the bed nor
lacking it nor suspecting anything. And as God commanded, never to go up into
her bed nor to mingle, which is the custom of humans, of men and women. She
sat at the hearth with serving women, a modest virgin, having gone up to the
upper room, turning sea-purple wool, a wonder to see; and a trusted servant
stood on either side of her. And then the messenger came, who should announce
to the woman; he stood very near and spoke a word to her: "Hail to me, O
queen, throughout, until old age come upon men and women on the
much-nourishing earth, and death, which befall humans; for I will speak truly
and will not hide. We asked God to show a sign; and he will show forth to us,
of the men who are of your blood's race, one who will rule over all the
neighbors, appearing as a vigorous and strong man, eating and drinking such
things as mortal men eat, casting an evil cloak about his shoulders, like a
household slave, struck and reviled with an enduring heart." So he spoke; and
he stirred the spirit in her breast, and the hairs stood up on her bent limbs;
and she sat in silence, bending her dear heart. And at last answering him with
words she said: "O friend, since indeed it is lawful for me also to answer,
why does that great God command me? And I am ashamed, that he always remembers
me as kindly, and I am not forgotten by him. But what could I do? God
accomplishes all things through all, however he may wish; for he is strongest
of all. But I will not speak thus, but with an oath, never to go up into the
bed nor to mingle, which is the custom of humans, of men and women." And
answering her the noble, high-speaking one said: "Hail, gracious woman; and as
the years revolve he will show forth who will rule over all the neighbors;
whose fame shall be as far as the dawn is spread, over all men, and there will
be a good gift for him. And another thing I will tell you, and you put it in
your mind: be silent and hold it in your thought, and do not ask." So he
spoke; and he stirred the spirit in her breast, but she could no longer
tremble nor draw back. But when he had spoken all the command, swiftly he
darted from the wide-wayed earth, a persuading herald, knowing prudent
counsels; and he reached steep Olympus, and came among those assembled. And
she, having spoken thus, went away to the fair house.

4 Concerning the birth and the star and the shepherds. But when Eileithyia who
brings birth-pangs, as one month waned and another was beginning, brought him
forth into the light, and he saw the rays of the sun as the year came round
again, and the seasons arrived, a brightness reached heaven, and all the earth
around laughed; all

gathered together, both young and old, marveled, and the spirit fell beside
the feet of all, when a most shining star rose over, which shines most
brightly, bathed in Ocean, showing a sign to mortals; and its rays are
conspicuous; which a shepherd in the field over woolly sheep marveled at in
his heart; for he thought it was a god. That the one born of a virgin is
himself the creator of all. "And coming here he promised me gifts and nodded
assent; he would not speak a lie; for he is very prudent; for I have not yet
seen nor heard one speaking of an immortal god so loving mortals face to face,
except one, who hates sleep and food for me, whom the father of men and gods
loved in his heart, an only well-beloved son among many possessions, a son
since he granted me that he be born and reared, and the mother who bore him
and nursed him when he was small, arise, that he might bring light to
immortals and to mortals, and first-born, who has been made the origin for
all, knowing well by the promptings of the wisdom of God." For in it he
fashioned the earth, in it the heaven, in it the sea, and in it all the signs
with which heaven is crowned, the unwearying sun and the full moon, fishes and
birds, and whatever dear thing should come to the hands, those of the sea,
whose care is the works of the sea, sea-monsters, which groaning Amphitrite
feeds in thousands, and horses and mules and the strong heads of oxen and
bears and wild swine and bright-eyed lions. And under them the divine earth
grew fresh grass and dewy lotus and crocus and hyacinth. And there came to be
wet water and a high-leaved tree, alder and poplar and fragrant cypress, pears
and pomegranates and apple trees of splendid fruit; last a man, most
excellent; and he was like a marvel to him in form and stature and the mind
within equal. And beside him a lady wife prepared the bed and couch for the
sake of his beauty, that he might be among the immortals. But it did not then
ward off baneful destruction from them; for they perished by their own
reckless deeds.

5 Concerning paradise and the serpent and the deceit. Then a great sign
appeared, a serpent red on the back, terrible and grievous and wild and not to
be fought, who used to do many evils, as many as all the others together, and
by persuasion he stole the mind of those who think closely. And he met a girl
drawing water before the city; at once he spoke a honeyed and crafty word, and
with a mortal voice he restrained her and spoke: "Would you now obey me, dear
child, in whatever I say. And I will not be a vain watcher for you nor off the
mark. Here fair trees have grown flourishing, sweet figs and flourishing
olives and many others besides. And put a gracious spirit in you; of these the
fruit never perishes nor fails. If somehow you could lie in wait and catch
that first, then you yourself would have benefit, if you should eat, and you
will make immortal and ageless all your days both man and house; and he would
grant like-mindedness." Speaking thus he persuaded her, when he had declared
all; for you know what sort of spirit is in a woman's breast. But when he had
shown where the tall trees had grown, at once then with words she asked her
husband each thing, begging him to dine; and he refused groaning, but still
somehow he remembered the commands which he had enjoined who rules over all
mortals and immortals. And she always with soft and winning words and many
infatuations led the man's mind aside to remember food and eating as quickly
as possible, having wrought murder for her wedded husband and for herself; and
she sent many strong souls forth to Hades, as there is nothing more dreadful
and more shameless than a woman. For six days only they toiled thus night and
day; but when fair-haired Dawn completed the seventh day, he delighted holding
in his hands the splendid gifts of God, then again he who rules over all
mortals and immortals thought another thing in mind and heart; appearing as a
vigorous and strong man, the insolence and good order of men

to look upon, either suspecting someone or even as God commanded. But he going
again like a child sank under his mother; and hiding him he likened him to
another man, all-tender, such as the children of lords are; and he was fixed
in the lower belly, near Eileithyia. But when the months and days were
completed, he loosed the maiden girdle, and poured sleep down. And she, having
conceived, bore a strong-minded child, a modest virgin having gone up to the
upper room, and she brought him forth into the light. And he saw the rays of
the sun, appearing as a man both in body and in voice, like him in all things,
in stature and in the fair eyes and in body; but his mind was firm, as before.
A brightness reached heaven, and all the earth around laughed. God made him
conspicuous, as he showed him, his own child; and she received him in her
fragrant bosom. And the mother who bore him and nursed him when he was small
was warmed as when dew is about the ears of grain. And so she reared the
child, and he gave playthings to the heart.

6 Concerning the magi. And each sent a herald to bring gifts; and the sun set
and the famous gifts were at hand. And the children of the blameless,
holy-bearing one, receiving the gifts, which they brought looking at the star
from the east, set the very beautiful gifts beside the modest mother, golden,
that he might remember me all his days.

7 Concerning foolish Herod. But when a year had passed, and the seasons had
turned, as many things as the messenger went quickly through the city
everywhere, then he stretched the most dreadful strife of war upon infants,
who have no care for the works of war, he who wished to kill and to tear out
the dear heart. And he did many reckless deeds by force and strength of hands,
wishing to master all and to rule over all, king of the Sidonians, whom his
own house covered, a fool; for many took the spirit from him. And he set
unspeakable wailing and grief upon the parents, thinking in his heart things
that were not going to be accomplished. Then in the halls the father and lady
mother, hiding him with their hands, led him out of the house to the door,
considering whether her blameless son might escape death; for she feared that
the terrible man might cut the throat with iron; soon he might even accuse the
guiltless.

8 Concerning the flight into Egypt. This counsel seemed best to her in her
heart: to go to Egypt, a long and grievous road: "But let us go, let us not
linger long on the way, before we suffer some evil; a fool knows after the
thing is done." Then word and deed were together for those hurrying. On the
fifth day they came to wide Egypt, fearing; for a strong trembling holds at a
man's shout.

9 Concerning the return from Egypt. But when that man too went into the house
of Hades, who wished to kill and to tear out the dear heart, again from Egypt,
of the Zeus-fallen river, they sailed (and God laid the great-monstered sea)
to the high-roofed house and to his own fatherland, Phoenicia, where his house
and possessions are. And they came to the wheat-bearing plain; and there then
are houses and dances and the risings of the sun. But he, having a reckless
spirit, as before

, he used to sleep in beds, in the arms of a nurse, gleaming with beauty and
graces; and the maiden gazed at her dear child newly born though he was in the
house, who rules over all mortals and immortals; for a monstrous wonder had
been made; for he did not seem to be the child of a mortal man, but of a god.

10 Concerning John the Baptist. But when the year came, as the years revolved,
late, late-fulfilled, whose fame will never perish, first the noble one fled,
and one far better pursued him, coming down from heaven; and he will have him
all his days, both glory and splendor and benefit, looking upon the insolence
and good order of men. And he had a herald, a little older than himself, who
was the most beautiful of mortal men, who knew what is and what will be and
what was before, who was of his own age; and he was pleasing to the heart,
being trusted; for the father and other elders had sent him forth to proclaim
to the assembly the long-haired Achaeans. Proclaiming he kept crying, thinking
friendly things in his heart, that a man would come clothed in great strength,
which should be a defense against evil for all the Danaans; whose light is
also sharpest to look upon. "The son boasts that his race is from a good
father, a protector of strangers, who most of all is angry at evil deeds. To
them is entrusted great heaven and Olympus and the lotus-growing plains and
the rich works of the fields and the shadowy mountains and the sounding sea;
therefore now let no man at all be lawless. And you yourselves be stirred and
all follow with me, lest the immortals, angered at evil deeds, turn us, and
those again empty as before, whom you know most of all to bear misery, adorned
with gold and amber and ivory, whom Hephaestus made with knowing mind. But
come, as I shall speak, let us all obey." Speaking thus he stirred the might
and spirit of each. He went to go to the assembly, and a great crowd followed
with him, and I could not tell nor name all. And swiftly the assemblies and
seats of mortals were filled. And when they were gathered and had become
assembled, standing in the midst the loud herald addressed them: "Hear, you
countless tribes of neighboring men. He is reverend, worthy of awe, who sent
me to inquire; him I fear and am ashamed of in my heart; he will not speak a
lie; for he is very prudent. But obey you also, since it is better to obey;
for if some other of those upon the earth commanded me, we would say it was a
lie and would rather withdraw; but now, for I myself heard God and looked upon
him face to face, and he enjoined each thing upon me, and he was like a marvel
to him, who rules over all mortals and immortals. He told me that all these
things would be accomplished afterward; for I know this well in mind and
heart, as one month wanes and another is beginning, that here he will come
clothed in great strength, who is better than I to perceive and to judge. And
he would know who are righteous and who lawless or hospitable, and whose mind
is god-fearing. And they, as soon as he goes, will plot evils afterward,
foolish country-folk, thinking daily things, dear sons of the men who are the
best here." So he spoke, and they all became silent in stillness. And they
reared him well in their houses and cherished him, and taking hand in hand
each pledged faith. And they washed themselves and threw the defilements into
the sea, as many as were leaders of the Danaans and lords.

11 Concerning the divine baptism. But when he reached the measure of glorious
youth, he went to go, like a princely boy, all-tender, such as the children of
lords are, to save the race

and offspring of all men. And as many things as the messenger went quickly
through the city everywhere; and the porticoes and courts and houses of men
were filled with those gathered; for many marveled seeing, and they asked one
another who he was and from where he came; for a monstrous wonder had been
made, and he did not seem to be the child of a mortal man, but of a god, who
rules over all mortals and immortals. Then his son came, and his name was
Theoclymenus; and he took his father's scepter, always imperishable. And the
herald, seeing him from nearby, recognized him, and kissed his head and both
fair eyes, and cried piercing, making himself heard to mortals: "The man is
near (we will not search long), if you wish to escape evil death and black
fate; for not even anyone can match his might. I liken him to a thick-fleeced
ram; for he was a ram, far the best of all the flock. And he himself, if he
wishes, will heal, and no other." Speaking thus he stirred the might and
spirit of each. And kissing him, clinging and leaping upon him, he addressed
him: "O friend, since you have returned to us who greatly longed, praying much
and hoping to see, health and great joy; and may God give you all blessings,
as many as you wish and as was dear to your heart." And when they saw one
another and marked each other face to face, they greeted with the right hand
and with honeyed words, and taking hand in hand each pledged faith. Meanwhile
his care was as firm as for a god. But when they came to the ford of the
fair-flowing river, where the Cydones dwelt about the streams of Jordan, which
sends forth far the most beautiful of rivers upon the earth; he recognized him
flowing forward, and he prayed in his heart and bringing him forth he washed
him in the streams of the river beside the walls of Pheia, about the streams
of Jordan, and anointed him with ambrosia, and put immortal clothes about him,
into the river, where the place seemed best to him; a brightness all-shining
through the aether reached heaven. And the herald, a little older than
himself, bringing him forth far, washed him in the streams of the river. But
when he had washed him and anointed him richly with oil, and he put on the
clothes which the unwed virgin had given him, and with the ambrosial robe,
which was scented for him, he prayed, stretching his hand to starry heaven.
And high above the clouds he saw a trembling dove between earth and starry
heaven, which came clear to him to the rich feast of God. And he put on the
clothes which the unwed virgin had given him. A brightness reached heaven, and
all the earth around laughed; and the wave rushed back down the fair streams.
But when he was about to enter the lovely city, the people followed, as sheep
follow after a ram, glad toward the town. And the city was filled with those
gathered, and the assembly was in turmoil, and the earth groaned beneath; and
different was the tongue of men scattered far. And he saved cities, and most
of all he himself would recognize honor, which bends the minds even of other
nobles. Such a one in mind to take up fair prizes, he made many cunning works
with knowing mind, many and graceful, and he warded off evil even so.

12 Concerning Jesus going out into the wilderness. But he, having his own
strong spirit, as before, doing favor to his dear father, who rules over all,
himself mortal, and he sucked a woman's breast, and he saw the cities of many
men and knew their mind. And he endured such things as no other mortal upon
the earth yet, having done nothing unlawful nor spoken anything. Leading good
counsels and weaving counsel he went; and much his heart surged as he went.
And when they came down from the city, they soon reached the field, in which
they used to eat and sit and sleep. And he tended him kindly in the field,
apart from the city. No plant, no fig, no vine, nor olive, no pear, no
garden-bed flourished in his place. There neither works of oxen nor of men
appeared; he went nearer, testing the many-fruited threshing-floor, bears and
wild swine and bright-eyed lions.

13 Concerning the calling of the disciples. But when he reached the measure of
glorious youth, then choosing twelve best of companions; for they seemed to
him to be distinctly the best, who would honor him in their heart as a god;
for these are the called of mortals upon the boundless earth after him among
the others; and he was distinguished even through all. Gathering them he
fitted close counsel: "Hear me, suitors of the illustrious kingdom, that I may
speak what the spirit in my breast commands; for I will not prophesy without
trial, but knowing well the end; for mine is not to be taken back nor
deceitful. And I too boast that my race is from a good father, a protector of
strangers, who most of all is angry at evil deeds; for I am his child; and he
boasts to be my father. Therefore he sent me forth, to teach all these things.
And we two know the signs of all things hidden; and we know whatever happens
upon the much-nourishing earth, both the portion and the portionless of mortal
men, looking upon the insolence and good order of men. But obey you also,
since it is better to obey, us, because we are harmless escorts of all, three,
turning both ways, grown from one neck, of the same age, equal-yoked, whose
strength is not weak, whose virtue and honor and force are even greater than
all things that breathe and creep upon the earth and the shadowy mountains and
the sounding sea." Speaking thus he stirred the might and spirit of each. And
he went to go forward, and with the other hand he took Peter, who was the most
trusted of his companions. And he also led three others, in whom he most
trusted for every purpose, that of themselves they might enter the divine
contest. And they obtained, those whom he himself would have wished to choose,
seafarers, whose care is the works of the sea.

And he stood having gone up to a rugged lookout, and they whirled about and
all stood opposite, who were the most trusted and dearest of his companions,
and the mother who bore him and nursed him when he was small. And he himself
appeared toward the close house of his mighty father, going together with
clouds into wide heaven. And of themselves the gates of heaven groaned, which
the Hours held, from the hiding-places on all sides; and they did not fail to
know the lord. And again he sat down upon the throne, from where he had risen,
being such as I first perceived him, like in body and form to his own father
God; and he sat before him exulting in glory, darting to the right; and all
the earth around laughed. To him is entrusted great heaven and Olympus and the
scepter and ordinances, that you may counsel for them, and the sun, who looks
upon all and hears all, and the Bear, which they also call by the name Wagon,
and rivers and earth and those who have toiled beneath. He judges many strifes

But whoever refuses and stubbornly denies, him seizing he will hurl into misty
Tartarus and Cocytus, which is a branch of Styx's water. But now I will
prophesy to you and will not hide, that Jesus will come; and let it be gospel
for you, that someone of men born later may also say thus, that God readily
granted you a divine song, late, late-fulfilled, whose fame will never perish,
who dwelt in Phrygia by the streams of Sangarius, and exulting he was
distinguished among all the heroes, as many as were leaders of the Danaans and
lords.
