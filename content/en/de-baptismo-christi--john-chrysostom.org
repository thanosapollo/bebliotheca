* On the Baptism of Christ
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

A discourse against those who stay away from the divine assemblies, and on the
holy and saving baptism of our Savior Jesus Christ, and concerning those who
communicate unworthily, and that those who leave the divine liturgy
unfinished, and go out before the last prayer, imitate Judas.
=49.363=
αʹ. All of you are in good cheer today, but I alone am in pain. For when I
look upon this spiritual sea, and behold the boundless wealth of the Church,
then consider that when the feast has passed this multitude too, having leaped
away
from us, will be gone, I am stung and I am in pain in my soul, that the
Church, having borne so many children, cannot enjoy them at every assembly,
but only at a feast. How great a spiritual exultation would it be, how great a
joy, how great a glory of God, how great a benefit of souls, if at every
assembly we saw the courts of the church filled in this way? But now sailors
and helmsmen do everything so that they may run through the sea and come to
harbor; we, however, are eager to toss through every sea, continually being
sunk in the billows of worldly affairs, and turning about in the marketplaces
and in the law-courts, and here meeting barely once or twice in the whole
year. Or do you not know that just as harbors in the sea, so God planted the
churches in the cities, that fleeing here from the storm of worldly tumults we
may enjoy the greatest calm? For here there is no surge of waves to fear, nor
raids of pirates, nor an onset of evildoers, nor violence of winds, nor plots
of wild beasts; for it is a harbor freed from all these, a spiritual harbor of
souls. And you are witnesses of what is said. For if any of you should now
unfold his conscience, he will find much stillness within; for anger does not
trouble, desire does not burn, envy does not waste, madness does not puff up,
love of vainglory does not corrupt, but all these beasts have been stilled, as
by some divine charm, the hearing of the divine Scriptures, entering through
each man's ear into the soul, and putting these irrational passions to sleep.
Of what misery then would it not be, when we are about to enjoy so great a
=49.364= philosophy, not to resort continually and go to the common mother of
all, I mean the Church? For what occupation more necessary than this could you
name to me? What company more profitable? And what is the hindrance to
spending time here? You will certainly tell me that poverty becomes a barrier
to you of this good gathering; but the excuse is not reasonable. The week has
seven days; God divided these seven between us; and he did not give the
greater part to himself and the lesser to us; rather he did not even divide
them in half, he did not take three and give three, but he allotted six to
you, and left one for himself. And not even on this whole one do you endure to
be freed from worldly affairs, but what those who plunder sacred money do,
this you too dare against this day, snatching it, though it is sacred and
dedicated to the hearing of the spiritual oracles, and using it up on worldly
cares. And why do I speak of a whole day? What the widow did in the matter of
alms


this do you also with the time of the day; just as she cast in two obols, and
drew much favor from God, so you too lend two hours to God, and you will bring
the gain of countless days into your house. But if you do not endure it, see
lest, not wishing to withdraw yourself for a small part of a day from earthly
gains, you lose the labors of whole years. For God knows, when he is despised,
how to empty even the money that has been gathered; as indeed he said also
when threatening the Jews, since they despised the care of the temple: You
brought them into your houses, and I blew them away, says the Lord. For tell
me, what of the necessary things shall we be able to teach you, who come to us
once or twice a year, concerning soul, concerning body, concerning
immortality, concerning the kingdom of the heavens, concerning punishment,
concerning Gehenna, concerning the long-suffering of God, concerning pardon,
concerning repentance, concerning baptism, concerning forgiveness of sins,
concerning this creation above and that below, concerning the nature of men,
concerning angels, concerning the malice of demons, concerning the devices of
the devil, concerning manner of life, concerning doctrines, concerning the
right faith, concerning the corrupted heresies? For these things and far more
than these a Christian must know, and of all these give an account to those
who ask you. But you will not even be able to know the smallest part of these,
once =49.365= gathering here, and that as a side-matter, and through custom of
a feast, not through piety of soul. For it is a thing to be desired if one who
meets here at every assembly should be able to grasp all these things with
accuracy. Many of those present here have servants and sons, and when you are
about to hand them over to the masters of the crafts you would choose, you
make your house once for all inaccessible to them, and having prepared bedding
and food and all the rest of service for them, you settle them with the
teacher, forbidding them to set foot in your house, so that the continual
spending of time there may make the teaching more exact, no care interrupting
the leisure. But now, when you are about to learn not some chance craft, but
the greatest of all, how one must please God and attain the heavenly things,
you think you can accomplish this as a side-matter. And of what folly is this?
For that the matter is a learning that needs much attention, hear what he
says: Learn from me that I am meek and lowly in heart; and again the prophet:
Come, children, hear me; I will teach you the fear of the Lord; and again: Be
still and know that I am God. So that much leisure is needed for him who is
about to enjoy this philosophy.
βʹ. But that we may not spend all the time in
accusation of those who stay away, having contented ourselves with what has
been said for the correction of their sloth, come, let us philosophize
something concerning the present feast. For many keep the feasts and know
their names, but are ignorant of the subjects from which they were born. That
the present feast is called Epiphany is clear to all; but what this epiphany
is, and whether it is one or two, this they no longer know; which would be of
the utmost shame and of much ridicule, that those who keep this feast every
year should be ignorant of its subject. First then it is necessary to say to
your charity that there is not one epiphany, but two: one this present one
that has already come to pass, and a second the one that is to come and will
take place gloriously at the consummation. And concerning each of them you
heard Paul today discoursing with Titus and speaking thus: concerning the


present one: The saving grace of God has appeared to all men, training us,
that denying ungodliness and worldly desires we should live soberly and justly
and piously in the present age; and concerning the one to come: Looking for
the blessed hope and appearing of the glory of our great God and Savior Jesus
Christ. And the prophet also spoke concerning that one thus: The sun shall be
turned into darkness, and the moon into blood, before the great and notable
day of the Lord come. But why is it not the day on which he was born, but the
day on which he was baptized, that is called Epiphany? For this is the day on
which he was baptized, and he sanctified the nature of the =49.366= waters.
For this very reason also at midnight on this feast all, having drawn water,
store the streams at home, and keep them for a whole year, inasmuch as the
waters were sanctified today; and the sign becomes evident, the nature of
those waters not being corrupted by the length of time, but for a whole year
and two and often three years the water drawn today remaining unmixed and
fresh, and after so long a time rivaling the waters just now snatched from the
springs. Why then is this called Epiphany? Because it was not when he was born
that he became manifest to all, but when he was baptized; for until this day
he was unknown to the many. And that the many did not know him, and did not
know who he was, hear John the Baptist saying: There stands one among you whom
you do not know. And what wonder if others did not know, when even the Baptist
himself did not know him until that day? For I too, he says, did not know him,
but he who sent me to baptize in water, he said to me: Upon whom you shall see
the Spirit descending and remaining upon him, this is he who baptizes in the
Holy Spirit. That there are two epiphanies, then, is clear from these things;
but why Christ comes to the baptism, it is necessary to say, and to what
baptism he comes; for this too it is necessary to know, just as that. And this
first we must teach your charity; for from this we shall know that also. There
was the Jewish baptism, which freed from bodily stains, not from the sins
according to the conscience. For neither if someone committed adultery, nor if
someone dared theft, nor if someone transgressed in any other such thing, did
it free him from the charges; but if someone touched dead bones, if someone
tasted food not according to the law, if someone came from corruption, if
someone associated with lepers, he washed, and until evening he was unclean,
then he was cleansed. For he shall wash his body with pure water, it says, and
he shall be unclean until evening, and he shall be cleansed. For these things
were not truly sin nor uncleanness, but God, making those who were more
imperfect more reverent through such things, prepared them from the first
to be more exact toward the observation of the greater things.
γʹ. The Jewish purification, then, did not free from sins, but only from
bodily stains; but ours is not such, but far greater and full of much grace;
for it both frees from sins, and wipes the soul, and gives a supply of the
Spirit. But that of
John was far higher than the Jewish, and lower than ours, being as it were a
kind of bridge of both these baptisms, leading by itself from that to this;
for it did not lead them to observation of bodily purifications, but having
withdrawn them from those it exhorted and advised them to change from vice to
virtue, and to have the hopes of salvation in the achievements of works, not
in baptisms of dif =49.367= ferent kinds and purifications of waters. For he
did not


say: Wash your garments, and wash your body, and you shall be clean; but what?
Bring forth fruit worthy of repentance. And in this it was higher than the
Jewish, and lower than ours; since John's baptism neither gave the Holy
Spirit, nor provided the pardon through grace; for he commanded to repent, he
was not lord to forgive. Wherefore he also said: I baptize you in water, but
he shall baptize you in the Holy Spirit and fire. Clearly he himself did not
baptize with the Spirit. And what is, In the Holy Spirit and fire? Recall to
me that day on which there appeared to the apostles tongues distributing
themselves as of fire, and it sat upon each one of them. And that John's
baptism was incomplete, having neither a supply of the Spirit nor forgiveness
of sins, is clear from this: For Paul, finding certain disciples, says to
them, Did you receive the Holy Spirit when you believed? And they said to him:
But we have not even heard whether there is a Holy Spirit. And he said to
them: Into what then were you baptized? And they said: Into John's baptism.
And Paul said: John baptized a baptism of repentance; of repentance, not of
forgiveness. And why did he baptize? Saying to the people that they should
believe in him who was coming after him, that is, in the Lord Jesus. And when
they heard they were baptized into the name of the Lord Jesus. And when Paul
laid his hands on them, the Holy Spirit came upon them. Did you see how
incomplete John's baptism was? For if it had not been incomplete, Paul would
not have baptized them again, he would not have laid his hands on them; but
now, having done both these things, he showed the superiority of the apostolic
baptism, and that the former was far lower than this. But the difference of
the baptisms we have learned from these things; but why Christ is baptized,
and with what baptism, it is now necessary to say. Neither the Jewish one, the
former, nor ours, the one after these; for he had no need of forgiveness of
sins; for how could he who has no sin? For he did no sin, it says, nor was
guile found in his mouth; and again, Which of you convicts me of sin? Nor was
that flesh without a share of the Spirit; for how could that which was formed
from the beginning of the Holy Spirit? If then that flesh was neither without
a share of the Holy Spirit, nor liable to sins, why was he baptized? But first
it is necessary to learn with what baptism he was baptized, and then this too
will become clearer to us. With what then was he baptized? Neither the Jewish,
nor ours, but John's. Why? That even through the very nature of the baptism
you may learn that he was not baptized for sin, nor as needing a supply of the
Spirit; for that baptism was barren of both these, as we have also shown. And
that he came to the Jordan neither for forgiveness of sins nor for a supply of
the Spirit is clear from =49.368= these things; but lest anyone of those then
present should think that he comes for repentance, as the rest, hear also how
John set this right beforehand. For he who says to the others, Bring forth
fruit worthy of repentance, hear what he says to this one: I have need to be
baptized by you, and do you come to me? And he said this showing that he did
not come to him for the same need as the many; and that he is so far from
being baptized for this, that he was even far greater than the Baptist
himself, and incomparably purer. Why then was he baptized, if he did this
neither for repentance, nor for forgiveness of sins, nor for a supply of the
Spirit? For two other causes, one of which the disciple says, and another
which he himself said to John. What then does John say is the cause of this
baptism? That he might be made known to the many, as Paul also said, that John
baptized a baptism of repentance,


that they should believe in him who was coming after him; this was the
achievement of the baptism. For to go around to each man's house, and coming
to the doors to call him out and say, holding Christ, that this is the Son of
God, made the testimony suspect and was very laborious; and again, taking him
into the synagogue to show him, this likewise made the testimony suspect
again; but when all the peoples from each city had poured out to the Jordan,
and were spending time about the banks of the river, he too having come to be
baptized, to receive the commendation from above through the voice of the
Father, through the descent of the Spirit in the form of a dove, made John's
testimony concerning him above suspicion. For this reason, And I, he says, did
not know him, making his own testimony trustworthy. For since they were
kinsmen of one another according to the flesh (For behold, he says, Elizabeth
your kinswoman, she also has conceived a son, the angel says to Mary
concerning the mother of John; and if the mothers were kinswomen, it is quite
clear that the children also were); since then they were kinsmen, lest he
should seem to testify to Christ because of the kinship, the grace of the
Spirit arranged that John should spend all his first age in the wilderness,
lest the testimony should seem to come about through friendship or from some
such contrivance, but that having learned from God he should thus proclaim
him. For this reason he says: And I did not know him. From where then did you
learn? He who sent me, he says, to baptize in water, he said to me. What did
he say to you? Upon whom you shall see the Spirit descending as a dove, and
remaining upon him, he is the one who baptizes in the Holy Spirit. Do you see
that for this reason the Holy Spirit came, not as then first descending, but
that he might show the one being proclaimed, making him known to all as with a
finger by the flight? For this reason then he came to the baptism. And for
another cause also, which he himself says; what then is this? When John said,
I have need to be baptized by you, and do you come to me? he answered saying:
Suffer it now; for thus it is fitting for us to fulfill all righteous =49.369=
ness. Did you see the fair-mindedness of a servant? Did you see the humility
of the Master? What is this, To fulfill all righteousness? Righteousness is
called the fulfillment of all the commandments; as when he says: They were
both righteous, walking in the commandments of the Lord blameless. Since then
it was necessary that all men fulfill this righteousness, and no one completed
it or fulfilled it, Christ coming fulfills this righteousness.
δʹ. And what righteousness, he says, is it to be baptized? To obey the
prophet was righteousness. Just as then he was circumcised, and offered
sacrifice, and
kept sabbaths, and fulfilled Jewish feasts, so he also added this that was
left, to obey a prophet baptizing. For that it was the will of God that all
should then be baptized, hear what John says: He who sent me to baptize in
water; and again Christ, The publicans and the crowd justified God, having
been baptized with the baptism of John; but the Pharisees and the scribes
rejected the counsel of God, not having been baptized by him. If then to obey
God is righteousness, and God sent John to baptize the people, Christ
fulfilled this also along with all the other things of the law. Suppose then
the commandments of the law to be two hundred denarii; this debt our race had
to pay; we did not pay it; death held us liable to these charges. Christ
coming and finding us held, paid the debt, fulfilled the obligation, snatched
away those who had nothing


to give. For this reason he did not say, It is fitting for us to do this and
that, but, To fulfill all righteousness. It is fitting for me the Master, he
says, who have, to pay on behalf of those who have not. This is the cause of
the baptism, that he might be seen to fulfill the whole law, both this, and
the one mentioned before this. Wherefore also the Spirit descends in the form
of a dove; for where there is reconciliation of God, a dove. For also upon the
ark in the time of Noah the dove came bearing an olive branch, a symbol of the
loving-kindness of God, and of deliverance from the storm; and now in the form
of a dove, not in a body (for one must know these things securely), the Spirit
comes, announcing the mercy of God to the world, and at the same time showing
that the spiritual man must be without malice and simple and harmless; as also
Christ says: Unless you turn and become as the children, you shall not enter
into the kingdom of the heavens. That ark then, when the storm was loosed,
remained upon the earth; but this ark, when the wrath was loosed, was caught
up into heaven, and now that blameless and undefiled body is at the right hand
of the Father. But since I have mentioned the Master's body, it is necessary,
having spoken a little to you concerning this, to bring the discourse to a
close. I know that many among us, through the custom of the feast, will run to
this sacred table. It would have been necessary, as I have also often said
before, not to observe feasts as to when one ought to communicate, but to
cleanse the conscience, and then to touch the sacred sacrifice. For the
=49.370= defiled and unclean would not even at a feast be right to partake of
that holy and fearful flesh; but he who is clean and has wiped away his
offenses through exact repentance would be right both at a feast and always to
partake of the divine mysteries, and would be worthy to enjoy the gifts of
God. But since this, I do not know how, has been overlooked by some, and many,
full of countless evils, when they see the feast arriving, as if pushed by the
day itself, touch the sacred mysteries, which it is not lawful even to see for
those so disposed; those who are known to us we ourselves will altogether
exclude, and those unknown to us we will leave to God, who knows the secrets
of each man's mind; at any rate the sin that is openly committed we will
attempt to set right today for all. What then is this sin? Not approaching
with awe, but kicking, striking, full of anger, shouting, reviling, pushing
those nearby, filled with confusion. These things I have said often, and I
will not cease saying. Do you not see at the Olympic contests, when the
president of the games walks through the marketplace, having a crown on his
head, wearing a robe, holding a rod in his hand, how great the good order, the
herald crying that there be quiet with seemliness? How then is it not absurd,
where the devil parades, that there should be so great a quiet, and where
Christ calls to himself, that there should be much tumult? In the marketplace
quiet, and in the church shouting? On the sea a calm, and in the harbor waves?
Why do you make a tumult, tell me, O man? And why do you hurry? Does some
necessity of affairs call you altogether? For do you even know at all that you
have affairs at that hour? For do you even remember at all that you are on
earth? And do you think you are with men? And how is this not of a stony mind,
to think that at that time you stand on earth, and not that you dance with
angels, with whom you sent up that mystical song, with whom you offered that
victory hymn to God? For this reason Christ also called us eagles, saying,
Where the carcass is, there the eagles will be gathered, that we may walk the
heavens, that we may fly high, lightened by the wings of the spirit; but we
crawl on the ground like serpents, and eat earth. Do you want me to say from
where the tumult and the shouting come? Because we do not shut the doors to
you throughout, but we allow you before the


last thanksgiving to leap away and withdraw home; which itself would be of
much contempt. What are you doing, O man? When Christ is present, the angels
standing by, this fearful table set forth, your brothers still being
initiated, you yourself leave and leap away? And when invited to a dinner,
even if you have been filled first, you do not dare, while the others are
still reclining, to withdraw yourself before your friends; but here, when the
fearful mysteries of Christ are being accomplished, the sacred rite still
standing, you leave everything in the midst and withdraw? And how are these
things worthy of pardon? And of what defense? Do you want me to say whose work
they do who withdraw before the completion, and do not brin =49.371= g the
thanksgiving hymns to the end of the table? Perhaps what is about to be said
is burdensome, but nevertheless it must be said because of the sloth of the
many. When Judas communicated at that last supper on that last night, while
all the others were reclining, he himself leaping forth went out. Those then
imitate him also, who leap away before the last thanksgiving. For if he had
not gone out, he would not have become a traitor; if he had not left his
fellow-disciples, he would not have perished; if he had not torn himself from
the flock, the wolf would not have found him alone and eaten him; if he had
not separated himself from the shepherd, he would not have become a prey to
beasts. For this reason that one went out with Jews, but these went out after
they had hymned with the Master. Do you see that the last prayer after the
sacrifice takes place according to that type? And now, beloved, let us think
these things, let us consider these things, fearing the judgment laid down
upon this. He himself gives you a share of his flesh; =49.372= and you do not
even repay him with words, nor give thanks for what you have received, but
when you enjoy bodily food you turn to prayer after the table, but when you
partake of that which is spiritual and surpasses all creation, visible and
invisible, being a man and of lowly nature, you do not remain giving thanks
both in words and in deeds? And how are these things not worthy of the utmost
punishment? I say these things, not that you may only praise, nor that you may
make a tumult and shout, but that remembering these words at the time you may
show the fitting good order. Mysteries both are called, and are; and where
there are mysteries, much silence. With much silence then, with much good
order, with the fitting reverence, let us touch this sacred sacrifice, that we
may draw God into greater favor, and cleanse the soul, and attain the eternal
goods; which may we all attain, by the grace and loving-kindness of our Lord
Jesus Christ, with whom to the Father together with the Holy Spirit be glory,
and power, and worship, now and always, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
