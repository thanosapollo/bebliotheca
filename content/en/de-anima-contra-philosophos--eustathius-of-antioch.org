ON THE SOUL AGAINST THE PHILOSOPHERS
1 God fills all things, being circumscribed by none; for he who is not
circumscribed by place will altogether be in every place according to
the hymnody of (David); for if he were said to be outside some place, it
is necessary that he be circumscribed by that of which he is deprived;
becoming then wholly in all, he in no way changes from some places to
others, filling all things.
2 The divine is not subject to stated circumscriptions of laws and
times.
3 But since they clearly know themselves to be caught from this,
changing they fabricate the water of forgetfulness, which they say that
those who drink forget the births of their own lives. And that these
things are mythical and far from philosophy, none of those who think
well seems to be ignorant. For what do we say, O Pythagorases and
Platos: have you yourselves taken and drunk the forgetfulness-making
water? or on the contrary did you escape, having gone unnoticed? If then
you were able to flee the drink of error, you are plainly some many; but
if, having drawn the air common to all, you drank the water of
forgetfulness with open mouth, how and from where, having taken it, do
you make myths concerning souls? From where do you know at all the kind
of these streams? For if you have not experienced the use of them, you
are ignorant of the giving-forth of the waters; or if having come inside
the experience of them, you have by forgetfulness destroyed the memory
of their use and have spat out the knowledge. But having turned to these
barbarous things by superstition they went astray, using Egyptians as
guides.
4 That the souls are not unbegotten, this the age of newly-born infants
shows more clearly. For if at the same time as being born the children
had laid hold of perfect understanding, it would perhaps have been
possible for some to feign mindlessness; but if they are lacking in mind
and speech and comprehension, the souls have not then been established
unbegotten in nature. We all then know that the soul has been stretched
together with the bulks of the bodies, sufficiently not only by
greatness or smallness of members, but also by the other irrationalities
of the mind; as far as these infants happen to be newly-formed, they are
altogether without every thought and counsel; but quietly and little by
little advancing they take on mind and prudence and speech. And if these
things are proper to soul, and they receive the better additions from
abundance, is it not then that it grows together with the body, and just
as this secretly gathers up the powers, so also the soul advancing the
mind; but as many as never go toward the better, but remain foolish, it
is clear that having some member mutilated they have incomplete souls,
as those who have been ill with the disease of phrenitis. Indeed also
the body of the first-formed, having been shaped, lay dead, breathless,
unmoved, having been most excellently fashioned in members and harmony,
and having been fittingly adorned with form and beauty inasmuch as it
had been generated by the Fashioner himself, God, a prototype statue and
a most excellent impressed likeness of the most divine image, but
unfeeling and voiceless and breathless; but when God breathed into his
face in a creative manner, immediately it received movement; and from
that point it walks and breathes and speaks, rules, reasons, acts,
administers.
6 Sleep is a common affection of body and soul.
