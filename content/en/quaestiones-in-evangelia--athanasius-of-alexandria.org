* Questions on the Gospels
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

=28.700= QUESTION 1. "Now the birth of Jesus Christ was thus. For when his
mother Mary was betrothed to Joseph." Why did he not say, "When the virgin was
betrothed," but "his mother"? ANSWER. So that the account might be readily
received, and that, when the hearer was expecting to hear something of the
usual kind, he might strike him by bringing in the unexpected matter, saying:
"Before they came together, she was found to be with child from the Holy
Spirit." QUESTION 2. "An angel of the Lord appeared to him in a dream,
saying:" Why does the angel not appear openly to Joseph, as he did to the
shepherds and to Zechariah? ANSWER. Because the man was exceedingly faithful,
and had no need of such a vision. QUESTION 3. "Do not fear to take Mary your
wife." Why did he say, "Do not fear"? =28.701= ANSWER. Showing that he was
afraid of offending God, as one who had an adulteress. QUESTION 4. Why did he
say, "your wife"? ANSWER. Because she had not been defiled. And he calls her
wife from the betrothal; just as we are accustomed to call the betrothed
"bridegrooms" and "men before the wedding." QUESTION 5. "She will bear a Son."
Why did he not say, "She will bear you a son"? ANSWER. Because he was not
going to be born for him alone, but for the whole world. QUESTION 6. "In the
days of Herod the king." Why did he add the dignity, saying "the king"?
ANSWER. Because there was also another Herod, the one who killed the
Forerunner. But that one was a tetrarch, this one a king. QUESTION 7. "Rise,
take the child and his mother." Why did he not say, "your wife," but "his
mother"? ANSWER. Because, once the birth had been completed, the suspicion was
dissolved, and Joseph was given assurance. For this reason, then, the angel
now speaks with boldness, and no longer calls her his wife. QUESTION 8. "And
flee into Egypt." Why, when he heard, "Flee into Egypt," was he not troubled?
ANSWER. Because the man was faithful, and so he obeys and is persuaded.
QUESTION 9. Why is he not sent into another country, but into Egypt? ANSWER.
That the prophecy might be fulfilled which says, "Out of Egypt I called my
Son." QUESTION 10. "When Jesus heard that John had been delivered up, he
withdrew into Galilee." Why did he withdraw? ANSWER. Teaching us to flee
temptations. For it is no accusation not to throw oneself into danger, but,
having fallen into it, not to stand nobly. QUESTION 11. "Seeing the crowds,
Jesus went up on the mountain." Why did he go up on the mountain? ANSWER.
Because of his freedom from love of honor and from ostentation; for he did not
lead them around with himself. But when it was necessary to walk about, he
went around everywhere alone, visiting both cities and countryside; and when a
great crowd had gathered, he sat in one place. QUESTION 12. "Opening his
mouth, he taught them." Why did he say, "Opening his mouth"? ANSWER. That we
might learn that he also taught while silent, not only when speaking.
QUESTION 13. "Take care," he says, "not to do your almsgiving before men, to
be seen by them." Why in the case of almsgiving did he add this saying, but in
the case of prayer and fasting not at all? ANSWER. Because it is altogether
impossible to hide almsgiving entirely, but prayer and fasting can be hidden.
=28.704= QUESTION 14. "In the beginning was the Word." Why, leaving the Father
aside, does he speak about the Son? ANSWER. Because the former was clear to
all, even if not as Father, yet as God; but the Only-begotten was unknown. And
reasonably he was eager from the outset to teach the knowledge concerning him
to those who did not know him. QUESTION 15. "And the Word was with God." Why
did he not say "in God"? ANSWER. First, showing us his eternity in his own
hypostasis; then also, going on more clearly, he revealed it by adding that
this Word was also God. "He was in the world": not as contemporaneous with the
world. For this reason he added: "And the world came to be through him."
QUESTION 16. "And looking up to heaven, he blessed the loaves." Why did he
look up, and so bless? ANSWER. First, that he might show that he is from the
Father; second, teaching us not to touch the table before we have given thanks
to the one who provides us with food. QUESTION 17. Why does he perform the
miracle of the loaves in the wilderness, and feed five thousand? ANSWER. That
the miracle might be beyond suspicion in the highest degree, and that no one
might suppose a nearby village had brought something to the table.
QUESTION 18. And they took up the leftover fragments. Why did whole loaves not
remain over, but fragments? ANSWER. That he might show that these are
fragments of the five loaves, and so that those who were absent might learn
what had happened, and that no one might suppose this to be a fantasy.
QUESTION 19. "Twelve baskets." Why did they fill twelve baskets? ANSWER. That
Judas too might carry one. For he could indeed have quenched the hunger of the
crowds; but then the disciples would not have known the power. QUESTION 20.
Why does Pilate not see, but this man's wife? ANSWER. First because she was
worthy, or rather because if he himself had seen it, he would not have been
believed in the same way; or he would not even have spoken it out. For this
reason it is arranged that the woman should see, so that it might become
evident to all. QUESTION 21. "And they crucify with him two robbers, one on
the right and one on the left." Why did they crucify him in the middle of the
robbers, and why, having cried out, did he give up the spirit? ANSWER. That he
might share in their reputation, or that what was spoken might be fulfilled:
that "He was numbered with the lawless"; and that it might be shown that the
matter takes place by authority; as he also said, "I have authority to lay
down my life, and I have authority to take it again." =28.705= QUESTION 22.
"Before a rooster crows, you will deny me three times." How is it that Matthew
says Christ said to Peter, "Before a rooster crows you will deny me three
times," but Mark says that when he had denied once, then the rooster crowed
first; and when the third time, then a second time? How then will both be
found true? ANSWER. Because in each round a rooster is accustomed to crow a
third and a fourth time, Mark, making clear that not even the voice held him
back and brought him to remembrance, says this. So both are true. For before
the rooster finished the one round, he denied a third time. And he was so
terrified and so utterly dead with fear that he not only denied, but did not
even remember of himself, even after the rooster had crowed, until the
teacher's look became a voice for him. And Luke, making this clear, says that
Christ looked at him. QUESTION 23. "And going out he wept bitterly." Why did
he go outside and weep? ANSWER. Because he did not dare openly before all,
lest he be accused through the tears, and be known to be his disciple.
QUESTION 24. "And as they were eating, Christ said to them, One of you will
betray me." Why, while they were eating, does he speak to them about the
betrayal? ANSWER. That from both the time and the table he might show the
wickedness of the betrayer. For he too was present there. QUESTION 25. "One of
you will betray me." Why did he not say, "So-and-so will betray me," but "One
of you will betray me," sparing even in this? ANSWER. Sparing the betrayer
even in this, so as to give him again the chance of repentance by remaining
hidden. And he chooses to frighten all for the sake of saving this one.
QUESTION 26. "And the women also were there, who had followed from Galilee."
Why does he mention the women who had followed him? ANSWER. To show their
close attendance, and that they were present even as far as the dangers. For
when the disciples fled, these persevered. QUESTION 27. "How is it," she says,
"that you, being a Jew, ask a drink of water from me, who am a Samaritan
woman?" How, recognizing him to be a Jew, does she say to him, "How is it that
you, being a Jew"? ANSWER. From his appearance, perhaps also from his speech.
QUESTION 28. "And she brought forth her son, the firstborn." Why did he say
"firstborn"? For the All-Holy One did not have another son. ANSWER. Because
our Lord Jesus Christ, who was made flesh from her, being true God, became our
brother through the incarnation; for this reason he was =28.708= named
firstborn, as himself taking first place in all things. QUESTION 29. "And she
laid him in the manger." Why was he laid in the manger? ANSWER. That he might
free us from irrationality, once we have exchanged the beast-like life, and
grant the understanding proper to human beings. QUESTION 30. "A man was going
down from Jerusalem to Jericho, and fell among robbers." Who is the man, and
what is Jerusalem, and what is Jericho, and who are the robbers? ANSWER. The
man is Adam; Jerusalem is paradise; Jericho is this world; and the robbers are
the demons. QUESTION 31. And by chance a priest, seeing him, passed by on the
other side; and likewise a Levite. But a Samaritan, seeing him, was moved with
compassion, and bound up his wounds. What was "by chance"? and who is the
priest? and who is the Levite? and who is the Samaritan? ANSWER. "By chance"
indicates what is from divine economy; the priest is the Law; the Levite is
the teachers from the Levitical priesthood; and the Samaritan is Christ.
QUESTION 32. And why is Christ called a Samaritan? ANSWER. Because the Jews
called him so: "You are a Samaritan, and you have a demon." QUESTION 33. "And
it came to pass," he says, "that Lazarus died and was carried by the angels
into Abraham's bosom." Why does Lazarus die first, rather than the rich man?
ANSWER. That no pretext of excuse might be left to the rich man, as one who
was going to do good to him. QUESTION 34. "And lifting up his eyes, the rich
man sees Lazarus in Abraham's bosom." Why does he see Lazarus not elsewhere,
but in Abraham's bosom? ANSWER. Because, just as when God cast Adam out of
paradise he settled him opposite it, so that the continual sight, renewing the
suffering, might give him a more exact sense of the fall from the good things,
so also he settled this man over against Lazarus, that he might be more
greatly worn down. QUESTION 35. And why does the rich man see Lazarus not in
another righteous man, but in Abraham's bosom? ANSWER. Because Abraham was
hospitable. So that there might be a reproof of his hatred of strangers, for
this reason he sees him with that man. For the one even entertained
passers-by; but this one overlooked even the man lying within. QUESTION 36.
"Father Abraham, have mercy on me." Why did the rich man not address his word
to Lazarus, but to Abraham? ANSWER. Because he was ashamed and blushed; for he
thought that Lazarus was holding a grudge for what he had done to him.
