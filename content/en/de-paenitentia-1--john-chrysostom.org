On repentance.
=60.765=
Wanting to glorify the heads of those who love to hear with a crown woven of
golden-tressed flowers, having plucked with our fingers most beautiful flowers
from the undefiled meadows of Christ, we ask the angels who have been
commanded to work with us by the divine Spirit. For you, the people of God, or
rather his temple, are worthy to be glorified with such a crown, as many of
you as have especially kept the temple of God spotless and undefiled, having
shone round the souls in darkness with purple mouths and the gleaming pearl:
flash upon these the light of piety. Let down to them hands of love, and as
from the depth of ignorance lead them up to the marvelous light of hope.
But as many of you as through carelessness have corrupted the temple of God
with pleasures, do not despair of yourselves to the end, but through
repentance renew it again. For just as a prudent man, having diligently set
right the house that through his own neglect had grown rotten and been ruined,
rebuilds it, so that it falls short in nothing of the house originally built;
so also the mind that dwells in our soul renews again by repentance the soul
that has slipped and fallen through sins. For repentance raises the fallen
soul, and makes the soul alienated from God a friend again; repentance drives
off the moonless night that has come upon the soul, and banishes the death;
repentance raises the accursed soul, sets the lame one upright, heals the
crushed, and makes the wounded well. Let us run toward repentance, the sponsor
of the kingdom, the wiping away of sins, the mother of salvation. Repentance:
a driving out of the devil, a throwing down of demons. O repentance, because
of which Jonah the prophet fled from the face of God, having overturned a
fixed death-sentence of God, and shown the herald of death a liar; for you,
meeting the decree of God's counsel against the Ninevites, blocked it, and
rained down springs of life without stint upon the Ninevites.
Repentance, falling before God with tears, granted Hezekiah the king life
instead of death. O =60.766= repentance, running up from earth to the heavens,
surpassing angelic powers, drawing near by the divine Spirit to the Lord's
throne, becoming a fellow-speaker with God, and taking life as from her own
treasuries, the treasuries of God, and granting it with boldness to those who
have you. O repentance, physician of the passions, you who soothe threats,
quench the fire of wrath, put angers to sleep, and furnish unquenchable
torches of friendship. O repentance, born in a human heart, and at once
lodging in the heavens; the blessed David, having found you, escaped the
sentence of eternal punishment for the two sins, I mean murder and adultery. O
repentance, loosing the bonds of sins, and saving souls that have been bound
fast. Repentance, sending up a groan from the midst of the heart, prevails
upon God; repentance, letting a hot tear fall like dew from the eyes, makes
the stained souls bright with baptism. O repentance, a medicine come from a
mortal man that grants immortality; a soul that has you says: I am black, yet
beautiful; black because of the gloomy mourning of sin, beautiful because of
the good light from repentance. O repentance, advocate of Peter, and champion
and fellow-vindicator of Paul; if the Christ-killing Pharisee finds you, he
becomes a Christ-bearing Galilean. Repentance, mining the shattered soul, at
once transforms the thorn-bearing heart. Some say that the eagle, when it has
grown old, having lifted itself to a height in the air, and having let itself
down into a most clear spring, is renewed. Such is repentance; for having
lifted the heart that has it to a height in the air, with the spring of tears
it washes away the oldness of sins, and puts on the newness of the Spirit.
Therefore the Spirit also says to the soul that is in repentance: Your youth
shall be renewed like the eagle's.
The great physician of our souls, knowing this to be more effective than all
the virtues, counsels saying: Repent, for the kingdom of heaven has drawn
near. Come =60.767= to repentance boldly. Look upon the ancient generations,
and see: who trusted in the Lord, and was put to shame? or who remained in his
fear, and was abandoned? or who called upon him, and he overlooked him? For
the Lord is compassionate and merciful, and he forgives sins, and saves in
time of affliction. And do not be ashamed, child, to confess your sins; for
there is a shame that brings on sin, and there is a shame that is glory and
grace. The soul that repents here receives the forgiveness of sins; for in
Hades what will one confess to the Lord? Groan over your sins, that you may be
saved; for when, it says, you have turned and groaned, then you shall be
saved. Speak you, it says, your sins, that you may be justified in your words,
and prevail when you are judged. Speak your sins. Remember not the sins of my
youth, and my ignorances. According to your mercy remember me, for the sake of
your goodness, O Lord. Take heed that this life is as a shadow running past.
Where are the enjoyments of the times gone by? where the empty care? where the
afflictions? where the respites? are they not driven like mist by a storm? All
things have run past. Let us look not only to the present life, but also to
the one to come. Remember, it says, your last things, and you will not sin
forever. Look upon the day and the hour of your departure, what sort of man
you wish to be when you are laid on a bed and powerless, having no one to help
yourself; see those standing round about you, friends, neighbors, kinsmen,
brothers, household servants; all together wailing, weeping, inconsolable,
lamenting because of you; and likewise the children standing on either side,
seeking as they were used the father's voice, whimpering and not hearing you;
the wife again in anguish, longing for death before you, for your sake;
fearful angels demanding the loan of your soul. If the soul has done things
worthy of honor, it will be escorted by the angels; but if it has been reared
without mercy =60.768= toward the poor of its own people, it will be condemned
without mercy; for the judgment, it says, is without mercy to the one who has
not done mercy. Enter into the storeroom of your soul; become a spectator of
the things you have done; set your good deeds against the bad, and if you see
that the bad exceed, bury them with good works, as one about to look your sins
in the face. Look, I pray you, at the sleepless worm, and the unlighted chaos,
the unquenchable fire, which is not quenched by water, but is overcome by
repentance. O repentance, quenching the unquenchable and eternal fire, dashing
the sleepless worm to the ground, and leveling and lighting the unlighted
chaos; O repentance, unbreakable wall of the soul, a city towered about, in
which Father and Son and Holy Spirit reign; O repentance, who have loosed the
sting of death, and set the devil underfoot, and slay the dragon, and hymn
Christ, and blossom forth life; O repentance, who in mourning make joy bloom
again, and exult in tears, and through tears take away the eternal tears; O
repentance, who sow in tears, and reap in gladness; O repentance, who in
sackcloth betroth the flashing robe of Christ; O repentance, who drive the
manner of Cain from the heart, and put in the longing and joy of chastity; O
repentance, who wash away the stench of sins, and array with the fragrance of
good deeds; O repentance, housemate of peace, tent-fellow of friendship,
yokefellow of love, hope-breathing flower; the silver-skinned lambs that feed
on you utter the purple scepters of the Trinity. But lest, having lengthened
the discourse, we should seem to talk idly, having sealed here the purse of
our words, let us send you on to the God-created meadow, in which are the
sweet-breathing trees, the trunks of the doctrines, which God through the
fathers planted for you as a roof of the Church through Jesus Christ our Lord,
to whom be the glory and the power with the Holy Spirit unto the ages of ages.
Amen.
