OF JOHN THE PRESBYTER OF DAMASCUS CONCERNING THE HOLY FASTS.
To the blessed and genuine servant of Christ, lord Cometas, spiritual brother,
John the least.
Of the many and boundless gifts granted by God to =95.65= men, the gift of
prudence stands as exceptional and first. For it is discernment of good and of
evil; and he who has divided them best has also chosen what is expedient best,
having reason as charioteer of those things obedient to him. Of this prudence
a ripe offspring is that saying of Solomon, "There is a time for every
matter;" the word making this clear, that the whole is the good; for evil is
not among the things that are, but rather a flight from the good and a taking
away of it. When therefore each thing has been judged good, I mean by right
reason (for what has been judged badly is rather indiscrimination than
judgment), there is a time to be silent, when this is good; and a time to
speak, whenever one is asked. For to speak before being asked, and to go
before being sent, is folly for the one speaking and walking. But the
circumstance of affairs, when there is necessity to speak, even not speaking
is not without danger. And there are times when even what is fitting must be
preferred to what seems good. For me, then, every time is a time to be silent;
for I have been deprived of speaking on account of my own inattention, and
besides, before the face of those wise and lofty in understanding, to whom
virtue has justly procured the rule, how should he who is poor in virtue
speak? Having these things in mind, I would have been silent even now, had not
the reverence of your God-guarded glory, O most beloved, compelled me toward
writing. For seeing the great urgency of our honorable letters, I judged that
you would not otherwise and in vain urge us toward speaking, unless some
necessity pressed toward this. I write therefore, compelled by your virtue and
friendship, and let everyone who knows the strength of those who compel
forgive me; and I write setting nothing, as far as possible, before the truth,
which is to be preferred to all things, even to living, with which living is
choiceworthy, and for which death is more choiceworthy than living.
βʹ. Your all-honorable virtue has written that some have rumored us to have
said that the weeks of the fasts are eight; and it urged us to write whether
the facts stand thus. To this we say that nothing is higher than the peace of
the Church, for which law and prophets, for which God became man: this, then,
the great and truly unsearchable mystery; which Christ came to proclaim as
good news; which Christ himself granted to his own disciples before the
Passion, and after the resurrection from the Passion; which also, going up
into the heavens with his flesh, from where he had come down without flesh, he
left as an inheritance to the apostles, and through them to the Church. And
peace is concord in the good. For that which agrees badly =95.68= will be said
rather to be in faction than to be at peace; and for me too, the least, the
aim was first of all to award the things of peace, and whatever tends toward
this, to say to those who ask as much as I am able.
γʹ. Having seen, therefore, the strife concerning the holy fasts piled up even
to the air, I was terribly distressed and grieved, that even through the good
sin works death for me. For what is the profit of the fast, for those who fast
unto strifes and fights? Wherefore to those who say that the weeks of the holy
fasts are seven I used to counsel not to contend, nor to throw into faction
the fair body of Christ, that is, the Church; but rather to obey those
entrusted with the presidency and the stewardship of the word. For to abound
in the good, and to advance, and to go up to the greater and higher things,
and to add upon every praise of the Lord, belongs to those who are well. And
to those who legislate the eight weeks I used to suggest that not even the
good is good, unless it come to be well. For virginity is good, but if you
marry, you have not sinned. It is good to fast every day; but let him who does
not eat not judge him who eats. In such things it is not fitting to legislate,
nor to force, nor to lead the entrusted flock by compulsion, but rather by
persuasion, and gentleness, and a word seasoned with salt; these things and
such we suggest. But if it is necessary to make public what seems good to us,
this we say: that since our holy Father Basil, the initiate of the truth, said
that the weeks of the holy fasts are seven in all, in the discourse Against
the Drunkards, and Gregory the Theologian granted these fasts as type
and imitation of the forty-day fast of the Lord in the discourse On Baptism,
it is necessary to reconcile both with one another, lest the doctrines of the
divine men be found discordant. For this is what stirs the strife, not to
discern the force of the things said; for we have not been legislated, as some
supposed, to fast tenths of the year; for there is a canon clearly laid down
legislating to fast the Forty; and the tenths of the year are not forty. How
then, fasting the five days of the week, shall we complete the number of the
=95.69= forty, unless we fast the pentads of the eight weeks? To these things,
then, we say that, having read the constitutions of the holy apostles, we
found it enjoined: It is necessary to fast the holy Forty, beginning from
Monday, and ending on Friday, after which we begin the week of the Passion. If
then the Forty is completed through eight weeks, and after these we begin that
of the Passion, it is necessary to fast nine weeks in all. Athanasius,
however, great in divine knowledge and virtue, in his festal discourses
declared more clearly that the weeks of the Forty are six; after which he
commands to begin the week of the Passion. From all of which we have arrived
at such a thought, that fasting is not only the complete abstinence from food
from dawn until evening, but also the abstention from certain foods; for we
are said to fast from wine and meats. Since therefore a canon of the holy
apostles has been laid down, not to fast on Saturday and Sunday, and to fast
the holy Forty, perhaps it commanded to fast the five days of the week with
complete abstinence from food, and the two days of Saturday and Sunday with
abstention from certain foods. For it must be known that when two labors are
set forth, the stronger prevails and is preferred; for example, circumcision
on the eighth day has been legislated, and the keeping of the Sabbath.
Therefore what has been born on the Sabbath will be circumcised on the
Sabbath, and the legislation of circumcision dissolves the law of the Sabbath;
so also here the Holy Spirit, having legislated through the servants of the
word the fast until evening for the five days of the week, appointed for
Saturday and Sunday the abstention from certain foods, because of the
prevailing of the resurrection, which we know as the Lord's day, and the
offering on behalf of all the saints who have fallen asleep before, which we
have been commanded to celebrate every Saturday, because of the Saturday
before Pascha, on which Christ bound the strong one and plundered his vessels,
which Saturday alone those awaiting the joy of the resurrection must fast.
εʹ. Thus then we also know the custom of the Church from unwritten tradition:
one pre-fast week, in which abstention from meats only and the fast until
evening was performed, the third, or sixth, or ninth hour, or the celebration
of the holy Presanctified, by no means taking place. And six, in which the
third-sixth-ninth hours and the Presanctified are celebrated, abstentions from
eggs, and cheese, and such things together with meats. And in the week of the
Passion dry-eating had been legislated, yet not the celebration of the
Presanctified; then on holy Thursday the celebration of the mysteries, in
which it also began. Then on the holy and fearful Friday complete abstinence
from food until the evening of Saturday; =95.72= and on the evening of
Saturday, the rest being eaten, they do not partake of meats; but on the holy
Sunday of the resurrection, the first day of the week, those who wish partake
also of meats.
ϛʹ. This order we have come upon; this is the common definition and law of the
Church, which we know to be performed also in the holy Resurrection of Christ
our God; but to abound in the evil, and to lavish virtue, is not unapproved,
nor to be rejected, but well-pleasing to God and to men, at least to those who
have understanding; yet not from force, or from necessity, but rather by
persuasion, and especially because of the difficulty of the time, and the
unevenness of affairs. For both are now rather useful and necessary, both to
add to the good, and to abstain from force. Who would grant that the whole
time of our life be performed as one fast? But the manner of exhortation is
one thing, and that of legislation another. Let the things legislated by the
Spirit suffice. Let the addition of the good be admonished.
ζʹ. These things seeming good to us, we also discuss thus with all concerning
them; but the citations concerning these things for the present, because of
the haste of the letter-bearer, though they are easily taken among us, we have
omitted.
And the God of peace, our peace, who made both one, and broke down the middle
wall of the partition, the enmity, in his cross; who reconciled us to his own
Father; will award his peace that surpasses all understanding to his holy
Church, and will take away the scandals, and will grant to fast an acceptable
fast, which he himself chose, to undo the knots of violent contracts, and to
break bread for the hungry, both that of the body, and no less that of the
soul, which I call the saving word, and will make us to fast spiritually, and
to eat, and to feast, doing and thinking and saying all things to his glory;
that his name may not be blasphemed among the nations because of us, but
rather glorified; because to him is the glory unto the ages. Amen.
Of the holy hieromartyr Ignatius, from the Epistle to the Philippians. If
anyone is chaste or practices continence, let him not be lifted up, lest he
lose the reward; do not dishonor the feasts. Do not despise the Forty; for it
contains an imitation of the Lord's way of life. Do not overlook the week of
the Passion; fasting Wednesday and Friday, supply the surplus to the poor. If
anyone fasts on Sunday or Saturday, except one Saturday, this one is a
murderer of Christ. =95.73= Constitution of the holy Apostles. With which the
fast of the holy Forty is to be kept by us, containing a memorial of the way
of life and legislation of Christ. And let this fast be performed before the
fast of Pascha, beginning from Monday, and being fulfilled on Friday. After
which, having finished the fast, begin the holy week of Pascha, all fasting it
with fear and trembling. Praying in it concerning those who are perishing, in
which he began to make the counsel against the Lord. For he himself commanded
us to fast these six days because of the impiety of the Jews. Of Saint Basil,
from the discourse against the drunkards.
For if upon so many exhortations, which both in the time before we did not
cease entreating you, and throughout these seven weeks of the fast, night and
day, we did not stop testifying to you the Gospel of the grace of God, no
profit has come about. Upon what hopes shall we discourse, unchaste women,
having forgotten the fear of God, having despised the eternal fire, on such a
day, when they ought because of the remembrance of the resurrection to sit in
their houses. Of Chrysostom, from the discourse Toward the end of the fast.
Forty days then have passed; if therefore the holy Pascha should pass, I will
no longer forgive anyone, nor will I bring exhortation. Of Saint Anastasius
patriarch of Constantinople, from the Encyclical. The holy Pascha of the
eleventh Indiction we celebrate with God; according to the Egyptians, on the
twenty-fifth of the month Pharmouthi; and according to the Romans, on the
twentieth of the month of April, twelve days before the Kalends of May;
beginning the fast of the seven weeks from this same Monday, according to the
Egyptians, on the eighth of the month Phanemoth; and according to the Romans
on the third of March. Of Saint Epiphanius of Cyprus, from that To the
Monks. Keep observantly the Forty of the holy Church, and the week of the
holy Pascha.
=95.76= Of John bishop of Athens. That through the august seven of the fasts
we may look to a safe and unfallen passage. For the week, always turning and
inclining together, and as it were from itself being wound about the same
point and circling, concludes for us the hebdomadal age. Of Saint Anastasius
patriarch of Theopolis, that is of Antioch of Syria. Come then let us consider
for what reason the forty-day fast is completed in seven weeks. By no means
departing from being a Forty, but taking on also another mystical elegance;
for the number seven has appeared complete and all-holy, and has rather graced
the days of our sacred contests, being completed seven times through the seven
weeks. Of Saint Peter patriarch of Jerusalem. And having mentioned fasting
most opportunely, we would say those things also to you, that by the grace of
God we begin the lawful fast of the seven weeks from the seventh of the month
of February, celebrating the festival of the saving Passion and of the
Resurrection of Christ on the twenty-seventh of the so-called month of March.
Proof that all the heretics say eight weeks of the holy fasts, talking
nonsense thus. Citations of Severus the Acephalus and heresiarch, and Hellene,
who became archbishop of Antioch of Syria, from his enthronement discourses,
on the Forty of the fasts; and it was spoken on the Friday before it, in the
Church of Cassian; of which the beginning: There is a law also in the bodily
wrestlings; and in the middle of the discourse: "For those who, cleansing
eight times the five senses, through which sin takes entrance (I mean hearing,
and sight, and touch, and taste and smell), fast forty days, that they may
attain that blessed day, the eighth and first. For the number five, being
wound eight times, completes the number of the forty days." Of the same, from
the book of various chapters noted with his own hand; this is the second note.
That it is necessary to fast the forty days without omission; which are
gathered from the eight weeks; the two days of each week, I mean Saturday and
Sunday, having been allotted to rest; so that from this it is clear that one
must not fast on Saturday and Sunday, not even in the holy Forty of the fasts.
=95.77= Of the accursed and God-hated Benjamin, who became false bishop of
Alexandria of Egypt, being of the worst pair of Dioscorus and Severus, from
the fourth book of his festal letters, in the thirtieth false-festal, of which
the beginning: "Bright and worthy of wonder."
And toward the ends. Beginning the forty days of the fast, that is the eight
weeks, and fasting soberly for the overthrow of the eight thoughts that rise
up against the soul, from the twenty-seventh of the month Mechir according to
the Egyptians, until the twentieth of the month Pharmouthi. Of the same, in
the thirty-first false-festal, of which the beginning is thus: "And now I seem
to hear." And toward the end. Beginning the eight weeks of the fast, that is
of the holy Forty, because Saturday and Sunday are not numbered together with
the holy fasts, from the nineteenth of the month Mechir according to the
Egyptians, and according to the Romans on the Ides of February, which is the
thirteenth of February; and of the holy week of the saving Pascha, from the
eighth of the month Pharmouthi according to the Egyptians; and according to
the Romans three days before the Nones of April, which is the third of April;
and next, having completed the holy days of Pascha, in the deep evening of
holy Saturday we shall cease the fast.
