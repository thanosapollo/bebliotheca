On the saying, "My grace is sufficient for you; for my power is made perfect
in weakness."
=59.507=
1. When God from the beginning fashioned this wholly harmonious cosmos, he
worked wonders upon wonders, not serving a law of nature, but crafting all
things by authority of power. What I mean: In the beginning God made the
heaven and the earth. Did you see order and sequence? First the roof, and then
the floor. For as I said, he was not serving a law of nature, nor an order of
art, but authority of power. The roof stood, and the floor did not appear; for
the power of the one working held all things together. Ask the heretic: Tell
me, how did the roof come to be, and the floor was not laid underneath? Tell
me the reasoning, tell me the manner. But you are silent, and you yield
yourself to faith. Of what pardon are you worthy, yielding to yourself by
faith the worldly and earthly things, yet being curious and meddlesome about
the divine and heavenly? He made the heaven and the earth; he made the earth a
floor, and he set waters under it. Did you see the foundation of the building?
Earth above, and waters below. And yet builders work otherwise: they drain out
the waters, and then they lay the foundation. But God set the foundation upon
the waters, so that you might marvel not at the power of the foundation, but
at the au =59.508= thority of the one working. But as I was saying, attend
with accuracy; for I am taking hold of the words of philosophers. And when he
was working this wholly harmonious cosmos, all the other things he made from a
monadic substance. And if the word is unclear, I will make it clearer, if you
attend: All the other things he made from a monadic substance, the things
above and the things below (for that too is a cosmos, and this is a cosmos;
these two cosmoi); but each of the things that came to be, from a monadic
substance then. What I mean: The things above are all bodiless, the things
below all bodies; the things above intellectual, the things below perceptible;
the things above invisible, the things below visible. All the things below are
bodies, even heaven, and sun, and moon, and earth, and sea, and trees, and
plants, and oxen, and sheep, and horses; all seen, all perceptible, all
bodies, all subject to touch, and seen by sight. The things above are all
invisible, intellectual, angels, archangels, thrones, dominions,
principalities, authorities, powers, the Cherubim, the Seraphim. Neither are
those visible, nor these invisible; neither are those bodies, nor these
bodiless, but both these are bodies, and those bodiless; all came to be from a
monadic substance, both the things below and the things above; and some from
bodies, others =59.509= from bodiless things, some bodies, others bodiless.
Man alone came to be from a double substance, the better and the worse, of
soul and of body; for the one is invisible and intellectual, the soul; but the
body is perceptible and visible. For what reason then did these two cosmoi
come to be from a monadic substance, and neither bodies above, nor bodiless
things below; but man alone has been composed from the bodiless and a body?
Hear the cause, and learn the wisdom of the one working. For since these two
cosmoi were distinct in substance, the one bodiless, the other embodied; the
one invisible, the other visible; the one intellectual, the one above, and the
other perceptible, the one below; lest the difference of the works introduce
different creators, and it be said that another made the things above, and
another the things below, he set in the cosmos a double living being to
proclaim the creation both of the things above and of the things below.


Man is a double cosmos, having as kindred of the things above the soul, and of
the things below the body, a kind of bond and bridge, having one creator, the
one who worked the things above and the things below; and like a virgin, the
soul has been woven in the midst of the body. Since then the substance has
become double, its need also is double. Attend with accuracy to the word; its
need also is double. There is a body, and there is a soul. He appointed food
for the body, and word for the soul; the body is fed with bread, and the soul
too is fed with the word; the body has clothing, garments; the soul too has
clothing, virtue. The queen stood at your right hand in gold-woven vesture,
clothed, embroidered; the Scripture is not speaking about perceptible
garments, but about those of virtue. For all the glory of the king's daughter
is within, in golden fringes. What then? Man is double, and the substance is
double; and the soul needs a word that teaches, that understands, that drives
away the passions, that corrects the wounds. Let us not then attend only to
the perceptible table, but let us also set before you the intellectual feast,
working for you the banquet from the words of Paul, and bringing into the
midst the tentmaker, the unlearned, the uneducated, the teacher of the world,
the one fearful to the demons, the one who drives out diseases, the one who
runs about above and below, the one who came into paradise, the one who went
up into the third heaven, the one who had such grace in his craft, the one not
hindered by the craft, but practicing a trade, and shining by his
achievements. Let us bring him into the midst; for I do not take my fill of
his praises. I am a lover of his beauty; for it is not bodily, that it should
rot, but spiritual, never withering. The body was dissolved, and the form
shines above the sun. The sun runs above, Paul runs opposite below; the one
showing perceptible light, the other introducing intellectual light into
souls; the one yielding in the night, the other shining even in the night. He
took kings, and filled them with piety; he took women, and made them more
manly than men; he took the young, and made them more prudent than the aged;
he set right either nature, every age, he who had a knife, who stitched hides;
for I will not cease continually speaking of his craft. A bride prepared, more
august than kings, wealthier than the rich, more powerful than soldiers, more
secure than walls; the tongue a lyre, sweeter than honey, deeper than the sea,
higher than heaven, hotter than fire, sharper than iron; having the grace of
God, a temple of God, a mouth of Christ, a lyre of the Spirit, a herald of the
catechumens, a trainer of the faithful, going about everywhere, a wall that
never falls, letters conquering the world and the ark. 2. For just as Noah,
having stitched planks, made an ark, so also Paul, having written letters,
constructed an intellectual ark; this one is better than that. For that one
received the irrational, and kept the irrational; but this one receives the
irrational, and changes them. What I mean: A wolf entered the ark, and a wolf
came out; a wolf entered the letters of Paul, and a sheep came out; a magician
entered, and was set right; a consul entered, and became better. And that ark
saved Noah alone with two or three names; but this ark netted the world. It
sails everywhere every day, and the planks are not loosed; for they have been
anointed not with pit =59.510= ch, but with the Spirit. Noah went out from it,
and it itself sails; Paul died, and the ark lives; every day it nets, and it
is not sunk; for it has the grace of the helmsman of the world holding it.
Since then such are the letters, such the planks, such the salvation, come
then, let us let the tongue go here, interpreting one saying for you, and
spending the whole homily upon this.


For such is the abundance of the Scriptures. For as I sat and heard him saying
that he was caught up into the third heaven, and heard unspeakable words,
which it is not lawful for a man to speak; then as he went on and said: In the
excess of the revelations, that I might not be exalted, there was given me a
thorn in the flesh, an angel of Satan, that he might buffet me; I was afraid
lest someone of those hearing, not knowing the meaning, be scandalized more
simply, and I made it a necessity to bring this saying into the midst, and to
feel it out thus. For if someone was ignorant, it will be clear; if not, it
will be more known and easy to grasp; for the one who knows will be reminded;
and the one who does not know will learn. For I have heard many not knowing
the meaning and saying that Paul was delivered to Satan. What are you saying,
tell me? He was delivered, he says, to Satan, that he might strike his head;
and the devil threw a headache upon him. Is this the whole? The teacher of the
world, the citizen of paradise, the temple of Christ, as I said, the lyre of
the Spirit, the vessel of election, the one who ran through the world, the one
who pulled up the thorns of sins, the one who cast in the seeds of piety, the
one running on land, the one contending on the sea, the one who spent a night
and a day in the deep, the one crowned in prisons, the one who set up a trophy
in the courtroom, the one who ran over the whole world, that sleepless one,
whose garments drove out diseases, whose life made death take flight: this one
the devil had under his hand? And yet I hear him doing the contrary. Someone
fornicated among the Corinthians; Paul heard, and what does he say? And you
are puffed up, and did not rather mourn, that he who has done this deed might
be taken away from among you. For I, being absent in body but present in
spirit, have already judged as present him who has so done this, in the name
of the Lord, you being gathered together, and my spirit, with the power of the
Lord Jesus Christ, to deliver such a one to Satan for the destruction of the
flesh, that the spirit may be saved in the day of the Lord Jesus. Consider the
authority of Paul; he delivered the fornicator to Satan, that Satan might
strike him, that this punishment might become a cleansing of the sin for him.
For that this happened, he says in the second letter: Confirm love toward him,
that we may not be overreached by Satan. You delivered him to Satan, and you
say, That we may not be overreached by Satan? For Satan, he says, does not
know measure. I delivered when it was needed, I take him back when it is
needed; I delivered him to Satan as to an executioner, that our man might only
be wiped clean; let us take him. For if we leave him longer, we are
overreached by Satan; for Satan does not know measure. I delivered him, that
he might set himself right; but if we leave him, he will kill him as he did
Judas. Therefore he says: Confirm love toward him, that we may not be
overreached by Satan. How well did he speak? He is no longer Satan's; for he
is not in fornication, but in repentance; so let not that one take what is
mine. When he was that one's, I gave him to him, that what was that one's
might become mine. O wisdom of Paul! Through the wolf he saved the sheep;
through the pirate he drew up the shipwrecked sailor; through the enemy he
brought the soldier back into rank. I have used the devil's blow as a bitter
drug. If only he will discipline, I withdraw from him. Let that one punish,
that the fornicator may perceive what he has done. When he has wiped off the
sin, when he has wiped off the rot, and has cleaned out the wound, then the
soul is shown strong. I do not leave the gold in the fire; lest it be roasted
away, I draw it up. For this belongs to the wise man, to know the measure, how
much to give to the fire, and how much to take. That we may not be overreached
by Satan. For we are not ignorant of his thoughts =59.511=. He then who knows
his thoughts, who has such authority as even to set bounds for him both of
time and of measure; of time,


snatching him from him; and of measure, delivering such a one to Satan for the
destruction of the flesh. For since the beast is insatiable, and never takes
fullness from our flesh, lest he kill him outright, therefore he says, For the
destruction of the flesh, that he may not touch his soul; which is what God
says to Job, Go, strike his flesh, but do not touch his soul, and God sets
bounds for the devil. So also the servant of God, imitating the Master by the
power of God, gives him a sentence which the devil did not dare to overstep.
For if he had overstepped, he would have prevailed against Paul; since even
then, when he was in the prison, the devil wished to work some such thing. For
when the prison was shaken, when the bonds were loosed, when the jailer became
awake, he took a sword, that he might slay himself, the devil pressing him to
be killed. For since the shepherd was within, lest the sheep be saved, the
wolf came to make the sheep a beast's prey. But the shepherd, though bound,
held him, saying: Do yourself no harm. O philanthropy of a shepherd! He is
bound, and he pities the one who bound him; he has let go his own affairs, and
he cares for that one's. Do yourself no harm. You see the doors opened, but I
am a well-disposed servant; the doors have been opened, but I remain within;
until I save you, I do not go out. For this I entered, that having loosed you
from the chains, I may free you from sins. You bound, I loose. Do yourself no
harm; for we are all here. What then? The wolf stood wishing to kill,
thrusting the sword against the jailer; the shepherd cried out; the wolf
heard, he let go the sheep. The shepherd took the one that was about to become
a beast's prey. And what shall I do, he says, that I may be saved? Be baptized
in the name of Christ. Wonderful, how easy is salvation, unspeakable the
philanthropy! The prisoner saved the jailer, he made the prison a church.
When, he says, shall I be baptized that I may be saved? The jail does not
hinder, the place does not impede; it is grace that needs no place, that
requires no season; let even the prison become a church. 3. O how not even
this man anywhere forgot his own craft! but everywhere he was fishing, both in
the courtroom, and on the sea, and on an island, and on land, and in the
desert, and in the inhabited world. For the fisherman, if he does not see the
sea, does not spread the nets. But since this man was not perceptible but
intellectual, he fished both in a boat, and on a mountain, and at springs, and
on roads, and in meadows, and in rivers, and no place became a hindrance to
the craft. But as I was saying; for even if I make many digressions, I will
not stand off from the question. What then is it? He then who has such
authority as to command the devil, and to set bounds for him, and a measure of
the pit; is this one delivered to the devil? Did you not hear Christ saying,
that Tread upon serpents and scorpions, and upon all the power of the enemy?
What then is the saying, There was given a thorn in my flesh, an angel of
Satan, that he might buffet me? The whole ignorance for the most part comes to
the many from this, from not knowing the properties of the words, nor their
interpretations. The word "to buffet," some took only of the head; but
buffeting is called ill-treatment, humiliation. His enemy buffeted him, He
humbled; This thing buffeted, instead of, He humbled; for it is not said only
of the head. And he himself uses the word again. For since he was unlearned,
he uses the customary word for the presentation of the thoughts. Unto this
present hour we both hunger, and thirst, and are naked, and are buffeted, and
are despitefully used by enemies, are plotted against, are hindered, are
impeded, are wronged, that is, We are buffeted, we are ill-treated, we are
scourged, we suffer terrible


things, we are thrown into prison, we are led away, we are banished, we are
delivered to famine. All this ill-treatment then he called buffeting and
humiliation. Attend, and do not run past it; I have need of the interpretation
in due time. What then? Let us furnish it clearly. Hold the interpretation
which I told you, attend to what is said. Buffeting then, that is, the
humiliation that comes from enemies, whether through prison, or through being
led away, or through a courtroom, or through famine, or through whatever
=59.512= thing. What then is it, In the excess of the revelations, that I
might not be exalted, there was given a thorn in my flesh, an angel of Satan,
that he might buffet me; for this I besought the Lord thrice, and he said to
me: My grace is sufficient for you; for my power is made perfect in weakness?
Attend who this angel of Satan is. For he did not say, There was given me
Satan an angel, but, An angel of Satan. It is a custom of Scripture, which has
often been said, and will be said now too; For to say the same things is not
grievous to me, but for you it is safe; for I teach, I do not furnish a
display of words; I plant, I do not simply pass by. I am not a traveler, but I
am casting seed. For the traveler runs, that he may finish the road; but the
one casting seed digs, then casts the grain, then brings on the earth, and
places it in the depth, lest the seed lying bare become a prey for the birds
above. What then the farmer does with seeds, this I do with thoughts. For as
upon seeds there are ravens, so upon thoughts there are demons and devils, and
cares of this life. Therefore I wrap it round, lest going out with the care of
this life it be seized by the devil. For if I spread it out, it is snatched;
but if I place it in the depth, it is kept. For there is no need of season,
but of a mind; no need of rain, but of eagerness. Hear with eagerness, wrap it
round with zeal; and no one can snatch it. What then was I saying is a custom
of Scripture? To set names in many places from the manners, and not to call
the man from that appellation which the parents set from the beginning; but
from that appellation which vice or virtue gives. What I mean: If it sees
someone shameless, it calls him a dog, not by his own name, but by the name of
the vice; if it sees someone rapacious, it calls him a wolf, not by the name
of the parents, but by the wickedness of the appellation; if it sees another
doing the works of a wicked man, it calls him from that name. But that I may
not simply assert this, and that I may also furnish proof of what is said, I
wish to bring witnesses in to you. The vision which Isaiah the son of Amoz
saw, against Judaea, and against Jerusalem. Here attend, Against Judaea, and
against Jerusalem. Hear, O heaven, and give ear, O earth, for the Lord has
spoken: I have begotten sons, and raised them up, but they have set me at
nought. The ox knows his owner, and the ass the crib of his master; but Israel
has not known me, and the people has not understood me. Woe, sinful nation! To
whom are these things? To the Jews. A people full of sins. He speaks of their
lawlessnesses, their sins, the uncorrectable, the tyranny, the punishment.
Beginning he said, Against Judaea, and against Jerusalem; and again he says,
Hear the word of the Lord, you rulers of Sodom; give heed to the law of God,
you people of Gomorrah. What to me is the multitude of your sacrifices? says
the Lord. I am full of burnt-offerings of rams, and the fat of lambs and the
blood of bulls and goats I do not wish. If you bring me fine flour, it is vain
incense, it is an abomination to me. Your new moons and your sabbaths my soul
hates. What sabbaths has he hated? what new moons? what incenses? what bulls?
what sheep? The men of Sodom knew none of these; those in Sodom came to be
before Moses. The men of Sodom were before Abraham, the men of Sodom were from
Lot, the men of Sodom perished. Does he converse with the men of Sodom who
were burned, who


perished? Here he said, Against Judaea, and against Jerusalem; he came upon
Sodom; it paid the penalty. With whom does he converse? The things said are
inconsistent, unless you see the interpretation of the words; but if you learn
it, you find the sequence. For he is not conversing with the men of Sodom, but
with Jews, calling them men of Sodom. Since they inherited their manner, he
also lays the name upon them. Men of Sodom, those who hear those things of
theirs. Again elsewhere: Dumb dogs the Jews, not able to bark; not because
they fell into the nature of a dog, but because they fell into dogs in their
choice. John, the citizen of the desert, the high mind, the unconquered
judgment, the one walking below, and touching the heavenly vault, the fruit of
the barren woman, the loosing of the father's speechlessness, the voice of the
voiceless, the one crying in the desert, in the womb (for the womb is a
desert, for she was barren), the double herald, despising children, ris
=59.513= ing against the things seen, came to the Jews, and says to them:
Serpents, offspring of vipers; not because they were serpents in nature, but
because they were crafty in choice. Another prophet again: They became horses
mad for females; each one neighed toward the wife of his neighbor. Does he
then converse with horses? No, but with the wanton, with the unbridled men.
Christ himself to the Jews: You are sons of the devil. Were they then children
of the devil, and did they have the nature of the devil? No, but they imitated
the choice, and they took his name. And he says, interpreting it: You are sons
of the devil, and you wish to do the desires of your father; he is a manslayer
from the beginning. Hold the canon, that the shameless are dogs, that the
rapacious are wolves, that the crafty are serpents, that the treacherous are
asps, the senseless are asses, horses mad for females are the wanton and
unbridled, crows are the unclean, men of Sodom are those who emulate those
things, those who imitate those deeds. Nature then is not wicked, but the mind
is what is corrupted. Hold with accuracy; attend, that names are set from the
manners, and appellations from the mind. And this not in Scripture, but also
in common custom. If we see today a gentle man, we say: The man is a sheep; if
we see someone being shameless, we say, What, is he a man? he is a dog; if we
see a crafty man, The man is a serpent. Do you see that common custom too
knows this law? If we see a man full of many wickednesses, we say: Satan; not
because he is Satan, nor because he is a partner of his according to nature,
but in mind, since he emulates those things according to human power.
Therefore names are from the manners. When then you hear Paul also saying,
There was given a thorn in my flesh, an angel of Satan, do not think the
devil, but those who work the things of the devil, the Greeks, the Jews, the
demons; such then was a demon, an angel of Satan, Alexander the coppersmith
showed me many evils. An angel of Satan; for he was doing the things of Satan.
Such were those who threw him into prison, such were those who scourged him.
Men then as angels of Satan, those who work the things of Satan, those who cut
off the Gospel, those who impede the preaching, those who scourge him, those
who drive him. Attend; the whole meaning has been loosed. 4. Attend with
accuracy, that you may know both the speaker laboring, and Paul suffering
human things. For when Christ went up, and entrusted to them the preaching and
the good embassy. For Christ came as ambassador to us, sent from the Father to
negotiate peace. For there was war of men toward God; and he sent an
ambassador, not a slave, but a Son. O care of the one sent! O philanthropy of
the one who sent! When you hear of one sending


and one sent, do not think a change of place, but a condescension of economy.
For even if he was with us, yet he was also with the Father. And Paul says: We
are ambassadors on behalf of Christ, as though God were entreating through us;
be reconciled to God. Are you an ambassador on behalf of Christ? Entirely. And
where is the ambassador? He has gone. Where has he gone? To the Father. Why?
See things more novel. In human affairs when a nation sends embassy to a
nation, the ambassador enjoys immunity by the law of embassy; but if the
ambassador should suffer something, a great war is moved between the nations.
In the case of God the contrary happened. He came from the Father to men, the
ambassador was slain, and then peace came to be. But ambassadors coming bring
gifts, and receive gifts. What then? What gift did he come bringing to me, and
the gift paradoxical? As the ambassador too is paradoxical, so also the gift
is paradoxical. He came bringing his blood as a gift, he came bringing his
body as a gift. And what gift did he receive from me? Faith. And who gave me
the grace of this? I believed, he granted, the enmity was loosed. Did you see
the ease of the affairs? did you see the speed of the reconciliation? The
ambassador, whenever he comes to a nation, does not deign to converse with a
man of the marketplace or with some one of the poor, but at once at dawn he
goes in to the king. This ambassador came, and at once conversed with a
harlot, a robber, and a magician, and a tax-collector. Did you see the
philanthropy of an ambassador? For he did not come to one and a second, but to
the common nature; and he came clothed in my garment, =59.514= that coming
with bare godhead he might not scare the prey; but he came to man as man.
Since then he came, he brought up our firstfruits, and having called the
apostles, he entrusted the embassy to them: Take the embassy. How can we, I
beg you? Receive the Holy Spirit. You were slain, and what have we to suffer?
Receive the Holy Spirit, and, I am with you, With you, because I am
ambassador; for I have not abandoned you. Since then he went up, and entrusted
the embassy to them. And why do you marvel, if even the king himself, the
Father, is ambassador? We are ambassadors on behalf of Christ, as though God
were entreating through us. The one who was insulted entreats. To what end? Be
reconciled to God. O philanthropy! O goodness unspeakable! Since then he went
up, and entrusted the embassy to the apostles; and the affairs were
uncorrected, and the world was full of thorns, and impiety tyrannized, and
wickedness prevailed, and altars were kindled, and the savor of fat polluted,
and bloods were poured out, and nature was trampled, and birth-pangs were
despised, and laws and new ordinances were being dissolved, and they
sacrificed their daughters and their sons to the demons; and all the rest were
festivals, and choruses of rhetors, and gatherings of philosophers, and
bacchanals of demons, and power of the devil, and swords sharpened, and
everywhere precipices, and everywhere reefs, and the custom that prevailed,
and the birth-pangs of fathers, and the impiety of ancestors, having a road of
pleasure, and laws of kings corrupted, and slaves and masters, and rulers and
private men, and rich and poor, all entwined with impiety, and having many
devices; but the apostles were all twelve, or rather eleven; for the one
became a beast's prey; they were then eleven. Paul then, not having acquired
power of words, not having abundance of money, not a sharpened tongue, not
greatness of fatherland, not distinction of race, not strength of body, naked,
isolated, more voiceless than fishes, obscure from the obscure, unlearned,
uneducated, went about sowing throughout the world the seeds of piety, and all
opposed; and one threw, and scourged; another took, and dragged into prison;
another took, and threw into frying-pans, another caused scandal; and
countless wars. Without, fightings, within, fears. Are they ministers of
Christ? I speak as a fool, I more; in labors more abundantly, in stripes above
measure, in prisons more abundantly, in


deaths often; forty stripes save one I received, thrice I was beaten with
rods, thrice I was shipwrecked, a night and a day I have spent in the deep, in
journeyings often, in perils of rivers, in perils of robbers, in perils from
my own race, in perils from the Gentiles, in perils in the city, in perils in
the wilderness, in perils in the sea, in perils among false brethren, in toil
and hardship, in watchings often, in hunger and thirst, in fastings often, in
cold and nakedness, besides the things without, that which comes upon me
daily, the care of all the Churches. Who is weak, and I am not weak? who is
scandalized, and I do not burn? The brother fell, this one was in pain; an
enemy plotted, this one suffered. You know this, that all who are in Asia
turned away from me. Again: My little children, of whom I travail again, until
Christ be formed in you. Where was he going to suffice for those without,
those within, the scourges, the griefs, the sea, the dangers, the prisons, the
leadings away, frying-pans, plots, peoples, kings, his own, strangers, false
brethren plotting, brothers being weak, strangers departing, his own, those
being tripped? where did the body suffice? where did the soul suffice? He wore
a chain, he dwelt in a prison, he wrote letters; from here he was cast out,
from there he was plotted against; from here angels of Satan, from there an
angel of Satan, many angels of Satan, some into prison, others into
courtrooms. It did not suffice, he was perishing, the soul was being cut
through. He did not see the dogs, and he was being bitten, he was plotted
against from every side. He comes to God, and says to him: I beg you, let me
breathe a little. Where shall I suffice? The angel of Satan strikes me, the
enemies, the plotters, the Greeks, the Jews, false brethren, weak brothers; I
travail, and there is a miscarriage; do I not give birth? and others plot;
today into prison, into the sea tomorrow, after that into the open sea, then

from the first; that you may be taught, even if you ask something
unprofitable, and receive the contrary, not to be grieved. You often ask what
you think is profitable, but it is not profitable. God does not attend to your
tears, but to the misfortune. Since even a child often asks bread from the
mother; but if it is something harmful, which one must not ask, the mother
does not give, nor does she attend to the pain, but to the benefit. And the
one with fever continually bothers the physician to be given something cold,
and the physician does not give; for he does not attend to the desire that
harms for the present, but to the health that profits for the future. So also
Paul did not know how to ask what was profitable. What pardon have we; if in
desperation he came to him, he says to him: Let me breathe a little, let me be
let go from the dangers, give me a little immunity; I am alone, and you have
entrusted the world to me. Once he came to him, and he said nothing; a second
time he came, a third time he came: For this I besought the Lord thrice. To
what end? That the angel of Satan may depart from me, the adversaries. What is
it? That I may preach the preaching without distraction, that I may not have a
plotter, that I may not have an enemy, that there may not be scourges, that
there may not be dangers, that there may not be prisons, that there may not be
leadings away, that there may not be nakedness. To this I come, this I
entreat. I grew weary, I toiled, I was distressed,


I was struck from every side. What then does God say to him, tell me? God did
not nod to his request, he did not give what he asked; but he said: My grace
is sufficient for you; for my power is made perfect in weakness. My grace is
sufficient for you. These things you see, which you suffer; what you do, you
do not see; what the enemies do to you, you see; what I do to you, you do not
see. They scourge you, I give grace. Did you enter the prison, or did you not
enter? I entered; I beg you. Did you see there an angel of Satan, or did you
not see? I saw the one throwing me in. Did he bind you, or did he not bind?
what did I do? did I not shake the walls? did I not open the doors? did I not
loose the bonds? did I not bring you the jailer as a captive? did an angel of
Satan harm you at all? did not the grace from me ben =59.516= efit you? did it
not make the sign more splendid? My grace is sufficient for you. And do you
seek both these and those? You do not want scourges; how do you seek signs?
you do not want dangers; how do you seek wonders? When you are scourged, you
see; when you raise a dead man, you do not see? when your garments were torn,
you saw; when your words made diseases take flight, you did not see? when you
came into the courtroom bound, you saw; when Festus said to you, In a little
you persuade me to become a Christian, you did not see? My grace is sufficient
for you. You received a great weapon, a power of the Spirit conquering the
plots, making you more splendid. What do you want? to suffer nothing? And how
was my power shown? It was shown, yet not so as now. How? For Paul was in pain
also because of this: Woe is me! I am a herald of a kingdom, I am a disciple
of Christ, I promise immortal things; and I am scourged, I am insulted, I am
led away; do not be disheartened because of this; these things make you more
splendid; if you did not suffer these things, you were not so conspicuous. You
entered into the prison. If you had not been bound, but had entered simply, he
held you and led you out; but now you were bound, and he loosed you who were
bound. The feet in the wood, and the grace shook the walls. I say these
things, that no one may be surprised even at the plots concerning us. We say
these things, not equating ourselves to Paul (we are not so mad), but that we
may show that even toward the humble God throws in the same. My grace is
sufficient for you. We were not cast out. Where is the grace? The praise has
become more. Countless enemies plotted, did they not? Where is the grace? More
lovers have been made. They were not seeking to make me give way from you.
Where is the grace? And they added Jews to me. My grace is sufficient for you;
for my power is made perfect in weakness. Never be surprised at affliction;
affliction has been allotted together with the Gospel. My grace is sufficient
for you; for my power is made perfect in weakness. How are you scourged, and
you set free those who scourge? you are plotted against, and you prevail over
those who plot? For my power is made perfect in weakness. What then Paul
heard, do you want this? is this sufficient for you? That I may be scourged,
dishonored, insulted, this I want. Therefore I take pleasure in weaknesses, in
insults, in persecution. O well-disposed mind of a servant! He came to him
asking to be freed from the afflictions; but when he heard that his Master not
only does not wish, but even wills the contrary, This seems good to you, that
I be afflicted, and I too will this, and for this I give thanks. He did not
say, I wish, only, but, I am pleased, I will, I desire to be scourged, to be
driven, to be straitened. Therefore I take pleasure in weaknesses, in insults,
in persecutions, in necessities, in distresses. Why? That the power of Christ
may tabernacle upon me, to whom be the glory unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
