Letter of Eusebius to Constantia the empress. And since you have also written
concerning some image as of Christ, wishing that this image be sent to you by
us: what do you mean, and of what sort is this which you say is an image of
Christ? I do not know from where you yourself, having been moved, command that
an image of our Saviour be drawn. What sort of image of Christ do you seek? Is
it the true and unchangeable one, which by nature bears his characters; or
this one which he assumed for our sake, having put on the shape of the form of
a servant? For the Word of God, who is also God, having taken the form of a
servant, condemned sin in the likeness of sinful flesh, as the divine Apostle
says. Thus, having redeemed us from the ancient bitterness and demonic slavery
by his precious blood, he set us free. Two forms then being present to him, as
for the form of God I myself do not even think that you are seeking it, having
once been taught by him that no one has known the Father except the Son, nor
would anyone ever know the Son worthily except only the Father who begot him
.... But rather you are surely seeking the form of the servant, and of the
little flesh with which he was clothed for our sake. But we have learned that
this too has been mixed with the glory of the Godhead, and that the mortal has
been swallowed up by life. And it is no wonder at all if after the ascent into
the heavens he has appeared such, when even while still living among men the
God Word, pledging beforehand to the chosen of his own words the vision of his
kingdom, having transformed the form of the servant, showed that very form on
the mountain as above human nature; when his face flashed like the sun, and
his garments like the light. Who then would be able to inscribe with dead and
lifeless colors and shadow-paintings the gleaming and flashing radiances of
such great dignity and glory, when the divine disciples did not even endure to
look upon him when he appeared in this manner, they who fell on their faces,
confessing that what they beheld was unbearable to them? If then even then his
form in the flesh obtained such great power, having been transformed by the
Godhead dwelling in it =20.1548=, what must one say when, having put off
mortality and washed away corruption, he transformed the appearance of the
form of the servant into the glory of the Master and God, after the victory
over death, and after the ascent into the heavens, and after the seating on
the royal throne at the right of the Father, after the rest in the unnameable
and ineffable bosom of the Father, into which as he went up and was restored
the heavenly powers sang his praises, saying: Lift up your gates, O you
rulers, and be lifted up, you everlasting gates, and the king of glory shall
enter.
Therefore the form of the servant too, having come to be in such things, has
been wholly changed throughout into his ineffable and inexpressible light, a
light befitting the God Word himself, which eye has not seen, and ear has not
heard, and which has not arisen upon the heart of man. How then could anyone
attain to things impossible? And how could anyone paint an image of a form so
marvelous and incomprehensible, if indeed one should still call form the
godlike and noetic essence? Unless, like the unbelieving nations, someone
should paint things in no way and in no manner resembling him, as a painter
who paints things that resemble nothing. For such also are those men, making
idols, whenever they would wish to fashion and liken God, as they suppose, or
some one of the heroes as they would say, or some such thing, being able to
paint nothing like, nor even near, they shadow-paint strange things, drawing
man-like figures.
But that such things are not right for us, you yourself surely also agree. But
if you say you ask from us not an image of the form transformed into God, but
of his little flesh before the transformation, that mortal flesh itself, did
this reading alone escape you, in which God legislates not to make a likeness,
neither of the things in heaven, nor of the things on the earth below? Or have
you yourself at some time in the Church heard such a thing, or even from
another? Have not such things been banished throughout the whole inhabited
world and driven far from the Churches, and has it not been proclaimed among
all that for us alone it is not permitted to do such a thing? .... For I do
not know how a certain little woman, once carrying in her hands two figures
drawn as if they were philosophers, threw out a word, as if they were of Paul
and of the Saviour; I cannot say either from where she had taken them, or from
where she had learned this. Lest she herself, and others as well, be
scandalized, I took it from her and kept it with myself, not thinking it well
to bring these things out to others at all, lest we seem, after the manner of
idolaters, to carry our God about in an image. And I hear Paul teaching us all
no longer to attend to the things of the flesh. For if we have known Christ
according to the flesh, he says, yet now we know him no longer. Report has it
that Simon Magus is worshipped among the godless heretics, drawn in lifeless
matter. And we ourselves have also seen the one named after madness being
escorted in an image by the Manichaeans.
But such things have been forbidden to us. Confessing then our Saviour as God
the Lord, =20.1549= we prepare ourselves to see him as God, ourselves having
cleansed our hearts with all zeal, that we may see him having been purified.
For blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God. But if from
abundance, before the coming face-to-face seeing and vision, you yourselves
set much store by the images of our Saviour, what better painter would we have
than the God Word himself?
