LETTER OF SAINT CYRIL BISHOP OF ALEXANDRIA to Calosyrius, bishop of the
Arsenoites, against those who say that the divine is anthropomorphic.

Some having arrived from the mountain of Calamon were asked by me
concerning the monks there, in what manner they continue, or also what
conduct of life they have. And they said that many indeed succeed in
asceticism, and greatly wish to accomplish the life befitting monks; but
that there are some who go about and from ignorance disturb those who
wish to be at rest, then they affirmed that they invent such things as
these: for they said, Since the divine Scripture says that man came to
be according to the image of God, one must believe that the divine is
either man-like or at any rate anthropomorphic; which is altogether
unintelligent, and able to bring those who choose to think thus under
the charges of the last impiety. 604 For man is admittedly according to
the image of God, but the likeness is not bodily; for God is bodiless.
And the Savior himself will teach this, saying "God is spirit."
Therefore he is not embodied, if he is spirit, nor in a bodily form.
For what is outside body would also be outside shape. For the divine is
without quantity and without shape. But if they think that according to
the nature of the human body even God over all himself was shaped, let
them say whether he also has feet that he may walk, hands that he may
work through them, and eyes that he may see through them. Where then
does he walk? Or from what places to what places does he who fills all
things go away? For he said "Do I not fill heaven and earth, says the
Lord?" Or what hands does he move to works, he who creates through the
living Word? And if he has eyes like us as lying in a face, he does not
see at all the things behind; but when he looks toward the east, does he
not know what those in the west are doing? And if again he looks to the
west, does he not see those in the east? I am ashamed even to write
these things, but because of the madness of some I have become a fool,
not willingly rather, but compelled by them. Let those then who babble
these things be muzzled, as ignorant, and let them be at rest, not
touching things beyond their power, or rather not speaking against 605
God. For God is above all creation, being thought neither a body, nor in
types or bodily shapes, but he is simple, immaterial, formless,
uncompounded, not composed of parts or members or portions as we are,
but rather spirit, according to the Scriptures, and overseeing all
things, being everywhere and filling all things, and left out of
nothing; for he fills heaven and earth. But that man has been made
according to the image of God has other indications and implications.
For he alone besides all the living things on earth is rational,
compassionate, having fitness toward every virtue, and having obtained
also the ruling of all things on the earth according to likeness and
image of God. Therefore according to being a rational living thing and
inasmuch as he is virtue-loving and ruling of the things on the earth,
he is said to have been made in the image of God. But if they think the
image is said according to the shape of the body, it does not hurt to
say that God is also conformed to the irrational living things. For we
see that they too are of the same portions as we, having feet and mouth
and eyes and nostrils and tongue and the other members of the body. Let
your piety then stop such people, or rather also rebuke those accustomed
to babble these things.

And I hear that they say the mystical blessing is idle toward
sanctification, if a remnant of it should remain unto another day. But
they are mad saying these things; for Christ is not 606 altered, nor
will his holy body be changed, but the power of the blessing and the
life-giving grace is continual in it.

And certain others also go about, as they say, pretending to devote
themselves to prayer alone, and working nothing, and they make piety a
pretext of sloth and of gain, not thinking right things. Since let them
say themselves even better than the holy apostles, who worked when the
time gave them leisure for this, and also labored toward the word of
God. And how have they also forgotten the blessed Paul writing to some:
"For I hear that some walk among you working nothing, but being
busybodies"?

The Church then does not accept those who do this. For one must
admittedly pray intensely, remaining quiet in the ascetic houses; but it
hurts nothing, or rather it is also exceedingly most beneficial even to
work, so that he not be found burdensome to others, receiving their
sweats for his own need, and so that he may also from his own labors
comfort a widow and an orphan, and some of the brothers who are weak.
But if they think it a good thing not to touch work, when all emulate
their things, who is the one who feeds them? Some then make idleness and
gluttony a pretext, thinking it necessary to devote themselves to prayer
alone, and not to touch work at all. And do not allow the orthodox to
assemble with those called Melitians, so that they not become partakers
of their apostasy. 607 But if those coming in repentance come to the
orthodox, let them be acceptable; but let no one be indifferent, nor
communicate with those who do not repent, so that they not, as I said,
become partakers of the bad faith that is in them.

Let your piety cause these things to be read in those monasteries for
the building of those there, and let it charge to keep them, so that
neither the orthodox be wearied, their conscience being paralyzed, nor
indeed those who wish to be idle-fed have some infiltration of seeming
to be good. I pray that you be well in the Lord, beloved and most
longed-for.
