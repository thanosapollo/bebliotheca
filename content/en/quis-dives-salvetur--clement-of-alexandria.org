CLEMENT'S DISCOURSE: WHO IS THE RICH MAN THAT SHALL BE SAVED 1.1 Those who
bring laudatory speeches as gifts to the rich would justly, in my judgment, be
deemed not only flatterers and illiberal, as pretending at a great price to
confer thankless favors, 1.2 but also impious and treacherous; impious,
because, omitting to praise and glorify the only perfect and good God, from
whom are all things and through whom are all things and unto whom are all
things, they attach ··········· the honor to men ··········· life rolling
······· 1.3 the capital that lies under the judgment of God; and treacherous,
because even the abundance itself is of itself sufficient to puff up the souls
of those who possess it and to corrupt them and turn them aside from the way
by which it is possible to attain salvation, while these still further stun
the minds of the rich, lifting them up with the pleasures of immoderate
praises and preparing them to despise all affairs whatsoever except the wealth
on account of which they are admired, transferring, as the saying is, fire
upon fire, pumping conceit upon conceit and adding bulk to wealth, a heavier
burden upon what is by nature already heavy, of which they ought rather to
take away and cut off, as of a dangerous and deadly disease; for to him who is
exalted and magnified there is fastened, as a counterpart, the change 1.4
toward the low and a fall, as the divine word teaches. But to me it seems far
more philanthropic than to court ············ those who are rich ·········· to
their harm, to join in helping ·········· their salvation ·············· in
every possible way, on the one hand asking it of God, who surely and gladly
grants such things to his own children, and on the other healing their souls
by the word through the grace of the Savior, enlightening them and leading
them on to the acquisition of the truth, which he who attains, and is made
illustrious by good works, alone will carry off the prize of eternal life 1.5.
And prayer too needs a soul sturdy and persistent, measured out even to the
last day of life, and the way of life needs a disposition good and lasting and
stretching itself toward all the commandments of the Savior. 2.1 And the cause
why salvation seems harder for the rich than for those of men who have no
money is in danger of being not something simple, 2.2 but varied. For some,
hearing on the spot and offhand the voice of the Lord, that a camel will more
easily pass through the eye of a needle than a rich man into the kingdom of
the heavens, despair of themselves as of those who will not live, and giving
all things to the world and hanging upon the life here as the only one left to
them, have withdrawn still further from the way there, no longer inquiring
either whom the Master and Teacher addresses as the rich, or 2.3 how what is
impossible among men becomes possible. And others have understood this rightly
and fittingly, but having neglected the works that bear upon salvation they
have not prepared the needed 2.4 preparation for attaining the things hoped
for. And I say both these things concerning the rich who have also perceived
the power of the Savior and the manifest salvation; but of those uninitiated
in the truth I have little care. 3.1 It is necessary then that those who love
the truth and love the brethren ··············· and who neither insolently
overbear the rich who are called, nor again fall down before them through
their own love of gain, first take away from them by the word the empty
despair and make clear, with the needed exposition of the oracles of the Lord,
that

the inheritance of the kingdom of the heavens has not been cut off from them
altogether, if they 3.2 obey the commandments; then, whenever they learn that
they fear a fear that is without dread, and that the Savior gladly receives
them when they are willing, then also to show beforehand and initiate them
into the mysteries, by what works and dispositions they may enjoy the hope,
since it is neither impossible 3.3 for them nor, on the contrary, comes to
them at random. But in the very way of athletes, that we may compare small and
perishable things with great and incorruptible, let the man rich according to
the world reckon this also 3.4 for himself. For of those, the one who has
despaired of being able to win and to obtain crowns has not even enrolled
himself for the contest at all, while the other, having conceived this hope in
his mind, but not taking on the toils and exercises and fitting foods, has
gone through 3.5 uncrowned and has missed his hopes. So also let one who has
put on this earthly wrapping neither from the start have himself proclaimed
out of the contests of the Savior, being faithful at least and seeing together
the greatness of the philanthropy of God, nor again, remaining unexercised and
without a fight, hope to share the crowns of incorruption without dust and
without sweat 3.6; but let him bring himself and submit, to the word as
trainer, and to Christ as president of the games; and let his appointed food
and drink be the new covenant of the Lord, and his exercises the commandments,
and his good form and adornment the fair dispositions, love, faith, hope,
knowledge of the truth, ··········, meekness, compassion, gravity, so that,
when the last trumpet signals the race and the departure from here, as from
the stadium of life, he may stand beside the awarder of the prizes with a good
conscience, a victor, confessed worthy of the fatherland above, into which he
returns with crowns and angelic proclamations. 4.1 May the Savior then grant
us, beginning the word from this point, to contribute to the brethren the true
and the fitting and the saving things, first toward the hope itself and second
toward the bringing 4.2 of the hope. And what he grants to those who ask, he
also teaches those who request, and he looses ignorance and shakes off
despair, introducing again the same words concerning the rich, which become
interpreters of themselves 4.3 and safe expositors. For there is nothing like
hearing again the very sayings which until now have been troubling us in the
gospels, as we listened untested and mistakenly through childishness. 4.4 "As
he was going out into the way, one came and knelt saying: 4.5 'Good teacher,
what shall I do, that I may inherit eternal life?' And Jesus says: 'Why do you
call me good? No one is good except one, God. You know the commandments: do
not commit adultery, do not murder, do not steal, do not bear 4.6 false
witness, honor your father and your mother.' And he answering says to him:
'All these I have kept from my youth.' And Jesus looking upon him loved him
and said: 'One thing you lack: if you wish to be perfect, sell what you have
and give to the poor, and you will have treasure in 4.7 heaven, and come,
follow me.' And he, becoming gloomy at the word, 4.8 went away grieving; for
he was one who had many possessions and fields. And Jesus looking round says
to his disciples: 'How hardly shall those who have possessions enter into the
kingdom of God.' 4.9 And the disciples were amazed at his words. And Jesus
answering again says to them: 'Children, how hard it is for those who trust in
possessions to enter into the kingdom of God; easily a camel will enter
through the eye of the needle than a rich man into the kingdom of God.' And
they were exceedingly astonished and said: 'Who then can be saved?' And
looking upon them he said: 'What is impossible with men =4.10= is possible
with God.' Peter began to say to him: 'See, we have left all and have followed
you.' And Jesus answering says: 'Amen I say to you, whoever leaves his own
things and parents and brothers and possessions for my sake and for the sake
of the gospel,

shall receive a hundredfold. Now in this time to have fields and possessions
and houses and brothers with persecutions, unto what? But in that which is
coming there is eternal life. And there the first shall be last and the last
first.'" 5.1 These things are written in the gospel according to Mark; and in
all the other acknowledged ones perhaps a little of the words is changed in
each place, but all display the same concord 5.2 of meaning. And we must,
knowing clearly that the Savior teaches his own nothing humanly, but all
things with divine and mystic wisdom, not hear the things said in a fleshly
way, but search out and learn thoroughly the mind hidden in them with worthy
inquiry and understanding 5.3. For even the things that seem to have been made
plain by the Lord himself toward the disciples, of the things spoken
riddlingly, are found to need no less attention still even now, but even more
still even now, 5.4 because of the exceeding excess of prudence in them. And
where even the things supposed to have been opened by him to those within,
even to those themselves who are called by him children of the kingdom, still
need more care, how much more the things that seemed to have been brought out
simply and for that reason were not even questioned by those who heard, yet
that bear upon the whole end itself of salvation, but that have been covered
with a wondrous and superheavenly depth of thought: it is not fitting to
receive them superficially with the ears, but to let the mind down upon the
very spirit of the Savior and the secret of his meaning. 6.1 For our Lord and
Savior has been asked with pleasure a question most fitting to him: the Life
concerning life, the Savior concerning salvation, the Teacher concerning the
chief point of the doctrines not of those being taught, the Truth concerning
the true immortality, the Word concerning the paternal word, the Perfect
concerning the perfect rest, the Incorruptible concerning the 6.2 sure
incorruption. He has been asked about those things for the sake of which he
also came down, which he trains, which he teaches, which he provides, that he
may show the subject 6.3 of the gospel, that it is a gift of eternal life.
And as God he foreknows both what he is about to be asked and what someone is
about to answer him. For who even more than the prophet of prophets and Lord
of every prophetic 6.4 spirit? And being called good, taking from this first
word itself the keynote, he begins the teaching from this point, turning the
disciple toward God the good and first and only treasurer of eternal life,
which the Son gives us having received it from that one. 7.1 Therefore the
greatest and most crowning of the lessons toward life must be laid down in the
soul from the very beginning: to know the God who is eternal and giver of
eternal things and first and highest and one and good God. Whom it is possible
to acquire through knowledge and 7.2 apprehension; for this is an unchangeable
and unshaken beginning and foundation of life, science of God who really is
and who grants the things that are, that is the eternal things, from whom both
being belongs to the others and the 7.3 remaining to receive. For ignorance of
this is death, but the knowledge of him and appropriation and the love toward
him and assimilation is alone life. 8.1 This one then he first commands the
man who is to live the real life to know, whom "no one knows except the Son
and he to whom the Son may reveal him"; then after that one to learn the
greatness of the Savior and the newness of the grace, because according to the
apostle "the law was given through Moses, the grace and the truth through
Jesus Christ"; and the things given through a faithful slave are not equal to
those granted by the genuine 8.2 son. If at any rate the law of Moses was
sufficient to provide eternal life, in vain does the Savior himself come and
suffer for us, running through the humanity from birth

as far as the sign, and in vain does he who has done all the lawful
commandments "from youth" ask 8.3 immortality from another, kneeling. For he
has not only fulfilled the law, but even beginning straightway from the first
age; since what is great or over-splendid in an old age barren of injustices
which youthful desires beget, or boiling anger, or love of money? But if
someone in the skip of youth and in the burning heat of the age has furnished
a mind ripe and older than the time, this one is a wondrous contestant 8.4 and
distinguished and gray in judgment. But nevertheless this man of such a sort
is precisely persuaded that nothing is lacking to him toward righteousness,
but that he wholly still needs life; wherefore he asks it from the only one
able to give; and toward the law he has frankness, but he supplicates the 8.5
Son of God. He changes station "from faith to faith"; as one tossing unsafely
in the law and lying at anchor dangerously, he shifts harbor to the Savior.
9.1 Jesus then does not refute him as not having fulfilled all the things from
the law, but even loves him and welcomes him exceedingly for the obedience in
the things he learned, yet says he is incomplete as toward eternal life, as
not having fulfilled perfect things, and a worker of law, but idle of true 9.2
life. Those things then are good (and who does not say so? for the
"commandment is holy") as far as a certain training with fear and a
preparatory education, advancing toward the legislation of Jesus, the highest,
and the grace; but the fullness of "law is Christ unto righteousness to
everyone who believes", not making slaves as a slave, but even sons and
brothers and fellow-heirs of those who accomplish the will of the Father. 10.1
"If you wish to become perfect." He was not yet perfect then; for nothing is
more perfect than the perfect. And divinely the "if you wish" made clear the
self-determination of the soul conversing with him. For the choice was upon
the man 10.2 as free, and the gift upon God as Lord. And he gives to those who
are willing and over-eager and who ask, in order that in this way the
salvation may become their own. For God does not compel, for violence is an
enemy to God, but he furnishes to those who seek and provides to those who ask
and 10.3 opens to those who knock. If then you wish, if you really wish and
are not deceiving yourself, acquire what is lacking. "One thing is left to
you", the one thing, the thing that is mine, the good, that which is already
beyond law, which law does not give, which law does not 10.4 contain, which is
proper to the living. At any rate he who fulfilled all the things of the law
"from youth" and who swaggered in the over-bulky things was not able to add
this one thing to the whole, the special thing of the Savior, in order to
receive eternal life, which he longs for; but being displeased he went away,
vexed 10.5 at the command of the life for which he was supplicating. For he
did not truly wish life, as he alleged, but was wrapping himself only in the
glory of a good purpose, and he was able to be busy about many things, but the
one thing, the work of life, he was unable and unwilling and weak to
accomplish; 10.6 such a thing as the Savior also said to Martha, busy about
many things and dragged about and troubled in a serving way, and blaming her
sister because, leaving the serving, she sits beside his feet spending a
disciple's leisure: "You are troubled about many things; but Mary has chosen
the good portion, and it shall not be taken from her." 10.7 So also he
commanded this man, leaving off the much-doing, to cling to one thing and sit
beside it, the grace of him who adds eternal life. 11.1 What then was it that
urged him to flight and made him desert from the teacher, from the
supplication, from the hope, from the life, 11.2 from the things already
toiled through? "Sell your belongings." And what is this? Not what some
receive offhand, that he commands them to throw away the substance they have
and to stand off from

the possessions, but to banish from the soul the doctrines concerning
possessions, the over-great sympathy toward them, the desire, the flutter and
disease about them, the cares, the thorns of life, which choke the seed of
life. 11.3 For neither is it a great and enviable thing to be in want of
possessions at random, not upon a reason of life (for so at least those who
have nothing anywhere, but are deserted and beggars of the things for the day,
the poor thrown along the roads, "not knowing" God and "the righteousness of
God", would by this alone, the being extremely in want and without resource of
life and lacking the least things, be most blessed and most dear to God and
the only ones having eternal 11.4 life), nor is it a new thing to renounce
wealth and to grant it to the poor or to fatherlands, which many have done
before the descent of the Savior, some for the sake of leisure for words and
dead wisdom, others for empty fame and vainglory, Anaxagorases and
Democrituses and Crateses. 12.1 Why then does he command as a new thing and
proper to God and alone life-giving, that which did not save those before? And
if the "new creation", the Son of God, indicates and teaches something
special, he does not enjoin the apparent thing, which others have done, but
some other thing signified through this, greater and more divine and more
perfect, the stripping of the soul itself and of the disposition of the
passions that lie under, and the cutting out and casting out of the things
alien to the judgment by the roots. For this is the lesson proper to the
faithful 12.2, and the teaching worthy of the Savior. For those before, having
despised the things outside, let go the properties and destroyed them besides,
but the passions of the souls I think they even stretched further; for they
came to be in contempt and boastfulness and vainglory and disdain of the other
men, as themselves having worked something beyond man. 12.3 How then would the
Savior have been advising those who are to live forever the things that will
harm 12.4 and outrage them toward the life which he promises? For that too is
so: someone, having unloaded the possession, can no less still have the desire
and the appetite for the possessions melted in and living with him, and have
thrown away the use, yet being in want and at the same time longing for what
he squandered be grieved with a double grief, both by the absence of the
service and 12.5 by the presence of the regret. For it is unreachable and
impossible that one who needs the things necessary for living should not have
his judgment broken and keep a busyness away from the better things, trying to
furnish these however and from wherever. 13.1 And how much more useful is the
contrary, that one who has acquired enough should both himself not suffer evil
about the possession and help those to whom it is due? For what communion
would be left among men, if no one had anything? 13.2 And how would this dogma
not be found plainly contrary to and fighting with many other and fair dogmas
of the Lord? 13.3 "Make for yourselves friends from the mammon of
unrighteousness, in order that when it fails, they may receive you into the
eternal tents." "Acquire treasures in heaven, where neither moth nor eating
makes away with them nor thieves dig 13.4 through." How could anyone feed the
hungry and give drink to the thirsty and cover the naked and bring in the
homeless, things for which he threatens fire and the outer darkness to those
who have not done them, if each himself should first be lacking all these 13.5
things? But indeed he himself is guest with Zacchaeus and Levi and Matthew,
the rich men and tax-collectors, and he does not command them to let the
possessions go, but imposing the just use and taking away the unjust 13.6 he
proclaims: "Today salvation has come to this house." So he praises the need of
them, that even with this addition he commands the communion, to give drink to
the thirsty, to give bread to the hungry, to receive 13.7 the homeless, to
clothe the naked. And if it is not possible to fulfill these needs except from
possessions, and he commands them to stand off from the possessions, what else
would the Lord be doing than advising to give

the same things and not to give, to feed and not to feed, to receive and to
shut out, to share and not to share, which is the most unreasonable of all
things. 14.1 Therefore the possessions that also benefit the neighbors are not
to be thrown away; for they are properties, being acquirable, and possessions,
being useful, and they have been prepared by God for the use of men, which lie
beside and have been put under, just as some matter and instruments, toward a
good use for those 14.2 who know. The instrument, if you use it artistically,
is artistic; if you fall short 14.3 of the art, it enjoys your lack of music,
being itself without blame. Such also is wealth, an instrument. You can use it
justly; it serves toward righteousness; someone uses it unjustly; again it is
found a servant 14.4 of injustice; for it is naturally to serve, but not to
rule. One must not then blame that which from itself has neither the good nor
the evil, being without blame, but that which is able both to use these things
well and badly, from whichever it may choose, being cause according to this
very thing. And this is the mind of man, having also a free criterion in
itself and 14.5 the self-determination of the handling of the things given; so
that one should not make away with the properties rather than the passions of
the soul, which do not allow the better use of the belongings, in order that
having become beautiful and good 14.6 he may also be able to use these
properties well. The renouncing all the belongings then and selling all the
belongings is to be taken in this way as having been said concerning the
psychic passions. 15.1 I at any rate would say that too: since some things are
within the soul and some outside, and if the soul uses them well, these too
seem fair, but if wickedly, wicked, does he who commands to alienate the
belongings first decline those things after whose removal the passions still
remain, or rather those after whose removal the properties also become useful
15.2? He then who has thrown away the worldly abundance can still be rich in
the passions even when the matter is not present; for the disposition works
its own and chokes and presses the reasoning and inflames it with the desires
that have grown with it; nothing then has been of use to him in being poor
15.3 in possessions while rich in the passions. For he has not thrown away the
things to be thrown away, but the things indifferent, and he has cut himself
round of the things that serve, but has kindled the matter of vice inborn by
the want 15.4 of the things outside. One must then renounce the belongings
that are harmful, not those that, if one understands the right use, are able
also to help together. 15.5 And the things managed with prudence and
temperance and piety benefit, but the things that bring damage are to be
thrust away; and the things outside do not harm. 15.6 Thus then the Lord also
introduces the need of the things outside, commanding to put away not the
things of life, but the things that use these badly; and these 16.1 were the
sicknesses and passions of the soul. The wealth of these, when present, is
death-bringing to all, but when it has perished, is saving; of which one must
present the soul clean, that is poor and naked, and so already hear the Savior
saying: "Come, follow 16.2 me." For he himself already becomes a way to him
who is pure in heart, but into an unclean soul the grace of God does not steal
in; and unclean is the soul that is rich 16.3 in the desires and in labor with
many loves and worldly ones. For he who has properties and gold and silver and
houses as gifts of God, and serving the God who gives from them unto the
salvation of men, and knowing that he has acquired these through the brethren
rather than himself, and being better than the possession of them, not being a
slave of what he has acquired nor carrying these about in the soul nor
defining and describing his own life in these, but always working through some
fair and divine work also, and if he must at some time be deprived of these,
being able with a gracious judgment to bear the riddance of them equally, just
as also the abundance, this is the one

blessed by the Lord and called poor in spirit, an heir ready of the kingdom of
heaven, not able to live as a rich man; 17.1 but he who carries the wealth in
the soul and instead of a spirit of God carries gold in the heart or a field
and always making the possession measureless and looking each time at the
more, bent down and shackled by the snares of the world, being earth and about
to go away unto earth, from where can he desire and take thought of the
kingdom of the heavens, a man wearing not a heart but a field or a mine, to be
found of necessity in these things in which he has chosen? "For where the mind
of the man is, there also is his treasure." 17.2 And the Lord knows two
treasures at least, the one good, for "the good man from the good treasure of
the heart brings forth the good", and the wicked, for "the bad man from the
bad treasure brings forth the bad, because out of the abundance of the heart
the 17.3 mouth speaks." As then a treasure is not one with him, as also with
us, the one that gives the sudden great gain in a finding, but also a second,
the one without gain and unenviable and hard to acquire and damaging, so also
wealth is one of good things, and one of evil things, if at least we know the
wealth and the 17.4 treasure not detached from one another in nature. And the
one wealth would be acquirable and to be put around, and the other
unacquirable and to be thrown away; and in the 17.5 same way poverty too is
blessed, the spiritual. Wherefore Matthew also added: "Blessed are the poor;"
how? "in spirit." And again: "Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for the
righteousness of God;" therefore wretched are the contrary poor, without share
in God, and more without share in the human possession, and without taste of
the righteousness of God. 18.1 So that the rich who will enter with difficulty
into the kingdom are to be heard in a learned way, not clumsily nor rustically
nor fleshily; for it has not been said so. Nor does salvation lie upon the
things outside, neither if these are many nor if few, or small or great, or
glorious or inglorious, or well-reputed or ill-reputed, but upon the virtue of
the soul, faith and hope and love and brotherly affection and knowledge and
meekness and 18.2 unconceitedness and truth, whose prize is salvation. For
neither will anyone live through beauty of body or on the contrary perish; but
he who uses the body given purely and according to God will live, and he who
corrupts the 18.3 temple of God will be corrupted. And someone ugly can be
wanton, and according to beauty be temperate; nor does strength and size of
body make alive, nor does nothing of the limbs destroy, but the soul that uses
these 18.4 provides the cause toward either side. Bear then, he says, being
struck on the face, which even someone strong and in good condition can 18.5
obey, and again someone weak by incontinence of judgment can transgress. So
also someone without resource and without a living might at some time be found
drunk with desires, and someone rich in possessions sober and poor in
pleasures, persuaded 18.6, intelligent, pure, chastened. If then that which is
especially and first to live is the soul, and about this virtue growing saves,
but vice puts to death, it is already clearly evident that this also, being
poor in the things by which one is corrupted by wealth, is saved, and being
rich in those 18.7 things which wealth wears down is put to death; and let us
no longer seek the cause of the end elsewhere than in the constitution of the
soul and its disposition toward obedience of God and purity, and toward
transgression of commandments and a gathering of vice. 19.1 He then is truly
and fairly rich who is rich in the virtues and able to use every fortune
piously and faithfully; and the bastard rich man is he who is rich according
to the flesh and has transferred the life into the outer possession that is
passing and being corrupted and becoming 19.2 at one time of one and at
another of another and in the end of no one anywhere.

Again in the same way there is also a genuine poor man and another bastard
poor man and falsely named. The one is poor according to spirit, the proper
thing, and the other according to the world, the 19.3 alien thing. To him then
who is not poor according to the world and is rich according to the passions,
he who is not poor according to spirit and is rich according to God says
"stand off" "from the properties alien in your soul, in order that having
become pure in heart you may see God, which is also through another voice to
enter 19.4 into the kingdom of the heavens. And how may you stand off from
them? By selling. What then? That you may receive money instead of properties?
Having made an exchange of wealth toward wealth 19.5, having turned the
apparent substance into silver? By no means; but instead of the things
formerly existing in the soul, which you long to save, introducing in exchange
another wealth that makes godlike and is a supplier of eternal life, the
dispositions according to the commandment of God, in return for which there
will be left over for you a wage 19.6 and honor, continuous salvation and
eternal incorruption. Thus you sell the belongings well, the many and
superfluous and those that shut the heavens against you, exchanging for them
the things able to save. Let the fleshly poor have those, needing these too,
and you, having received the spiritual wealth in exchange, would already have
treasure in the heavens." 20.1 Not understanding these things in the right
way, the man of many possessions and of the law, nor how the same man can be
both poor and rich and both have possessions and not have them and use the
world and not use it, went away gloomy and downcast, leaving the post of the
life which he was able only to desire, but not also to attain, having himself
made the hard thing 20.2 impossible for himself. For it was hard that the soul
not be led about nor be struck with lightning by the delicate things present
with the obvious wealth and by the blooming enchantments, but it was not
impossible even in this to lay hold of salvation, if someone should transfer
himself from the sensible wealth to the intelligible and God-taught and should
learn to use the things indifferent well and properly and as one would set out
unto eternal life 20.3. And the disciples too at first themselves became very
fearful and struck with astonishment when they heard; why then? Was it because
they themselves also had acquired many possessions? But even these very nets
and hooks and the serving skiffs they left long ago, which were the only
things 20.4 they had. Why then in fear do they say: "Who can be saved?" They
heard well and as disciples the thing said parabolically and unclearly 20.5 by
the Lord and perceived the depth of the words. For the sake of want of
possessions then they were of good hope toward salvation; but since they were
conscious to themselves of not yet having put away the passions completely
(for they were newly taught and only recently enlisted by the Savior), they
"were exceedingly astonished" and despaired of themselves no less than that
man of many possessions and who was clinging terribly to the possession, which
at least he preferred 20.6 to eternal life. It was then altogether worthy of
fear for the disciples, if both the man who has acquired possessions and the
one pregnant with the passions, in which they themselves also were rich, will
similarly be driven from the heavens; for salvation belongs to passionless and
pure souls. 21.1 And the Lord answers that "what is impossible among men is
possible with God". Again this too is full of great wisdom. That the man
practicing and working through passionlessness by himself accomplishes
nothing, but if he becomes clear as over-desiring this and as having been
earnest about it, 21.2 he prevails by the addition of the power from God; for
God breathes together with souls that are willing, but if they should stand
off from the eagerness, the spirit given from God also was contracted; for to
save the unwilling 21.3 is of one using force, but to save those who choose is
of one granting a favor. Nor does the kingdom of God belong to those who sleep
and play the sluggard, but the "violent snatch it"; for this alone is fair
violence, to do violence to God and to snatch life from God, and he who has
known those

who hold on violently, or rather firmly, has yielded and given way; for God
rejoices to be defeated in such things 21.4. Therefore having heard these
things the blessed Peter, the elect, the special, the first of the disciples,
for whom alone and for himself the Savior also pays 21.5 the tax, quickly
snatched and put the word together. And what does he say? "See, we have left
all and have followed you." And if he means by "all" his own properties,
perhaps leaving four obols, as the saying is, he magnifies himself, and if he
should declare these a match 21.6 he would escape notice making the kingdom of
the heavens of equal worth with them; but if, as we are saying just now,
having thrown away the old intelligible properties and psychic diseases they
follow in the track of the teacher, this would already be kindled 21.7 for
those who will be enrolled in the heavens. For this is truly to follow the
Savior, going after his sinlessness and perfection and adorning and
rhythmizing the soul toward him as a mirror and disposing all things through
all things likewise. 22.1 "And Jesus answering: Amen I say to you, whoever
leaves his own things and parents and brothers and possessions for my sake and
for the sake of the 22.2 gospel, shall receive a hundredfold." But let not
this trouble us, nor the thing still harder than this brought out elsewhere in
the voices: "Whoever does not hate father and mother and children, and besides
22.3 even his own soul, cannot be my disciple." For the God of peace does not
introduce hatred and dissolution from the dearest, 22.4 he who even advises to
love the enemies. And if the enemies are to be loved, analogously, going up
from those, also those nearest of kin; or if those of blood are to be hated,
much more does the word going down teach to throw the enemies out, so that the
words, destroying one another, would be 22.5 convicted. But they do not
destroy, nor nearly; for from the same judgment and disposition and upon the
same definition one would hate a father and love an enemy, he who neither
defends himself against an enemy nor 22.6 respects a father more than Christ.
For in that word he cuts out hatred and evil-doing, and in this the
over-regard toward the things grown with one, 22.7 if it should harm toward
salvation. If at any rate a father or son or brother should be godless to
someone and should become a hindrance of the faith and an obstacle of the life
above, let him not be carried with this one nor be of one mind, but let him
dissolve the fleshly kinship through the spiritual enmity. 23.1 Reckon the
matter to be a lawsuit. Let the father seem to you to stand by and say "I
sowed you and nourished you, follow me and do injustice together and do not
obey the law of Christ" and whatever a blasphemous 23.2 man and dead by nature
would say. And from the other side hear the Savior: "I regenerated you, having
been badly generated by the world toward death, I freed you, I healed, I
ransomed; I will provide you life without pause, eternal, above the world; I
will show you the face of the good Father God; 'do not call for yourself a
father upon earth'; 'let the dead bury the 23.3 dead, but you follow me'; for
I will lead you up into rest and enjoyment of unspeakable and untellable good
things, 'which neither eye saw nor ear heard nor rose upon the heart of men,
into which angels long to stoop and see what good things God has prepared for
the 23.4 saints and for the children who love him,' I your nourisher giving
myself as bread, of which no one who has tasted still takes a trial of death,
and giving a drink of immortality day by day; I a teacher of superheavenly
lessons; for you I contended toward death and I paid out your death, which you
owed upon the things previously 23.5 sinned and the unbelief toward God."
Having heard these words from either side, judge for yourself and bring up the
vote for your own salvation; and if a brother says similar things, and if a
child, and if a wife, and if anyone at all, let Christ who conquers be in you
before all; for he fights for you.

24.1 Can you also be superior to the possessions? Speak, and Christ does not
lead you away from the possession, the Lord does not envy. But do you see
yourself being defeated by them and being overturned? Leave, throw. 24.2 Hate,
renounce, flee; "and if your right eye causes you to stumble, quickly cut it
out"; it is more choiceworthy with one eye to have the kingdom of God than
with the whole to have the fire; and if a hand and if a foot and if the soul,
hate it. For if it perishes here for Christ, it will be saved there. 25.1 And
the thing that follows also holds of this same judgment. "But now in this time
to have fields and possessions and houses and 25.2 brothers with persecutions,
unto what?" For he does not call to the life those without possessions nor
those without a hearth nor those without brothers, since he has also called
rich men, but in the way we have said before, and brothers according to the
same, as Peter with Andrew and James with John, the children of 25.3 Zebedee,
but being of one mind with one another and with Christ. And the "with
persecutions" he rejects as a way of having each of these; and one persecution
comes from outside of the men who drive the faithful either through enmity or
through envy or through love of gain or according to a diabolic working; 25.4
and the hardest is an inward persecution, sent forth from the soul itself of
each, being outraged by godless desires and various pleasures and cheap hopes
and corrupting dreamings, whenever, always reaching after the more and raging
under wild loves and burning, as with goads or gadflies by the passions lying
upon it, it is made to bleed toward mad eagernesses and despair of life 25.5
and contempt of God. This persecution is heavier and harder, setting out from
within, always being with one, which the one pursued cannot even flee 25.6;
for he carries the enemy about in himself everywhere. So also a burning that
falls from outside works a testing, but the one from within accomplishes
death. And a war brought on is easily 25.7 dissolved, but the one in the soul
is measured out as far as death. If with such a persecution you have the
sensible wealth, and if brothers according to blood and the other pledges,
leave the all-possession of these that is unto harm, provide peace for
yourself, be freed from a long persecution, turn toward the gospel from those,
choose the Savior before all, the advocate and comforter of your soul, 25.8
the president of the unbounded life. "For the things seen are temporary, but
the things not seen are eternal"; and in the present time short-lived and
unstable, "but in that which is coming there is eternal life." 26.1 "The first
shall be last and the last first." This is many-meaninged according to the
suspicion and the clarification, yet it does not demand the inquiry in the
present at least; for it does not only incline toward those of many
properties, but simply toward all men who once for all give themselves by
faith. So let this be reserved 26.2 for now. But the thing set before us I
think has been shown in nothing more deficient than the promise, that the
Savior has in no way shut the rich out, according to the wealth itself at
least and the wrapping of the possession, nor has he trenched salvation off
from them, if at least they should be able and willing to bow to the
commandments of God and should honor their own life before the temporary
things and should look toward the Lord with an intent gaze, just as having
looked toward the nod of a good helmsman, what he wishes, what he commands,
what he signals, what sign he gives to his own 26.3 sailors, where and from
where he promises the anchorage. For what injustice does anyone do, if,
applying his judgment and being sparing, he collected a sufficient living
before the faith? Or, even more than this, a thing without blame, if
straightway by God who distributes the soul he was settled into a house of
such

men and a wide-spread family, strong in the possessions 26.4 and ruling by the
wealth? For if through the involuntary birth in wealth he has been driven from
life, he is rather done injustice by God who generated him, having been deemed
worthy of a temporary sweet-suffering, but having been deprived of everlasting
26.5 life. And why at all was it necessary that wealth ever be raised from the
earth, if it is a supplier and 26.6 sponsor of death? But if someone is able
to turn further inward than the belongings of the authority and to think
moderate things and to be temperate and to seek God only and to breathe God
and to live as citizen with God, this one has presented himself poor to the
commandments, free, unconquered, without disease, 26.7 unwounded by
possessions; but if not, a camel will enter through a needle more quickly than
such a rich man will pass 26.8 unto the kingdom of God. Let the camel then
also signify something higher, arriving through a strait and compressed way at
the rich man, which it is possible to learn in the exposition concerning
beginnings and theology as a mystery of the Savior; 27.1 nevertheless let at
least the apparent thing first, and that on account of which the parable has
been said, be provided. Let it teach those well-resourced that they must not
neglect their own salvation as already condemned beforehand, nor again drown
the wealth nor condemn it as a plotter and enemy of life, but must learn in
what way and how one must use wealth 27.2 and acquire the life. For since
neither does anyone perish from every side, fearing because he is rich, nor is
he saved from every side, being confident and believing that he will be saved,
come, we must consider what hope the Savior sketches for them and how the
unhoped-for may become a surety, and the thing hoped for may arrive at
possession. 27.3 The teacher then says, having been asked what is the greatest
of the commandments: "You shall love the Lord your God from your whole soul
and from your whole power", that no commandment is greater than this, and 27.4
very reasonably. For it has been commanded both concerning the first and
concerning the greatest, of God himself our Father, through whom both all
things have come to be and 27.5 are and unto whom the things being saved
return again. Having then been loved first by this one and having obtained the
coming-to-be, it is not holy to lead anything else as more senior and more
honorable, paying back this grace only, a small one upon greatest things, and
having nothing else whatever to invent for repayment to a God without need and
perfect, but by the very loving of the Father bringing incorruption unto their
own strength and power. For as much as one loves God, by so much also he
steals further inward into God. 28.1 And he says that a second in order and in
nothing smaller than this is: "You shall love your neighbor as yourself";
therefore God above 28.2 yourself. And when the one conversing asked "who is
neighbor?", he did not mark off beforehand in the same way as the Jews the one
of blood, nor the citizen, nor the proselyte, nor the one similarly 28.3
circumcised, nor the one using one and the same law; but he leads by the word
someone going down from above from Jerusalem into Jericho and shows this one
stabbed together by robbers, thrown half-dead upon the road, passed by by a
priest, overlooked by a Levite, but pitied by the Samaritan, the one
reproached and set apart, who did not pass by according to chance as those
did, but came packed with the things the one in danger needed, wine, oil,
bandages, a beast, a wage for the innkeeper, the one already being given, the
other promised besides. 28.4 "Who", he said, "of these has become neighbor to
the one who suffered the terrible things?" And when he answered that "the one
who displayed the mercy toward him; you then also go and do so", as of the
love sprouting good-doing. 29.1 In both commandments then he introduces love,
but he has divided it by order, and where he kindles the first-prizes of love
to 29.2 God, and where he

distributes the second-prizes to the neighbor. And who else would this one be
except the Savior himself? Or who has pitied us more than that one, us who
have been all but put to death by the world-rulers of the darkness with the
many wounds, fears, desires, angers, griefs, deceits, 29.3 pleasures? And of
these wounds the only physician is Jesus, cutting out the passions altogether
by the roots, not as the law the bare results, the fruits of the wicked
plants, but bringing his own axe 29.4 toward the roots of vice. This is he who
poured out the wine, the blood of the vine of David, upon our wounded souls,
this is he who brought and lavished the oil, the mercy from the bowels of the
Father, this is he who showed the unbreakable bonds of health and salvation,
love, faith, hope, this is he who commanded angels and rulers and authorities
to minister to us upon a great wage, because they themselves also will be
freed from the vanity of the world at the 29.5 revelation of the glory of the
sons of God. This one then one must love equally with God. And he loves Christ
Jesus who does his will 29.6 and keeps his commandments. "For not everyone who
says to me 'Lord Lord' will enter into the kingdom of the heavens, but he who
does the will of my Father." And: "Why do you call me 'Lord Lord' and do not
do what I say?" And: "You are blessed who see and hear what neither righteous
men nor prophets, if you do what I say." 30.1 First then is this one who loves
Christ, and second he who honors and treats those who have believed in that
one. For whatever someone works unto a disciple, this the Lord receives unto
himself and makes 30.2 everything his own. "Come, you blessed of my Father,
inherit the kingdom prepared for you from the foundation of the world. For I
hungered and you gave me to eat, and I thirsted and you gave me to drink, and
I was a stranger and you brought me in, I was naked and you clothed me, I was
sick and you 30.3 visited me, I was in prison and you came to me. Then the
righteous will answer him saying: Lord, when did we see you hungering and feed
you, or thirsting and give you drink? And when did we see you a stranger and
bring you in, or naked and wrap you? Or when did we see you sick 30.4 and
visit you? Or in prison and come to you? Answering, the king will say to them:
Amen I say to you, inasmuch as you did it to one of 30.5 these my brothers,
the least, you did it to me." Again from the contrary he throws those who have
not provided these things to them into the eternal 30.6 fire, as not having
provided them to him. And elsewhere: "He who receives you receives me; he who
does not receive you sets me aside." 31.1 These he also names children and
little children and infants and friends and small here as toward their future
magnitude above. "Do not despise", saying, "one of these little ones; for
their angels always see the face of my Father who is in 31.2 the heavens." And
in another place: "Do not fear, little flock; for the Father has been well
pleased to hand over to you the kingdom" of the heavens. 31.3 According to the
same things he also says that the least in the kingdom of the heavens, that is
his own disciple, is greater than John, the greatest among those born of 31.4
women. And again: "He who receives a righteous man or a prophet unto the name
of a righteous man or a prophet will receive their wage, and he who has given
a disciple a cup of cold water to drink unto the name of a disciple will not
lose the wage." Therefore this wage alone is the one that does not 31.5
perish. And again: "Make for yourselves friends from the mammon of 31.6
unrighteousness, in order that when it fails, they may receive you into the
eternal tents." Declaring by nature every possession which someone has
acquired by himself as being his own and does not put down unto the common for
those in need, to be unjust, and from this injustice it being possible also to
work a

just and saving deed, to give rest to one of those who have an eternal tent
beside the Father. 31.7 See first that he has not commanded you to be demanded
nor to wait to be troubled, but yourself to seek those who will be well
treated, being also worthy 31.8 disciples of the Savior. Fair then also is the
word of the apostle: "for God loves a cheerful giver", one rejoicing in the
giving and not sowing sparingly, in order that he may not so also reap,
sharing without murmurings and distinction and grief, which is a pure 31.9
beneficence. But better than this is that of the Lord spoken in another place:
"Give to everyone who asks you"; for such a love of giving is really of God.
But this word is above all divinity, not to wait even to be asked, but
yourself to search out whoever is worthy to be well treated, 32.1 then to
define so great a wage of the communion, an eternal tent. O fair merchandise,
O divine market; someone buys incorruption with possessions, and giving the
things of the world that perish he receives in exchange an eternal dwelling of
these in the 32.2 heavens. Sail to this gathering, if you are temperate, O
rich man, and if you must, go round the whole earth, do not spare dangers and
32.3 toils, in order that here you may buy a heavenly kingdom. Why do
transparent stones and emeralds gladden you so much, and a house, food of fire
32.4 or a plaything of time or a by-work of an earthquake or an outrage of a
tyrant? Desire to dwell in the heavens and to reign with God; this kingdom a
man will give you, imitating God; here having received small things 32.5 he
will there through whole ages make you a housemate. Supplicate that he may
receive; hasten, be in agony, fear lest he dishonor you; for he has not been
32.6 commanded to receive, but you to provide. Nor indeed did the Lord say:
"give" or "provide" or "benefit" or "help", but "make a friend"; and the
friend does not come to be from one giving, but from a whole rest and a long
being-together; for neither the faith nor the love nor the endurance is of one
day, but "he who has endured unto the end, this one will be saved". 33.1 How
then does the man give these things? Because through the honor and goodwill
and appropriation of that one the Lord gives; "for I will give not only to the
33.2 friends, but also to the friends of the friends." And who is this, the
friend of God? Do you not judge who is worthy and who unworthy; for it is
possible that you miss concerning the opinion; and as in an ambiguous case of
ignorance it is better even to do well to the unworthy on account of the
worthy than, guarding against 33.3 those less good, not even to fall in with
the serious. For from the sparing and pretending to test those who will
reasonably or not obtain, it is possible that you also neglect some of those
dear to God, whose penalty is a fiery eternal punishment; but from the putting
forth to all in need in succession it is necessary altogether to find also one
of those able to save with God 33.4. "Do not judge" then, "in order that you
may not be judged; with what measure you measure, with this it will also be
measured back to you; a fair measure, pressed and 33.5 shaken, overflowing,
will be given back to you." Open the bowels to all those enrolled as disciples
of God, not looking toward a body disdainfully, not being disposed carelessly
toward age, nor if someone appears without property or ill-clad or ill-formed
or weak, at 33.6 this be displeased in the soul and turn away. This is a form
wrapped around us from outside on the pretext of the passage into the world,
in order that we may be able to enter into this common training-place; but
within, hidden, dwells the Father and his child who died for us and rose with
us. 34.1 This form that is seen deceives death and the devil; for the wealth
within and the beauty is unseen to them; and they rage about the little flesh,
which they despise as weak, being blind of the properties within, not knowing
how great a "treasure in an earthen vessel" we carry, walled round by the
power of God the Father and by the blood of the child of God and by the dew of
the Holy Spirit.

been deemed worthy of the great redemption, but contrary to the other men
collect for yourself an army without weapons, without war, without blood,
without anger, unstained, old men god-fearing, orphans dear to God, widows
armed with 34.3 meekness, men adorned with love. Acquire such bodyguards with
your wealth and for the body and for the soul, whose general is God, through
whom even a ship being dipped is lightened, steered by holy prayers alone, and
a disease at its height is tamed, driven out by layings-on of hands, and an
attack of robbers is disarmed, stripped by pious prayers, and a violence of
daemons is shattered, convicted by intense commands. 35.1 All these soldiers
are active and sure guards, no one idle, no one useless. One can ask you off
from God, another comfort you when you toil, another weep and groan
compassionately for you toward the Lord of all, another teach something of the
things useful toward salvation, another admonish with frankness, another
advise with goodwill, and all love truly, without deceit, without fear, 35.2
without hypocrisy, without flattery, without pretence. O sweet attendance of
those who love, O blessed ministries of those who are confident, O sincere
faith of those who fear God only, O truth of words among those unable to lie,
O beauty of works among those persuaded to minister to God, to persuade God,
to please God; they do not seem to touch your flesh, but each his own soul,
not to speak to a brother, but to the king of the ages dwelling in you. 36.1
All the faithful then are fair and God-fitting and worthy of the appellation
which they wear about as a diadem. Nevertheless there are already also some
more elect of the elect, and by so much more as they are less notable, in a
certain way hauling themselves out of the swell of the world and bringing
themselves back onto the safe, not wishing to seem holy, and if someone says
it, being ashamed, hiding in a depth of judgment the unspeakable mysteries,
and being too proud for their own nobility to be seen in the world, whom the
word calls "light of the world" and "salt of the 36.2 earth". This is the
seed, image and likeness of God, and his genuine child and heir, sent here as
upon some sojourn by a great economy and analogy of the 36.3 Father; on
account of which both the apparent and the unapparent things of the world have
been created, some unto slavery, some unto exercise, some unto learning for
it, and all things, as long as the seed remains here, are held together, and
when it has been gathered together all things will be dissolved most quickly.

look upon the bosom of the Father, which the only-begotten God alone has
expounded. 37.2 And God himself also is love, and through love he was seen by
us. And his ineffable is Father, and what has become sympathetic toward us has
become Mother. The Father, having loved, was made feminine, and a great sign
of this is he whom he himself 37.3 generated from himself; and the fruit born
from love is love. For this reason he himself also came down, for this reason
he put on man, for this reason he willingly suffered the things of men, in
order that, having been measured toward our weakness, those whom 37.4 he
loved, he might measure us back toward his own power. And being about to be
poured as a libation and giving himself as a ransom he leaves us a new
covenant: "I give you my love." And what is this and how great? For each of us
he laid down the soul, the one matching 37.5 the whole; this he asks back from
us for one another. And if we owe the souls to the brothers, and have
confessed such a compact toward the Savior in return, shall we still shut up
the things of the world, the poor and alien and flowing past, storing them?

Shall we shut 37.6 one another off from the things which after a little the
fire will have? Divinely at least and with inspiration John says "he who does
not love the brother is a man-killer", a seed of Cain, a nursling of the

the brother; it is fluttered about this one, about this one it is temperately
mad. 38.2 "Love covers a multitude of sins; perfect love casts out the fear;
it does not vaunt, it is not puffed up, it does not rejoice at injustice, but
rejoices with the truth; it bears all things, believes all things, hopes all
things, endures all things. Love never falls away, prophecies are abolished,
tongues cease, healings are left behind upon earth. And these three remain,
faith, hope, love; and greater among these is love." 38.3 And justly; for
faith goes away whenever we are persuaded by seeing God with our own eyes. And
hope vanishes when the things hoped for have been given back, but love comes
together unto fullness and rather grows when the perfect things 38.4 have been
handed over. If someone throws this into the soul, he can, even if he has come
to be in sins, even if he has worked many of the things forbidden, having
increased the love and having taken a pure repentance, 38.5 fight back the
things stumbled. For let not this either have been left to you unto despair
and mindlessness, if you also learn who is the rich man 39.1 who has no room
in the heavens and in what way, using the things that are, one might flee
through both the ill-repute of wealth and the thing hard toward life and be
able to enjoy the eternal good things, and he should have happened, either
through ignorance or through weakness or an involuntary circumstance, after
the seal and the redemption, to fall in with some sins or trespasses, so as to
have been carried under completely, that this one has been condemned 39.2
altogether by God. For to everyone who has turned toward God with truth from
the whole heart the doors have been opened, and a Father thrice-glad receives
a son truly repenting; and the true repentance is no longer to be liable to
the same things, but to root out altogether from the soul the sins upon which
he has condemned himself to death; for when these 39.3 have been taken away
God will again be housed into you. For he says there is a great and
unsurpassable joy and feast in the heavens for the Father and the angels at
one sinner who has turned and repented. 39.4 Wherefore he has also cried: "I
wish mercy and not sacrifice; I do not wish the death of the sinner, but the
repentance; and if your sins are as scarlet wool, I will whiten them as snow,
and if blacker than 39.5 the darkness, having washed I will make them as white
wool." For to God alone is it possible to provide forgiveness of sins and not
"to reckon trespasses". Since indeed the Lord also commands us each day to
forgive 39.6 the brothers who repent. And if we being wicked know how to give
good gifts, how much more "the Father of mercies", the good Father "of all
comfort", the much-bowelled and much-merciful, is of a nature to be
long-suffering; he waits for those who have turned; and to turn is really to
cease from the sins and no longer to look to the things behind. 40.1 Of the
things that have come to be before then God gives forgiveness, but of the
things coming on each himself to himself. And this is to change the mind: to
condemn the things gone by and to ask amnesty of these from a Father, who
alone of all is able to make the things done undone, wiping out by the mercy
from himself and by the dew of spirit the 40.2 things previously sinned. "For
upon what things I find you", he says, "upon these 40.3 I will also judge";
and at each thing the end of all cries; so that even for him who has done the
greatest things well

according to the life, but at the end has run aground toward vice, all the
former toils are useless, when he has become prize-less at the catastrophe of
the drama, and for him who has lived worse and in a dragged way before it is
possible later, having repented, to conquer a long-time wicked citizenship by
the time after the repentance; 40.4 and there is need of much exactness, just
as for bodies that have toiled with a long disease 40.5 there is need of a
regimen and of more attention. The thief, do you wish to receive forgiveness?
Steal no longer; he who has committed adultery, let him no longer be burned;

swear no longer; and cut short the other passions, anger, desire, grief, fear,
in order that you may be found at the 40.6 exit to have been already dissolved
toward the adversary here. It is then perhaps impossible to cut off at a heap
passions grown with one, but with the power of God and human supplication and
help of brothers and sincere repentance and continuous practice it is
accomplished. 41.1 Wherefore you, the solemn and powerful and rich, must
altogether set over yourself some man of God just as a trainer and helmsman.

frankly and astringes and at the same time heals. For it is not beneficial for
the eyes to remain unchastened the whole time, but 41.3 also to weep and to be
bitten at some time for the sake of the greater health. So also for a soul
nothing is more destructive than continuous pleasure; for it is blinded from
the melting, if it remains unmoved by the word that speaks frankly. 41.4 Fear
this one also when he has been angered, and when he has sighed † be grieved,
and when he stops anger, respect him, and when he asks off punishment,
anticipate. 41.5 Let this one keep awake many nights for you, ambassadoring
for you toward God and by accustomed entreaties charming the Father; 41.6 for

myth not a myth, but a being word handed down concerning John the apostle and
guarded by memory 42.2. For when the tyrant had died he passed from the island
of Patmos to Ephesus, he went away, being called, also to the neighboring
places of the nations, where he would set down bishops, and where he would fit
whole churches, and where he would allot some one clergy 42.3 of those
signified by the spirit. Having come then also upon one of the cities not far,
whose name also some tell, and having rested the brothers in the other things,
looking upon the established bishop at all, seeing a young man sufficient in
body and graceful in look and warm in soul, "this one", he said, "I deposit
with you with all eagerness, upon the church and upon Christ as witness". And
as he received and promised all things, he again stretched the same things
42.4 and bore witness. Then the one departed to Ephesus, and the elder taking
the young man handed over into his house nourished him, held him, warmed him,
at last enlightened him; and after this he let go of the more care and
guarding, as having set over him the perfect phylactery 42.5, the seal of the
Lord. And to him who had taken a letting-go before the hour some idle and
broken age-mates, habituated to evils, attach themselves corruptingly; and
first through costly feastings they lead him under, then somewhere also

going out at night upon clothes-stealing they take him along, then they
thought it right that he also 42.6 work together something greater. And he by
little became accustomed, and through greatness of nature, having stood out
like a mouthless and sturdy horse from the straight road and having bitten the
bit, was carried more greatly down the 42.7 ravines. And having despaired
completely of the salvation in God he no longer planned anything small, but
having done some great thing, since he had once perished, he thought it right
to suffer equal things with the others. Taking these very ones then and
putting together a robber-band, he was a ready robber-chief, most violent,
most blood-stained, hardest. 42.8 Time in the middle, and some need having
fallen they call John back. And he, when he had settled the other things for
the sake of which he had come, "come then", he said, "O bishop, give back to
us the deposit which I and Christ deposited with you upon the church over
which you sit, as witness." 42.9 And he at first was struck, thinking he was
being slandered about money, which he had not received, and he could neither
believe concerning things he did not have nor disbelieve John; but when he
said "I demand the young man and the soul of the brother", the elder groaning
from below and also weeping a little besides, "that one", he said, "has died".
"How and what death?" "He has died to God", he said; "for he went away wicked
and abandoned and, the chief point, a robber, and now instead of the church he
has occupied the mountain with a similar =42.10= military band." The apostle
having torn his garment and with a great wail having struck his head, "a fine
guardian of the brother's soul at least", he said, "I left; but let a horse
already be present for me and let someone become a leader of the road for me."
He was driving, as he was, from there =42.11= from the church. And having come
to the place he is caught by the advance-guard of the robbers, neither fleeing
nor begging off, but =42.12= shouting: "I have come for this, lead me to your
ruler." Who until then, as he was armed, was waiting; but when he recognized
John approaching, being ashamed he turned to flight. And he pursued with all
his might, =42.13= having forgotten his own age, crying: "Why do you flee me,
child, your own father, the naked, the old man? Pity me, child, do not fear;
you still have hopes of life; I will give a word to Christ for you; and if it
is needed, I will willingly undergo your death, as the Lord that for us; for
you I will give my soul in exchange. Stand, believe =42.14=; Christ has sent
me." And he having heard first stood looking down, then he threw the weapons,
then trembling he was weeping bitterly; and he embraced the old man who had
come forward, defending himself with wails as he was able and being baptized
with the tears a second time, hiding only 42.15 the right hand. And he,
guaranteeing, swearing that forgiveness has been found for him from the
Savior, asking, kneeling, kissing the right hand itself as having been
cleansed by the repentance, led him back to the church, and asking him off
with lavish prayers, and fighting together with continuous fasts, and charming
his judgment with various sirens of words, he did not go away before, as they
say, he set him over the church, giving a great example of true repentance and
a great token of regeneration, a trophy of a resurrection being seen. =42.16=
