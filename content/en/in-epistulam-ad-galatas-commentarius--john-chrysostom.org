serves our present life, the life that they themselves call evil, by feeding

of yours introduce so great a ministry toward the constitution of an evil
life? But neither are the stars gods, God forbid, but works of God made for
our need, nor is the world evil. And if you mention to me murderers and
adulterers and tomb-robbers, these have nothing to do with the present life;
for they are not sins of life in the flesh, but of a corrupted choice. For if
these belonged to the present life, as allotted to it in every case, no one
would be free or clean. See then the properties of life in the flesh, which no
one can escape. And what are they? Eating, drinking, sleeping, growing,
hungering, thirsting, being born, dying, and all things like these. And of
these no one would get the better, neither sinner nor righteous, neither king
nor private person, but we are all subject to the necessity of nature. So
also, if doing what is base were allotted to the nature of life, no one would
have escaped those things either, just as no one escapes those others. Do not
tell me that those who succeed are few. For you will never find anyone who has
got the better of natural necessities. So that as long as even one is found
who has accomplished virtue, the argument will take no harm. What do you say,
wretched and miserable man? Is the present life evil, in which we have come to
know God, in which we philosophize about the things to come, in which we have
become angels from men, and dance with the powers above? And what other proof
shall we seek of your evil and distorted mind?


5. Why then, he says, did Paul call the present age an evil age? By using the
common custom. For we too are accustomed to say, I had a bad day, not blaming
the time, but the deed or the circumstance. So also Paul, blaming evil
choices, has used the custom; and he shows that Christ both set us free from
former sins and made us secure for the future. For by saying, Who gave himself
for our sins, he made that clear; and by adding, That he might deliver us from
this present evil age, he indicated the security toward the future. For the
law was weak even toward the one, but grace has become strong toward both.
According to the will of our God and Father. For since those people thought
they were disobeying God, as the one who had given the law, and were afraid,
if they left the Old and came to the New; he also corrects this suspicion of
theirs, saying that these things are pleasing to the Father as well. And he
did not simply say, Father, but, Our Father, and he sets it down continually,
putting them to shame by showing that Christ has made his own Father our
Father. To whom be the glory unto the ages. Amen. And this too is new and
unusual. =61.620= For we nowhere find Amen lying at the beginning and in the
prefaces of an Epistle, but after many things. Here, however, showing that
what had been said already contained a sufficient accusation of the Galatians,
and that the argument is complete, he has set this preface. For the obvious
among charges needs no long construction. For having recalled the cross and
the resurrection, the ransom from sins, the security toward the future, the
Father's mind, the Son's will, the grace, the peace, all his gift, he closed
the argument in a doxology. And he has done this not only for that reason, but
also because he is struck beyond measure by the greatness of the gift, the
excess of the grace, and considering what we were and what God has worked all
at once and in an instant of time; which, unable to set forth in speech, he
ended in a doxology, sending up not the praise that is due, but the praise
possible to him, on behalf of the whole world. For this reason he also uses
the speech that follows more vehemently, as if strongly kindled by the thought
of God's benefits. For having said, To whom be the glory unto the ages, amen,
he begins from a more vehement rebuke, speaking thus: I marvel that you are so
quickly transferring yourselves from him who called you in the grace of
Christ, unto another gospel. For since they suspected that they were pleasing
the Father by the keeping of the law, just as the Jews also did when they
persecuted Christ, he shows this first, that by doing this they provoke not
only Christ, but the Father as well. For it is not from Christ, he says, but
also from the Father that you are moving away by doing this. For just as the
Old is not of the Father only, but also of the Son, so also the grace is not
of the Son only, but also of the Father, and all things are common; For all
things that are mine are yours. Having said then that they are standing off
from the Father as well, he sets down two charges, both the transfer and the
very swift transfer. And yet the contrary is worthy of accusation, standing
off after a long time; but here the argument is about deceit. For the one who
stands off after a long time is also worthy of charges; but the one who has
fallen from the first assault, and in the very skirmishes, has shown an
example of extreme weakness, which he also charges against these, saying: What
is this, that those who deceive you do not even need time, but the first
approach is enough to overturn and take you all? And what pardon could you
have? For if this, when it happens among friends, is a charge, and the one who
has left former and useful companions is worthy of condemnation, the one who


leaps away from God who is calling, consider of how great a punishment he
would be liable. And when he says, I marvel, he is not only putting them to
shame by saying that after so great a gift, after so great a forgiveness of
sins and excess of lovingkindness, they have deserted to a yoke of slavery;
but at the same time he also shows what sort of opinion =61.621= he has about
them, that it is some great and earnest one.
For he would not, if he thought them people of no account and easily deceived,
have marveled at what had happened, but since you are among the genuine, he
says, and those who have toiled much, for this reason I marvel; which was
enough to recover them and lead them back to the former things. Which he also
made clear in the middle of the Epistle, saying: Did you suffer so many things
in vain, if indeed it is even in vain? You are transferring yourselves. He did
not say, you transferred yourselves, but, You are transferring yourselves,
that is, I do not yet believe, nor do I think the deceit is complete; which
again belongs to one who is recovering them. Having said this more clearly
beforehand, he says, I have confidence in you, that you will think nothing
else. From him who called you in the grace of Christ. The calling is of the
Father, but the cause of the calling is of the Son; for he is the one who
reconciled and gave the gift; for we were not saved from works of
righteousness. Or rather both these things are of that one, and those of this
one; For what is mine is yours, he says, and what is yours is mine. And he did
not say, You are transferring yourselves from the gospel, but, From God who
called you. For he has set down what was more dreadful and more able to strike
them. For those who wished to deceive them did not do this all at once, but,
quietly moving them away from the facts, they did not move them away from the
names. For such is the deceit of the devil, not to set the snares bare. For if
they had said, Stand off from Christ, they would have guarded against them as
deceivers and destroyers; but now, leaving them in the faith for the time, and
putting the name of gospel on the deceit, they undermined the building with
much license, the things said, like certain curtains, hiding the housebreakers
through these names.
6. Since then they called their own deceit a gospel, he himself also fights
well even against the name, and speaks freely, saying: Unto another gospel,
which is not another. Well said; for it is not another. But nevertheless what
the sick suffer, being harmed by healthy foods, this the Marcionites also
suffered here. For he seized on the words, saying that Behold, Paul also said,
There is not another gospel. For they do not accept all the evangelists, but
one only, and him too cut and confused as they wished. What then, when he
himself says, According to my Gospel and the preaching of Jesus Christ? What
they have said is indeed matter for much laughter; yet even if it is
laughable, it is necessary to refute it because of those who are easily
deceived. What then shall we say? That even if ten thousand write Gospels, but
write the same things, the many are one, and nothing from the multitude of
writers will harm their being one; just as, again, even if there is one
writer, and he says contrary things, what is written is not one. =61.622= For
the one and the not-one is judged not by the number of writers, but by the
identity and otherness of what is said. From which it is clear that the
Gospels of four are also one Gospel. For when the four say the same things, it
is not another and another because of the difference of persons, but one
because of the agreement of what is said. For Paul is not speaking now about
number, but about disagreement of the things said. If then that of Matthew is
another, and that of Luke different according to the force of what is written
and the accuracy of the doctrines, they seize on the argument well; but if it
is one and the same, let them cease being foolish, and pretending not to know
things that even to very


young children are obvious.
Except there are some who trouble you, and wish to overturn the Gospel of
Christ. That is, As long as you are sound in mind, you will not recognize
another gospel, as long as you look straight and not distorted, imagining
things that are not. For just as the eye when disturbed sees some things in
place of others; so also a mind muddied by the confusion of evil reasonings is
accustomed to suffer this same thing. For this is why the mad also imagine
some things in place of others. But this madness is more grievous than that,
working the harm not in the things of sense, but in the things of the mind,
preparing the overthrow not in the pupil of the body's eyes, but in the eyes
of the understanding. And wishing to overturn the Gospel of Christ. And yet
they were introducing only one, or a second, commandment, innovating only that
of circumcision and that of the days; but showing that a little alteration
ruins the whole, he said that the Gospel is overturned. For just as in royal
coins the one who has cut away a little of the stamp has made the whole coin
counterfeit; so also the one who has overturned even the least of the sound
faith ruins the whole, going on from the beginning to what is worse. Where
then are those who judge us of contentiousness because of the separation from
the heretics? Where now are those who say there is no difference between us
and them, but that the difference comes from love of rule? Let them hear what
Paul says, that those who innovate even a little have overturned the Gospel.
But these have not done some little thing; for how could they, who say that
the Son of God is a creature? Did you not hear that in the Old as well,
someone gathering sticks on the sabbath, and transgressing one commandment,
and not even this the greatest, paid the last penalty? and Uzzah, having
steadied the ark when it was about to overturn, died at once, because he
touched a ministry that did not belong to him? Then the transgression of a
sabbath, and the touching of the ark as it was falling, brought God to such
indignation that those who dared these things obtained not even a brief
pardon; and the one who has ruined doctrines that are fearful and ineffable
=61.623= , will this one obtain a defense and pardon? It is not so, it is not.
But this very thing is the cause of all the evils, not being indignant even
over these small things. For this reason the greater sins have come in,
because the lesser do not obtain the fitting correction. And just as in bodies
those who have despised wounds have generated fevers and putrefactions and
death; so also in souls those who overlook the small introduce the greater.
So-and-so, he says, fails concerning the fast, and it is nothing great;
another is sound in the right faith, but by playing a part because of the time
has betrayed his frankness; nor is this some great horror. Another, provoked,
has threatened that he will stand off from the right faith; but this too is
not worthy of punishment; for he sinned in anger, he says, and in wrath. And
one would find ten thousand such

laughable both to Jews and to Greeks, the Church being split into ten thousand
pieces. For if those who from the beginning attempt to leap away from the
divine statutes and to move even a little had obtained the fitting rebuke, the
present plague would not have been born, and so great a storm would not have

the Gospel.
7. But now there are many among us who also fast on the same day as the Jews,
and keep sabbaths likewise; and we bear it nobly, or rather wretchedly. And
why


do I speak of the things of the Jews? For many customs of the Greeks also are
kept among some of our people, omens and bird-watchings and tokens and
observations of days, and zeal about nativity, and the documents full of every
impiety, which they compose at once when children are born, to the harm of
their own head, teaching them from the start to undo the labors for virtue,
and leading their own part under the deceived tyranny of fate. And if Christ
profits those who are circumcised nothing, those who drag in so many evils,
how much will the faith profit them for salvation anymore? And yet
circumcision had been given by God, but nevertheless, since it was ruining the
Gospel when performed out of season, Paul did everything so as to cut it away.
Then Paul made so great a zeal over Jewish customs, because they were kept out
of season, and shall we not cut away even the Greek custom? And what defense
should we have? For these reasons our affairs are now in disturbances and
uproars, and the disciples, filled with much pride, have overturned the order,
and the things above have become below. And if someone makes even a small
charge, they spit on the rulers, because we have trained them badly. And yet
even if those who preside were more wicked, and full of ten thousand evils,
not even so was it lawful for the disciple to disobey. For if he says
concerning the Jewish teachers that, since they sat on Moses' seat, they would
be right to be heard by those being taught =61.624= , and yet they had works
so evil that he commanded the disciples not to emulate or imitate those
things, of what pardon would those be worthy who spit on and trample the
presidents of the Church who live with gentleness by the grace of God? For if
it is not lawful to judge one another, much more the teachers. But even if I,
or an angel from heaven, preach a gospel to you contrary to what you received,
let him be anathema.
See apostolic understanding. For lest anyone say that he is supporting his own
doctrines for the sake of vainglory, he even anathematized himself. And since
they were also taking refuge in dignities, James and John, for this reason he
mentioned angels as well. Do not tell me James, he says, and John; for even if
one of the first angels from heaven is corrupting the preaching, let him be
anathema. And he did not simply say, From heaven, but since the priests also
were called angels; For the lips of a priest shall keep knowledge, and they
shall seek the law from his mouth, because he is an angel of the Lord
Almighty; lest you think these are the angels spoken of now, by the addition
of heaven he hinted at the powers above. And he did not say, If they announce
contrary things, or overturn the whole, but, Even if they preach a gospel even
a little contrary to what we preached, even if they move the slightest thing,
let them be anathema. As I have said before, I also say now again. For lest
you think the words are of wrath, or spoken hyperbolically, or by some
snatching, he sets down the same things a second time. For the one led to say
something in wrath might quickly repent; but the one who says the same things
a second time shows that he spoke having judged so, and having first ratified
it in his mind, thus brought forth what was said. Abraham then, being asked to
send Lazarus, said, They have Moses and the prophets; if they do not hear
them, they will not hear even the dead rising. And Christ introduces him
saying these things, showing that he wishes the Scriptures to be more
trustworthy even than the dead being raised. And Paul (and when I say Paul, I
say Christ again; for he was the one moving his soul) prefers them even to
angels coming down from heaven; and very reasonably. For the angels, even if
they are great, are nevertheless servants and ministers; but all the
Scriptures were written and sent not from servants, but from God the Master of
all. For


this reason he says: If anyone preaches a gospel to you contrary to what we
preached to you. And he did not say, So-and-so and so-and-so, very wisely and
without offense. For what need was there anymore to mention names, when he had
used such hyperbole as to include all, both those above and those below? For
by anathematizing the evangelists and angels he marked off every dignity; and
by anathematizing himself, every intimacy and genuineness. Do not tell me that
Your fellow apostles and companions say these things; for I do not spare even
myself if I preach such things. And he says these things =61.625= not as
condemning the apostles, nor as if they were transgressing the preaching, far
from it; For whether we or they, he says, so we preach; but wishing to show
that he does not admit the dignity of persons when the argument is about
truth. For am I now persuading men, or God? or do I seek to please men? For if
I were still pleasing men, I would not be a servant of Christ. For even if I
were deceiving you, he says, by saying these things, can I cheat God, who
knows the secrets of the mind, and whom it is my whole zeal always to please?
Did you see apostolic mind? did you see evangelical height? And writing these
same things to the Corinthians as well he said, We are not making a defense to
you, but giving you an occasion of boasting; and again, But to me it is a very
small thing that I should be examined by you, or by a human day. For since,
being a teacher to disciples, he is forced to make a defense, he endures it,
but is impatient, not from arrogance, God forbid, but because of the ease of
the mind of those being deceived, and because he is not strongly believed
among them. Therefore he has also said these things, all but saying: Is my
argument toward you? are men about to judge me? our argument is toward God,
and we do everything with a view to the accounts there, and we would not have
come to such misery as, when we are about to make a defense to the Master of
all for what we preach, to corrupt the doctrines.
8. So that, making a defense and at the same time rising against them, he has
set these things down. For those being taught ought not to judge the teachers,
but to believe; but since the order has been reversed, and you sit as judges,
he says, learn that I have not much concern for a defense toward you, but we
do the whole for God, and so as to make a defense to him about these
doctrines. For the one who wishes to persuade men does many underhanded and
distorted things, and uses deceit and falsehood, so as to persuade and take
the mind of the hearers; but the one who is persuading God, and zealous to
please him, needs a simple and pure mind; for the Divine is not to be
deceived. From which it is clear that we too, he says, write these things not
for love of rule, nor so as to have disciples, nor desiring glory from you;
for we have not been eager to please men, but God. For if I had wished to
please men, I would still be with the Jews, I would still be persecuting the
Church. And the one who has despised a whole nation and household and friends
and kinsmen and so great glory, and has exchanged these for persecutions and
enmities and wars and daily deaths, it is quite clear that even these things
that I say now I say not desiring the glory of men. And he has said this,
because he is about to narrate also his former life, and the sudden change,
and to show by clear proofs that he transferred with truth =61.626= , lest
they think that he does these things making a defense to them, and be puffed
up. For this reason he said beforehand: For am I now persuading men? For he
knows at the fitting time, for the correction of those being taught, to utter
also something high and great. And yet it was possible to make the proofs of
preaching with truth from elsewhere, from the signs, from the wonders, from
the


dangers, from the prisons, from the daily deaths, from hunger and thirst and
nakedness, from the other such things; but since his argument now was not
toward the false apostles, but toward the apostles, and those had also shared
in these, I mean the dangers, he manages the argument from elsewhere; since,
when he stretched himself toward the false apostles, he brings on the
comparison thus, bringing into the midst the endurance in dangers and saying:
Are they ministers of Christ? (I speak as a fool:) I more; in labors more
abundantly, in stripes above measure, in prisons more abundantly, in deaths
often; now however he speaks of his former way of life, and says: But I make
known to you, brethren, the Gospel that was preached by me, that it is not
according to man. For I neither received it from a man, nor was I taught it,
but through revelation of Jesus Christ. See how up and down he insists on
this, that he has become a disciple of Christ, no man mediating, but he
himself through himself having deigned to reveal all the knowledge to him. And
what proof could there be to unbelievers that God revealed to you through
himself, and not through some intermediary, those ineffable mysteries? The
former way of life, he says; for I would not, unless it was God who was
unveiling, have had so sudden a change. For those taught by men, when they are
vehement and burning in the contrary things, need time and much contrivance in
order to be persuaded; but the one who transferred so suddenly, and in the
very peak of the madness sobered cleanly, it is quite clear that, having
obtained a divine vision and teaching, he returned all at once to clean
health. For this reason he is forced to narrate his former way of life, and he
calls them as witnesses of what happened. For that the only-begotten Son of
God deigned to call me through himself from the heavens, you do not know; for
how could you, who were not present? but that I was a persecutor, you know
very well. For my vehemence was reported even as far as you; and yet the
distance of Palestinians and Galatians is great; so that the report would not
have traveled so far, unless there had been a great excess of what was
happening, and unbearable to all. Therefore he also says: For you heard of my
former way of life in Judaism, that beyond measure I persecuted the Church of
God, and was laying it waste. Do you see how he sets down each thing with
intensity, and is not ashamed? For he did not simply persecute, but with all
excess, and he not only persecuted, but also laid waste, that is, he attempted
to quench the Church, to overturn it, to pull it down, to make it vanish; for
this is the work of one laying waste. And =61.627= I was advancing in Judaism
beyond many of my own age in my race, being more exceedingly a zealot of my
ancestral traditions.
9. For lest you think that the matter was of wrath, he shows that he did the
whole in zeal, even if not according to knowledge, not persecuting from
vainglory, nor from enmity, but also Being a zealot of my ancestral
traditions. And what he means is this: If I was doing the things against the
Church not for a man, but for a divine zeal, mistaken indeed, but still zeal;
how now, running for the Church and having recognized the truth, do I do these
things for vainglory? For if, when I was in error, no such passion mastered
me, but the zeal of God led me to this, much more, when I recognized the
truth, I would be right to be free of every such suspicion. For at the same
time I transferred to the doctrines of the Church, and I stripped off every
Jewish prejudice, showing much more zeal here; which is a sign of transferring
with truth, and of being held by divine zeal. Since if this were not so, what
else, tell me, was it that prepared so great a change to come about, and to
exchange insult for honor, and dangers for ease, and


misery for security? There is nothing, but the love of the truth alone. But
when it pleased God, who set me apart from my mother's womb, and called me
through his grace, to reveal his Son in me, that I might preach him among the
nations, immediately I did not confer with flesh and blood. See what he is
eager to show here, that even the time in which he was left behind was left
for some ineffable economy. For if he had been set apart from his mother's
womb to become an apostle and to be called to this ministry; and he was called
then, and as soon as called he obeyed; it is clear that God was postponing it
for the time for some ineffable cause. What then is this economy? Perhaps you
have opened your mouths to hear a preface from me, why he did not call him
with the twelve; but lest, standing off from what is pressing, I make the
argument longer, I too ask your love not to learn everything from me, but also
to seek from yourselves, and to ask God to reveal. And a certain argument
about these things has been spoken by us as well, when we were discussing with
you about the change of his name, and why he called him Paul who was called
Saul; and if you have forgotten, by reading that book you will know all these
things. For the time let us hold to the sequence, and let us consider how
again he shows that nothing human has happened about him, but that God is

managing the things concerning him with much providence. And having called
through his grace. God, he says, called him because of his virtue. For he is a
chosen vessel to me, he said to Ananias, to bear my name before nations and
kings, that is, sufficient to minister and to show a great work. And he sets
this down as the cause of the calling =61.628= ; but he himself everywhere
says that the whole is of grace and of his ineffable lovingkindness, speaking
thus: But I received mercy, not because I was sufficient, nor because I was
fit, but that in me he might show all longsuffering, for a pattern of those
who were going to believe on him unto eternal life. Did you see an excess of
humility? For this reason I, he says, received mercy, that no one might
despair, since the worst of all men has enjoyed lovingkindness. For this is
what he makes clear in saying, That in me he might show all longsuffering, for
a pattern of those who were going to believe on him. To reveal his Son in me.
But elsewhere Christ says, No one knows the Son except the Father, and no one
knows the Father except the Son, and he to whom the Son may wish to reveal
him. Did you see that both the Father reveals the Son, and the Son the Father?
So also in the matter of glory, both the Son glorifies the Father, and the
Father the Son; Glorify me, he says, that I may glorify you; and, As I have
glorified you. And why did he not say, to reveal his Son to me, but In me?
Showing that he did not hear the things about the faith through words only,
but was also filled with much spirit; the revelation shining upon his soul,
and he had Christ speaking in himself. That I might preach him among the
nations. For not only the believing, but also his being ordained, has come
from God. For he revealed him to me thus, not that I might only see him, but
that I might also carry him out to others. And he did not say others simply,
but That I might preach him among the nations, already from here striking up
beforehand no small head of the defense, from the persons of the disciples.
For it was not necessary to preach in the same way to Jews and to the nations.
Immediately I did not confer with flesh and blood. Here he hints at the
apostles, calling them from their nature. And if he says this also about all
men, we will not contradict either. Nor did I go up to Jerusalem to those who
were apostles before me. If someone examines these words by themselves, they
seem to be full of much boastfulness, and far from the apostolic mind


to be. For to vote for oneself, and to take no one as a partner of one's
judgment, seems to be folly. For I have seen, he says, a man who seemed to
himself to be prudent, but the fool has more hope than he; and, Woe to those
who are wise in themselves, and understanding before themselves; and he
himself again, Do not become wise in yourselves.
10. The one then who has heard so many things from others, and himself advises
the same things to others, would not have fallen into these, not Paul, but not
even an ordinary man. But as I said, this saying, examined bare, can both lie
in wait and give offense to some of the hearers; but if we add the cause for
which these things were said, all will both applaud and marvel at =61.629= the
one who spoke.
Let us do this then. For we must not examine the words bare, since many sins
will follow; nor torture the diction by itself, but attend to the mind of the
writer. For even in our own conversations, if we do not use this manner, and
examine the mind of the speaker, we shall sustain many enmities, and
everything will be overturned. And why speak of words, when even in deeds, if
someone does not keep this rule, everything will become above and below? For
physicians also both cut and break some of the bones, and robbers often do

righteousness for us, this one they also declare cause of sin, as Paul also

the absurd, he no longer needed construction toward the overturning, but was
content with the prohibition only, saying, God forbid. For toward the
exceedingly shameless and impudent one does not even need constructive
arguments, but it is enough even to prohibit only. For if I build again these
things which I pulled down, I constitute myself a transgressor. See Paul's
understanding. Those men wished to show that the one who does not keep the law
is a transgressor; but this man turned the argument to the contrary, showing
that the one who keeps the law is a transgressor, not of the faith, but even
of the law itself. Which things I pulled down, these I build again, he means
the law. And what he means is of this sort: the law has ceased, =61.645= and
we have confessed this, by which, having left it, we have fled to the
salvation from faith. If then we contend to set it up, by this very thing we
become transgressors, contending to keep the things undone by God. Then he
also shows how it was undone. For I through law died to law. This has a double
contemplation; for either he means the law of grace; for he knows how to call
this too a law, as when he says: But the law of the Spirit of life has set me
free; or he here means the old law, showing that through the law itself he
died to the law; that is: The law itself led me into no longer attending to
it. If then I am going to attend to it, I also transgress it. How and in what

brethren, like me; you shall hear him; speaking about Christ. So that those
who are not persuaded by this one transgress the law. And otherwise again we
must understand this, Through law I died to law. For the law commands to do
all the things written, and punishes the one who does not. Therefore we have
all died to it; for no one fulfilled it. And see how here too he makes the
fight toward it measuredly; for he did not say, the law died to me, but, I
died to the law. And what he means is this: Just as it is not possible for the
dead and the one who has died to obey the commandments of the law, so neither
for me who have died from that curse; for I died by its word. Let it not then
command the dead, whom it itself also killed, and a death not only the bodily,
but also the psychic, through which it also brought on the bodily.

crucified with him, and, I no longer live, but I have died, and it seemed to

spiritual things, you have ended in the fleshly; for to do signs is spiritual,
but to be circumcised is fleshly. And you after the signs have come to
circumcision, after having grasped the truth you have fallen to the types,
after having looked down on the sun you seek the lamp, after the solid food
you run to the milk. And he did not say, You are completed in flesh, but, You
are being completed in flesh, showing that like irrational cattle those men
were cutting them down =61.650= , these giving themselves over to suffer
whatever those wished; as if someone from generals and champions after ten
thousand trophies and victories, having given himself over to the dishonor of
deserters, furnishes his body to those who wish to brand it. Did you suffer so
many things in vain? if indeed it is even in vain. This is much more striking
than the former. For the reminder of the signs could not so, as the display of
the contests and the endurance of the sufferings for Christ. For all those
things, he says, which you endured, these men wish to make you lose, and to
snatch your crown. Then, lest he shake their soul, and slacken the sinews, he
did not remain as far as the sentence, but added: If indeed it is even in
vain. For if you should wish, he says, to sober up and recover yourselves, it
is not in vain. Where now are those who cut away repentance? For behold these
also received Spirit, and worked signs, and became confessors, having endured
ten thousand dangers and persecutions for Christ, and after so many successes
they fell from the grace. Yet, he says, if you wish, you can recover
yourselves. He then who supplies the Spirit to you, and works powers in you,
from works of law, or from hearing of faith? Of so great a gift, he says, were
you counted worthy, and did you accomplish so many wonders, because you kept
law, or because you kept faith? Clearly through the faith.
For since those men turned this up and down and carried it about, saying that
the faith has not strength, unless the law is added; he shows the contrary,
that when the commandments have been added the faith will profit nothing
anymore; for the faith then has strength, when nothing is added from the law.
For as many as are justified in law, he says, you have fallen from the grace.
But this he says going on, when he also uses greater frankness, taking the
occasion from the things already


constructed; for the time he constructs the occasion from the past. For when,
he says, you did not attend to the law, but to the faith, then you received
the Spirit, and you accomplished the signs. Then since the argument was about
law, he also moved another most contentious place, bringing Abraham into the
midst, and very seasonably, and with much abundance, speaking thus: Just as
Abraham believed God, and it was reckoned to him unto righteousness. For it
makes clear, he says, the power of the faith, and the wonders done by you; but
if you wish, I would also persuade you from old narratives. For since there
was much talk of the patriarch among them, he brings him too into the midst,
and shows that he too was justified thus. And if the one before the grace was
justified from faith, though he also flourished in works, much more you. For
what harm did that man take by not becoming under law? None, but the faith was
enough unto righteousness for him. For neither was there law then, he says.
For neither is there law now, just as there was not then. For this reason,
having taken away the need of the law, he then introduced the one justified
before the law, lest he receive this objection. For just as then it had not
yet been given, so also now, having been given, it has ceased. Then since they
thought greatly of having been born from Abraham, and =61.651= feared, lest
having left the law they be alienated from that kinship; this too again Paul
turns to the contrary, and undoes the fear, showing that the faith especially
works the nearness to that man. And this he constructed more clearly in that
to the Romans, and he works it no less here too, speaking thus: Know then that
those from faith, these are sons of Abraham. Then he also constitutes this
very thing from an old testimony. For the Scripture, he says, having foreseen
that God justifies the nations from faith, preached the gospel beforehand to
Abraham, that In you all the nations shall be blessed. If then it is not those
who have the natural kinship toward him who are sons, but those who have
imitated that man's faith (for this is, In you the nations), it is clear that
one is brought into this kinship.
3. And through these things another great thing is also shown. For since it
troubled them that the law is more ancient, and the faith after the law, he
also takes away this suspicion of theirs, showing that the faith is older than
the law, and this is clear from Abraham; for before the law appeared, that man
was justified. And he shows that the things that have now happened also
happened according to prophecy; For the Scripture, he says, having foreseen
that God justifies the nations from faith, not from the law, preached the
gospel beforehand to Abraham. And what is this? He himself who gives the law,
he says, even before giving this law, had determined this, that the nations be
justified from faith. And he did not say, He revealed, but, He preached the
gospel, that you may learn that the patriarch also rejoiced in this manner of
righteousness, and strongly longed for this to come out. And since they had
another fear as well (for it was written: Cursed is everyone who does not
remain in all the things written in the book of this law, to do them), he also
dissolves this dread, wisely and understandingly again turning it to the
contrary, and showing that those who have left the law are not only not
cursed, but even blessed; and those who hold it are not only not blessed, but
even cursed. For those men said that the one who does not keep the law is
cursed; but this man shows that the one who keeps is cursed, and the one who
does not keep is blessed. Those men again said that the one who attends to the
faith only is cursed; but this man shows that the one who attends to the faith
only is blessed. How then does he show all these things? for we have not
promised some chance thing.


Therefore one must also attend with accuracy to what follows. For he showed
this also before, saying that the Scripture said to the patriarch that In you
all the nations shall be blessed. And then there was no law, but faith;
therefore also reasoning, he added: So that those from faith are blessed with
the faithful Abraham. And lest they take this into an objection, and say:
Reasonably he was justified from faith; for there was not yet law; but you
show me after the placing of the law the faith justifying; he comes to this,
and shows more than they seek, not only the faith justifying, but also the law
making those who use it cursed. And that you may learn these things, hear the
apostolic =61.652= words themselves: For as many as are from works of law are
under a curse. But this is a sentence, not yet having the proof. What then is
the proof? From the law itself; For cursed is everyone who does not remain in
all the things written in the book of this law, to do them. And that no one is
justified in law is clear to everyone. For all have sinned, and are under a
curse. But he does not say this, lest he seem himself to be sentencing, but
again he constitutes it from a testimony, which has both in brief, both that
no one fulfilled law (therefore they were also cursed), and that the faith
justifies. What then is the testimony? From the prophet Habakkuk speaking
thus: But the righteous shall live from faith. For this shows not only the
righteousness from faith, but that it is not possible to be saved through law
either. For since, he says, no one kept law, but all were under the curse
because of the transgression, some easy way was invented, that from the faith;
which is also a very great sign that no one was able to be justified from the
law. For the prophet did not say, But the righteous shall live from law, but,
From faith. And the law is not from faith, but he who has done them shall live
in them. For the law, he says, does not seek faith only, but also works; but
the grace saves and justifies from faith. Did you see how he showed that those
who attend to the law, because of the impossibility of it being fulfilled,
came under a curse? And how does the faith also have this power of justifying?
For these things he promised before, and he set them forth with much strength.
For since the law was weak toward bringing the man to righteousness, no small
medicine was found, the faith, making the impossible of the law possible
through itself. If then the Scripture also says that The righteous shall live
from faith, despairing of the salvation through the law, and Abraham was
justified from faith, it is clear that the strength of the faith is much. But
that the one who does not remain in the law is cursed, and that the one who
attends to the faith is righteous, is clear. But that that curse no longer
stands, from where do you have to prove to us, he says; For Abraham was before
the law; but we, having once become under the yoke of slavery, have
constituted ourselves liable to the curse; who then is the one who has undone
that curse? Toward this see how he meets swiftly; for the things said are also
enough. For the one who has once been justified, and has died to the law, and
has taken hold of a strange life, how would he be liable to the curse? Yet he
is not content with these, but contends also otherwise, writing: Christ has
bought us out from the curse of the law, having become a curse for us; for it
is written, cursed is everyone who hangs upon a tree. And yet the people was
liable to another curse, that which says: Cursed is everyone who does not
remain in the things written in the book of the law. And what of this? For the
people was liable; for it did not remain, nor was there anyone who had
fulfilled the whole law. But Christ exchanged another curse for this, that
which says: Cursed is everyone who hangs upon a tree. Since then both the one
who hangs upon a tree is cursed, and the one who transgresses the law is
cursed, and the one who was going to undo =61.653= that curse ought not to
have become liable to it, but must receive a curse


instead of that one, he received such a one, and through it he undid that. And
just as when someone has been condemned to die, another not liable, having
chosen to die for that one, snatches him from the punishment; so also Christ
did. For since he was not subject to a curse of the transgression, Christ took
this one upon himself instead of that, that he might undo theirs; For he did
no sin, nor was deceit found in his mouth.
4. Just as then by dying he took those who were going to die out of death, so
also by receiving a curse he delivered from the curse. That the blessing of
Abraham might come unto the nations. How unto the nations? In your seed, he
says, all the nations shall be blessed, that is, in Christ. And if this was
said about Jews, how would it have reason that those who were liable to the
curse because of the transgression should become causes of blessing to others?
for no one of the cursed shares with another a blessing of which he himself
has been deprived. From which it is clear that the whole has been said about
Christ; for he was seed of Abraham, and through him the nations have been
blessed, and so the promise of the Spirit comes on. Which he himself also
making clear said: That they might receive the promise of the Spirit through
the faith. For since it was not possible for the grace of the Spirit to come
upon an unthankful and offending one, they are first blessed, the curse having
been taken away; then having been justified through the faith, they draw the
grace of the Spirit. So that the cross undid the curse; and the faith
introduced the righteousness; and the righteousness drew the grace of the
Spirit. Brethren, I speak according to man, nevertheless no one sets aside a
covenant of a man previously ratified, or adds ordinances. What is, I speak
according to man? From human examples. For since he constituted the argument
both from Scriptures, and from the wonders happening among them, and from what
Christ suffered, and from the patriarch, he also goes out thereafter to the
common custom. For it is his custom always to do this, so as both to sweeten
the argument, and to make it acceptable and easier to learn for the thicker.
So also discussing with Corinthians, he says: Who shepherds a flock, and does
not eat from its milk? who plants a vineyard, and does not eat from its fruit?
and to Hebrews again: For a covenant is firm over dead; since it never has
force when the one who disposed is alive. And in many places one would find
him fond of lingering in these reasonings. For God too in the Old continually
does it, speaking thus: Shall a woman forget her child? and again, Shall the
thing formed say to the potter, What are you doing? And in Hosea he imitates a
husband despised by the wife. And in the types too one would see much of human
examples, as when the prophet takes the girdle, and went down into the
potter's little house. What then does the example mean for him? That the faith
was more ancient, and the law both younger and for a time, and given for this
reason, that it might make a way for the =61.654= faith. Therefore he says:
Brethren, I speak according to man. Having said foolish above, here he calls
them brethren, at once stinging and comforting. Nevertheless a covenant of a
man ratified. If a man, he says, disposes, no one dares, coming after these
things, to overturn, or to add something; for this is, Or adds ordinances.
Therefore much more in the case of God. And toward whom did God dispose? To
Abraham, he says, the promises were spoken, and to his seed. He does not say
And to his seeds, as upon many, but as upon one, And to your seed, which is
Christ. And this I say, a covenant previously ratified by God unto Christ, the
law that has come after four hundred and thirty years does not make invalid,


unto the making of no effect of the promise. For if the inheritance is from
law, it is no longer from promise; but God has granted to Abraham through
promise. See then, God also disposed to Abraham, discussing unto the nations,
that the blessings would come in his seed; how then can the law overturn
these? And since the example as a whole did not have power to draw toward the
subject, for this reason he also said beforehand: I speak according to man.
Take nothing from the example unto the magnificence of God. And consider the
example from above: He promised to Abraham that the nations would be blessed
through his seed; and his seed according to flesh is Christ. The law came
after four hundred and thirty years. If then the law grants the blessings, and
the life and the righteousness, that promise is invalid. Then no one makes a
man's covenant invalid, and is the covenant of God made invalid after four
hundred and thirty years? For if what that promised, that does not give, but
another instead of that, that has been cast out. And how would these things
have reason? Why then did he give the law, he says? For the sake of the
transgressions. For this too is not superfluous.
Do you see how he sees all things together? how he uses ten thousand eyes? For
since he lifted up the faith, and showed it older, lest anyone think the law
superfluous, he also corrects this part, showing that it was not given in
vain, but very usefully; For the sake of the transgressions, that is, so that
it might not be possible for Jews to live without fear, and to slip out into
the last of wickedness, but that the law might lie upon them as a bridle,
educating, regulating, hindering from transgressing, even if not all, yet at
least even from one of the commandments. So that the gain of the law is not
small. But until when? Until the seed come, to whom it has been promised;
speaking about Christ. If then it has been given until the presence of Christ,
why do you drag it further and out of season? Having been arranged through
angels in the hand of a mediator. Either he calls the priests angels, or he
says that the angels themselves also served the legislation. And he here means
Christ as mediator, showing that he was before, and he himself gave the law.
And the mediator is not of one; but God is one.
5. What would heretics say here? For if the only true does not allow the Son
to be true God, then not God either, because of the being said, But God is
one. And if, when the Father is called one God, the Son also is God, it is
quite clear that when the Father is called true, the Son also is true. And the
mediator, he says, becomes mediator of two someones. Of whom then was Christ
mediator? Or it is clear, of God and men. Do you see how he shows that he
himself also gave the law? If then he himself gave, he would also be lord to
undo it again. Is the law then against the promises of God? for if the
blessings have been given in the seed of Abraham, and the law introduces a
curse, then it is against the promises of God. How then does he solve the
objection? First he prohibits, saying: God forbid; then he also constructs,
speaking thus: For if a law had been given that was able to make alive,
righteousness would truly have been from law. And what he means is this: If we
had the hope of life in it, he says, and it was the one that had the authority
of our salvation, perhaps you said these things well; but if it saves from
faith, even if it makes cursed, you have taken no harm, that coming and
undoing all things. For if the promise were through that, you reasonably
feared lest you fall from the righteousness, falling from the law; but if it
was given for this reason, that it might shut up all, that is, that it might
convict, and show their own offenses, it not only does not hinder you from
obtaining the promise, but even cooperates toward the obtaining. This then he
making clear said: But the Scripture has shut up all things under sin, that
the


promise from faith of Jesus Christ might be given to those who believe. For
since Jews did not even perceive their own sins, and those who do not perceive
do not even desire forgiveness, he gave the law convicting the wounds, that
they might long for the physician. For the, Has shut up, is has convicted, and
having convicted held in fear. Did you see that it is not only not against the
promises of God, but was even given for the promises? For if the law
vindicated the matter for itself and the authority, these things were said
well; but if it runs for another, and did all things through that one, how is
it against the promises of God? For if law had not been given, all would have
run aground into wickedness, and there was going to be no one of Jews who
would hear Christ; but now, having been given, it accomplished these two
things, having educated those who attended in a measured virtue, and having
persuaded them to know their own sins, which especially made them more eager
toward seeking the Son.
Those then who did not believe him, from not condemning their own sins, did
not believe. And this he making clear said: For being ignorant of the
righteousness of God, and seeking to set up their own righteousness, they did
not submit to the righteousness of God. But before the faith came, we were
guarded under law, having been shut up unto the faith about to be revealed
unto us. Did you see how clearly he has set forth what we have said? For the,
We were guarded, and, Having been shut up, is of one making nothing else clear
than the security that came from the commandments of the law. For as in some
little wall the law, holding them by the fear =61.656= and by the life
according to it, kept them for the faith. So that the law has become our
pedagogue unto Christ, that we might be justified from faith. And the
pedagogue does not oppose the teacher, but even cooperates, delivering the
young from all wickedness, and preparing him with all leisure to receive the
lessons from the teacher; but when he has come into habit, thereafter the
pedagogue stands off. For this reason he says: But the faith having come that
leads unto the perfect man, we are no longer
under a pedagogue. For you are all sons of God through the faith in Christ
Jesus. If then the law is a pedagogue, and we were guarded under it having
been shut up, it is not contrary to the grace, but even a fellow worker; but
if, the grace having come, it should remain holding, then it is contrary. For
if it shuts up those who ought to go out toward it, then it ruins our
salvation. For just as the lamp that has given light in the night, if when day
has come it forces one not to look at the sun, has not only not granted, but
has even ruined; so also the law, if it becomes a hindrance toward the greater
things. Those then who keep it now, these especially slander it. For the
pedagogue also then makes the young laughable, when, the season calling him to
stand off, he holds him with himself. Therefore Paul also says: But the faith
having come, you are no longer under a pedagogue. So that we are no longer
under a pedagogue. For you are all sons of God. Wonder, how great is the power
of the faith, and how he unveils it as he goes on! For formerly he showed that
it made sons of the patriarch; For you know, he says, that those from faith,
these are sons of Abraham; but now he declares that they are of God as well;
For you are all sons of God, he says, through faith that is in Christ Jesus.
Through the faith, not through the law. Then since he said a great and
marvelous thing, he also says the manner of the adoption. For as many as were
baptized unto Christ, you put on Christ. And why did he not say, For as many
as were baptized unto Christ, you were born from God? for this was the
sequence of showing sons. Because he sets it down as much more fearful. For if
Christ is Son of God, and you have put him on, having the Son in yourself and
having been likened to him, you have been brought into one kinship and one
form. There is not Jew nor Greek, there is not slave nor free, there is not
male or female. For you are all one


in Christ Jesus. Did you see an insatiable soul? For having said that We have
become sons of God through the faith, he does not stand as far as this, but
seeks to find something more, which can more clearly set forth the nearer
union toward Christ.
And having said that You have put him on, he is not even content with this
diction, but interpreting it he goes further inward of such a conjunction, and
says that You are all one in Christ Jesus; that is, you all have one form, one

perish being faint-hearted, so also this blessed man. For these travails are
also sharper than the bodily, by as much as the affection is greater, and the
thing sinned was not some chance thing.
3. For what I always say, and will not cease saying, even the little that has
been transgressed has made the whole face vanish, and has ruined the form. I
wished to be present toward you now, and to change my voice. Consider for me
the impatient one, the hot one, the one not able to contain such things. For
such is love; it is not content with the words, but also seeks the presence.
So as to change my voice, he says. That is, to change the lament, to make
tears as well, and to draw all things into a dirge. For through a letter it
was not possible to show tears nor a wailing; therefore he burns, seeking the
presence. Because I am at a loss in you. For I do not have, he says, what to
say, I do not have what to reason. From where have those who have climbed to
the very peak of the heavens, both through the dangers which you endured

For this he also said discussing with Milesians: For three years I did not

Since then he has rebuked and put to shame, and treated again, then he

neither makes harsh, like a rebuke, nor slackens, like the treatment, but it

well-born. So great is the power of the faith. But the one from the
maidservant has been born according to flesh; and the one from the free
according to promise. What is, According to flesh? Since he said that the
faith joins us to Abraham, and it seemed to the hearers unbelievable, if he
says that those who have not been born from that man are sons of that man, he
shows that this paradox has happened from of old. For Isaac, not according to
a sequence of nature, nor according to a law of marriages, nor according to a
power of flesh having come to be =61.662= , was both a son and genuine, sprung
from dead bodies, and from that womb. For neither did the flesh work the
conception, nor the seed the birth; for the womb was dead, both because of the
age and because of the maiming, but the word of God formed him. But in the
case of the slave it was not so, but that one is born by the bonds of nature,
and by the custom of marriage. Yet the one not according to flesh was more
honorable than the one born according to flesh. Let it not then trouble you
that you have not been born according to flesh; for for this reason especially
you are his kinsmen, because you were not born according to flesh. For this
according to flesh does not make more honorable, but even more dishonorable;
for the birth not according to flesh is more marvelous and more spiritual; and
it is clear from those born from of old. For Ishmael was born according to
flesh, but he was a slave, and not this only, but he was also cast out of the
paternal house; but Isaac born according to promise, as being a son and free,
was lord of all. Which things are allegorized. He called the type an allegory
by a catachresis. And what he means is this: This history does not only
indicate this, which appears, but also proclaims some other things; therefore
it has also been called allegory. And what did it proclaim? Nothing else, than
all the present things. For these are the two covenants, he says, one from
mount Sinai generating unto slavery, which is Hagar. These; who? The mothers
of those children, Sarah and Hagar. What are two Covenants? Two laws. But
since there were names of women in the history, he remained in the title of
the kind, and showing much sequence from the names. And how from the names?
For Hagar is Sinai mountain, he says, in Arabia. The slave was called Hagar;
and Sinai mountain is so translated in their local tongue.
4. So that all those born from the Old must be slaves. For that mountain,

children. What then from this? That not only was that one a slave, and bore
slaves, but this one also, that is, the Covenant, of which the slave is a
type. For Jerusalem also lies from neighbors to the mountain homonymous with

is the type of Sarah as well? But the Jerusalem above is free. Therefore those
born from her are not slaves either. Of the Jerusalem below Hagar was a type,
and it is clear from the mountain so called, and of the one above the Church.
Yet he is not content with the types, but he also adds Isaiah

the whole world.
Did you see how through deeds Sarah, and through words the prophet, sketched
the things to come for us? And consider: Isaiah called her barren, and showed
this one become many-childed. This also happened typically in the case of

And to Paul not even these things are enough, but he also is a busybody as to
how the barren became a mother, that from here too he may bring the type to
the truth. Therefore he also adds, saying: And we, brethren, according to
Isaac are children of promise. For the Church was not only barren, as Sarah,
nor only became many-childed, as that one, but she also bore thus, as Sarah.
For just as nature did not work that one a mother, but the promise of God (for
the one who said, According to this season I will come, and Sarah shall have a
son, this one having gone into the womb formed the infant); so also in the
case of our regeneration there is no nature, but the words of God spoken
through the priest, which the faithful know, these in the pool of the waters,
as in some womb, form and regenerate the one being baptized. Therefore if we
are children of the barren, we are also free. And what sort of freedom is
this, someone might say, when the Jews hold and whip those who have believed,
and those who seem to be free are persecuted? for these things happened then,
the faithful being persecuted. But let not this trouble you either, he says;
for this too was taken beforehand in the type; for Isaac being free was
persecuted by the slave Ishmael. Therefore he also adds, saying: But just as
then the one born according to flesh persecuted the one according to spirit,
so also now. But what does the Scripture say? Cast out the son of the
maidservant; for the son of the maidservant shall not inherit with the son of
the free. What then? is this =61.664= whole comfort, to have been shown that
the free are persecuted by the slaves? No, he says; for I do not stand here
either, but hear also what follows, and then you will receive sufficient
comfort, not being small-souled at the persecutions. What then are the things
that follow? Cast out the son of the maidservant; for he shall not inherit
with the son of the free. Did you see the wages of the temporary tyranny, and
of the untimely arrogance? The child falls from the paternal things, and
becomes a fugitive and a wanderer with the mother. But you consider for me the
wisdom of what is said. For he did not say, because he persecuted, for this
reason he was cast out, only, but, that he may not inherit. For he did not
demand this of him as a punishment of the temporary persecution (for this is
small, and nothing toward that); but he did not allow him to become a partner
of the things prepared for the child, showing that even without the
persecution this had been typed from of old, not having taken its beginning
from the persecution, but from the mind of God. And he did not say, The son of
Abraham shall not inherit, but, The son of the maidservant, calling him from
the cheaper kind. But Sarah was barren; for the Church from the nations is
also such. Do you see the type kept through all things? For just as in all
those former years that one not bearing becomes a mother toward last old age.

1. For did you free yourselves, that you run again upon a former mastery? but
is he himself the one who has bought you out, another the one who has given
the price for you? Do you see through how many things he leads them away from
the Jewish deceit? First showing that it is last folly, having become free
instead of slaves, to desire to be slaves instead of free; second, that they
will be shown unthankful and ungrateful toward the benefactor, despising the
one who freed, and loving the one who enslaved; third, that this is not
possible. For the law does not have the mastery, once another has bought us
all from it. And having said Stand =61.664= , he showed the tossing. And do
not again be held in a yoke of slavery. By the name of the yoke he indicates
to them the heaviness of the matter; and by saying, Again, he shows much their
lack of feeling. For if you were inexperienced of that burden, you were not
worthy of so many charges; but those who have learned by experience the
burdensome of the yoke, then submitting themselves to that again, of what
pardon would you be right to obtain? See, I Paul say to you, that if you are
circumcised, Christ will profit you nothing. See how great a threat.
Reasonably then he also anathematized angels.
And how will Christ profit them nothing? For he himself has not constructed
this, but has declared, as the trustworthiness of the person being enough
thereafter instead of every proof (for this is why he said beforehand: See, I
Paul say to you, which belonged to one taking heart in what he says); but we
will add the things from ourselves as far as =61.665= possible. How will
Christ profit the one being circumcised nothing? The one being circumcised is
circumcised as fearing law; and the one who fears disbelieves the power of the
grace, and the one who disbelieves gains nothing from what is disbelieved.
Again otherwise, the one being circumcised makes the law lord; and thinking it
to be lord, and transgressing it from the greater part, and keeping it from
the lesser, he again sets himself under the curse; and submitting himself to
the curse, and having thrust away the freedom from the faith, how can he be
saved? For if one must also say something paradoxical, this one believes
neither in Christ nor in the law, but stands in the middle, wishing to gain
both from there and from here; therefore he will harvest nothing from nowhere.
Then having said that He will profit nothing, he sets down the proof
enigmatically and through brief things, speaking thus: And I testify to every
man being circumcised, that he is a debtor to fulfill the whole law. For lest
you think these things are said according to enmity, I do not say to you only,
he says, but also to every man being circumcised, that he is a debtor to
fulfill the whole law; for the legal things hold one another. And


just as the one who has registered himself to be a slave from a free man no
longer does what he wishes, but is held by all the laws of slavery; so also in
the case of the law, when you take on some little of the law, and bring
yourself under the yoke, you have drawn the whole mastery. So also in the case
of the outside inheritance; the one who has touched nothing is delivered from
all the things brought upon the lot of the one who has died; but if he handles
some little, even if he does not take all, he has constituted himself liable
to all through the part. This also happens in the case of the law, not through
this manner only which I have said, but also through another; for the legal
things hold one another. As I mean: Circumcision has a sacrifice yoked with it
and an observation of days; the sacrifice again a keeping of both day and
place, the place manners of ten thousand purifications, the purifications
introduce a swarm of various observations. For it is not lawful for the
unclean to sacrifice, nor to go upon the inner shrines, nor to do any other
such thing. Therefore the law also draws many things even through the one
commandment. If then you are circumcised, but not on the eighth day, or on the
eighth, but there is no sacrifice, and there is a sacrifice, but not in the
defined place, or in the defined place, but not the things that have been
lawful, or the things that have been lawful, but you are not clean, or being
clean, but not having been purified by the fitting statutes, all those things
are gone. For this reason he says that He is a debtor to fulfill the whole
law. If the law is lord, do not fulfill a part, but the whole; and if it is
not lord, not even the part. You have been made of no effect from Christ,

constructed, thereafter he declares, and the danger about the last cliffs. For
when the one who runs to the law can neither be saved from there, and falls
from the grace, what is left, but unavoidable =61.666= punishment, that being
weak, and this not admitting him?

and through it righteousness, and the many and great goods. For in Christ

through love. Do you see how thereafter he strips with more frankness? For the
one who has put on Christ, he says, let him no longer be a busybody about
these things. And yet he said that circumcision is harmful; how then does he
set it down as indifferent? Indifferent in the case of those who already had
it before the faith, not in the case of those being circumcised after the
faith. And see where he has cast it out, having set it with uncircumcision.
For what makes the difference is the faith. Just as then if someone lists
athletes, whether hook-nosed or snub, whether black or white, these make no
difference toward the testing, but that they be strong and skilled only, this
one must seek; so also the one who is going to be registered unto the New

sought only, but also the remaining loving. As if he said: If you loved Christ
as one ought, you would not have deserted to the slavery, you would not have

freed. And here he also hints at those who plot against them, showing that
they too,

You were running well, who has cut you off? This is not of one asking, but of

freedom unto an occasion for the flesh. Here thereafter he seems to enter into
the ethical argument, but he suffers something new, and what he suffered in
not even one of the other Epistles. For dividing all into two parts, and
discussing the first about doctrines, and the last about life, here having
fallen into the ethical argument, he again mixes in the dogmatic. For this
again holds to that in the fight toward Manichees. And what is, Only not the
freedom for the flesh unto an occasion? Christ has delivered us, he says, from
the yoke of slavery, he has left us lords of doing whatever we wish, not that
we may use the authority toward wickedness, but that we may receive a pretext
of a greater wage, going up to a greater philosophy. For since up and down he
calls the law a yoke of slavery, and the grace a deliverance from a curse;
lest anyone suspect that he commands to stand off from the law for this
reason, that it may be possible to live unlawfully, he corrects the suspicion,
saying: Not that the way of life may become unlawful, but that the philosophy
may be above the law; for the bonds of the law have been undone. And I say
these things, not that we may become lower, but that we may become higher.
For both the one who fornicates, and the one who lives as a virgin, have both
gone out of the measures of the law, but not upon the same things, but the one
having been carried to the worse, and the other having been lifted to the
better; the one having transgressed, and the other having gone beyond the law.
This then Paul says, that He has undone the yoke from you, not =61.670= that
you may skip, nor that you may kick, but that without a yoke you may walk in
good rhythm. Then he also shows a manner through which such a thing would
easily be accomplished. What then is the manner? Through the love serve one
another, he says. Again here he hints that contentiousness and faction and
love of rule and arrogance have become causes of this deceit for them; for the
mother of the heresies is the desire of love of rule. For having said that
Serve one another, he showed that this evil took its beginning from arrogance
and conceit; therefore he introduces a fitting medicine. For since wishing to
rule one another you have been split, Serve one another; for thus you will be
gathered again. But he does not say the sin openly, yet he sets down the
correction openly, that through it they may learn that too; as if someone
should not say to the wanton that he is wanton, but should continually advise
to be temperate. For the one who loves the neighbor as one ought does not
decline to serve him more humbly than every slave. For just as fire having
kept company with wax softens it easily, so also the heat of love dissolves
all conceit and arrogance more vehemently than fire. For this reason he did
not say, Love one another, simply, but,


Serve, indicating the intensified friendship. For since he has taken away the
yoke of the law, lest they skip away, he lays upon them another yoke, that of
love, stronger than that, but lighter and much sweeter. Then showing its
success, he says: For the whole law is fulfilled in one word, in the, You
shall love your neighbor as yourself. For since they turned the law up and
down, if you wish to fulfill it, he says, do not be circumcised; for it is
fulfilled not in the circumcision, but in the love. See how he does not forget
his own pain, but continually moves what hurts, even having gone out into the
ethical argument.
And if you bite and eat one another, see that you be not consumed by one
another. He does not declare, lest he weigh heavily, but he knows it
happening, yet he says it with doubt. For he did not say, Since you bite one
another; and what follows likewise; for there again he did not declare that
You have been consumed by one another; which belongs to one fearing and making
secure, not condemning. And he has also used the dictions emphatically. For he
did not say, You bite, only, which belongs to one in wrath, but, And you eat,
which belongs to one remaining in the wickedness. For the one who bites has
filled a passion of anger; but the one who eats has furnished a proof of last
beastliness. And he means bites and eatings not the bodily, but those much
more grievous. For the one who has tasted human flesh has not so harmed, as
the one who fixes bites into the soul; for by as much as soul is more
honorable than body, by so much more grievous is the harm of this. See that
you be not consumed by one another, he says. For since those who wrong and
plot come to this as consuming others, for this reason he says: Look lest the
matter be turned around upon you. For the standing apart and the fight is
destructive and consuming both of those who receive it, and of those who
introduce it, and more than a moth it gnaws through all things. And I say,
walk in spirit, and you shall not complete a desire of flesh.
5. Behold he also shows another road making virtue easy =61.671= , and
accomplishing what has been said, a road that begets love, and is bound by
love. For nothing, nothing so makes loving, as being spiritual, and nothing so
persuades the spirit to remain with us, as the strength of love. Therefore he
says: Walk in spirit, and you shall not complete a desire of flesh. For since
he has said what makes the disease, he also says the medicine that grants the
health. And what is this, and what is the power of the good things said, but
to live in spirit? Therefore he says: Walk in spirit, and you shall not
complete a desire of flesh. For the flesh desires against the spirit, and the
spirit against the flesh; and these are opposed to one another, that you may
not do the things you would. And here some attack, saying that the Apostle has
divided the man into two, introducing him as constructed from a contrary
substance, and showing a fight of the body toward the soul. But it is not so,
it is not; for here he does not call flesh the body; for if he means this, how
does what is said next have reason? For it desires, he says, against the
spirit. And yet this belongs not to the things that move, but to the things
that are moved, not to the things that work, but to the things that are worked
upon; how then does it desire? for the desire is not of flesh, but of soul.
For elsewhere too he says: My soul is very desirous; and what does your soul
desire, and I will do for you, And, Do not go according to the desire of your
soul; and again, So my soul longs. How then does Paul say, The flesh desires
against the spirit? He knows how to call flesh not the nature of the body, but
the evil choice, as when he says: You are not in flesh, but in spirit; And
again, And those


who are in flesh cannot please God. What then? Must one take away the flesh?
And he himself who says these things, was he not clothed with flesh? These
teachings are not of the flesh, but of the devil; For he was a man-killer from
the beginning. What then does he say? Here he calls flesh the earthly
reasoning, the slack and neglected.
And this is not an accusation of body, but a charge of a slack soul; for the
flesh is an instrument, and no one turns away from and hates the instrument,
but the one who handles the instrument badly. For we both hate and punish not
the iron, but the murderer. But this very thing, he says, is an accusation of
the body, that the sins of the soul are called by the name of the flesh. And I
confess that the flesh is less than the soul, and that it itself is also
beautiful. For the less of the beautiful is itself also beautiful; but the
evil is not less than the beautiful, but contrary. And you, if you have to
show the wickedness being constructed from the body, accuse the flesh; but if
you attempt to slander from the title, it is your season to accuse the soul as
well; for a psychic man is also said of the one deprived of the truth, and
spirits of wickedness is said of the multitudes of the demons. And by the name
of the flesh again the Scripture is accustomed to call even the mysteries, and
the whole Church, saying it is a body of Christ. And if you wish also to call
successes the things that happen through =61.672= it, quench the senses in the
argument, and you will see the soul empty of all knowledge, and knowing
nothing of the things it knows. For if the power of God, being understood from
the creation of the world, is seen in the things made, how would we see
without eyes? and if the faith is from hearing, how shall we hear without
ears? And the preaching and the going about, through tongue and feet. For how
shall they preach, unless they are sent? and the writing through hands. Do you
see that the ministry of the flesh is cause of ten thousand goods for us? And
if he says, The flesh desires against the spirit, he means about two
reasonings; for these are opposed to one another, virtue and wickedness, not
the soul and the body. For if these are opposed, they are destructive of one
another, as fire of water, as darkness of light; and if soul takes care of
body, and makes much providence of it, and suffers ten thousand things so as
not to leave it, and being torn away hinders, and the body ministers to it,
and introduces much knowledge to it, and this has been constructed toward that
one's working; how would these be contrary and fighting one another? For I not
only do not see them contrary, but even strongly of one mind, and holding to
one another through the deeds. He does not then mean about these, that they
are opposed to one another, but he introduces a fight of evil reasonings and
good. For the wishing and not wishing is of the soul. For this reason he says:
and these are opposed, that you may not allow the soul to go in its evil
desires. For as some pedagogue and teacher frightening, he has uttered these
things. And if you are led by spirit, you are not under law.
6. What sequence is this? Very great and clear. For the one who has spirit as
one ought quenches through this every evil desire; and the one delivered from
these does not need the help from the law, having become much higher than that
one's command. For the one who is not angry, how does he need to hear, You
shall not murder? the one who does not look with unchaste eyes, how does he
need the one who educates not to commit adultery? For who discusses about the
fruit of the wickedness with the one who has pulled up the root itself? For
root of murder is wrath, and of adultery the busy contemplation of the sights.
For this reason he says: If you are led by spirit, you are not under law. And
here he seems to me also to have said a great and marvelous praise of the law;
and if the law was in the rank of the spirit according to its own power before
the


visiting of the spirit, yet one must not for this reason remain in the
pedagogue. Then reasonably we were under law, that we might punish the desires
by fear, the spirit not yet having appeared; but now, the grace having been
given that not only commands to hold off from them, but also withers them, and
leads up to a greater way of life, what need of law? For the one who from
himself accomplishes the greater things, where is need of a pedagogue? for
neither does a philosopher need a grammar-teacher.
Why then do you make yourselves cheap, having first given yourselves over to
the spirit, and now sitting by the law? And the works of the flesh are
manifest, which are fornication, adultery, uncleanness, wantonness, =61.673=
sorcery, idolatry, enmities, strifes, jealousies, wraths, factions, divisions,
heresies, envies, murders, drunkennesses, revels, and the things like these,
which I tell you beforehand, as I also said before, that those who practice
such things shall not inherit a kingdom of God. Here thereafter, you who
accuse your flesh, tell me, and think that these things have been said as
about an enemy and a foe (for let adultery and fornication be of the flesh
according to you), the enmities, and the strifes, and the jealousies, and the
factions, and the heresies, and the sorceries (for these are of a corrupted
choice only, just as the others too) how would they be of flesh? Do you see
that he does not mean the flesh here, but the earthly reasoning dragged on the
ground? Therefore he also frightens, saying that Those who practice such
things shall not inherit a kingdom of God. And if these things are of an evil
nature, and not of a wretched choice, he has said superfluously, They
practice, but, They suffer. And why do they also fall from a kingdom? For
neither of the things in nature, but of the things in choice, are both the
crowns and the punishments. Therefore Paul has also threatened these things.
And the fruit of the spirit is love, joy, peace. He did not say, the work of
the spirit, but, The fruit. Therefore the soul is superfluous; for if the
argument is about flesh and spirit, where is the soul? Does he then discuss
about things without soul? For when the evil things are of the flesh, and the
useful of the spirit, the soul is superfluous. By no means; for the continence
of the passions is of this and about it; and lying in the middle of wickedness
and virtue, if it uses the body as needed, it has worked it spiritual; but if
it stands off from the spirit, and gives itself over to the evil desires, it
has constructed it more earthly. Do you see from every side how his argument
now is not about a substance of the flesh, but about a wretched choice, and
not such? And why does he call it fruit of the spirit? Because the evil works
come from us =61.674= only; therefore he also calls them works; but the
beautiful things need not only our care, but also the lovingkindness of God.
Then being about to narrate them, he first sets down the root of the goods,
speaking thus: Love, joy, peace, longsuffering, kindness, goodness, faith,
meekness, continence. Against such there is no law. For what would someone
command to the one who has all things from himself, and love as a complete
teacher of the philosophy? For just as the well-reined of horses, and doing
all things from themselves, would not need a whip; so also a soul
accomplishing the virtue from spirit does not need the law that admonishes.
And here too he has cast out the law very much with marvel, not as base, but
as less than the philosophy given by the Spirit. And those of Christ have
crucified the flesh with the passions and the desires. For lest they say: And
who is such? he shows upon the deeds those who accomplish such things, here
again calling flesh the evil practices. For they have not taken away the
flesh; since how were they going to live? For what has been crucified is dead
and without working; but he makes clear the accurate philosophy. For the
desires, even if they trouble, rage empty; since then so great is the strength
of the spirit, let us live by that, let us be content with that; which he
himself also adds


saying: Let us then live in spirit, and let us also walk in spirit, living
according to that one's laws. For this is the, Let us walk; that is, Let us be
content with the power of the spirit, and let us not seek the addition from
the law. Then showing that those who introduce the circumcision do this
through ambition, he says: Let us not become vainglorious, which is cause of
all the evils, challenging one another, envying one another. For from
vainglory is the envy, and from envy these ten thousand evils.

1. For since under a form of rebuke they were vindicating their own passions,

If he practices, but, If he is overtaken, that is, if he is snatched. You who
are spiritual restore such a one. He did not say, Punish, nor, Condemn, but,
Set right. And he did not even stand here, but showing that they must be very
gentle to those who are tripped, he so added: In a spirit of meekness. He did
not say, in meekness, but, In a spirit of meekness; making clear that these
things also seem good to the Spirit, and that being able to set right those
who sin with gentleness is a spiritual gift. Then, lest the one who sets
another right be puffed up, he has set him too under the same fear, speaking
thus: Looking to yourself, lest you also be tempted. For just as those who are
rich contribute collections to those in need =61.674= , that, if ever they
themselves also fall into poverty, they may obtain the same; so we too must
do. Therefore he also sets down a necessary cause, speaking thus: Looking to
yourself, lest you also be tempted. And he makes a defense for the one who has
sinned, first saying, Even if he is overtaken, second the name of much
weakness, then, Lest you also be tempted, charging a spite of a demon rather
than slackness of soul. Bear one another's burdens.
For since it is not possible for one who is a man to be without a failing, he
advises not to be accurate examiners of the things offended by others, but
also to bear the failings of the neighbors, that his own also may be borne by
others. For just as in the building, not all the stones have the same seat,
but one is fit toward a corner, but no longer toward foundations, and another
toward foundations, but no longer toward a corner; so also in the body of the
Church. And in our flesh too one would see this very thing but =61.675=
nevertheless one endures another, and we do not seek all things from all. For
the collection that happens from the common both constitutes a body and a
building. And so fulfill the law of Christ. He did not say, Fulfill, but, Fill
up, that is, in common all fulfill, through which you bear one another; as,
so-and-so is irascible, and you are sleepy; bear then his vehement impulse,
that he himself also may bear your sluggishness. And so neither will that one
sin, being borne by you, nor do you offend in the things in which you are
heavy, your brother enduring you. And so stretching a hand to one another in
the things in which you are going to fall, through one another in common
fulfill the law, each filling up what is lacking to the neighbor through his
own endurance. And if you do not so, but each is going to go after the things
of the neighbor, never will any of the needed things be yours. For just as in
the body if someone should demand the same use from all, the body will never
stand; so also in the case of the brethren


there is much war, if we seek all things from all. For if someone thinks he is

contracts him with the fear; having said above, Let him test his work, and

especially one must pride oneself. Through whom the world has been crucified
to me, and I to the world. He means the world now, not the heaven nor the
earth, but the things of life, the praise from men, the escort, the glory, the
wealth, all such things that seem to be splendid. For these have become dead
to me. Such must the Christian be, always to carry about this voice. For he
was not even content only with the former manner of the putting to death, but
he also added another, saying: And I to the world; hinting at a double death,
and saying that Both those things are dead to me, and I to those, and neither
can they take me and master me; for they are dead once; nor can I desire them;
for I too am dead to them. Nothing is more blessed than this putting to death.
For this is the subject of the blessed life. For neither circumcision avails
anything, nor uncircumcision, but a new creation. And as many as shall walk by
this rule, peace upon them and mercy, and upon the Israel of God. Did you see
a power of the cross, unto how great a height it has raised him? For it has
not only put to death for him all the matters of the world, but has also
constituted him much higher than the old way of life. What is equal to this
strength? For the one who was being slaughtered for this circumcision and
slaughtering others, this one the cross has persuaded, having left it in equal
with the uncircumcision, to seek some strange and paradoxical things and the
matters above the heavens. For he calls this new creation our way of life,
both because of the things that have happened, and because of the things that
will be; the things that have happened, because our soul, having grown old in
the old age of sin, has all at once been renewed through the baptism, as if
created again; therefore we are demanded a new and heavenly way of life; and
because of the things that will be, because both the heaven and the earth and
the whole creation will be transferred into incorruption with our bodies. Do
not then tell me, he says, circumcision that can do nothing anymore; for how
will this appear, all things having been changed unto so much? but seek the
new matters of the grace. For those who pursue these things, these will also
enjoy peace and lovingkindness, and would properly be called by the name of
Israel; as those who think the contrary things, even if they have been born
from that one, and carry about his title, have fallen from all =61.680= these
things, both from the kinship and from the title itself. For those who can
properly be Israelites are those who keep this rule,


those who hold off from the old things, and pursue the things of the grace.
For the rest, let no one furnish me toils. Here he does not utter these things
as having toiled, nor as having lost heart; for the one who chooses both to do
and to suffer all things for the disciples, how would he have grown weary and
fallen back now? the one who says: Be instant in season, out of season; the
one who says: If perhaps God may give them knowledge of truth, and they may
sober up from the snare of the devil. Why then does he say these things?
Checking their slack mind, and throwing them into more fear, and fixing the
laws set down by him, and not allowing them always to move them. For I bear
the marks of our Lord Jesus Christ in my body. He did not say, I have, but, I
bear, as someone thinking greatly over trophies, or royal signs; even if this
again seems to be a reproach. But this man prides himself in the wounds; and
just as the standard-bearing of the soldiers, so also this man rejoices
carrying about wounds. And why does he say this? Through these I make a
defense more splendid than every argument, every voice, he says.
For these send forth a voice higher than a trumpet toward those who
contradict, and say that I play a part in the doctrine, and say something
toward a pleasing of men. For neither if someone saw a soldier come out from a
battle stained with blood, and having ten thousand wounds, would he endure to
judge him of some cowardice and betrayal, bearing on the body the proof of the
valor. And in my case then one must so vote, he says. And if someone wishes to
hear my defense, and to thoroughly learn my mind, let him look at the wounds,
furnishing more proof than these words and the letters. Beginning the Epistle,
from his sudden change he clearly showed his unhypocritical mind; and ending,
from the dangers of this. For lest anyone say that he transferred from a right
mind, but did not remain upon the same choice, that he also remained he brings
as witnesses the toils, the dangers, the stripes. Then having made a defense
through all things clearly, and having shown that he has said none of the
things said in wrath and enmity, but has unmoved the affection toward them, he
constructs this very thing again, closing the argument into a prayer full of
ten thousand goods, and speaking thus: The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be
with your spirit, brethren. Amen. With this last saying he has sealed all the
things before. For not simply, With you, as in the case of the others, but,
With your spirit, leading them away from the fleshly things, and everywhere
showing the benefit of God, and reminding of the grace which they have
enjoyed, through which he was sufficient to lead them away from all Jewish
deceit. For both the receiving the Spirit was not of the legal poverty, but of
the righteousness according to the faith; and the holding it having received
it came not from the circumcision, but from the grace again. For this reason
he closed the advice with a prayer, and having reminded of grace and spirit,
at once also having addressed them as brethren, and having asked God that they
continually enjoy these things, and having made the men secure in a double
=61.681= manner; for the same was both a prayer, and a complete teaching of
the things said, becoming for them instead of a double wall. For the teaching,
reminding them of how many goods they have enjoyed, has held them more in the
doctrines =61.682= of the Church; and the prayer, calling the grace, and
persuading it to remain with them, did not allow their spirit to stand off.
And this being in them, all the deceit of such doctrines was shaken off, like
dust, in Christ Jesus our Lord, to whom be the glory and the might, with the
Father and the holy Spirit, now and always, and unto the ages of the ages.
Amen.
