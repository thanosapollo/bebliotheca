* On Saint Antony
:PROPERTIES:

:END:

Of the most blessed Hesychius, presbyter of Jerusalem, an encomium on our holy
father Antony.
1 At the memory of the righteous the world also rejoices, at the memory of the
righteous the world is also enlightened, at the memory of the righteous
humanity also leaps, at the memory of the righteous everyone who hears also
rejoices. Now Antony is remembered and we are all enlightened by the memory,
Antony the citizen of the heavens, the teacher of every virtue, the one who in
the flesh imitated the bodiless, the lamp of the churches, the trainer of the
priests, the flower of virginity, the radiance of chastity, the crown of those
in "mountains and caves", the pattern of endurance, the worker of continence,
the unconquered boxer, the pillar of the prophets, the mirror of the apostles,
the one mighty with God and men, who has nothing upon earth but looks wholly
toward heaven, stretching his eyes upward and hastening the course toward that
prize.
2 Faithful as Abraham, as Isaac obedient, enduring as Jacob, as Joseph feeding
Egypt with grain, like Moses charioteering by teaching wonders of every kind,
he sang as David and drove out the demons, he offered himself as a greater
temple to God, as Daniel he commanded the lions, as the Baptist he gathers the
desert into a church, as the Theologian "reclining upon the breast of Jesus"
through evangelical study, "dying daily" as Paul.
3 But indeed we too draw, and the winepress overflows, the jars are full, "the
vats overflow"; as far as we run, the stadium becomes greater; as far as the
voyage is accomplished, so much is the immensity of the sea made plain. Come
then, let us too say the words of Paul: "Time will fail me as I narrate
concerning Gideon, Barak, Samson, Jephthah, David and Samuel and the
prophets"; for the father of the assembled spectacles took these up in
himself; for there is need of many words, and I am a guide to the deeds, since
speech grows weary explaining the work. One thing is the stamp of a shadow,
another the energy of truth. An image is one thing, and another is that of
which what is shown happens to be the image; words portray the work, but the
tongue does not speak as the hand does good. One thing is the voice of words,
another the fragrance of achievements; that one weighs upon the air, this one
reaches heaven and fully assures those above, this one stands present to those
above.
4 "Time will fail me, he says, as I narrate." And if time failed Paul as he
spoke the achievements of the saints, Paul in whom Christ was speaking, in
whom the fullness of the church was attained, an unfailing treasure of
knowledge was hidden (he was a mine of knowledge, he searched from above the
law and the prophets, he had a tongue trumpeting in the whole world as in one
theater), what shall we poor and needy do? What shall we do, whose speech is
untaught, whose tongue is weak, mind slow and heart uneducated, who have
neither learned the outer knowledge, nor are we exercised even in the least in
the inner?
5 There is no way of life drawing frankness of speech, nor a life speaking in
place of words. From where shall we weave for Antony the tunic of praises? Let
the kithara of David now crown him; for concerning him and his holy works it
has been written in the heading of a book: "Blessed is the man who fears the
Lord, he will greatly delight in his commandments; his seed shall be mighty
upon the earth"; for his seed is the companies of the saints throughout Egypt,
or rather throughout the whole earth. They consider him father and farmer,
they order their own lives by his patterns and consider the absent one present
through the keeping of the laws. One word of those trained in succession, if
it is preserved, is sufficient to establish a way of life from philosophy. Let
Isaiah the prophet also speak concerning him: "Blessed is he who has seed in
Zion and kinsmen in Jerusalem."
6 Let these words of the prophecy of Jeremiah also be spoken concerning
Antony: "Before I formed you in the belly, I know you, and before you came
forth from the womb I have sanctified you"; for he practiced holiness as a
companion from youth. From the womb he possessed holy humility as an
inheritance, and he set as the beginning of philosophy the temperance that is
through Christ. Come, let us also from the history of Job clothe the head of
the righteous man with this crown: "For there was a man in the land" toward
Egypt, Antony by name, "a man blameless, true, God-fearing, righteous,
abstaining from every evil deed," and "that man was noble among those from the
risings of the sun." Of what sun? Of righteousness; for the apostles were that
one's risings, whose kinship Antony had; let the mantle of nobility be called
that of the Spirit.
7 And Solomon himself can also speak a proverb concerning this righteous man:
"A merciful man is a great thing and honorable, but a faithful man is a work
to find." We have found then, we have found the one sought by the wise man,
but he said it was a work to find a faithful man, and Paul not so. But what
does he say? "Who through faith overcame kingdoms," hinting at Antony and his
zealots; for this man, despising the kingdoms of the world, glory, honor and
wealth, wrestled them down, "obtained promises" of what is to come, "stopped
the mouths of lions" of the pleasures, "escaped the mouths of the sword" the
outer wisdom, "was strengthened from weakness" of incontinence, "became mighty
in war" against the devil, "turned to flight the camps of aliens" of the
demons. "Women received their dead from resurrection," as many mothers as
brought sinful children to the righteous man.
8 Yet also he "was tortured," "died by slaughter of the sword," "took trial of
bonds and prison and of scourgings," "was stoned, was sawn"; for while many
suffered these things for piety, he himself suffered with all as father of
all; "he went about in sheepskins, in goatskins," emulating Elijah in
meekness; "being in want" in the flesh and "afflicted," for he knew that
afflictions are the road of the kingdom of the heavens; "being ill-treated,"
since Antony preferred rather "to be ill-treated with the people of God than
to have a temporary enjoyment of sin." "The world was not worthy of him";
therefore while still living he transferred himself into heaven. Then let us
also go through the great body of the prophets. Let Christ the head seal the
encomia in our hands, saying even now: "the kingdom of the heavens is violent
and the violent seize it"; for Antony melted the body, bridled the soul,
chastised the pleasures, abstained from luxury, was unsparing of satiety, used
sleep only so much as to benefit nature, considered even the name of
fornication abominable, enjoyed little of the earth. And he was wholly toward
heaven, wholly of the life above, considering the things here a loss. He lived
by the virtues alone, being dead to the passions.
9 He had the enemy as a plaything, the devil fell at his feet. Trusting, he
"went up upon asp and basilisk, he trampled the noetic lion and dragon." He
considered temptations a gain, afflictions a commerce. He thought joy to be in
mourning over sins, wealth in being poor. He had all things, since he had
nothing. He was lord of all, for he was slave to none. He looked down on all
things, for he needed nothing. Let us too come into zeal for him, as many of
us as have gathered today at the festival of his holy memory, as many as
marvel at his august life, and as many as from faith celebrate his blessed
falling-asleep, by the grace and love of mankind of our Lord Jesus Christ,
with whom to the Father be glory, honor, might, with the Holy Spirit, now and
always and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
