* On the Resurrection of the Lord
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of our father among the saints, Leontius, presbyter of Constantinople, on the
resurrection of the Lord.
It is timely and wholly harmonious, and proper to the present season, that we,
persuaded by the sacred psalmist David, should say what we have just been
singing: This is the day which the Lord has made; let us rejoice and be glad
in it. Truly let us rejoice and be glad, because we have seen what we longed
for, because we have handled what we sought, because we have beheld what we
expected, because the spring of the Christians has risen, because the flowers
of the holy have sprung up, because the lilies of the newly illumined have
been lifted high, because the children of the font shine. Truly this is the
day which the Lord has made; let us rejoice and be glad in it. O day, day of
every day; O day that has ended the darkness of ignorance and made known the
light of knowledge; O day that has made worthless the night of the synagogue
and shown forth the dawn of the resurrection; O day that has abolished the
gloom of the unbelievers and calmed the word of the faithful; O day that has
made known king and private citizen alike; O day that has plundered the devil
and left no one a debtor; O day that has shown those who were formerly slaves
now as priests of the masters; O day that has presented so many rational doves
at the altar. Where now do the ravens of the Sabbatians feed? Where are the
vain problems of those who stole the Pascha? When did the devil suddenly lose
children, then or now? When did the Master's net of teaching take alive so
many rational nestlings, then or now? When did the heaven of the resurrection
display so many rational stars upon the earth, then or now? When did the
lights watered with oil and wax outstrip the rays of the sun, then or now?
When did the windows of houses, and gateways and vestibules, wear so great a
rank of lamps, then or now? Do the words not persuade you, you thief of the
Pascha? Let the tidings fully assure you. Do you not see today all things
under heaven being adorned with bright robes? But you neither at what you call
your Pascha, out of fear, put on a clean garment, nor now at the public feast,
out of shame, have you clothed yourself in a bright robe. How then did you
keep the feast, you who have never been clothed in clean things? Yet someone
among them may well say: What then? Does a clean garment save a man? If one
does not put on the robe while keeping the feast, does he not keep the feast?
Hear, you who say this: in the proper sense a clean garment does not save a
man, but a radiant soul and a virtuous life; yet in every way the one who
keeps the feast of the resurrection must wear the garment in a manner matching
the feast, according to what he has, not according to what he has not. For so
also today among us even the poor are adorned, by intention, with royal robes.
Therefore the sons of the resurrection must be bright-white and clean both in
the garment of the soul and in that of the body, lest, if we are found
unprepared, we hear from the bridegroom Christ: Friend, how did you come in
here without a wedding garment? For so also Christ himself, the trophy-bearer
of the resurrection, when he announced the resurrection on the mountain of the
transfiguration, not only showed his face bright-white, but also made his
clothing flash beyond measure, so that one of the evangelists, recalling it,
says: And his garments became glistening, white as snow, such as no fuller on
earth can whiten. For this reason the sons of the resurrection also, as you
see, wear not only a bright robe but also shoes


of like form, having put off the oldness of Adam and having put on the newness
of the Master Christ, according to the blessed Paul who says: As many of you
as were baptized into Christ have put on Christ. And that in the resurrection
we must possess all things white and bright-white, not only on earth but also
in the heavens, you heard Matthew saying: Late on the sabbath, as it began to
dawn toward the Lord's day, Mary Magdalene and the other Mary came to the tomb
of Jesus, and behold, there was a great earthquake; for an angel, having come
down from heaven, rolled away the stone; he sat upon it; and his appearance
was as lightning and his garment white as snow.
Truly this is the day which the Lord has made; let us rejoice and be glad in
it, because no longer does the yoke of the law weigh heavy, but the lightness
of grace consoles. Let us rejoice and be glad in it, because no longer does
the ark save Noah alone, but he who is from the Virgin saves the whole world.
Let us rejoice and be glad in it, because no longer is a gigantic house
overthrown, but a house of prayer is raised; because no longer does fire rain
upon Sodom, but a rain bedews the Virgin; because no longer is Lot's wife
formed into a pillar of salt, but on a day of light the Lord's bride is
glorified. This is the day which the Lord has made; let us rejoice and be glad
in it. Let us be glad; for the Jews mourn, not having been persuaded by David
the prophet, who from many years ago declared concerning the incorrupt
resurrection of the Lord and spoke thus: His soul was not left in Hades, nor
did his flesh see corruption. What do you say, O Jew? If another rose from the
dead incorrupt, say so and we believe. But if there is no other except the
Master Christ, who from many years ago cried through the prophet to the
Gentile people: Wait for me until the day of my resurrection, says the Lord,
for my power is among the assemblies of the nations, why do you not run to
him, but flee? This is the day which the Lord has made; let us rejoice and be
glad in it. Let us be glad; for the children of the Pharisees mourn. Why? Not
having been persuaded by the patriarch Jacob, who from many years ago cried
out concerning the kingly resurrection of the Lord and spoke thus: He lay down
and slept as a lion and as a lion's cub; who shall raise him? What do you say,
O Jew? Give us another who rose in kingly fashion after the likeness of a
lion, and we believe what you say. But if there is no other except the Master
Christ alone, who slept as a lion for the three days and rose as a cub, as one
may hear the Lord himself saying from many years before the incarnation: I am
as a panther to Ephraim and as a lion to the house of Judah, why do you not
worship the one who has risen? Yet someone may well say: And why did the Lord
act in the tomb after the likeness of the lion? Why? Because of the natural
habit of the lion: a lion sleeping in its own cave, in which it sleeps, has
its eyes open; in the same way the Master Christ also, having slept in death
for three days, did not close the eyes of his divinity. To him be the glory
and the power unto the ages of ages. Amen.
