ΙΑʹ. Why does he use the subdivisions in the genealogy, not joining together
at once from Abraham to Christ forty-two generations, but dividing the
successions according to the distinctions he set out.
Because of the different conditions of the people presented from the history.
For one was that from Abraham even to David, and again another that from
David, which also


as far as the captivity, as again that from this until Christ. For from
Abraham to David they do not appear to have been governed under kings; but
after Moses and Joshua those called among them judges ruled the nation; and
there was a certain proper manner of their descent. But neither had Jerusalem
been established in any way, nor yet the temple in it. Therefore the
evangelist, knowledgeably not confusing the history, stopped as far as David,
having numbered those from Abraham separately; then again, since from David
and as far as the captivity they used their own kings, both those who began
from David and those who came to be in the division of the people; and the
sanctuary in Jerusalem lasted from that time even as far as the captivity,
reasonably having distinguished these too again separately, he brought them
together under one number; just as also those from the captivity until Christ,
among whom the kingdom of David no longer stood, but the things of the
government had passed from the tribe of Judah to the race of the priests, who
also ruled them from the times of Cyrus even to the birth of Christ; hence
having distinguished these too separately, he numbered them by themselves.
Not, then, without calculation has he made the three distinctions, because of
the causes that have been given.

ΙΒʹ. Why, when from the times of David to Jeconiah and the captivity into
Babylon seventeen had reigned, the evangelist says that there are fourteen
generations.
αʹ. If it had been set before him to write down successions =22.924= even
reasonably someone might have blamed him as having set forth the succession of
the kings amiss. For in the Kingdoms and in the Chronicles in agreement, after
Joram the son of Jehoshaphat, three having reigned in succession, Ochozias and
Joash and Amasias, then after them Ozias and Joatham and Achaz, the
evangelist, having passed over the former three, after Joram the son of
Jehoshaphat joins next Ozias and Joatham and Achaz, having passed over those
said in between. And if he had done this having set as his aim to set out the
succession of the kings, it would have been necessary to set forth the writing
with him as having erred. But since he proposed not to number successions but
generations, for this is what his statement means when he said "Therefore all
the generations from Abraham until David, generations fourteen; and again from
David until Jeconiah and the migration of Babylon, generations fourteen" but
not successions fourteen, reasonably he would be released from every
accusation; since why did he not name successions, and the history in the
Kingdoms and Chronicles relating successions but not generations, there would
not come to be a contradiction from the juxtaposition of both.
βʹ. For it is not possible to name a generation a time of a man's life, since
it happens often that some live for a short while and are quenched more
quickly at the age of the child, and some reach as far as the lad, and some
proceed to the young man, and some to the man, and some even extend life to
extreme old age. What generation, then, will one number? If one brings it as
far as the tenth year, another as far as the twentieth, another as far as the
fiftieth, another as far as the seventieth, and another is seen even having
exceeded the hundred, not only among the ancients but also among us; how then
do they think to name a man's life a generation? Nor even as far as the
begetting of children: for some, having married before the twenty years, have
begotten children, and some not even when they have come beyond the thirty;
and of those of equal age you might see some having stopped at first sons, and
some having driven on to a fourth generation; so that in fifty years some have
seen grandchildren, and others in seventy have been deemed worthy of no child.
How then are the generations to be numbered? Whether from the long-lived or
from the short-lived? And from those


who have quickly begotten children, or from the slow? And from those who have
come to a stand at first sons, or from those upon more successions? These
things having been examined thus, the divine evangelist, not having proposed
to speak of successions, but of generations, numbering according to the
accounts he himself knew, has taken less thought for the succession in the
histories, and takes up into the genealogy as many as sufficed for him for the
fulfillment of the fourteen generations; and thus the account is preserved
sound for him, and in no way contrary to the writing of the histories.

ΙΓʹ. Why, of those genealogized after Jeconiah to Joseph being twelve, the
evangelist says again that there are fourteen.
αʹ. For the same cause: for he wished to write down not successions, but
generations, as I said. And it happens often among the long-lived and
many-yeared, that the successions of the men have come to be, but the number
of the generations has been rendered full. By which account, then, among those
from David even to the captivity, who were more in number among those borne in
the succession, the generations were rendered fewer: for in successions of
seventeen men, generations have been said to be fourteen; according to the
same reckoning now also upon successions of twelve men, the fourteen
generations would have been filled out, they themselves, the twelve, having as
is likely become long-lived and of many years, and sufficing for the
filling-up of the fourteen generations. This is one account of the thing
sought.
βʹ. And according to another sense you would find, having examined accurately
according to the history, fourteen named also in the present succession, if in
addition to the twelve you should number Jesus Christ himself, who was styled
son of Joseph; and should add to these also Jeconiah who was born in Babylon,
not the one who reigned in Jerusalem before the migration. For two homonyms,
Joakim, came to be after Josiah, both his, Josiah's, son, who after this one
reigned in Jerusalem, and this one's child, another Joakim; and these also
were styled Jeconiahs, their name having been Hellenized. Let the first
Joakim, who is also Jeconiah, being son of Josiah, be counted together with
the generations before the captivity; and this one's child, the second Joakim,
himself also Jeconiah, being son of the first Joakim, and grandson of Josiah,
being numbered among those genealogized after the captivity and unto Christ,
would render complete the number of the fourteen generations.
γʹ. And concerning there having been two Joakims, the Scripture of the
Kingdoms will bear witness, having it in this manner: "And Pharaoh Nechao made
to reign over Israel Eliakim son of Josiah king of Judah, in place of Josiah
his father; and he turned his name to Joakim." To which he next adds: "Joakim
was a son of twenty-five years when he began to reign; and he reigned eleven
years in Jerusalem"; to which after other things he adds: "And the rest of the
words of Joakim, and all that he did, are they not written upon the book of
the words of the days of the kings of Judah? And Joakim slept with his
fathers, and Joakim his son reigned in his stead; Joakim was a son of eighteen
years when he began to reig =22.928= n; and he reigned three months in
Jerusalem; and the name of his mother was Estha; and he did what was evil in
the eyes of the Lord according to all that his father had done. In that time
Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon went up to Jerusalem; and the city came into
siege; and the king of Babylon entered into the city, and his servants were
besieging it; and Joakim king of Judah went out to the king


of Babylon, he and his servants, and his mother, and his eunuchs; and he led
away the strong of the land as a colony from Jerusalem to Babylon." This
second Joakim, then, having been led away to Babylon, this one was himself the
one named Jeconiah by Jeremiah, being grandson of Josiah, but not son;
therefore he would reasonably be numbered together in the third genealogy of
the fourteen generations from Jeconiah until Christ; his father, who was child
of Josiah, being numbered with the father in the generations above; and thus
for us the number also of the last fourteen generations is constituted full.

ΙΔʹ. Why our Savior was styled son of the carpenter, but not of some notable
and glorious man.
He did not come in order to display his divine kingdom; since neither did he
come to us ostentatiously and for show. And the way of his arrival into the
heavens was coming to be for this, that he might thoroughly cleanse the life
of men, himself giving himself over as the Lamb of God from the flock of men,
a ransom and a purification on behalf of us all. In order then that this might
be brought to an end without hindrance, he both hid and overshadowed the
greater part of his wonders, at one time exhorting not to carry out to all the
things done by him, at another time pursuing the deserts and the sojourns in
the mountains; and his divine transfiguration he displayed not even to all his
disciples, but to three alone; and he commanded even these to tell the vision
to no one, until the Son of man should be raised from the dead. And he would
not have suffered on our behalf the things that have been written, if as some
glorious king he had come on with a divine bodyguard and array, at once both
working the divine wonder-workings, and at once showing himself greater than
every nature. Reasonably therefore according to the Apostle, "he emptied
himself, taking the form of a slave"; and he did not refuse to be styled son
of the poor Joseph; in order that in this way too the saying concerning him
might prove true: "That being rich, he became poor for our sake, that we by
his poverty might become rich."
ΙΕʹ. How he is said to have sat upon the throne of David. αʹ. The throne of
David is understood in different ways. According to one manner, according to
which one might say that there is indicated that upon which =22.929= he sat
while reigning, perhaps somehow made of ivory and woods, and adorned with gold
and royal stones; and according to another, according to which we are
accustomed to call the rule itself and the government over the whole nation a
throne of kingdom; a third manner besides those that have been said might be
spoken, according to which the throne promised by God to David might be named
his, not that upon which he himself sat, but that which the divine words
contain through the things spoken to him in a manner befitting God. For it is
written in the eighty-eighth psalm: "As I swore to David my servant, unto the
age I will prepare your seed; and I will build up unto generation and
generation your throne." And again: "And I will set unto the age of the age
his seed, and his throne as the days of heaven." And again: "Once I swore in
my holy one, if I shall lie to David, his seed remains unto the age, and his
throne as the sun before me, and as the moon established unto the age, and the
witness in heaven is faithful."
βʹ. Since then God had promised through the things that have been said that he
would give to David a throne as the days of heaven, and remaining as the age,
there was on account of these things a great expectation for the whole nation
of the Jews concerning the throne that had been indicated; but David having
governed a short time, and Solomon after him, and indeed also the successors
of their kingdom to Jeconiah and the captivity into Babylon


having come to an end, so that from that time the throne of the kingdom of
David had been dissolved, the things of the promise of the divine oracles
seemed not to stand. This very thing then again the same psalm, by the divine
Spirit, foretells next after the things previously set forth, saying: "Where
are your ancient mercies, Lord, which you swore to David in your truth?" And
the taking-down of his kingdom, and of his succession, and the overthrow of
the throne, he indicates expressly again next through these: "But you have
thrust away and set at nought, Lord; you have put off your Christ, you have
overthrown the covenant of your servant, you have profaned his sanctuary to
the earth"; and he adds: "you have broken his throne down to the earth."
γʹ. All these things the Spirit gathers together at the same time, all but
wishing to teach us that the promises to David were not concerning the
sensible kingdom, nor concerning the throne understood more corporeally; for
the ones prophesied beforehand concerning some eternal throne, likened to sun
and heaven, and remaining unto an age; but the sensible kingdom of David had
been dissolved in time not far off. Reasonably therefore, having sketched the
taking-down of the throne of the sensible kingdom of David, and having said,
you have broken his throne down to the earth, he next makes the prayer
concerning the eternal and heavenly throne promised through the divine
oracles, saying: "Where are your ancient mercies, Lord, which you swore to
David in your truth?" all but demanding that the outcomes of the things
promised to him with confirmation of an oath attain an end.
δʹ. This very one then, that throne which God swore he would give to David,
the one as the days of heaven, and the one as the sun before God, and the one
as a moon established unto the age, when the whole nation was praying that it
should stand over them, the great angel Gabriel announces good news to the
Virgin, prophesying that it would be given to the one who would be born from
her. Therefore he says to her: "And you shall call his name Jesus; this one
will be great, and will be called Son of the Highest; and the Lord God will
give him the throne of David his father." He adds then next and says: "And he
will reign over the house of Jacob unto the ages; and of his kingdom there
will be no end"; interpreting things in agreement with those from the oracles.
For the one was foretelling to David concerning a throne of an eternal and
heavenly kingdom, and the other likewise says that the one who would be born
from the Virgin will receive the throne of David, that is, the one promised to
David, but not indeed also given; and this was the heavenly one remaining unto
an age. This too then was a conclusion of a very great prophecy that had been
oracled to David, and was expected by the whole people, and has been fulfilled
upon our Savior Jesus Christ, according to the testimony of Gabriel who said:
"And he will reign unto the ages, and of his kingdom there will be no end."
Therefore also our Savior and Lord himself, to the one who asked him whether
he himself were the king of the Jews, answered: "My kingdom is not of this
world"; for nothing mortal nor timely was brought upon him by the throne of
the kingdom; but it was truly over the whole inhabited world of men, shining
forth after the manner of light, as the moon established unto the age,
illuminating intellectual souls through his divine and heavenly teaching.
εʹ. And if he should be said to reign over Jacob, do not think that the nation
of the Jews is indicated through Jacob. Luke the evangelist, at any rate, who
relates these things, after the taking-up of our Savior into the heavens,
having handed down the voice of Gabriel in his own writing, knew clearly that
Jesus the Savior had not reigned over the nation of the Jews, nor had he ruled
them unto the ages, whose plot against him, and whose design unto death, he
also relates accurately; and not only, but also in the Acts of the apostles
their uprisings against the disciples of Jesus


of them. He would not then, having thought that the voice of Gabriel had said
that the Christ would reign over them, have received it as true, unless,
adopting all those who through the calling of our Savior from all the nations
are brought into the sonship of the saints, he had considered the house of
Jacob to be indicated according to the sense. Hence also the divine Apostle,
knowing most clearly, presents it saying: "For he is not a Jew who is one in
the open, nor is circumcision that which is in the open in the flesh, but he
who is a Jew in secret, and circumcision of heart in spirit, not in letter,
whose praise is not from men, but from God."

Ιςʹ. How from Bethlehem Matthew relates that Jesus was borne into Egypt, but
Luke into Jerusalem, and from there into Nazareth, by the parents.
αʹ. Luke, relating the time of the birth of our Savior, makes mention of the
kingdom of Augustus and of the registration according to him; and he says that
they had not even had lodging in Bethlehem, a multitude being present, as is
likely, in Bethlehem of those from the race of David for the sake of the
registration; therefore Joseph was not even well supplied with a house; hence
he says that Mary, having given birth, swaddled the infant and laid it in a
manger, because there was not a place for them in the lodging; and it was
likely, with very many being present because of the registration, that they
were not well supplied with a place of sojourn, but also, "When," he says,
"the days were fulfilled for him to be circumcised" and this must come to be
on the eighth day after the birth, "they brought the child up to Jerusalem,
and having completed upon him the things that were customary, they go away to
Nazareth." Of these things lying in Luke, Matthew, having made mention of
none, and having yielded the things that have been said to Luke, himself
narrates other things. And what were these, but the arrival of the Magi from
the East? who set out from their own land at the same time as Jesus was born,
a star having indicated to them the knowledge of the birth, but who had not, I
suppose, accomplished so great a journey in a short time; for it is not likely
that in eight days the road from the East to Judaea had been completed for
them, so as to think that the time of their arrival and of the birth of our
Savior was the same. And if also, inquiring, they say, "Where is the one born
king of the Jews? for we have seen his star, and have come to worship him,"
they do not indicate the one born today, as someone might suppose, according
to the time at which they were inquiring these things, but the one who came to
be then when the star appeared to them.
βʹ. And how great this intervening time was, both of the star that appeared to
the Magi at the birth of our Savior, and of their presence at Jerusalem, the
evangelist himself will teach you, saying: "Then Herod, having called the Magi
secretly, inquired accurately from them the time of the appearing star." And
as, having inquired accurately

labor-loving son Stephanus, being genuine proofs of disposition, be dedicated.
