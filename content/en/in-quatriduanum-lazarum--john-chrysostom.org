On Lazarus of the Four Days
Ninth Discourse.
=48.779=

1. Today Lazarus, being raised from the dead, grants us the resolution of many
and various scandals. For I do not know how this reading has given both a
handle to the heretics and an occasion of contradiction to the Jews, not from
the truth, God forbid, but from their unscrupulous soul. For many of the
heretics say that the Son is not like the Father. Why? Because, he says,
Christ needed prayer in order to raise Lazarus; for if he had not prayed, he
would not have raised Lazarus. And how, he says, will the one who prayed be
like the one who received the supplication? For the one prays, and the other
received the prayer from the one who was entreating. But they blaspheme, not
understanding that the prayer was of condescension, and for the sake of the
weakness of those present. For tell me, who is greater, the one who washes the
feet, or he whose feet he washes?

Surely he is greater whose feet the washer washed. But the Savior washed the
feet of the traitor Judas; for he was among the disciples. Is the traitor
Judas then greater than the Master Christ, since Christ washed his feet? God
forbid. And what is more humble, to wash the feet, or to pray? Surely to wash
the feet. He then who did not refuse to do what is more humble, how would he
have refused to do what is higher? But everything happened because of the
weakness of the Jews who were present, as the discourse will show as it
proceeds. =48.780= But the Jews also, taking from this an occasion of
contradiction, say: How do the Christians hold this man to be God, who did
not even know the place where Lazarus lay dead? since the Savior said to
Martha and Mary, the sisters of Lazarus, Where have you laid him? Did
you see, he says, ignorance? Did you see weakness? He who did not even know
the place, is this man God? But I will speak to them, not as holding this, but
wishing to put their objection to shame. Christ did not know, you say, O Jew,
because he said, Where have you laid him? Then the Father also did not know in
paradise where Adam was hidden; for he went about as if seeking him in
paradise, saying: Adam, where are you? that is, where did you hide?

Why did he not first name the place from which Adam used to speak with God
with boldness? Adam, where are you? And what does that one say? I heard your
voice as you walked in paradise, and I was afraid, because I am naked, and I
hid myself. If you call this ignorance, O Jew, call that ignorance too; for
Christ said to Martha and Mary, Where have you laid him? Do you
then call this ignorance? What then do you say when you hear God saying to
Cain, Where is Abel your brother? What do you say? If you call this ignorance,
=48.781= call that ignorance too. Take also another proof from the divine
Scripture. God said to Abraham, The cry of Sodom and Gomorrah has come to me.
I will therefore go down and see whether they are doing altogether according
to their cry that is coming to me; and if not, that I may know. He who knows
all things before their generation, God who searches hearts and inward parts,
he who alone knows the thoughts of men, said: I will therefore go down and see
whether they are doing altogether according to their cry that is coming to me;
and if not, that I may know. If that is of ignorance, this too is to be
ignorant. But neither was the Father ignorant according to the old
Covenant, nor the Son according to the new Covenant. What then is, I will
go down and see whether they are doing altogether according to their cry that
is coming to me; and if not, that I may know? A report, he says, has come to
me.
But I wish again to take the proof more exactly through the facts themselves,
not because I am ignorant, but because I wish to teach men not to attend
simply to words, nor, if someone says something against another, to believe
readily; but they must first feel the matter out exactly and learn the proof
through the facts themselves, and so believe. And for this reason he said in
another Scripture: Do not believe every word. For nothing so overturns the
life of men as for someone to believe reasonings quickly. This the prophet
David also said, prophesying: Him who secretly slandered his neighbor, him I
drove out.

2. You see how there was no ignorance in the Savior in saying, Where have you
laid him, just as there was none in the Father in saying to Adam, Where are
you, or to Cain, Where is Abel your brother, or, I will go down and see
whether they are doing altogether according to their cry that is coming to me;
and if not, that I may know. It is time, then, at last to array ourselves
against those who say that it was through weakness that Christ, having prayed,
raised Lazarus. Attend then, beloved, with all exactness, I beg you. Lazarus
then died, and Jesus was not in those places, but in Galilee, and he said to
his disciples: Lazarus our friend has fallen asleep. But they, thinking that
he was speaking of this sleep, say to him: Lord, if he has fallen asleep, he
will recover. Jesus says to them plainly, Lazarus has died. The Savior then
comes to Jerusalem, to the place where Lazarus lay, and the sister of Lazarus
meets him, and says to him: Lord, if you had been here, my brother would not
have died.

If you had been here. You are weak, O woman. The woman does not yet understand
that even when Christ was not present in the body, he was present by the
authority of the Godhead; but she measures the teacher's power by the presence
of the body. Martha says to him, Lord, if you had been here, my brother would
not have died. And now, she says, I know that whatever you ask of God, he
gives you. The Savior then makes the prayer in answer to her request. For God
did not need prayer in order to raise the dead man. Did he not also raise
other dead? When he met a dead man being carried out at the gate, he only
touched the bier, and raised the dead. Did he need prayer then in order to
raise the one who had died? And again at another time he said by word alone
=48.782= over the girl, Talitha koum, and at once he gave her back healthy to
her parents. Did he need prayer then? And why do I speak of the teacher? His
disciples raised the dead by word alone. Did Peter not raise Tabitha by a
word? Did Paul not work many signs through his garments?

Learn also what is more paradoxical than these. The shadow of the apostles
raised the dead. For they brought, it says, those who were sick on beds, that
at least the shadow of Peter might overshadow some of them, and at once they
were raised. What then? The shadow of the disciples raised the dead, and the
teacher needed prayer in order to raise the dead? But it is because of the
woman's weakness that the Savior makes the prayer; for she says to him, Lord,
if you had been here, my brother would not have died; and now I know that
whatever you ask of God, God gives you. You asked for a prayer, I give a
prayer. The fountain lies ready: as, if someone brings a vessel, it fills it;
if it is large, he receives a large measure; if it is small, he receives a
small measure. She then asked for a prayer, and the Savior gives a prayer.
Another said, I am not worthy that you should come under my roof, but only
speak the word, and my servant will be healed; and the Savior said to him:
According to your faith let it be done for you. Another said, Come, heal my
daughter; and he said to him, I will follow you. According to the dispositions
of men, then, the physician's healing is also applied.

Another again touched the hem of his garment in secret, and in secret she
reaped the healing; each received the healing as he believed. This woman says,
I know that whatever you ask of the Father, the Father gives you; and since
Martha asked for a prayer, the Savior gives a prayer, not himself needing the
prayer, but bearing with the woman's weakness, and that he might show that he
is not an adversary of God, but that whatever he himself does, this the Father
also does. God formed man from the beginning; the forming was of both; for Let
us make, he says, man according to our image and according to likeness. Again
he willed to bring the robber into paradise, and at once he spoke a word, and
brought the robber into paradise, and he did not need prayer, although Christ
had barred all after Adam from that entrance; for he had set the flaming sword
to guard paradise. But by his own authority Christ opened paradise, and
brought the robber in. A robber, Master, you bring into paradise? Your Father
for one sin only led Adam out of paradise, and you bring in the robber, who is
liable for countless evils and countless lawless deeds? and so simply, by a
single utterance, you bring him into paradise? Yes; for neither did that
happen without me, nor this without my Father; but this too =48.783= is mine,
and that is my Father's. For I am in the Father, and the Father in me.

3. And that you may see that the raising of the dead man was not a work of
prayer, hear the prayer. For what does he say? I thank you, Father, that you
have heard me. What then? Is this a form of prayer, is this a pattern of
supplication? I thank you that you have heard me. But I, he says, knew that
you always hear me. If then you know, O Lord, that the Father always hears
you, why do you still trouble him about things you know? I for my part, he
says, know that the Father always hears me. But I said it for the crowd
standing around, that all may know that you sent me. Did he pray at all
because of the dead man? Did he entreat that Lazarus might rise? Did he say:
Father, command death to obey? Did he say, Father, command Hades not to shut
the gates, but to give back the dead man readily? But I said it, he says, for
this crowd standing around, that all may know that you sent me. So then what
is taking place is not the sign, but instruction of those present. You see how
the prayer was not made because of the dead man, but because of the
unbelievers present, that they may know, he says, that you sent me. And how
shall we know, he says, that he sent you?

Attend, I beg you, with all exactness. See, he says, with my own authority I
call the dead; see, by my own power I command death; I call the Father Father;
I also call Lazarus from the tomb. If the first is not true, let this not
happen either; but if the Father is truly Father, let the dead man also obey,
for the teaching of those present. For what did Christ say? Lazarus, come
forth. When the prayer was made, the dead man did not rise; but when he said,
Lazarus, come forth, then the dead man rose. O tyranny of death; O tyranny of
that power that holds the soul; O Hades, a prayer has been made, and you do
not release the dead? No, he says. Why? Because I have not been commanded. I
am bound there, holding the one who is liable; =48.784= if I am not commanded,
he says, I do not release; for the prayer, he says, was not made for me, but
for the unbelievers present. For I, if I am not commanded, do not release the
one who is liable; I wait for the voice, that I may release the soul. Lazarus,
come forth.

The dead man heard the Master's command, and at once he dissolved the laws of
death. Let the heretics be put to shame, and let them perish from the face of
the earth. For the discourse has shown that the prayer was not made for the
resurrection of the dead man, but because of the weakness of the unbelievers
then present. Lazarus, come forth. And why did he call the dead man by name?
Why? Lest, stretching out the voice freely toward the dead, he should raise
all who were in the tombs; for this reason he says, Lazarus, come forth. For
the present I call you alone back before the crowd, that through the part I
may also display the power of the things to come; for I who raised one raise
the world; for I am the resurrection and the life. Lazarus, come forth. And
the dead man came out bound with grave-bands. O the paradoxical things: he who
loosed the soul from the bonds of death, who burst the gates of Hades, who
crushed gates of bronze and bars of iron, and freed the soul from the bonds of
death, could he not also loose the grave-bands of the dead man?

Yes, he was able; but he commands the Jews to loose the grave-bands which they
themselves had bound when they wrapped him for burial, that they might
recognize the tokens of their own bonds, that by experience they might learn,
from what they themselves had done, that it is Lazarus who was buried by them,
and that it is Christ who according to the Father's good pleasure came into
the world, who has authority over life and death. To him be the glory and the
might, with your unoriginate Father and your all-holy and life-giving Spirit,
now and always, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
