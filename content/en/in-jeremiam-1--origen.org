On the "and you shall say to this people: thus says the Lord the God of
Israel: every wineskin will be filled with wine" until "and your eyes will
bring down tears, because the flock of the Lord has been crushed". Homily 12.
12.1 What the prophet is commanded to say by God ought to be worthy of God,
[not] but it appears that it is not worthy of God, if we remain upon the
letter, so as for some other person who has heard the letter to say:
foolishness are these letters. But this the soulish man will say: for "the
soulish man does not receive the things of the Spirit of God; for it is
foolishness to him". See then the wording, what it says. "And you shall say to
this people: thus says the Lord the God of Israel" what "the Lord the God of
Israel" says, let it be worthy of the Lord the God of Israel: every wineskin
will be filled with wine; and it will be if they say to you: "not knowing
shall we not know that every wineskin will be filled with wine?" And those
answering <if> standing upon the wording say these things and say they have
known that "every wineskin will be filled with wine", they lie; for not "every
wineskin will be filled with wine". There are at any rate wineskins filled
with oil or another liquid substance, <and some also remain empty.> They lie
then; for not "every wineskin will be filled with wine". And the people
answers saying: "not knowing shall we not know that every wineskin will be
filled with wine?" Which things will obtain an interpretation of this sort
according to our power. If we know the differences of the wines and the things
said about them, and accordingly about the wineskins we shall see that it is
true that "every wineskin will be filled with wine". For whether someone is
good, that we may so name him, a wineskin among wineskins, he will be filled
with wine according to his own goodness. Or if he is, as in a comparison of
wineskins and the judgment concerning them, wicked, he too according to his
wickedness will be filled with wicked wine. How then is it possible to take
from the scripture concerning the different wines? Concerning the worse such
things have been written: "for from the vine of Sodom is their vine, and their
tendril from Gomorrah; their grape is a grape of gall, a cluster of bitterness
to them; the wrath of dragons is their wine, and the wrath of asps incurable".
But concerning the better: "your cup intoxicating me as strongest", and wisdom
summons to her own mixing-bowl saying: "come, eat my bread, and drink wine
which I have mixed for you". There is then wine from Sodom and there is wine
which wisdom mixes. And again: "a vineyard came to be for the beloved in a
horn in a fat place", planted by God, the one called vine Sorek, being some
Elect and Wonderful. And there is also a vine of Egyptians, which God strikes
according to what is written that "God struck with hail their vine, and their
sycamores with frost". 12.2 Understand then for me all men to be tropically
now capable of wine. And I name them according to this wineskins and I say
that the worthless one has been filled with "wine of the vine of Sodom", has
been filled with Egyptian wine and wine of the enemies of Israel, but the holy
and benefited one has been filled with wine from the vine Sorek and wine
concerning which it has been written: "your cup intoxicating as strongest",
and again the holy one has been filled with wine, from which wisdom mixed. Let
these things then be understood by me according to vice and virtue, in order
that "every wineskin is filled with wine" may be contemplated. But if it is
necessary also to see the things through vice and virtue, punishments the
things through vice, but blessings and promises the things through virtue, let
us present from the sacred letters, in what way the punishments and the
promises are called wine: "take the cup of this unmixed wine, and you shall
make all the nations drink toward whom I shall send you out toward them".
Jeremiah says these things; to whom he adds: "and they shall drink and vomit
and go mad and fall".


Therefore he named the punishments here unmixed wine, which those drink who
are worthy of unmixed wine, that is of unmixed punishment. And there are also
others who drink punishment not unmixed, but mixed; for "a cup is in the hand
of the Lord, of unmixed wine full of mixture, <and> he inclined from this to
this; but its dregs were not emptied out, all the sinners of the earth shall
drink". If you wish also to see a cup of blessing, which the righteous drink,
the one of wisdom would suffice, upon which she said: "drink wine which I
mixed for you", but see for me also the savior going up to the passover into
"a large upper room spread and adorned" and feasting with the disciples and
giving them a cup, concerning which it has been written not that he mixed; for
Jesus gladdening the disciples gladdens with unmixed and says to them: "take,
drink, this is my blood, that poured out for you for forgiveness of sins; do
this, as often as you drink, unto my remembrance", and: "amen I say to you, I
shall not drink it from now, until I drink it with you new in the kingdom of
God". You see the promise being the cup "of the new covenant", you see the
punishments a cup of unmixed wine, and another form of punishment a mixed cup;
so that for each there is mixed according to the worth of the useful deed
mixed with a wicked deed the things he will drink. And understand for me those
wholly alien from piety and in no way attending to themselves, but living as
it happens, drinking the unmixed wine concerning which we set out the things
from Jeremiah, but those not wholly having fallen away and sinners, but
un<worthy> of the cup "of the new covenant", doing at one time more useful at
another contrary <deeds>, drinking wine "of unmixed mixture"; for God
"inclined from this to this". Of what "this"? I see two cups according to what
is said: "he inclined from this to this but its dregs were not emptied out".
Understand for me the cup of your good works in the one hand of God; and if
you wish me to speak more boldly, let the cup of your good works be in the
right hand of God, then let your cup of sins be in the left hand of God.
Whenever then you are about to be punished because of the sins, since also
more useful works have come about for you, a cup "is in the hand of the Lord
of unmixed wine full of mixture; and he inclined from this to this", from the
one in the left into the one in the right. For you can neither drink only the
cup of the goods, as not having done only good works, nor can you drink only
the cup of the sins, for some useful things have been done by you. For this
reason "he inclined from this to this". According to the proportion of your
works the wrath and the punishment is mixed for you, in order that either the
thing of the punishment may be more watery for you for you or more pungent and
more toilsome. For according to the proportion, as I said before, of the sins
compared with the goods the thing from the cup of wrath is somehow blunted or
is not blunted † being given in some measure to each of the sins. But if you
are wholly fair and good, you say: "I will take the cup of salvation, and I
will call upon the name of the Lord". Therefore "every wineskin", whether fair
or wicked, "will be filled with wine", and according to the fitness of the
wineskin wine will be thrown into the wineskin according to the account of the
wineskins named here. Oil then is not thrown into the wineskins nor any other
liquid matter, but every wineskin must be filled with wine. 12.3 Then he
teaches on account of those who sinned, as far as concerns the wording, in
Jerusalem then and in Judaea, of what sort of wine God is about to fill these
wineskins, the sinners. For it has been written: "if they say to you: not
knowing shall we not know that every wineskin will be filled with wine? And
you shall say to them: thus says the Lord: behold I fill all those inhabiting
this land and the kings sitting sons of David upon his throne and the
priests". The one about to punish spares no one. Not because someone was
styled prophet, but has sins, will he not be filled with the so-called
threats. Not because someone was styled priest and seemed


to have superiority of a more honorable name beside the people, does God spare
him so that he is not punished having sinned. But the things written about
those, the apostle says, were written on account of us, "unto whom the ends of
the ages have arrived". If then someone also among these priests (and I point
to us the elders) or among these Levites standing around the people (and I
mean the deacons) sins, he will have this punishment; as again there are
certain priestly blessings, concerning which, God granting, not far off, but
after the examination of the prophetic word we shall know when Numbers is
being read; for concerning priests some things are about to be said there.
"And the priests" then "and the prophets and Judah and all those inhabiting
Jerusalem" God says he will fill "with drunkenness" and will scatter "them a
man and his brother, and their fathers <and their sons>". And let us
understand these things also thus: God gathers the righteous, but scatters the
sinners. For this reason also when the men were not moving from the east, God
did not scatter them; but when they moved "from the east" and "a man said to
his neighbor: come and let us build for ourselves a city and a tower, whose
head will be as far as heaven", God says concerning these: "come and going
down let us confuse their tongue there", and each is confused and is scattered
upon some place of the earth. And the people of Israel not sinning was in
Judaea, but having sinned is scattered then † from the inhabited world and is
sown everywhere. Understand some such thing for me also concerning all of us.
There is a "church of firstborns enrolled in heavens, where Zion a mountain
and city of living God heavenly Jerusalem". The blessed will be gathered
there, in order that they may be together. But also in this the sinners are
punished, in not being with one another. I know some in this life wishing for
punishment to hand over to some island and for torture the household of
someone who has grieved the kingdom and scattering them, here the wife, here
the one son, then elsewhere the other, in order that not even in the
misfortune the mother may enjoy the son, or the brother the brother.
Understand some such thing also upon the unjust. You the sinner must taste of
something more bitter being administered by God, in order that having been
educated you may be saved. And just as you do not punish the household-slave
or son being punished by you simply wishing to torture, but in order that by
the toils you may turn him, so also God those not turning by the word, those
not healed, will educate by the toils from sufferings. For education he casts
around what he casts around according to what has been said: "through all you
shall be educated with toil and scourge, Jerusalem". In order then that the
educating toil may increase, those toiling are scattered from one another so
as not to be together this one and that one. For the vehemence of the toil
would be loosened


through the consolation of each one toward that from the other. 12.4 And if it
is necessary to add to the account also another cause of being scattered, this
too I will set out. Bad men being with one another consider the bad things and
increase them, just as good men being with good consider about good things.
[And] therefore the bad counsel is dissolved and divided, which would have
been strong with those like them, when the wicked are scattered <from> one
another. For this reason God arranges that the worthless not be with one
another, perhaps also providing for them themselves, in order that their vice
may not increase together, but may be diminished being dissolved. These things
on account of "I will scatter them a man and his brother, and their fathers
and their sons upon the same, says the Lord". "I will not long for and I will
not spare and I will not pity from their destruction". 12.5 Upon such wordings
those from the heresies step saying: do you see of what sort the craftsman is?
The God of the prophets who says: "I will not spare and I will not pity from
their destruction"? How can this one be good? But if I take an example of a
judge not pitying upon the common good and a judge well not having mercy, I
will be able from the example to persuade


that God, sparing many, does not spare one. And I will take also an example of
a physician, showing that sparing the whole body he does not spare one member.
Let this for instance be the thing set before a judge, to craft peace and to
construct the things beneficial for the nation under him. And let someone have
come forward being led, a murderer fair in face and beautiful to look upon.
Let a mother have come forward bringing pitiable words to the judge, in order
that her old age may be pitied, let the wife of this one unworthy to be pitied
exhort, let children standing around him beg concerning him. Upon these what
is beneficial for the common? That this one be pitied or not be pitied? But
having been pitied he will return to the same things; but not having been
pitied he himself will die, but the common will be improved. Thus if God
should spare the sinner and have mercy on him, and pity so as not to punish
him, who will not be corrupted? <Who> of the worthless, even of those who
because of the fears of the places of punishment cease from sins, will not be
corrupted, will not be worse? Such things it is possible to see happening also
in the churches: someone sinned, he begged after the sin concerning communion.
If he is pitied too quickly, the common is corrupted, the sin of others
increases. But if by reasoning not as unmerciful nor as savage <the> judge,
but as providing also for the one, but providing more for the many than for
the one, he considers the coming damage to the common from the communion of
the one and the concession of his sin, it is clear that he will cause the one
to be cast out, in order that he may save the many. See for me also a
physician, in what way if he is sparing of cutting whatever it is necessary to
cut, if sparing of cauterizing <whatever it is necessary to cauterize> because
of the toils that follow such helps, in what way the disease increases and
becomes worse. But if more boldly he should as it were come to the cutting and
the burning, he will heal through not having mercy, through seeming not to
pity that one being cauterized and the one being cut. Thus also God does not
administer one man, but administers the whole world, the things in heaven, the
things on earth he manages everywhere. He considers then what is beneficial
for the whole world and all who are, according to what is possible he
considers also the beneficial for the one, yet not in order that the
beneficial of the one may come about upon damage of the world. For this reason
also eternal fire was prepared, for this reason also gehenna has been made
ready, for this reason there is also some outer darkness, of which there is
need not only because of the one being punished, but especially because of the
common. 12.6 And if you wish to take the scripture as witness, that also for
education of others the sinners are punished, even if these at some time have
been despaired of concerning healing, hear Solomon in Proverbs saying: "when a
pestilent man is scourged a fool will be more shrewd". He did not say that the
one being scourged himself will be more shrewd and more prudent because of the
scourges, but he says the fool changes from folly to prudence because of the
scourges brought upon the pestilent man; for this is signified from the name
here of shrewdness. And the fool changes through seeing others being scourged.
Therefore it is beneficial for us, if at any rate we become worthy of
salvation through others being punished, the punishment of others. And as the
trespass of Israel contributed to the salvation of the nations, so the
punishment <of some> will contribute to the salvation of others. For this
reason God being good says: "I will not spare and I will not pity from their
destruction". 12.7 And the one chapter having been delimited let us see also
the other, what it teaches us: "hear and give ear, and do not lift yourselves
up, because the Lord has spoken. Give glory to the Lord our God before it
grows dark, and before your feet stumble upon dark mountains; and you will
wait for light, and there is a shadow of death, and they will be placed into
darkness. But if you do not hear hiddenly, your soul will weep from the face
of outrage, and your eyes will bring down tears, because the flock of the Lord
has been crushed". He wishes [these] same ones to hear and to give ear, not
being content either with their hearing only or with giving ear. Wherefore he
says: "hear and give ear".


Then after this he commands them not to lift themselves up, and he teaches
what must be done. What then is the hearing and what is the giving ear, let us
understand from the wording itself. The "give ear" receive into the ears, and
the "hear", if it is said toward distinction from the "give ear", perhaps it
is receive into the mind. And upon the things said in the scriptures some are
more unspeakable and more mystical, but some useful from themselves to those
who understand. Concerning the more unspeakable I think the "hear" is said,
but concerning the things useful from themselves and able without
interpretation to benefit the hearer the "give ear". If then we examine the
whole scripture, we shall say "having become approved bankers": this hear, but
this give ear. Then whenever we have hearkened and given ear, he commands us:
"and do not lift yourselves up"; for "everyone who exalts himself will be
humbled". And the savior also in saying: "learn from me, that I am meek and
humble in heart, and you will find rest for your souls", teaches us the not
lifting ourselves up. For among the other human evils this sin also is much in
us, at one time lifting ourselves up quite irrationally and upon what it is
not even according to quantity necessary to lift up, at another time with
persuasiveness, that the thing upon which we lift ourselves is reasonable, yet
not even upon that is the lifting up sound. 12.8 And what I say will be clear
thus: there are some lifting themselves up, because they are sons of governors
and because they are of a family of great worldly dignities. Such ones lifting
themselves up upon an unchosen and indifferent matter do not even have a
reasonable persuasiveness that throws them upon lifting themselves up. There
are those lifting themselves up, because they have authority to destroy men,
and lifting themselves up, because they have received the so-called among them
advancement of such a sort as to cut off heads of men. "The glory" of such
ones "is in their shame". Others lift themselves up upon wealth, not the true
but the one below; and others lift themselves up, say, upon having a fair
house or many fields. None of these is worthy of an account, it is not
necessary to lift oneself up upon any of these. But the persuasive about
lifting up, whenever someone lifts himself up, because he is wise, and lifts
himself up being conscious to himself, that already from ten years he has not
touched sexual things or even from childhood he has not touched; and again
another lifts himself up, because he wore bonds the ones concerning Christ.
There is persuasiveness here suggesting, that reasonably someone lifts himself
up, yet not even upon these as toward the true account does someone reasonably
lift himself up. Thus it is not even upon these reasonable to lift oneself up.
Paul had the matter of lifting himself up through the visions, through the
sights, through the wonders and signs, through the toils which he toiled for
Christ, through the churches which he planted being ambitious, where Christ
was not named, to found a church. All these things were matter of lifting him
up, if it is necessary to speak with the persuasiveness concerning lifting up,
that it would have seemed to some well that he lift himself up. But
nevertheless since not even the lifting up upon such things is without danger,
the useful father as he gifted him visions and sights, so he gave him in a
share of a gift "an angel of satan, in order that he might buffet him, in
order that he might not be overly lifted up. And concerning this three times
he exhorted the Lord, in order that the angel of satan might stand away from
him", the one given to him according to arrangement in order that he might not
lift himself up. And the Lord answered him (for Paul was worthy of an answer
of the Lord) and said to him: "my grace is sufficient for you; for my power is
perfected in weakness". Upon nothing then is it necessary to lift oneself up;
for to fall follows lifting up according to "before crushing the heart of a
man is exalted and before glory it is humbled". These things unto the "hear
and give ear, and do not lift yourselves up, because the Lord has spoken".
12.9 And let us see also what things he commands us after these to do: "give"
he says "glory to the Lord our God before it grows dark, before your feet
stumble upon dark mountains; and you will wait for light". He wishes the one
giving "glory to God" to give "glory to God" while there is light, as glory of
God not being able to be announced, whenever it has grown dark and darkness
has come about. When then does it grow dark, and when does the


growing-dark not come about? "Work as the light is in you". <"The light is in"
you>, if you have in you the one who said: "I am the light of the world". As
long as this rises for you, glorify God; but know that some darkening can come
about, and it is necessary that this darkening not remain, but before it grows
dark to give the glory to God. =12.10= Perhaps we shall understand what has
been written using a gospel wording =12.10= said by the savior having thus:
"work as it is day; night comes when no one can work". There he named this age
day (but I added the there of necessity; for I know in other places other
things again being indicated) he named then this age day, but darkness and
night the consummation because of the punishments. For "why do you desire for
yourselves a day of the Lord? And this is darkness and not light" says Amos
the prophet. If you see what is the grim thing after the consummation of the
world, the thing following as almost the whole race of men being punished upon
the things that have been sinned, you will see that the surrounding then grew
dark and no longer can anyone glorify God, if indeed also to the righteous the
word of God commanded saying: "go, my people, enter into your chamber, shut
your door, hide yourself a little as much as much, until the fury of my wrath
should pass". And together in these things, if someone can, let him keep that
he has said: "a little as much as much". But that "little as much" is little
of God, it is not little for a man; for it is necessary to see that for each
there is some little and great. And from an example I will present that for
each there is some little or great. For each living thing small food is the
so-much as toward comparison of its constitution, and much food <the> so-much
as toward the comparison again of its construction. And thus the little of the
man is great for another living thing. The little, say, for a man is much for
a child. Thus small time is that of the human life all of it and that of the
long-lived as toward the whole of the whole present age. Thus then also the
little of God is much as toward us, and a whole age is the little of God. If
then it is said: "go, my people, enter into your chambers, shut your door,
hide yourself a little as much as much", that little must be thought to be
said not toward the relation of the one commanded to go and enter into his own
chambers, but toward the relation of the one commanding these things, for whom
the much for this one is little. For if "until the fury of the wrath" of God
"should pass", some must enter into their own chambers, and there are those
for whom "the sins are not forgiven not only throughout this whole age but
also throughout the whole coming one", it is clear that the little is
stretched alongside the things said. =12.11= "Give" then "glory to the Lord
our God". How do we give glory to the Lord our God? Not in voices and little
wordings do I seek the giving glory to the Lord our God, but in deeds the one
giving glory to the Lord God gives glory to him. In temperance glorify God, in
righteousness, in well-doing glorify God; give glory to God in courage and
endurance, give glory to God in piety and holiness and the remaining virtues.
And if these things are thus and thus someone glorifies God, if I say the
contraries, do not think me to blaspheme; for I will present also of those the
scripture as witness. The temperate man glorifies God, the one behaving
licentiously dishonors God. For the temple of God as Nebuchadnezzar he
overturns and "destroys the temple of God" and "through the transgression of
the law he dishonors God" (this wording too is apostolic). Therefore the
sinner places around God ingloriousness. And the things concerning providence
are sought, so that some doubt if there is providence [providence], because of
nothing else as because of vice. Take away vice, and you do not stumble at
providence. But up and down those stumbling at providence say these things:
why "so many adulterers and so many soft ones", why so many atheists and so
many impious? And those who generated the ingloriousness for providence, the
stumbling-blocks for God, the blasphemy for the one who created the world are
the ones sinning. <Some then give glory to God>, but they do not give


glory to God who do the things contrary to the glory of God through the sins.
=12.12= "Give glory to the Lord. to our God before it grows dark, before your
feet stumble upon dark mountains". There are some dark mountains, <there are
some bright mountains>; yet since both are mountains, both are great. Bright
mountains are the holy angels of God, the prophets, Moses the servant, the
apostles of Jesus Christ. All these are bright mountains, and concerning these
I think it has been said in the Psalms: "his foundations are in the holy
mountains". But of what sort are dark mountains? Those lifting up the heights
against the knowledge of God. The devil is a dark mountain, the rulers of this
age who are being abolished are dark mountains and the demon the lunacy was a
mountain, and it was a dark mountain, concerning which the savior said: "you
will say to this mountain". For concerning lunacy of the word being sought and
the disciples saying: "why were we not able to cast it out", the savior
answers that "if you have faith as a grain of mustard, you will say to this
mountain", concerning which you put forward, concerning which you sought, "you
will say to this mountain: pass from here there, and it will pass". "From
here" from the man, "there" upon its own place. Those then who stumble do not
stumble upon bright mountains, but upon dark mountains, whenever they come to
be with the devil and his angels, the dark mountains. "And you will wait for
light". It can however be joined to the "give glory to the Lord our God" <the
"and you will wait for light". If you give "glory to the Lord our God> before
it grows dark, before your feet stumble upon dark mountains", it is clear
that, even if it grows dark, "you will wait for the light" and the light will
succeed you. But another would say (I do not know whether understanding
soundly or not), that also those stumbling "upon dark mountains" will wait
beside the dark mountains waiting for the light of mercy; for this will seem
to be the "and you will wait for light". And whenever someone comes upon the
dark mountains, let us see what is there: "shadow of death". Where the dark
mountains, there shadow of death being generated from the dark mountains
themselves; =12.13= "and they will be placed into darkness". "But if you do
not hear hiddenly, your soul will weep from the face of outrage". Of those
hearing some hear hiddenly, but some, even if they hear, do not hear hiddenly.
What then is the hearing hiddenly or the "but we speak wisdom of God in a
mystery the one having been hidden, which God preordained before the ages unto
our glory"? And again elsewhere it is said, that "the more of the works of God
are in hidden things". If I hear the law, I either hear hiddenly or I do not
hear hiddenly. The Jew does not hear the law hiddenly; for this reason he is
circumcised manifestly, not knowing that not "the one in the manifest is a
Jew, nor the circumcision in the manifest in flesh". But the one hearing of
the circumcision hiddenly, will be circumcised in secret; the one hearing of
the things legislated concerning the passover hiddenly, eats from the sheep
Christ (for "our passover was sacrificed, Christ"), and knowing of what sort
the flesh of the word is, and knowing that "it is true food", he partakes of
this; for he heard of the passover hiddenly. But this common Jew for this
reason killed the Lord Jesus and is liable even today to the murder of Jesus,
since he heard neither of the law nor of the prophets hiddenly. If you read
concerning the unleavened things, it is possible to hear hiddenly, it is
possible to hear the command manifestly. As many of you (for the passover is
near) keep unleavened things, the bodily unleavened things, you do not hear
the command saying: "if you do not hear hiddenly, your soul will weep". And
concerning sabbath women not having heard the prophet do not hear hiddenly,
but hear manifestly. They do not bathe on the day of the sabbath, they return
"upon the poor and weak elements", as if Christ had not sojourned, the one who
perfects us and carries us across from the legal elements upon the gospel
perfection. For this reason let us attend reading the law and


the prophets, lest we fall under the prophecy saying: "but if you do not hear
hiddenly, your soul will weep from the face of outrage". As many as keep the
Jewish fast as not understanding the day of atonement [the] after the sojourn
of Jesus Christ, you did not hear of the atonement hiddenly, but only
manifestly; for to hear of the atonement hiddenly <is> to hear, how "God set
forth Jesus as atonement concerning our sins" and that "he himself is
atonement concerning our sins, and not concerning ours only, but also
concerning the whole world". And if gospel parables are read and the hearer is
of those outside, he will not hear them hiddenly. But if the hearer is an
apostle or of those entering "into the house" of Jesus, he comes to Jesus, he
inquires also concerning the unclarity of the parable, and Jesus interprets it
and that hearer of the gospel comes to be hearing it hiddenly, in order that
his soul may not weep; for of those not hearing hiddenly the soul weeps. How
