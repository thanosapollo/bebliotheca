On the parable of the ten virgins, and concerning almsgiving. On Holy and
Great Tuesday.
=59.527=
αʹ. When I consider the easily-spent nature of life, and the declining cycle
of the year, and the much-harassed conduct of men, and the circumstances of
living, both the passing-by of the present age, and the shadow of affairs, the
transience of glory, the easily-dissolved nature of power, the fantasy of
prosperity, and the dream of wealth; then when I consider the day of the end,
and that inexorable speed of the consummation, both the time of the
accounting, and the judge who cannot be flattered, and the state filled with
shuddering, how the judge comes from heaven flashing, how the powers run ahead
being troubled, how the fearful throne is prepared, how the heaven is rolled
up as a book, how the elements are burned being dissolved by fear, how the
ground is shaken receiving the coming of the judge, how trumpets cry out
fearfully, how the tombs are opened, how graves are shaken out, how the dead
leap up as from sleep, how the dust in the twinkling of an eye returns to its
own order, how the souls run back to the bodies, how the righteous run to the
meeting, how the bridegroom stands at midnight, how the righteous are counted
worthy of the entrance, how the door of the bridal chamber is shut against the
negligent; when I ponder all these things in my soul, I count blessed those
wise virgins whom the book of the Gospels has just now brought before us,
because they contended against the passion of sleep, because, having abhorred
the slippery and passionate nature of perishable life, they worthily took
thought for the uncorrupted and divine life, because, having considered the
inexorable nature of time, they watched for the hour of the coming, and they
called to mind the fear of that immortal bridegroom's presence, and having
tested the nocturnal =59.528= darkness, they took careful thought for the
lamps. And nothing hinders us from going through the very divine syllables of
the Gospel.
For the kingdom of heaven, he says, was likened to ten virgins, who, having
taken their lamps, went out to meet the bridegroom. When did they go out? Was
it when the appointed term of life overtook them? When the command arrived?
When the sentence of death stood over them? When the angels who hasten the end
were sent, then did they go out to meet the bridegroom? By no means. But when
did they go out? When they bid farewell to the distractions of this life, when
they chose to walk the narrow and afflicted way, when they loved voluntary
hardship, when they were not subjected to the laws of marriage, when they
despised the pleasures of life, when they chose the good portion of
incorruption, when they loved the manner of gladness, when they abhorred the
filth of sin, when they covenanted the things of chastity, when they loved the
pure bridegroom, when they desired the beauty of the kingdom, when they
stripped off every worldly care, then they went out to meet the bridegroom.
And five, he says, were wise, and five foolish. And what is the mark of the
wise? That they joined almsgiving to chastity, they adorned virginity with
well-doing, they knew that faith without works is dead, they proved that one
achievement does not suffice for salvation; for an eagle does not reach the
height with one wing. They remembered the voice of the bridegroom saying: I
desire mercy, and not sacrifice; and again, that Mercy boasts against
judgment. Counseling these things well, they filled their vessels with oil.
What vessels, then? The bellies of the hungry. For they procured the poor as
mediators of the spiritual marriage, they furnished themselves with the
provisions of compassions; the hungry were fed, and the lamps were brightened;
the poor gave thanks, and the =59.529= bridegroom was delighted; almsgiving
was sown, and the expected reward was being prepared. And the wise cried the
things of David: We were prepared, and we were not troubled; but the foolish,
holding the lamps without oil, were seen far from the bridegroom. How did they
cling to one achievement? How did they acquire purity, but thrust away
philanthropy? How did they procure impassibility for the body, but did not
show sympathy toward those in need? How did they love chastity, but turned
away from hospitality?
What then is the end? While the bridegroom delayed, he says, they all grew
drowsy and slept. But those who had already stored away the oil had security,
because their lamps would not be quenched; but the foolish awaited the time of
necessity. Why then do I not speak briefly? The hour stood over them, the time
arrived, the noises began, the trumpets sounded, the elements were shaken, man
was being tossed, the heaven was bending, the firmament trembled, stars were
being dissolved, the powers were troubled, angels ran ahead, lightnings went
before, creation had a great tumult. For it is not in the day, but at
midnight, that the judge arrives. Then what? There is a cry calling to the
meeting: Behold, the bridegroom comes, go out to meet him. What then happens?
All the virgins rose up, they shook off sleep, they took hold of the lamps;
but some were burning, and some did not shine. Those of the wise were watered
with the oil of well-doing, but those of the foolish were being quenched.
Truly the matter was pitiable. Inescapable necessity encircled the women; the
failure had no occasion for consolation. Then therefore, having come to the
wise, they sought what they could not receive. For give us of your oil,
because our lamps are being quenched. They ought long ago to have imitated,
and not now to be begging; for they ought to have bought the oil from those
who sell. Give you oil, they say, to us. In vain you entreat the wise, O
negligent ones; the festival of life has already been dissolved, the theater
of this life has passed; it is not now a time of bargains; now the display of
works is sought. You ought to have foreseen the danger of the present age; you
ought to have taken thought for this meeting; you ought to have considered
that lamps without oil cannot give light. You say, Give us of your oil. No one
is adorned with another's works; each reaps what he has sown. "Give you oil to
us." What then do the wise say to them? Lest it not suffice for us and you;
but rather go to those who sell, and buy. Even if some small hour is still
left, run, toil; the bridegroom has not yet stood over you; hasten before the
doors are shut, go away to those who sell. And who, O wise ones, are those who
sell? Tell us; for these do not know, not having acquired the custom of such a
bargain. Who are those who sell? The poor who sit by the doors of the
churches, the rational swallows, who announce a rational spring to the souls,
the venerable mediators toward the Master, the unconquered orators on the day
of examination. And while they were going away to buy the oil, the bridegroom
came, and the doors were shut. O the harmful negligence; O the incurable pain;
O the inconsolable loss; O mourning without consolation. And while they were
going away to buy, the bridegroom came. The expected joy came, the boast of
the righteous came, the light came at midnight. The wise went to meet him,
they entered together with the bridegroom, and the doors were shut.
βʹ. I shudder, considering what happened; I tremble, narrating the failure
that befell the women. They desired to see the fearful bridal chamber; for
because of it they bid farewell to the delights of the world, they despised
luxury, =59.530= they overlooked glory, they kept hard ways, they mastered
passions, they contended against pleasures; and since they had no oil, they
found the bolts of the kingdom unopenable. For having come they knocked,
saying: Lord, Lord, open to us; while from within the Judge cried out
fearfully: Amen, amen I say to you, I do not know you. O the last sentence;
and he did not give the painful answer through an angel, but through himself;
so that, having heard the voice, and being unable to see the face, they might
endure a greater torment. Amen I say to you, I do not know you. Do you not
know us, Master? From the womb we were cast upon you, we followed after you
from our youth, we kept purity, the body which you formed for us we preserved
incorrupt, we did not betray the members to the passions, we expected to
receive crowns from your right hand; and now you have shut the doors and you
say, "I do not know you"? Yes, truly I do not know you. Why, O Master? The
Master: Why? Because I hungered, and you did not give me to eat; I thirsted,
and you did not give me to drink; I was a stranger, and you did not take me
in; naked, and you did not clothe me; sick and in prison, and you did not come
to me. I charged you in writing, saying: Inasmuch as you did not do it to one
of these my brothers, the least and small, you did not do it to me. And they
said: In vain then, Master, did we endure the toil and the hardship of the
flesh? In vain did we waste ourselves with vigils and fasts? In vain, having
longed for the heavenly bridegroom to the end, did we keep virginity
unwounded? Yes, he says, you are virgins, but you have no dowries; virgins
indeed, but you are not clothed with bridal adornment; you practiced the
purity of the flesh, but by inhumanity you wounded the purity; your body is
undefiled, but the manner is unmerciful, not of nature, but of inhumanity.
My bridal chamber belongs to those who have acquired the oil of well-doing.
Therefore those who are betrothed to me must also wear a manner corresponding
to the adornment. I cannot take a bride who wars against my chambers; I do not
bring war into peaceful bridal rooms. Therefore go away from me, do not vainly
trouble my doors; my kingdom is the dwelling of the merciful. He easily enters
with me who has imitated my compassion toward the poor, who has become poor in
spirit, who has bent his ear to those in need, who has made the neighbor's
sufferings his own, who has wept over others' calamities, who has filled the
soul of the hungry with good things, who has warmed the shoulders of the
naked, who has not ignored the voice of the sick, who has lent a word of
consolation to the faint-hearted, who has received the stranger under his
roof, who with the sponge of sympathy has wiped away the tear of widows, who
by protection has lightened the weight of orphanhood. Knowing these things,
brothers, do not marvel with the tongue, but imitate with the works. For the
hand is the seal of the tongue, and the giving of the right hand interprets
this, being jealous of the words. Let us make love of the poor of much
account, let us not spare scattering the obols to the poor, I entreat; let us
hearken to the Master's voices; having learned the experience of fear, let us
turn aside; let us take thought for ourselves through the poor.
For the virgins who did not honor philanthropy are cast out of the bridal
chamber. What hope then remains for sinners who have insulted by inhumanity?
Saying these things to the multitude, I make secure the conscience of the more
negligent, harming the well-disposed in nothing, but even making them warmer
toward piety. Therefore scatter, brother, while the festival of life still
stands. Transact the things of your defense; before the thrones are set, show
forth lamentation; anticipate the coming of the bridegroom; run more swiftly
than sin; forestall the appointed examination; =59.531= serve the judge; do
not consent to stand before the tribunal naked. Now scatter money,
that then you may dissolve charges; speak with the judge in private, that he
may not condemn you before all; take the poor man as mediator toward his
service; through him bribe the judge; to him alone be bold with such a
mystery; through him sell justice to the condemned. The poor man receives, and
the judge subscribes forgiveness; he receives without writing, and he brings
forward written bonds.
For he who has mercy on a poor man lends to God. Whatever you now throw into
the hand of the poor man, you will recognize in the palm of the judge. If
therefore we are eager to escape the judgment, from here already let us
entreat the judge through the poor. Even when you are arranging last things,
write with the kinsmen or friends the soul departing from life; give her even
small provisions of necessity; let the letters of the will have the name of
the judge; let the paper not be without memorial of a poor man.
Make death a producer of joy, and not worthy of lamentations; do not stand at
the fearful tribunal without advocates; fill the bellies of those who owe to
plead with their tongues for you before the Lord. Let us acquire =59.532=
through them the Creator as a debtor, so as not to give account on that day;
let us buy with money orators of such success. For you have, he says, the poor
man as a witness of friendship toward me; you know me making the things of the
poor my own. I cause grass to spring up for the cattle, and I supply food to
all; and yet when you do the almsgiving, I bend my hand together with the poor
man, and I receive the obol. I put on the light as a garment; and if only you
clothe the poor man, I feel the warmth. You know me sitting with the Father in
heaven; and if only you set out to visit those in prison, you will find me
sitting with them in the bonds; and if you run to the sick, I am not absent
from the bed.
For I am everywhere, and I stand before those in necessity. Whatever you give
to the poor man, you will see this multiplied by me. If you bring the homeless
into your house, and through him receive me in your house, I will grant you
these three things: I will both increase the gain, and I will guard your
house, and I will prepare a dwelling for you in the heavens; in which pain and
sorrow and sighing have fled away, in God the Father, together with the Holy
Spirit, to whom be the glory and the power, now and always, and unto the ages
of ages. Amen.
