OF GEORGE THE MONK, LIVES OF THE NEW EMPERORS.
REIGN OF LEO THE ARMENIAN.
In the #22 310th year of the world, and of the divine incarnation 810, Leo
the Armenian, the patrician, and later appearing as an apostate, reigned 7
years and 5 months, being crowned by patriarch Nicephorus. For after two
years, revolting toward impiety, and setting aside the written pledge which
he had made concerning orthodoxy, he ran aground like Saul; for that man
too, having reigned two years lawfully, 764 then turning aside and being
stripped of the divine grace and delivered to an evil spirit, was armed
against his benefactor David and worked the murders of the priests. Likewise
this wretched and god-opposing man also, after two years, raging and going
mad against piety, banished the divine Nicephorus who had crowned him. 2.
He has Theodotus ordained in his place as patriarch, so called, an irrational
man, or rather a slave and more voiceless than fishes and knowing nothing
more than impiety; and from then on he fanned an implacable persecution
against the church. If then one must marvel at a change from one worthless
thing to another, this man and those about him rather than the Galatians
must be marveled at, who thus change over from him who called them in the
grace of Christ to another gospel; which is nothing else except to provoke
God and to draw down the divine wrath. 3 For the new Sennacherib, Krum,
puffed up by the former judgment, leaving his own brother to besiege
Adrianople, after six days of Leo's autocracy came against the city and went
round the walls from Blachernae as far as the Golden Gate, displaying the
force about him. Having also performed polluted sacrifices in the meadow of
the Golden Gate he asked 765 the emperor also to make peace, if he should
plant his spear in the Golden Gate. When the emperor would not accept it he
returned to his own tent, greatly admiring the walls of the city and the
well-ordered array of the emperor. He therefore turns toward a compact of
peace, making probing speeches. 4 But the emperor, seizing an occasion,
tried to spear him; but he was prevented from bringing this to completion
by the ineptitude of those who served, who struck him but did not deal a
mortal blow. Enraged at this the accursed one, sending horsemen to Saint
Mamas, burned the palace that was there, and loading on wagons the bronze
lion of the Hippodrome together with the bear and the dragon of the fountain
and the finest marbles he returned, sitting down against Adrianople and
taking it; and having crossed over with a very great people of the noble
Macedonians as well, he encamped at the river Danube. But Leo was performing
the works of impiety in the city. 5. In his time there appeared a comet in
the shape of two bright moons uniting and again dividing into various
shapes, so as even to be formed into the fashion of a headless man; which
foreshowed the headless head of those who afterward rebelled against the
Christians and that they had the vain Thomas as head and leader of the
division from the other Christians. And 766 moreover there came to pass
fearful and successive earthquakes and famines and droughts and burnings of
the air and civil strifes in every city and country, beginning from the days
of the god-hated and accursed man; and for a long time the terror of the
civil disaster happened to prevail. 6 Imitating therefore the founder of
this abominable heresy, his namesake and of like manners, he began the
heresy. Then seeking allies and fellow-initiates he found John the so-called
Grammarian, or rather another Jannes or Simon, notorious for dish-divinations
and drugs and shameful works, and certain others of like mind with him,
Jannites and Jambrites and Simonites; whom indeed, holding them with himself
in the



palace, he summons the celebrated patriarch Nicephorus and the leading
bishops and priests with him before the senate, and says "Know clearly that
certain have risen up saying that the images ought not to be venerated, whom
if you wish to be present and to discuss concerning this, the truth will
appear more manifest." 7 To whom the divine Nicephorus answered nothing, but
said to the magnates standing by "Tell me, can that which is not fall?" And
when they answered with the security of the faith the patriarch asked again
"Did the holy images fall under Leo and Constantine the Isaurians?" And when
they nodded and added the yes, "Then 767 they were standing altogether and
had their setting-up as ancient. For how can that which is not and is not
standing fall?" 8 Then when the other bishops put forward such things for
the overthrow of the heresy, the holy bishop of Sardis, Euthymius, speaking
freely says "Attend, O emperor, to what will be said in brief. From when
Christ came upon the earth until now eight hundred years have passed, and
everywhere Christ is depicted in images and is venerated in an image. What
insolent man then would dare to set aside or shake the apostolic and
patristic tradition of so long a time? Especially when the apostle says,
Therefore, brethren, stand and hold the traditions which you received either
by word or by letter. Even if an angel from heaven should preach to you a
gospel contrary to what you received, let him be anathema." 9 After whom
Theodore also answered, the zealot and hot champion of orthodoxy, the abbot
of Studios, saying "Do not shake, emperor, an ecclesiastical constitution.
For the apostle has said, and those whom God set in the church, first
apostles, second prophets, third pastors and teachers. He did not say
emperors. For to you the political constitution was entrusted and the army.
Take thought for these, and leave the church to pastors and teachers
according to the divine word. But if you do not wish to attend to these and
to be with our faith, even if an angel coming down from heaven should wish
768 to turn us aside, we will not listen to him, much less to an emperor."
10. The tyrant therefore, hearing these things and boiling over with wrath,
and the ape roaring a lion's roar, and driving all away with insult, and
driving away Nicephorus the great of the city, and likewise exiling Theodore
of Studios, has ordained in his place Theodotus Cassiteras, who was from the
spatharii and had taken the name of the stage. And thus the criminal and
beast-mannered rather than beast-named man, lifted up against piety,
persecuted the church of God the more and plundered it greatly, until the
divine justice justly came upon him. Whose most evil birth and wickedness
and god-hated purpose and impiety, and moreover the harsh catastrophe of his
life, the divine Nicephorus openly posting as on a stele says "God looks on,
and a man has begun to rule over us (I do not know if one ought to call him
a man, and not a beast, and wilder and more bitter than beasts. 11 For what
is more pitiable than this disaster and more wretched than this misfortune?
How great an evil has burst into the empire! Two-natured in race and
mixed-barbarian, many-formed in manner and illiberal and unstable in mind."
769 That which was near to him then and well known, 769 for he sprang from
the land of the Armenians, whence one may speak of the ill-tempered and
evil-natured. But that which is farthest and ancient, as some of the elders
recount, who know the things of him and of his fathers handed down from long
ago, that he did not even from there purely spring from anywhere, if indeed
from the tribe of the Assyrians he came forth as a most evil offshoot, from
whom he advertised his paternal blood. For he was descended from the
children of Sennacherib, who once ruled the Assyrians and had received a
mortal blow from those born of him; for they raised murderous right hands
against their begetter, being defiled with the gore of parricide, and
becoming themselves the slayers of the slaughter of him who begot them they
at once go off as fugitives to the districts of the Armenians and are saved.
And of this parricidal



seed, bitterly kept through our generation and sprouting beside as a
man-slaying fruit, I know not whence this terror had been allotted as a
condemnation of the Romans. Who, brought into this light by judgments which
he who permitted it knows, is nourished and grows together with evil and
destructive habits and deeds. 12. From the lioness of the Assyrians then and
the leopard of Armenia there sprang a mongrel composite and monstrous form
of a tiger 770 from a bastard and counterfeit race, an Armenian or an
Assyrian of a more hideous kind, and Leo in name, possessing indeed the
rapacious of a lion unto security, but in no way admitting the liberal, and
a chameleon, as in form, so also in the many-shaped of the soul, because of
the festering and hidden of the race, from which he vaunted the hidden and
unspeakable of ill-temper and deceit. For he contrived that the lurking
darkness of his mind, shifting and inclined toward harming, should escape
notice. And somehow it had been labored and studied by him that heart should
never agree with lips, so as to meditate some things in mind and put forth
others in the mouth. But his life ran aground upon so great a lack of love
of God and wickedness that even his most hard-to-bear and hard-to-grasp
demonic mind and wickedness became evident to all. When therefore, having
taken the imperial rule, or rather tyranny, by God's permission, he pours
out all his wrath and deceit against the blameless faith, and vies in all
things with the impious Copronymus before him, and likewise trusts the whole
of his life to certain polluted and all-wicked men, wanton and notorious for
shamefulness of life and corrupted in soul and body, sorcerers and 771
poisoners and people-deceivers, who have practiced every kind of evil and
have simply denied the piety of Christians. 13 And these he gladly takes
into his house and embraces, and yields to their vain and soul-destroying
teachings and initiations, and as a slave of sin he is enslaved, and the
fallen man, baited by the hook of deceit and lawlessness, is terribly
pierced round. For they set before him three things promised, the denial of
our exact and orthodox faith, the taking down of the divine images, the
persecution of the pious; "and thus you will come to the height of
prosperity," they say, "so as to complete the present life for a very long
and longest time, if anyone else does." 14. Fooled then by these and such
destructive deceits the vain-minded and brutish man, and as he was hideous
and hard to approach and more shameful in appearance (for if the name was a
lion's, what was seen was an ape's), becomes worse in manner, more pompous
in habit, more beastlike in mind, untamed in impulse, unrestrained and
irresistible and more irrational and heavier and more tyrannical. 15 For the
wretched and miserable man, lifted up by the false-divination and plot of
those criminals, so quickly stretches out the war. And 772 first he moves
against our sacred doctrines his God-fighting tongue, then he emulates here
too the most impious Copronymus Conon; and gathering like him a wicked and
vagabond assembly, or rather a Jewish sanhedrin, of which the accursed man
became both assessor and president, the abomination of desolation is seen
again in the temple of the Lord, and a mouth is opened speaking great things
against the glory of the Lord and talking big and boasting against the
divine economy. And thus the second Caiaphas with that Christ-fighting band,
having emptied many vain things against the truth, takes down every sacred
setting-up of the church from hatred toward Christ and his servants, since
Christ was heavy to him even when seen in an image and borne in memory, and
his friends and slaves. 16 Therefore also being foolish he babbled that
every iconic setting-up is rejected by the divine scripture and quite
unrecognized. What then would one say to these things? That the words of the
wisdom of God are unsearchable,



and the fool will speak foolish things. For being exceedingly unlearned and
full of nonsense he did not at all know those gold-wrought Cherubim, which
the hierophant Moses fashioned at God's command and which the old and lawful
tent bore above the ark, which were glorified not among Jews only, but the
herald of grace also 773 clearly calls them Cherubim of glory, that is the
glorified. And moreover also as many things as the wisdom of Solomon
constructed and that were seen by the divine Ezekiel, and if anything of
such a kind is related and honored in the sacred books, which the wretched
and profane man did not shudder to set in the class of idols, having chosen
rather to set aside than to admit the God-inspired scriptures; from which
remaining he would reasonably be thought more faithless and more ungrateful
than Jews together with those of like mind with him. For they have thought
and uttered things truly worthy of their own abomination and unbelief and
irrationality. 17 For they fight openly against Christ here too, who taking
a bright cloth and wiping off upon it the all-bright and all-beautiful
divine form sends it to Abgar, the ruler of the Edessenes, who asked in
faith. And from that time until today, by apostolic tradition and
introduction, for the sake of knowledge and memory of what Christ did and
suffered for us, as indeed it is also related in the sacred tablets of the
gospels, we reverently depict and venerate, even if the Christ-fighters
burst. 18 And moreover besides these a tradition and a word holding
persuasion comes down even to us, that the divine evangelist Luke depicted
in an image our Lord Jesus Christ, and also his all-undefiled 774 mother,
and that the city of the Romans keeps these sacred types. And what need is
there to speak of the rest of those in Jerusalem, as some who were there
have related, how very many sacred depictions from of old, set up with
accuracy until now, they piously and reverently tend and venerate? Likewise
again it is written that Peter and Paul, the chiefs of the apostles,
preaching the great things of God in Rome, and the wonders which Christ has
done, first depicting the divine transfiguration handed it over to the
Romans, as it was also seen by Moses and Elijah in the midst of the holy
prophets; which things are preserved even until now. 19. How then will the
disobedient and ungrateful look against the wonder sung and seen until the
present in the temple of the Mother of God, that in the city called Lydda?
Which temple the apostles built while she was still living in this life. For
many have seen that unhandmade and venerable likeness being piously
venerated and honored, formed on bright and translucent slabs, and having
gone through the whole depth; which also certain of the hostile Greeks and
Jews, having been eager to scrape it off, 775 were made vain, failing of
their absurd attempt. For the more they scraped the impression, the more it
shone forth. For the form stands no less in shape, preserving face and robe
and the rest of the sight inerasable and unharmed. For it is related that
the holy apostles by the promise of the all-pure Theotokos raised a temple
in her name; and when she entered at their request for a viewing of the
temple and stood at one of the columns, her impression was found in the
column; which also, as has been said, Greeks and Jews, having forced it
much, were not able to scrape off. 20. And that the making and tradition of
these is most ancient, a witness also besides the others is the divine
statue which the woman with the issue of blood set up to the healer Christ
by her own house, a most clear stele and an indelible herald of the wonder
that had come to be upon her, publishing to later generations the paradox
that had been worked as a wonder in her case; concerning which also the
great teacher among those who form images, Eusebius, in the ecclesiastical
history, having mentioned the wonders accomplished from the sacred and
God-grown herb growing from there, and that it was for all an aid that
warded off evil, adds saying that we have also related the images of the
apostles 776 Peter and Paul and of Christ himself in colors in paintings



being preserved. 21 And the sight also is a witness, an unlying teacher. The
length of time also clearly shows it, and the building of the venerable
temples. And still also the making and dedication of the sacred vessels from
of old by the pious, being infinite in number. And not only so, but the very
covers of the holy gospels also, bearing the sacred types on the outside,
venerably venerated by all the faithful, and the divine columns and the
so-called soleae, which wall off the venerable of the sacred sanctuary. For
they have been depicted here from the beginning piously for no other reason
at all except to be venerated, together with the type of the cross lying
alongside. 22. But the beast-named and lion-mannered man, having struck all
these things out, was eager as much as possible to turn away every divine
image and to send it into deep forgetfulness. Fighting God in this way, the
thunderstruck man, having dared such things before the people, speaks
freely, saying "Just as we have destroyed the images from your eyes,
brethren, so also you, as slaves and friends of God and of the emperor,
hasten to take them away from your souls." For the fool will speak foolish
things, and his heart will think 777 vain things, to accomplish lawless
deeds and to speak error toward the Lord. And from there therefore the
accursed and blood-drinking man condemned those found having an image of
Christ or of some saint and venerating it to terrible punishments and
torments. And thus being butchered every day and sacrificed the
God-fearing were destroyed everywhere; and he has murdered many glorious
and notable men. 23. And these things were done unsparingly and without
ceasing against those who were orthodox; but against those in office and
the more well-off the wicked and all-contriving man finds another wicked
device. For wishing to make them pitiable and poor, what does he do? He
surrounds them with inescapable charges and accusations, putting forward
pretexts in sins, in order that by fear of the accusations they may count
out all their substance for their salvation from there. And there were
times when he also forced together against certain of the more manly or
more gifted, envying them their manliness or gift, as suspecting that they
would make a revolution. Of some he dug out the eyes, of others he
mutilated the limbs; holding others in bonds he after some days sent them
to death. Among whom was Michael 778 who held the command of the tagma of
the Excubitors; for he was slandered to the emperor as plotting against
him; whom he had kept in a prison and in fetters. 24 And when the birthday
of our Saviour came on he wished to kill this man by night; but being
prevented by his own wife on account of the feast he left him. But Michael,
being in the prison, having learned this, made it known to all the
counselors with him that if you do not hasten to take me out of the guard,
I will denounce you all to the emperor. And with them was also the papias
of the palace, being a kinsman of Michael. These, being armed by night,
wearing the swords within, when the papias opened entered as priests with
phelonia. And when the emperor had entered the church they leapt in [and
took] his unholy soul ... in the palace, in which none of those who had
reigned before him had been slain. 25 And at that very hour going to the
prison they bring Michael out and bring him in at the feast, a
crown-wearer instead of a bond-wearer, so that the saying of the psalmist
was fulfilled upon him, weeping shall lodge in the evening, and in the
morning gladness. 779 And after these things wrapping the limbs of the
criminal in cheap rags, and putting his all-polluted body into a boat, they
carry it out to the island called Prote and bury it, where they also
tonsured his children and made them monks, I do not know for what this
island had been condemned to receive the disaster of so many evils. 26. And
it must be attended to that if the word of these impious and ill-named men
had any force and the things constructed by them had an appearance of
truth, they would have been kept and known somewhere at all in the other
hierarchies; but now it is possible to recognize this from nowhere. For
both the eastern and the western lot, tending this gospel



and apostolic preaching they continue; and never in another authority,
where there are Christians, is that lawless and unlawful and alien to the
piety of Christians sprouting god-hated dogma caught, nor so much as to
have come into the memory of the wicked and abominable compositions, but
only in this enslaved and sold-into-slavery [city] that plays with divine
things at will 780, in which the passionate and material earthy and
boastful and proud mind of those who turn about the earth and wallow
governs. Where there is self-love, love of rule, love of pleasure, love of
glory, the enjoyment of all things according to desire, the wishing to
rule all and not even to be ruled by God himself, and the acquisition and
procuring of human glory, or rather shame. From there the insult against
God and the saints and the persecution of the church. From there the
subject, being lorded over or tyrannized, is forced to yield to their
dogmas and wills; for which the living weapon has been forged and
improvised, gathered in form for the guarding of the body, but in truth
devised for the sacking of the church of Christ and for plunder, on the
pretext of the Godhead of Christ being circumscribed together in the
image, talking nonsense; as if someone should show this solar and
disk-shaped body drawn in some place, as it is seen happening in certain
places, they would have dogmatized that the substance and ray of the light
is also drawn together and circumscribed, or at all events that the light
is cut off from the sun, or again if they should see a ray of the sun
striking somewhere on a tree, and the tree should be cut, it was madly
thought that the ray also would be cut together. And moreover also
concerning fire 781 and certain other things they will reason, in order
that they may justly be mocked by children at play, thinking things more
senseless. For if in the things said it is not in the nature of these
things to come to be or to be seen, nor will it have a place to be spoken,
how much more in the unspeakable and divine nature or in the ineffable and
uninterpretable economy of Christ are these things impossible and
unattemptable to say or to think! For neither when he was being
incarnate or suffering for us in the body did he suffer this, since thus
also if a man of those among us should be imaged in body, the soul must
also be imaged together, or at all events they will die when they are
separated; for what else is death than a separation of soul and body?
Whence in these things they appear plainly no less impious than ignorant;
for they do not know how much the peculiar and distinct of the drawn and
the circumscribed, and of drawing and circumscribing, and for this they
talk vanity, being fooled. And where at all has a body unwritten or
uncircumscribed been heard of from all the age? Or how will the definition
and account of the body be saved, if it should not have these things? He
therefore who does not glorify that Christ is drawn or circumscribed in
body, either has not known what a body is, from which he has not even
acquired a right opinion, or knowing he refuses that body and soul be
saved. He then who confesses that Christ has taken up a body such as ours,
and this is drawable 782 and circumscribable, will of necessity also
confess together that the body of Christ is drawable and circumscribable.
But if he will not grant the second, then not the first either, which is
altogether irrational and laughable. 27 We therefore do not circumscribe,
but we draw. And the wretched men talk another more absurd emptiness; for
him whom they say is uncircumscribable, I know not how by simplicity they
marvel that he is circumscribed together in the image. And if again they
will empty that Christ ought not to be imaged, the Charybdis of their
teachers has gaped greatly about them, of Manes and Valentinus and
Marcion. For thinking that the divine Word has been incarnate in seeming
and in fantasy they will be no less pierced round by the former blasphemy
of the lie, they who, arraying themselves impiously against the whole
economy, do not blush. And who are these? The accusers of the praised, the
dark about the light, the uneducated about wisdom, the ungrateful
creatures according to the theologian's voice.



{REIGN OF MICHAEL THE STAMMERER.}
In the #22 310th from the world, and from the incarnate economy 810,
Michael the Stammerer the Amorian reigned 8 years 9 months, and 783 died
from strangury and the most violent pain of the kidneys; who, yielding a
little from the evil that had previously held, so much as for those in
prisons and toils and exiles to dream of a dreamlike freedom and ease,
cherished the god-hated mind of the preceding ill-named and impious man,
and likewise was pierced round by the same hook of the terrible heresy
from extreme irrationality and lack of education. Whence at a silentium he
said "Those before us who inquired into the exactness of the dogmas will
be required to give account concerning them, whether they legislated well
or badly; but we judge that the church remain also in that in which we
found it walking." 2. And he had a son from Euphrosyne named Theophilus,
whom he also crowned in the Great Church. And he legislated that no one
should have license to move a word concerning images, but let both the
synod of Constantine and that of Tarasius and that now come to be under
Leo concerning such inquiries be out of the way and gone, and let there be
a deep silence of the memory of the images. 784 3. In his time Thomas the
rebel, having already set out from the eastern parts, and having collected
and dragged after him a vagabond and mixed people, moved upon Byzantium,
desiring the empire beyond his desert; for setting out from the land of
the Romans, being of low birth and obscure, he came to the parts of Syria,
renaming himself Constantine and son of the empress Irene. And from there
indeed, having deceived many of the barbarians and of the Christians, and
having gathered an infinite people from various nations, he came against
Constantinople in a heavy crowd and a great fleet, gathering the
many-formed races of the nations like some monstrous and many-shaped and
varied and many-headed beast, and like some other Sennacherib arming
himself against the new Jerusalem, he vaunted in the multitude of those
who followed and boasted, not altogether, as it seems, having understood
that a king is not saved by much power and a giant will not be saved in
the multitude of his strength, and a lying horse for salvation and the
Lord scatters the counsels of nations, and the rest. For even if the city
did not have a Hezekiah, I mean a pious and virtuous emperor, yet the
governor of the whole 785 and the philanthropic Lord did not overlook his
city and his people being besieged, but for the misery of the poor and the
groaning of the needy he said then also "Now I will arise and I will
shield my city and my people," not for my sake and for David my servant,
as he says then and now, but for my sake and for my son born from the seed
of David and for the new Jerusalem, which I have chosen. 4 For also under
the impious Ahab, when the Syrian had collected many myriads against him
and was swaggering and saying "If Samaria will suffice for all the people
for my foot-soldiers," the pitying God easily at once dissolved the
reproach of the thrice-wretched king, saying "Behold, I give him into your
hands, and you will know that I am the Lord." Which also came to be, and
at once he showed both to his own and to the strangers his own strength
and David speaking truly "The Lord is strong and mighty, the Lord mighty
in war;" for when the clash came Israel shot down twelve myriads of the
adversaries. And in order that both those and these might learn that the
blow was God-sent, the wall falling upon a certain city of those who had
escaped buried twenty-seven thousands. 786 5. Using then this
boastfulness of the Syrian and of the Assyrian the vain-minded Thomas also
was sacking Constantinople, or rather Theoupolis, for one year, the
citizens arraying themselves manfully and fighting from the walls and
fighting at sea; for they burned most of their ships, and routed the
picked men of their enemies. And he, being taken by helplessness, leaving
the city went upon Thrace, plundering it.



Having therefore plundered it moderately, but not as he hoped, but as the
divine providence allowed on account of our sins, he accomplished nothing
more of what he expected. And indeed not only did the endeavor stand
against him and was overturned and turned out contrary, but also what he
seemed to have and imagined, both glory and strength, he was all but
stripped of and utterly perished; 6 for the citizens, as has been said,
arraying themselves against him nobly and violently by the turn and
alliance of the Better, and with such a hope fighting from the walls and
on foot and at sea manfully and steadfastly, dissolved almost all his
rotten swagger. For having burned the fortifications on which he
senselessly prided himself and was overbold, I mean his ships, and 787
having routed the picked men of the enemies and the camp-commanders, much
dizziness and helplessness surrounded the unstable and most light Thomas.
Yes, and all his many-contrived and many-formed siege-engines, being
unconquerable, as he thought, and hard to take, they destroyed and
dissolved easily altogether. And thus the house of David, that is the city
of Christ, went and was strengthened, but the house of Saul, the throng of
the enemies, went and was weakened in truth, and God was glorified. And
one might see clearly indeed the other sacred saying fulfilled upon him,
"You have taken down all his fences, you have set his fortifications as
cowardice" and the rest of the psalm. Whence also those of the city sang
"These in chariots and these in horses" and the remainder of the psalm. 7
And the divine word, pledging and pre-evangelizing to them very well the
sudden and swiftest fall of their enemy, answered all but saying "Take
courage, children, and cry to God; for there will be for you a remembrance
from him who brought [the evils] on. For he who brought the evils upon you
will bring upon you the eternal gladness with your salvation." 788 8.
When therefore the wretched and deceived man, having worn out, fighting
unprofitably and toiling in vain, and having quite despaired as having
missed the mark from the diameter, and with affairs clearly going behind
