Homily 1. On Prayer
=64.461=
Having first gone through with you the subject of fasting, and having
recounted its advantages as far as we were able, and having again on the
previous occasion gone through the subject of baptism for your love of
hearing, we wish today to recount to you also the subject of prayer: what its
power is, and what benefit from it comes to those who continue in it. I exhort
you therefore, listen with a well-disposed and reverent mind, laying a
straight hearing under the things that are said. This has truly come to be the
greatest and eldest thing, and the mother of all the commandments, begetting
all the ordinances of the Lord.
For who while praying can overlook the neighbor in poverty, so as to have the
labor concerning it unprofitable? And who can desire such wealth and
abundance, so as to take upon the body only a vain toil, while the heart is
captured somewhere else? For the disease of love of money is like some
hard-to-heal and lingering affection, neither quickly bringing on death, nor
granting perfect health, but little by little loosening the body from its own
strengths, until, having wasted away under the inward contractions that arise,
after countless labors it sends it forth to the common fall of death. And who
that is a lover of prayer will be able to neglect fasting, being taught by the
Scripture itself that Fasting and petition deliver from death? Besides, it is
not possible to call upon the Lord from the heart while living together with
gluttony and drunken revel; as also the Lord in the Gospel charged his own
disciples: Take heed, he says, lest your hearts be weighed down in reveling
and drunkenness. For the vapor of the multitude of foods that enter, around
the hollow parts of the brain, clouds what is useful about the reasoning
itself, throwing the heart itself into confusion. Thus true prayer is a kind
of starting-point of all the commandments, compelling them all to run
alongside in their own order.
Prayer is the crowning good, and converse with God; for it is communion and
union with God. And as the eyes of the body, when they behold light, are
illuminated, so also a soul stretching itself toward God, being lit by his
unspeakable light, is illuminated. Prayer, then, is not that which comes about
in outward form, but that which is accomplished from the heart; not shut in by
seasons and intervals of hours, but having its work unceasing by night and by
day. For one must not only, when going to prayer, swiftly lead the thought
away toward God; but one must also, when occupied with certain needs, whether
care for the poor, or other concerns, or profitable =64.464= good works,
mingle the longing and the memory of God, so that, being seasoned as with salt
by the love of God, they may become a most sweet food for the Master of all.
And we can acquire the benefit from this as something inalienable throughout
the whole of life, assigning to it the greater part of our time.
Prayer is illumination of the soul, true knowledge of God, a mediator of God
and men, a physician of passions, an antidote of diseases, calm of the soul, a
heavenly guide, not turning about the earth, but journeying into the very
vault of the heavens. It overleaps created things, cuts through the air in
thought, travels above the air, crosses the choruses of the stars, opens the
gates of heaven, comes to be above the angels, passes Thrones and Dominions,
crosses the Cherubim, and having come to be above every generated nature,
arrives at the inaccessible Trinity itself. There it worships the Godhead;
there it is counted worthy to become a fellow-speaker with the heavenly king.
Through it the soul, lifted aloft into the heavens, twines itself about the
Lord with unspeakable embracings, as an infant toward its own mother cries
with tears, yearning for the divine milk; and it asks for its own desires, and
receives gifts better than all visible nature. For prayer is a venerable
ambassador; it makes the heart glad, it gives rest to the soul, it begets fear
of punishment, desire of the kingdom of heaven, it teaches humility, it grants
knowledge of sin, in a word it arrays the human being with all good things,
putting around the soul, as some embroidered robe, every kind of virtue. This
also granted Samuel to Hannah, and showed him a prophet of the Lord; this also
made Elijah a zealot of the Lord; and it became the guide of the descent of
the heavenly fire upon the sacrifice. For when the priests of Baal were
calling upon the idol all day long, he, lifting up his voice from a pure
heart, and crying with heart and mouth, brought down the fire from heaven, a
testimony of the righteous prayer.
For like some eagle having flown upon the altar, by the vehemence of its
nature it consumed all as a burnt offering. And this the great servant of God
Elijah the zealot did, training us toward the spiritual things through the
events of that time, so that we too, crying to God from the heart, may urge
the unspeakable fire of the Spirit to come down upon the altar of our heart,
becoming through this a perfect whole burnt offering to the Lord. Prayer also
made Moses the lawgiver a mercy-seat of God for Israel, and planted trophies
against the enemies for the Israelites. Through prayer also Joshua, the son of
Nun, made the great and most conspicuous star in the heavens, the sun, already
cutting the ether in the midst of the vault, spend a period of day in that
same place. So then the Lord gave to this the privileges of the commandments,
so as, fleeing cities =64.465= and villages and his own disciples, to withdraw
into the deserts for the sake of prayer, that apart from tumults and human
circumstances he might converse with it in stillness with great longing,
teaching us at the same time that one who professes such a way of life must
take to deserted places, doing such a thing without distraction.
Through prayer also the great company of the apostles was counted worthy to
receive the Paraclete today; for this reason I was compelled today to go
through this subject with you. Prayer, while the body lies upon the earth,
carries the soul away to that heavenly spring, and having filled it with that
drink makes it in itself a spring of water leaping up into eternal life. This
gives a true full assurance of the things to come, furnishing henceforth a
knowledge of the goods there that is better than faith; it opens hidden,
invisible, dark treasures; it shows you the adversary; it cuts the
battle-lines of the aliens, it makes the opposing powers vanish, and it leads
to the king himself. When I say prayer, do not suppose it to be words.
It is longing toward God, unspeakable love, not accomplished from men, but
energized from divine grace, concerning which the Apostle also says: For we do
not know what we should pray as we ought, but the Spirit itself intercedes for
us with groanings unutterable. If the Lord should grant such prayer to anyone,
it is inalienable wealth, and a heavenly feeding that fills the soul; he who
has tasted it possesses an everlasting longing toward the Lord, as some
vehement fire burning his heart.
And accomplishing this first of all, with gentleness and a humble heart, paint
your house, make it bright with the light of righteousness, with good works,
as with some proven tablet, adorn your own house, and instead of walls and
mosaic beautify it with faith and long-suffering; above all laying prayer as a
roof for the completion of the house, so that you may prepare your own house
perfect for the Master, and as in a royal and conspicuous house you may
receive the Lord, having him henceforth as an image established in the temple
of the soul through his grace, because to him is the glory and the power unto
the ages of the ages. Amen.
