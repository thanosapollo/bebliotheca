* In Joannem theologum
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

A discourse of the same on the holy John the Theologian.

=59.609=

αʹ. John in Ephesus of Asia, John the boast of Asia, or rather the pillar of
the inhabited world, John the beloved disciple of Christ, and trumpet of the
Word of God, the glory of the Ephesians, and the herald of the ends, the lyre
of theology, worthily called Theologian; the Cherubic mouth, and the Seraphic
mystagogy, or even to speak beyond these I shall not refuse. For him whom the
powers above of angels glorify with trembling, John with a great voice
proclaimed with boldness: In the beginning was the Word. Blessed are you, city
of the Ephesians, because in you first sounded such a God-spoken thunder;
blessed are you, city of the Ephesians, because in you such a writing was
composed; blessed are you, because in you was such a John. For there are other
Johns in the whole inhabited world and in you, but no such John, neither in
you, nor in the whole inhabited world. Wherefore he who knows the mind of men,
and knows the hidden things of the heart, having found him such, holy and
undefiled, lofty in life, pure in heart, adorned with chastity, perfected in
love, to this one as faithful in all things he entrusted his own mother,
having handed her over, the undefiled dove, the spotless ewe-lamb, the
unblemished heifer,
the incomparable among men, the one higher than all the works of God; which no
other of the saints has been entrusted with. Blessed are you, city of the
Ephesians, because the feet of such a man have trodden you, whose pure conduct
and traces angels marveled at; blessed are you, city of the Ephesians, because
you were shown higher than every city, through John the beloved; blessed are
you, because all the Churches of the inhabited world are your disciples,
having been taught by you to theologize, In the beginning was the Word. For
just as if there is a lamp burning in some place, and many running from every
side, each having kindled from there thereafter goes with confidence, in no
way stumbling, nor wandering; so also it has happened in Ephesus. You first
having kindled the lamp of theology, all the Churches of the ends having run
to you, each kindled her own torch, theology, and returned rejoicing. In the
beginning was the Word. And in Ephesus the proper light of theology was not
diminished, and the inhabited world, holding equal lights, proclaims, In the
beginning was the Word.

=59.610= And it is necessary also to bring his contests into the midst, as
much as is possible for us, a few from the many; for all time would not
suffice for the narration of all his achievements. For the most we have heard
from the book of the Acts, how many things he, being with the apostles,
contended well. Afterward he becomes an exile under Domitian the king of the
Romans to the island called Patmos, because of the word of God and the
preaching of piety, and he writes a Church, which God showed him, and a
Revelation of mysteries unspeakable and fearful, then also his three holy
Epistles.

But consider the unspeakable goodness of God, sojourning everywhere with those
who love him; whether they are held in prison, or happen to be in exiles,
whether in the depth of the sea, or are shut up in a pit, or wherever they may
be cast, he is not separated from them, fighting with them and strengthening
them, and lightening their toils. Then having returned from the exile, he
takes Ephesus, and dwelling there he composes the Gospel being a hundred years
old, having lasted even unto a full hundred and twenty. Dwelling there he
writes the theology, or rather in the heavens, from where he fetched it; for
the citizenship of such as these is in the heavens. Dwelling in Ephesus, in
the flesh, to the ends of the inhabited world he spread out the net of
theology, saying, In the beginning was the Word. Truly Great is our Lord, and
great is his strength, and of his understanding there is no number; who said
to them, I will give you a mouth and wisdom. How many kings and rulers and
wise men have often cried out in great power, but their voice did not even
reach as far as the walls of the city? and the poor fisherman in Ephesus
uttered: In the beginning was the Word, and the voice covered all the earth.
Did you see a mouth higher than every voice? did you see a tongue sharper than
lightning? did you see a voice clearer than every thunder? did you see such a
power? a teacher from Ephesus making disciples of all the nations, and
teaching them to theologize: In the beginning was the Word. This voice fished
the inhabited world, and set it fast when it was shaken; this voice
enlightened the world, and strengthened it against the adversary; this voice
is a resurrection of many and a cause of salvation, but of some a fall and
=59.611= a demolition because of the sign spoken against; of those believing
that he was in the beginning, a resurrection; of those saying that he came to
be from things that are not, a fall and silence. For a great multitude of the
heretics then having risen up were shaking the Church, speaking injustice
against the Height, and saying that there was a time when he was not, and
before he came to be he was not, and that he came to be from things that are
not. O filthy mouths, and abominable tongues, and lips filled with the poison
of asps!

What is worse than this blasphemy? what is more dishonorable than this
atheism? Wherefore the apostle John, no longer bearing to hear their filthy
voices, composes the Gospel, depositing with the Church a great treasure, the
fourth river, the precious pearl. For having returned, as was said before,
from the exile to Ephesus, and having perceived then that the books of the
remaining evangelists had been completed, and almost all God-inspired
Scripture for the building of the Church, but the fourth foundation of the
inhabited world is lacking, and that without this it is not possible for the
inhabited world to stand, he was reckoning in himself saying: Why do I delay?
why, he says, do I still forbear? why do I not bring into the midst the
mystery hidden from the ages? why do I hide for myself the wisdom from the
ages, which falling upon the immortal fountain I drew? why do I not publish
him whom angels do not know? why do I not reveal to the ends him whom no one
knows except the Father? why do I not write that which Matthew and Mark and
Luke, having kept silence through a praiseworthy timidity, passed by, having
fulfilled the things commanded to them? Wherefore I too will speak according
to the gift given me from above. Matthew wrote as much as he had room for,
according to his own power, and Mark and Luke likewise according to the supply
of the Holy Spirit set forth their own books as doctrine in a manner befitting
God. I too will write and add to those before the fourth fountain of life.

For the word concerning theology is lacking for a God-constituted voice, and
the world is in danger in this part. I will write a book, through which every
mouth speaking injustice against God may be stopped; I will write a book
covering all wisdom in the world; I will write a book, not narrating
concerning a man. For the Church does not lack the things Moses wrote
concerning these, concerning heaven and earth and seas and fishes and birds
and four-footed things, and creeping things and plants and seeds and
luminaries and foods and the rest of creation; but I, having left behind all
things that come to be from time and in time, will speak concerning the
timeless and uncreated God Word, who before all the ages was unspeakably
begotten of the Father, concerning whom this Moses was not able to speak; but
I Can do all things in Christ who strengthens me. These things the apostle
John considering in himself, and holding the writing reed in his hand, and
thinking how he should begin the theology, rejoicing in soul, but trembling in
the hand, he becomes lifted up, and being in body in Ephesus, in the pure
heart he was aloft in the spirit.

Having left behind then all the earth, he crossed through the air poured out
in the midst, he ran past the arrays of the stars, he passed the armies of the
angels, the standing choirs of the archangels, and all the noetic powers, the
ranks of Cherubim and Seraphim, and all things, as many as were brought forth
from things that are not, he left behind, and he went beyond all the heavens,
and whatever was not he ran past. He approached the bounds of the
unattainable, and there was no one hindering or impeding his ascent. For
everyone spotless in body, and pure in heart, and innocent in soul, and humble
in spirit, is neither impeded by the aerial spirits, nor hindered by the
heavenly powers from going up into the Jerusalem above, where all the blessed
are gathered, as this blessed apostle, the lesser =59.612= John, and greater
than the great holy John.

Through all things then he passed, and no one was opposing. For angels seeing
from earth a man more glorious than angels, having nothing to say, were
silent; and the choirs of the angels did not dare to hold back another angel
from earth, bright with virginity, full of sanctification, pure in heart,
filled with fragrance. The gates of the heavens again having seen a strange
face glorified, flashing forth divine rays above the sun, and supposing this
to be he whom Jesus loved, who reclined on his breast in much purity, from
where also such glory is poured around him, having been opened eagerly they
granted the entrance. Then he drew near, leaving the untrodden things; he
arrived at the depth above. What then shall we say to these things? True is
the divine voice through the prophet saying, But them that glorify me I will
glorify; as also concerning this the God-father David prophesied, that The
mighty of God of the earth were exceedingly lifted up; and again, To the
saints that are in his earth the Lord has made wonderful all his wills in
them. For what is greater than this wonder, that an earthen man should arrive
at such a height? In much boldness, and as many things as it is possible to be
seen, he beheld, and him wishing to contend even into the things beyond, Whom
no one of men has seen, nor can see, as Paul the apostle writes; as also John
himself the like, that No one has seen God at any time. What then shall we say
to these things? That Paul wrote well saying: Whom no one of men has seen, nor
can see. For here it is not the things according to men, nor the things
according to nature, but the things above nature; nor of slavery, but of
adoption and friendship (For you are my friends, I will no longer call you
slaves); nor things not clean, but clean and being spoken, as the divine voice
says to them, that You are clean; and the clean in heart shall see God.

These greatest and divine gifts then John bearing, and having taken courage,
having stepped upon the high things, arrived at the boundless sea, and having
beheld with the undefiled eyes of his heart in the bosom of the beginningless
Father the co-beginningless Word, who before all ages was unspeakably begotten
of the Father, and no longer being strong enough to hold that unutterable joy
in himself, his holy and seraphic mouth, even with a great voice he cried out
saying: In the beginning was the Word. See for me the understanding of a
fisherman, consider the wisdom of an unlettered man. Did you see for me an
unspeakable height of a teacher, sitting in Ephesus, and beholding the things
above the heavens? In the spirit he went abroad to the depth above, and having
fished the theology from the paternal bosom, with the body below he was
writing: In the beginning was the Word. Hear, you faithful, and be
established, In the beginning was the Word; hear, you garrulous contradictors,
and be ashamed, In the beginning was the Word. No longer speak boldly,
blasphemers, no longer war against the Church, antichrists; no longer
transgress, no longer lift up a horn, Lift not up your horn on high, and speak
not injustice against God, saying that There was a time when he was not, and
before he came to be he was not, and that he came to be from things that are
not. Cease your tongue from evil, and your lips from speaking deceit. For he
was and was before, and always was, neither having begun, nor having ceased to
be, nor in time the timeless one, nor a creature, or a work; for he himself is
maker and craftsman of all things, both seen and unseen. For all things came
to be through him. Truly also now is the time to say that prophetic saying,
This is our God; another shall not be reckoned =59.613= beside him.

He found out every way of knowledge, and gave it to John who was beloved by
him. For see for me through one voice how He set right the whole inhabited
world, which shall not be shaken; see for me one man sitting in Ephesus, and
having taught the whole world theology; consider, how many commonwealths
toward the east, how many countries, how many Churches, how many again toward
the settings of the sun, and great Rome likewise, and upon the sides, and upon
the parts of the south, and the country of the Cappadocians, and all the
things in the midst, islands, deserts, and the extremities of the earth. Think
again of the multitudes of the Churches, the clergy, and the multitudes of the
people, both of men and of women, if you are able to number these.

But it is easier to number the sand of the sea than such multitudes. And these
all are disciples of John, having been taught by him to theologize, In the
beginning was the Word; and in the marketplace they chant this, In the
beginning was the Word; and travelers, and men of the sea and sailors sing
this, In the beginning was the Word, and in the deserts, and in the mountains
both unlearned and wise, and small and great and everyone who understands,
theologizes these things, In the beginning was the Word; and then in the
multiplying of those crying everywhere unceasingly the Was and was, those
saying the Was not have failed. Those theologizing have been multiplied, and
those talking vanity have failed; the pious have advanced, and the impious
have stumbled; the faithful have been gladdened, and the unfaithful have
melted. The bow of the mighty has been weakened, and they that were weak
have girded themselves with power. The foolish things of the world have been
exposed, and the wise of the world have been brought to nothing.

But until when shall I not stop the word concerning them? For even if I
persist in speaking, I shall not arrive at narrating the height of their
glory. For the glory which the teacher received from the Father, he has given
to them. And why then do I persist traversing unattainable things? For even
the things we have dared to say are not as much as is worthy of the present
choir of the holy disciples, but as much as is possible to our power; for
every man is at a loss to praise these worthily. Wherefore I too speak with
fear the things I speak concerning these, lest somehow through my own
insufficiency, and the smallness and poverty of the word, I make small their
praises, and prepare them to be indignant against me. Nevertheless as being
sympathetic toward all, they will even now forgive our weakness. For if being
reviled they bless, being persecuted they endure, being defamed they exhort,
much more being praised according to our power they will not be indignant,
knowing the weakness of nature, even if they have now received the thing above
nature from the God who loved them. And then those who were once poor have
become rich, the inglorious glorious, the unlettered wise, the fishers of
fishes, fishers of men; and thus having finished the course they went abroad
toward the life that has no end, toward =59.614= their own teacher and Lord
Jesus Christ; to him the glory. Amen.

βʹ. Having handed over to their successors the nets and the hunting-gear, and
the authority, which they have received from him who has all authority in
heaven and on earth. For behold, he says, I give you the authority to tread
upon serpents and scorpions, and upon all the power of the enemy, and nothing
shall in any way harm you.

These then as was said before, their authority and their rule and their zeal
the honorable teachers succeed to, the hierarchs, and shepherds of the flock
of Christ, walking in the track of the apostles, the noble champions of the
Trinity, who have run past the matter of the law, and have received the
life-giving Spirit, who after the manner of a plough dig up, and deepen, and
search, and have manifested the mind in the divine Scriptures clearly to all,
who have made the faith distinct and have driven out the evil-belief, who have
established the orthodox with the divine dogmas, and have broken the
evil-doctrined with the divine arrows, who have found the hidden wine of
compunction, and have given the thirsty flock to drink, who have taken up the
pastoral pipe, and have trumpeted the divine things; the ministers and
guardians and workers of the vineyard, the orators of the divine dogmas, and
swords against the heretics, those set as bishops by the Holy Spirit, as holy
Paul said: Shepherd the Church of God; who have not declined the coming
afflictions, and have received the promised crown of life; who have fled the
youthful desires, and have inherited the blessed hope; the generals of the
great king, rich in the word of God, and poor in spirit; imitators of the
apostles and types of the faithful; herd-leaders of the cities and heralds of
the inhabited world; who have laid down their souls for the flock, and have
poured out their blood for the faith; who have imitated that ancient Abraham,
and have rested in his bosom; in the same manner also these avengers of the
Lord, having received the report concerning the ill-named and evil-doctrined,
who having twisted the divine Scriptures and confused the truth, and having
turned aside from the straight way take souls captive, dragging them into the
eternal darkness of heresy, having been gathered together all at once of one
mind the faithful, having taken up a Davidic zeal, after the manner of stones
the divine oracles from the torrent, that is from the Gospel, and the
unbreakable faith of the holy Trinity, they arrayed a war against the
numberless and godless heretics, of one soul agreeing in the one and saying
with the Apostle, One Lord, one faith, one baptism, One God and Father of all,
one Holy Spirit, the illumination of all, one Godhead, one God; to him the
glory and the might always, now and ever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
