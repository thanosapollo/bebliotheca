Of the holy Clement
bishop of the Romans
Second Epistle to the Corinthians.
CHAPTER 1.
Brethren, thus we ought to think concerning Jesus Christ, as concerning God,
as concerning the judge of living and dead; and we ought not to think little
concerning our salvation. For in thinking little concerning him we also hope
to receive little; and they who hear as concerning little things sin, and we
sin not knowing from where we were called and by whom and to what place, and
how much Jesus Christ endured to suffer for our sake. What recompense then
shall we give him, or what fruit worthy of that which he himself gave us? And
how many holy things do we owe him? For he granted us the light, as a father
he addressed us as sons, he saved us who were perishing. What praise then
shall we give him, or wage of recompense for what we received? Being maimed in
understanding, worshipping stones and wood and gold and silver and bronze,
works of men; and our whole life was nothing else but death. Being therefore
wrapped in dimness and full of such mist in our seeing, we recovered our
sight, putting off that cloud which we were wrapped in, by his will. For he
had mercy on us and being moved with compassion he saved us, having seen in us
much wandering and destruction, and that we had no hope of salvation except
that from him. For he called us when we were not, and he willed that we
should be from not-being.
CHAPTER 2.
"Rejoice, barren one who does not bear, break forth and cry, you who do not
travail, because many are the children of the desolate more than of her who
has the husband." What he said, "Rejoice, barren one who does not bear," he
said of us; for our church was barren before children were given her.
And what he said, "Cry, you who do not travail," this he means: that we should
offer our prayers simply to God, and not grow weary as those in travail. And
what he said, "Because many are the children of the desolate more than of her
who has the husband": since our people seemed to be desolate from God, but now
having believed we have become more than those who seemed to have God. And
another scripture also says that I did not come to call righteous, but
sinners. This he means, that one must save those who are perishing. For that
is great and marvelous, not to make firm the things that stand, but the things
that are falling. Thus also Christ willed to save the things that were
perishing, and he saved many, having come and called us already perishing.
CHAPTER 3.
Therefore, he having done so great mercy toward us, first, that we who live do
not sacrifice to the dead gods and do not worship them, but we have known
through
him the Father of the truth: what is the knowledge toward him, except not to
deny him through whom we knew him?
And he himself also says: "Him who has confessed me before men, I will confess
before my Father." This then is our wage, if therefore we confess him through
whom we were saved. And in what do we confess him? In doing what he says and
not disobeying his commandments, and not honoring him with lips only, but from
the whole heart and from the whole understanding. And he says also in Isaiah:
"This people honors me with the lips, but their heart is far away from me."
CHAPTER 4.
Let us therefore not only call him Lord; for this will not save us.
For he says: "Not every one who says to me, Lord, Lord, shall be saved, but he
who does righteousness." So then, brethren, let us confess him in the works,
in loving one another, in not committing adultery nor speaking against one
another nor being jealous, but being self-controlled, merciful, good; and we
ought to suffer with one another, and not to be lovers of money. In these
works let us confess him and not in the contrary ones; and we ought not to
fear men more, but God.
For this reason, when you do these things, the Lord said: "If you are gathered
with me in my bosom and do not do my commandments, I will cast you off and I
will say to you: Go from me, I do not know you, from where you are, workers of
lawlessness."
CHAPTER 5.
Whence, brethren, leaving the sojourning of this world let us do the will of
him who called us, and let us not fear to go out from this world. For the Lord
says: "You shall be as lambs in the midst of wolves." And Peter answering says
to him: "If then the wolves tear the lambs in pieces?" Jesus said to Peter:
"Let the lambs not fear the wolves after they have died; and you, do not fear
those who kill you and are able to do nothing to you, but fear him who after
you have died has authority of soul and body to cast into Gehenna of fire."
And know, brethren, that the sojourn in this world of this flesh is little and
of short time, but the promise of Christ is great and marvelous, and rest of
the coming kingdom and of eternal life. What then is it, having done, to
attain them, except to walk holily and righteously and to consider these
worldly things as alien and not to desire them? For in our desiring to acquire
these things we fall from the righteous way.

CHAPTER 6.
And the Lord says: "No household servant can serve two lords." If we wish to
serve both God and mammon, it is unprofitable for us. For what is the profit,
if someone gain the whole world, but lose his soul? And this age and the
coming one are two enemies. This one speaks adultery and corruption and love
of money and deceit, but that one bids farewell to these. We cannot therefore
be friends of the two; but we must
having bid farewell to this one, make use of that one. We think that it is
better to hate the things here, because they are little and of short time and
corruptible, and to love those, the good things that are incorruptible. For
doing the will of Christ we shall find rest; but if not, nothing will deliver
us from the eternal punishment, if we disobey his commandments. And the
scripture also says in Ezekiel, that if Noah and Job and Daniel arise, they
will not deliver their children in the captivity.
And if even such righteous men cannot by their own righteousnesses deliver
their children, we, if we do not keep the baptism pure and undefiled, with
what confidence shall we enter into the kingdom of God? Or who will be our
advocate, if we are not found having holy and righteous works?
CHAPTER 7.
So then, my brethren, let us contend, knowing that the contest is in hand and
that into the corruptible contests many sail down, but not all are crowned,
except those who have toiled much and contended well. Let us therefore
contend, that we all may be crowned. So let us run the straight way, the
incorruptible contest, and let many of us sail down into it and contend, that
we also may be crowned; and if we cannot all be crowned, at least let us come
near the crown. We must know that he who contends in the corruptible contest,
if he is found corrupting, is whipped, removed, and thrown outside the
stadium.
What do you think? He who has corrupted the contest of incorruption, what
shall he suffer? For of those who have not kept, he says, the seal, their worm
shall not die and their fire shall not be quenched, and they shall be for a
spectacle to all flesh.
CHAPTER 8.
While therefore we are on earth, let us repent. For we are clay in the hand of
the craftsman; for in the manner that the potter, if he makes a vessel and it
is twisted or crushed in his hands, shapes it again, but if he has already
cast it into the furnace of fire, he will no longer help it; so also we, while
we are in this world, in the flesh let us repent of the evil things we have
done from the whole heart, that we may be saved by the Lord, while we have a
time of repentance.
For after we have gone out from the world we are no longer able there to
confess or still to repent.
So, brethren, having done the will of the Father and having kept the flesh
pure and having guarded the commandments of the Lord we shall receive eternal
life. For the Lord says in the Gospel: "If you did not keep the little, who
will give you the great? For I say to you, that he who is faithful in least is
faithful also in much." So then this he means: keep the flesh pure and the
seal spotless, that we may receive the eternal life.
CHAPTER 9.
And let none of you say that this flesh is not judged nor rises. Know: in what
were you saved, in what did you recover sight, except being in this flesh? We
must therefore
keep the flesh as a temple of God. For in the manner that you were called in
the flesh, you will also come in the flesh.
If Christ the Lord who saved us, being first spirit, became flesh and thus
called us, so also we in this flesh shall receive the wage.
Let us therefore love one another, that we may all come into the kingdom of
God. While we have time to be healed, let us give ourselves over to God who
heals, giving him a recompense.
What sort? To repent from a sincere heart. For he is a foreknower of all
things and knowing the things in our heart. Let us therefore give him praise,
not from mouth only, but also from heart, that he may receive us as sons. For
the Lord also said: "These are my brethren who do the will of my Father."
CHAPTER 10.
So, my brethren, let us do the will of the Father who called us, that we may
live, and let us pursue virtue rather; and let us leave wickedness as a
forerunner of our sins, and let us flee impiety, lest evils overtake us. For
if we are eager to do good, peace will pursue us.
For on account of this cause it is not possible to find a man, such as bring
in human fears, having chosen rather the enjoyment here than the coming
promise.
For they do not know how great a torment the enjoyment here has, and what
luxury the coming promise has.
And if they alone were doing these things, it would be tolerable; but now they
persist in badly teaching the unblamable souls, not knowing that they will
have a double judgment, both themselves and those who hear them.
CHAPTER 11.
Let us therefore serve God in a pure heart, and we shall be righteous; but if
we do not serve because we do not believe the promise of God, we shall be
wretched. For the prophetic word also says: "Wretched are the double-minded,
who doubt in the heart,
who say: These things we heard of old also in the time of our fathers, but we,
waiting day from day, have seen none of these things. Fools, compare
yourselves to a tree; take a vine: first it sheds leaves, then a shoot comes
to be, after these an unripe grape, then a standing cluster; so also my people
had unsettlements and afflictions; afterward it will receive the good things."
So, my brethren, let us not be double-minded, but having hoped let us endure,
that we may also carry off the wage. For he is faithful who promised to render
the recompenses to each of his works. If therefore we do righteousness before
God, we shall enter into his kingdom and receive the promises which ear has
not heard nor eye seen, nor has it gone up upon a heart of man.


CHAPTER 12.
Let us therefore await the kingdom of God every hour in love and
righteousness, since we do not know the day of the appearing of God.
For the Lord himself, being asked by someone when his kingdom will come, said:
"When the two shall be one, and the outside as the inside, and the male with
the female, neither male nor female." And the two are one, when we speak truth
to one another and in two bodies there would be unfeignedly one soul. And the
outside as the inside, this he means: he means the inside is the soul, and
the outside he means the body. In the manner therefore that your body
appears, so also let your soul be manifest in the good works.
And the male with the female, neither male nor female, this he means: that a
brother seeing a sister may think nothing concerning her that is female, nor
she think anything concerning him that is male. When you do these things, he
says, the kingdom of my Father will come.
Brethren then, let us at last repent sometime, let us sober up toward the
good; for we are full of much folly and wickedness. Let us wipe out from us
the former sins and having repented from the soul let us be saved, and let us
not become men-pleasers nor wish to please ourselves only, but also the people
outside, in the righteousness, that the name may not be blasphemed on our
account.
For the Lord says: "Continually my name is blasphemed among all the nations,"
and again: "Woe through whom my name is blasphemed." In what is it blasphemed?
In your not doing what I wish. For the nations, hearing from our mouth the
oracles of God as good and great, marvel; then having learned that our works
are not worthy of the words which we speak, from there they turn to blasphemy,
saying it is some myth and wandering. For when they hear from us that God
says: "No thanks to you if you love those who love you, but thanks to you if
you love the enemies and those who hate you"; when they hear these things,
they marvel at the excess of the goodness; but when they see that not only do
we not love those who hate, but not even those who love, they laugh at us, and
the name is blasphemed.
So, brethren, doing the will of our Father God we shall be of the first
church, the spiritual one, that was created before sun and moon; but if we do
not do the will of the Lord, we shall be of the scripture that says: "My house
has become a den of robbers." So then let us choose to be of the church of
life, that we may be saved.
And I do not think that you are ignorant that a living church is a body of
Christ; for the scripture says: "God made the man male and female"; the male
is Christ, the female the church; and further the books and the apostles say
that the church is not now, but from above. For it was spiritual, as also
our Jesus, but was manifested in the last of the days, that he might save us.
And the church, being spiritual, was manifested in the flesh of Christ,
showing us that if anyone of us keep her in the flesh and not corrupt her, he
will receive her in the Holy Spirit; for this flesh is an antitype of the
spirit; no one therefore having corrupted the antitype will partake of the
authentic. So then this he means, brethren: keep the flesh, that you may
partake of the spirit.
And if we say the flesh is the church and the spirit Christ, then he who has
insulted the flesh has insulted the church. Such a one therefore will not
partake of the spirit, which is Christ.

So great a life and incorruption can this flesh partake of, the Holy Spirit
having been joined to it, that no one is able either to declare or to speak
the things which the Lord prepared for his elect.
And I do not think that I have given a small counsel concerning self-control,
which if someone does he will not repent, but he will both save himself and me
who counseled. For it is no small wage to turn back a wandering and perishing
soul unto being saved. For this recompense we have to render to God who
created us, if the one speaking and the one hearing both speak and hear with
faith and love. Let us therefore remain upon the things we believed, righteous
and holy, that with boldness we may ask God who says: "While you are still
speaking I will say: Behold I am present." For this saying is a sign of a
great promise; for the Lord says he is more ready toward giving than the one
asking.
Partaking therefore of so great kindness let us not begrudge ourselves to
obtain so many good things. For as much pleasure these words have for those
who have done them, so much condemnation they have for those who have
disobeyed.
So, brethren, having received no small occasion unto repenting, having time
let us turn to God who called us, while we still have him who receives us.
For if we bid farewell to these sensual pleasures and conquer our soul in not
doing its wicked desires, we shall partake of the mercy of Jesus. And know
that the day of judgment is already coming as a burning oven, and some of the
heavens shall melt and all the earth as lead melting upon fire; and then the
hidden and the manifest works of men shall appear. Almsgiving then is good as
repentance of sin; fasting is better than prayer, and almsgiving than both;
and love covers a multitude of sins, and prayer from a good conscience
delivers from death. Blessed is everyone found full in these; for almsgiving
becomes a lightening of sin.
Let us therefore repent from the whole heart, that none of us may perish.
For if we have commandments that we should also do this, to draw away
from the idols and to catechize, how much more must a soul already knowing God
not perish? Let us therefore assist one another, and lead up those who are
weak toward the good, that we may all be saved and turn one another and
admonish. And let us not only now seem to believe and attend while we are
being admonished by the presbyters, but also when we have departed to home,
let us remember the commandments of the Lord and let us not be dragged aside
by the worldly desires, but coming more frequently let us try to advance in
the commandments of the Lord, that all thinking the same thing we may be
gathered unto the life.
For the Lord said: "I come to gather all the nations, tribes and tongues." And
this he means the day of his appearing, when having come he will redeem us,
each according to his works. And the unbelievers will see his glory and the
might, and they will be astonished seeing the kingdom of the world in Jesus,
saying: "Woe to us, because you were, and we did not know and we did not
believe and we were not persuaded by the presbyters who announced to us
concerning our salvation"; and their worm shall not die and their fire shall
not be quenched, and they shall be for a spectacle to all flesh. He means that
day of judgment, when they shall see those who among us acted impiously and
reasoned away the commandments of Jesus Christ.
But the righteous, having done well and having endured the torments and having
hated the sensual pleasures of the soul, when they see those who missed the
mark and denied
Jesus through the words or through the works, how they are punished with
terrible torments in unquenchable fire, will be giving glory to their God,
saying that there will be hope for him who has served God from the whole
heart.
Let us therefore also become of those who give thanks, who have served God,
and not of the impious who are judged. For I myself also, being altogether a
sinner and not yet having fled the temptation, but still being in the midst of
the instruments of the devil, am eager to pursue righteousness, that I may be
able at least to come near it, fearing the judgment that is coming.
So, brothers and sisters, after the God of the truth I am reading you an
appeal to attend to the things written, that you may both save yourselves and
the one who reads among you; for as a wage I ask of you to repent from the
whole heart, giving yourselves salvation and life. For having done this we
shall set a mark for all the young, who wish to labor about the piety and the
kindness of God.
And let us not be displeased and vexed, we the unwise, when someone admonishes
us and turns us from injustice to righteousness. For sometimes doing wicked
things we do not know it, on account of the double-mindedness and unbelief
that is in our breasts, and we have been darkened in the understanding by the
vain desires.
Let us therefore do righteousness, that we may be saved unto the end. Blessed
are those who obey these ordinances; even if for a little time they suffer
hardship in this world, they will gather the immortal fruit of the
resurrection. Therefore let the pious man not be grieved, if he is distressed
in the present times; a blessed time awaits him; that man above, having come
to life again with the fathers, will rejoice unto the age without grief.
But let not that either trouble your understanding, that we see the unjust
growing rich and the servants of God being in straits. Let us therefore
believe, brothers and sisters: we contend in a trial of the living God and are
trained by the present life, that we may be crowned in the coming one.
None of the righteous received a quick fruit, but he awaits it. For if God
were rendering the wage of the righteous speedily, we would immediately
practice commerce and not godliness; for we would seem to be righteous,
pursuing not the godly thing but the profitable. And for this reason a divine
judgment harmed a spirit that was not righteous, and weighed it down with
bonds.
To the only invisible God, Father of the truth, who sent forth to us the
savior and pioneer of incorruption, through whom he also manifested to us the
truth and the heavenly life, to him the glory unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
