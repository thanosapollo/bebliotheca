Of our father among the saints Athanasius, Homily on the Seed.

We have gathered on the day of the Sabbath, not being sick with Judaism; for
we do not lay hold of false Sabbaths; but we have come on the Sabbath, about
to worship Jesus, the Lord of the Sabbath. For of old among the ancients there
was the honorable Sabbath; but the Lord transferred the day of the Sabbath to
the Lord's Day; and it is not we who of ourselves have despised the Sabbath,
but it is the prophet who rejected it and said: «Your new moons and your
Sabbaths my soul hates.» For as long as things worthy of the matters of the
legislation were being done, or until the Teacher had not yet come, the things
of the pedagogue were in force; but when the Teacher came, the pedagogue was
abolished, and when the sun rose, the lamp ceased. And the reading also has
become timely for us, in sequence, concerning the Sabbath. For we were hearing
just now how Jesus was going through the grainfields, and how he healed the
withered hand; and it seems to me to be well to say a few things on the
reading of the Gospel.

Our Savior was going through the grainfields, and the disciples were following
him, and plucking ears of grain, they were eating; and Jews were rebuking
them, saying: «It is not lawful to do this on the Sabbath.» According to what
law, O Jews? According to what legislation of Moses? According to what saying
of a prophet? =28.145= Where has the hungry man been forbidden to be fed?
Where has it been written not to fill an empty belly? But those who reproach
feeding on the Sabbath were themselves working murders on the Sabbath against
the master of the house; for the Scripture says in the Gospel concerning the
day on which God suffered: «Now that day was a great day of the Sabbath.» And
they slay the lawgiver on the Sabbath, but they do not want disciples to be
fed on the Sabbath. For this reason the Savior, in the things just now read,
was asking them: What is it lawful to do on the Sabbath? to do good or to do
evil? to save a soul, or to destroy it? You reproach feeding, but you
accomplish murder? For always the Jews strain out the gnat, but swallow the
camel; always they are exact about tithing the rue and the herb; but the
weightier things of the law they do not keep. And as the disciples were going
through, they were indignant that they were rubbing ears of grain with their
hands and eating. They were not cutting with iron, since they would have been
accused as of a work; nor did they use other tools, lest they be accused as
not being fed, but as working. But now what do you blame in that which was
given by God for salvation? For if God did not forbid feeding on the Sabbath,
why do you reproach this, which God did not forbid?

The Savior then was going through the grainfields; the grain of wheat, through
the grainfields, the intelligible grain of wheat, who fell into one place, and
rose up abundant into the inhabited world; for he himself was saying
concerning himself, that «Unless the grain of wheat, having fallen into the
earth, die, it remains itself alone; but if it die, it bears much fruit.» Let
us say it also otherwise: Jesus then was going through the grainfields; for he
himself is sometimes called a grain of wheat because of the nourishing
quality; and at another time he is a sower, according to what is said in the
Gospels: «Behold, the sower went out to sow.» Jesus then sows abundantly; but
the fruit-bearing comes according to the underlying soil; for where the earth
is rocky, the seed is easily dried up, not by powerlessness of the sowing, but
by the poor condition of the earth; for the seed is active; but the earth is
barren, not possessing depth. And the earth not holding out against moisture,
the solar rays striking more, dry up the seed. And the cause comes not from
the feebleness of the seed, but from the unsoundness of the earth. Again the
seed is sown into thorny earth; and the seed is active; but it is choked by
the thorns, and the energy stored within is not permitted to bear fruit
because of the disturbance from without. But if it enjoys the good earth, it
again does not make the fruit alike, but in thirty, and sixty, and a hundred.
The seed is of one kind, but the fruit is various; and various are the
dispositions of those being taught.

The sower then went out to sow; and some things he worked by being present
himself, and some through the disciples. And you have it written in the Acts
of the Apostles, =28.148= that after Stephen was stoned the Scripture speaks
thus: «Those then who were scattered,» not scattered through weakness; for
they were not divided in the faith; but scattered. For having been made into
heavenly bread by the energy of the one who sowed, having gone through every
teaching of life-bearing, they sowed operations. Jesus then, the only-begotten
Son of God, who sows the thoughts, was going through the grainfields, not only
the sower of the seeds, but also of the sensible things. For to this one the
Father was saying from the beginning: «Let the earth bring forth herb of
grass, sowing seed according to kind and according to likeness.» The Father
was speaking, the Son was accomplishing; the one was commanding, the other was
bringing the things commanded to completion; and thus from the Father through
the Son there was one completed work, one world-making being completed through
Father and Son. He then who sows the thoughts, and who worked the sensible
things by the Father's nod, this one was passing through the grainfields.

And there is a great wonder-working of the seeds. And let us see in seasons of
sowing the things concerning seeds; conversing in winter hours about the
sprouting of the earth, not in order that we may give a natural account of the
things seen, but in order that we may worship the wonder-worker. For men,
working the things in their power, having yoked ploughing oxen, cut open the
earth and soften its face, so that rains may not run past, but sinking in may
give back fruit-bearing; and so that the seeds, thrown into the simplicity of
the earth, may have a double enjoyment: one of the depth and of the softening,
and a second of being hidden, and not being consumed by birds. But the man
does his own part; the fruit-bearing is not from man. For it is of man to sow;
but of God to give increase. And by the command of God clouds, having drawn up
the waters from the depths of the sea, and having changed them from saltiness
into sweetness, pour them upon the face of the earth; and rain of one kind,
having come down, works seeds of many kinds. And it works wheat, and bean and
millet and spelt. One thing came down, and many are the things that sprout;
and the single kind of the rains is scattered into abundant fruit-bearing; in
order that it may be shown that what bears fruit comes about not by the ease
of the one who sows, but by the energy of God. And first the seed comes up as
a kind of green shoot, and often a tare has been sown together with wheat; but
the likeness of the leaves does not permit it to appear which is wheat and
which is tare. But when the seed becomes upright, and comes into height, the
ear distinguishes, and the fruit-bearing shows the underlying thing, which is
wheat, and which is tare.

Have you understood what is said? Come then with me to the more spiritual.
Jesus sowed through the apostles the word of the kingdom of the heavens
throughout the whole inhabited world. Hearing, having received the preaching,
holds it with itself; and for a while it works a sprouting of foliage, a zeal
concerning the Church, and we are gathered together in the same place, both
the wheat-givers and the tares, the faithful and the hypocrite, that we may
speak more truly =28.149= what is being proclaimed; and we, the farmers of the
Church, having applied the mattock of words to the things sown, farm toward
fruit-bearing; but we do not yet know the underlying thing; for the likeness
of the foliage often deceives those who preside. But when the teaching
proceeds into work, and the fruit of the works is thickened, then it appears
who is the faithful, and who is the hypocrite. And how and in what manner
there often arrives in a city a man able to teach in Greek, who, having the
hearing charmed, hastens to the Church, not to snatch the healing of the soul,
but only the beauty of the words. The one who speaks fluently has withdrawn;
the tare of the Church has also withdrawn; for it does not have the
wheat-quality, the faithful. But the faithful man is eager to listen, whether
the things said are spoken fluently, or in Syriac, or in Latin, or in a
different tongue. For he does not seek words, but works. «For our word and our
preaching was not in persuasive words of human wisdom, but in demonstration of
Spirit and of power.» What is the profit, if we speak in Greek, but are
barbarians in our disposition? What is the profit, if the word is
well-composed, but the manner is ill-composed? It is far better neither to
know the speech of the Greeks, and to possess the law of God in the heart. For
if the Church were a sophist's school, it would be a time for fluency; but
since a contest of manners, and fruit-bearing is the thing set before us, and
an expectation of heavens is the thing expected, let not tongue be sought, but
let the manner be set right.

Again others are sick with the people-pleasing of men, and they play-act a
season; and if a notable person comes either from the streets or from among
men, and one professing Christianity, they run to the Church, in order to show
the face to a man. The notable one has gone out, the hypocrite has gone out
with him, and he has his reward. He came because of men, he pleased a man, he
received nothing from God; for he did not show his mind to that one. Such are
the tares of the Church. But the Word, the farmer of the Church, has something
marvelous; for he even makes tares into wheat, if he wills. For since the
movement of men is of their own choice, and the disposition is self-governed,
the matter lies upon you; if you wish tares, you are food of irrational
animals; if you wish, you are changed and become wheat. Do you seek an image
of the words? Take what is sought from trees. The wild olive, having been
grafted, becomes a cultivated olive, and a wild vine, having received a shoot
of a most tame vine, forgets the old things of the root, and takes up the
newness of the old. You too forget the first things, and take up the things
that are said; and let go of the tare, and take up the wheat-quality; in order
that you yourself also may be able to come within the intelligible
ecclesiastical threshing-floor.

And all these things have been said to you because of the Gospel reading that
converses about seeds, because of the one who sows in the souls, Jesus the
redeemer of souls, who goes through the grainfields. =28.152= He then was
going through on purpose on the Sabbath, leading the disciples through the
grainfields, and the disciples going through were plucking ears of grain, and
rubbing them with their hands they were eating, working their hands as a newer
threshing-floor for the ears. But the Jews were rebuking the disciples who
were being fed, and they are indignant toward the teacher: «See, what are your
disciples doing?» Having alienated themselves from righteousness, they did not
say, The partners of our disciples; for they had banished themselves from the
learning; but, «See, what your disciples are doing.» They point out the things
that appear. Do you point out the things seen to the knower of hearts? Have
you not often received the proof, that he knows also the knowledges of the
hearts? Does he who said: «Why do you reason evil things in yourselves?», he
who knows reasonings, not know the rubbing of ears of grain?

A newer dead man, free among the dead. The other dead, as if wrapped in bonds,
were being led away in prison on the way; but Jesus became dead for us, and
went down into the underworld among the dead; and having come among the dead
he was not only free, but also a liberator of the dead. And that you may learn
that he also became a liberator of the dead, hear the Gospels saying: «And
many bodies of the saints who had fallen asleep he raised.» He was placed in a
tomb, and many tombs were opened. He then was the one free among the dead,
whom even acquaintances left. And the Gospel says that all the disciples,
having left him, fled. While he was still living they flee, and when he had
been crucified they die for him; since nothing remained on the cross, but
after the cross he received life again. And do not attend to me alone, but to
the psalm that was just now read to me. Attend to what is said; I read, you
see the resurrection from the sequence. «I became as a man without help, free
among the dead. They set me in the lowest pit,» as we have said. And after
these things: «But I with my prayer to you, O Lord, and in the morning my
prayer would anticipate.» The dead man prays; if he has not risen, does he
pray? For after narrating the burial and the deadness, then he narrated the
prayer that came to be toward the Father.

We then honor the Lord's Day because of the resurrection; but the Jews even
now still cling to the Sabbath, even after Isaiah has said: «And your Sabbaths
my soul hates.» I have nothing in common with the Sabbath which God hated. I
do not mean the cycles of the days, but the supposed Judaism. For God does not
hate the days, but those who work badly in the days. And now they cling to the
Sabbath, and then they were rebuking the Savior, saying: «See, what your
disciples are doing, which it is not lawful to do on the Sabbath.» The Savior
to them: =28.153= Have you not even read this, which David did? The one from
David remembers David; the one from David according to the flesh, but before
David according to the spirit; the one according to the flesh son of David,
but according to the spirit master of David; the one who sprouted from the
root of Jesse, the marvelous rod, which crushes the race of the Jews, but
gathers the race of the nations as a flock. For he himself is the one who has
said: And I will lead you under my rod; and much is the word concerning the
rod of Jesus. He was saying then to the Jews: «Have you not even read this,
which David did, when he himself hungered, and those with him? how he entered
into the house of God, and took the loaves of the presentation and ate, which
it is not lawful to do except for the priests alone?»

The wise hearer then, and one accustomed to the ecclesiastical doctrines and
readings, knows the history, and has no need of the narration; but since a
great people has lately flowed together to us, having a superficial hearing of
the divine oracles, but not having taken the depth of the understanding into
the breast, it seems to me to be well to run over and show the history, in
order that we may know for the sake of what the Savior mentioned this.

There was a king among the ancients, David, a shepherd first, and a king
second. And he did not take the kingdom by seizing it, but, when Saul had
sinned and been rejected, David was anointed by Samuel. And having been
anointed for the kingdom, he did not at once seize the palace, but having
served Saul for many times, later, when God willed, he laid hold of the
kingdom. He was then plotted against by Saul, having done good to this one;
and he was also plotted against by Absalom, his own child. These things truly
happened to David; but this man had certain images of the presence of the
Savior. For it was necessary that the forefather also have images of the
descendant. «The book of the generation of Jesus Christ, son of David.» The
likenesses of the matters of someone are not of equal honor. For let the slave
be likened to the master; he does not however become of equal honor, except
that he has certain likenesses. David is a shepherd, and Christ is a shepherd;
«I am the good shepherd,» he was saying. But the one is a shepherd of cattle,
and the other a shepherd of souls; a likeness of matters, but a great
variation of the dignity. Samuel the priest anointed David as king, and John
baptized the Savior also, as being himself a priest too; a likeness of the
matters, but a great difference of the underlying things. And David, having
been anointed as king, did not at once seize the kingdom, but endured serving
Saul for many times; and our Savior, having been begotten king before the
ages, and not having a temporary kingdom, but king before all the ages,
begotten from God the King, endured; «He did not consider being equal to God a
thing to be grasped; but he emptied himself, taking the form of a slave,» in
order that he might complete the economy. Saul was persecuting David, Herod
was persecuting =28.156= Christ. But neither did Saul harm David by
persecuting, nor did Herod harm Christ by persecuting. Absalom, being a son,
rose up against David; Judas, being himself also another son, rose up against
the Savior; for the Savior knows to name the disciples sons, saying: «Behold,
I and the children whom God has given me;» and again he was saying to the
disciples, «Little children.» The place of the destruction of Absalom is
called Hand of Absalom even to this day. And look at the exactness of the
image of the matters. It is not written, Foot of Absalom, but Hand of Absalom;
it is not written Head of Absalom, although he perished from the hairs of the
head, but Hand of Absalom. For first was the hand of the head, which betrayed
against the image of the matters; but as the hand having gone forth through
the naming of the matters, the Scripture signaled sideways the thing that
would be: Hand of Absalom, and images of the matters. How many likenesses
David has toward the Savior!

This David then, having been anointed as king by Samuel, with sensible oil;
but our Savior was anointed otherwise. How and in what manner, hear the
forty-fourth Psalm saying: «Your throne, O God, is unto the age of the age; a
rod of uprightness is the rod of your kingdom. You have loved righteousness,
and hated lawlessness. For this reason God, your God, has anointed you with
oil of gladness beyond your fellows;» in order that you may not, by the
similar wording of the anointing, suppose there to be an equal honor of the
things said. David was anointed, and Christ was anointed; but the one was
anointed by a man, and the other was anointed from the Father; and the
anointing is unspeakable, and the matters incomprehensible. For this reason
the Psalmist says: «Oil of gladness beyond your fellows.» They are fellows of
the likeness, but they do not have an anointing of equal honor; for everywhere
he both keeps the likeness, and guards the superiority of the matter.

David then, having been anointed by Samuel, remained by himself. But Saul,
having transgressed the commandments of God, was delivered to an unclean
spirit. And David was singing psalms, and had a shepherd's lyre, and spending
time in deserts, and not having any men to converse with; and having fitted a
lyre, and by striking up the melodies he was working two things; for he was
also comforting the desert of the places through the sound, yet he was not
falling into dissolute melodies, but he was singing psalms, in order that the
well-sounding might delight, and the pious might benefit. And he was singing
in the deserts, saying that also «The heights of the mountains are his;» and
he was adding again: «Waters will pass through the midst of the mountains, and
they will water all the beasts of the field. Upon them the birds of the heaven
will nest.» He was singing the things concerning man, saying: «You have
subjected all things under his feet, sheep and all oxen.» And he was also
singing the wonder-workings of God. And when Saul was being pained =28.157= by
the unclean spirit, a certain informant comes to Saul; and in this manner
David is called. And as often as the wicked spirit was attacking him, David,
taking the lyre, was striking it up, singing psalms and saying hymns to God.
For do not mention the lyre to me, but the thing being sung; nor, having
snatched the words, fall into theater-mad pleasures, but, having remembered
the psalms, remain with the Church. Taking then the lyre into his hands, and
singing to God, David was driving away the choking demon. O great power of
psalms! The unclean spirit that was choking was being choked invisibly, and
Saul was recovering.

But he was envious of David for such a reason. Aliens once attacked the race
of the Hebrews; and a certain Goliath, huge in body, and bold in soul, and
fearful to behold, for forty days was reproaching Israel, and was asking one
for single combat, wearing a huge shield, and having taken out a spear, and
fearful greaves; and those who saw fled, and Saul was cowardly. David came to
visit his brothers, and he joins battle, the unarmed with the armed, the
inexperienced in war with the much-warring, the young with the man; and when
all had cowered, and no one dared to go out, David takes up the war. And
knowing, he was singing, that the war is of God, and that «A horse is false
for salvation, and in the abundance of its power it shall not be saved.» He
knew the things he was singing; he had faith as a greatest weapon, and he put
on a helmet of salvation, and having taken up a staff and a sling, having
wrapped the stone, having struck the adversary, he brought him down quickly.
For we pass over the words; since this is not now the thing set before us.
Great Goliath fell by the small, and the experienced by the inexperienced and
more simple. And taking his sword, he cut off his head. And the head was
carried away to Jerusalem; but his sword was kept with Abimelech the priest.
The people was redeemed from the adversary. Women went out to meet, dancing;
for joy was the thing set before them; and fear was loosed, the enemy fell,
the people was flourishing; dances, and drums, and harmony, and gladness was
the thing set before them. And the dancing women were singing, not looking
toward worth, but toward the working; for they were saying: «David has struck
in his ten thousands, and Saul in his thousands.» And to the victor they gave
the thousands, and they did not say how many thousands, nor how many ten
thousands. And ten thousands can also be resolved into thousands, and
thousands multiplied into ten thousands. Saul, having heard, was struck in his
mind. O great folly! Ought he not at least to have respected the present? Why
do you envy a man who is a victor, being well-spoken of in one hour, you who
were hiding for forty days? You have been redeemed from the enemies, why do
you envy a man being praised, who has worked works worthy of praises? But he
was saying to himself: They gave David the ten thousands, and to me the
thousands. And he was looking askance at David. =28.160= See whether they were
not his descendants, whose withered right hand the Savior healed today. And
those having gone out were considering not in order to glorify God, but how
they might plot against the benefactor. For the Gospel says that they
themselves were filled with folly, and having gone out, they were reasoning,
what they might do to Jesus? Descendants of the persecutor, and descendants of
the madman, and descendants of the one who plots against benefactors.

Saul then was looking askance at David, and no longer was looking at him with
straight eyes. For such are the envious, and such are the hypocrites. For this
reason Scripture says to you: «Let your eyes look straight things,» having
nothing sideways, nor envious, whether of desire or of madness, or of anything
of such a kind. Look at the friend straightly, nothing hidden, nothing
venomous. But Saul was looking askance at David. And look at a contrivance:
having seen him being honored, he wants to give him his daughter in
marriage-alliance. O temptation of envy! He plots, and takes him as a
bridegroom; and he does not spare a daughter, in order that he may plot
against the one who has been honored. And he wants as a prize a hundred
foreskins of aliens. For he was suspecting that, if he should throw him into
war, the hundred, having engaged with the one, would kill the one; and thus he
would be rid of him. And it came about; but the enemy as an enemy, and God as
God. David goes away, and alone conquers a hundred. And Saul, having seen God
as ally of the enemy, was not even thus put to shame; or rather not of the
enemy, but of the benefactor; he remained in the counsel. And he was angry
with his son Jonathan, because of his having friendship toward David, and he
attempted to kill his own son, being blinded by the envy of his mind. And
David, having seen himself being plotted against, hides until the third; and
David knows the third, and makes covenants with Jonathan, and thus the victor
flees; not being too weak to ward off the enemy, but not wishing to bring a
sword against the one who had been benefited; and having once delivered the
judgment to God, and fleeing, he comes with his household servants to
Abimelech the priest.

And we say all these things because of the history of the Gospel, because of
the word of the Savior that was said: «Have you not read what David did, when
he hungered, how he came into the house of God, and ate the loaves of the
presentation?» David then comes to Abimelech the priest, traveling with a few.
Abimelech was astonished; and, What is this that has happened, he says, that
the general travels alone, and the notable one makes the journey more simple?
But David does not speak the plot, but is silent about the matter and says
that it has been commanded to the young men in the place that is called Faith
in God. Note this also =28.161= even with David the faith; because of the one
who was going to be born from him, the one who says: «According to your faith
let it be to you.» Images also here of matters and likenesses. David asks for
loaves from Abimelech. And he says to him: There are no loaves except those
consecrated from the face of the Lord. Again we must mention another law. The
law of Moses wrote in the rites of the worship that take place on the day of
the Sabbath, that twelve hot loaves baked should be set upon the table. And
where there is heat, there is fire. Let the Jews now tell us how they keep the
Sabbath, and decline to kindle fire, where the priests kindle fire, and having
baked hot loaves they set them forth? Do I alone transgress the Sabbath?
Behold, the priests bake the hot ones on the Sabbath, and they give
circumcision on the Sabbath. And where there is circumcision, there also is
iron, and sponges, and the things that follow. Where there are bread-makings,
there also are ovens, there also a gathering of firewood, and altogether a
work for them, that of bread-making. To the people of the Jews then it was
forbidden to work on the Sabbath; but to the priests it was not forbidden. For
this reason also for us and for all it is permitted to work on the Sabbath;
for we too are all a royal priesthood. Of old then it was permitted to the
priests; since we too have become a royal priesthood and a holy nation. The
law having written: On the day of the Sabbath the twelve loaves are to be set
on a table; those loaves being consecrated, David was asking the food from the
table. And Abimelech was saying: «If the young men have been kept, except from
a woman.» Here then for me, in a digression of the word, you who are faithful,
make the thing said secure, and understand for me what is said; and take up
purity of flesh. For if Abimelech then commanded these things, how is it not
also to be guarded? And thus David took, and was eating the loaves, and
perhaps he took them on a Sabbath. Since then the Jews were blaming the
Savior, because on the Sabbath the disciples were plucking ears of grain and
eating; for this reason he most opportunely mentioned the history that took
place on a Sabbath; in order to show them that, if for saving food it was
permitted to David and those with him to lay hold of loaves from the table, it
was not forbidden also for the disciples, taking ears of grain, and rubbing
them, to eat. «Have you not even read this which David did, when he himself
hungered and those with him, how he entered into the house of God, and ate the
loaves of the presentation?»

You have next also the history concerning the withered hand joined together;
how our Savior, having entered into the synagogue (for we must run past the
history because of the length of the words, lest the hearing become satiated;)
and having seen a certain one having a withered hand, and knowing that the
Jews are ready to accuse, =28.164= he says to the one who has the withered
hand: «Rise up into the midst.» The resurrection commanded the rising up;
«Rise up, and stand into the midst.» Not working a boastful healing; for he
knows to say opportunely, «See that you tell no one;» but when it is
opportune, he hides the wonder-working; and when it is necessary to work
wonders on the Sabbath, then he works most openly. Seek the things written in
the Gospels, and you will find the truth of the words. He was keeping on the
Sabbath the more glorious of the wonder-workings; how and in what manner?
«From the age it has not been heard that anyone opened the eyes of one born
blind.» This wonder he did not do on another day, but on a Sabbath. For having
made clay by spitting on the ground, having anointed the eyes, he sent him to
Siloam; and the blind man came seeing. But those who saw the blind man seeing
did not look up. And of that one the sensible pupils of the eyes were opened,
but of the Jews the eyes were not opened; but having their eyes opened, they
were not seeing, and they condemned the Lord saying, that «This man is not
from God, because he does not keep the Sabbath.» O much folly! For what work
has taken place? Spittle from lips, from fingers only the clay, a simple
anointing, not with medical tools, but worked by a God-made hand. Why do you
blame what was done? You have a wonder-working on the Sabbath. The blind man

I have also another wonder-working. In Jerusalem there was a sheep-pool, and
it is even now; it had five porticoes; for now the surrounding buildings have
been taken away. And a paralytic was lying, being weak for thirty and eight
years. For the time of the disease becomes long, in order that, even thus
seeing, the Jews might be put to shame. For if the disease had been of short
time, the wonder would have escaped notice; but now many years bore witness to
the disease, in order that, even thus seeing, they might look up. And the
Savior, having come, and passing by, says to that one: «Do you wish to become
healthy?» and the things that follow. And that one too, having been raised,
takes up the pallet, and walks. And it was a Sabbath. But the Jews were not
seeing the wonder-working that took place on the Sabbath, but the pallet being
carried, when they ought to have reasoned: Such physicians have come, no one
healed; the wretched man was lying as a poor man, not even having the one who
throws him into the pool. Such is the wonder-worker and one who works newer
things, who gave so great a power to the paralytic, as not only to rise, but
also to carry the pallet. Blindness of wickedness. And they judge the one who
has been saved, and they say to him: «It is not lawful for you to take up the
pallet on the Sabbath.» That one answered: «The one who made me healthy, that
one said to me.» The thing commanded is a work of God. It is also unaccustomed
that a paralytic of thirty and eight years should be healed. From the same
mouth I heard the: To carry the pallet. If the body obeyed and became healthy,
shall I be ungrateful, =28.165= by not carrying the pallet? Again the Jews
were not seeking him, in order that they might learn how he healed, and
worship the one who gave such things, but in order that they might plot
against him.

That third one also has taken place which has now been read. He entered into
the synagogue, a synagogue of those doing evil. For what is more wicked than
those who are benefited and plot? In the synagogue of the Jews there was a man
having a withered hand. A certain one was withered in the hand, and those
present were withered in the mind, and they were not seeing the one then
present; nor were they suspecting the wonder-working of the one who was
working. But the Savior, before doing the work, was farming the mind through
words. For knowing the wickedness of the mind, and the bitter depth, he first
was comforting them beforehand with the words, and he was wishing to tame the
wildness of the mind, and he says to them: «Is it lawful to do good on the
Sabbath, or to do evil? to save a soul, or to destroy it?» For if he had said
to them, is it lawful to work? they would immediately have said, You speak
contrary to the law. But now he said to them what the law also was wishing;
for the one who legislated concerning the Sabbath was adding, that Except
this, which shall be done for a soul. Immediately if on a Sabbath a man falls
into depths, it is lawful for Jews to draw him up; not only a man, but also an
ox and an ass. Thus the law was granting the things for salvation to take
place, and Jews were being fed on the Sabbath. Someone now asked them not the
thing disputed, but the thing confessed: Is it lawful to do good? But they do
not even say, Yes; for they were not of a good choice. «For the good man from
the good treasure brings forth the good.» And the Jews, being wicked, do not
agree concerning good things, and they neither were confessing to do good, nor
were they able to say to do evil; for the word was shameless; but they are
silent. But when it is for idle talk, they talk much idle talk; when for
accusation, they cry great things; «Away, away, crucify.» This man did this
and that. That it is necessary to do good, or to do evil, to save a soul, or
to destroy it, they are silent; but he, being grieved in the heart at the
hardness of their heart, said to the man: Let those who see see, let the
unhearing, as they wish to act, act; let the hard-hearted be turned to stone;
but let your right hand be softened. Rise, beg no longer. For the, rise, was
making clear the sitting. And the one who had the withered hand, why was he
sitting, or altogether in order to beg? I do not give you silver, lest you
spend it; I give you health, never being spent. Do not beg because you have a
withered hand, but having received it healthy, and having worked it sound
hereafter, stretch out your hand to a poor man. Rise, and stand into the
midst; become a theater for those who see. In you the contest concerning the
Sabbath is now being contested. Stand into the midst, in order that those also
who are lame in the hams may stand, if perhaps they hear, if perhaps they see.
Stand into the midst, and having spoken to the =28.168= Jews the things that
have been said, that it is lawful to do this or not? Then he says to him:
Stretch out your hand; I do not touch, lest the Jews accuse; lest they suppose
the touching to be a work, I speak with a word. God did not say, Do not speak
on the Sabbath. But if the word becomes a work, let the one who spoke be
wondered at. Stretch out your hand. Look at a difference of matter for me:
Peter at the Beautiful Gate, saving a paralytic, having taken hold of the
right hand, raised him. The slave, holding, stretches out; the master,
commanding, stretches out. Stretch out your hand; I give you the power, and
the energy of the healing. Having commanded through the words I put it in;
Stretch out your hand. The one said, and the other did. And the withered hand
was restored; but the witheredness of the mind of the Jews was not healed; but
having gone out, according to the reading just now, they were reasoning what
they might do to Jesus. Do you reason what you will do? worship as God,
worship the wonder-worker, worship a man who has worked the things beyond man.
He did not apply plasters, he did not soften with certain fomentations, he did
not bring a thousand medical drugs. The one standing was in the midst, all
were seeing what took place; for the thing being worked was not hidden; lest
someone say: He brought an herb, he applied a plaster. Rise, stand into the
midst, in order that they may not at all accuse the truth; in order that the
matter may become unslandered. And those having gone out were reasoning what
they might do to Jesus; as also here at this hour, someone stands having a
withered hand, the one who does not stretch out almsgiving to a poor man. This
one even if he is healthy in the body, is withered in the mind. Hear you also
at this hour the saving words saying: Stretch out your hand; and from today
begin to do almsgiving to a poor man. And let us take it also otherwise. There
are many who are careless toward prayers, and they occupy themselves the whole
day about human works, but they despise the divine work of the prayers. And to
this one let the Savior say: Stretch out your hand, just as the Apostle says:
«I wish the men to pray, in every place lifting up holy hands.» And since our
words have proceeded sufficiently, let us also, having risen, stretch out our
hands, and not only in day, but also in night; «In the nights lift up your
hands toward the holy things, and bless the Lord.» And let us spread out holy
hands, having washed in innocents; in order that, calling upon the God of all,
we may enjoy the help from him, that in Christ Jesus our Lord, through whom to
God is the glory together with the Holy Spirit now and always and unto the
ages of the ages. Amen.
