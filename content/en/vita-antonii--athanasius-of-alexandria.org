LIFE AND CONDUCT OF OUR HOLY FATHER ANTONY
COMPOSED AND SENT TO THE MONKS IN FOREIGN PARTS
BY OUR FATHER AMONG THE SAINTS ATHANASIUS THE BISHOP
OF ALEXANDRIA
=26.837= PREFACE.
You have started a good contest with the monks in Egypt, having chosen either
to equal them or even to surpass them in your practice of virtue. For among
you too there are now monasteries, and the name of monks is established.
This purpose of yours one would justly
praise, and, as you pray, God would bring it to completion. But since you have
also asked of me concerning the conduct of the blessed Antony, wishing to
learn how he began the ascetic life, and who he was before it, and what sort
of end he had of his life, and whether the things said about him are true, so
that you may also bring yourselves to his zeal: I received your charge with
much eagerness. For to me too it is a great gain of benefit even only to
remember Antony. And I know that you also, when you hear, besides marveling at
the man, will wish to rival his purpose. For the life of Antony is a
sufficient pattern for monks toward ascetic practice. Do not disbelieve, then,
the things you have heard about him from those who reported them; rather think
that you have heard only a little from them. For they too, at any rate, barely
related so much. Since I also, urged by you, will write in the letter as much
as I indicate, remembering only a little of his things. And you, do not cease
to question those who sail from here. For perhaps, if each says what he knows,
the account about him may barely become worthy of him. I had wished, when I
received your letter, to send =26.840= for some of the monks, those who were
especially accustomed to come to him more often, so that by learning something
more I might write to you more fully. But since the season of the
sailing-ships had also closed, and the letter-carrier was in haste, for this
reason I was eager to write to your piety what I myself know (for I often saw
him) and what I was able to learn from him, having followed him no short time
and pouring water on his hands, taking thought of the truth everywhere, so
that no one, hearing more, may disbelieve, nor again, learning less than is
due, may despise the man. Antony was Egyptian by race, of well-born parents
who possessed sufficient means, and =26.841= they themselves being Christians,
he too was brought up in a Christian way. And while he was a child he was
reared with his parents, knowing nothing else besides them and the house. But
when he grew and became a boy and advanced in age, he did not endure to learn
letters, wishing to be outside even of the companionship with the children.
And he had all his desire, according to what is written about Jacob, to dwell
as a simple man in his house. He would gather, however, with his parents in
the Lord's house. And neither as a child was he slack, nor as he advanced in
age did he despise; but he was subject to his parents, and attending to the
readings he kept the benefit from them in himself. Nor again, as a child who
happened to be in moderate means, did he trouble his parents for the sake of
varied and costly food, nor did he seek the pleasures from it; but only with


those things he found was he content, and he sought nothing more. After the
death of his parents he was left alone with one very small sister. And he was
near eighteen years old, or even twenty, and he himself took thought for the
house and for his sister. Not yet had six months passed from the death of his
parents, and, going out as usual to the Lord's house, and gathering his mind
to himself, he considered as he walked how the apostles, leaving all things,
followed the Savior; and how those in the Acts, selling their own things,
brought them and laid them at the feet of the apostles for distribution to
those in need, and what and how great a hope is stored up for them in heaven.
Thinking on these things he entered the church, and it happened then that the
Gospel was being read, and he heard the Lord saying to the rich man: If you
wish to be perfect, go, sell all that you have, and give to the poor, and come
follow me, and you will have treasure in heaven. And Antony, as if he had
received from God the memory of the saints, and as if the reading had been
=26.844= for him, going out at once from the Lord's house, the possessions he
had from his ancestors (they were three hundred arable acres, fertile and very
fine) he gave these as a gift to the people of the village, so that they might
trouble him and his sister in no respect whatever. And the rest of their
movable things, selling them all and gathering sufficient silver, he gave to
the poor, keeping a little for his sister. And when, entering the Lord's house
again, he heard in the Gospel the Lord saying, Do not be anxious about
tomorrow, not enduring to remain any longer, he went out and distributed those
things too to the modest. And committing his sister to known and faithful
virgins, and giving her to be brought up in a house of virgins, he himself
from then on devoted himself before the house to the ascetic life, attending
to himself and leading himself with endurance. For there were not yet in Egypt
monasteries so continuous, nor did a monk know the far desert at all, but each
of those who wished to attend to himself practiced alone not far from his own
village. There was then in the nearby village at that time an old man who from
youth had practiced the solitary life. Seeing him, Antony rivaled him in what
is good. And first he too began to remain in the places before the village.
And from there, if he heard of anyone earnest anywhere, he would go out and
seek him like a wise bee. And he would not return to his own place until he
had seen him, and, as if taking from him some provision for the road of
virtue, he would come back. Spending the beginnings there, then, he weighed
his mind, how he might not turn back toward the things of his parents, nor
remember his relatives, but have all his longing and all his zeal about the
tension of the ascetic life. So he worked with his hands, having heard: Let
the idle man not even eat. And one part =26.845= he spent on bread, the other
on those in need. And he prayed continually, having learned that one must pray
privately without ceasing. For he attended to the reading in such a way that
nothing of what was written fell from him to the ground, but he held all
things, and thereafter his memory became books for him. Leading himself thus,
Antony was loved by all. And he himself was genuinely subject to the earnest
men to whom he went, and by himself he learned the advantage of each in zeal
and in ascetic practice. And he observed the graciousness of one, and the
intensity of another toward prayers. And he noted the freedom from anger of
one, and the love of humanity of another. And he attended to one who kept
vigil, and to another who was devoted to study. And he marveled at one in
endurance, and at another in fasts and in sleeping on the ground. And he
watched the meekness of one, and the long-suffering of another. And of all
together he marked the piety toward Christ, and


their love for one another. And so filled, he returned to his own place of the
ascetic dwelling, gathering thereafter into himself the things from each, and
striving to show in himself the things of all. For toward those equal in age
he was not contentious, except only that he might not appear second to them in
the better things. And this he did so as to grieve no one, but that they too
might rejoice over him. All those from the village, then, and the lovers of
the good with whom he had companionship, seeing him thus, called him
God-beloved. And some greeted him as a son, others as a brother. But the
devil, hater of the good and envious, could not bear to see such a purpose in
a younger man. But as he has practiced doing, he attempts to act against this
man too =26.848=. And first he tried to drag him down from the ascetic life,
suggesting memory of the possessions, care for his sister, intimacy of family,
love of money, love of glory, the varied pleasure of food, and the other
relaxations of life, and finally the roughness of virtue, and how great its
toil is. And he put forward the weakness of the body, and the length of time.
And altogether he raised a great dust of thoughts in his mind, wishing to cut
him off from the right choice. But when the enemy saw himself weak against
Antony's purpose, and himself rather being thrown by that man's firmness, and
overturned by the much faith, and falling by Antony's continual prayers: then
indeed, trusting in the weapons of his belly at the navel, and boasting in
these (for these are his first snares against the younger), he comes against
the younger man, disturbing him by night, and by day so harassing him that
even those who saw perceived the wrestling that was taking place of both. For
the one suggested filthy thoughts, and the other overturned them with prayers.
And the one tickled, and the other, as if seeming to blush, walled the body
with faith and prayers and fasts. And the devil, the wretch, even endured to
be shaped as a woman by night, and to imitate every manner, only that he might
deceive Antony. But he, thinking of Christ, and of the nobility through him,
and considering the intellectual nature of the soul, quenched the coal of that
one's deceit. And again the enemy suggested the smoothness of pleasure. But
he, as if angry and grieved, thought of the threat of the fire and the pain of
the worm. And setting these against them, he passed through unharmed. And all
these things were happening to the shame of =26.849= the enemy. For he who
thought to become like God was now played with by a young man. And he who
boasted against flesh and blood was overturned by a man wearing flesh. For the
Lord who wore flesh for us worked with him, and gave the body the victory
against the devil, so that each of those who truly contend may say: "And not
I, but the grace of God which is with me." At last, then, when the dragon
could not throw Antony even in this, but also saw himself being thrust out
from his heart, gnashing his teeth, according to what is written, and as if
beside himself, such as he is in mind, such he later appears to him also in
phantasy, a black boy. And as if falling under, he no longer attacked with
thoughts (for the crafty one had been cast out), but thereafter using a human
voice he said: I have deceived many, and I have thrown very many. But now,
having attacked you too as I did many, and your labors, I have become weak.
Then when Antony asked: Who are you who say such things beside me? at once
that one sent out pitiable voices: I am the friend of fornication. I am the
snares into it, and I took up its


ticklings against the young, and I have been called a spirit of fornication.
How many who wished to be chaste have I deceived! How many who practiced
self-control have I afterward persuaded by tickling! I am the one through whom
the prophet also blames those who fell, saying: You were led astray by a
spirit of fornication. For through me those men were tripped. I am the one who
often harassed you, and so many times was overturned by you. And Antony,
giving thanks to the Lord, and taking courage against him, says to him: You
are then very much to be despised. For you are black in =26.852= mind, and
weak as a child. I have no longer even one care about you. For the Lord is my
helper, and I shall look upon my enemies. Hearing these things that black one
at once fled, cowering at the words, and fearing even to come near the man any
longer. This was Antony's first contest against the devil. Or rather this too
became the Savior's achievement in Antony, of him who condemned sin in the
flesh, so that the just requirement of the law might be fulfilled in us who
walk not according to flesh but according to spirit. But neither did Antony,
as if the demon had fallen under, thereafter grow slack and despise himself.
Nor did the enemy, as if defeated, cease from laying snares. For he went about
again as a lion, seeking some occasion against him. And Antony, having learned
from the Scriptures that the methods of the enemy are many, used the ascetic
life with intensity, considering that if he had not been able to deceive the
heart in pleasure of the body, he would certainly try to lie in wait by
another method. For the demon is a lover of sin. So more and more he buffeted
the body and enslaved it, lest, having conquered in other things, he be
dragged under in others. He therefore resolves to accustom himself to harsher
trainings. And many marveled, but he himself bore the toil more easily. For
the eagerness of the soul, remaining a long time, worked a good habit in him,
so that even taking a small occasion from others he showed much zeal for this.
For he kept vigil so much that often he even spent the whole night sleepless.
And doing this not once, but very often, he was marveled at. And he ate once
in the day after the setting of the sun. And there were times when he partook
after two days, and often even after four. And =26.853= his food was bread and
salt, and his drink water only. For about meats and wine it is superfluous
even to speak, since not even among the other earnest men was anything of that
sort found. And for sleep he was content with a rush-mat. And for the most
part he even lay on the earth alone. And he refused to be anointed with oil,
saying that it is more fitting for the younger to have the ascetic life from
eagerness, and not to seek the things that loosen the body, but also to
accustom it to toils, considering the saying of the Apostle: When I am weak,
then I am strong. For then, he said, the tension of the soul is strong, when
the pleasures of the body are weak. And this reasoning of his too was truly
paradoxical. For he did not think it right to measure the road of virtue by
time, nor the withdrawal for it, but by longing and by choice. He himself,
then, did not remember the time that had passed, but each day, as if having
the beginning of the ascetic life, he had a greater toil toward progress,
saying to himself continually the saying of Paul: Forgetting the things
behind, and stretching out to the things in front. And remembering also the
voice of the prophet Elijah saying: The Lord lives, before whom I have stood
today. For he observed that, saying "today," he did not measure the time that
had passed, but as if always laying a beginning, he was eager each day to
present himself such as one must appear to God, pure in heart, and ready to
obey his will, and no one else. And he said in himself that the


ascetic ought to learn from the conduct of the great Elijah, as in a mirror,
his own life always. Having thus tightened himself, then, Antony went away to
the tombs that happened to be far from the village, and having charged one of
his acquaintances to bring him bread after many days, he himself entered one
of the tombs, and when that man had shut =26.856= the door against him, he
remained alone inside. There the enemy, not bearing it, but indeed also
fearing lest little by little he fill even the desert with the ascetic life,
coming on one night with a multitude of demons, so beat him with blows that he
lay on the ground speechless from the tortures. For he confirmed that the
pains had become so violent that he said the blows from men could never
produce such a torture. But by the providence of God (for the Lord does not
overlook those who hope in him), on the next day the acquaintance arrives,
bringing him the loaves. And opening the door, and seeing him lying on the
ground as dead, he took him up and carried him to the Lord's house of the
village, and sets him on the earth. And many of the relatives, and those from
the village, sat beside Antony as over a dead man. And around midnight Antony
came to himself, and being aroused, when he saw all sleeping, and only the
acquaintance keeping watch, nodding for him to come to him, he asked him to
take him up again and carry him away to the tombs, waking no one. He was
therefore carried away by the man, and with the door shut as usual, he was
again alone inside. And he could not stand because of the blows, but lying
down he prayed. And after the prayer he said with a cry: Here I am, I, Antony.
I do not flee your blows. For even if you make more, nothing will separate me
from the love of Christ. Then he also sang: If a camp be drawn up against me,
my heart shall not fear. The ascetic, then, thought and said these things. But
the enemy, hater of the good, marveling that even after the blows he had taken
courage to come, calling together his dogs, and being torn, said: You see that
neither by a spirit of fornication nor by blows have we stopped this man. But
he even grows bold against us. Let us come against him otherwise. Easy
=26.857= for the devil are the shapes toward vice. Then indeed in the night
they make such a noise that that whole place seemed to shake. And the demons,
as if breaking the four walls of the little house, seemed to enter through
them, transformed into a phantasy of beasts and reptiles. And the place was at
once filled with a phantasy of lions, bears, leopards, bulls, and snakes,
asps, and scorpions, and wolves. And each of these moved according to its own
shape. The lion roared, wishing to come upon him, the bull seemed to gore, the
snake crawling did not arrive, and the wolf rushing was held back. And
altogether the noises of all that appeared together were terrible, and the
rages hard. And Antony, being whipped and pricked by them, perceived a more
terrible bodily pain. Unmoved, and rather keeping watch in the soul, he lay.
And he groaned because of the pain of the body. But sober in mind and as if
mocking, he said: If there were any power in you, it would have been enough
for even one of you alone to come. But since the Lord has unnerved you, for
this reason you try somehow to frighten even by multitude. A mark of your
weakness is that you imitate the forms of irrational creatures. Taking
courage, then, he said again: If you can and have received authority against
me, do not delay, but attack. But if you cannot, why do you disturb in vain?
For a seal to us and a wall for safety is the faith in our Lord. Having
attempted many things, then, they gnashed their teeth against him, because
they were rather playing themselves, and not him. =26.860= And the Lord even
in this


did not forget Antony's contest, but came for his help. Looking up, then, he
saw the roof as if opening, and a certain ray of light coming down toward him.
And the demons suddenly became invisible. And the pain of the body at once
ceased, and the house was whole again. And Antony, perceiving the help, and
breathing more, and being lightened of the pains, begged of the vision that
had appeared, saying: Where were you? Why did you not appear from the
beginning, so that you might stop my pains? And a voice came to him: Antony, I
was here, but I waited to see your contest. Since then you have endured and
were not defeated, I will be a helper to you always, and I will make you
become named everywhere. Hearing these things, he rose and prayed. And he
became so strong that he perceived he had more power in the body than he had
before. And he was then thereafter near thirty-five years old. And going out
the next day he was still more eager toward piety, and coming to that old man,
the ancient one, he asked him to dwell in the desert with him. But when he
declined both because of age and because there was not yet such a custom, he
himself at once set out for the mountain. But again the enemy, seeing his zeal
and wishing to hinder it, put in the roads a phantasy of a great silver disk.
And Antony, understanding the craft of the hater of the good, stood, and
looking at the disk he rebuked the devil in it, saying: Whence a disk in the
desert? This road is not worn, nor is there a track of any who have traveled
here. If it had fallen it could not have escaped notice, being very large. And
the one who lost it, turning back and seeking, would have found it, because
the place is desert. This has become a craft of the devil. =26.861= You will
not hinder my eagerness in this, devil. For this would be with you unto
destruction. And as Antony said this, that thing vanished, as smoke from the
face of fire. Then again, no longer a phantasy, he saw true gold thrown in the
roads as he was going. Whether the enemy showed it, or some better power
training the athlete and showing the devil that he does not even care for true
riches: he himself did not report, nor did we know, except that what appeared
was gold. And Antony marveled at the quantity, but as if stepping over fire,
he passed it so as not even to turn, but even to hurry with a run so much as
to hide and leave the place unnoticed. Stretching his purpose more and more,
then, he set out for the mountain. And finding a deserted fort, full of
reptiles because of time, on the other side of the river, he transferred
himself there and dwelt in it. The reptiles, then, as if someone were chasing
them, withdrew at once. And he, blocking the entrance, and laying up loaves
for six months (the Thebans do this, and often they even remain a whole year
unharmed), having water inside, remaining as if sinking into inner shrines, he
stayed alone inside, neither himself going out, nor seeing any of those who
came. He himself, then, continued a long time practicing thus, receiving the
loaves only twice a year from above from the roof. And those of his
acquaintances who came to him, since he did not allow them to enter, often
spending days and nights outside, heard as of crowds making a tumult inside,
making noises, sending out pitiable voices, and crying: Depart from our
things. What have you to do with the desert? You do not bear our plot. At
first those outside thought that some men were fighting with him, and that
these had entered to him by ladders. But when, peeping through a certain hole,
they saw no one, then =26.864= they reckoned these were demons


and, themselves fearing, they called Antony. But he rather heard these than
cared for those. And coming near the door he exhorted the men to withdraw and
not to fear. For thus, he said, the demons make phantasies against those who
are cowardly. You then seal yourselves and go away taking courage, and leave
these to play among themselves. They therefore went away, walled by the sign
of the cross. And he remained, and was harmed by them in nothing. But neither
did he grow weary in contending. For the addition of the things that happened
to him of the contemplations above, and the weakness of the enemies, afforded
him much rest from the toils, and prepared him for more eagerness. For the
acquaintances continually came, thinking to find him dead, and they heard him
singing: Let God arise, and let his enemies be scattered, and let those who
hate him flee from his face. As smoke vanishes, let them vanish. As wax melts
from the face of fire, so let the sinners perish from the face of God. And
again: All the nations surrounded me, and in the name of the Lord I warded
them off. Near twenty years, then, he continued, practicing thus by himself,
neither going out, nor being seen continually by any. And after these things,
many longing and wishing to rival his ascetic life, and other acquaintances
coming and throwing down the door by force and thrusting it out, Antony came
forth as if initiated from some inner shrine and God-borne. And then first
from the fort he appeared to those who had come to him. They then, when they
saw, marveled, seeing his body having the same habit, and neither fattened, as
if unexercised, nor thinned as from fasts and a fight =26.865= of demons. For
he was such as they had known him even before the withdrawal. And of the soul
again the character was pure. For it was neither contracted as by distress,
nor dissolved by pleasure, nor held by laughter or by dejection. For neither
having seen the crowd was he disturbed, nor as being embraced by so many did
he become glad. But he was wholly equal, as governed by the Word, and standing
in what is according to nature. Many, then, of those present who suffered in
their bodies the Lord healed through him. And others he cleansed from demons.
And he gave Antony grace in speaking. And thus he comforted many who were
grieved, and others who were at odds he reconciled into friendship, saying to
all to prefer nothing of the things in the world to the love toward Christ.
And conversing and exhorting them to remember the good things to come and the
love of humanity of God that has come to us, who did not spare his own Son,
but delivered him up for us all, he persuaded many to choose the solitary
life. And thus thereafter there came to be monasteries even in the mountains,
and the desert was settled by monks who had gone out from their own things and
enrolled themselves for the citizenship in the heavens. And when a need arose
for him to cross the canal of the Arsenoite (and the need was the visiting of
the brothers), the canal was full of crocodiles. And only having prayed, he
himself and all those with him embarked, and they crossed unharmed. And
returning to the monastery he held to the same solemn and youthful toils. And
conversing continually he increased the eagerness of those already monks, and
he moved the most of the others into a love of the ascetic life, and quickly,
as the word drew them, very many monasteries came to be, and he led them all
as a father. On one day, then, going out, and all the monks coming to him, and
asking to hear a word from =26.868= him, he said these things to them in the
Egyptian speech: That the


Scriptures are sufficient for teaching. But it is good for us to exhort one
another in the faith, and to anoint with words. You then, as children, bring
to the father, saying what you know. And I, as older than you in age, give a
share of what I know and of what I have experienced. And let this zeal be
first of all common to all, not to give way after beginning, nor to grow faint
in the toils, nor to say: We have spent a long time in the ascetic life. But
rather, as beginning each day, let us increase the eagerness. For the whole
life of men is very short, being measured toward the ages to come, so that
even all our time is nothing toward the eternal life. And every thing in the
world is sold for its worth, and one exchanges equal for equal. But the
promise of the eternal life is bought for some little thing. For it is
written: The days of our life in themselves are seventy years, and if in
powers, eighty years, and what is more of them is toil and labor. When
therefore we remain all the eighty years, or even a hundred, in the ascetic
life, we shall not reign equal to the hundred years, but instead of the
hundred we shall reign ages of ages. And having contended on earth, we do not
inherit on earth, but we have the promises in heaven. And again, having put
off the corruptible body, we receive it back incorruptible. So, children, let
us not grow faint, nor think that we delay, or that we do some great thing.
For the sufferings of the present time are not worthy toward the glory about
to be revealed to us. Nor looking at the world let us think we have withdrawn
from some great things. For even all the earth itself is very small toward the
whole heaven. If then we even happened to be lords of all the earth, and we
renounced all the earth, it would again be nothing worthy toward the kingdom
of the heavens. For as if someone should despise one =26.869= bronze drachma,
so that he might gain a hundred gold drachmas, so he who is lord of all the
earth and renounces it leaves a little, and receives a hundredfold. And if not
even all the earth is worthy of the heavens, he then who has left a few acres,
as leaving nothing, even if he leaves a house or sufficient gold, ought not to
boast or to grow listless. Besides we ought to consider that even if we do not
leave them for virtue, yet later when we die we leave them often even to those
we do not wish, as the Ecclesiast remembered. Why then do we not leave them
for virtue, so that we may also inherit a kingdom? For this reason let none of
us take a desire of possessing. For what gain is it to possess these things
which we do not even take with us? Why do we not rather possess those things
which we can also take with us, which are prudence, justice, temperance,
courage, understanding, love, love of the poor, the faith in Christ, freedom
from anger, hospitality? Possessing these we shall find them before us there,
making us a lodging in the land of the meek. So even from such things let one
persuade himself not to be negligent. And especially if he considers himself a
slave of the Lord, and owing it to serve the master. As then the slave would
not dare to say: Since I worked yesterday, I do not work today; nor measuring
the time that has passed will he cease from the days that follow, but each
day, as it is written in the Gospel, he shows the same eagerness, so that he
may please his lord and not be in danger: so also let us remain each day in
the ascetic life, knowing that if we neglect one day, he will not forgive us
because of the time that has passed, but because of the neglect he will be
indignant against us. Thus we have also heard in Ezekiel. Thus Judas too,
through one night, lost even the labor of the time that had passed. =26.872=
Let us hold, then, children, to the ascetic life, and let us not grow
listless. For we have


in this also the Lord as fellow-worker, as it is written: To everyone who
chooses the good, God works together for the good. And toward our not being
negligent it is good to meditate the saying of the Apostle, the, I die each
day. For if we too live thus, as dying each day, we shall not sin. And what is
said is of this sort, that, rising each day, we may think we will not remain
until evening, and again, being about to sleep, we may think we will not rise,
our life being by nature uncertain, and measured each day by Providence. And
being so disposed, and living thus each day, we shall neither sin, nor have
desire of anything, nor be wrathful at anyone, nor treasure upon the earth.
But as expecting to die each day, we shall be without possessions, and we
shall forgive all things to all. And of desire of a woman, or of another
filthy pleasure, we shall not hold at all, but we shall turn away from it as
passing. Contending always and looking forward to the day of judgment. For
always the greater fear and the contest of the tortures dissolves the
smoothness of pleasure, and raises the soul that is leaning. Therefore having
begun and already set foot on the road of virtue, let us rather stretch out,
so that we may arrive at the things in front. And let no one turn to the
things behind, as the wife of Lot. Especially since the Lord has said: No one
putting the hand to the plow and turning to the things behind is fit in the
kingdom of the heavens. And to turn is nothing else than to repent and again
to think worldly things. Do not fear when you hear about virtue, nor be
strangers about the name. For it is not far from us, nor is it constituted
from outside us, but =26.873= the work is in us, and the thing is easy, if
only we wish. The Greeks, then, go abroad and cross the sea, so that they may
learn letters. But we do not have need to go abroad for the kingdom of the
heavens, nor to cross the sea for virtue. For the Lord already said: The
kingdom of the heavens is within you. Virtue, then, has need of our wishing
only, since it is in us and is constituted from us. For when the intellectual
part of the soul is according to nature, virtue is constituted. And it has it
according to nature whenever it remains as it came to be, and it came to be
good and very straight. For this reason Joshua the son of Nun, charging, said
to the people: Make straight your heart toward the Lord the God of Israel. And
John: Make your paths straight. For the soul being straight, this is its
intellectual part according to nature as it was created. And again whenever it
turns aside and comes to be in a twisting of what is according to nature, then
it is called vice of the soul. Therefore the thing is not hard. For if we
remain as we have come to be, we are in virtue. But if we consider the base
things, we are judged as bad. If then the thing had to be provided from
outside, it would truly be hard. But if it is in us, let us guard ourselves
from filthy thoughts, and as having received a deposit, let us keep the soul
for the Lord, so that he himself may recognize his work, it being thus as he
also made it. And let our contest be, so that anger may not tyrannize us, nor
desire rule us. For it is written that The anger of a man does not work the
justice of God. And desire, having conceived, gives birth to sin. And sin,
when it is completed, brings forth death. And living thus let us be sober
safely. And, as it is written, with all watch let us keep our heart. For we
have terrible and cunning enemies, the evil demons. And toward these is our
wrestling, as the Apostle said: Not toward blood and flesh, but toward the
principalities, and =26.876= toward the authorities, toward the world-rulers
of this darkness, toward the spiritual things of wickedness, in the heavenly
places. Great then is their crowd


in the air around us, and they are not far from us. And there is a great
difference among them. And about their nature and the difference there would
be a long account, and such a narrative belongs to others greater than we. But
what now presses and is necessary for us is to know only their cunning against
us. First then let us know this, that the demons have not come to be as they
are called demons. For God made nothing evil. But they too came to be good,
and having fallen from the heavenly mind, and thereafter rolling about the
earth, they deceived the Greeks with phantasies, and envying us Christians
they move all things, wishing to hinder us from the ascent to the heavens, so
that we may not go up from where they themselves fell. Wherefore there is need
of much prayer and of the ascetic life, so that one, receiving through the
Spirit a gift of discernment of spirits, may be able to know the things
concerning them: which of them are less base, and which are baser than those,
and about what practice each of them has his zeal, and how each of them is
overturned and cast out. For many are their cunning works, and the movements
of the plot. The blessed Apostle, then, and those according to him knew such
things, saying: For we are not ignorant of his thoughts. And we, from what we
have experienced from them, ought to be corrected by one another from them. I
then, having in part a trial of them, speak as to children. These then, if
they see even all Christians, =26.877= and especially monks, laboring and
advancing, first they attempt and try, setting snares next to the path. And
their snares are the evil thoughts. But we must not fear their suggestions.
For by prayers and fasts and the faith in the Lord those fall at once. But
even having fallen they do not cease, but come again cunningly and craftily.
For whenever they cannot deceive the heart from what is open and from filthy
pleasure, they attack again otherwise. And thereafter fashioning phantasies
they pretend to frighten, transforming themselves, and imitating women,
beasts, reptiles, and sizes of bodies, and a multitude of soldiers. But not
even thus must one be cowardly at their phantasies. For they are nothing, but
they also quickly vanish, if especially one fences himself with the faith and
with the sign of the cross. And they are daring and very shameless. For if
they are defeated even thus, they attack again in another way. And they
pretend to divine, and to foretell the things coming after days, and to show
themselves high, reaching as far as the roof, and broad in size, so that those
whom they could not deceive with thoughts they may snatch even with such
phantasies. And if even thus they find the soul secured by faith and by the
hope of the mind, thereafter they bring on their ruler. And he said they often
appeared such as the Lord revealed the devil to Job, saying: His eyes are as
the appearance of the morning star. From his mouth go forth burning lamps, and
hearths of fire are scattered. From his nostrils goes forth smoke of a furnace
burning with a fire of coals. His soul is coals, and a flame goes forth from
his mouth. And appearing such, the ruler of the demons frightens, as I said
before, speaking great things, the cunning one, as again the Lord convicted
him, saying to Job =26.880=: For he has thought iron chaff, and bronze as
rotten wood. And he has thought the sea as an ointment box, and the tartarus
of the abyss as a captive. He reckoned the abyss as a walk. And through the
prophet: The


enemy said: I will pursue, I will overtake. And again through another: I will
take the whole inhabited world with my hand, as a nest, and I will take it as
eggs left behind. And altogether they attempt to boast such things, and they
promise these, so that they may deceive those who are godly. But we the
faithful must not even thus again fear his phantasies, and attend to his
voices. For he lies, and he speaks nothing true at all. At any rate, speaking
such and so many things and growing bold, as a dragon he was drawn with the
hook by the Savior, and as a beast he received a halter about the nostrils,
and as a runaway he is bound with a ring at the nostrils, and his lips are
pierced with a bit. And he has been bound by the Lord as a sparrow, so as to
be played with by us. And he himself and the demons with him have been set, as
scorpions and snakes, so as to be trampled by us Christians. And a mark of
this is that we now live against him. For he who promised to wipe out the sea
and to take the inhabited world, behold now he cannot hinder your ascetic
life, nor even me speaking against him. Let us not then attend to whatever he
may say (for he lies), nor let us be cowardly at his phantasies, they too
being false. For it is not true light that appears in them. Rather they bear
the preludes and the images of the fire prepared for them. And in the things
in which they are about to be burned, in these they try to frighten men. At
any rate they appear, and at once again they vanish, having harmed none of the
faithful, but bearing =26.881= with themselves the likeness of the fire that
is about to receive them. Whence not even thus is it fitting to fear these.
For all their practices, through the grace of Christ, are unto nothing. And
they are crafty and ready to change and transform themselves into all things.
Often, then, they even pretend to sing with a song, not appearing, and they
remember the words from the Scriptures. And there are times when, as we are
reading, at once as an echo they themselves say the same things many times
which have been read. And they wake us when we are sleeping unto prayers. And
they do this continually, almost not even allowing us to sleep. And there are
times when they also stamp themselves into shapes of monks, and pretend to
speak as pious, so that by the like shape they may lead astray, and thereafter
draw those deceived by them where they wish. But one must not attend to them,
even if they wake unto prayer, even if they advise not to eat at all, even if
they pretend to accuse and to reproach, in the things they once knew with us.
For they do these things not through piety or truth, but so that they may
bring the simple into despair, and may say the ascetic life is unprofitable,
and may make men nauseous, as if the solitary life were burdensome and very
heavy, and may hinder those who live against them. The prophet, then, sent by
the Lord, called such people wretched, saying: Woe to him who gives his
neighbor a turbid overturning to drink. For such practices and thoughts are
overturning of the road that leads to virtue. And the Lord himself through
himself, although the demons were saying the true things (for they said true
things: You are the Son of God), nevertheless muzzled them, and forbade them
to speak, lest along with the truth they also sow their own vice, and so that
he might also accustom us never to attend to such, even if they seem to say
the true things. For it is unseemly, when we have the holy Scriptures and the
freedom from the Savior, to be taught by the devil, who did not keep his own
rank, but thought other things instead of others. For this reason even when
this one speaks the words from the Scriptures he forbids him, saying: To the


sinner God said: Why do you recount my =26.884= just requirements, and take up
my covenant through your mouth? For they do all things, and speak, and make a
tumult, and play a part, and disturb, toward the deceit of the simple. And
they make noises, and they laugh foolishly, and they hiss. And if one does not
attend to them, thereafter they weep and lament as defeated. The Lord then, as
God, muzzled the demons. And we, having learned from the saints, ought to do
according to them, and to imitate their courage. For they too, seeing these
things, said: When the sinner stood against me, I became deaf, and I was
humbled, and I was silent from good things. And again: And I as one deaf did
not hear, and as one speechless not opening his mouth. And I became as a man
not hearing. Therefore let us too neither hear them, as being foreign to us,
nor obey them, even if they raise us unto prayer, even if they speak about
fasts. But let us rather attend to the purpose of our own ascetic life, and
not be deceived by those who do all things with craft. And one must not fear
them, even if they seem to come upon us, even if they threaten death. For they
are weak, and they can nothing, except only to threaten. Already then in
passing I have said about this. And now one must not shrink from speaking more
broadly the things about them. For the reminder will be safe for you. When the
Lord sojourned, the enemy fell, and his powers became weak. For this reason,
then, being able to do nothing, nevertheless as a tyrant, even having fallen
he is not quiet, but even with words only he threatens. And let each of you
=26.885= consider this, and he can despise the demons. If then they were bound
in such bodies as we are, it would be possible for them to say that When men
are hidden we do not find them, but when we have found them we harm them. And
we too, being hidden, could escape their notice, shutting doors against them.
But if they are not thus, but they can enter when the doors are shut, and they
themselves and their first, the devil, happen to be in all the air: and they
are ill-willed and ready toward harming, and, as the Savior said, from the
beginning the devil, the father of vice, is a man-killer: and we now live and
rather live against him: they are clear as having no strength. For neither
does place hinder them from plotting, nor do they see us as friends of theirs
so that they may spare, nor are they lovers of the good so that they may
correct. But they are rather evil. And nothing is so much sought by them as to
harm the lovers of virtue and the godly. And because they can do nothing, for
this reason they do nothing, except only they threaten. For if they were able,
they would not delay, but they would at once work the evil, having the choice
ready for this, and especially against us. Behold then now we have come
together and we speak against them, and they know that, as we advance, they
themselves become weak. If then they had authority, they would have left none
of us Christians to live. For piety is an abomination to a sinner. But since
they can nothing, for this reason they rather wound themselves, because they
can do nothing of the things they threaten. Then that too must be considered,
toward not fearing these. If the power belonged to them, they would not come
with a crowd, nor make phantasies, nor scheme by transforming themselves. But
it would have been enough for even one alone to come and to do this which he
can and wishes. And especially since everyone who has the authority does not
kill with a phantasy, nor frighten with crowds, but at once uses the authority
as he wishes. But =26.888= the demons, being able to do nothing, play as on a
stage, changing the forms, and frightening the children, by the phantasy of
crowds and by the


disguises. From which they ought rather to be despised as weak. The true
angel, then, sent by the Lord against the Assyrians, had no need of crowds,
nor of a phantasy from outside, nor of noises, nor of clappings. But quietly
he used the authority, and he at once killed a hundred and eighty-five
thousand. But the demons who can nothing, such as these are, try to frighten
even with the phantasies. And if someone considers the things of Job, and
says, Why then did the devil, going out, do all things against him? and he
stripped him of the belongings, and killed the children, and struck that man
with an evil sore? let such a one know again that it was not the devil who was
strong, but God who delivered Job to him for trial. At any rate, being able to
do nothing, he asked and, having received, he did it. So even from this the
enemy is rather to be condemned, because, although wishing, he was not strong
even against one just man. For if he were strong, he would not have asked. And
having asked not once, but even a second time, he appears weak and able to do
nothing. And it is not marvelous if he was not strong against Job, since not
even against his cattle did destruction come, unless God had allowed. But
neither against swine does he have the authority. For they asked, as it is
written in the Gospel, the Lord, saying: Allow us to go away into the swine.
And if they do not have authority even of swine, much more they do not have it
of men who have come to be according to the image of God. God then alone must
be feared. And these must be despised, and one must not fear them at all. But
rather, as much as they do these things, let us stretch the ascetic life
against them. For a great weapon against them is =26.889= a right life, and
the faith toward God. They fear, then, the fasting of the ascetics, the vigil,
the prayers, the meekness, the quiet, the freedom from love of money, the
freedom from empty glory, the humility, the love of the poor, the alms, the
freedom from anger, and first of all the piety toward Christ. For this reason
they also do all things, so that they may not have those who trample them. For
they know the grace given against them to the faithful by the Savior, he
himself saying: Behold I have given you authority to tread upon snakes and
scorpions, and upon all the power of the enemy. If then they also play a part
of foretelling, let no one pretend. For often days before they say the
brothers who meet after days. And those come. And they do this not caring for
those who hear, but so that they may persuade them to believe them, and then
thereafter, having them under their hand, they may destroy them. Whence one
must not attend to them, but even when they speak one must overturn them,
because we have no need of these. For what is marvelous if, using finer bodies
than men, and having seen those who have begun to travel, they anticipate by
the run and report? This even one sitting on a horse foretells, anticipating
the one traveling with the feet. So not even in this must one marvel at them.
For they know beforehand nothing of the things that have not come to be. But
God alone is the one who knows all things before their coming-to-be. And
these, as thieves, running ahead, report what they see. To how many now do
they signify our things, and that we have come together, and that we converse
against them, before anyone going away from us reports! And this even a boy
who is a fast runner can do, anticipating the one who is slow. And what I say
is of this sort. If someone begins to walk from the Thebaid, or from some
other country, before he begins to walk they do not know if he will walk. But
having seen this man walking, they run ahead, and before he comes they report.
And thus it happens that these =26.892= come after days. And often, when those
walking turned back,


they themselves lied. Thus also about the river water there are times when
they talk nonsense. For having seen many rains coming to be in the parts of
Ethiopia, and knowing that from those the flood of the river comes to be,
before the water comes into Egypt, running ahead they speak. And men too would
have said this, if they were able to run as much as those. And as the watchman
of David, going up to a high place, saw beforehand the one coming more than
the one remaining below. And he himself running ahead spoke before the others,
not the things that had not come to be, but the things already traveling and
coming to be. Thus these too choose to toil, and they signify to others, only
so that they may deceive. If however Providence in between plans something
about the waters or about those traveling (for it is allowed to it), the
demons have lied, and those who attended to them have been deceived. Thus the
oracles of the Greeks were constituted, and thus they were led astray by the
demons formerly. But even thus the deceit has thereafter ceased. For the Lord
came who with that very cunning of theirs also made the demons idle. For they
know nothing from themselves. But as thieves, what they see from others, these
things they slander. And they are rather guessers than foreknowers. Wherefore,
even if they sometimes say such things truly, let no one marvel at them even
thus. For physicians too, having experience of the diseases, whenever they
observe the same disease in others, often guessing from the custom they
foretell. And pilots and farmers again, looking from the custom at the state
of the air, foretell that there will be either a storm or fair air. And not
for this reason would one say they foretell from a divine inspiration, but
from experience and from custom. Whence even if the demons, guessing the same
things, sometimes speak, let no one marvel at them for this, nor attend to
them. For what is useful =26.893= to those who hear to know from these, days
before, the things that are coming? Or what zeal is it to know such things,
even if one truly knows? For this is not productive of virtue, nor is this
altogether a mark of a good character. For none of us is judged because of why
he does not know, and no one is blessed because he has learned and known. But
in these each has the judgment, if he has kept the faith, and has genuinely
guarded the commandments. Whence one must not make much of these things. Nor
must one practice and toil for these, so that we may know beforehand, but so
that living well we may please God. And one must pray, not so that we may know
beforehand, nor to demand this as a wage of the ascetic life, but so that the
Lord may become a fellow-worker to us unto the victory against the devil. And
if once we also care about knowing beforehand, let us be pure in mind. For I
believe that a soul being pure from everywhere, and standing according to
nature, can, becoming clear-sighted, see more and farther than the demons,
having the Lord who reveals to it: such as was that of Elisha, seeing the
things concerning Gehazi, and seeing the powers standing beside it. When then
they come to you by night, and wish to speak the things to come, or they say:
We are the angels: do not attend, for they lie. And if they also praise your
ascetic life, and call you blessed: neither obey, nor pretend them at all. But
rather seal yourselves and the house, and pray. And you will see them even
becoming invisible. For they are cowardly, and they very much fear the sign of
the Lord's cross, since on it the Savior, stripping them, made a show of them.
And if they also stand more shamelessly, dancing out and varying themselves
with the phantasies: do not be cowardly, nor cower, nor attend to them as to
good ones. For the presence of the good and of the base it is easy and
=26.896= possible to discern,


God granting it so. For the vision of the saints is not disturbed. For he will
not strive, nor will he cry out, nor will anyone hear their voice. And it
comes to be quietly and meekly thus, so that at once joy and gladness and
courage come to be in the soul. For with them is the Lord, who is our joy, and
the power of God the Father. And its thoughts remain undisturbed and without
wave. So that, being shone upon by it, it contemplates those who appear. For a
longing of the divine things and of the things to come also enters it, and it
will altogether wish to be joined to these, if it were going away with them.
And if even, as men, some fear the vision of the good, those who appear take
away the fear at once by love: as Gabriel did to Zechariah, and the angel who
appeared in the divine tomb to the women, and the one saying to the shepherds
in the Gospel: Do not fear. For the fear of those is not according to
cowardice of soul, but according to recognition of the presence of the better
ones. Such then is the vision of the saints. But the onset and phantasy of the
base is disturbed, with noise and sound and crying: such as would be a
movement of untrained younger men and of robbers. From which at once comes
cowardice of soul, disturbance and disorder of thoughts, dejection, hatred
toward the ascetics, listlessness, grief, memory of the household, and fear of
death. And thereafter desire of evils, negligence toward virtue, and
unstableness of character. When then, having observed someone, you fear, if
the fear is at once taken away, and instead of that there comes to be
unspeakable joy, and good cheer, and courage, and recovery, and undisturbance
of the thoughts, and the other things I said before, both courage and love
toward God, take courage and pray. For the joy and the settled state of the
soul shows the holiness of the one present. Thus Abraham, seeing the Lord,
rejoiced. =26.897= And John, when a voice came from the God-bearing Mary,
leaped in gladness. But if, when some appear, disturbance comes to be, and
noise from outside, and a worldly phantasy, and a threat of death, and as many
things as I said before: know that the onset is of the base. For let this too
be a mark for you: whenever then the soul remains being cowardly, it is a
presence of the enemies. For the demons do not take away the cowardice of such
people, as the great archangel Gabriel did to Mary and to Zechariah, and the
one who appeared in the tomb to the women. But rather when they see them being
cowardly, they increase the phantasies, so that they may cower them more. And
thereafter attacking they mock, saying: Falling down, worship. The Greeks,
then, they deceived thus. For thus they were thought among them false-named
gods. But us the Lord did not allow to be deceived by the devil, when, as he
made such phantasies, rebuking him he said: Go behind me, Satan. For it is
written: You shall worship the Lord your God, and him only shall you serve.
More and more then let the cunning one be despised by us because of these
things. For what the Lord said, this he has done for us, so that the demons,
hearing such voices also from us, may be overturned because of the Lord who
rebuked them in these. And one must not boast upon casting out demons, nor be
lifted up upon the healings. Nor must one marvel only at the one who casts out
demons, and set at nothing the one who does not cast out. But let one learn
the ascetic life of each, and either imitate and rival, or be corrected. For
to do signs is not ours. This is a work of the Savior. To the disciples, then,
he said: Do not rejoice that the demons are subject to you, but that your
names have been written in the heavens. For the names having been written in
heaven =26.900= is a testimony


of our virtue and of the life. But to cast out demons, this grace is of the
Savior who has given it. Whence to those who boast not in virtue but in signs,
and say: Lord, did we not cast out demons in your name, and in your name do
many powers? he answered: Amen I say to you, I do not know you. For the Lord
does not know the ways of the impious. And in general one must pray, as I said
before, to receive a gift of discernment of spirits, so that, as it is
written, we may not believe every spirit. I wished then to be silent, and to
say nothing from myself, and to be content with these only. But so that you
may not think I say these things simply, but may believe I relate these from
experience and truth: for this reason, even if I become as a fool, yet the
Lord who hears knows the purity of the conscience, and that not for myself,
but for the sake of your love and exhortation, what I saw of the practices of
the demons, these I say again. How many times they called me blessed, and I
cursed them in the name of the Lord. How many times they have spoken
beforehand about the river water, and I said to them: And what care have you
about this? They came once threatening, and they surrounded me as soldiers
with full armor. And at another time they filled the house with horses and
beasts and reptiles. And I sang: These in chariots, and these in horses, but
we shall be magnified in the name of the Lord our God. And by the prayers
those were overturned by the Lord. They came once in darkness, having a
phantasy of light, and they said: We have come to shine for you, Antony. But
I, shutting my eyes, prayed, and at once the light of the impious was
quenched. And after a few months they came as singing, and speaking from the
Scriptures. And I as one deaf did not hear. They shook the monastery once. But
I prayed, remaining unmoved in thought. And after these things again =26.901=
coming they clapped, they hissed, they danced. And as I prayed, and lay
singing to myself, at once they began to lament and to weep, as if they had
grown exhausted. And I glorified the Lord, who had taken down and made a show
of their daring and their madness. A demon appeared once, very high, with a
phantasy, and he dared to say: I am the power of God. And, I am the
providence. What do you wish that I grant you? But I then rather blew against
him, naming Christ, and I attempted to strike this one. And I seemed to have
struck, and at once so great a one with all his own demons vanished in the
name of Christ. He came once as I was fasting, and the crafty one as a monk,
having a phantasy of loaves. And he advised, saying: Eat, and cease from the
many toils. You too are a man, and you are about to be weak. But I,
understanding his method, rose to pray. And that one did not bear it. For he
vanished, and appeared going out through the door as smoke. How many times in
the desert he showed a phantasy of gold, only so that I might touch and look!
But I sang against him, and that one melted. Often they beat me with blows,
and I said: Nothing will separate me from the love of Christ. And rather they
themselves after these things beat one another to pieces. And I was not the
one stopping those and making them idle. But it was the Lord, who says: I
beheld Satan as lightning fallen from heaven. And I, children, remembering the
apostolic saying, transformed these things unto myself, so that you may learn
not to grow faint in the ascetic life, nor to fear the phantasies of the devil
and of his demons. =26.904= And since I have become a fool in relating,
receive this too toward safety and fearlessness. And believe me. For I do not
lie. Someone once knocked at my door in the monastery. And going out, I saw
someone appearing long and high. Then when I asked: Who are you? he said: I am


Satan. Then when I said: Why then are you present here? that one said: Why do
the monks blame me in vain, and all the other Christians? Why do they curse me
every hour? And when I said: Why then do you harass them? he said: I am not
the one who harasses them, but they disturb themselves. For I have become
weak. Have they not read that The swords of the enemy have failed unto the
end, and you have taken down cities? I no longer have a place, no dart, no
city. Everywhere they have become Christians. Thereafter even the desert has
been filled with monks. Let them keep themselves, and let them not curse me in
vain. Then marveling at the grace of the Lord I said to him: Always being a
liar, and never saying truth, nevertheless this now, even not wishing, you
have said true. For Christ, having come, has made you weak, and having thrown
you he has stripped you. That one, hearing the name of the Savior, and not
bearing the burning from this, became invisible. If then even the devil
himself confesses he can do nothing, we ought altogether to despise him and
his demons. The enemy then with his own dogs has so many cunning works. And
we, having learned their weakness, can despise them. In this way then let us
not fall beforehand in the mind, nor consider cowardices in the soul, nor
fashion fears for ourselves, saying: Lest perhaps a demon coming overturn me.
Lest perhaps taking me up he throw me, or suddenly standing over me =26.905=
he disturb me out. Let us not think such things at all, nor be grieved as if
we were perishing. But let us rather take courage and always rejoice, as being
saved. And let us consider in the soul that the Lord is with us, who has
turned them and made them idle. And let us also always think and remember
that, the Lord being with us, the enemies will do nothing to us. For having
come, whatever sort they find us, such they themselves also become toward us,
and toward the thoughts they find in us, thus they themselves also liken the
phantasies. If then they find us being cowardly and being disturbed, at once

canon of the Church beyond nature, and he wished every cleric to go before
himself in the honor. For to the bishops and the presbyters he was not ashamed
to bend the head. And if a deacon ever met him for the sake of benefit, the
things


toward benefit he conversed. And the things of the prayer he yielded to him,
not being ashamed to learn himself too. For often he asked, and =26.940= he
thought it right to hear from those with him. And he confessed to be
benefited, if someone said something useful. And indeed also his face had much
and paradoxical grace. And he had this gift too from the Savior. For if he was
present with the multitude of the monks, and someone wished to see this man,
not knowing him before, coming at once, he passed over the others, and ran
toward him, as drawn by his looks. And he did not differ from the others in
height, nor in breadth, but in the settled state of the characters, and in the
purity of the soul. For the soul being without tumult, he had also the outer
senses undisturbed. So as from the joy of the soul to have also the face
cheerful, and from the movements of the body to perceive and to understand the
state of the soul, according to what is written: When a heart is gladdened, a
face blooms; and when it is in griefs, it is sullen. Thus Jacob recognized
Laban thinking a plot, and he says to the women: The face of your father is
not as yesterday and the third day. Thus Samuel recognized David. For he had
glad-making eyes, and the teeth white as milk. Thus Antony too was recognized.
For he was never disturbed, his soul being calm. He never became sullen, his
mind rejoicing. And in the faith too he was very marvelous and pious. For he
never communed with the Meletians the schismatics, knowing their vice from the
beginning and their apostasy. Nor did he converse friendly with Manichaeans,
or with some other heretics, except only as far as an admonition of the change
unto piety, thinking and charging that the friendship and converse of these is
harm and destruction of soul. Thus then he also abhorred the heresy of the
Arians, and he charged all neither to come near them, nor to have their
evil-faith. Some of the Ariomaniacs then having gone away once to him, having
examined and learned they were impious, =26.941= he drove them from the
mountain, saying their words were worse than the poison of snakes. And when
the Arians once lied, as if he thought the same things as those, he was
indignant and was wrathful against those. Then being asked by the bishops and
all the brothers, he came down from the mountain. And entering Alexandria, he
denounced the Arians, saying this was a last heresy, and a forerunner of the
Antichrist. And he taught the people that the Son of God is not a creature,
nor has come to be from things that are not. But that he is eternally Word and
Wisdom of the essence of the Father. Wherefore it is also impious to say:
There was when he was not. For the Word always was, coexisting with the
Father. Whence have not even one communion toward the most impious Arians. For
there is no communion of light toward darkness. For you, being pious, are
Christians. But those, saying that the Son of God and Word from the Father is
a creature, differ in nothing from the pagans, serving the creation beside
the God who created. And believe that even all the creation itself is
indignant against them, because the creator and Lord of the all, in whom all
things have come to be, this one they number with the generated things. All
the peoples then rejoiced, hearing from such a man the Christ-fighting heresy
being anathematized. And all of the city ran together to see Antony. And
Greeks too, and those called their priests themselves, came into the Lord's
house saying: We ask to see the man of God. For all called him thus. For there
too the Lord through him cleansed many from demons, and healed those harmed in
the mind. And many Greeks asked even only to touch the old man,


believing they were benefited. At any rate so many became =26.944= Christians
in those few days, as many as one would have seen coming to be in a year.
Then, some thinking he was disturbed from the crowds, and for this reason
turning all away from him, he himself, not being disturbed, said these were
not more than those demons with whom we wrestle on the mountain. And when he
went away, and we sent him forward, as we arrived at the gate, a woman behind
cried: Wait, man of God, my daughter is terribly harassed by a demon. Wait, I
beg, lest I too be in danger as I run. The old man hearing, and being asked by
us, wishing he remained. And as the woman drew near, the child was thrown on
the ground. And when Antony prayed, and named Christ, the child was raised
healthy, the unclean demon having gone out. And the mother blessed God, and
all gave thanks. And he himself too rejoiced going away, as to his own house,
to the mountain. And he was very prudent. And the marvelous thing, that, not
having learned letters, he was a sharp and intelligent man. Once then two
Greek philosophers came to him, thinking they could try Antony. And he was on
the outer mountain. And he, understanding the men from the face, going out
toward them, said through an interpreter: Why have you been so troubled, O
philosophers, toward a foolish man? And when they said he was not foolish, but
even very prudent, he said to them: If you came toward a foolish man, your
labor is superfluous. And if you think I am prudent, become as I am. For one
must imitate the good things. And if I were coming toward you, I would have
imitated you. And if you toward me, become as I am. For I am a Christian. And
they, marveling, withdrew. For they also saw demons fearing Antony. =26.945=
And when others of such again met him on the outer mountain, and thought to
mock, because he had not learned letters, Antony says to them: And you, what
do you say? What is first, mind or letters? And what is cause of what, the
mind of the letters, or the letters of the mind? And when they said the mind
is first, and inventor of the letters, Antony said: To whom then the mind is
healthy, to this one the letters are not necessary. This also struck those
present and them themselves. They went away then marveling, that they saw so
much understanding in an unlettered man. For he did not, as having been reared
on
a mountain, and having become an old man there, have a wild character. But he
was also gracious and civic. And he had the word seasoned with the divine
salt, so that no one envied, but rather all who came to him rejoiced over him.
At any rate after these things again when certain others came (and these were
of those who among Greeks seem to be wise), and demanded of him a word about
our faith in Christ, and attempted to syllogize about the proclamation of the
divine cross, and wished to mock: Antony, holding a little, and first pitying
them upon their ignorance, said through an interpreter, who interpreted his
things well: What is more beautiful, to confess a cross, or to attach
adulteries and corruptions of boys to the gods spoken of among you? For what
is said from us is a proof of courage, and a mark of despising of death. And
your things are passions of wantonness. Then what is better, to say that the
Word of God was not changed, but being the same, for the salvation and
benefaction of men he took up a human body, so that, having shared in the
human coming-to-be, he might make men share in a divine and intellectual
nature: or to liken the Divine in irrational creatures, and for this reason to
reverence =26.948= four-footed things, and reptiles, and images of men? For
these are the objects of reverence of you the wise. And how do you dare to
mock us, who say that


Christ has been manifested as a man: where you, separating the soul from
heaven, claim it has been led astray and has fallen from the vault of the
heavens into a body: and would that into a human one only, and not that it
passes over and falls into four-footed things and reptiles. For our faith says
the presence of Christ is for the salvation of men. And you are led astray,
because you relate about an ungenerated soul. And we think the power and the
love of humanity of Providence, that this too was not impossible for God. And
you, saying the soul is an image of the mind, attach falls to it, and you
mythologize it as changeable. And thereafter you also introduce the mind
itself as changeable because of the soul. For of whatever sort the image was,
such of necessity is also that one of whom it is the image. And whenever you
think such things about the mind, you consider that you also blaspheme against
the Father of the mind himself. And about the cross what better would you say,
to endure a cross when a plot is brought on by evil men, and not to cower at
death brought on in whatever way: or to mythologize wanderings of Osiris and
Isis, and plots of Typhon, and a flight of Kronos, and swallowings of
children, and father-killings? For these are your wise things. And how,
mocking the cross, do you not marvel at the resurrection? For those who said
this also wrote that. Or why, remembering the cross, do you keep silent about
the dead who were raised, and the blind who looked up, and the paralyzed who
were healed, and the lepers who were cleansed =26.949= and the walking on foot
upon the sea, and the other signs and wonders, which no longer show Christ a
man, but God? You seem to me very much to wrong yourselves, and not to have
genuinely encountered our Scriptures. But you do encounter them, and you see
that the things Christ has done prove him God, having sojourned for the
salvation of men. And you too tell us your things. And what would you say
about the irrational creatures, except irrationality and wildness? And if, as
I hear, you wish to say these things are said mythically among you, and you
allegorize the seizure of Kore into the earth, and the lameness of Hephaestus
into the fire, and Hera into the air, and Apollo into the sun, and Artemis
into the moon, and Poseidon into the sea: you no less do not reverence God
himself, but you serve the creation beside the God who created all things. For
if, because the creation is beautiful, you composed such things: yet you ought
to have come to be only as far as marveling, and not to make gods of the
things made, so that you may not give the honor of the Craftsman to the
generated things. Since it is time for you also to transfer the honor of the
architect unto the house that has come to be by him, or that of the general
unto the soldier. What then do you say to these things, so that we may know if
the cross has something worthy of mockery? And when those were at a loss, and
were turning here and there, Antony, smiling, said again through an
interpreter: These things even from the sight itself have the conviction. And
since you rather lean on demonstrative words, and having this craft you wish
us too not without the demonstration through words to be godly: tell first
you, how the things, and especially the knowledge about God, =26.952= are
exactly discerned, through a demonstration of words, or through an energy of
faith? And what is more senior, the faith through energy, or the demonstration
through words? And when they answered that the faith through energy is more
senior, and that this is the exact knowledge, Antony said: You have spoken
well. For the faith comes to be from a disposition of soul. And the dialectic
is from a craft of those who compose. Therefore to those for whom the energy
through faith is present, for these the demonstration through words is not
necessary, or perhaps even superfluous. For even what we


understand from faith, this you try to construct through words. And often you
cannot even phrase what we understand. So that the energy through faith is
better and more fortified than your sophistic syllogisms. We Christians then
do not have the mystery in wisdom of Greek words, but in a power of faith
supplied to us through Jesus Christ from God. And that the word is true,
behold now, we not having learned letters, we believe in God, recognizing
through his things made the providence unto all things. And that our faith is
active, behold now we lean on the faith in Christ, and you on sophistic
word-fights. And the apparitions of the idols among you are made idle, and the
faith among us stretches out everywhere. And you, syllogizing and playing the
sophist, do not persuade away from Christianity unto Hellenism. And we,
teaching the faith in Christ, strip you of the superstition, as all recognize
that Christ is God, and Son of God. And you with fine speech do not hinder the
teaching of Christ. And we, naming the crucified Christ, drive all demons,
whom you fear as gods. And where the sign of the cross comes to be, magic is
weak, and drug-craft does not work. Tell then, where now are your oracles?
Where the charms of the Egyptians? Where the phantasies =26.953= of the magi?
When have all these things ceased and become weak, except when the cross of
Christ came to be? Is this then worthy of mockery, or rather the things made
idle by it and convicted as weak? For this too is marvelous: that your things
were never persecuted, but are even honored by men according to city. And the
things of Christ are persecuted, and rather the things among us bloom and
multiply above your things. And your things, being well-spoken of and fenced
around, are destroyed. And the faith of Christ and the teaching, being mocked
by you, and often persecuted by kings, has filled the inhabited world. For
when did knowledge of God so shine? Or when did temperance and a virtue of
virginity so appear? Or when was death so despised, except when the cross of
Christ came to be? And no one doubts this, seeing the martyrs despising death
because of Christ, seeing the virgins of the Church keeping the bodies pure
and unstained because of Christ. And these are sufficient proofs to show that
the faith according to Christ alone is true unto piety. Behold still you
disbelieve, seeking the syllogisms from the words. We do not demonstrate in
persuasive words of Greek wisdom, as our teacher said, but with the faith we
persuade, clearly anticipating the construction from the words. Behold there
are present here those who suffer from demons. (And there were some who had
come to him harassed by demons.) And leading them into the middle, he said:
Either you, with their syllogisms, and with whatever craft or magic you wish,
calling upon your own idols, cleanse them. Or, if you cannot, put down the
fight toward us, and you will see the power of the cross of Christ. And having
said these things, he called upon Christ, and he sealed those who suffered
with the sign of the cross a second and a third time. And at once =26.956= the
men stood whole, being in their senses and thereafter giving thanks to the
Lord. And the so-called philosophers marveled, and were truly struck at the
understanding of the man, and at the sign that came to be. And Antony said:
Why do you marvel at this? We are not the ones who do it, but it is Christ,
who does these things through those who believe in him. Believe then you too.
And you will see that the things among us are not a craft of words,


but a faith being worked through love toward Christ: which if you too should
have, you will no longer seek the demonstrations through words, but you will
think the faith in Christ sufficient. These were the words of Antony. And
those, marveling even in this, withdrew, embracing him, and confessing they
were benefited by him. And the report about Antony arrived even as far as
kings. For Constantine Augustus, having learned these things, and his sons
Constantius and Constans the Augusti, wrote to him as to a father, and they
prayed to receive answers from him. But he neither made much of the letters,
nor became glad upon the epistles. And he was the same as he was even before
the kings wrote to him. And when the letters were brought to him, he called
the monks, and said: Do not marvel if a king writes to us, for he is a man.
But rather marvel that God wrote the law to men, and has spoken to us through
his own Son. He wished then not to receive the letters, saying he did not know
how to write back toward such things. But being urged by the monks, because
the kings are Christians, and so that they might not be scandalized as if cast
aside, he allowed them to be read. And he wrote back, accepting them, because
they worship Christ, and he advised the things unto salvation: and not to
think the present things great, but rather to remember the judgment to come,
and to know that =26.957= Christ only is true and eternal king. And he asked
them to be lovers of humanity, and to take thought of the just and of the
poor. And those, receiving, rejoiced. Thus he was dear among all, and all
thought it right to have him as a father. Being known such then, and answering
thus toward those who met him, he returned again to the inner mountain. And he
held to the customary ascetic practice. And often sitting with those who
entered to him, and walking, he became speechless, as it is written in Daniel.
And he himself after an hour conversed the following things to the brothers
with him. And those with him perceived that he saw some contemplation. For
even the things coming to be in Egypt he often, happening to be on the
mountain, saw, and he related to Serapion the bishop, who was inside and saw
Antony occupied with the vision. Once then sitting and working, he became as
in an ecstasy, and he was much in the contemplation groaning. Then after an
hour turning toward those with him, he groaned, and becoming trembling he
prayed, and bending the knees he remained a long time. And rising the old man
wept. Those with him then becoming trembling, and fearing very much, asked to
learn from him. And they troubled him much until, being forced, he spoke. And
he even thus groaning greatly said: O children, it is better to die, before
the things of the contemplation come to be. And when they asked again, weeping
he said: Wrath is about to overtake the Church, and it is about to be
delivered to men like irrational cattle. For I saw the table of the Lord's
house, and around it mules standing in a circle from everywhere, and kicking
the things inside thus, as kicks would come to be of cattle leaping
disorderly. =26.960= And you altogether perceived, he says, how I groaned. For
I heard a voice saying: My altar shall be abominated. These things the old man
saw. And after two years came to be the present onset of the Arians, and the
seizure of the churches, when also having seized the vessels with force they
made them be carried through the pagans: when they also forced the pagans
from the workshops to gather with them: and with them present they did upon
the table as they wished. Then we all recognized that the kicks of the mules,
these things beforehand signified to Antony, which now the


Arians do irrationally as the cattle. And when he saw this contemplation, he
exhorted those with him, saying: Do not be disheartened, children. For as the
Lord was wrathful, so he will again heal. And again quickly the Church will
receive back her own adornment, and will shine as usual. And you will see
those
who were persecuted being restored, and the impiety again withdrawing into its
own dens, and the pious faith speaking freely with all freedom everywhere.
Only do not stain yourselves with the Arians. For this is not the teaching of
the apostles, but of the demons, and of their father the devil. And it is
rather barren, and irrational, and of a mind not right, as the irrationality
of the mules. Such then are the things of Antony. And we must not disbelieve
if through a man so many marvels have come to be. For it is a promise of the
Savior, saying: If you have faith as a grain of mustard, you will say to this
mountain: Pass from here, and it will pass. And nothing will be impossible for
you. And again: Amen, amen I say to you, if you ask the Father anything in my
name, he will give it to you. Ask, and you will receive. And he himself is the
one saying to the disciples, and to all who believe in him: Heal those who are
weak. Cast out demons. You received as a gift, give as a gift. =26.961= Antony
then did not heal by commanding, but by praying and naming Christ. So that it
became clear to all that it was not he who did it, but the Lord, showing love
of humanity through Antony, and healing those who suffered. And of Antony only
was the prayer and the ascetic practice, because of which sitting on the
mountain he rejoiced in the contemplation of the divine things, and was
grieved being harassed by many and being dragged to the outer mountain. For
even all judges asked him to come down from the mountain, since it was not
possible for them to enter there, because of those following of those being
judged. And they asked nevertheless, so that he might come and they might only
see him. He himself then turned away and declined the roads toward these. And
they persisted, and rather sent forward those who were liable under soldiers,
so that even on the occasion of those he might come down. Suffering necessity
then, and seeing them lamenting, he came to the outer mountain. And his
trouble was again not unprofitable. For to many his arrival came to be unto
profit, and unto benefaction. And he benefited the judges, advising them to
prefer the just above all things, and to fear God, and to know that with what
judgment they judge, they will be judged. Only he loved the spending of time
on the mountain above all things. Once then having suffered such force from
those in need, and the commander through many having asked him to come down,
coming, and conversing a few things that arrived unto salvation, and about
those in need, he was in haste. And when the so-called duke asked him to
delay, he said he could not spend time with them, and with a gracious example
he persuaded this one, saying: As the fishes, spending time on the dry land,
die, so the monks, delaying with you, and spending time among you, are
dissolved. One must then, as the fish into the sea, so us hurry into the
mountain, lest delaying, =26.964= we forget the things inside. And the
commander, hearing these things from him, and many others, marveling, said
this one was truly a slave of God. For whence to an unlettered man such and so
great a mind, if he were not loved by God? And one certain commander, and
Balacius was his name, bitterly persecuted us Christians because of the zeal
for the ill-named Arians. And since he was so cruel that he even struck
virgins,
and stripped and whipped monks, Antony sends to him, and writes a letter

you take thought about the monks, already I will come after you too. And five

admonished that they forgot at once the judging, and called blessed those who
withdraw from this life. And thus he stood before those who were wronged, so
that =26.965= they thought not others, but he himself was the one who
suffered. And thus again he was sufficient toward benefit for all, so that
many who were in military service, and of those who possessed many things, put
away the burdens of life, and thereafter became monks. And altogether he was
as a physician given by God to Egypt. For who came grieved, and did not return
rejoicing? Who came lamenting because of his own dead, and did not at once put
away the mourning? Who came angry, and was not changed into friendship? Who a
poor man coming listless, and hearing him and seeing him, did not despise the
wealth, and was comforted of the poverty? Who a monk, having become negligent,
and coming to him, did not rather become stronger? Who a younger man coming to
the mountain, and observing Antony, did not at once deny the pleasures, and
love temperance? Who came to him being tried by a demon, and was not given
rest? And who came harassed in thoughts, and did not become calm in the mind?

said before, having a gift of discernment of spirits, he recognized their
movements. And toward what each of them had the zeal and the impulse, this he
knew. And not only was he himself not played with by them, but
also exhorting those harassed in thoughts he taught how they might be able to
overturn the plots of those, relating the weaknesses and the cunning works of
those who work. Each then, as if anointed by him, came down taking courage
against the thoughts of the devil and of his demons. And how many virgins
having suitors, and only seeing Antony from the other side, remained virgins
for Christ? And they came also from the outer parts to him. And they
themselves, having had the benefit with all, returned, as being sent forward
by a father. At any rate when he fell asleep, all, as having become orphans of
a father, comfort themselves with his memory only, holding at once his
admonitions and his exhortations. =26.968= And of what sort the end of his
life also came to be for him, it is worthy for me too to remember, and for you
who long to hear. For this too became something to be rivaled of his.
According to custom he visited the monks on the outer mountain, and having
learned beforehand from Providence about his own end, he spoke to the brothers
saying: This visit of yours I make last, and I marvel if


we shall again see one another in this life. It is time for me too thereafter
to depart. For I am near a hundred and five years. Those then who heard wept,
and they embraced and kissed the old man. And he, as setting out from a
foreign city into his own, conversed rejoicing. And he charged them not to be
negligent in the toils, nor to grow faint in the ascetic life, but to live as
dying each day. And as I said before to be eager to keep the soul from filthy
thoughts. And to have the zeal toward the saints, and not to come near the
Meletians the schismatics. For you know their evil and profane choice. Nor to
have any communion toward the Arians. For their impiety too is clear to all.

cease, and their phantasy is mortal and toward a little. Keep yourselves then
rather pure from these, and keep both the tradition of the Fathers, and first
of all the pious faith in our Lord Jesus Christ, which you have learned from
the Scriptures, and of which you have often been reminded by me. And when the
brothers forced him to remain with them, and to be perfected there, he did not
endure, because of many things, as he himself even being silent indicated, and
especially because of this. The Egyptians love to bury the bodies of those who
die who are earnest, and especially of the holy martyrs, and to wrap them in
linens, and not to hide =26.969= them under earth, but to set them upon
couches, and to keep them inside with themselves, thinking in this to honor
those who have gone away. And Antony often about this asked even bishops to
charge the peoples. And likewise he also put laymen to shame, and he rebuked
women, saying this was neither lawful, nor holy at all. For even the bodies of
the patriarchs and of the prophets are kept until now in tombs. And the body
of the Lord itself was set in a tomb, and a stone being set upon it hid it,
until he rose on the third day. And saying these things, he showed that the
one who after death does not hide the bodies of those who die acts against the
law, even if they happen to be holy. For what is greater or more holy than the
Lord's body? Many then, hearing, thereafter hid them under earth, and gave
thanks to the Lord, having been well taught. And he himself, knowing this, and
fearing lest they also do his own body thus, hurried himself, having taken
leave of the monks on the outer mountain. And entering the inner mountain,
where he was also accustomed to remain, after a few months he became ill. And
calling those with him (and they were two, who also remained inside,
practicing fifteen years, and serving him because of old age), he said to
them: I, as it is written, travel the road of the fathers. For I see myself
being called by the Lord. And you, be sober, and do not lose your long-time
ascetic practice. But as now having a beginning, be eager to keep your
eagerness. You know the demons who plot, you know how they are wild, but weak
in power. Do not then fear them, but rather always breathe Christ, and believe
in this one. And live as dying each day, attending to yourselves, and
remembering the exhortations you heard from me. And let there be no communion
for you toward the schismatics, nor at all toward the heretic Arians. For you
know =26.972= how I too turned away from these because of their
Christ-fighting and other-doctrine heresy. And be eager rather you too always
to join yourselves, first of all to the Lord, and then to the saints, so that
after death they themselves too may receive you into the eternal tents, as
friends and acquaintances. Think these things, mind these things. And if you
care about me, and


remember me as about a father, do not allow any to take my body into Egypt,
lest they set it away in the houses. For for the sake of this I entered the
mountain, and I came here. And you know also how I always put to shame those
who do this, and I charged them to cease from such a custom. Bury then our
body, you, and hide it under earth. And let the word from me be kept by you,
so that no one know the place, except you only. For I in the resurrection of
the dead will receive it back from the Savior incorruptible. And divide my
garments. And to Athanasius the bishop give the one sheepskin, and the cloak
which I was laid upon, which he himself gave me new, and by me it has grown
old. And to Serapion the bishop give the other sheepskin. And you have the
hairy garment. And thereafter be saved, children. For Antony is passing over,

had given them charges, having buried and wrapped, hid his body under earth,

things are small toward that man's virtue, yet from these consider you too of
what sort the man of God Antony was, who from younger years until so great an
age kept equal the eagerness of the ascetic practice. And neither because of
old age was he defeated by costly food, nor because of slackness of his own
body did he change the shape of the garment, or wash even the feet with water.
And nevertheless in all things he remained unharmed. For he also had the eyes
unhurt and whole, seeing well. And not even one of the teeth fell from him.
Only they had become worn under the gums, because of the great age of the old
man. And in the feet and the hands he remained healthy, and altogether he
himself appeared more bright than all who used varied food and baths and
different garments, and more eager toward strength. And that this man has been
spoken of everywhere, and is marveled at by all, and is longed for even by
those who have not seen him, is a mark of the virtue and of his God-loving
soul. For not from writings, nor from the wisdom from outside, nor through
some craft, but through piety only was Antony made known. And this as a gift
of God no one would deny. For whence into the Spains and into the Gauls, how
into Rome and Africa, hidden on a mountain and sitting, was he heard, except
if it was God, who makes his own men known everywhere, who also promised this
to Antony according to the beginning? For even if they themselves act
hiddenly, even if they wish to escape notice, yet the Lord shows them as lamps
to all, so that even thus those who hear may know that the commandments are
possible unto achievement, and may take a zeal of the road upon virtue. Read
these things then to the other brothers, so that they may learn of what sort
the life of the monks ought =26.976= to be, and may be persuaded that the Lord
and Savior of us Jesus Christ glorifies those who glorify him, and those who
serve him until the end he not only leads into the kingdom of the heavens, but
