* History of the Arians
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of the same, to the monks everywhere, concerning the things that have been
done by the Arians under Constantius. 1.1 ... but they themselves, for whose
sake they had contrived these things, fulfilled them not long after. For as
soon as they had formed their plot they immediately received Arius and his
fellows into communion. And they overlooked the so many judgments against
them, and again alleged imperial authority in their behalf; and writing they
were not ashamed to say that "when Athanasius has suffered, envy has ceased,
and let us now receive Arius and his fellows," adding, for the fear of the
hearers, that "for 1.2 the Emperor commanded it." Then they were not ashamed
to add that the men also think rightly, not fearing what is written: "Woe to
those who call the bitter sweet, who put darkness for light." For they are
ready to endure all things for the heresy. Is it not then from this also
plainly shown to all that not by an ecclesiastical judgment, but from the
Emperor's threat, because of piety toward Christ, we both suffered then and
are now persecuted? For they also plotted in this way against other bishops,
fabricating pretenses against those men too, of whom some fell asleep in the
exiles having the boast of the confession toward Christ, and others are still
even now in banishment, acting the more manfully against that heresy of theirs
and saying: "Nothing shall separate us from the love of Christ." 2.1 And it is
possible from this again to learn it thoroughly and to condemn it the more.
For the friend and fellow-impious with them, even if he is liable in other
offenses and countless charges, even if he has the proofs and the
demonstrations most clear, this man is approved among them and immediately
becomes a friend of the Emperor, having his introduction from the impiety, and
being paid he takes very much and has freedom to do what he wants before the
judges. 2.2 But he who exposes their impiety and genuinely advocates the
things of Christ, this man, even if he is pure in all things, even if he is
conscious of nothing in himself, even if he has no accuser, yet when they have
fabricated pretenses is immediately seized and by the Emperor's judgment
himself becomes an exile 2.3 as one liable, to whatever they may wish, or as
having insulted the Emperor, like Naboth. But he who advocates the things of
their heresy is sought out and immediately sent into that man's church 2.4 and
then confiscations and insults and all the terrible things against those who
do not receive him. And the most paradoxical thing: the one whom the peoples
want and know to be blameless, this one the Emperor takes away and banishes;
but the one whom they neither want nor know, this one he sends from far off
with soldiers and his own letters. And then there is a strong necessity to
hate the one they love, who catechized them and became a father in godliness,
and to love the one they do not want and to entrust their children to him,
whose life and conduct and who he is they do not know, or plainly to suffer
punishment if they do not obey the Emperor. 3.1 These things they both do now
and long ago the impious have done against the orthodox, furnishing everywhere
among all a mark of their own malice and impiety. 3.2 For granted they accused
Athanasius; what have the other bishops also done? What pretenses then did
they have, or what dead Arsenius was found there too? What presbyter Macarius
among them, and a cup fabricated? What Meletian played the hypocrite? But as
it seems, from those things the charges against Athanasius also are shown to
be false. And from what they attempted against Athanasius, 3.3 the things
against those men too are clear as fabricated. A great


beast this heresy came forth upon the earth. For not only with words as with
teeth does it injure the innocent, but it also hired the external power for
plotting. And the paradox is that, as I said before, no one from among them is
accused, and if he is accused he is not judged, or seeming to be heard he is
acquitted against those who convict him, and rather the one who convicts is
plotted against than the one liable is at all 3.4 put to shame. All then who
are among them happen to be full of filth, and their spies among them, for
they are not bishops, are rather filthier than all. And if anyone among them
wants to become a bishop, he does not hear: "A bishop must be blameless," but
only: "Think against Christ and do not care about manners, for that is enough
3.5 for you toward introduction and toward friendship with the Emperor." These
things concerning those who think the things of Arius; but the zealots of the
truth, even if they appear holy and pure, as I said before, yet become liable
whenever these men wish and as it seems good to them when they have fabricated
a pretense. And this it is possible, as I said before, to perceive from the
things done by them. 4.1 There was a certain Eustathius, bishop of Antioch, a
man a confessor and pious in the faith. This man, since he was much in zeal
for the truth and hated the Arian heresy and did not receive those who thought
its things, is slandered to Constantine the Emperor, and a pretense is
invented as if he had done insult to his mother. And immediately he himself
becomes an exile and a great number of presbyters and deacons with him. And
then those whom he would not 4.2 receive into the clergy because of the
impiety, these after the bishop had been banished they not only brought into
the church, but they also appointed most of them bishops, so that they might
have confederates of themselves for the impiety. From these is Leontius the
eunuch, the one now in Antioch, and the one before him, Stephanus, and George
who is in Laodicea, and Theodosius who was in Tripolis, and Eudoxius who is in
Germanicia, and Eustathius now in Sebasteia. 5.1 Did they then stop at these?
No; for also Eutropius who became bishop in Hadrianople, a good man and
perfect in all things, since he often exposed Eusebius and advised those who
traveled through not to obey the impious words of Eusebius, suffers the same
things as Eustathius and is cast out of the city and the church. For Basilina
5.2 was the one very eager against him. For Euphration who is in Balaneae and
Cymatius who is in Paltus and Carterius [another] who is in Antaradus and
Asclepas who is in Gaza and Cyrus who is in Beroea of Syria and Diodorus who
is in Asia and Domnion who is in Sirmium and Hellanicus who is in Tripolis
were only known as hating the heresy, and some with a pretense, others without
it, transferring them by imperial letters and casting them out of the city
they appointed others in their place, whom they knew to be impious, into those
men's churches. 6.1 And concerning Marcellus the bishop of Galatia it is
perhaps superfluous to speak, for all have known how, being first accused by
him of impiety, Eusebius and his fellows themselves also counter-accused 6.2
him and caused the old man to be banished. And he himself, going up to Rome,
made his defense and, being required by them, gave in writing his own faith,
which the council at Sardica also accepted; but Eusebius and his fellows
neither made a defense nor, being convicted from what they wrote as impious,
were they put to shame, but they even grew more bold against all; for they had
introduction to the Emperor from the women and were fearful to all. 7.1 And
concerning Paul the bishop of Constantinople I think no one is ignorant. For
as much as the city is eminent, so much also what happened has not been
hidden. And against this man too then a pretense was fabricated. For the one
who accused him, Macedonius, who is now bishop in his place, in our presence
at the accusation communicated with him and was a presbyter 7.2 under Paul
himself. And


yet since Eusebius had his eye on it, wanting to seize the bishopric of the
city (for thus he also moved from Berytus to Nicomedia), the pretense against
Paul remained, and they did not neglect the plot, but they remained
slandering. 7.3 And first he was banished into Pontus by Constantine, and the
second time by Constantius, bound with iron chains, he was banished to Singara
of Mesopotamia, then from there he was transferred to Emesa, and the fourth
time to Cucusus of Cappadocia about the deserts of Taurus, where also, as
those with him reported, having been strangled by 7.4 them he died. Yet having
done this, those who speak nothing true were not ashamed even after death to
fabricate again a pretense as if he had died from disease, 7.5 although all
who inhabit that place know this. For even Philagrius, being then vicar of
those places and playing the hypocrite in all their things as they might wish,
yet marveling at this and perhaps grieved that not he himself but another
worked the evil, reported to many others and to acquaintances of ours and to
the bishop Sarapion, that Paul, shut up by them in a certain very small and
dark place, was left to be destroyed by hunger, then after six days, when they
went in and found him still breathing, they then came upon him and strangled
the man. And 7.6 thus he had an end of this life. And they said that a servant
of such a death had been Philip who became prefect. But divine justice did not
overlook this either; for a year did not pass, and with much dishonor Philip
was deposed from the office, so that having become a private man he was mocked
by those he did not want. He himself then being very grieved and, according to
Cain, "groaning and trembling" and expecting day by day the one who would kill
him, outside his own country, both himself and his own people, as if struck
out of his wits, since he did not want it so, 7.7 died. Except that they do
not spare even against the dead, against whom while living they also
fabricated pretenses; for thus they were eager to show themselves fearful
toward all, and the living they banish, but the dead they do not pity. But
even then they alone among all men hate those who have departed and they plot
against their own people, being in truth inhuman and haters of the good and
having a manner more savage than enemies because of their own impiety, who
have been eager to plot against us and all the others not from truth but from
fabricated pretenses. 8.1 Seeing these things the three brothers Constantine,
Constantius, and Constans caused all after the death of the father to return
to the country and the church, writing concerning the others privately to each
one's church, but concerning Athanasius these things, which again show the
violence of the affair and convict the murderous purpose of Eusebius and his
fellows. 8.2τ Copy. Constantine Caesar to the people of the catholic church of
the city of the Alexandrians. I think that it has not escaped the knowledge of
your sacred mind, for this reason Athanasius the interpreter of the worshipful
law ... (It was written behind into the 75th discourse without change.) These
then are the things written; and of their conspiracy who else would become a
more trustworthy witness than this man? For knowing these things thus he
himself also wrote. 9.1 Eusebius and his fellows, however, seeing their heresy
diminishing, write to Rome, and they write also to the Emperors Constantine
and Constans against Athanasius. And when those sent by Athanasius also
refuted the things written by them, they were put to shame by the Emperors,
but Julius the bishop of Rome wrote that there ought to be a council, wherever
we should wish, so that they might show what they accuse, and concerning the
things of which they are accused they themselves also might defend themselves
with confidence. For this even the presbyters sent by them, 9.2 seeing
themselves being convicted, requested to be done. These men then


who happen to be suspicious in all things, seeing themselves not prevailing in
an ecclesiastical judgment, approach Constantius alone and then wail as to the
patron of the heresy, saying "spare the heresy; you see all have withdrawn
from us; few of us are left now. Begin to persecute, since we are being left
even by the few and we remain desolate. For those whom we forced when these
men had been banished, 9.3 these, having returned, they persuaded again to
think against us. Write then against all and send Philagrius a second time as
prefect of Egypt, for he himself is able to persecute conveniently, having
already shown it by trial, and especially being also an apostate. And send
also Gregory as bishop to Alexandria, for this man too is able to establish
our heresy." 10.1 Constantius then writes at that time, and he persecutes all
and sends Philagrius as prefect and a certain Arsacius a eunuch, and he also
sends Gregory with military power. And such things happened as also before.
For gathering a multitude of herdsmen and shepherds and other market-folk and
wanton younger men with swords and clubs they attacked 10.2 all at once the
church called that of Quirinus. And some they killed, some they trampled, and
others, cutting them down with blows, they threw into prison and banished, and
dragging many women they hauled them into the court publicly and insulted
them, dragging them by the hair; others they confiscated, from others they
took away bread for no other reason than that they might attach themselves to
the Arians and receive Gregory who had been sent from the Emperor. 11.1
Athanasius then, even before these things happened and only hearing, sailed to
Rome, knowing the rage of the heretics, and so that, as it had been decided,
the council might take place; and Julius writes and sends presbyters, Elpidius
and Philoxenus, setting also a 11.2 time-limit, so that they might either come
or know themselves to be suspicious in all things. But Eusebius and his
fellows, as soon as they heard that there would be an ecclesiastical judgment,
in which a count is not present, soldiers are not before the doors, the things
of the council are not completed by imperial command (for in these things they
have always had strength against the bishops, and without these they do not
dare even to speak at all), they were so terrified that they detained the
presbyters even after the time-limit, and fabricated an unseemly pretense that
"we are not able to come now because of the 11.3 wars arising from the
Persians." But this was not true, but fear of conscience. For what does war
have in common with bishops? Or why, being unable because of the Persians to
come to Rome, although it is far away and beyond sea, did they go about the
places of the East and those near them like lions seeking who opposes them, so
that slandering they might banish them? 12.1 At any rate, dismissing the
presbyters on this unconvincing pretense they talked with one another: "since
we are not able to prevail in an ecclesiastical judgment, let us display the
accustomed daring." They write then to Philagrius and make him go out after a
little with Gregory into Egypt, and then bishops are scourged and were bound
12.2 bitterly. Sarapammon then, a bishop and confessor, they banish, but
Potamon, a bishop and confessor who himself also lost the eye in the
persecution, they so cut down with blows on the neck that they did not stop
before the man was thought dead. Thus then he was thrown aside and scarcely
after hours, being treated and fanned, he breathed again, God having given the
living. But after a little time from the pain 12.3 of the blows he died,
having in Christ the boast of a second martyrdom. And how many other monks
were scourged while Gregory sat with Balacius the so-called duke, how many
bishops were cut, how many virgins were beaten. 13.1 Then the wretched Gregory
after these things exhorted all to communicate with him; and yet if


you thought it right to have communion from them, they were not worthy of
blows, but if you cut them as worthless, why did you exhort them as holy? But
nothing was set before him except to fulfill the things of those who sent him
and to establish the heresy. For this reason the foolish man became a murderer
and an executioner and 13.2 an insolent man and a deceitful man and a profane
man and, in sum, a fighter against Christ. The bishop's aunt then he so
persecuted that even when she had died he did not allow her to be buried. And
this would have happened and she would have been thrown out unburied, if those
who received her had not carried her out as their own dead; 13.3 thus even in
these things he had an unholy manner. For also when widows and other destitute
people had received alms he ordered the things given to be seized and the
vessels in which they carried the oil and the wine to be broken, so that he
might not only act impiously by taking, but also by deeds dishonor the Lord,
about to hear from him very soon: "Inasmuch as you dishonored these, you
dishonored me." 14.1 And he did many other things which even exceed the
expression of speech, and which someone hearing would think unbelievable. And
he acted thus for this reason, since he neither had the appointment by
ecclesiastical canon nor had been called bishop by apostolic tradition; but he
had been sent from the palace with military power and display as if entrusted
with a worldly office. 14.2 For this reason he also prayed rather to be a
friend of rulers than of bishops and monks. If ever then even father Antony
wrote from the mountain, as godliness is an abomination to a sinner, 14.3 so
he abhorred the letters of the holy man. But if ever the Emperor or a general
or another magistrate sent a letter, he became so overjoyed as those in the
Proverbs, as the Word said in indignation: "O you who leave straight paths,
who rejoice 14.4 in evils and are glad at the perversion of the wicked." At
any rate those who brought these things he honored with money, but when Antony
once wrote he made the duke Balacius spit on the letter and throw it away. But
divine justice did not overlook it, for not long after, when the so-called
duke was sitting on a horse and going to the first station, the horse turned
and biting him in the thigh threw him down, and within three days he died.
15.1 Those men then acted thus against all, but in Rome bishops coming
together, about fifty, did not receive Eusebius and his fellows as suspicious
and as having feared to come, but they also annulled the things written by
them, and they received us and 15.2 loved the communion toward us. And while
these things were happening, there came to the knowledge of the Emperor
Constans both the council that took place in Rome and the things that had
happened in Alexandria and all the East against the churches, and he writes to
his brother Constantius and then it pleases both that a council take place and
the affairs be examined, so that those who had been wronged might no longer
suffer, and those who do wrong might no longer be able to dare such things.
15.3 There come together then from both the East and the West in the city of
the Serdi more or less 170 in number of bishops. And those from the West were
bishops only, having Hosius as father, but those from the East brought with
them as tutors and advocates the count Musonianus and Hesychius the
castrensian, because of whom they also came eagerly, thinking that with their
power everything would again be done. For thus always through these they
showed themselves fearful to whom they wished and plotted against whom it 15.4
seemed good to them. But when, having met, they saw that only an
ecclesiastical trial was taking place, when they saw the accusers from each
church and city and the proofs against them, when they saw Arius and Asterius,
honorable bishops, who had come up with them, but had sprung away from them
and come with us and were relating their knavery, that they were suspicious in
the affairs and feared that a judgment take place, lest they be convicted by
us as slanderers, and by those whom they fabricated


as accusers, that they themselves had suggested all things and contrived such
things. Seeing these things, although they had come with eagerness, thinking
that we would not even meet as being afraid, when they saw 15.5 also our
eagerness, they shut themselves in the palace; for they were lodging there.
And then they talked with one another: "We came for other things and we see
other things; we met with counts and without counts the judgment takes place;
we are condemned altogether. You all know the orders; Athanasius and his
fellows have the records in the Mareotis, from which he himself is cleared,
and we are put to shame. Why then do we delay? Why do we linger? Let us
fabricate pretenses and go away, lest remaining we be condemned. It is better
blushing as we flee than, being convicted as slanderers, to be condemned. If
we flee, we can somehow still stand for the heresy; and if they condemn us
even as we flee, yet we have an Emperor as patron who does not allow us to be
cast out by the peoples from the churches." 16.1 Such things then those men
reasoned; but Hosius and all the other bishops more frequently indicated to
them the eagerness of Athanasius and his fellows and that "they are ready for
a defense, promising to convict you as slanderers," and they said "if you fear
16.2 the judgment, why did you meet? For you ought either not to have come or,
having come, not to flee." Those men hearing these things and being the more
terrified, using another pretense more unseemly than that at Antioch, as if
the Emperor had written them victory-news against the Persians, they attempted
to flee. And not being ashamed of this pretense they sent it through
Eustathius a presbyter 16.3 of the church at Sardica. But not even thus did
their flight turn out as they wished; for immediately the holy council, of
which the great Hosius was spokesman, wrote to them openly: "either come to
defend yourselves against the accusations brought against you and the slanders
you have worked, or know that the council condemns you as liable, and declares
Athanasius and his fellows free and clean from every charge." Those men then
were driven more by fear of conscience than they obeyed the letters; for
seeing those who had been wronged by them they did not even turn to those who
were speaking, but they fled more sharply. 17.1 Of those men then the flight
took place thus shamefully and unseemly, but the holy council gathered from 35
and more provinces, recognizing the malice of the Arians, received Athanasius
and his fellows for a defense concerning the things they had suffered and
those men had accused, 17.2 and thus, when they had made their defense, the
things which we have said in what is before, it received them and greatly
marveled, so as to love both them and their communion and to write everywhere
and to write also into each one's parish and especially into Alexandria and
Egypt and into the Libyas, that Athanasius and those with him are clean and
outside every blame, but that those who became slanderers against them happen
to be evildoers and anything rather 17.3 than Christians. They dismissed them
then with peace, but they deposed Stephanus and Menophantus, Acacius and
George who is in Laodicea, Ursacius and Valens and Theodorus and Narcissus.
For Gregory who had been sent from the Emperor to Alexandria they so denounced
as not even having become a bishop at all nor owing to be named a Christian.
They then also annulled the appointments he seemed to make, ordering that they
not even be named at all in churches because of the novelty of the
lawlessness. 18.1 Thus then Athanasius and those with him were dismissed with
peace, and the letters have been written at the end because of the length of
the epistle, and the council is dissolved, but those who had been deposed,
though they ought even thus to have been quiet, yet even after the so shameful
flight, going down, 18.2 they did such things that from these their former


deeds were shown to be small. For since those in Hadrianople did not want to
communicate with them as having fled from the council and become liable, they
reported to the Emperor Constantius and caused ten of the laity from the
Fabrica so called there to have their heads cut off, Philagrius again serving
them even for this, having become count there. And the memorials of these are
before the city, which we too have seen as we passed by. Then as if they had
succeeded, because they fled for this, that they might not be convicted as
slanderers, the things they wanted, these the Emperor also 18.3 commanded.
They caused then also two presbyters and three deacons to be banished from
Alexandria into Armenia. And Arius and Asterius, the one from Petra of
Palestine, the other from Arabia, bishops who had sprung away from them, they
not only banished into upper Libya, but they also caused them to have a share
of insults, 19.1 but Lucius the bishop of Hadrianople, since they saw him
using much frankness against them and exposing their impiety, they caused
again, as also before, to have his neck and hands bound with iron chains and
thus they banished him; 19.2 where he also died, as those men know. And
Diodorus a bishop they remove, but Olympius from Aenus and Theodulus from
Trajanopolis, both bishops from Thrace, good and orthodox men, since they saw
them hating the heresy, they slandered, first Eusebius and his fellows and the
Emperor Constantius wrote, and the second time these men reminded. And the
things written were not only that they be cast out of the cities and the
churches, but also that they pay a capital penalty, if they should be found
anywhere. 19.3 And this, even if marvelous, is not alien to their purpose, for
as having learned such a thing from Eusebius and his fellows and as being
heirs of their impiety and purpose they wanted, as their fathers in Thrace, so
these too in Alexandria to show themselves fearful, and they cause it to be
written that the harbors and the entrances of the cities be watched, lest
perhaps because of the permission from the 19.4 council they return into the
churches. And they cause it to be written also to the magistrates in
Alexandria concerning Athanasius and certain presbyters by name, so that,
whether a bishop or anyone of those men should be found setting foot on the
city or its boundaries, it should be permitted to the magistrate to cut off
the heads of those found. Thus the new Jewish heresy not only denies the Lord,
but has also learned to murder. 20.1 And not even thus were they quiet, but as
the father of their heresy "goes about as a lion seeking whom he may swallow,"
so these, having received the power of the public post and going about,
whomsoever they found reproaching them for the flight and hating the Arian
heresy, these they scourged, they bound, they caused to be banished from their
own country, and they made themselves so fearful that they made many
hypocrites, and many 20.2 flee into deserts or not wish even at all to meet
with these men. Such were the daring deeds of their madness after the flight.
For they also do another new thing, which is suitable to their heresy, but has
not been heard before, nor perhaps will it ever happen even among the more
wanton of the Greeks, much less among Christians. For when the holy council
had sent as ambassadors bishops, Vincentius from Capua (and this is the
metropolis of Campania), and Euphrates from Agrippina (and this too is the
metropolis of upper Gaul), so that, as the council judged, the Emperor might
permit the bishops to return into the churches, since he himself also had cast
them out, and the most pious Constans also writing to his own brother and
introducing the 20.3 bishops. The


marvelous men and daring toward all things, when they saw them at Antioch,
take counsel in common, but Stephanus alone undertakes the drama as being
convenient for such things. They hire then a public harlot in the very days of
the most holy Pascha and, having stripped her, they let her go by night upon
Euphrates the 20.4 bishop. And the harlot at first, thinking the one who
called her to be a younger man, followed eagerly, but when, thrown in by them,
she saw the man sleeping and not knowing what was happening, then as she
perceived and saw an old man's face and a bishop's condition, immediately
crying out she shouted the violence. And those men thought it right that she
be silent and 20.5 speak falsely against the bishop. Day then having come the
affair was noised about and the whole city ran together, and those from the
palace were moved, marveling at what was noised and thinking it right that
this not be silenced. A judgment then took place, and the keeper of companions
exposed those who came for the harlot, and those men exposed Stephanus; for
they were his clerics. Stephanus then is deposed, and in his place Leontius
the eunuch is made, so that only a patron not be lacking to the Arian heresy.
21.1 But the Emperor Constantius, being pricked a little, came to himself and
reasoning from what they did to Euphrates that the attempts against the others
also are such, he immediately orders the presbyters and deacons banished from
Alexandria into Armenia to be released, and he writes to Alexandria openly
that the clergy and laity with Athanasius 21.2 no longer be persecuted. Then
after about ten months, Gregory having died, he sends for Athanasius also with
all honor, writing not once nor a second time, but even a third, friendly
things to him, through which he urged him to take courage and come. And he
sends a presbyter and a deacon, so that taking courage still more he might
return. For he thought 21.3 that because of fear of the things that happened
before I was making light of the return. And he writes also to his own brother
Constans, so that he too might urge me to return. For he affirmed that he was
waiting a whole year for Athanasius and would never allow any innovation to
take place or even an appointment, keeping the churches for Athanasius the
bishop. 22.1 When he had thus written then and urged through many (for he also
caused his counts to write, Polemius, Datianus, Bardio, Thalassus, Taurus, and
Florentius, whom it was even more possible to trust), and having given the
whole to God who also pricked Constantius to this, Athanasius came with those
with him to him, and he saw him genuinely and dismissed him to enter into the
country and the churches, writing also to the magistrates in each place, since
he had before ordered that the 22.2 passages be watched, so that they might
have the way unhindered. Then when the bishop lamented concerning the things
he had suffered and concerning the things written against him by him, and set
forth that after the departure slanders not again take place from the enemies,
and said: "call these men, if you wish, (for it is possible that they stand
because of us) and we convict them," this he did not do, but all things, as
many as had before been written against him from slander, he orders to be
destroyed and wiped out, affirming: "no longer for the rest to endure

catholic church in Alexandria, which is under Athanasius the most reverend
bishop, publicly testify through those who subscribe next. 81.2 Already we
have testified concerning the night attack which we and the Lord's house have
suffered, even if there was no need of a testimony, upon the things which the
whole city both has known and knows. For the bodies found of those killed were
set forth publicly and the arms and bows in the Lord's house 81.3 have cried
the lawlessness. But since even after the testimony the most illustrious duke
Syrianus forces all to agree with him, as if neither a disturbance had taken
place nor anyone had died, and this is no small proof that these things have
not taken place according to the 81.4 mind of the most philanthropic Augustus
Constantius. For he would not have feared at the things that thus happened, if
he had done these things from an order. For even when we went away to him and
thought it right that he do violence to no one nor deny the things that
happened he ordered us being Christians to be cut down with clubs, showing
even from these the war that took place by night 81.5 against the church. For
this reason even now we testify these things, some of us already also being
about to go abroad to the most pious Augustus. And we adjure by the almighty
God on behalf of the salvation of the most pious Augustus Constantius both the
prefect of Egypt Maximus and the curiosi to report all things to the piety of
the Augustus and the power of the most illustrious prefects. And we adjure
also all the shipmasters to proclaim these things everywhere and to report
them into the hearing of the most pious Augustus and into the prefects and
into the magistrates in each place, so that the war that took place against
the church may be known, and that in the times of the Augustus Constan 81.6
tius Syrianus caused virgins and many others to become martyrs. For as it was
dawning on the fifth before the Ides of February, that is the 14th of the
month Mecheir, when we were keeping vigil in the Lord's house and occupying
ourselves with the prayers (for an assembly was about to take place on the
Preparation), suddenly about midnight the most illustrious duke Syrianus
attacked us and the church with many legions of soldiers having arms and bared
swords and arrows and other warlike implements and the helmets upon the heads.
And while we were truly praying and a reading taking place they broke the
doors, and when by the violence of the multitude the doors were opened, he
ordered. And some shot arrows, and others raised the war-cry 81.7 and a crash
of the arms took place and the swords flashed back with the light from the
lamps. And thereafter the things from these, virgins were killed and many were
trampled and they fell upon one another
