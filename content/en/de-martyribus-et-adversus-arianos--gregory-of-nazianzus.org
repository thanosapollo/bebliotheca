ORATION XXXV.
On the Martyrs and Against the Arians.
A. What can one say that is as great as what the eyes are given to see? What
word is of equal weight with the goods that appear? Behold, set before our
eyes is the spectacle we scarcely believed, which we often prayed to see, and
the thing longed for has proved greater than prayer. Again here are the honors
of the martyrs, long neglected in the time before this; again the gatherings
of the priests of God; again choirs and spiritual festivals. The assembly, a
multitude of people, is eager to keep feast, not to fight in arms. O the
wonder: the weapons have been cast from the hands, the battle line is
dissolved, the war is left unheeded. No longer are the voices of those who
raise the war-cry heard; instead of these, feasts and gladness and peaceful
joys dance round the whole city, the city that of old was mother of the
martyrs, yet for a long time in between was left without a share in the honor
due her children. But now she has all things and abounds, as the Apostle says.
Well done, O martyrs: this contest too is yours; you have conquered the long
war, I know it well. These also are the achievements of your sweat alone. You
have raised the trophy for peace. You have drawn the priests of God to
yourselves. You have set the dancers of the Holy Spirit at the head of this
assembly. O how great a loss they have suffered, those whose span of life did
not last until this spectacle, that after coming to satiety of grim things
they might enjoy the goods of peace!
B. The deceit of the heresy has gone and vanished, like a mist scattered by
the Holy Spirit; and the clear sky of peace has shone out, and along it appear
the stars of the city, gleaming in the bright light of truth, not allotted to
night and darkness, but all of them visible by day, having shone out with the
true light of righteousness. And since, as the Apostle says, the night is far
spent, or rather has vanished altogether, and everything is made bright with
the light of day, the night-wandering beasts flee and are driven together
toward the thickets and the caves; and heretical bats flee, squeaking,
dim-sighted toward the light of truth, and they sink into the holes of the
rocks, clinging to one another head downward. =36.260= By this day the bands
of revelers and of drunkards have been stopped. Clothes-snatchers,
housebreakers, thieves, and whatever else is a work of night, have been hidden
now that the light of peace has risen. But while that night was darkening all
things with the gloom of deceit, what sort of deeds were done? Things worthy
of forgetfulness and of a deep silence, so that the present grace might not be
stained by the memory of what is more offensive. For how could one speak the
evils done against it? And how could one keep silent? What suffering from
tragedy is of this kind? What myth, inventing disasters? What poet of dramas
has cried out such a passion upon the stage? The disaster is beyond speech;
the sufferings are beyond the power of those who tell them.
C. The chorus was a base of operations for the devil. Here he set his camp for
himself, and here he stationed his own shield-bearers. Here was the army of
the lie, the champions of deceit, the expedition of the demons, the legions of
unclean spirits; and if one must use names from outside as well, from here the
wicked army of demonic Furies revelled against the Church. For I am led to
name in this way the women who, beyond nature, played the man toward evil.
There was one Jezebel in the days of Elijah, murderous against the prophets of
the Lord, and the God-inspired history pillories her, so that, I think, by the
memory of that unbridled woman all later life might be instructed. So many,
then, after the manner of that Jezebel, sprang up all at once from the land,
like some hemlock, outdoing the one remembered by the excess of their
bitterness. And if you distrust the word, look at the history. She bestowed
the vineyard of Naboth on weak-willed Ahab, that he might make the vineyard a
garden, an ornament of luxury, a woman's finery; these women forced the living
vineyard of God, I mean the Church, to be turned over into utter
disappearance, working the evil through themselves. What example shall I find
for the speech? What image of this wickedness shall I invent? I have seen such
a painting on a plastered wall; and bear with my heart as it swells at the
memory of the evils; or rather, share the pain with me as well; for we tell
sufferings that are ours, and not another's. What then is the painting that I
liken to the facts? It was a dance of women behaving unseemly, each twisting
herself into some other dancing figure. The myths call such women Maenads.
Hair tossed up by the breezes, a look of passing madness, torches carried in
the hands, and flames from these winding round with the spinning of the
bodies; a breeze in the folds ruining the seemliness of the robes; =36.261=
feet tiptoeing and leaping up; decent shame touching none of what was done.
D. And through the midst of such a dance, an image of a man of this kind: some
mixed-female thing, ambiguous in nature, and womanish in form, lying on the
border of nature, loosened in posture, as if carried away by some revel and
drunkenness. This one, lying on his back upon a wagon, was borne through the
chorus of Maenads by beasts. And much unmixed wine from a mixing-bowl was
about him, and certain talkers, strange in nature, were around the image,
shaggy in the face, skipping about the image on goat-legs. Such are the tales
of that night. Women known for the dishonor of their kind, having danced away
by a common accusation against nature the seemliness customary among women,
led a procession through the midst of the city, making a spectacle through
themselves of nature behaving unseemly, arming their hands with stones instead
of shields, looking murder, with a shameless cast of the eyes, and having come
within the divine precincts they set their own Corybant upon the sacred seat.
Then drunkenness, and unmixed wine, and the Pans from the ascetic houses, and
night, and mixed-up living, and all that the Apostle forbids us to speak,
having said that the things done by them in secret are shameful even to
mention. For who could go through in speech with exactness the fire, the
stones, the slaughters, the wounds? How they strove to overturn from the
foundations the ministers of the sacred houses? How through the midst of the
city they handled with clubs a certain zealot of the truth, and the suspected
murder prevented the true murder? But I do not know how the speech has drifted
from the fitting pleasure, lingering in earthly tales. So we must go back to
the place from which we departed, so as to wash out that briny hearing with
speech that is sweet and drinkable. For in a day of gladness, it says, there
is a forgetting of evils; so I return again to the same word, that that night
and the gloom have vanished, and the rays of peace shine round through the
clear sky with the light of truth. Therefore everything foul and profane has
been cast out of the divine precincts; and in their place the houses of the
pious have been brought in, full of every gladness, and nothing is lacking for
gladness: the guests, the hosts, the table filled with good things to eat.
