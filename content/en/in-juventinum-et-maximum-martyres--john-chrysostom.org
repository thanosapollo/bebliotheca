* On Juventinus and Maximus the Martyrs
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

A panegyric homily of the same on the holy martyrs Juventinus and Maximinus,
who were martyred under Julian the Apostate.
=50.571=
1. The blessed Babylas recently gathered us here together with three children;
today a holy pair of soldiers has set the camp of Christ in battle array; then
a chariot of martyrs, now a pair of martyrs. Different is the time of life,
but one is the faith; varied are the wrestling-bouts, but the same is the
courage; those were ancient in time; these are new and recent. Such is the
treasure of the Church, having pearls both new and old; but one is the beauty
of them all. Their bloom is not dimmed, it does not fall away with time; the
nature of this brightness does not admit the rust of old age; for bodily
wealth yields and gives way to the length of time; for garments are worn out,
and houses are pulled down, and gold is rusted, and the whole nature of this
sensible wealth falls apart with time and vanishes. But with spiritual
treasures it is not so; but always and continually the martyrs remain in equal
vigor =50.572= and youth, shining and flashing forth the glory of their own
brightness.
Which you also knowing, do not honor the ancient in one way and the new in
another; but with the same eagerness, with the same affection, with the same
disposition, you honor and welcome them all. For you do not examine time, but
you seek out courage, and piety of soul, and unwavering faith, and a winged
and warm zeal, and such as those who have gathered us today displayed. For
they so boiled with longing toward God, that they were able without a
persecution to bind on the crown of martyrdom, without a battle to set up a
trophy, without a war to carry off a victory, without an engagement to snatch
the prize; and how, I will tell; but bear with me a little as I narrate from
the beginning. =50.573= There was a certain emperor in our generation, who
surpassed all those before him in impiety, concerning whom I also spoke to you
recently; this emperor, seeing our affairs becoming brilliant from this, I
mean from the death of the martyrs, and not men only, but also tender
children, and virgins unacquainted with marriage, and simply every age and
either sex leaping toward the slaughters for piety, was stung and pained,
and did not wish to sound the trumpet of war openly. For all, he says, will
come flying to martyrdom as bees to the honeycomb; and these things he learned
not from another, but from his own ancestors. For both tyrants warred against
the Church, and peoples continually rose up, because the spark of piety was
still small; but nevertheless they did not quench it, they did not destroy it,
but they themselves were destroyed; and that spark, increasing, was raised on
high, and seized the whole inhabited world, while all the faithful were being
slaughtered, burned, thrown from cliffs, drowned, given over to wild beasts.
For they trampled the coals as mud, and they looked on the seas and the waves
as meadows, and they ran to the sword as to a diadem and a crown, and they put
every kind of punishment to shame not only by bearing it nobly, but also with
pleasure and much eagerness.
For just as plants are naturally increased when watered, so also our faith,
when warred against, blooms the more, and when harassed it abounds, and the
watering of waters is not accustomed to make gardens so flourishing as the
blood of the martyrs is naturally able to water the Churches. Knowing all
these things then and more than these, that man feared to set in motion openly
the war against us: lest, he says, we should cause them to set up trophy after
trophy, and work continual victories, and bind on crowns. And what does he do?
Mark the villainy: he commanded all physicians, and soldiers, and sophists,
and rhetors to withdraw from their professions, or to abjure the faith; thus
skirmishing against us, so that if they should yield, the defeat might become
ridiculous, because they did not prefer piety to money; but if they should
stand nobly and prevail, the victory might not be altogether brilliant, nor
the trophy conspicuous; for it is no great thing to despise a craft and a
profession for the sake of piety. And he does not even stop only there; but
also if there was anyone in the former times, when the emperors were pious,
who had either thrown down altars, or razed temples, or taken offerings, or
done anything else of this sort, he was dragged into court, and was
slaughtered, not only the one who had done something of this sort, but also
the one who was simply accused. And he also fabricated countless other and
manifold pretexts, so as to cut down all those living in piety.
And he did all these things, wishing to dim the crown of martyrdom; so that
the murder might go forward for him, and slaughters might take place, but the
prizes of the martyrs might not appear brilliant. But nothing more came of it
for him. For it is not from his vote, nor from his villainy at all that those
who suffered these things are going to receive the crown, but from the
unbribed judgment, I mean the one from above. =50.574=
2. Therefore, while affairs were in this state, and that man was in travail to
bring forth the war, and fearing the defeat, it happened that there was a
soldiers' banquet, of which the martyrs who have gathered us today partook;
and as is likely at a banquet, many speeches being put forward, and some
conversing of one thing and others of another, these men were lamenting the
present evils, and counting the former time blessed, and they said to
themselves and to those present: Is it still at all worth living? or
breathing, or seeing this sun, when the sacred laws are being trampled, piety
is being insulted, the common Master of creation is being dishonored?
Everything is full of the smell of fat and of smoke, everything of unclean
sacrifices, we cannot even breathe clean air. But do not simply run past what
has been said; but consider the time at which these things were spoken; and
mark the piety of those who spoke. For if at a soldiers' banquet, where there
is unmixed wine and drunkenness and a contest of extreme dissipation, and a
struggle of drunken outrage and frenzy, they so groaned, they so lamented,
what were they when they were at home, and associating with one another alone?
what were they in their prayers, who at the very time of luxury were so
restrained, and displaying apostolic compassion? Others were falling, and
these were lamenting; another was acting impiously, and these were being
burned; they did not even perceive their own health, because of the sickness
of the brethren; but as if given as common patrons of the inhabited world,
they so suffered and wept over the evils that were taking place. Having said
these things they did not escape notice; but a certain one of those sharing
that table, a dissembler and a flatterer, wishing to court the emperor,
reported all that had been said to him; and he, having found what he had long
been seeking, and laying hold of a pretext able to deprive them of the rewards
of martyrdom, commands that their whole property be confiscated, charging
them with tyranny on account of these words, and he ordered them to be led
naked into the prison. But they rejoiced and were glad.
For what is wealth to us, they say, and a costly garment? for even if it
should be necessary to strip off the last garment, the flesh, for Christ's
sake, we will not cling even to this, but we will yield even it. And there
were seals then upon the houses, and all that was theirs was being plundered.
And just as men about to travel abroad to a fatherland standing far off often
convert the house into money and send it ahead; so indeed it happened also in
their case. Since they were about to travel abroad to heaven, their property
was being sent ahead, their very enemies serving for the escort. For not only
the money given according to the account of almsgiving is transferred to
heaven; but also whatever those who war against the faith and persecute those
living in piety may plunder, these things too are stored up there. For that
this is not less than that, hear what Paul says: And you accepted the plunder
of your goods with joy, expecting to have a better and remaining possession in
the heavens. The martyrs then were in the prison, and the whole city was
streaming together there; although great fears and threats and dangers were
hanging over, if anyone should approach, and converse and share speech with
them. But the fear of God thrust all those things aside, and through them many
then became martyrs, despising the present life for the sake of association
with them, and as many ran together, continual psalmodies =50.575= were
performed, holy all-night vigils, gatherings full of spiritual teaching, and
when the church had been shut, the prison then became a church. For not only
those coming in from outside, but also those spending time within, received a
very great teaching of virtue and self-control from the endurance and the
faith of these saints.
Hearing these things the emperor was pained more, and wishing to trip them up,
and to slacken that eagerness, he sent down certain polluted men and impostors
for the plot; who sitting with them often, if ever they should catch them
alone, as counseling from themselves, but not as sent from the emperor, were
advising them to stand off from piety, and to desert to impiety. For not only
will you thus beat off the danger that lies upon you; but you will also lay
hold of more and better honor of office, having thus dissolved the emperor's
wrath. Do you not see others also of those ranked with you who have done this
very thing? But they said, And for this very reason we will stand nobly, that
we may offer ourselves as victims for those fallen ones. We have a loving
Master; He knows even when He has received one sacrifice how to be reconciled
to the whole inhabited world. And just as the three children said: that There
is not in this time a ruler, and a prophet, and a leader, nor a whole burnt
offering, nor a sacrifice, nor a place to offer before you, and to find mercy;
but in a contrite soul and a spirit of humiliation may we be accepted; so
indeed also the martyrs, seeing the altars being razed, the churches being
shut, the priests being driven out, all the faithful being banished, were
eager to offer themselves to the Master in place of all; and leaving military
ranks, they were hastening to mix themselves with angelic choirs. For even if
we do not die now, he says, we shall die altogether a little later at any
rate, and we shall suffer this very thing; it is better to die for the King of
the angels, than for a man who is acting so impiously; it is better to take up
arms for the fatherland above, than for the one below and being trampled.
Here even if someone dies, he will receive nothing from an emperor worthy of
the eagerness. For what will a man still grant as a favor to the one who has
died? Often indeed, not even being thought worthy of burial, he will be laid
out for the mouths of dogs. But if we die for the King of the angels, we shall
receive back the bodies with greater glory, we shall return with much
brightness, and we shall carry off rewards and crowns far greater than the
labors. Let us take then spiritual arms; there is no need of darts and bows,
there is no need of any other sensible weapon; the tongue is enough for us for
all things, he says. For the mouths of the saints are a quiver, bringing
continual and successive blows upon the head of the devil.
3. These things and things like these were reported to the emperor; but he did
not stand off, but he attempted again to set the baits through those men.
These things at any rate the treacherous and knavish man, wise for doing evil,
was arranging, so that if the martyrs should be softened and give in, the
agents might lead them out under the eyes of all, and he might thus then
prepare them to sacrifice; but if they should persist, displaying intense
courage, and standing =50.576= nobly toward the wrestling-bout, the things of
the victory might not become manifest for them, but having led them away on a
pretext of tyranny he might behead them. But He who reveals deep and hidden
things did not allow the ambush to escape notice, nor the plot to become
unseen; but just as the Egyptian woman then, laying hold of Joseph in a
chamber and much solitude, expected to escape the notice of all men, but she
did not escape the unsleeping eye, and not only that, but not even men
afterward, but what she said to Joseph, no one being present, this is sung
everywhere in the inhabited world; so indeed this man also hoped to escape
notice, conversing in prison through those pretended counselors, but he did
not escape notice; but all afterward learned the ambush, the plot, the
victory, the trophy.
For as time was being worn away and much was flowing by, and the multitude of
days did not slacken the eagerness, but rather stretched their zeal, and was
making more zealots, he commands then that they be led away in the middle of
the nights to the pit. And in the midst of darkness the luminaries were being
led out, and they were beheaded. Then the heads were more terrible thereafter
to the devil than when they were sending forth a voice; since John's head also
was not so terrible while speaking, as voiceless lying upon the platter. For
the blood of the saints also has a voice, not heard through ears, but laying
hold of the conscience of those who slay. And after that blessed slaughter,
those who hunt such relics, having staked their own salvation, took up these
champions; and those too were living martyrs. For even if they were not
beheaded, yet having chosen first to suffer this, they thus rushed upon the
catch of the bodies. Those then who were present at that time and were thought
worthy to see these bodies newly slain say that, as they lay beside one
another before the burial, a certain grace bloomed upon their faces, such as
Luke says came to be upon Stephen when he was about to make his defense to the
Jews, and there is no one who stood by them then without a shudder. So this
sight also struck all who saw with amazement, and all cried out that word
spoken by David: In their life they were not divided, and in their death they
were not separated. For in common they both confessed and inhabited the
prison, and were carried away to the pit, and laid down their heads, and one
coffin has the bodies of both; just as also one tent will receive them again
in the heavens, when they are about to take them up with greater glory. These
it is right to address together as pillars, and watch-rocks, and towers, and
luminaries, and bulls.
For as pillars they hold up the Church, and as towers they wall it, and as
watch-rocks, beating off every plot, they make much calm for those within; and
as luminaries they drove away the darkness of impiety, and as bulls in soul
and body, with equal eagerness, they drew the good yoke of Christ. Let us then
continually resort to them, and touch the coffin, and with faith embrace their
relics, that we may draw some blessing from there. For just as soldiers,
having shown the wounds which they received from the enemies, converse with
the emperor with boldness; so also these, bearing on their hands the heads
which were cut off from them, and bringing them into the midst, can easily
accomplish all things, whatever they wish, with the King of the heavens. With
much faith then, with much eagerness, let us go there =50.577=, that both from
the sight of these holy tombs, and from the thought of the contests, and from
every side having taken many and great treasures, we may both be able to
accomplish the present life according to what seems good to God, and with much
merchandise sail into that =50.578= harbor, and obtain the kingdom of the
heavens, by the grace and loving-kindness of our Lord Jesus Christ, with whom
to the Father, together with the Holy Spirit, be glory, power, honor and
worship, unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
