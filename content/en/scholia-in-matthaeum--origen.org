ORIGEN ON THE GOSPEL ACCORDING TO MATTHEW. =17.289= CHAP. I. Verse 18. "Now
the birth of Jesus was thus." ....... Here the evangelist makes mention of
birth, but at the beginning of the Gospel, of generation. For here he says:
"Now the birth of Jesus Christ was thus," but there, "Book of the generation
of Jesus Christ." What then is the difference of birth and of generation? and
how are both taken concerning Christ? Just as this spoken word according to
its own nature is intangible and invisible; but when it is written in a book,
and as it were embodied, then it is both seen and handled; so also the
fleshless Word of God and bodiless, neither seen nor written according to the
godhead, when he was incarnate, is both seen and written. Therefore, as of one
who has been incarnate, there is also a book of his generation. But it has not
been asked here why he said book, and did not say vision, or word; but what
birth is, and what generation is. For generation and birth differ. For
generation is the first fashioning from God; but birth is the succession from
one another out of the condemnation of death through the transgression. And
further, generation has the incorruptible and sinless; but birth the passible
and able to sin. The Lord therefore, having taken by nature the sinless of
generation, did not take on the incorruptible; and having taken the passible
of birth, did not take on the able to sin, bearing the first Adam undiminished
in both parts. Therefore in the case of Christ generation would be, not a
progress from not-being into being, but a way from existing in the form of God
to taking the form of a servant; but his birth has become twofold, according
to us and above us. For to be born of a woman is according to us; but not of
the will of flesh, or of a man, but of the Holy Spirit, above us. Which also
was announcing beforehand the =17.292= future birth, which he was going to
grant us from the Spirit. CHAP. VII. Verse 5. "Do not give the holy to the
dogs, nor cast your pearls before swine" ...... Verse ʹ. "Ask, and it shall be
given you; seek, and you shall find; knock, and it shall be opened to you."
Lest the disciples be ignorant who are dogs, or swine among the hearers, seek
persistently, he says, from God, and all things are revealed to you. For the
beginning of virtue is to know the good through prayer; then to know how one
must travel the virtue; third, to knock through the works that the door be
opened to him. For this is what the knocking through the hands signifies. For
the truth of the saving doctrines is taken in three ways, by careful prayer,
and by the settling of the ruling faculty in seeking the divine things, and
concerning the finding, with perception, of the depth of the security of the
hidden Scriptures, by precise inquiry; for thus he says the Scriptures are
opened. Verse 9. "What man is there of you, whom if his son ask bread, will he
give him a stone?" Verse 10. "And if he ask a fish, will he give him a
serpent?" See whether ...... knowledge is food, above which it is not possible
to be saved; for example the precise account concerning how one must eat. And
a fish is also as it were a relish for the lover of learning, to know the
constitution of the world, the working of the elements, beginning and end and
middle, and whatever wisdom says next. Neither instead of bread does God give
a stone, which the devil wished to be eaten by Jesus; nor instead of a fish a
serpent, which the Ethiopians eat who are unworthy to eat fish. And how did he
call the disciples evil? For even if they followed from choice, yet they had
the changeable of the human. And only the Son of God was unchangeable both in
godhead and in manhood. And he calls good gifts the spiritual gifts. But Luke
said Holy Spirit, instead of saying good things. And there is no difference.
For the Holy Spirit himself is the good light; wherefore also to those who
partake


of him he gives good things. But to those who are exceedingly evil, because
they sin unyieldingly, wickedness becomes in a way nature to them; such is
Satan and the demons. For one can also call these evil by nature, although
they were not created evil by nature, but have grown hard from self-chosen
wickedness. CHAP. VIII. Verse 11. "Many shall come from east and west, and
shall recline with Abraham and Isaac and Jacob in the kingdom of the heavens."
If the way is narrow and straitened, and few are they who find it, through
what will the many enter? For concerning the broad and spacious he says that
many will enter through it. And we say, =17.293= that the word of the Savior
has been spoken, since few from Israel were being saved; for not many believed
in him. Wherefore he also said: "Fear not, little flock, for God has been well
pleased in you." But prophetically was said, "Many shall come"; as concerning
those who would believe in him from the nations. Many, he says, as compared
with those who after the sojourn did not believe, those from the nations who
will recline with Abraham and those from him in the kingdom of the heavens;
and in number too these are many times more than those from Israel who also
confess Jesus Christ. CHAP. XI. Verse 11. "But the lesser in the kingdom of
the heavens is greater than he." Verse 12. "And from the days of John the
Baptist until now the kingdom of the heavens is forced, and the violent snatch
it." We say that the lesser speaks of Christ himself; lesser according to the
seeming of those who hear. And "in the kingdom of the heavens" shows that in
the spiritual things, and in all the things according to heaven and the
godlike, Christ is greater than that one. And we shall read it thus: "Lesser";
then, leaving a little interval, we shall bring on, "In the kingdom of the
heavens he is greater than he." And the kingdom of the heavens is Christ
himself, urging all to repentance, and drawing them to himself through grace.
CHAP. XII. Verse 14. "And the Pharisees, having gone out, took counsel against
him, how they might destroy him." As much as one draws near to Jesus, he does
not take counsel. For the counsel of the wicked does not draw near to Jesus.
But when someone comes outside of Jesus, he takes counsel to destroy the
light, the good way, the life, the treasure, him who witnessed the love, the
peace. If one destroy this one, he is called son of destruction. Verse 15.
"But Jesus, knowing, withdrew." For he ought not to remain with the sons of
destruction. Those destroyed; we, not having sought, found. "For I was found,"
he says, "by those who did not seek me; I became manifest to those who did not
ask for me." For he himself came upon the lost sheep; but they forgot their
own shepherd. He withdraws, not fearing the plot, but checking the wickedness;
and lest anyone suppose that he withdrew through cowardice, he therefore
healed all, showing his all-powerful might. Yet he charges that it not be
published, as being gentle and without vainglory. CHAP. XIII. Verse 44. "The
kingdom of the heavens is like a treasure," etc. And according to another
interpretation it would be, the field the =17.296= Scripture, planted with
many legal and scriptural and historical sayings; and the treasure the various
thoughts present in these, both manifest and hidden; and Christ the precious
treasure of the Scripture. Or the field is Christ, in whom Paul says the
hidden treasures of the wisdom and of the knowledge of God have been hidden.
The heavenly things therefore, and the kingdom of the heavens, have been
written as in an image in the Scriptures, which are a kingdom of the heavens;
and it is Christ himself, whose knowledge is in us according to power; and it
is found through faith, and bought through the virtues. Having found then this
treasure in the Scriptures, one hides it, knowing it is not without danger to
impart the unspeakable things to chance persons. And having hidden it he goes
away, to buy the field for as much as he may wish; which the Hebrews formerly
were entrusted with, from whom he who takes this, which according to another
parable is a vineyard, makes it his own possession, all


having sold his belongings; and these were wickedness, by no longer living to
these, but having died to the whole world and to the wills of it. Those at
least who have written about pearls say there are six differences of them, of
which the better are those that come to be in India in shells from the
heavenly dew. The lesser pearls then are the inquiries of the law and of the
prophets and of the rest of Scripture, which he who seeks laboriously, selling
his merchandise, seeks the pearl better than all these, Christ, counting all
these even present good things as loss and dung toward the knowledge of
Christ. Further a net is the weaving from the old words of Scripture, which
seeming incomplete Christ having come has perfected. This, being cast into the
sea of this life, gathers not of different natures, but of self-determined
judgments and choices; so that that which is gathered from every kind is the
praiseworthy and the blameworthy in their inclinations toward the species of
the virtues or of the vices. And besides this, that which is gathered from
every kind can show the calling from every race. But the angels would not draw
the net upon the shore, that is the things outside this life, unless it were
filled with the fullness of the nations; but when it is filled they bring it
up, and each of the good things under the net they will restore to its own
order according to what are here named their vessels, and the rotten they will
cast outside. And the outside is the furnace of fire, as the Savior says in
what follows; for the worthless of the fishes are thrown outside, but those of
men into the furnace of fire. Verse 47. "Again: The kingdom of the heavens is
like a net cast into the sea." The intelligible weaving of a net is the old
and new Scripture from various thoughts; Sea, the wave-tossed life of men. The
whole race the varied =17.297= of the choices among men, the calling of
nations of every kind. Workers of the net, the Lord and the angels who have
come to minister to him. Vessels the different mansions. And the net is not
brought up, until the fullness of the nations enter. Or thus: A net is the
apostolic teaching, which indiscriminately takes Jews and Greeks, barbarians,
Scythians, slaves, free, wicked and good. Sea, the life of men. Shore, the end
of this. And in the consummation, when the net is drawn up, the good are
separated from the worthless, the one being honored, the other punished. For
not of all the faithful is the manner like. And lest anyone suppose that the
unbelieving and wicked are only cast outside, he indicated that they are sent
away into continual punishment: "And they shall cast them into the furnace of
fire," and what follows. CHAP. XVIII. Verse 5. "And whoever receives one such
child upon my name, receives me." He exhorts to receive the faithful upon his
name, one such child, or one of such of the spiritual and those formed as the
Christ, according to the humbling of one's own spirit. And according to Luke,
the child shown by the Savior must be received in his name, in order that in
him one may receive the Christ who is also in the Spirit. Or one such, since
all the spiritual are one. And the word also urges the receiving of the simple
and meek in heart in the word of Christ. Cutting short the contentiousness of
the disciples (since when the teachers fight, schisms come to be, many being
scandalized, the scandalized wishing to draw the hearers to themselves), he
foretells the punishment with them. And this was said with intensity. That is,
even if you scandalize a cheap man of the faithful, the punishment against you
will be heavy. For through the millstone and the drowning he signifies the
unbearable punishment that will be with those who insult such. For scandal
here he calls the insult. And I say these things, since the little ones are
easily scandalized, that we may not despise them. For the great and perfect in
virtue despises every scandal. And from the things he foretells and laments
such, he gives them a season of repentance. And having said, "It is
necessary," he does not take away


the self-determining, but shows the foreknowing of what will altogether come
to be. Wherefore he also cries woe to them, as stumbling willingly. And he
calls punishment the men who live unseemly. Verse 20. "For where two or three
are joined into my name, there I am in the midst of them." Verse 21. "Then
Peter having come to him said: Lord, how often shall my brother sin against
me, and I forgive him? until seven times?" Two joined upon the name of Christ,
body =17.300= and spirit, not campaigning against one another; the body having
been subjected, and the spirit imparting to it divine life; and three, he
keeps complete, spirit, soul, body, for the presence of Christ; Or the two
covenants, when their gatherer the Holy Spirit is in them; and three, law,
prophets, Gospel; or the irascible and the appetitive, and reasoning; or the
calling and election, and the race ranked in first honor with us. And note
that he did not say I shall be, but I am. For as God being everywhere, he is
in the midst of those joined through piety. For the all-overseeing power of
God, being undivided, becomes divided to the worthy. Since of the faults
against us we ourselves are lords of forgiving, not of those that reach to God
unless God himself; therefore Peter said, "against me." For asking until when
there is need not to remember evil, as a great thing he added, "seven times."
But the Lord, signifying the infinite of forbearance, said, "seventy times
seven"; that is: As often as he sin against you, if he repent, forgive him.
Since upon those being shut out Christ set a measure, but not also upon those
repenting, reasonably Peter asks: Until how much must one receive the one who
repents? And see whether it is possible: Since the number six is working and
in the generation of the world, but seven of rest; he who loves the things of
the world and material things, sinning, has as end the hebdomad; which Peter
having understood, wishes to forgive in the seven. But the Lord stretches this
in the same manner, analogously of monad toward tens and hundreds, so that he
who has gone beyond the worldly things no longer has forgiveness of sins; for
the forgiveness is upon the things here, whether slowly or more quickly. CHAP.
XXI. Verse 33. "There was a man, a householder, who planted a vineyard, and
put a hedge around it, and dug a winepress in it, and built a tower, and gave
it out to farmers, and went abroad," etc. The parable calls householder God
and Father, who from the beginning showed much providence toward the Jews. And
vineyard simply the first people, that is Israel, planted by God in the land
of promise. And hedge the law, not letting them mix with the nations.
Winepress the altar, or the place of the drink-offerings. Tower Zion, or
heaven. Farmers the elders of the people and the wise, and the teachers
according to times. And the householder went abroad, when he no longer appears
to them by day in a pillar of cloud and by night in a pillar of fire. Or the
long-suffering of God, which does not on the spot =17.301= demand of the Jews
the punishment of the faults. And demand of fruits the lawful life; Their
season the prophetic demand; First and second servants who suffered what was
unseemly, the prophets sent at different times, of whom they beat one, as
Micah, Zedekiah having struck him on the cheek; and they killed one, as
Zechariah, between the temple and the altar; and they stoned one, as Zariou
son of Jodae the priest. And perhaps vineyard, or the kingdom of God, and his
mysteries in law and prophets, and the rest of natural philosophy. And fruits
the virtuous life in habits. Hedge the rational place, and the letter, so that
the hidden mysteries may not be surveyed by those outside. Winepress the depth
of that which receives the fruits of the soul, which casts everything upon
war. Tower also a divided word concerning God. Temple the of the divine temple
in it. Farmers the first who were entrusted with the oracles of God; to whom
he goes abroad into his own lookout, having given occasion, from the things he
himself planted, and set a hedge, dug, built, of bearing fruit. Season of
fruits, which the householder knows, perhaps not


that they put forth leaves, or bloom, or grow sour grapes, or be harvested;
such as those of infancy, or of the completion of the word, or those of
progress in virtue, or of beginning; and those of love, and joy, and peace,
and the rest. Servants the prophets demanding the spiritual fruitages to bring
them as priests to God. CHAP. XXIV. Verse 45. "Who then is the faithful
servant and prudent, whom his lord set over his service?" Perhaps urging to
seek some one rare. For by the doubt of "then" he showed the rarity of such a
steward, and knowing no other faithful and prudent steward than himself, being
set over the works of the Father, I mean over the manhood. For he speaks as
concerning another, overshadowing it by the excess of wisdom. And he called
himself servant because of taking the form of a servant. And see whether the
word does not put to shame bishops and teachers entrusted with such things, to
use prudently for each the things according to worth, and scientifically to
arrange the spiritual foods by season of quantity, of quality. And he said,
"Who then," not as being ignorant, but that they may not be over-curious. And
the word fits also according to everyone entrusted with something from God,
whether money, or rule, or some other power. And second the steward is
required faith and prudence, that he may neither steal the master's things,
but also manage them as he ought. Or him who has a good conscience toward God,
and does not stand off from any of the things that ought to be. And him who
teaches rightly he calls faithful. And prudent him who is not carried away by
the foolish-talkings of the heretics, but distinguishes the precise of the
word, or him who is not tried =17.304= by sins, because of the unvaried and
many-formed character of men. Him who knows when he must rebuke, or censure,
or exhort, or teach; and whom he must give milk to drink, and to whom to
impart the solid food. CHAP. XXV. Verse 1. "Then the kingdom of the heavens
shall be likened to ten virgins, who, having taken their lamps, went out to a
meeting of the bridegroom." They say the ten virgins are the senses in each
soul of the inner and outer man, having stood away from idolatry and worldly
desires, by receiving, whether in pretence or in truth, the pure word of God,
and having been made virgin. And there are both those commonly understood, and
those lying in proverbs, which also follow, as the virtues of the hypostasis
of Christ. Therefore also entopeiat'. not some foolish and some prudent. But
one and all went out to a meeting, those who received the mystery concerning
Christ. For they go out from the error of the world, hastening toward him who
readily rushes toward them. Another of Origen. Virginity is other than
almsgiving, and the mind is other than the body; and you will take this unto
the ten virgins. Prudent then are the senses of him who lives according to
reason; but foolish those of him who conducts himself contrariwise and beside
the right reason. Lamps the all-virtuous practice; and oil the word poured
upon the psychic, of him who works the good practice. They sleep who have
departed from the vital tension. The prudent therefore do not lose the lamps,
nor are they inattentive concerning the keeping of the oil. The angels cry out
to the sleeping senses, raising them; that is the ministering spirits sent for
service because of those who are about to inherit salvation. Adornment of
lamps is the evangelical use according to the senses; of eyes in wondering at
the maker, and closing that they may not see injustice; of hearing both not
receiving a vain thing, nor a judgment of blood, but of the equal word; of
taste perceiving Christ the Lord; of smell enjoying the fragrance of Christ,
and of the myrrh that was emptied; of touch touching the Word, as John says:
"Our hands handled concerning the Word of life." And the voice comes to be at
midnight; at that hour at which the sons of Israel went out from Egypt, in
which also Christ rose from the dead. A voice is the trumpet, which raises the
dead, and leads them out from the tombs. And "go out" also has a mystical
sense. For if we do not


go out, we cannot meet the bridegroom. Lamp of virginity the illumination of
knowledge; and oil into the lamps the zeal in recognition, and the working of
the commandments. For this preserves the light in us, =17.305= keeping the eye
simple, so that our whole body is illuminated, according to the word of the
Lord. Those then who took the careful zeal toward the working of the
commandments are prudent; but the careless foolish, having spent the fitting
season upon zeal for vain things. And "they slept" he says of the sleep of
death; and midnight shows the sudden and the unexpected, and at once the
fearful. And to those who have risen there is present what they toiled at; but
the slackness concerning piety, of those bringing nothing, the soul begins to
be gloomy, and as it were to be quenched, and to go into madness, so as to
suppose themselves through the virtue of others. Wherefore they are rejected,
those saying, "Lest there be not enough for us and you." And they sell the oil
who teach for hire of attendance, of attention, of love of learning, of love
of labor. Or the sellers are prophets, apostles, evangelists, through whom one
having been discipled works the virtue. CHAP. XXVII. Verse 11. "And the
governor asked him, saying: Are you the king of the Jews?" As having been
condemned upon a public charge Pilate asks him saying: "Are you the king of
the Jews?" He seeks, sneering, or doubting he thus asks. But Jesus quietly,
hinting the yes, as king of all men, said, "You say." Since the high priest
asked judicially saying: "Tell us if you are the king," but Pilate
declaratively. And see whether "You said," spoken to the high priest, does not
convict his hidden doubt; but "You say," to Pilate, confirms his declaration.
For he was king of Israel, neither having said yes, lest he give Pilate a
reasonable occasion to condemn. Because of their unchangeableness he does not
even answer them back. And well he did not even answer Pilate, because he
doubts whether he ought to defend himself toward the unjust accusations, and
because he is of two minds concerning them. For it was clear that, if he had
defended himself and shown himself liable to no charge, the judge would have
released him, having taken a way of declaring good things concerning him.
Whence also the governor wondered at him, how, being able to speak many
defenses, he was silent. Wherefore also as wishing to release him, he urged
him to this; for that he was delivered through envy was clear even to Pilate.
And he does not simply wonder, but even very much; perhaps because of his
unchangeableness and undisturbedness toward death, and in these things the
calm of the face; or he also wondered at his long-suffering beyond man. It is
likely, the Jews having lately become bound by treaty to the Romans, they
thought the favor to them was granted at the feast. And Saul also, having
granted Jonathan to the people, shows that such a thing was theirs even
before. And as thinking =17.308= that they would ask either the innocent, or
him liable to countless charges, Pilate said this, as being to just men. For
he knew that they had no clear proof against him. And having come upon very
old copies, I found even Barabbas himself called Jesus. At least the question
of Pilate stood thus there: "Whom do you wish that I release to you of the
two? Jesus Barabbas, or Jesus who is called Christ?" For as it seems, Barabbas
was a patronymic of the robber, which is interpreted son of a teacher. The
name of Baramban then being put together signifies son of our teacher. And of
what teacher then must one think the notable robber a son, if not of the man
of bloods, the man-slayer from the beginning, whom even until now those who
have learned from him to slay men rather choose, than him who makes men live,
our Lord Jesus Christ? Being judged by Pilate, he gave an oracle to this man's
wife, in order that by the silence she might be struck by the courage, and by
the oracle she might know that he judges not a man but God. And through this
he himself did not see, either that he might not be silent at the dream, or
because he was not believed, or because he was unworthy, or that having heard
what the woman suffered, he might sympathize. For she not


only saw the dream, but also suffered many things, and in the night toward its
becoming more fearful. And otherwise the dream is a work of Providence, not
being said what it was, but showing that it was not shown in order that Jesus
might not suffer, but in order that the woman might be saved. And blessed is
she who in dreams holds off from such suffering, that she may not over-suffer.
And if she is a symbol of the Church once ruled by Pilate, and no longer being
under his hand because of the faith toward Christ ....... for, for the woman
is of another race. Verse 45. "And from the sixth hour darkness came to be
upon all the earth until the ninth hour." Concerning this eclipse the prophet
Zechariah also having spoken: "In that day," he says, "there shall not be
light. And cold and frost shall be one day. And that day known to the Lord.
And not day, and not night; and toward evening there shall be light." And
having said the darkness the having been darkened of those who laid hands on
the light. For as to Egyptians darkness, and to the sons of Israel light; so
also now to the Church light, but to the Jews darkness of three days, of
paternal light, of the radiance of Christ, of the shining of the Holy Spirit.
And since on the sixth day man came to be, and it is likely that he also
slipped at the sixth hour; perhaps because of this he who suffers for the
salvation of men was hung at the sixth hour. This is the sign, which before
they asked he promised to give, saying: "An evil and adulterous generation
seeks a =17.309= sign," and what follows; and, "When you lift up the Son of
man then you shall know that I am." For it was much more wonderful that these
things came to be while he was nailed upon the cross than while walking upon
earth. And not in this only was the paradox, but that it also came to be from
heaven which they sought, and upon all the inhabited world, which had not
happened even before, but in Egypt only, when also the Passover was
accomplished; for those things too were a type of these. For lest they say the
thing that came to be was an eclipse, therefore it comes to be on the 14th,
when it is impossible for an eclipse to happen. And in mid day, in order that
all who dwell the earth may learn that everywhere of the inhabited world it
was day. And a certain Phlegon, a philosopher among the Greeks, mentions this
darkness, as having come to be paradoxically on the 14th of the moon; when an
eclipse is not naturally come to be, when the interval of the sun is far, as
much as from the beginning of heaven to the end. For then eclipses come to be,
when these two lights draw near to one another. For an eclipse of the sun
comes to be synodically, the moon having run under it, not at full moon, when
it is diametrical to the moon. And this came to be, as of the creation
mourning over what had come to be, and through this showing that, this outrage
having been done from Jews, their mind was darkened, and the intelligible sun
departed from them. For whether they thought he himself had done this, they
ought to have believed and feared; or if not he, but the Father, even from
this they ought to have been pricked. For that darkness was of one being
angry, and a proof of what the fighters against God and the blood-stained were
about to receive. For therefore this comes to be after all the outrage, when
satiety took hold of the mockeries, and they uttered all they wished. CHAP.
XXVIII. Verse 18. "There was given to me all authority in heaven and upon
earth." The, "There was given to me all authority," and what follows,
understand unto the inhumanation. And if also unto the godhead ...... to
behold, and thus you will take "There was given to me authority," instead of
"It was contained." There was given, it was brought to me, I was entrusted
concerning the self-determining. Life first had place in the things above,
then now also in the things below; whom being confident baptize into the name
of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. One is he who saves,
one the salvation. One is the living Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit. He
is one not by blending of the three, but by one substance; and three
hypostases perfect in all things, and corresponding. The Father begot
according to nature; wherefore he was begotten consubstantial. God is not a
body; therefore not flux or motion, or any of such things as are observed in
bodies, did God the bodiless beget. The begetting is hypostatic; the Son was
brought forth from the substance of the Father.
