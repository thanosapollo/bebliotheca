** Hymn on the Trinity
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1-h1
:END:

God is a threefold highest, a triple form, one force.
Wisdom is begotten from the Father's heart; the Son himself is he;
the Holy Spirit subsists from eternity from the mouth.
The Father is neither older in time nor greater in godhead,
for God, wise always from of old, brought forth from himself
the Word through which he always was, for the ages to be born;
but to bring forth the Word is the Father's, the rest is of the Word:
to bear the assumed man, to restore the slain,
to reconcile him to the Father, and place him in the seat at the right.
The Spirit of God completes these things, God himself: ready
to pour out his gift upon faithful peoples,
he transfers the power of both Father and Christ into bodies.

Preface

Is it the true sect? I consult you, Master:
do we keep the right faith?
or do we not beware the viperine doctrines,
and slip, not knowing?
It is hard to discern the narrow way of salvation
among the bent paths,
so many crossroads of the faithless rise
smoothed with brands of errors,

oblique forks join themselves
from this side and that with woven tracks.
If anyone wandering and roaming follows them,
leaving the right path,
he will fall headlong into the pit of a hidden trench
which a hostile hand has dug,
a hand of robbers, which waylays travelers
following the byway.
What does the lust of the human mind not contrive?
What of evils does not itch?
They challenge the standing of almighty God
with slanderous disputes,
they cut the faith with minute ambiguities
as each is more wicked in tongue;
they loose and bind the bonds of questions
through pliant syllogisms.
Woe to the captious tricks of sycophants!
Woe to the shifting cunning!
The right rule breaks the stubborn knots,
hostile to the disputers.
Therefore God has chosen the foolish things of the world
that the sophistries may fall,
and from the weak he has subdued the strong,
that believing might be simple.
Lo, a stone is fixed as our stumbling-block,
on which vanity may strike,
a sign for the wary, a scandal for the unwary:
this one it lays low, that one it directs,
while the blind tries many things with uncertain step,
he runs upon what is in the way.
The torch of faith alone must be carried before the steps,
that the footprints may be right.
Who is the enemy in the darkness who nevertheless
strikes, drags, and tramples those who wander,

who savagely devours the scattered grains
at the very passage of those going by,
who as a thief interpolates the glad crops of Christ
adding rival oats.
Which, fertile from the milk of poison into herb,
the farmer suffers to grow,
lest perhaps the empty fiber, torn out together,
kill the ear-bearing stalk.
He waits therefore until hot ripeness cook
the deceitful 1 and the wheaten,
that with the winnowing-fan he may give what is chosen to the barns,
and burn the refuse on the hearths.
But it is useful for us to know the seeds of the tares
which kill the harvest.

There are very many, but I will speak few, lest doctrines dire to tell,
evilly brought forth, stain a catholic tongue.
That man, driving the Father from the throne, thrusts him into the narrow
bearing of a human body, and does not fear to expose
him himself to death and to fasten him to the hard wood.
Is God passible? whose form and image
no one has ever seen: for that majesty is not easy
to be grasped by sense or eyes or hand.
The famous sentence of great John is at hand,
which has testified that God could never be seen.
He is the Father, whom no violent gaze by looking

has pierced from afar with the point of a burning sight,
who has not clothed himself with the form of man, and does not temper
the immensity of Godhead by assumed face or measure.
Either the piety of the gospel book is now to be scorned
by you, blasphemer, or the untouched power of the blessed
Father, never seen, is not to be mixed with things that fall.
Yet there is also a form of the Father which it is lawful to discern,
ready at times to meet human eyes,
which, though dull, the glance with gray speculation
and moist eye, clouded, could approach.
Whoever of men is remembered to have seen God, saw the Son
poured forth from him himself; for the Son is this,
that, flashing from the Father, he offered himself to be looked upon
through forms which a man can grasp with sight.
For the mere majesty is infinite, and does not enter
the gaze, unless it form itself by some governing.
This Abraham, prince of the noble seed, saw,
already then a human host of Christ who deigned to visit the lands,
that the godhead had shone in a triple figure.
This the arms of wrestling Jacob handled.
He himself, the giver of the divine law, bidden to draw near
in presence, who stood with face of friendship brought
close and joined words with sacred speeches,
perceived that he was seeing Christ in the likeness of flesh.
But seeking greater things he stretched his mind through vows
unallowed to man, striving more than mortal
to see Christ himself, as great as he was, without body.
At last after mutual things of much speech, and after
the sight of the present Lord and long companionship,

/I ask,/ he says, /that it be allowed me now, best God, to know you./

The Lord answered 'I will give the just to see my hinder parts,
not me.' What is clearer, than that the Word cannot be seen
apart from an alien face which it may take,
yet that the same, when it so wills, though the Father was never seen
by earthly eyes, can show itself in our habit,
and often, moderated, be led into angelic or mortal
forms, so that it can be discerned under an image?
This is the Word which, flashed from the Father's kindly mouth,
took a fragile form from a virgin body.
Hence the figure of a man, not yet under flesh, set before Moses
had marked the likeness of our face,
because God, when he would take a body compelled by the power
of the Word, was bearing back the same countenance.
Yet also a flame stirred was seen to burn the thorny
bush. God was flitting in the sharp thorns,
and a harmless fire was driving the wounding tresses,
that it might be an example that God would slip into
thorny limbs, which crimes weave with dense stakes
and sins fill, bristling with evil pains.
For from an untilled stock a vicious shrub had begun
to pour out luxury with unjust twigs by dishonest sap
and was binding knots through frequent points.
One could see the sterile leaves suddenly shine,
and God, with the leaves kindled, swiftly, with great onset,
shine far and wide, and not harm the thorny texture,
lick the blood-red fruits and the gory apples,
graze the living germs of the death-bearing wood,
since sad faults are purged by the blood

which the twisted bush brings forth with dense torments.
Therefore nothing was seen except what was to be seen under flesh,
the light the image of God, the Word God and God fire,
who fills the thorny sin of our body;
for the begetter of light, sower of the Word, and author of fire
is believed outside the eyes, as the apostle author teaches,
who denies that the fountain of Godhead is approached by looking.
Believe, no one has seen God, believe me, no one.
God from the fountain is visible, the fountain of God itself
is not visible; he who is born can be discerned, but the
unborn cannot be discerned: that mouth of the Father lies hid
whence is the God who once offered himself visible,
forming in himself something such as the passion followed,
which claims a body for itself; for the high power
is impassible, since the nature of the supernal
fire does not know how to descend to horrible punishments,
nor admits being tortured by human anguish,
pure, serene, flashing, free beforehand with liquid motion,
subject to none, inasmuch as mistress of things,
who has no beginning from time, but above all
time and before the day a majesty with the highest Father,
nay the mind of the Father and reason, the way of counsels,
which, not made by hand nor created by the voice of one commanding,
brought forth the empire, belched from the paternal deep.
This therefore the scourges do not cut, the spittings of saliva
do not sprinkle, the palm with hands struck back does not vex with blows,
nor do wounds of nails, pierced through, drive it to the cross.
The flesh of man was affected by these, which a pregnant woman

brought forth under the law of the womb, without the law of a husband.
He suffers hunger, he drinks gall and drains vinegar,
he fears the face of death, he trembles at pain.
Speak, sacrilegious teachers, who contend that the throne
lay deserted by the highest Father at that
time when God slipped into fragile limbs:
therefore did the Father suffer? What would wicked error not dare?
Did he himself grow, conceived from a girl's blood?
Did he himself stretch the womb of a modest virgin?
And is the page of the divine book already false-speaking,
which says that the Word flowed into the figure of flesh?
But he who is Father of the Word is not held to have been made flesh.
Fix your step, Scripture; it is fitting to have affirmed nothing
movable and twofold: it is the Father whom it is permitted
to none to discern; it is the Father who was never seen in the world,
nor shone in presence among the worldly lights.
He sent the conspicuous Word, and received it sent
when he willed: by the Word he touched the vitals of a pure
virgin, and by the Word he built the boyish limbs.
He himself indeed, present on earth by power and godhead,
is always at hand in every place, nor is any part vacant
of the Father's majesty; nowhere is God the begetter absent,
but through the Word he is always at hand; and thence to Philip
Christ says 'Have I already been with you so long a time,
and do you deny that you know the Father, whom you see clearly in me?'
It is the gift of the invisible Father to bring forth a visible
Son, through whom the Father himself can be seen,
and not only by the eyes of the saints, but by those lacking light,

the blind; I speak of the blind, whose black dullness
does not know how to weigh the true in a dark heart.
If you deny that he is clear to mortals,
come, say, whom the tyrant from the citadel of Babylon sees
walking far among the harmless flames,
treading the rapid fires with brothers unburnt.
Surely he says 'O nobles, the vast fires have received
three men in the panting furnaces; one added,
lo, laughing, cuts through the steaming fires.
That is the Son of God; I confess it and, conquered, I adore.
Take away the mocked torches, and withdraw the warming
brands; the heat of the kindled sulphur grows cold.
The Son (there is no doubt) works these miracles of things,
whom I see, God himself, most certain offspring of God,
he commands the immense heats and tames the angers,
insulting with the pyre serving, and he compresses
the pitchy rages and forces the madness of the flames to grow cold,
the hot air bidden not to touch the barbarian bosoms
passes by and, hissing, runs across the thin robes,
the vapor itself fears to go through the Assyrian tiaras,
lest the hair poured on the shoulders grow sordid with bitter smoke.'
He says these things, and bids the various songs be silent,
organs, sambucas, citharas and reeds and trumpets.
Stupid superstition was silent, the festal voice grew quiet,
which was celebrating the gold of a badly conspicuous image.
The songs of the holy men now alone resound
with triple concert praising the king of heaven,
who made the sea, who the lands, who the bright stars,

and covered his pupils safe in the midst of the fires.
Always the Word of the almighty Father has come down
to the help of man to be saved, always he has taken care
that mortal things be mixed with himself by his own love,
that the flesh to be associated and to be filled by the everlasting Lord,
which then was living a degenerate animal life,

might change by the example of the master, and through like limbs 1
grow used to discern a partner of earthly face,
and know its participant by sight as by a hostage,
and little by little draw near to kindred Christ.
Therefore man was once animal, now the Spirit has transferred
him to the nature of the supernal seed, by pouring
God himself who makes mortal things live.
Now a new matter, solidified by breath under the skin,
yet our matter still, drawn from a virgin,
has put off the corrupted beginnings of the ancient life,
taking immortal good by its own breathing,
that Son of man, but that Son of the Thunderer,
now he alone looks upon the face of the Father and sees him himself.
No one has known the Father except the Son and he to whom the Son
shows him, mediator both of us and of the almighty.
At last I will conclude, that there may be a brief abridgment of the sum:
the Father did not descend into flesh, but the assumed flesh
ascended the Father's citadel; the Born ran through both.
Yield, profaner of Christ, yield now, Sabellius,
deposer of the Father and mad denier of the Born,
do you not violate the Father while you refuse to know the Born?

since there is no begetter unless a son exist,
nor can you call him father whom you cheat of offspring;
but perhaps you would wish to despoil God of the honor
of paternal piety, content with the bare name
that he is God, and take away the glory of the Father and the force of race.
Is there anyone reclining in an idol-house among a thousand rites
and worshipping ridiculous gods with salt, turf, incense,
who does not think there is a highest God and the only one above all?
Although he consecrate smoking altars to Saturns, Junos and Cythereas,
and other portents,
yet as often as he has looked up into heaven, he has set
all right in one god, whom the huge reason of virtues
should serve, furnished with various ministers.
What nation is so stupid in minds, so barbarous in tongues,
or what superstition so sordid, that it would set dog-shaped
and barking Anubis on the throne of heaven?
No one gives Cloacina or Epona a throne above the stars
to the goddesses, although he pay a stinking censer
and with sacrilegious hands rummage the sacrificial meal and entrails.
Consult the ravings of bearded Plato,
consult also what the goatish Cynic dreams and what
nerves Aristotle has woven with twisted vertigo.
Although an uncertain labyrinth and a bent error
drive all these, although they themselves are accustomed
to promise a hen or a cock, that the bedridden god
may deign to furnish himself equal to the dying,

yet when it has come to the norm of reason and art,
they shut their turbid senses and litigious arguments
in rugged modes into one godhead,
at whose will the movable and round sphere
is rolled, and the wandering stars keep their courses.
The nature of man does not admit, provided he is not a quadruped
and looks at heavenly things with erect face,
does not admit that he deny that a supreme government prevails.
That even Numa's soothsayer felt silently for himself,
and the half-wild Scot feels it, worse than a soldier-dog.
But we who have already seen the Lord twice, in books and in body,
first by faith, soon in flesh and blood 1 in presence,
and who, with the volumes of the prophets proved by the witnessing cross,
searching, have plunged fingers into the raw wounds
of the ribs, and having scrutinized the hollows of the hands
with doubting sight have known the eternal king Jesus,
we believe it is impiety to abjure for God the title and paternal name,
who brought forth a king from himself,
not a king of the people of the Parthians or of Romulus' sons,
but a king of the highest and middle and lowest reason,
and therefore lord of things and king above all:
he has the middle of flesh, the highest of the Father, and the lowest of Styx.
He flows down by these steps and is rolled back again into himself;
he is God, he is also man; he becomes dead and the same is God.


He runs through all the duties of prone nature,
that, folded back, he may carry up into the Father's bosom
what had been dead, and may also lift the limbs themselves.
When the instruments of the ancient prophets had sung that these things would
be,
we, having tried them with eyes, hands, meeting, voice, speech,
have at last understood the undertakings of the old heroes
and the oracles opened with Christ seen.
This is our salvation, from here we live, from here we are animated.
This we follow: never, with the name of the Born taken away,
to call the Father, and never without the Father's name
to know the Born God, never unless the Holy and one
Spirit is between to call Born and Father;
yet so these three stand, that I may not by separate leading
make three, but one God subsist in these three.
Nor is the Father himself however he who is the Son, so that, because we know
one born from the unborn, he may be truly Father and the sown
offspring truly, and the Son not be begetter to himself.
It is exceedingly ridiculous and futile, that one born from himself
or super-born 1 should have been, to himself suddenly
a new matter of being born, and that God of his own accord
should have brought himself forth and made himself born to himself.
Divine names fashion nothing false or lying.
He who is Father is Father by begetting, then the Son from this
is Son, because he is begotten with the highest Father as author,
yet himself also highest; for he is not less or unlike the Father.
Whence in both is the form of the work undivided, unless the noble

Son had all the force of the paternal majesty,
and God the begetter were that which the Son is?
They go further, searching what that very
begetting is, if it is lawful to stretch human senses
as far as the secret which all times and days
of old precede, and which stands out above the beginning itself
and passes whatever a man can seek.
Since the way to know the seeds of beginnings
is difficult, how will it be given to a mortal to inquire what
God has done beyond the beginning, or by what pact
he has brought forth the Word, which lacks every beginning?
This only we know, which is handed down, that he is God, whom
the unbegotten begetter has generated, one
and one, whole and whole, not begun but yet sprung,
and co-everlasting backward of the Father and born from the Father.
But the Father is not cut down, so that the Son should be a part of the
Father,
nor did the substance, stretching itself in a drawn-out way,
produce itself and diminish something from the full godhead,
while a changed portion forged a new Born.
God is never convertible nor mutable
ever, or by begetting takes anything from himself, and yet
he is whole God and from the whole, light from light.
But when is light without light? When does flashing
light lack brightness? When does a fire brought forth
diminish fire? When is the Father both God and light
not God and Father of light? Who, if he was not Father once,
and late after times begot a Born,
becomes new, and advances into a new right. Far be it that ever
the full should advance, who does not need increase.
And God and begetter and light and glory always

he was, nor afterward conferred on himself that he should be Father.
So it comes that we believe Christ everlasting with the Father,
sown by that author, whom no author preceded.
If you doubt these mysteries of the Born Christ,
lost one, you are not of the catholic people, but one
of the incense-bearing flock, worshipper of Deucalions,
devoted to a stake, anointer of a fig-wood trunk.
Nay rather search the signs of God in the very
fountain of antiquity, run through the book-chests of the first
writer, whom no stupid father or grandfather-augur,
nor a tale of old fame, nor a chattering nurse,
nor a talkative and shrill crow with cloak-clang
taught the thing of God, but he himself brought forth in presence,
God himself, mild, imbued the trembling mortal with friendly
address and unweaved himself and his highest things.
Doubtless the most learned writer remembers that in that
beginning of the world the Father did not form the clay of the new making
alone nor without Christ.

/He made,/ he says, 'God, forming man, and gave him
the face of God.' What else is it than to say 'he was not
alone, and God was standing by God as he acted,'
when the Lord was making the clay under the image of the Lord?
Christ is the form of the Father, we the form and image of Christ;
we are founded into the face of the Lord by paternal goodness,
Christ to come into our face after ages.
I can excerpt many examples from the sacred books,

unless you flee, which may teach you that the force of majesty
is not placed in the Father alone, but that with the Father Christ
is God, as that same genealogist says:

/The Lord from the Lord rained flame on the Sodomites./

What Lord, from which Lord, if the Father alone from the starry
citadel looks or burns into angers?
The Son, the Lord, was scattering the armed anger of the Lord Father
with fires: both are one thunderbolt.
If these things so understood had watered Jewish
hearings so that they could 1 touch the stupid fibers,
they would have heard the Lord of virtues, who had come
that he might save the perishing sheep; but from the ears all
adornment had flowed, and already a molten Baal
had formed the head and had despoiled the little ears of their own honor.
The leader of the sinning people is at hand from the mountain of flashing
light and the address of God, and carries down to the blind tents
the tablets cut by the tremendous finger,
but the people falls on its face, not about to see Christ
written through enigmas in the likeness of the deep law,
unhappy, which, dazzled by light, has covered fearful eyes
and veiled the face with a pressed cloak!
But we, with the veil cast back, discern Christ in presence
and look upon God with open face,
nor do we lie with forehead pressed down under a heavy law,
but we recognize the ray of the law with lifted face.
Alas, tree leafy before with happy branches,
alas, olive root once with rich berries!
Lo, now the trunk grows green again for you with a grafted wild olive

and is clothed with the bark of an alien book.
Now pity yourself. The shoot of the woodland olive
does not, exulting, boast itself on an alien stem,
but warns that, mindful of your own race, you grow used
not to sadden the tresses with bitter ointment, nor at the lowest stock
envy the twigs sent into the high tops.
You blaspheme the Lord, most ungrateful nation, Christ.
Speak your passover, speak, of whose blood the feast
is so solemn to you? What yearling lamb at last is slain?
Sacred is that to you as the years return,
but sacred in the beast. It is stupid so to believe a sacred thing,
to touch the topmost posts with the blood of a bleater,
to wanton with dances, to be unleavened of fine flour,
when the manners swell with the crime of what is fermented.
You do not taste, unwise, that you are copying our passover,
and painting in the pre-drawn furrows of the ancient law
every sacrament which the true passion holds,
the passion which defends our brow with blood
and smears the bodily house with a signed mouth?
This the Egyptian plague flees, with the storms shut out, 1
this loosed the deadly kingdom of the Pharian king,
and from the dense hail of worldly power
snatches Abraham with the stock and the faithful nation.
The true race of Abraham is he whose blood on the mouth
is believed and written red, to whom God was seen in the world
with faith not doubting, God true from the Father.
He saw God, and soon believed what was seen: but you,


posterity of flesh, discerning all things carnally,
you carry the work of the flesh under the law, which the inner
spirit fills; for a fleshly law did not flow from heaven,
which you worship in flesh, but one pregnant with Christ and about to bring
forth
my hope from the womb, which hope, unless the kindly
light of the godhead and the coming of the Lord, whom the first faith
of Abraham had seen, and the Father had once promised to us
to be looked upon with eyes and to be proved by the voice of the law?
And not only of the law; for what letter now does not
have Christ, or what book-chests of the writers, stuffed
with the praise of Christ, do not celebrate miracles in new books?
The Hebrew pen composes, the Attic abundance composes,
and the eloquence of the third, Ausonian tongue composes.
Pilate, not knowing, commands 'Go, scribe, set out in triple-painted
little verses what power is fastened,
let the title on the brow of the cross be triple, in triple tongue
let Judaea reading recognize, and Greece know,
and golden Rome, having venerated God, count it.'
Whatever the curved trumpet, resounding in hollow bronze, bellows back,
whatever a huge breath vomits from a secret draught,
whatever the chaste lyre, whatever the tortoise-shell rebounds,
what organs mix, harmonious, with unequal reeds,
what rival caves give back to the voices of shepherds,
celebrates Christ, sounds Christ, all things Christ
even mute, animated by holy strings, speak.
O name most sweet to me! light and glory and hope

and my defense, o sure rest of labors,
bland savor in the mouth, fragrant odor, watering fountain,
chaste love, beautiful form, sincere pleasure!
If a deaf nation denies that so many proclamations about you,
so many voices of things and elements, messengers of so great
a joy, enter through stupid ears,
let it hear the mad working of the raving monster,
what the rabid demon shouts among captured vitals,
and let the pitiable believe her own. Apollo is twisted,
struck by the name of Christ, nor can he bear the thunderbolts
of the Word; so many lashes of the tongue drive the wretch,
as many as the praised miracles of the God Christ resound.
The priest of the Lord thunders 'Flee, cunning serpent,
put yourself off from the limbs, and loose the hidden coils,
you vex a slave of Christ, most corrupt thief.
Cease, Christ is at hand, avenger of the human body:
it is not permitted that you seize as spoil him to whom Christ has clung.
Go, driven, windy liquor; Christ commands, go out.'
Amid these voices in the midst Cyllenius burning
wails, and Jupiter sighs the known fires.
Lo, a legion, fierce, rushes upon the Gerasene swine,
and after manifold chains under the rock of the tomb,
it gives forth groans of punishments with long gruntings.
It had cried, but from the mouth of a man 'We know, Jesus
born of God, born of the scepters and seed of David,
what you are, why you come; by what power you drive us off
we know, and we lie by the terror of your coming.'
Has this voice not come to your ears, Judaea?
It has come, but it has not pierced a mind needy
of light, and shut out from the first doors it has fled back.

The west of the Iberian sun has heard the coming of the Lord,
and he who, new, receives 1 the rosy risings.
The gospel voice has loosened Scythian frosts with penetrating
word, and fervid has also loosed Hyrcanian winters,
so that the river, already softer, stripped of ice,
may flow from the Caucasian rock, the Rhodopeian Hebrus.
The Getae have grown tame, and the bloody fierceness of the Gelonian,
thirsting, mixes bloodless cups with glad wine
about to taste sacred drinks from the blood of Christ.
The once faithless region of the Moor of Atlas also knows
to give long-haired kings to the altars of Christ,
from the time when the Spirit touched a mortal womb,
that Spirit God, and God clothed himself with the body of a mother
and created man from virginity.
The Delphic caves have been silent with lots condemned,
the cauldron does not rule the tripods, 2 the panting fanatic does not
foam the fates given forth in Sibylline books,
lying Dodona has lost its mad vapors,
already dead, the oracles of mute Cumae mourn,
nor does Ammon bring back answers in the Libyan Syrtes.
The Romulean Capitol itself mourns that Christ
shines as God to its princes, and that the destroyed temples

have fallen by the command of the leaders. Already the suppliant purple
is laid before the halls of Christ, ruler of the Aenead,
and the highest lord adores the banner of the cross.
Yet from all the princes there was not wanting one
when I was a boy, as I remember, a leader strongest in arms,
a founder also of laws, most famous in mouth and hand,
counsellor of the fatherland, but not counsellor of religion
to be kept, loving thrice a hundred thousand gods.
Faithless he to God, though not faithless to the world,
to bend the august head before the feet of Minerva
and to lick the sandals of clay Juno, to roll at the soles
of Hercules, to wax the knees of Diana,
nay even to submit the brow to Apollonian plaster
or to perfume the horse of Pollux with burning entrails.
By chance sacrificing he was placating Hecate with much blood;
there herds of cows had stood to be struck with the festal axes
of the priests, and a twisted cypress was shading
heifers crowned, with brow bound.
And already the old man, filleted according to custom, had unlocked the vitals
with knife inserted and with bloody hands
was handling the fibers trembling with lethal cold,
and the last pulses of the soul in the warm heart
the cunning interpreter was noting by numbers and by end:
when suddenly the priest cries out amid the sacred things,

pale, 'Lo, what am I doing? A greater, best king, a greater
godhead I know not what has come between our altars
than the bowls foaming with milk can bear,

the blood of slain cattle, vervain, crowns.
I see the summoned shades scattered far,

Since God had decreed to mix Christ with uncorrupt
soil, what he wished to fashion he held worthy
of holy fingers and to found a dear pledge.
Nature indeed, destroyed, deserted the form of the compelled
earth, and, liable to death, yielded:
but the nature of God never to be loosed willed
that the falling earth, first vitiated by our use,
be its own, that it might no longer be vitiable.
Christ is our flesh: for me he is loosed and for me he rises;
I am loosed by my death, I rise again by the power of Christ.
When Christ dies, when he is tearfully entombed,
I see myself: when he already stands returnable from the tomb,
I discern God. If it is a phantom of my limbs,
it is also a phantom of God; it is necessary that Christ be lying
in both, if Christ knows how to deceive by appearance.
If he is not a true man, whom death itself proves a man,
nor is he true God, whom the glory of the work shows
to be God. Either believe that he dies, or refute that he is at hand,
and deny true Christ by a twin reason.
For what is magnificent, if Jesus is not dead,
and returns? That power of God is memorable, that


the slain returns to those above and the buried rises.
Whoever wishes to say Christ God, let him say the same
is man, lest his majesty lose strong things.
I know my body rises in Christ. Why do you bid me
despair? I shall come by the ways by which he came back
from death trodden: this is what we believe.
And I shall come whole; for I shall not be restored less or other than
I now am. Face, force and color the same,
which now lives, will be, nor will the opened ditch of the tomb
vomit me back cheated even of a tooth or a nail.
He who commands that I return will not give back anything feeble;
for if feebleness returns, it is not a restoration.
What chance has snatched, what disease, what pain has drained,
what eating old age has truncated with devastating decay,
all will return to the one returning into repaired limbs.
For conquered death owes faith, lest by the fraud of the tomb
it give back anything cut short, although it has already devoured
cut-short bodies; yet feebleness and the violence of disease
was the power of death: it will give back what little by little
it had swallowed in whatever way, lest the whole dead man
not return, if anything is lacking from the full body.
Drive fear from the heart, my limbs, and believe yourselves
about to return with Christ to God; for he bears you
and calls you back with himself. Laugh at threatening diseases,
despise inflicted chances, spit upon foul
tombs; go where Christ rising calls.
