6 (The siege of the Jews under Hadrian):
And when the things of the revolt of the Jews had again gone forward to a
great and much, Rufus, prefect of Judea, a military alliance having been sent
to him by the emperor, went out using their madness unsparingly, destroying
myriads together of men and children and women, and enslaving their countries
by the law of war. And the Jews were then led by one named Barcochebas, which
means a star; in the other things a murderous and robber-like man, but upon
the appellation as upon slaves, as if a luminary had come down to them from
heaven, and pretending to shine upon those being harmed. And when the war was
at its height in the eighteenth year of the rule of Hadrian at the city
Biththera, which was most fortified, not standing very far from Jerusalem, and
the siege from outside having become long, and the innovators having been
driven by hunger and thirst into an extreme of destruction, and the cause of
their madness having paid the worthy justice, the whole nation from that time
is forbidden by a decree of law and ordinances of Hadrian to set foot at all
even upon the land around Jerusalem, he having commanded that they might not
even from a look see the paternal soil, as Ariston of Pella relates. Thus then
the city having come into a desert of the Jewish nation and a costly
destruction of the old inhabitants, and having been settled from a foreign
race, the Roman city later constituted, having changed the name, unto the
honor of the ruling Aelius Hadrian, is called Aelia.
