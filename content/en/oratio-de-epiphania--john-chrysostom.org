* On the Epiphany
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of John, bishop of Constantinople, on the Theophany.
1 I perceive my soul being divided in two: for today's reading of the
Gospels, hinting at the duality of the natures, calls me toward itself;
but the hymn composed by the one who went before draws me back toward
the praise of the Epiphany. Come then, let us also share with the one
who went before in the hymn for the day of the Epiphany. For even if
the day of the feast has passed, the benefaction of the feast has not
passed.
2 Now the day of the Nativity too was full of a God-befitting
manifestation, but it was hidden from the crowds of the Jews and not
apparent to the eyes of the peoples. For the one born was known only by
the report which the arrival of the magi produced, and they themselves
were inquiring as about an unknown birth: Where is he who has been born
king of the Jews? Therefore a star again pointed out to them with the
finger the place of the infant, since the one born was not manifest.
For the unknown was advantageous to the birth, and first of all because
of Herod's wrath: if he had found the infant once it was recognized, he
would have slaughtered beforehand the end of the economy.
3 But at the Epiphany the spectacle of the one born was public, and the
knowledge more manifest, and from this comes the name of the Epiphany,
so called, because he who before was known to few then became manifest
to multitudes, when all Jerusalem was present with the Baptist, all
Judea was emptied toward him, and all the region of the Jordan ran to
him. For it says, Jerusalem went out, and all Judea, and all the region
around the Jordan, and they were baptized by him, confessing their
sins.
4 Meanwhile the Master was coming, using the common approach of the
crowd and not being recognized by John. For, he says, I did not know
him, but he who sent me to baptize in water, that one told me. But the
grace of the Spirit, secretly touching the Baptist's heart, made known
the dignity of the one present. And John, having received the knowledge
of the Master's presence, drawing back his hand, was offering his head;
leaving the rank of the one who baptizes, he was taking the rank of the
one being baptized. From this a dialogue was born, John hiding his
hand, but Christ pressing toward the baptism, the one saying Baptize,
the other refusing, the one shuddering at the touch, but the Master
commanding him to take courage. But the time of the words was hindering
the crowd that was hastening toward baptism. And being hindered they
sought the cause, and seeking, though they had been spread far, they
ran together to John, and running together they all heard from nearby
what was being said.
5 And first of all, of John's words filled with astonishment: I have
need to be baptized by you, and do you come to me? Put off, he says,
toward me the form of the one being baptized; leave the pretense of one
who needs to be cleansed. What you are by nature accustomed to bestow,
do not pretend to borrow; your wealth, as in these things, do not feign
to be poor. I do not have, while you are present, a right hand for the
use of baptizing, but a head in need of being baptized. I do not know
how to wash solar rays; for the sun is not by nature illuminated by a
lamp; mud does not cleanse a spring.
6 When these things were being said by the Baptist, the crowd listening
to the words from nearby was whispering to one another, as was likely:
Who is this at whom this Baptist shudders? Who is this toward whom the
Baptist displays the rank of a sinner? Who is this who by his presence
frightens so great a man? He shudders at him as at one expert in the
law. How then did he not shudder to baptize Pharisees and Sadducees,
the heads of the law? He was struck with awe as at a worker of a lofty
way of life, and who could display a life higher than one who eats
herbs and locusts and lives with deserts? But not even what appears in
this one is more august than John's: a civilized garment on this one,
but a hair tunic on John; a face free of austerity on this one, but a
parched form on John. From where then is this one's fearful presence to
him?
7 While these were saying these things to these, and those to those,
and others to others, in astonishment, John yields, being compelled.
And the compulsion also dissolved the perplexity of what was perplexing
to the crowds. For they were learning through what was said in the
baptism of what sort the one baptized was. And it did not allow the
devil to say: He appeared righteous and clean of unclean deeds, but
extremely arrogant. For trusting in the righteousness that was his, he
did not fulfill the law of circumcision, he did not bring gifts of
sacrifices, he spat upon the coming to the temple, he did not touch the
Mosaic books, he dismissed the baptism from John.
8 For this reason he descended to circumcision, he offered sacrifices,
he held discourses in the temple, he read the Mosaic books under the
hearing of all, he shared with all in the baptism from John, washing
the waters rather than being washed by them. Yet he still bore the
pretext of need, so that none of the human just requirements might be
left unfulfilled for him. For since he conducted his life on behalf of
the whole nature, which owed the complete keeping of the commandments,
he goes carefully through every commandment, so that none of nature's
debt might be left unpaid and again leave nature a debtor to
punishment.
9 And teaching the Baptist these things the Master cried: Permit it
now; for thus it is fitting for us to fulfill all righteousness. Permit
it now; you offer me a doxology out of season; it is not the time for
me; you strip the dignity [...] to the devil. I do not want the hidden
mystery of my Godhead. You do not allow him, approaching me as one of
those from Adam, to be shattered by the Godhead wisely joined to me;
you drive away the hunt.
10 Permit it now; there will be a time for my doxology; the time of
theology concerning me will come. But now it is fitting for us to
fulfill all righteousness. I have now been shown forth as the person of
the nature of men. I conduct my life on behalf of the indebted race; I
came to pay what was owed by them until now, not now to receive what is
owed to my Godhead by them. I sojourned among the races of men as a
lamb, a sacrifice according to what appears, soon to be slain. Now is
the time of a human way of life for me, not a demand of divine worship
toward myself. Permit it now, for thus it is fitting for us to fulfill
all righteousness.
11 And at once a fearful spectacle was to be seen. John was touching
the Master's head with trembling. From above, the heavens having been
opened, angels were looking down on what was happening, and the grace
of the Spirit in the form of a dove, approaching the Master's head, was
thrusting aside John's right hand. And the crowd stood still more
perplexed, speaking under their teeth: Who is this? Then, as if
answering the crowds, the Divine: Whom is this you seek among
yourselves? This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased.
12 You have forgotten the toil and have been recast toward longing for
the Master, and you cry, slightly altered, the words of Paul: I toil,
yet no longer I, but Christ is strong in me. But let us run back to the
sovereign voice: Whom is this you seek among yourselves? This is my
beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased. The Begetter cries out his
affection toward the Son, so that we may learn the Creator's
disposition toward the world, that he has given over such a Son for
such slaves, the beloved for the hated, the sinless for those who have
offended, the glorious for the inglorious.
13 For see the glory even beside the very lowly things of the flesh,
the things of the flesh, not of the divine essence, even if some are
indignant. For I no longer say, even if many are indignant; for the
many have changed over to few. The lowly things of the flesh, then, as
I said, are not of the divine essence. For the nature of Godhead is
unhumiliated, which the Master's body had remaining inseparably; and
even these lowly things of the flesh were filled with the highest
glory. He was born an infant, but he was of a nature of births beyond
man. For there was for him no marriage but a birth, a virgin mother.
Born, he was cast upon a manger, but angels danced at the birth; he was
swaddled in rags, but from Persia he received gifts in the
swaddling-clothes; he fled Herod's assassination, but having first
mocked him through the magi; he shared with all in the baptism from
John, but being worshiped by the tongue of the one baptizing.
14 He was sold to Israel by Judas, but the sale sold the seller with a
noose; he was crowned with a crown of thorns, but through that he had
the diadem of the kingship of the universe; he was given over to a
cross, but being crucified he turned the sun away; a robber was hung
with him, but on that very cross he changed a robber into an apostle.
He was shut in a tomb, but from the tombs he winged the dead; he was
besieged as a corpse, but he loosed those guarding with an earthquake;
for three days he was held with Hades, but in the three-day period he
prepared for Hades the eternal defeat. And if someone speaks of the
inglorious things, the thanksgiving is greatest to him who has suffered
such things according to the flesh for us; and if someone enumerates
the narrative of the glorious things, the reverence of worship is more
abundant for him, by the dignity of the glory.
15 Therefore let us all together offer to the Master as a common
thanksgiving the voice of the blessed Paul: Thanks be to God for his
inexpressible gift. And on behalf of the Christ-loving emperor let us
ask from Christ the Master of all that a tablet of many years'
consulship be given to him, and that he too, by the help from him, over
a son being baptized and administering the kingship of the world with
him, may say to all the inhabited world: This is my beloved Son; hear
him. In Christ Jesus our Lord, to whom be glory unto the ages of ages.
Amen.
