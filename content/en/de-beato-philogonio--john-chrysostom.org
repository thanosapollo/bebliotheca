* On Blessed Philogonius
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

On the blessed Philogonius who became a bishop from being an advocate, and
that nothing is equal, for good repute with God, to providing for the things
of common benefit, and that to approach the divine mysteries slothfully has
unbearable punishment, even if we dare this once in the year. And it was
spoken five days before the Nativity of Christ.

Discourse sixth.

=48.747=

αʹ. I for my part was preparing today also to strip for the wrestling-bouts
against the heretics, and to pay you the remainder of the debt; but the day of
the blessed Philogonius, whose feast we now keep, has called our tongue to the
narration of his achievements. And we must obey in every way. For if he who
speaks evil of father or mother dies the dea =48.748= th, it is quite clear
that he who speaks well will altogether enjoy life, and if our natural parents
must enjoy so great goodwill from us, much more the spiritual ones, and
especially when the praise makes those who have departed no more splendid, but
makes us who are gathered, both those who speak and those who hear, better.
For he who has gone up into heaven would have no need of human =48.749=
praise, having departed to a greater and more blessed lot; but we who are
still turning about here, and needing much consolation from every side, have
need of the encomia of that man, that we may be stirred up to the same zeal.

For this reason a certain wise man also exhorts, saying: The memory of a
righteous man is with encomia; not as if those who have departed, but as if
those who praise are by this benefited the most. Since therefore so great is
the gain for us from the matter, let us obey and not contradict; for the
season too is suitable for such a narration. For today the blessed one
passed over to the untroubled life, and moored the ship where it is no longer
possible to suspect shipwreck, nor any despondency and pain. And what wonder
if that place is pure of despondency, when Paul, conversing with men still
living, says: Rejoice always, pray without ceasing? And if here, where there
are diseases, and insolences, and untimely deaths, and slanders, and envies,
and despondencies, and angers, and evil desires, and countless plots, and
daily cares, and continual and successive evils bringing countless griefs from
every side, Paul said it is possible always to rejoice, if one, having lifted
his head a little from the surge of worldly affairs, should order his own life
well, much more after the departure from here is it easy to obtain this good,
when all these things have been taken away, both weaknesses and passions and
the occasion of sins, when mine and yours is not, this cold word, which
introduces all the terrible things into our life, and has begotten the
countless wars. Wherefore I especially count this holy man blessed, because
even if he passed over and left the city among us, yet he went up into another
city, that of God, and having left this Church, he belongs to that of the
firstborn who are enrolled in heaven, and having left these feasts, he has
passed over to the festal gathering of the angels.

For that there is both a city above and a Church and a festal gathering, hear
Paul saying: You have come to the city of the living God, the heavenly
Jerusalem, and to the Church of the firstborn enrolled in heaven, and to
myriads of angels, to a festal gathering. And not through the multitude of the
powers above only, but also through the abundance of the goods, and the
continual joy and gladness, he calls all the things there a festal gathering;
for nothing else is wont to make a festal gathering, except the multitude of
those assembled, and the plenty of the wares, when wheat and barley and all
kinds of produce, and flocks of sheep, and herds of oxen, and garments, and
other such things are brought in, some selling, others buying. And what of
these is in the heavens, he says? Of these, nothing, but things much more
august than these. For it is not wheat and barley and kinds of produce, but
the fruit of the Spirit is there everywhere, love and joy and gladness and
peace and goodness and gentleness with much plenty; not flocks of sheep and
herds of oxen, but spirits =48.750= of the righteous made perfect, and virtues
of souls, and achievements of manners are to be seen everywhere in the
heavens; not garments and coverings, but crowns more precious than all gold
and prizes and rewards and the countless goods laid up for those who succeed.
And the multitude of those assembling is much more august and greater; for it
is not from city-men and natives, but where there are myriads of angels, there
thousands of archangels, elsewhere a company of prophets, in another place
choirs of martyrs, ranks of apostles, gatherings of the righteous, various
peoples of all who have been well-pleasing. Truly the festal gathering is a
marvelous thing, and what is greatest of all, that in the midst of the festal
gathering the king of all these is turning about; for having said, To a festal
gathering of myriads of angels, he added, And to God the judge of all. Who
ever saw a king present at a festal gathering? Here indeed no one ever saw it,
but there those present continually see him, as it is possible for them to
see, both present and adorning all who assemble with the brightness of his own
glory. And these festal gatherings of the day, when it is mid-day, are often
dissolved, but that one is not so. For it does not wait for circuits of
months, nor cycles of years, nor a number of days, but it stands continually,
and all the goods in it have no limit, know no end, cannot grow old nor
wither; but they are unaging and immortal. There is no tumult there as here,
no disturbance, but much the order of all with the fitting good order, as in
some lyre, sending up with rhythm to the Master of either creation the
all-harmonic melody sweeter than all music, and the soul there, as in sacred
inner shrines and certain divine mysteries, accomplishing the divine
mystagogy.

βʹ. To this blessed and unaging lot, then, the blessed Philogonius passed over
today. What word then could become a match for a man counted worthy of so
great good fortune? There is none. What then, tell me, shall we be silent
because of this? and for what have we come together? But shall we say that we
shall not be able to reach the magnitude of the works? For this very reason it
is necessary to speak, since this too is a greatest part of an encomium, when
the words cannot be made equal to the works; for of those whose achievements
are beyond mortal nature, it is quite clear that the encomia too are beyond a
human tongue. Yet he will not on this account thrust away our things, but will
imitate the same Master; for that one, to the widow who cast down two obols
only, did not give a reward of two obols only. Why then? Because he attended
not to the quantity of the money, but to the wealth of the mind. For if you
examine the money, the poverty is great; but if you unfold the choice, you
will see the unspeakable treasure of magnanimity. So that even if our things
are small and cheap, yet they are what we have; and if they fall short of the
magnanimity of the noble and just Philogonius, yet this too would be a
greatest proof of his magnanimity, that he does not thrust away even the small
things, but =48.751= does the same as those who are rich.

For those, having received from the poor small things of which they have no
need, add what is from themselves, giving in return to those who brought in
what they had. So then this one too, having received from us a praise of
words, of which he has no need, will give in return the blessing through
works, of which we always have need. From where then must we begin the
encomia? From where else than from the office which the grace of the Spirit
entrusted to him. For the offices from without would not altogether become a
proof of the virtue of those entrusted with them, but often they are even
accusers of vice. For what reason? Because both the patronage of friends and
canvassing and flatteries, and many other things more shameful than these, are
wont to procure such offices; but when God ordains and votes, and that hand
touches the holy head, the vote is unbribed, the judgment is unsuspected, the
dignity of the one ordaining would become an undisputed proof of the one
ordained. And that God ordained that man is clear even from the very manner.
For having been snatched from the midst of the marketplace, he was led to this
throne; so august and splendid did he display his former life, even though he
had a wife and a daughter, and was turning about in the law-court; so he shone
beyond the sun, that at once from there he appeared worthy of this office, and
from a judicial bench he was led to a sacred bench. And then he advocated for
men against men who were plotting, making the wronged stronger than those who
wronged; but having come here, he advocated for men against demons who were
insolent. And how great a proof of virtue it is to have been counted worthy of
so great an office by the grace of God, hear what Christ, having risen, says
to Peter. For when he asked him: Peter, do you love me? and when that one
said, You, Lord, know that I love you, Christ did not say: Throw away your
money, practice fasting, hard living, raise the dead, drive out demons; he
brought none of these, nor of the other signs, nor of the achievements into
the midst; but having passed by all those, If you love me, he says, shepherd
my sheep. And he said this, not only wishing to show us the greatest sign of
the love toward him, but also of the friendship which he displays concerning
the sheep, he has already made this the greatest proof of goodwill toward him,
all but saying, he who loves my sheep loves me. See then how many things
Christ endured for this flock; he became man, he took the form of a slave, he
was spat upon, he was slapped; last of all he did not refuse even death, and a
death that is shameful; for on a cross he poured out the blood. So that if
anyone should wish to be well-reputed with him, let him care for these sheep,
let him seek the common benefit, let him be concerned for his own brethren;
for nothing is a more precious achievement than this with God; for this reason
also elsewhere he says: Simon, Simon, Satan has asked to sift you as the
wheat; and I have prayed concerning you, that your faith may not fail. What
return then of this care and providence do you give me? what return then does
he seek? This same again; For you also, he says, when you have turned,
strengthen your brethren. Thus Paul also says: Become imitators of me,
=48.752= as I also of Christ. How did you become an imitator of Christ?
Pleasing all in all things, and not seeking my own benefit, but that of the
many, that they may be saved. And elsewhere he says: For Christ also did not
please himself, but the many. And nothing would so become a mark and character
of the faithful man who loves Christ, as to be concerned for the brethren, and
to care for their salvation.

γʹ. Let all monks also hear these things, both those who have occupied the
peaks of the mountains, and those who have crucified themselves to the world
in all things, that according to their own power they may support those who
preside over the Churches, anoint them with prayers, with concord, with love;
knowing that if they should not support in every way those put forward by the
grace of God, and who have taken up so many cares, even though sitting far
off, the chief point of their life is lost to them, and all their wisdom has
been maimed. But that this is a greatest proof of friendship concerning Christ
is clear from here; but let us see also how he administered this episcopate;
or rather there is no longer need of a word here, nor of our voice, your own
zeal itself making this clear. For just as if someone, having entered a
vineyard, and having seen the vines luxuriant with leaves, heavy with fruit,
walled from every side with fences and hedges, will need no word and other
proof, so as to learn the virtue of the vinedresser and the husbandman; so
also here, if someone, having entered and having seen these spiritual vines,
and your fruit, will need no word and teaching, so as to learn the one who
presides over you; just as Paul also says: You are our letter, written and
read.

For the river shows the spring, and the fruit the root. It would have been
necessary then to speak also of the season in which he was entrusted with this
office; for this too is not a small part of an encomium, but even exceedingly
sufficient to show the virtue of the man. For there was much difficulty then,
the persecution having just ceased, and the remnants of that most grievous
storm still remaining, and the affairs needing much correction. And to these
it would have been necessary to add again, that the heresy of the heretics,
having taken its beginning in his time, was being checked, his wisdom
foreseeing all things; but nevertheless the word is pressing us on to another
necessary subject. For this reason, having left these things to the common
father and zealot of the blessed Philogonius to speak, as one who knows all
the ancient things more accurately than we, we shall walk toward another path
of public speech. For a feast is about to draw near, the most august and most
awesome of all the feasts, which one would not err in calling the metropolis
of all the feasts.

And what is this? The nativity of Christ according to the flesh. δʹ. For from
this the Theophany, and the sacred Pascha, and the Ascension, and Pentecost
took their beginning and their subject. For if Christ had not been born
according to the flesh, he would not have been baptized, which is the
Theophany; he would not have been crucified, which is the Pascha; he would not
=48.753= have sent down the Spirit, which is Pentecost. So that from here, as
from some spring, various rivers having flowed, these feasts were born for us.
And not according to this only would the day be just in enjoying this
presidency, but because also what happened in it is much more awesome than all
the others. For that Christ, having become man, should die, was thereafter of
the sequence; for even if he did not commit sin, yet he took a mortal body;
and this too was marvelous; but that being God, he should will to become man
and endure to descend so far as not even thought can receive, this is the most
awesome and full of astonishment. Which indeed Paul also, wondering, was
saying: And confessedly great is the mystery of godliness. What great thing?
God was manifested in flesh. And again elsewhere: For God does not take hold
of angels, but he takes hold of the seed of Abraham; whence he was obliged to
be made like the brethren in all things. For this reason especially I embrace
this day and love it, and I set the desire into the midst, that I may make you
partners of the affection; for this reason I beg all of you and entreat that
with all zeal and eagerness you be present, each having emptied his own house,
that we may see our Master lying in the manger, wrapped in swaddling-clothes,
that awesome and paradoxical spectacle. For what defense will there be for us,
and what pardon, when he himself comes down from the heavens for us, and we do
not even come from the house to him? when Magi, barbarian men and of another
race, run from Persia, so as to see him lying in the manger; and you the
Christian do not even endure a small distance, so as to enjoy this blessed
spectacle? For if we come with faith, we shall altogether see him lying in the
manger; for this table fulfills the rank of the manger. For here too the
Master's body will lie, not wrapped in swaddling-clothes, as then, but wrapped
from every side by the Holy Spirit. Those who have been initiated know the
things said. The Magi then only worshipped; but you, if you approach with a
pure conscience, we shall also permit you to take it and go home. Approach
then you too bringing gifts, not such as those, but much more august. Those
offered gold; offer you temperance and virtue; those offered frankincense;
offer you pure prayers, the spiritual incense; those offered myrrh; offer you
humility and a humbled heart and almsgiving. If you approach with these gifts,
you will enjoy this sacred table with much confidence. For I too for this
reason am moving these words now, since I know that many will altogether
approach on that day, and will fall upon this spiritual sacrifice. In order
then that we may not do this for evil, nor for judgment of our soul, but for
salvation, from here already I protest beforehand and exhort, that having
purified ourselves in every way we thus approach the sacred mysteries.

δʹ. And let no one say to me, I am full of shame, I have the conscience filled
with sin =48.754= s, I carry a most heavy burden; for the appointed time of
these five days is sufficient, if you are sober and pray and keep vigil, to
cut away the greater part of the sins. For do not look at the time being
short, but consider this, that the Master is philanthropic; since the
Ninevites also in three days beat off so great wrath, and the narrowness of
the season hindered nothing, but the eagerness of the soul, the philanthropy
of the Master having taken hold, was able to accomplish the whole. And the
harlot too, having approached Christ in a brief moment of time, washed off all
the reproaches, and when the Jews were accusing, that Christ received her and
gave her a share of so great freedom of speech, he muzzled them, and having
freed her from all the evils, and having accepted her zeal, thus sent her
away. Why then? Because she approached with a warm mind and a kindled soul and
boiling faith, and touched those holy and sacred feet, having loosed her
tresses, having sent forth springs of tears from the eyes, having emptied the
myrrh. For by the things with which she bewitched men, by these she prepared
the medicines of repentance; by the things with which she excited the eyes of
the unchaste, by these she wept; by the locks, by which she tripped many into
sin, by these she wiped the feet of Christ; by the myrrh, by which she baited
many, by this she anointed his feet. And you then, by the things with which
you provoked God, by these make him propitious again.

You provoked him by plunder of money? by these reconcile him, both having
given back the things plundered to those who were wronged, and having given
yet others in addition to these, say according to Zacchaeus: I give back
fourfold of all that I plundered. You provoked by tongue and reviling, having
insulted many? propitiate him by tongue again, sending up pure prayers,
blessing those who revile, praising those who speak evil, giving thanks to
those who wrong you. These things do not need days, nor many years, but choice
only, and they are accomplished in one day. Depart from wickedness, take hold
of virtue, cease from evil, and promise no longer to do these things, and this
is sufficient for you for a defense. I protest and I guarantee, that each of
us who sin, if having departed from the former evils he promise to God with
truth no longer to touch them, God will seek nothing else for a greater
defense. For he is philanthropic and merciful, and just as she who is in
travail desires to give birth, so also he himself desires to pour out his own
mercy; but our sins cut this off. Let us then take down the little wall, and
from here already let us begin the feast, having bid farewell to all things
for these five days; let law-courts be gone, let councils be gone, let worldly
affairs and contracts and dealings groan; I wish to save my soul. What is a
man profited, if he gain the whole world, but lose his soul? The Magi went out
from Persia; go out you from worldly affairs, and journey toward Jesus; it is
not =48.755= a great interval, if we wish. For there is no necessity to cross
a sea, nor to go over peaks of mountains, but sitting at home, displaying
piety and much contrition, it is possible to dissolve all the little wall,
take away the hindrance, contract the length of the way; for I am
a God who draws near, he says, and not a God from afar; and, The Lord is near
to all who call upon him in truth. But now many of the faithful have been
driven to so great mindlessness and contempt, that even being full of
countless evils, and taking no care of themselves at all, they approach this
table in the feasts simply and as it happens, not knowing that the season of
communion is not a feast and a festal gathering, but a pure conscience and a
life released from charges.

And just as the one who is conscious of nothing base in himself must approach
every day, so for the one preoccupied in sins, and not repenting, it is not
safe to approach even in a feast. For it is not approaching once in the year
that releases us from the charges, if we approach unworthily, but this very
thing condemns us more greatly, that approaching once, we do not even then
approach purely. For this reason I exhort all of you not simply through the
necessity of the feast to touch the divine mysteries; but if ever you are
about to partake of this holy offering, for many days beforehand to cleanse
yourselves thoroughly through repentance and prayer and almsgiving and leisure
concerning the spiritual things, and not to turn again like a dog to his own
vomit. For how is it not absurd to take so great care of the bodily things,
that when a feast is coming, for many days beforehand taking out of the chests
the finest garment they prepare it, and buy shoes, and use a more abundant
table, and invent many resources from every side, and in every way brighten
and adorn themselves; but of the soul, neglected, filthy, squalid, being
destroyed by famine, remaining unclean, to make no account, but to bring in
here the body adorned, and to overlook that one naked and unseemly? And yet
this the fellow-servant sees, and there is no harm even if it be somehow dispo
=48.756= sed, but that one the Master sees, and he brings the greatest
punishment of the neglect. Do you not know that this table is full of
spiritual fire, and just as the springs bubble up the nature of water, so also
this has a certain unspeakable flame?

Do not then approach having straw, nor wood and hay, lest you work a greater
conflagration, and burn up the soul that partakes; but having precious stones,
gold, silver, that you may make the matter purer, that having received much
merchandise you may go away. If there is anything wicked, banish it, drive it
as a fugitive from your soul. Has anyone an enemy, and has been greatly
wronged? let him dissolve the enmity, let him settle the mind that is
inflamed, that is swollen, that there may be no tumult within nor disturbance.
For you are about to receive a king through the communion; and when a king is
entering the soul, there must be much calm, much stillness, deep peace of the
thoughts. But you have been greatly wronged, and you cannot bear to let go the
anger? why then do you wrong yourself much more greatly and more grievously?
For the enemy will not dispose you such things, whatever he may do, as you
yourself not being reconciled toward that one, but trampling the laws of God.
Did that one insult you? for this reason then do you insult God, tell me? For
not being reconciled to the one who has grieved you is not so much of one
defending himself against that one, as of one insulting God who legislated
these things. Do not then look to the fellow-servant, nor to the magnitude of
his wrongs; but throwing God and the fear of him into mind, consider this,
that by how much you suffer greater violence according to the soul, being
compelled after countless evils to be reconciled to the one who has grieved
you, by so much you will enjoy more honor from God who commanded these things;
and just as you receive him here with much honor, so also he himself will
receive you there with much glory, paying you back ten-thousand-fold the
returns of this obedience; of which may it be that we all obtain them by the
grace and philanthropy of our Lord Jesus Christ, with whom to the Father be
glory, honor, power and worship, together with the Holy Spirit, unto the ages
of the ages. Amen.
