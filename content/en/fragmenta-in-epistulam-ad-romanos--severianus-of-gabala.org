Rom 1:1 As though the world were ignorant of the Godhead of Christ, he
proclaims this. And why then does he make the common thing his own? Because
many both who had seen the Lord and who had not seen believed, but Paul is
called from heaven, "Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?" And there is also
something more belonging to him besides the rest of the apostles; for those
around Peter and James and John the Lord himself, having called and having had
as disciples, did not immediately make them apostles or name them, but Paul he
both called at once and brought into apostleship. And that the gospel is
preached by the will of the Father. Rom 1:2 But he said "of his prophets,"
because there are also prophets of the idols, distinguishing by his own voice
both prophets of prophets and gospel of gospels; for there are many gospels,
but mortal and temporary, whereas that of Christ promises the enjoyment of the
eternal goods in holy scriptures. His, then, are also the prophets, and not of
another god, but of the Father of Christ. Rom 1:7 And "to the saints" has not
been said simply, but (since many are called, but few chosen; for not all
persisted in the calling) that he may not cast the holy things to the dogs.
214 Rom 1:7 But according to the heretics: if Christ is Lord, and God is our
Father, the Father also will be a slave of Christ; for as the Son is, such
also is the Father. But these things are not so; for he said "our Father,"
that he may show the grace, and "Lord Jesus," lest having thought highly of
themselves as being themselves also sons of God they pull down the glory of
the Son, or raise themselves up above nature. Yet he called God Father,
because he judges no one, and the Son Lord, because he himself is judge. And
he says Father, that he may display the care concerning us; and he calls the
Son Lord, that we may know that by the goodness of God we are called sons, but
Jesus himself, as true God by nature, is our Lord. Rom 1:13 And again, since
many were hastening to Rome for certain human reasons, he both shows the
longing, and that this is pious by the addition. And he is shown to long for
the Romans, perhaps because the faith of the Romans had something more,
becoming an exhortation to all those under them. Rom 1:25 That God is truly
God, but the idols to which they bowed down are falsely called gods. Rom 1:27
And he does not mention such shamefulness simply, but because he heard that in
Rome there was also companionship of males. 215 Rom 1:32-2:1 And he calls the
ordinance of God his just recompense which he renders to each according to
desert. This then he says: even if men know from their natural reasoning that
those who offend have to be punished by God, nevertheless they do not depart
from the worst things, and the most terrible is that they also consent with
those who do them. The ordinance of God, then, he says is this very thing,
that those who practice such things are worthy of death, or simply his
righteousness or the commandments or the Mosaic law; for thus clearly and
sharply he commands, judging those who practice such things worthy of death.
Therefore you are without excuse, O man, everyone who judges; for in that you
judge the other, you condemn yourself; for you who judge do the same things.
Rom 2:5 He made clear through "treasuring up" that the sins gathered part by
part become an addition of punishment through the longsuffering of the judge
for those who are incurably sick. And he does not say that God is angry as
charging a passion of God, but using the custom of men he discourses about the
judgment of God, which in the present life is covered and escapes notice,
being stored up unto the time of judgment. God also wisely administering this,
not according to the order of justice, because the impious even while faring
well now do not perceive, nor did the just, having been tried by the terrible
things in life, change the manner of piety, which some, not knowing, are
scandalized, saying that all things are without providence, thinking that God
does not oversee the things among men. But when the appointed time is in the
repayment, then also the light of the righteous judgment of God is revealed
and shines forth, each obtaining the things according to the desert of what he
has lived. "Day of wrath," then, is an image; he taught that now is a time
neither of punishment nor of wrath nor of the righteous judgment of God. Rom
2:8-9 And just as what is from faction is inexcusable, so also that of the one
who works out thoroughly compared with the one who works; for it is possible
to work while wrestling yet being defeated; but to work out thoroughly is of
the working of evil with diligence. Rom 2:10 Upon evil he appointed
condemnation, not simply but upon those who work it out thoroughly, but upon
the good, glory, not only 216 upon those who work it out thoroughly, that
through both the goodness of God may be shown; and that he condemns not simply
those who work but those who work out thoroughly, and that he honors those who
simply work the good. And well here he says Jew first, and Greek second; for
those nearer the Master are deemed worthy both of the rebukes and of the
honors above the others. Rom 3:9 What then do we hold as surplus? For we have
already charged both Jews and Greeks, and what follows. It has been said
elliptically; for to "what then do we hold as surplus" it was consequent to
add the righteousness from faith, which going forward he says in what follows:
a righteousness of God through faith of Jesus Christ. What then do we
ourselves hold as surplus? The righteousness from faith; for we have already
charged both Jews and Greeks. And how did he charge them beforehand? In
convicting the Greeks of having corrupted the natural knowledge, in which he
said: for God manifested it to them. For the invisible things of him from the
creation of the world are clearly seen, being understood by the things made,
and because having known God they did not glorify him as God or give thanks;
and the Jews as having transgressed the law (through the things he says: you
who boast in law, through the transgression of the law do you dishonor God?),
having shown also that with some nature was weak, and with others both nature
and the law, he consequently introduces the grace from the gospel and says:
what also do we have from grace as surplus and exceptional? The faith, which
is productive of righteousness through Jesus Christ. Rom 3:23 And nowhere does
he say that all were impious, but they sinned, the common thing of sin, not
that of impiety. And the one who falls short, having something 217 lacking,
seeks what is missing; the Jews had the law, but the perfection of grace was
lacking to them. Rom 3:25 For God forbore, that he might show his own
righteousness. For if he had gone out against them, he would certainly have
destroyed; and if he destroyed, there would be no one being saved. But since
he forbore even while the sins were being added, he showed that wishing not
only to free from the sins but also to justify, he first forbore. Having then
allowed them to sin, he granted the forgiveness all at once. And we call God
just either as rendering in return or as granting the righteousness. That then
by which I am justified is a righteousness of God, since it is not from my
works, but from the grace of the one who justifies. Rom 4:11 Circumcision was
given for these three causes, so as to be a sign of faith and indicative of
the Abrahamic race and a symbol and riddle of a pure and temperate way of
life; so that it was not given as productive of righteousness, but as a seal
and sign of the righteousness from faith of Abraham. Rom 4:13 He says that the
just will be heirs of the world, because when the impious have been taken out
of the way and handed over to punishment on the day of judgment, the just will
thereafter have the whole world renewed, and the heavenly goods and the
earthly will be theirs. Rom 4:17 Before whom he believed: i.e., before the
face of the God in whom he believed. 218 Rom 4:18 Against hope of his own
nature, upon hope of the power of the one who promised. And "so shall your
seed be" is not only in multitude, but also in splendor. Rom 4, Having said
"he was strengthened," he shows that the faith is accomplished with much toil
and power of soul and labor. And how was he strengthened? By giving glory, he
says, to God, that is, not being over-curious how this can come to be, but
immediately believing and blessing God as a benefactor, as having already
given what he promised. Rom 5:19 You saw how he distinguishes here too,
placing "many" upon sin and upon righteousness (for not all who were before
the law sinned, nor did all who are in grace practice righteousness; for many
are called, but few chosen), but upon bodily death and bodily resurrection,
"all" and "all." Rom 6:3 Because being baptized we confess that we have died
to the world and renounce sin and the devil. Rom 6:4 To live in newness is to
begin some new and fresh life, having put off the old one in sins; for
regeneration also promises a citizenship of some new and fresh life. Rom 6:6-7
Those living according to the old man have a body of sin, set before the devil
for plots; but those according to the 219 new, our Lord Jesus, a body of
righteousness, since they are also members of the Lord. Therefore, the devil
stirring men to sin, having been defeated through Christ and the flesh of the
Word having been nailed to the cross, the evil one has been cast out and those
who wish now live as free; for the one who has died according to the account
of baptism has died to the world and has been justified and has been released
from punishment, having been freed from sin. Rom 7:5 The law that commanded
not to sin did not furnish the stumbling, but through allowing luxury to the
passions. Furnishing a pretext to the passions, then, was not of the aim of
the law, but what happened was of the economy, to be given then. I mean that
eating and drinking was being worked in our members (for desire is moved even
in those who fast), but it is not worked unto bearing fruit for death. Thus
Moses has been written a ministry of death, since the law weighed heavily, and
what it wished it did not accomplish, and what it did not want it procured.
Rom 7:7-8 The law, he says, is not evil, nor is it sin, but it made sin known,
yet not having been able to make it depart, it furnished it an occasion of
increase. And why this, he says. Showing the magnitude of sin, that he may
prove together with it the excess of grace. Thus, he says, was sin strong,
that through the things forbidding it it rather prevailed and was more
powerful; just as even a chance thing quenches a flame that has begun, but
when it has been kindled further even the quenching water kindles it more, so
the law convicted the sin that had prevailed over the whole inhabited world
and was grazing upon all, but was not able to quench it. Because it cannot
even condemn the one who has sinned in ignorance, but the law having been
given and having shown the sin, gave power to sin. And this is not an
accusation of the law, but a conviction of the negligence of those who do not
keep it. And just as without law sin is dead, so also when the law is kept,
sin is dead. Rom 7:12 He calls law the Mosaic, and commandment the one given
to Adam in paradise, holy as having taught what is due, just 2 as having
brought the vote rightly upon the transgressors, and good as preparing life
for those who keep it. Rom 7:24-25 Having then contemplated with the mind the
war that has come about through the body against the soul and through it the
man being taken captive, he seeks a refuge of salvation and looks about for
the one who delivers, that he may transform the very body of death into a body
of life. Then, as the one who delivers having been found and being ready
toward the grace of salvation, he sends up the fitting thanksgiving to God
through Jesus Christ, through whom the being delivered from the enemy also
accrued to him. And he does not simply say, nor pray, to be delivered from the
body, but from the death that has taken hold of the body through the sin of
death; for he wants his own body to be a body of life, not a body of death nor
a body of sin. Rom 8:9 He called "Spirit of Christ" the gifts of the new
covenant. Rom 8:15 And some say "spirit of slavery" is of the evil one, but it
is not; for here he calls the law a spirit of slavery and those under law
slaves; therefore as being punished, if they stumbled, they feared, which is a
sign of slavery. And to have been permitted to call God Father through the
purity of life promises hopes of adoption; and it is the same Spirit that has
worked upon both peoples, that of slavery and of adoption according to what is
beneficial and fitting according to the desert of both. Rom 8:19 Eager
expectation, however, is said to be the intense watching of the things hoped
for, from the one who expects perhaps even bending the head and stretching the
eyes, as being about to see immediately what is expected. 221 Rom 8:29 Or of
the image of the Son, i.e., of the Holy Spirit; for just as the Son is an
unchanging image of the Father, so also the Spirit of the Son. As many, then,
as having been deemed worthy of the Holy Spirit live spiritually, these are
conformed to the Spirit, which is the image of the Son. Rom 8:34 Through these
he shows the measureless love of Christ which he has concerning us; for he was
not content with dying for us, but also intercedes with the Father for us. And
it is clear that he intercedes these things which also when he was with men
according to the flesh; for he was asking the Father, saying, I will that
where I am they also may be; before this the Savior intercedes. And he does
not teach, interceding, as though the Father were ignorant of the things
concerning us, nor as though unwilling; for what the Father wills the Son also
wills. What then do the embassy and the intercession mean? The word cuts off
in advance the coming impiety of Sabellius, teaching that, even if the essence
of the Trinity is one, yet it is not one hypostasis; for through the
intercession another is shown as the one asking and another as the one asked;
for it is not absurd that the Son ask and the Father grant, so that a fitting
order may be kept upon both persons. Or was it not absurd that the Son,
knowing the mind of the Father, anticipate the gift of the Father, and
snatching it give the grace? Thus indeed all things were created through the
Son, but by the will of the Father, that throughout the consequent and fitting
may be kept upon both persons. The word then teaches us the unconfused order
concerning the Father and the Son; and if you also reckon Christ in the flesh
as asking, the word will appear to you much more easily surveyed. Rom 8:37
Having shown splendidly the disposition of God concerning us and the love of
his Son, he consequently teaches of what sort the grateful ought thereafter to
be concerning the benefactor, so that not even the very fearful things can
separate us from Christ. 222 Rom 9:20-21 Some think this is a solution,
especially Asterius the Cappadocian thinking thus: you who contradict me, how
do you contradict? Not from the authority according to the will? Do you not
answer back to God, wanting to show that God does not compel and make just?
For the authority remains with men. But such a sense has no reason, for he
adds: will the thing formed say to the one who formed it, why did you make me
thus? And this is not of one showing that we are in authority, but even the
contrary. What then is the meaning? This has been said from abundance, a
solution has not been set. And what is "from abundance"? But first of all, who
are you? For let us suppose that we do not even have a will, nor are we
self-determining, did God not have authority as he wished? But a potter forms
some things unto honor and others unto dishonor, and God does not have the
authority which the potter has, so as to honor whom he wishes and punish whom
he wants? Having said this from abundance he thereafter makes the solution.
Rom 9:22 He calls "showing of wrath" that which, being longsuffering toward
men through free will, he allows them to do what they want; and he calls
"vessels of wrath fitted for destruction" the men who are under the bound of
death. Some others, however, have referred to "but if God, wishing to show the
wrath" and what follows, the "what do you know?", understood elliptically from
outside. And "in much longsuffering" i.e., after much longsuffering he brought
the wrath. Rom 10:4 Not as a part of law is Christ, but as a beginning of
another life. He has been called "end of law," then, as having become an end
of the law, that is, having stopped it. For two reasons then "end of law,"
both because he used it and because he stopped it. And "to everyone who
believes," i.e., not only to a Jew but also to a Greek, not, since end of law
already to a Jew alone. 223 Rom 11:24 Having mentioned the cultivated olive
and the wild olive he presented that there is one nature of all just as also
of the olive, but not one choice, just as in the case of the wild and the
tame; calling wild what has been alienated and useless to the master, and tame
the fruit-bearing. And he calls grafting the being grafted in the faith of
Abraham whom he names a root; and he says the grafting furnishes life. Rom
11:28-29 Except it has been said elliptically, and for this reason it is also
asyndetic. Thus then he wants it, so that from the comparison with you the
Jews are convicted of being enemies of God, since you who are from the
Gentiles believed, but those who ought before all to believe were hardened in
heart and according to this account they are enemies of God, but beloved
because of the fathers, in whose honor he will be humane toward their
descendants, and will consider them beloved when they have believed and
displayed a way of life able to save. Rom 12:2 The present constitution of the
world urges the man toward luxuries and pleasures, If someone denies this
form, he will also transfer himself into an altered and foreign way of life.
Having become a new man he is transformed from one into another. Rom 12:4-8
Having said beforehand that God measures the measure of faith, by the
following example he both makes a defense for the one who gives and persuades
not to be grieved, if someone should have the gift less. For whatever humble
part there might have been in a body is useful to the whole, and the great and
the small are completed into one body; for it is an inference of what was
before this, that we are members of one another. "Having different gifts"
i.e., of one having different gifts. The one who shares in simplicity, that is
what the Lord says: give to everyone who asks you, not seeking whether good or
bad and to whom you give, since this exactness often prevents us from giving
to those to whom it ought to be given, suspicion in us often going wrong. 224
Rom 13:1 It is fitting to be subject to the kings who persist in the ministry
of the will of God, unless perhaps there is a case of persecutions in the
midst; for concerning enduring all things because of the faith he taught in
advance, saying: who shall separate us from the love of Christ? and what
follows. For the one who does not allow even angels or authorities to be
obeyed toward lawlessness, how would he advise to obey in the human
authorities when they force one to depart from God. But perhaps someone might
say: if kingdoms are established by God, how do they sometimes also campaign
against the godly? To whom it must be said that, even if the case of
persecutions seems most heavy to the many, yet how much is useful to them must
be considered; for God does not allow such things simply, but that both the
virtues of the just may shine and the vices of those who rule may be shown.
And the greatest, then also those who pretend the form of piety while being
impious are convicted, and the genuine lovers of piety, as having found the
time of persecutions as material of approval, will show the fruit of virtuous
contest. Thus indeed God also said to Job: for do you think that I have dealt
with you otherwise, than that you might be shown just. Rom 13:9 Such then
being love, it reasonably bears many advantages in itself; for the one who
loves does not commit adultery, does not murder, does not do any other of the
forbidden things, so that the one who has love has thus all at once fulfilled
the law through love. Rom 13:11 Fulfill, he says, the work of love, knowing
the time that is pressing and demanding virtue. Now our salvation is nearer
than when we believed, that is, now when we have known the more beneficial of
the works, it has become lighter nevertheless to be saved than when we were
still being catechized in the word of faith; for we did not at the same time
as believing in Christ also have the exact grasp of the things to be done, but
now we have known exactly. Rom 14:4 The household servant of Christ whom
Christ received is alien to the law. Who then are you, judging from the law
the one alien to the law? 225 Rom 14:14 Through him, that is, through God. Rom
15, Not as praying away the communion of the rest of the apostles, but as
judging it greedy and unjust to snatch the glory of the works of others. Rom
16:19 That is, to use prudence so as not to be wronged by others, and
simplicity so as not to plot against others.
