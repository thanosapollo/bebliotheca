OF THE SAME CYRIL From the third discourse toward the things of Theodore,
bishop of Mopsuestia, whose beginning is: For perhaps our words toward us are
burdensome. 1.
Hear then also from us. Understand, O man, the depth of the mystery, go
straight to the aim of the sacred writings. For godhead and flesh, that is
humanity, are one thing and another, as far as it pertains to the accounts
inherent in the properties of both. For how has he come to be according to us,
though remaining what he was, the Word being God? Only give him his flesh
according to an unbreakable union, do not strip him of the veil; for thus you
will worship one Son, of the same substance with the Father in a divine
manner, and of the same substance with us, the same one, in a human manner. To
those who have chosen to think thus Christ will turn back the knowledge of the
mystery according to himself.
2. For it was the nature of man in Christ that was being honored and
sanctified; for that, as far as it pertains to being, he would neither have
needed baptism, nor indeed to partake of the Holy Spirit, the fact that he
himself is the supplier of the Spirit will especially suffice for full
assurance.
