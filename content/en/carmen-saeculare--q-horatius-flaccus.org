#+BEGIN_QUOTE
Carmen Saeculare edited by Paul Shorey
#+END_QUOTE

Phoebus and Diana, powerful of woods,
bright glory of the sky, O to be worshiped
always and worshiped, grant what we pray
in the sacred time,

in which Sibylline verses have warned

chosen virgins and chaste boys
to speak a song to the gods to whom
the seven hills have been pleasing.

Kindly Sun, who with shining chariot
bring forth and hide the day, and other and the same

are born, may you be able to see nothing greater
than the city of Rome!

Gentle to open ripe births
in due rite, Ilithyia, protect mothers,
whether you approve to be called Lucina

or Genitalis:

Goddess, may you lead forth offspring and prosper
the decrees of the fathers concerning women
to be yoked and the marital law

fertile of new progeny,

that a sure cycle through ten times eleven years
may bring back songs and games

thrice in bright day and as often in pleasing
night, crowded.

And you, Fates, truthful to have sung

what once has been said, and may a stable bound
of things keep it, join now good fates
to those already accomplished.

May the earth, fertile of crops and of flock,
give Ceres a spiky crown;

may wholesome waters and the breezes of Jove
nourish the young.

Mild and calm with weapon put away
hear the suppliant boys, Apollo;
queen of stars, two-horned, hear,

Moon, the girls:

If Rome is your work, and Ilian
squadrons held the Etruscan shore,
a part ordered to change Lares and city

with a safe course,

for whom through burning Troy without fraud
chaste Aeneas, survivor of the fatherland,
paved a free way, about to give
more than what was left:

Gods, give honest manners to docile youth,

gods, quiet to calm old age,
to the Romulean race both wealth and offspring
and every glory.

And whatever he who is of the blood of Anchises and Venus,
famous, venerates you with white oxen,
let him obtain, prior to the warring one, mild
toward the lying enemy.

Already by sea and land the Mede fears
powerful hands and the Alban axes,
already the Scythae, lately proud, seek answers,

and the Indians.

Already Faith and Peace and Honor and ancient
Modesty and neglected Virtue dares
to return, and blessed Plenty appears

with full horn.

Phoebus, augur and comely with gleaming bow
and accepted by the nine Camenae,
who with healing art lifts tired
limbs of the body,

if he looks kindly on the Palatine citadels,

he prolongs Roman affairs and fortunate Latium
into another lustrum and an age always
better.

And she who holds Aventine and Algidus,
Diana cares for the prayers of the fifteen men

and applies friendly ears to the vows
of the boys.

That Jove and all the gods feel these things
I bring home a good and sure hope,
I the chorus taught both of Phoebus and of Diana

to speak praises.
