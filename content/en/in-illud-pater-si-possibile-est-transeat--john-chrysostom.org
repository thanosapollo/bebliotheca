* On that saying, Father, if it is possible, let this cup pass
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

On the text, "Father, if it is possible, let this cup pass from me;
nevertheless not as I will, but as you"; and against Marcionites and
Manichaeans; and that one must not rush upon dangers, but prefer the will of
God to every will.

=51.31=

αʹ. We lately gave a deep cut to those who seize and wish to overreach; not
that we might strike, but that we might correct; not hating the men, but
turning away from the wickedness. For a physician also cuts the sore, not
warring against the sick body, but fighting the disease and the wound. Come
then, today let us yield to them a little, so that they may breathe from the
pain, and not, being struck continually, leap away from the treatment. So also
physicians do: after the cuttings they apply plasters and drugs, and they
leave intervals of days, devising the things that soothe the pain.

Imitating these then, we too today, devising so as to harvest the benefit from
our discourse, let us also move the word concerning doctrines, sending the
word upon the things that have been read. For I think many are at a loss why
these things were said by Christ; and it is likely that heretics also, being
present, will leap upon what was said, and from this trip up many of the
simpler brethren. Therefore, that we may both wall off their assault, and free
those who are at a loss from tumult and confusion, taking in hand what was
said, let us dwell on the wording, and go down to the depth of the thoughts.
For the reading is not enough, if knowledge is not also present. For even the
eunuch of Candace was reading, but until the one who taught him what it was
that he was reading came, he harvested nothing great. Therefore, that you too
may not suffer the same, attend to what is said, stretch the mind, furnish me
a soul at leisure, let the eye be keen-sighted, the judgment intent; the soul
freed from worldly cares, so that we may not cast what is said among the
thorns, nor upon the rocks, nor by the wayside; but farming a deep and rich
field, we may reap the waving ear. For if you thus attend to what is said, you
will both make the labor lighter for us, and prepare the finding easier for
yourselves. What then is the thing that was read? Father, if it is possible,
let this cup pass from me. What in the world is it that he says?

For we must interpret more clearly what was said, and so bring on the
solution. What then is it that he says? Father, if it is possible, take the
cross away. What do you say? Does he not know whether this is possible or
impossible? And who would say this? And yet the words are of one who does not
know; for the addition of "If" is of one who doubts =51.32= ; but, as I said,
one must not attend to the words, but turn to the thoughts, and know the aim
of the speaker, and the cause, and the time, and gathering all things, thus
hunt the sense that lies stored within. How then would the unspeakable Wisdom,
who knows the Father as the Father knows the Son, have been ignorant of this?
For this knowledge concerning the Passion is not greater than the knowledge
concerning the essence, which he alone knows exactly. For as the Father knows
me, he says, I also know the Father. And why do I speak of the only-begotten
Son of God? For not even the prophets appear to have been ignorant of this,
but even knowing it clearly and proclaiming it beforehand with


much assurance, that this must come to be, and that it will certainly be. Hear
then how all of them in different ways announce the cross.

First the patriarch Jacob; for stretching the word toward him he says: From a
shoot, my son, you have gone up; showing the Virgin as a shoot, and the
undefiled character of Mary. Then making the cross clear he said: Having lain
down you slept as a lion, and as a lion's cub, who shall raise him? For he
called his death a sleep and a slumber, and he joined the resurrection to the
death, saying: Who shall raise him? No one else, but he himself. Therefore
Christ also said: I have authority to lay down my soul, and I have authority
to take it; and again: Destroy this temple and in three days I will raise it.
And what is, Having lain down you slept as a lion? For just as the lion is
terrible not only when awake, but even when sleeping; so also Christ was
terrible not only before the cross, but even on the cross itself, and in the
very end, and then he worked the great wonders, turning back the sun,
splitting rocks, shaking the earth, rending the veil, terrifying the wife of
Pilate, convicting Judas.

For then he said: I have sinned in betraying innocent blood. And the wife of
Pilate made it clear: Have nothing to do with this just man; for I have
suffered many things in a dream because of him. Then darkness was seizing the
inhabited world, and night appeared in midday; then death was wasting away,
and his tyranny was being dissolved; many =51.33= bodies of the saints who had
fallen asleep were raised. The patriarch, foretelling these things from above,
and showing that even when being crucified he will be terrible, Having lain
down, he says, you slept as a lion; and he did not say, You will sleep, but
You slept, because of what will certainly be. For it is a custom of the
prophets in many places to foretell the things that are to come as having
already happened. For just as it is not possible that the things that have
happened have not happened, so neither is it possible that this, though it is
still to come, not come to be. Therefore they proclaim the things to come
beforehand in the form of past time, showing through this that they are
unfailing and will certainly be. So also David, making the cross clear, said:
They dug my hands and my feet. He did not say, They will dig, but, They dug.
They counted all my bones. And he does not say this only, but also the things
done by the soldiers: They divided my garments among themselves, and upon my
clothing they cast a lot. And not this only, but that they also fed him gall,
and gave him vinegar to drink. For they gave, he says, gall into my food, and
for my thirst they gave me vinegar to drink. And another again says that they
struck him with a spear: For they shall look on him whom they pierced. And
Isaiah again in another way, proclaiming the cross beforehand, said: As a
sheep he was led to slaughter, and as a lamb before its shearer is voiceless,
so he does not open his mouth. In his humiliation his judgment was taken away.

βʹ. But you, observe for me how each of these speaks as of things past, making
clear through this tense what will certainly be and will unanswerably come
out. So also David, sketching this court, said: Why did nations rage, and
peoples meditate empty things? The kings of the earth stood by, and the rulers
were gathered together against the Lord and against his Christ. And he does
not speak of the court only, and the cross, and the things on the cross, but
also of the one who betrayed him, that he was a companion at meals and at
table; For he who eats, he says, my bread, magnified a heel against me.

And he also thus foretells the voice which he was about to send forth on the
cross, saying: My God, my God, why have you forsaken me? And the tomb again:
They set me in the lowest pit, in dark places, and in the shadow of death. And
the resurrection: For you will not leave my soul in Hades, nor will you give
your holy one to see corruption.


And the ascension: God went up with a shout, the Lord with the voice of a
trumpet. And the sitting at the right hand: The Lord said to my Lord, Sit at
my right hand, until I make your enemies a footstool of your feet. And Isaiah
also speaks the cause, saying that From the sins of my people he has come to
death; and that Since all have gone astray as sheep, therefore he is
sacrificed. Then he also brings on the achievement, saying: By his bruise
=51.34= we all were healed, and that He bore the sins of many. Then the
prophets both knew the cross, and the cause of the cross, and the things
accomplished from the cross, and the tomb, and the resurrection, and the going
up, and the betrayal, and the court, and they described all things with
exactness; and the one who sent them, and commanded them to say these things,
is himself ignorant? And who with a mind would say these things? Do you see
that one must not attend simply to the words? For this is not the only
difficulty, but what follows is more difficult still. For what does he say:
Father, If it is possible, let this cup pass from me? For he will be found not
only ignorant, but even declining the cross; for what he says is this: If it
is possible, he says, may I not fall into being crucified, nor be put to
death. And yet when Peter, the chief of the apostles, said this very thing to
him, Be it far from you, Lord, this shall not be to you, he rebuked him so
severely as to say, Get behind me, Satan, you are a stumbling-block to me,
because you do not think the things of God, but the things of men; and this
although a little before he had blessed him. But so absurd did it seem to him
not to be crucified, that he called him who had received the revelation from
the Father, who had been blessed, who had received the keys of the heavens,
Satan and a stumbling-block, and blamed him as not thinking the things of God,
because he said to him, Be it far from you, Lord, this shall not be to you,
the being crucified.

He then who so insulted the disciple, and used such a charge against him as
even to call him Satan after so many praises, because he was saying, Do not be
crucified, how did he himself wish not to be crucified? And how after these
things, sketching the image of the good shepherd, did he say that this
especially is a proof of his virtue, to be slain for the sheep, speaking thus:
I am the good shepherd; the good shepherd lays down his soul for the sheep?
And he did not even stop at this, but also added: But the hireling, and one
who is not a shepherd, sees the wolf coming, and leaves the sheep, and flees.
If then it belongs to the good shepherd to be slain, and to the hireling not
to wish to endure this, how does he himself, saying that he is a good
shepherd, beg not to be sacrificed? And how did he say: I lay down my soul of
myself? For if you lay it down of yourself, how do you beg another that you
may not lay it down? And how does Paul marvel at him on this account, speaking
thus: Who, being in the form of God, did not count it robbery to be equal with
God, but emptied himself, taking the form of a slave, becoming in the likeness
of men, and being found in fashion as a man, he humbled himself, becoming
obedient unto death, and that a death of a cross? And he himself again says
somehow thus: For this the Father loves me, because I lay down my soul, that I
may take it again. For if he does not wish it, but declines and begs the
Father, how is he loved because of this? For love belongs to things according
to will. And how does Paul say again: Love one another, even as Christ also
loved us, and gave himself up for us? And Christ himself again, being about to
be crucified, said: Father, the hour has come; glorify your Son, calling the
glory =51.35= the cross; and how here does he decline, and there even press it
on? For that the cross is glory, hear what the evangelist says: For the Holy
Spirit was not yet, because Jesus was not yet glorified. And what he says is
this: The grace had not yet been given, since the enmity toward men had not
yet been dissolved, because the cross had not yet gone forward.


For the cross dissolved the enmity of God toward men, brought the
reconciliations, made the earth heaven, mixed men with the angels, brought
down the citadel of death, unstrung the strength of the devil, made the power
of sin vanish, freed the earth from error, brought truth back, drove out the
demons, pulled down temples, overturned altars, made the sacrificial steam
vanish, planted virtue, rooted the Churches. The cross, the will of the
Father, the glory of the Son, the exultation of the Spirit, the boast of Paul;
For far be it from me, he says, to boast, except in the cross of our Lord
Jesus Christ. The cross, brighter than the sun, more radiant than the ray; for
when that one is darkened, then this shines; and the sun is darkened then not
by being made to vanish, but by being conquered by the radiance of the cross.
The cross tore up our handwriting, made the prison of death useless, the cross
is the proof of the love of God.

For God so loved the world, that he gave his only-begotten Son, that everyone
who believes in him may not perish. And again Paul: For if, being enemies, we
were reconciled to God through the death of his Son. The cross, the
unbreakable wall, the unconquerable weapon, the safety of the rich, the plenty
of the poor, the wall of those plotted against, the weapon of those warred
upon, the destruction of the passions, the acquisition of virtue, the wondrous
and paradoxical sign. For this generation seeks a sign; and a sign shall not
be given it, except the sign of Jonah. And again Paul: Since Jews also ask a
sign, and Greeks seek wisdom; but we preach Christ crucified. The cross opened
paradise, brought in a robber, and led the race of men, which was about to
perish and was not even worthy of the earth, by the hand to the kingdom of the
heavens. So many goods both have come and come from the cross, and he does not
wish to be crucified, tell me? And who would say this? And if he did not wish
it, who compelled him? who forced him? And why did he even send prophets ahead
announcing that he will be crucified, if he was not going to be crucified, nor
wishing to endure this? And for what reason does he also call the cross a cup,
if he did not wish to be crucified? For this belongs to one showing the desire
which he has concerning the matter. For just as the cup is sweet to those who
thirst, so also to him was the being crucified; therefore he also said: With
desire I have desired to eat this Passover with you, not saying this simply,
but for this cause, since after the evening the cross was waiting to receive
him.

γʹ. He then who both calls the matter glory, and rebukes the disciple because
he was hindering him, and shows the good shepherd from this, from being slain
for the sheep, and says that he desires with desire =51.36= the matter, and
comes to this willingly, how does he beg that this not come to be? And if he
did not wish it, what was hard about preventing those who were coming upon
this? But now you even see him running upon the matter. For when they came
upon him, he says: Whom do you seek? And they say: Jesus. And he says to them:
Behold, I am he; and they went backward and fell to the ground. Thus, having
first blinded them and shown that he was able to escape, then he gave himself
up, so that you may learn that he did not endure this unwillingly by
necessity, nor by force, nor by the tyranny of those who came upon him, but
willingly, and choosing and wishing, and disposing these things from long
before. Therefore also prophets were sent ahead, and patriarchs foretold, and
through words and through deeds the cross was typed beforehand. For the
slaughter of Isaac also made the cross clear to us; therefore he also said:
Abraham your father rejoiced that he might see my glory, and he saw, and was
glad. Then the patriarch was glad seeing the image of the cross, and he
himself declines the matter? And


Moses also thus prevailed over Amalek, since he was displaying the type of the
cross; and one might see countless things happening in the Old Testament,
prefiguring the cross. For what reason then did it so happen, if the one who
was going to be crucified did not wish this to happen? And what comes after
this is still more difficult. For having said, Let this cup pass from me, he
added, Nevertheless not as I will, but as you. For from this, as far as the
wording, we find two wills opposed to one another, if the Father wishes him to
be crucified, and he himself does not wish it. And yet everywhere we see him
wishing the same things as the Father, choosing the same things. For even when
he says, Give them, as I and you are one, that they also may be one in us, he
says nothing else except that the mind of Father and Son is one. And when he
says, The words which I speak, I do not speak, but the Father who remains in
me, he does these works, he shows this.

And when he says, I have not come of myself, and, I cannot do anything of
myself, he does not say this as showing that he has been deprived of authority
either to speak or to do (far from it!), but wishing to show with exactness
that his mind is in agreement, both in words and in deeds and in all
economies, being one and the same toward the Father, as we have already often
demonstrated. For the, I do not speak of myself, is not a taking away of
authority, but a proof of agreement. How then does he say here, Nevertheless
not as I will, but as you? Perhaps we have thrown you into a great struggle,
but rise up; for even if the things said are many, I know well that your
eagerness is young; for the word is now hastening toward the very solution.
For what reason then has it been so said? Attend with exactness. The word of
the incarnation was very hard to receive. For the excess of his philanthropy,
and the greatness of the condescension, was full of shuddering, and needed
much pre =51.37= paration, so as to be received. For consider how great it was
to hear and learn that God, the unspeakable, the incorruptible, the
inconceivable, the invisible, the incomprehensible, in whose hand are the ends
of the earth, Who looks upon the earth and makes it tremble, who touches the
mountains and they smoke, whose inclination of condescension not even the
Cherubim were able to bear, but hid their faces with the projection of the
wings; this one who surpasses every mind, and conquers every reasoning, having
passed by angels, archangels, all the intellectual powers above, consented to
become man, and to take up flesh molded from earth and clay, and to come into
a virgin womb, and to be carried for a nine-month time, and to be nursed with
milk, and to suffer all human things. Since then what was going to be was so
paradoxical, that even when it had happened it was disbelieved by many, he
first sends prophets ahead, announcing this very thing. For the patriarch also
proclaimed it beforehand, saying, From a shoot, my son, you have gone up;
having lain down you slept as a lion; and Isaiah saying, Behold the virgin
shall have in the womb, and shall bear a son, and they shall call his name
Emmanuel; and another again, We saw him as a child, as a root in a thirsty
land. And he calls the virgin womb a thirsty land, because it did not receive
a man's seed, nor enjoy intercourse, but bore him without marriage.

And again, A child was born to us, a son also was given to us; and again, A
rod shall come out of the root of Jesse, and a flower shall go up from the
root. And Baruch in Jeremiah says: This is our God; no other shall be reckoned
beside him; he found out every way of knowledge, and gave it to Jacob his
child, and to Israel his beloved. After these things he was seen upon the
earth, and consorted with men. And David, making his incarnate presence clear,
said:


He shall come down as rain upon a fleece, and as a drop that drips upon the
earth, because he has entered the virgin womb without noise and without
disturbance.

δʹ. But these things alone were not enough, but even when he had come, so that
what happened might not be thought a fantasy, he confirms the matter not by
sight only, but also by much time, and by going through all human things. For
he does not simply enter into a man already completed and filled out, but into
a virgin womb, so as to endure both gestation and births, and nursing with
milk, and growth, and by the length of time and the difference of all the ages
to confirm what happened. And not even here do the things of the demonstration
stop only, but even carrying about the flesh, he lets it endure the defects of
nature, both to hunger and to thirst and to sleep and to grow weary; finally
even coming to the cross, he lets it suffer the things of the flesh.

For this reason also streams of sweats were being carried down from it, and an
angel was found holding it up, and he is grieved and is in anguish; for even
before saying these things, he says: My soul is troubled, and is very
sorrowful unto death. If then, when all these things had happened, the wicked
mouth of the devil through Marcion of Pontus and Valentinus, and Manichaeus
the =51.38= Persian, and many other heresies, attempted to overturn the word
concerning the economy, and sounded some satanic sound, saying that he was not
even incarnate, nor put on flesh, but this was an appearance and a fantasy,
and a stage-play and a hypocrisy, although the sufferings were crying out, the
death, the tomb, the hunger; if none of these had happened, how would the
devil not much more have sown these wicked doctrines of impiety? For this
reason, just as he hungered, just as he slept, just as he grew weary, just as
he ate, just as he drank, so also he declines death, showing the human, and
the weakness of the nature which does not bear being torn without passion from
the present life. For if he had said none of these things, one would have been
able to say that if he was a man, he ought to have suffered the things of the
man. And what are these? That one who is about to be crucified both fears and
is in agony, and is not torn without passion from the present life; for the
affection toward present things is laid in nature; for this reason, wishing to
show the clothing of the flesh to be true, and to confirm the economy, he sets
forth the sufferings naked with much demonstration. This then is one account;
and there is also another not less than this. And what is this? Christ, having
come, wished to train men in every virtue; and the one who trains does not
teach by word only, but also by deed; for this is the best teaching of the
teacher. For a helmsman also, seating the disciple beside him, shows him how
he holds the tillers, and also adds word to the deed, and neither only speaks,
nor only works; likewise also a builder, setting beside him the one who is
going to learn from him how a wall is woven, shows it to him through the deed,
and shows it to him also through the word; so also a weaver, and an
embroiderer, and a goldsmith, and a coppersmith, and every art has the teacher
through both words and deeds. Since then he himself also came to train us in
every virtue, for this reason he both says the things that must be done, and
does them. For he who has done, he says, and taught, this one shall be called
great in the kingdom of the heavens.

But consider: he commanded them to be humble-minded and meek, and he taught
through the words. See how he trains these also through the deeds. For having
said, Blessed are the poor in spirit, blessed are the meek, he shows how one
must accomplish them. How then did he teach? Taking a towel he girded himself,
and washed the feet of the disciples. What is equal to this humility? For he
no longer trains this through the words only, but also through the deeds.
Again gentleness and forbearance


he teaches through the deeds. How? He was struck by the servant of the high
priest, and he says: If I spoke badly, bear witness concerning the bad; but if
well, why do you beat me? He commanded to pray for enemies; again he trains
this also through the deeds; for having gone up on the cross he says: Father,
forgive them; for they do not know what they do. Just as then he commanded to
pray, so he himself also prays, training you to pray, not himself being una
=51.39= ble to forgive. Again he commanded to do good to those who hate us,
and to do well to those who despitefully use us; and this he did through the
deeds; for he cast out the demons of the Jews who were calling him
demon-possessed, he was doing good to those who were driving him out, he was
feeding those who were plotting, he was leading by the hand into the kingdom
those who wished to crucify him. He said again to the disciples, Acquire
neither gold, nor silver, nor copper into your belts, anointing them toward
possessionlessness; this again he trained through the deeds, speaking thus:
The foxes have dens, and the birds of the heaven nests; but the Son of man has
nowhere to lay his head. And he had no table, no house, nor any other such
thing; not because he was in want, but because he was training men to come
this way. According to this manner then he was also teaching them to pray.

They said then to him: Teach us to pray. For this reason he also prays, so
that they may learn to pray. But they needed to learn not only to pray, but
also how one must pray; for this reason he also handed down a prayer having
thus: Our Father who are in the heavens, hallowed be your name; your kingdom
come; your will be done, as in heaven, also on earth. Give us today our daily
bread; and forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors; and lead us
not into temptation, that is, into danger, into plots. Since then he commanded
to pray, Lead us not into temptation, he teaches them this very thing through
the matter itself, saying, Father, if it is possible, let this cup pass from
me, training all the saints not to rush upon dangers, nor to throw themselves,
but to wait for them when they come on, and to display all courage; yet not
themselves to leap forward, nor to go first to meet the terrible things. Why
then? Both teaching humility, and freeing them from the charges of vainglory.
For this reason here also, when he was saying these things, Having gone away,
he says, he prayed; and after the praying he says to the disciples thus: Were
you not able to watch one hour with me? Watch and pray not to enter into
temptation. =51.40= Do you see that he does not only pray, but also exhorts?
For the spirit is willing, he says, but the flesh is weak. And he said this,
emptying their soul of pride, and freeing them from folly, making them
contracted, preparing them to be moderate. What then he wished to teach them
to pray, this he also himself prayed humanly, not according to the divinity
(for the Divine is without passion), but according to the humanity. And he
prayed training us to pray, and always to seek release from the terrible
things; but if it should not be possible, to be content with the things that
seem good to God.

For this reason he said: Nevertheless not as I will, but as you; not because
his will is one thing, and the Father's another; but so that he may train men,
even if they are in agony, even if they tremble, even if danger comes on, even
if they do not wish to be torn from the present life, nevertheless to prefer
the will of God to their own will; just as Paul also, having been trained,
showed both of these through the deeds; for he asked that the temptations be
moved away from him, speaking thus: For this I begged the Lord three times;
and since it did not seem good to God, he says: Therefore I am well pleased in
weaknesses, in insults, in persecutions. And perhaps what has been said is
unclear; therefore I make it clearer: Paul was in many dangers, and he prayed
not to be in danger. He heard then Christ saying, My grace is sufficient for
you; for my power in weakness


is perfected. Since then he saw that it was the will of God, thereafter he
subjects his own will to the will of God. These two things then he trained
from this prayer, neither to rush upon dangers, but also to pray not to fall
into them; and if they should come on, to bear them nobly, and to prefer the
will of God to one's own will.

Knowing these things then, let us pray never to enter into temptation; and if
we should enter, let us beg God to give us endurance and courage, and let us
prefer his will to every will of ours. For thus we shall both finish the
present life with safety, and attain the goods to come; which may it be that
we all obtain, by the grace and philanthropy of our Lord Jesus Christ, with
whom to the Father, together with the Holy Spirit, be glory, might, honor, now
and always, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
