FROM THE COMMENTARIES ON HOSEA.
=13.825= Since the things said solecistically according to Scripture, as many
as according to the wording confuse the one who encounters them, so as to
suppose, not rightly, nor consistently, nor as it is, that the sayings have
been written; so that some even dare, on the pretext of correction, to alter
them, transposing the sense that lies in the sayings that seem to have been
written consistently; necessarily here too, the like having fallen out, so far
as concerns the wording about the present sayings, let us see the sense that
lies in them. For having spoken in the plural, They wept and they entreated
me; and again having indicated the next in the plural, Being in the house,
they found me, he adds the next upon the singular, saying: And there it was
spoken to him. For someone looking to the bare wording, thinking it has been
written in error, will either write even the last also in the plural; or would
change the preced =13.828= ing things into the singular. For having read,
They wept and they entreated me; and, Being in the house, they found me, he
would say that the next also then is, There it was spoken to them; that is, to
those who wept and entreated, and being in the house, found God. But from
observation of like sayings we shall show that this too has been said
consistently. In Genesis God, giving a commandment to Adam, speaks thus: From
every tree that is in the paradise you shall eat for food; but from the tree
of knowing good and evil, you shall not eat from it; but on whatever day you
eat from it, you shall die by death. For having begun there too from the
singular in having said, of every tree that is in the paradise you shall eat
for food, he adds in the plural, saying, but from the tree of knowing good and
evil, you shall not eat from it; but on whatever day you eat from it, you
shall die by death. For when it is concerning the commandment through which he
wished him, keeping this, to be in life, he commands in the singular, and
says: From every tree that is in the paradise you shall eat for food. For
those who walk according to God and hold to his commandments, even if they be
many, by being of one mind the many are one. Therefore when a commandment
concerning good is given, it is said in the singular, you shall eat for food;
but when already it distinguishes concerning transgression, not in the
singular, but already in the plural he says: You shall not eat from it; but on
whatever day you eat, you shall die by death. Thus then also here, when they
still weep and entreat toward God, it is said in the plural, They wept and
they entreated; but when they find God, he no longer says in the plural, There
it was spoken to them, but, to him. For already the many have become one in
having found God, and in hearing his word. For the one, when he sins, is
manifold, being split off from God and divided, and falling out of unity; but
the many following the commandments of God are one; as also the Apostle
testifies, saying: That we the many are one bread and one body; and again: One
God, and one Christ, and one faith, and one baptism; and elsewhere: That we
all are one in Christ Jesus; and again: For I betrothed you all to one
husband, to present a chaste virgin to the Lord. And that those who are
well-pleasing to the Lord are one, it is possible to see from the prayer which
the Savior prays to the Father concerning his disciples; For, he says, Holy
Father, grant that, as I and you are one, so also these may be one in us. And
when the saints are also said to be members of one another, what else are they
if not one body? And in the Shepherd also, the building of the tower being
built through many stones, yet the building appearing to be from one stone,
what else does the Scripture signify than the concord from many and unity?
