* Refutation of Proclus
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

From the refutations against the theological chapters of Proclus, a
refutation of Procopius of Gaza of chapter 140.
A divine procession without beginning and without end would be said to
be only the generation of the one and only Son from the one and only
Father, and from the same the sending-forth of the one and only Holy
Spirit, according to which the paternal and originating, or rather
pre-beginning, monad, having been moved into the dyad contemplated in
the Son and the Spirit, stood as far as Trinity, as our Theologian
Gregory says. And this also is the only procession by divine nature, if
indeed one must speak of nature in the case of the supernatural and
divine; and of this the beginning without beginning is the Father, for
it is from him; the middle co-beginning is the Son, for it is through
him; and the most-beyond-complete and endless end is the Holy Spirit,
for it is into him; and this is also exempted from the other
processions; for it does not at all make the things from it stand
outside the beginning, but keeps them altogether not going forth from
it, because of the identity of essence; even if they proceed as proper
persons; wherefore they do not even need the return according to
desire; but all the processions of beings besides this are co-divided
with the beings; and none of these is without beginning, because
neither is anything of beings without beginning, but all things have
come to be from the Father through the Son, completed by the Spirit;
and not every one is without end; nor indeed altogether, but of the
everlasting things remaining after the production from there, the one
according to essence has in fact been completed; for neither are other
things besides those produced from the beginning produced in addition;
nor do those very things that were produced, undergoing the change
according to essence, again need another production; but the one
according to illumination proceeds from there everlasting and without
end, to some as first-given, to others as second-given, and so on; the
divine radiances being poured out according to communication,
analogously to the orders, or rather stations, of each, even as far as
the last among those; and thus also all things turn back toward the one
beginning in an upward-tending way; and the everlasting and endless
circle is preserved for them both in the processions and in the
returns; but that of the things under generation and corruption and
time, according to the universal, has already been completed. For in
six days the whole sensible cosmos has been completed; and on the
seventh God rested from all his works; and a procession of another
cosmos is no longer still expected to be completed at all, according to
the consummation of the present age; but according to the particular,
it is still being worked, as has been said by the Savior: "My Father
works until now, and I work;" but it will altogether also cease at the
common consummation; and then there will be another procession,
according to which God will be all in all; and all things will turn
back toward him, the trans-elementing of the wholes, and the remaking
from corruption into incorruption.
