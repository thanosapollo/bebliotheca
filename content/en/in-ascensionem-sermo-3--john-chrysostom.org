On the Ascension of our Lord Jesus Christ. Sermon 3.
=52.797=
The theater of the Church is bright for me, not producing vain laughter
for men, but a vehement lament for the devil. For he sees above the
resurrection-root of the dead below; he sees the one delivered by him
to the cross below, looking down on him from heaven; he sees the earth
filled with angels; he sees the air being walked upon paradoxically; he
sees the heavenly powers coming to meet him, and some saying: Lift up
your gates, you rulers, and the King of glory shall enter in, and
others again coming to meet him and shielding him: The Lord is strong
and mighty in battle. In what battle? The one he undertook for us
against the common enemy, whom no one of men, of prophets, of the
righteous, has been able to throw in wrestling.
For all had been tyrannized under Death who was then reigning, until
the King of the ages arrived, and having bound the Strong One, took his
vessels. For when the Lord was being crucified, the powers of the
heavens were astonished, and all creation was shaken, seeing the common
mystery and the fearful spectacle taking place upon the earth. For the
earth trembled, the sea was moved, the abyss was tossed with waves, all
creation was thrown into confusion; the luminaries of the heavens were
afraid, the sun and the moon fled, the stars failed, day did not
endure, an angel leapt out of the temple in confusion, the veil of the
house having been torn; darkness filled the earth, the elements turned
away, and the day was altered; for they could not bear seeing their own
Master and maker hanging upon a tree. Then Hades was overthrown, and
his gates were crushed, and his bars were broken. Concerning which
David, prophesying, said, For he crushed gates of bronze, he broke bars
of iron. Then Death who was reigning fell under the feet of Christ, and
was dragged as a captive being led in triumph. For having emptied the
Enemy's strength with the unchangeable and noble body, and having
crushed his devices, he ran down into the kingdoms of Hades, and pulled
down his throne, took down the diadem, dissolved the scepters, and
uttered a voice, saying: Come, all you families of the nations, to the
Father and God; come, and receive freedom from slavery. For I lead you
forth from slavery into freedom, from darkness into light, from tyranny
into a kingdom. For I, Christ, proclaim life as good news to you.
Therefore no longer fear the tyrant, but despise fear and death, famine
and affliction, bonds and prison and distress. For these things the
army of the tyrant has feared, which has been overthrown; but I alone
of all am the victorious king, who also suffered for you in the flesh.
This one then is truly the strong and mighty Lord; this one is the
mighty in battle; this one is the King of glory, who leads out those
bound in manliness, those dwelling in tombs; to whom David said: You
have gone up on high, you have led captivity captive. Every feast of
Christians, then, is a condemnation of the devil; but this one
especially. For in all the others, having stripped his own wickedness
bare, and having benefited himself in nothing, he was led in triumph.
What sort of thing I mean: When the Virgin was conceiving
paradoxically, he spread the rumor of the deed of adultery; when she
had given birth unspeakably, he slandered her as not being a virgin;
when the one who feeds all creation was being reared, he taught Herod
to murder the infants, thinking to seize in them the one who cannot be
held; when he was hastening to baptism, he leads him back to
temptation; when he was discoursing with the Jews, he caused him to be
blasphemed; when he was working wonders, he arranged for him to be
stoned, he preferred the murderer and robber instead of him; when he
was crucified, he crucified robbers with him; when he was buried, he
sealed the =52.798= tomb; when he rose, he slanders the resurrection;
but when he was being taken up and going away into the heavens, he had
nothing to utter, but wounded his own head the more.
Therefore today's feast has been established as more grievous to him
than every festival, seeing the whole army of the angels dancing
everywhere, and some following, others running ahead, others coming to
meet, others again speaking with the choir of the apostles, and saying:
Men of Galilee, why do you stand looking into heaven? This Jesus who
was taken up from you into heaven shall so come again in the manner you
saw him going into heaven; this Jesus who displayed the multitude of
signs. The day of the Master's ascension, then, as has been said,
prepared the devil to lament, but the faithful to be made bright. For
now the delightful spring has risen, and the beauties of the flowers
have grown up; the vine-shoots are heavy with fruits, the olive trees
bloom, the fig trees have brought forth their early figs, and the
thick-standing grain-fields are moved by the west wind, imitating the
delight of the waves of the sea; all things with us are made bright at
the Master's ascension. Come then, I too will strike up among you the
words of David, which he was just now crying out to us on account of
the Master's ascension: All you nations, clap your hands, shout to God
with a voice of gladness. The Lord went up with the voice of a trumpet,
where he was; he was taken up, whence he was not separated. For he who
came down is himself also the one who went up above the heavens. Not
another who appeared to the prophets, and another who spent time with
the apostles; not another who is in the bosom of the Father, and
another who was judged before Pilate; not another who was nailed with
the nails upon the cross, and another who rides upon the Cherubim; not
another who was wrapped by Joseph in the linen cloth, and another who
grasps creation with his palm; not another who was laid in the tomb,
and another who is hymned by the Seraphim; but he himself sitting
together with the Father, who sprouted seedlessly in the virginal womb:
God has gone up with a shout, the Lord with the voice of a trumpet; the
maker of the ages, who brought all things from not being into being,
the fashioner of Adam, the one who brought forth human nature, who
translated well-pleasing Enoch into the place of life, who guarded Noah
in the midst of the inhabited world, who called the patriarch Abraham
from the land of the Chaldeans, who prefigured in Isaac the mystery of
the cross, who granted to Jacob the twelve-branched offspring, who
furnished endurance to Job, who set Moses forward as leader of the
people, who filled Samuel from the womb with prophecy, who chose David
from the prophets as king, who furnished wisdom to Solomon, who took
Elijah up in a whirlwind on a fiery chariot, who breathed foreknowledge
into the prophets, who granted to the apostles the gift of healings,
who cried out to the same: Take courage, I have overcome the world.
This is the Lord of glory, who was taken up into the heavens with a
shout, and sat at the right hand of the Father. And angels and
authorities and powers having been subjected to him, may he himself
take up our laborious prayers, and show us victors (sic) over the
trials of the world; he will subject to us the whole phalanx of unclean
spirits, saying to us: Behold, I have given you the authority to tread
upon serpents and scorpions.
Therefore he will keep us unharmed and blameless, healthy and whole in
soul and body and spirit, filled with fruits of righteousness and
well-doing, the God of all, who has deemed us worthy to be =52.799=
gathered together, and to accomplish the saving feast; because to him
belongs all glory, honor and worship, to the =52.800= Father and to
the Son and to the Holy Spirit, now and always, and unto the ages of
ages. Amen.
