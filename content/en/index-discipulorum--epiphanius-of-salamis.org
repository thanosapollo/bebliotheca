Concerning the seventy apostles.
αʹ. James, the brother of the Lord according to the flesh, who was also
surnamed the Just and was ordained first bishop of Jerusalem, being struck
with stones by the Jews in Jerusalem itself, fell asleep and was buried
there in the temple near the priests.
βʹ. Timothy, who was made bishop of Ephesus by Paul, preached the gospel of
the Lord Jesus Christ in Illyricum and in all Greece, beginning from
Ephesus; there, then, he died, and there he was buried gloriously.
γʹ. Titus, the bishop of Crete, preached the gospel of Christ in Crete
itself and in the islands round about, and having died there he was buried
gloriously.
δʹ. Barnabas, who ministered the word with Paul, first preached Christ in
Rome; afterward he became bishop of Milan.
εʹ. Ananias, who baptized Saint Paul, became bishop of Damascus.
ς ʹ. Stephen the protomartyr, one of the seven deacons, was perfected by
being stoned by the Jews in Jerusalem, as Luke testifies in the Acts of the
Apostles.
119 ζʹ. Philip, himself also one of the seven, who also baptized Simon and
the eunuch, became bishop in Tralles of Asia.
ηʹ. Prochorus, himself also one of the seven, became bishop of Nicomedia
in Bithynia.
θʹ. Nicanor, himself also one of the seven, likewise ended his life on the
very day on which his fellow deacon and protomartyr Stephen also did,
together with two thousand others of those who had hoped in Christ.
ιʹ. Simon, himself also one of the seven, who also became bishop of Bostra
of the Arabs; he was burned by the pagans.
ιαʹ. Parmenas, himself also one of the seven: this man died in the sight of
the apostles in his ministry.
ιβʹ. Nicolas, himself also one of the seven: this man became bishop of
Samaria; but having fallen into heterodoxy together with Simon, he fell
away.
ιγʹ. Cleopas, who is also Symeon, the cousin of the Lord, who also saw him
and spoke with him, having traveled together with Luke as well, after he
had risen from the dead, as is written in the 120 Gospel; this man became
second bishop of Jerusalem.
ιδʹ. Silas, who ministered the word with Paul, became bishop in Corinth.
ιεʹ. Crescens, of whom the apostle Paul makes mention in the Second
Epistle to Timothy, who also became bishop of Chalcedon of those in Gaul,
preached the gospel of Christ there; and under the emperor Trajan he both
suffered martyrdom and was buried there.
ις ʹ. Epaenetus, of whom the apostle makes mention in the Epistle to the
Romans, became bishop of Carthage.
ιζʹ. Andronicus, of whom the apostle Paul also makes mention in the
Epistle to the Romans, became bishop of Pannonia.
ιηʹ. Amplias, of whom Paul also makes mention in the Epistle to the
Romans, became bishop of Odessus.
ιθʹ. Urbanus, of whom Paul also makes mention in the Epistle to the
Romans, became bishop of Macedonia.
κʹ. Stachys, of whom Paul also makes mention in the same epistle, was
appointed first bishop of Byzantium by Andrew the apostle in Argyropolis of
Thrace.
καʹ. Apelles, of whom 121 Paul also makes mention in the same epistle,
became bishop of Smyrna before Saint Polycarp.
κβʹ. Aristobulus, of whom Paul also makes mention in the same epistle,
became bishop of Britain.
κγʹ. Narcissus, of whom the same also makes mention in the same epistle,
became bishop of Athens.
κδʹ. Herodion, of whom the same also makes mention in the same epistle,
became bishop of Patras.
κεʹ. Rufus, of whom the same also makes mention in the same epistle, became
bishop of Thebes.
κς ʹ. Asyncritus, of whom the same also makes mention in the same epistle,
became bishop of Hyrcania.
κζʹ. Phlegon, of whom the same also makes mention in the same epistle,
became bishop of Marathon.
κηʹ. Hermes, of whom the same apostle also makes mention in the same
epistle, became bishop of Dalmatia.
κθʹ. Hermas, of whom the same apostle also makes mention in the same
epistle, became bishop of the city of Philippi.
122 λʹ. Patrobas, of whom the same apostle Paul also makes mention, became
bishop at Ne Puteoli.
λαʹ. Agabus, who is mentioned in the Acts of the Apostles: this man was
also counted worthy of the prophetic gift.
λβʹ. Linus, of whom the apostle Paul also makes mention, became first
bishop of Rome after Peter the chief.
λγʹ. Gaius, of whom the apostle Paul also makes mention, became bishop of
Ephesus after Timothy.
λδʹ. Philologus, of whom Paul also makes mention, became bishop of Sinope
under Andrew.
λεʹ. Olympas, of whom Paul also makes mention: this man suffered martyrdom
in Rome together with Peter the chief, having his head cut off.
λ ςʹ. Rhodion, of whom Paul also makes mention: he likewise was perfected
by martyrdom in Rome with Peter and Olympas, himself also having his head
cut off.
ληʹ. Jason, of whom Paul also makes mention, became bishop of Tarsus. 123
λθʹ. Sosipater, of whom Paul likewise also makes mention, became bishop of
Iconium.
μʹ. Lucius, of whom Paul also makes mention, became bishop in Laodicea of
Syria.
μαʹ. Tertius, who wrote that same Epistle of Paul to the Romans, became
second bishop of Iconium.
μβʹ. Erastus, of whom Paul also makes mention in the same epistle, became
steward of the church in Jerusalem, then became bishop of Paneas.
μγʹ. Phygellus, of whom Paul also makes mention as having fallen into
heterodoxy and having held the opinions of Simon, became bishop of Ephesus.
μδʹ. Hermogenes, of whom Paul likewise also makes mention as having fallen
into heterodoxy, became bishop in Megara.
μεʹ. Demas, of whom the apostle Paul likewise also makes mention as having
fallen away; for he makes it clear concerning the three in his Second
Epistle to Timothy, that Phygellus and Hermogenes, having fallen into
heterodoxy, opposed his teaching. But Demas, having loved the present age,
left the evangelical word, and in Thessalonica became a priest of the
idols. Concerning these John the apostle also wrote: they went out from us,
but they were not of us.
μς ʹ. Quartus, of whom Paul also makes mention in the Epistle to the
Romans, became bishop of Berytus.
124 μζʹ. Apollos, of whom Paul also makes mention in the Epistle to the
Corinthians, became bishop of Caesarea.
μηʹ. Cephas, whom the apostle Paul rebuked in Antioch, having discoursed
with him, a namesake of Peter, became bishop of Conia.
μθʹ. Sosthenes, of whom Paul also makes mention, became bishop of
Colophonias.
νʹ. Epaphroditus, of whom Paul also makes mention, became bishop of
Adriace.
ναʹ. Caesar, of whom Paul also makes mention, became bishop of
Dyrrhachium.
νβʹ. Mark the cousin of Barnabas, of whom Paul also makes mention, became
bishop of Apollonia.
νγʹ. Joseph, who is also called Justus and Barsabbas, of whom Luke makes
mention in the Acts of the Apostles as having been put to the lot with
Matthias, became bishop of Eleutheropolis.
νδʹ. Artemas, of whom Paul also makes mention, became bishop in Lystra.
νεʹ. Clement, of whom Paul also makes mention, saying: with Clement and
the rest of my fellow workers, who also was the first 125 of the Gentiles
and Greeks to believe in Christ, became bishop of Sardica.
νς ʹ. Onesiphorus, of whom Paul also makes mention, became bishop of
Coroneia.
νζʹ. Tychicus, of whom Paul also makes mention, became first bishop of
Chalcedon in Bithynia.
νηʹ. Carpus, of whom Paul also makes mention, became bishop of Beroea of
Thrace.
νθʹ. Euodius, of whom Paul also makes mention, became first bishop of
Antioch after Peter the chief.
ξʹ. Philemon, to whom Paul wrote a letter of his own, became bishop of
Gaza.
ξαʹ. Zenas the lawyer, of whom Paul also makes mention, became bishop of
Diospolis.
ξβʹ. Aquila, of whom Paul also makes mention, became bishop of Heraclea.
ξγʹ. Priscas, of whom Paul also makes mention, became bishop of Colophon.
ξδʹ. Junias, of whom Paul also makes mention, became bishop of Apamea of
Syria.
126 ξεʹ. Mark, who is also called John, of whom Luke makes mention in the
Acts of the Apostles, became bishop of Byblos.
ξς ʹ. Aristarchus, of whom Paul also makes mention.
ξζʹ. Pudens, of whom Paul also makes mention.
ξηʹ. Trophimus, of whom the apostle Paul also makes mention. These three,
having suffered together with the apostle Paul in all his persecutions,
last of all
were also perfected through martyrdom together with him under Nero, having
their heads cut off together with him. With these being numbered also Mark
and Luke the evangelist together
οʹ. Of Saint Epiphanius, Archbishop of Cyprus.
These are the names of the twelve apostles.
αʹ. Peter: this man was also called Simon and Cephas and Symeon.
βʹ. Andrew his brother.
γʹ. James. δʹ. John: these were called Boanerges, which is interpreted
sons of thunder.
εʹ. Philip.
ςʹ. Bartholomew.
ζʹ. Matthew the tax collector.
ηʹ. Thomas: this man was also called Didymus.
θʹ. James of Alphaeus.
ιʹ. Thaddaeus: this man is called Lebbaeus in Matthew, but in Luke Judas of
James, and in Mark Levi sitting at the tax office.
ιαʹ. Simon the Cananite.
ιβʹ. Judas Iscariot, and in his place Matthias entered. And brothers:
Peter and Andrew, sons of Anna, and James and John, sons of Zebedee, James
and Judas, sons of Alphaeus, fishermen. Peter and Andrew, James and John.
James who is called the brother of the Lord, which one he was who became
bishop of Jerusalem, surnamed the Just: the one was a son of Zebedee and
the other of Alphaeus, and the other of Joseph the betrothed, as the Jews
used to say. Simon and Judas, Jose the brother: Matthew says they are
called tax collectors, and Luke that Levi also was called a tax collector.
And Jonas is interpreted dove.
