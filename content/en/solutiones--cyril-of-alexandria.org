FIRST CHAPTER OF THE SOLUTION OF DOGMATIC QUESTIONS PROPOSED TO THE MOST
HOLY CYRIL. Whether the God over all, hands and feet and eyes and ears also
and wings being named of him by the divine scripture, ought thus to be
understood by us, not anthropomorphically as the members are taken of a
body; for he is incorporeal; but as these things are of the substance, so
also the things said to be of the substance, being divine members and
themselves according to the substance.

SOLUTION. Being of sound mind, and in the words concerning the unutterable
divinity casting in the eye of their own mind sparingly, they see it
existing beyond every generated thing, and surpassing every keenness of mind
and of bodily imagination, being altogether beyond these, and as the
all-wise Paul says, dwelling in light unapproachable. But if the light
concerning it is unapproachable, how could anyone scrutinize it? For we see
in a mirror darkly, and we know in part. The divine therefore is altogether
incorporeal, without part and without magnitude, and not in circumscribed
form. 550 But how could that which so exists in its own nature be conceived
of parts and members? For if anyone should grant this to be true, it is no
longer understood as incorporeal. For that which is altogether in shape is
certainly also in quantity, and that in quantity also in place; and that
conceived in place is not outside circumscription. These things are
properties of bodies, and utterly alien to the incorporeal nature.
Therefore neither eyes nor ears, nor yet hands and feet or wings are to be
conceived of God, even if perhaps someone should choose to conceive such
things not as in tangible and gross bodies, but as in a subtle and
immaterial one, and indeed according to the nature of God; for it is
altogether foolish to wish to conceive such a thing. For God is a spirit;
and being this, he has the knowledge of all things, he sees all things and
looks over them, and nothing of the things that are escapes him. But if the
divine scripture makes mention of members or parts, speaking to us the
things concerning him, one must know that it speaks with us from the things
we know and are naturally able to be. For otherwise we could not understand
the things concerning God. The cause therefore and true pretext of the
God-inspired scripture making the words concerning God bodily toward us, is
the poverty of mind and tongue in us. For the things concerning him are
altogether unutterable; and those being in tangible and gross bodies could
not understand any of the necessary things, unless receiving their own
members in the order of examples, thus with difficulty they might rise to
the subtle conceptions concerning God.

551 God created man of earth, and breathed into his face the breath of
life, and man became a living soul. And some say the soul was fashioned by
the breathing, as the body by hands; others that that breathing became a
soul to him; others again that to the man created complete that breathing
gave a vital power; others that the mind is this breathing, and is
distinguished from the soul, and this is that according to the image, so
that man consists of these three, of mind and soul and body distinctively
in union. And is this breathing proper to the substance of God or alien to
it?

SOLUTION. The things so subtle and not easy of the questions do not need a
dogmatic decision, but rather a raising of questions and a testing of the
conjectural, with not allowing the word to fall out onto what it should
not, that is, to be carried outside the fitting. For it is written, that
seeking seek, and dwell by me. But what the sacred letter clearly did not
say, how could anyone interpret clearly? Such as I say is written in the
book of the world-making, that in the beginning God made the heaven and the
earth. And that he made, the sacred letter said, and this we receive as
true in faith. But to inquire how or whence, or in what manner 552 the
heaven and the earth and the other of the creatures were brought to
existence is not unprofitable; for one need not plant the mind into the
deeper things. As many things therefore as the divine scripture does not
say very clearly, these one must pass over, and run by in silence. But if
one must infer with a reasoning looking to rightness, we say that the maker
of all things fashioned from earth man, either the body; and ensouling it
with a living and intellectual soul, in whatever manner he knows, implanted
in him naturally the desire and knowledge of every good thing. For this I
think the word said through the blessed evangelist John declares, That was
the true light, which lightens every man coming into the world. For the
living thing is born having a natural aptitude toward the good. And this
the all-wise Paul will teach, writing that we are his workmanship, created
unto good works, which God prepared that we might walk in them. For man is
governed by choice, and the reins of his own understanding have been
entrusted to him, so that he runs toward whatever he wishes, either toward
the good, or indeed toward the opposite. But nature has implanted in itself
both the desire and eagerness toward every good thing whatsoever, and to
wish to cultivate goodness and righteousness. For thus we say man became
according to image and likeness, 553 in that the living thing is naturally
good and just. But since it was necessary that he be not only rational, and
aptly disposed toward good-doing and righteousness, but also a partaker of
the Holy Spirit, that he might have the characters of the divine nature
more resplendent in himself, he breathed into him the breath of life. And
this is the Spirit ministered through the Son to the rational creation, and
shaping it into the highest form, that is, the divine. For that the thing
breathed to man did not become a spirit for a soul, nor yet into a mind, as
some suppose, hence it is possible to see. For first the breather is
understood to be God, and the thing breathed from him is certainly
understood to be his own, either of his substance. Then how could the
Spirit from God have changed into the nature of a soul, or become a mind?
For it is impossible for the Spirit to be turned. But if one should grant
it to be, and to have become by a turning a soul or mind; which is among
the impossibilities; yet that much at once it is possible to see. For if
the divine Spirit became a soul to man, the soul and the mind would have
remained unsusceptible of sin. But if the Spirit from God, changed into a
soul, has fallen into sins, a double accusation is brought against him by
us. First, that he underwent a turning into what he was not, then besides
this we say that he also became susceptible of sin. Therefore the living
thing was ensouled by the unutterable power of God, and became in likeness
to him, in that it is naturally good and just and susceptible of all
virtue; and was sanctified, 554 shown to be a partaker of the divine
Spirit, which it also cast off because of sin. For God somewhere said, My
Spirit shall not abide in these men for that they are flesh, that is,
minding only the things of the flesh.

Since therefore God the Father was well pleased to recapitulate all things
in Christ, that is, to bring up into the ancient state, restoring to us
again the Holy Spirit that had fallen away and departed from us, he
breathed it into the holy apostles, saying Receive the Holy Spirit. For a
renewal of that ancient gift, and of the breathing given to us, has come to
pass through Christ, reshaping us first unto sanctification, and bringing
back the nature of man, as in firstfruits to the holy apostles, unto the
sanctification given to us from above and in the first fashioning.

Whether the according-to-image and the according-to-likeness are other and
other or the same; for they say that the according-to-image we received
immediately on being created, but the according-to-likeness, not, but it is
reserved for us unto the age to come. Wherefore, he says, it is written,
When Christ shall appear, we shall be like him. And again it is said, he
says, Let us make man according to image and according to our likeness, and
after the making of man he said, And God made man, according to image of
himself he made him, having been silent here of the according-to-likeness,
that, he says, he may show that we have not yet received this, but that it
is reserved for us in that blessed life.

555 SOLUTION. If they say the according-to-image and the according-to-
likeness are other and other, let them teach the difference. For we are
disposed, that the according-to-image signifies nothing other than
according-to-likeness, and likewise the according-to-likeness the and
according-to-image; but the likeness toward God we received in the first
fashioning, and are images of God. For susceptible, as I said, is the
nature of man both of goodness and righteousness and sanctification, and
has implanted in it from God the desire in these. And this is possible to
see hence. The turning happened in the understanding of man, not from the
bad to the good, but from the good to the bad. First then that must be
presupposed, which indeed we having abandoned have turned away from. That
in the soul of man from the first fashioning was sown the desire and
eagerness and knowledge of every good, the all-wise Paul will make clear,
saying, When the nations that have not the law do by nature the things of
the law, these not having law are a law to themselves, who show the work of
the law written in their hearts, their conscience bearing witness with
them. But if also to the nations outside law it is naturally possible to
know law, either the purpose of the lawgiver; it will be clear to everyone
henceforth that the nature of man was made just and good in the beginnings,
556 and was brought to this by God, bearing his own form and the image of
goodness. For holy also was the first time of the life of man; but sin
having stolen in, the characters of the likeness toward God no longer
remain resplendent in us. But since man became the Only-begotten of God,
the Word, holy again has become the nature of man, being reformed toward
him through sanctification and righteousness. Thus indeed the all-wise Paul
says that we all, beholding with uncovered face the glory of the Lord as in
a mirror, are transformed into the same image from glory to glory, as from
the Spirit of the Lord. But the Lord is the Spirit. Therefore a renewal and
as it were a re-formation has happened to the nature of man in Christ, our
flesh being ordered down into holy life in the Spirit. But if the sacred
letter somewhere said that God made man according to image of himself, and
was silent of the according-to-likeness, one must conceive that it was
satisfied to say the according-to-image, as signifying nothing other than
the according-to-likeness. For it is superfluous to say that this is
reserved for us unto the age to come. For God having said, Let us make man
according to our image and according to likeness, who is so bold as to say
that he became according to image, but no longer according to likeness? But
we shall be like Christ at least in incorruption, and in being beyond
death, and yet also in the glory which he himself might grant to us. For
the apostle Paul writes again, sometimes 557 that you died, and your life
is hidden with Christ in God; when Christ your life shall appear, then also
you with him shall appear in glory, sometimes again who shall transform the
body of our humiliation, that it become conformed to the body of his glory.
Since also now we are not outside being according to his likeness, if it be
true, as in us he is being formed through the Holy Spirit. For Paul writes
again to the Galatians, My little children, of whom I again travail in
birth, until Christ be formed in you. For whenever we keep ourselves
faithful and holy, then he is seen being formed in us, and flashing forth
into our understandings the characters of himself noetically.

That we are not, he says, image of God, but image of an image. For the Son
and Word of God and of the Father is image of him; but man not of the
archetype, but of the image, that is, of the Son, so that we are an image
of an image. For it is not said, he says, that God made man image of
himself, but according to image, that man may be according to the image of
God and Father, that is, an image of the Son, which is an image of an
image.

SOLUTION. The divine and consubstantial Triad is beyond every [created]
and bodily imagination. But one must believe that the Father is in the Son
and the Son in the Father, and he who has seen the Son has seen the Father;
and the Son is seen 558 in the consubstantial Spirit. For it is written
that the Lord is the Spirit. And where there is altogether identity of
substance, there certainly would be nothing varying. But whatever you
conceive the Father to be, this the Son also is, save only to be Father;
and whatever you suppose the Son to be, this the Spirit also is, save only
to be Son. For each of the named subsists idiosyncratically, and truly is
what it is said to be; but the likeness in every thing whatsoever of the
holy Triad holds without variation. Therefore even if man has become
according to the image of the Son, thus he is according to the image of
God. For of the whole consubstantial Triad the characters shine in him,
seeing that the divinity according to nature is one, that in Father and Son
and Holy Spirit. For the divine Moses writes, And God said, Let us make man
according to our image and according to our likeness. But Our has not a
declaration of one person, because the fullness of the divine and
unutterable nature is in three hypostases. Superfluous therefore perhaps
the thing to work over curiously and speak subtly and say, that we are not
images of God rather, nor of the archetype, but of the image of God; but it
suffices to believe with simplicity, that we have become according to a
divine image, having received the formation toward God naturally. But if
one must say something also not incredible, it was necessary for us being
about to be named sons of God, to become rather according to the image of
the Son, that the character of sonship may beseem us also.

559 That in the coming state the soul having the rational faculty, and
therefore not deprived of knowledge, progresses; but the others say if the
soul will have progress, certainly also diminution and passion and decay;
hence also death, and resurrection again.

SOLUTION. Those calling these to mind seem to be ignorant of the grace
that will be given to the nature of man after the resurrection from the
dead. For if this corruptible must put on incorruption, and put off
corruption, we shall of course cast away together with corruption also the
passions from it; and these are every fleshly desire; and we shall be
translated henceforth into a holy and spiritual life, distributing to us
the fitting in these things of our Savior Christ of all. For if now having
the earnest of the Spirit we live holily, what shall we be, having received
the fullness? Where there is fullness of Spirit, there certainly also is
safety of mind and steadfastness of heart, looking to the good and to pure
vision of God. Therefore we shall be better than ourselves, putting off
corruption, and having the body spiritual, that is, looking only to the
things of the Spirit; but no toil will there then be bringing us down into
badness, the Maker holding us to his own will through the Holy Spirit, as
indeed also the holy angels. Such a thing Christ pronounced, saying, In the
resurrection they neither 560 marry nor are given in marriage, but are as
the angels of God in heaven.

Why dying in Adam do we pay a paternal penalty, and each of us owes the
transgression of him; but my father, made alive in Christ, and purified
through the Holy Spirit both of the ancestral offence and of his own
transgression, did not impart to me who was begotten of the purity, nor did
the grace of the righteousness toward him benefit me, although overpowering
against sin?

SOLUTION. One must examine how the first father Adam transmitted to us the
sentence laid upon him because of the transgression. He heard, Earth you
are, and to earth you shall go, and from incorruptible became corruptible,
and was brought under the bonds of death. But since falling into this he
begat children, we who came from him have become corruptible from
corruptible. Thus also we are heirs of the curse in Adam. For not
altogether as disobedient with him of the divine commandment which he
received are we punished, but because, as I said, having become mortal, he
transmitted to the seed from him the curse; for mortal we have become of
mortal. But our Lord Jesus Christ, appointed second Adam, and a second
beginning of our race after the first, re-formed us into incorruption,
assailing 561 death, having abolished it by his own flesh, and the power of
the ancient curse is loosed in him. Wherefore the all-wise Paul says, that
as by man death, so also by man the resurrection of the dead, and again, As
in Adam all die, so also in Christ all shall be made alive. Therefore the
universal and most general sentence through the transgression in Adam is
corruption and death; likewise the universal and most general redemption is
completed in Christ. For the nature of man put off in him the death cast
upon it, through the first man becoming corruptible. But the father of each
of us, even if he be sanctified through the Holy Spirit, and receive the
remission of transgressions, will not transmit also to us the gift. For one
is he who sanctifies all and justifies and brings back into incorruption,
our Lord Jesus Christ, and to all equally through him and from him the gift
comes. But remission of sins is other, and loosing of death other. And each
of his own transgressions gains the remission in Christ through the Holy
Spirit; but in common all of us are freed from the sentence laid upon us in
the beginnings, that of death I say, running unto all, according to the
likeness of the first fallen into death. Wherefore also the all-wise Paul
says, that death reigned from Adam until Moses even over those who had not
sinned after the likeness of the transgression of Adam; for until the law
the sentence of death had prevailed. 562 But Christ henceforth having shone
forth, righteousness has come in, justifying by grace, and warding off from
our bodies the corruption.

Whether the resurrection has already happened, which Ezekiel the prophet
saw; when bone came to bone, and joint to joint, and flesh and skin and
hairs and spirit, and there was seen a resurrection of a great multitude;
or an image of the coming universal resurrection the divine scripture
showed us in prophetic vision.

SOLUTION. The great things of the facts, and because of the excess of the
wonder concerning them, being under suspicions even of being disbelieved by
some, were not taught by the prophets through report alone at the times,
the Holy Spirit flashing upon them the knowledge of each; but they also saw
the very facts, in order that believing before others, they might thus also
run through to the others. Having promised therefore the God of all to
bring back also those already dead upon the Babylonians into Jerusalem, not
indeed surely the one upon earth, but the upward and conceived in the
heavens, he showed clearly to the prophet the resurrection, and in what
manner it will be at the times; which already the divine David
pre-announced, saying concerning us, either concerning every man, When you
turn away your face they will be troubled, and to their 563 dust they shall
return. You shall send your Spirit and they shall be created, and you shall
renew the face of the earth. For having been struck in Adam because of the
transgression, we have become in a turning away from God. And because of
this cause we returned to their own dust, having become cursed. For the
maker said, Earth you are, and to earth you shall go. But in the last times
of the age, in the power of the life-giving Spirit, in Christ God the
Father will raise all the dead. But that not yet has the resurrection of
the dead happened, but will be at the times, the all-wise Paul will make
credible, writing that Hymenaeus and Alexander have made shipwreck
concerning the faith, saying the resurrection already to have happened. But
if he saying this endures the shipwreck concerning the faith, clearly it
would be henceforth that the blessed prophet Ezekiel saw usefully the power
of the resurrection as in prophetic contemplation.

To those saying that to each a worthy recompense was allotted; for not said
the Savior that a certain poor man, but Lazarus, that by the appellation he
may show that this was done in experience and in truth.

564 SOLUTION. That the judgment will be after the resurrection from the
dead the divine scripture says everywhere. But there will not be a
resurrection, unless Christ come again upon us from the heavens in the
glory of the Father with the holy angels. Thus also the all-wise Paul says
that himself the Lord in command, in voice of archangel and in trumpet of
God will descend from heaven. For he will sound the trumpet, and the dead
in Christ will rise incorruptible. Not yet therefore the Judge of all
having come down from heaven, nor yet has the resurrection of the dead
happened. Then how is it not incredible to conceive, that already to some a
recompense either of evil works or of good has happened? It is therefore a
parable's manner fashioned elegantly, the things said concerning the rich
man and Lazarus by Christ. And the word has, as the tradition of the
Hebrews has, Lazarus to be someone at that time in Jerusalem suffering last
poverty and weakness, of whom also the Lord made mention, as taking him
into an example and into a more manifest declaration of the word. Not yet
therefore the Savior of all Christ having come down from the heavens,
neither has a resurrection happened, nor has a recompense of deed followed
to some, but as in an image, in the parable is written a rich man
luxuriating and unmerciful, and a poor man in weakness; in order that those
having 565 riches on earth may know, that if they are unwilling to be good
and bountiful and communicative, and to choose to bring help to the
necessities of the poor, they will fall into a terrible and unavoidable
judgment.

Whether the God of all is able to make the things now happening and done
not to have ever happened, according to the, Nothing shall be impossible to
him. For we do not say as not having happened, but not to have been
generated in the beginning; as if he can make the harlot a virgin from the
womb of a mother, that she who fornicated not be a harlot, because the
things impossible with men are possible with God.

SOLUTION. The power of God must be sought from us, whether great and worthy
of wonder, when the thing being done is not unbefitting the divine glory.
For not because he can do all things, for this reason is it fitting for him
also to be seen a maker of the unseemly. For let us conceive that it is
unseemly to say, Whether God can make himself not to be God, whether he can
make himself susceptible of sin, whether he can make himself not to be good
or life or righteous. One must therefore decline with all strength the
unseemly questions so. But why is God not able to make the woman who
fornicated not to have ever been a harlot? Because he cannot make 566
falsehood truth. And this is not an accusation of weakness, but a proof of
nature, not enduring to suffer anything that does not befit it; but alien
to God altogether is falsehood, for a false-making is to make the woman who
fornicated not to have ever fornicated.

But one must, as I said, not even admit the beginning of the questions so
foolish and having much of the unseemly; these things ought not even to
have been set down in writing; but that your piety itself, coming upon it,
may have well its own conceptions in themselves, I was minded to make clear
as far as possible the things upon each of the chapters.
