1 Following the only sure and true teacher, the Word of God, Jesus Christ our
Lord, who through his exceeding love became this which we are, that he might
fit us to be that which he himself is. For we were not otherwise able to learn
the things of God, unless our teacher, being Word, became man. Nor was there
any other son to expound the things of the Father, except his eternal Word. 2
The Lord therefore having redeemed us by his own blood and given his soul in
place of our soul and his own flesh in place of our flesh. 3 For these things
were not in seeming, but came about in the substance of truth. And if, not
being man, he appeared as man, he neither remained what he was in truth,
Spirit of God, since the Spirit is invisible, nor was there any truth in him,
for that was not what he appeared. And we said before that Abraham and the
rest of the prophets saw him prophetically, prophesying through vision what
was going to be. If then even now he appeared such, not being what he
appeared, a prophetic vision has come about for men, and it is necessary to
await another presence of his, in which he will be such as he is now seen
prophetically. And we have shown that it is the same thing to say he has
appeared in seeming and that he took nothing from Mary. For he would not truly
have had flesh and blood, through which he purchased us, unless he
recapitulated in himself the ancient formation of Adam. Vain therefore are
those from Valentinus, teaching this, that they may cast out the life of the
flesh. 4 Since we are his members and are nourished through the creation, and
he himself supplies the creation to us, making his sun to rise and raining as
he wills, he confessed the cup from the creation to be his own blood, from
which he will moisten our blood, and he confirmed the bread from the creation
to be his own body, from which our bodies grow. When therefore the mixed cup
and the bread that has come to be receive the word of God and become Eucharist
and body of Christ, and from these the substance of our flesh grows and is
constituted, how do they say that the flesh is not capable of receiving the
gift of God, which is eternal life, the flesh nourished from the body and
blood of the Lord and being his member? As the blessed Apostle says in the
letter to the Ephesians that "We are members of his body, of his flesh and of
his bones," not saying these things concerning some spiritual and invisible
man, "for the spirit has neither bones nor flesh," but concerning the economy
according to the true man, consisting of flesh and sinews and bones, which is
also nourished from the cup which is his blood and grows from the
bread which is his body. And in the same way that the wood of the vine, bent
down into the earth, bore fruit in its own season, and "the grain of wheat
falling into the earth" and being dissolved was raised manyfold through the
Spirit of God who holds all things together, and then, having come through the
wisdom of God into the use of men and receiving the word of God, they become
Eucharist, which is body and blood of Christ, so also our bodies, nourished
from it and placed into the earth and dissolved in it, shall rise in their own
season, the Word of God granting them the raising unto the glory of God and
Father, who truly procures immortality for the mortal and freely grants
incorruption to the corruptible, because the power of God is perfected in
weakness, that we may not, as having the life from ourselves, be puffed up and
ever be lifted up against God, taking on an ungrateful thought, but learning
by experience that from his superiority and not from our nature we have the
abiding unto always, that we may neither miss the glory concerning God as it
is, nor be ignorant of our nature, but may know what God is able to do and
what man is benefited, and may never fail of the

true comprehension concerning the things that are as they are, that is, of
God and of men. And is it not so, as we said before, that God also for this
reason endured our dissolution into the earth, that having been trained in
every way in all things we may be exact for what is to come, being ignorant
neither of God nor of ourselves? And Paul most clearly showed that man was
delivered over to his own weakness, that he might not, being lifted up at some
time, miss the truth, saying: "Most gladly therefore I will rather boast in
the weaknesses, that the power of Christ may tabernacle upon me." 5 For if he
does not make the mortal live and does not raise the corruptible into
incorruption, God is not able. But that he is able in all such things, we
ought to consider from our beginning, that God, taking dust from the earth,
made man, although it was much more difficult and more unbelievable to make
from things that were not both bones and sinews and the rest of the economy
according to man into being, and to work it into an ensouled and rational
living thing, than what has come to be, then having been dissolved into the
earth, to restore again, it having gone into those things from which at the
beginning, not yet having come to be, man was made; for he who made what was
not at the beginning, when he willed, much more will restore again those
already made, when he wills, into the life given by him. And the flesh will be
found both capable of receiving and able to contain the power of God; for if
at the beginning it received the art of God, and one part became an eye for
seeing, another an ear, and another another. What then? It is not possible to
speak the number of all the composing of members according to man, which did
not come about without much wisdom. And the things that partake of the art and
wisdom of God also partake of his power. The flesh therefore is not without
share of the artistic wisdom and power of God, but his power, which is
life-giving, is perfected in weakness, that is, in flesh. Let those who say
the flesh is not capable of receiving the life given from God tell us whether
they say these things while now living and partaking of life, or while
partaking of life in no way at all, and confess themselves dead at present.
But if they are dead, how do they also move and speak and do the rest, which
are works not of dead men but of living? And if they live now and their whole
body partakes of life, how do they dare to say that the flesh is not capable
of sharing in life, confessing that they have life in the present? It is like
someone holding a sponge full of water or a torch of light and saying that the
sponge cannot partake of the water nor the torch of the fire; in the same way
these also, saying they live and boasting that they bear the life in their own
members, then contradicting themselves, say their members are not capable of
receiving the life. And if the temporary living, much weaker than that eternal
life, nevertheless is able so much as to make our mortal members live, why
would the more effective and eternal life of this not make live the flesh that
has already practiced and been accustomed to bearing the life? For that the
flesh is capable of receiving life is shown from its living; and it lives as
long as God wills it to live. And that God also is able to furnish life to it
is clear; for when that one furnishes life to us, we live. And God therefore
being able to make his own formation live, and the flesh being able to be made
to live, what then is there remaining that prevents it from partaking of
incorruption, which is the blessed and unending life given by God? 6 And what
do we say also concerning those, seeing that Enoch also, having been
well-pleasing to God, was translated in the body, pre-signifying the
translation of the just, and Elijah, as he was in the substance of the
formation, was taken up, prophesying the taking-up of the spiritual? And the
body hindered them in nothing toward the translation and taking-up; for
through the hands by which they were formed at the beginning, through these
they received the taking-up and translation. For the hands of God had been
accustomed in Adam to fashion and hold and bear their own formation and to
carry and place it where they themselves will. Where then was the first man
placed? In paradise, clearly, as it is written. And

thence he was cast out into this world, having disobeyed. Wherefore also the
elders, the disciples of the apostles, say that those who have been translated
were translated there (for paradise was prepared for just men and bearers of
the Spirit, into which also Paul the apostle, having been brought in, heard
unspeakable words as toward us in the present) and that those translated
remain there until the consummation, making a prelude of incorruption. And if
someone should suppose it impossible for men to endure for so many times, and
that Elijah was not taken up in flesh, but that his flesh was consumed in the
fiery chariot, let him consider that Jonah, having been thrown in the deep,
was again spat out safe upon the earth by command of God, and Ananias and
Azarias and Misael, thrown into a furnace of fire burning sevenfold, were
neither harmed at all nor was the smell of fire found in them. The hand of God
therefore being present with them and accomplishing in them things paradoxical
and impossible to the nature of men, what wonder if it has also worked a
paradox in those translated, serving the will of the Father? Neither therefore
the nature of any of the things that have come to be, nor indeed the weakness
of flesh, will prevail over the counsel of God. For God is not subjected to
the things that have come to be, but the things that have come to be are
subjected to God, and all things serve his will. 7 As we also hear of many
brothers in the church having prophetic gifts and speaking through the Spirit
in all kinds of tongues and bringing the hidden things of men into the open
for what is profitable and expounding the mysteries of God. 8 And those
unbridled, he says, and carried down into their own desires, having no desire
of the divine Spirit, the Apostle justly calls fleshly. 9 Since without the
Spirit of God we are not able to be saved, the Apostle exhorting us through
faith and pure conduct to keep the Spirit of God, that we may not,
becoming without share of the divine Spirit, miss the kingdom of the heavens,
cried out that the flesh by itself is not able in the blood to inherit the
kingdom of God. For if one must speak the truth, the flesh does not inherit
but is inherited. As therefore the bride is not able to marry, but is able to
be married, when the bridegroom comes and takes her, so also the flesh by
itself is not able to inherit the kingdom of God, but is able to be inherited
into the kingdom of God. For the living inherits the things of the one who has
died, and it is one thing to inherit, another to be inherited; for the one is
lord and leads and disposes the things inherited as he himself may will, and
the things are subjected and obey and are lorded over by the one inheriting.
What then is the living? The Spirit of God. And what are the belongings of the
one who has died? The members of man, which also decay in this earth. And
these are inherited by the Spirit, being transferred into the kingdom of the
heavens. 10 For as the flesh is capable of receiving corruption, so also of
incorruption, and as of death, so also of life. And both do not remain
according to the same, but the one is driven out by the other, and when the
one is present the other is destroyed. If then death, having prevailed over
man, drove out his life and showed him dead, much more life, having prevailed
over man, drives away death and will restore the living man to God. For if
death made man dead, as Isaiah the prophet says: "Death, having become strong,
swallowed," and again: "God has taken away every tear from every face." 11
Other is the breath of life, which also works man into a soulish one, and
other the life-giving Spirit, which also makes him spiritual. And for this
reason Isaiah says: "Thus says the Lord who made the heaven and established
it, who fixed the earth and the things in it, and who gives breath to the
people upon it and Spirit to those who tread it," having said that the breath
was given commonly to all the people upon the earth, but the Spirit privately
to those who tread down the earthly desires. Wherefore also again the same
Isaiah, distinguishing the

aforesaid things, says: "For Spirit shall go forth from me, and I made every
breath," having ranked the Spirit privately upon God who in the last of the
times pours it out through the adoption upon humanity, but the breath commonly
upon the creation, and having proclaimed it a thing made. And other is the
thing made than the one who made. The breath therefore is temporary, but the
Spirit everlasting. And the breath, having flourished for a short time and
remained for some season, after this goes, leaving breathless that about which
it was formerly; but the Spirit, having embraced the man from within and from
without, inasmuch as it remains always, will never leave him. 12 As therefore
the one who came to be into a living soul, having inclined toward the worse,
lost the life, so again that same one, having returned toward the better and
having taken on the life-giving Spirit, will find the life. For it is not
another that dies and another that is made to live, as neither is it another
that was lost and another that is found, but that lost sheep the Lord came
seeking. What then was it that was dying? Wholly the substance of the flesh,
which also cast off the breath of life and became breathless and dead. This
therefore the Lord came making live, that, as in Adam we all die because we
are soulish, in Christ we may live because we are spiritual, putting off not
the formation of God, but the desires of the flesh, and taking on the Holy
Spirit. 13 Vain therefore truly and wretched are those who, not wishing to see
together things so evident and manifest, but fleeing the light of truth, blind
themselves after the manner of the tragic Oedipus. And as unskilled wrestlers,
being contentious against others, having gripped fast one part of the body,
fall through that which they hold, and falling think they conquer because they
cling contentiously to that member of which they first laid hold, and besides
having fallen they become ridiculous; so also the heretics, taking two words
from Paul, "flesh and blood are not able to inherit the kingdom of God,"
neither saw the Apostle nor having first searched out the force of the things
said, but holding the words themselves barely only, they die about them,
overturning as far as is in them the whole economy of God. 14 "He will
transfigure," he says, "the body of our humiliation, that it may become
conformed to the body of his glory." Manifest that it is the body, which is
the flesh, which also is humbled falling into the earth. And the transfiguring
of it is that, being mortal and corruptible, it becomes immortal and
incorruptible, not from its own substance, but "according to the working" of
the Lord, "his being able" to procure immortality for the mortal and
incorruption for the corruptible. And for this reason he says: "That the
mortal may be swallowed by life; and the one who has worked us for this very
thing is God, who has given us the earnest of the Spirit," saying this most
manifestly concerning the Spirit; for neither is the soul mortal, nor the
Spirit. For the mortal is swallowed by life, because no longer a dead flesh
but a living and incorruptible awaits, hymning God who has worked us for this
very thing. 15 In the former times we said that man had come to be according
to the image of God, but it was not shown; for the Word was still invisible,
according to whose image man had come to be; for this reason indeed he also
easily cast off the likeness. But when the Word of God became flesh, he
confirmed both; for he both showed the image truly, himself becoming this
which his image was, and he firmly established the likeness, making man like
together with the invisible Father through the seen Word. And not only through
the things said before did the Lord make known both the Father and himself,
but through the passion itself. For loosing the disobedience of man that came
about from the beginning in the wood, "he became obedient unto death, and
death of a cross," healing the disobedience in the wood through the obedience.
And he would not have come through the same things, loosing the disobedience
toward the one who formed us, if he announced another Father. And since
through those things by which we disobeyed God and

were unpersuaded of his word, through the same he introduced the obedience
and the persuasion toward his word, showing him most manifestly God; for in
the first Adam we stumbled, not having done his commandment, and in the second
Adam we were reconciled, having become obedient unto death; for we were
debtors to no other, but to that one whose commandment also we transgressed
from the beginning. And this one is the Demiurge, who according to love is
Father, and according to power Lord, and according to wisdom our Maker and
Fashioner, whose commandment also having transgressed we were made enemies
toward him. And in season the Lord restored us into friendship through his own
incarnation, having become "mediator of God and of men." 16 Here through a
work the prophet showed that the solid Word of God, which having carelessly
cast off we were not finding, we shall receive again through the economy of
the wood. And that the Word of God is like an axe, John the Baptist says
concerning him: "And already the axe is laid to the root of the trees." And
Jeremiah likewise says: "The Word of the Lord is as an axe cutting a rock."
This one therefore, hidden from us, the economy of the wood made manifest, as
we said before. For since through wood we cast him off, through wood again he
became manifest to all, showing in himself the height and length and breadth
and depth and, as one of those who have gone before said, through the divine
stretching-out of the hands gathering the two peoples into one God; for the
hands are two, because also two peoples are scattered unto the ends of the
earth, but one head in the middle, because "one God who is over all and
through all and in us all." 17 For this reason also the rulers themselves, he
says, having the laws in a lodging of righteousness, as many things as they
shall do justly and lawfully, they shall not be examined concerning these, but
as many as they shall accomplish by turning aside from the just, unjustly and
lawlessly and tyrannically, in these they shall also perish, the just judgment
of God reaching all alike. An earthly rule therefore was set by God for the
benefit of the nations, and not by the devil, as some say, that men, fearing
it, may not swallow one another after the manner of fishes, but through the
setting of the laws may beat back the great injustice of men. And according to
this also "the authorities that are have been arranged by God as ministers of
God," as Paul says. Manifest therefore that the devil was lying when he said
"that they have been delivered to me, and to whomsoever I will I give them";
for kings are not appointed by this one's command. For some of them are given
for the correction and benefit of those subjected and the keeping of justice,
and some for fear and punishment and rebuke, and some also for mockery and
insolence and pride, as they also are worthy of the just judgment of God, as
we said before. 18 And well did Justin say that before the presence of the
Lord Satan never dared to blaspheme God, inasmuch as he did not yet know his
own condemnation, because it lay in parables and allegories, but after the
presence of the Lord, having learned openly from his words and those of the
apostles that eternal fire has been prepared for him, having stood away from
God according to his own judgment, and for all who remain unrepentantly in the
apostasy, through such men he blasphemes the Lord who brings on the judgment,
as already condemned, and ascribes the sin of his own apostasy to the one who
created him, and not to his own self-chosen judgment, as also those who
transgress the laws, then paying the penalties, accuse the lawgivers, and not
themselves. 19 As many as keep the friendship toward God, to these he
furnishes his own communion; and communion of God is life and light and
enjoyment of the good things from him. 20 As many as stand away from God
according to their own judgment, to these he brings on the separation from
him; and separation from God is death, and separation from light is darkness,
and separation from God is the casting-off of all the good things from him.
Those therefore who through the apostasy have cast off the things said before,
inasmuch as they have been deprived of all the good things, come to be in
every punishment, God

not punishing of his own leading, but the punishment following upon those
because they have been deprived of all the good things. 21 And the good things
from God are eternal and unending, and for this reason also the deprivation of
them is eternal and unending, as, the light being continual, those who have
blinded themselves or also have been blinded by others are continually
deprived of the enjoyment of the light, not the light bringing upon them the
punishment in the blinding, but the blinding itself bringing on the
wretchedness. Since then in this age some run toward the light and through
faith unite themselves to God, and some stand away from the light and separate
themselves from God, the Word of God awaits, bringing upon all the fitting
dwelling, upon those in the light that they may enjoy the good things in it,
and upon those in the darkness that they may partake of the wretchedness in
it. For this reason he says that he will call those on the right into the
kingdom of the Father, and will send those on the left into the eternal fire;
for they have deprived themselves of all the good things. 22 The Apostle says:
"Because they did not receive the love of God unto their being saved, for this
reason God sends them a working of error that they may believe the lie," the
one who is coming recapitulating the unbelief unto himself by his own judgment
and having done of his own authority as many things as he will also do and
having sat into the temple of God, that those deceived by him may worship him
as Christ, wherefore also they shall justly be thrown into the furnace of
fire, and God according to his own foreknowledge, having foreknown all things
and bringing on at a fitting time such a one who is going to be "that they may
believe the lie," whose presence John here made known thus. 23 Concerning the
shield-bearer, whom he also calls false prophet, "he was speaking," he says,
"as a dragon." 22 In order then that no one may think him to do the signs by a
divine power, but by a magical working, he said: "And he deceives those who
dwell upon the earth." And it is nothing even wonderful, if the demons and
apostate spirits serving him he does signs through them, in which he will
deceive those who dwell upon the earth. And "the number of his name" he says
and certain other things "and that the number is 666," which is six hundreds
and six tens and six units, unto a recapitulation of all the apostasy that has
come about in the six thousand years. For in as many days as the world came to
be, in so many thousand-year periods it is completed; and for this reason the
scripture says: "And the heaven and the earth were completed and all the world
of them. And God completed on the sixth day his works which he made and God
rested on the seventh day from all his works." And this is both a narration of
the things that have come about, as they came to be, and a prophecy of the
things that will be. For if "a day of the Lord is as a thousand years," and in
six days the things that have come to be were completed, it is manifest that
their consummation is the six-thousandth year. And for this reason in all this
time man, formed in the beginning through the hands of God, that is, of Son
and Spirit, becomes according to the image and likeness of God, the chaff
being cleared away, which is the apostasy, and the wheat being taken up into
the storehouse, which is those who bear as fruit the faith toward God.
And for this reason also the affliction is necessary for those being
saved, that,
having been in a manner thinned and mixed together through endurance with the
Word of God and having been fired, they will be fit for the banquet of the
King, as one of ours said, having been condemned to beasts because of the
witness toward God, that "I am wheat of God and through teeth of beasts I am
ground, that I may be found pure bread of God." And we have given the causes
in the books before this, for which God endured that this should come about
thus, and we have shown that all such things have come about on behalf of the
man being saved, ripening his self-authority toward immortality and fitting
him more suitably toward the subjection unto always of God. 24 And for this
reason, at the end, the church being taken up from here all at once, "there
shall be,"

he says, "affliction such as has not come about from the beginning, nor shall
at all come about." For this is the last contest of the just, which having
conquered they are crowned with incorruption. And for this reason into the
beast that is coming there comes about a recapitulation of all injustice and
of every deceit, that every apostate power, having flowed together in him and
been storm-tossed together, may be thrown into the furnace of fire. Fittingly
therefore also his name will have the number 666, recapitulating in himself
all the display of evil before the flood that came about from angelic apostasy
(for Noah was six hundred years and the flood came upon the earth, wiping out
the stature of the earth because of the counterfeit generation upon Adam), and
recapitulating also all the idolatry from the flood, until the image of
Nebuchadnezzar that was set up, which had a height of sixty cubits, and a
breadth of six cubits; for that whole image was a prefiguration of the
presence of the antichrist. The 600 years of Noah therefore, upon whom the
flood came about because of the apostasy, and the cubit-measure of the image
signify, as has been said, the number of the name, into which is summed all
the apostasy and injustice and wickedness of the six thousand years. And these
things being so, and this number lying in all the diligent and ancient copies,
and those themselves who had seen John with their eyes bearing witness, and
the word teaching us that the number of the name of the beast is according to
the reckoning of the Greeks through the letters in it, I do not know how some
have erred, having followed a rustic idiom and having set aside the middle
number of the name, subtracting 50 votes and wishing there to be one ten
instead of the six tens. And others, having taken this without examination,
some simply and unseasonably practiced it, and some through want of taste
dared even to search out names having the erred and mistaken number. But for
those who have done this simply and without malice it is likely there will
also be pardon from God; but as many as through empty glory will define names
containing the mistaken number and will define the name invented by them, such
will not come out unharmed, having deceived both themselves and those who
trusted them. And first the harm is in missing the truth and supposing what is
not as what is; then, the one who has added or taken away something from the
scripture having no ordinary penalty, such a one must fall into it. And
another not ordinary danger will also follow those who have falsely taken
beforehand that they know the name of the antichrist; for if these think one
thing, and that one will come having another, they will easily be deceived by
him, as that one whom it is fitting to guard against not yet being present. 25
It is safer therefore and less dangerous to wait for the outcome of the
prophecy than to guess and divine at random names, many names being able to be
found having the aforesaid number, and this inquiry will remain none the less;
for if there are many names found having the number, it will be inquired which
of them the one who comes will wear. And that it is not through lack of names
26 We at least do not take the risk of declaring concerning the name of the
antichrist confirmingly; for if it were necessary that his name be proclaimed
openly in the present time, it would have been said through that one who also
saw the revelation. For it was not seen a long time before, but almost in our
generation, toward the end of the rule of Domitian. 27 The souls go away into
the place appointed for them by God and there until the resurrection they go
about waiting for the resurrection; then, having received back the bodies and
having risen wholly, that is bodily, as also the Lord rose, thus they will
come into the sight of God. 28 And these things Papias also, who was a hearer
of John and a companion of Polycarp, an ancient man, bears witness in writing
in the fourth of his own books; for he has five books composed. 29 For neither
the

substance nor the essence of the creation is made to vanish (for true and
sure is the one who constituted it), but "the fashion of this world passes,"
that is, in the things in which the transgression came about, because man grew
old in them. And for this reason this fashion became temporary, God having
foreknown all things. And when this fashion has passed and man has been
renewed and has flourished toward incorruption so as no longer to be able to
grow old further, "there shall be a new heaven and a new earth"; in the new
things man will remain always new, conversing with God. 30 And Isaiah says:
"For in the manner that the new heaven and the new earth, which I make, remain
before me, says the Lord, so shall your seed and your name stand." As the
elders say, then those who have been counted worthy of the sojourn in heaven
will go there, and those will enjoy the delight of paradise, and those will
possess the beauty and the brightness of the city, all the good things about
it being supplied by God, and everywhere the Savior will be seen, according as
those who see him will be worthy. And that this distinction of dwelling is of
those who bear fruit a hundred and of those the sixty and of those the thirty,
of whom some will be taken up into the heavens, and some will sojourn in
paradise, and some will inhabit the city; and for this reason the Lord said
that in the things of the Father there are many mansions. For all things are
of God, who furnishes to all the fitting dwelling.
