* Sermon on the Annunciation of the Theotokos

:CUSTOM_ID: h1

Of our father among the saints Athanasius, A kerygmatic discourse on the
Annunciation of our most holy Lady the Theotokos.

The divine sacred-heralds must not look toward the weakness of the hearing,
but toward the power that is required, and the subject, and the cause, which
those who consider it ought to take as the aim of the proclamation. Wherefore
now also, as we have come to the proclamation of the divine Gospel of the
Theotokos and Mother of God, and are about to announce this toward the
gathering of the feast, it is necessary, according to the subject and cause of
the unfleshly and superdivine Trinity, to set forth the incarnation of the one
of the holy Trinity. For if the things concerning the Trinity are not first
distinguished, how shall we go forth to the things concerning the one who
became incarnate? Therefore it is needful, having brought the discourse back
from there, to proceed to the things according to the incarnation, and thus to
hear the things according to the Annunciation. And first marking it, we
remind, that one of the Master's first and all-venerable feasts, according to
the order of the matters and the arrangement of the things laid down in the
proclamations concerning Christ, being the called holy day of the divine
Annunciation, constructs the proclamation concerning the descent from heaven
of the Son of God. For we believe that he came down from the heavens, just as
we also confess that he went up. It is needful therefore, having taken hold of
the divine proclamation, to begin from there, from where the things said will
also be able to be distinguished.

We believe therefore in Father, and Son, and Holy Spirit, a Trinity of
hypostases, having the division undissolved, and the union unconfused. For we
do not advocate the division of the three holy thearchic hypostases according
to a local distance, nor a confusing union; but an undistant division, and an
unconfused union, having one name, God, according to one monad of
constitution. Since we also confess one =28.920= God in a Trinity of persons,
ranking the beginningless and timeless according to each of the three holy
thearchic hypostases, according to their characterizing property: in the
Father the unbegotten, in the Son the begotten, in the Spirit the proceeding;
glorifying the Father as beginningless and timeless, announcing the Son as
beginningless and timeless, confessing the Holy Spirit as beginningless and
timeless; not first the Father, and later the Son, and the Spirit, but
together Father, and Son, and Holy Spirit, worshipping according to one
beginningless and timeless supersubstantial monad of existence, believing and
truly glorifying one God in three properties, not as another God the Father
preexisting, and another God the Son later introduced, and another God the
Holy Spirit invented, according to the heretics who blaspheme; but
theologizing one God in three hypostases, having one essence, and power, and
energy, and as many other things as are contemplated around the essence as
theologized and hymned. And that we may give a type to the discourse, and an
assemblage or fullness, let us have the things according to theology. And what
these are, or concerning what these are, let us hear in order: that the
uncreated, the incorporeal, the timeless, the beginningless, the everlasting,
the endless, the infinite, the eternal, the unknown, the uninterpreted, the
unfigured, the untraceable, that he himself is called God of gods, Lord of
lords, King of kings, the almighty, the maker, the craftsman, the light, the
life, the holy, the good, the immortal, the strong, the all-powerful, and as
many other things as are according both to preeminence and to causal account,
each is not called essence, but around the essence; as we have learned that
they have the reference from two and more upon one according to what is
distributed, which are also called assemblage and fullness of godhead
according to the Scripture; not referring according to one hypostasis alone,
but contemplated and theologized equally according to each of the holy three.
Wherefore also the only-begotten God himself says: "All things whatsoever the
Father has are mine;" and speaking toward the Father: "All mine are yours, and
yours are mine, and I have been glorified in them." For in all things in which
the Father is glorified as theologized, in them the Son also is glorified, and
the Holy Spirit. And from this the Father is called perfect God, and the Son
perfect God, and the Holy Spirit perfect God. Since nothing of the fullness
concerning the godhead is lacking to each; but it has an isia of all the
properties, in which equally also the fullness of the godhead is contemplated.
For from a derivation of the equal, isia is said in feminine utterance, the
constitutive comprehension of the equality of the many. For of those of whom
there is some equal essence, of differing properties, it is called isia, just
as also hosia and one. And since it is called isia according to etymology, by
change of =28.921= into , and by addition of the letter, it is interpreted as
essence, as being some constitutive existence, of those having the assemblage
from more upon one monad.

And thus looking toward the man who came to be according to the image of God,
we canonize, that the constitution of the properties concerning him, according
to his existence, is named essence, as being a comprehension and encompassing
of the many, having their own assemblage upon one. For that man is created,
the intelligent, the rational, the ensouled, the bodily, the passible, the
fluid, the temporal, the mortal, the begotten, the corruptible, the growing,
the changeable, the alterable, the liable to grief, and as many other such
things as are taken equally upon man according to analogy and calculation
according to the equal, is called isia, and by change and addition of the
letters, is named essence, and from there is interpreted as fullness of
humanity, that which fills up its assemblage, according to its monadic system.
In which we note, that none of these by itself is called essence of the
properties according to man being assembled. Nor is any of these called
hypostasis; but commonly, as absolutely gathered upon all, the assemblage of
the properties according to man is called essence; but particularly and upon
one, hypostasis. Whenever therefore these are contemplated commonly upon many,
it is named essence; but whenever it is also gathered particularly upon one,
it is named hypostasis. And just as again looking toward the man who came to
be according to the image and likeness of God, we behold him at once mind and
word and spirit, and none of these earlier nor later, but he was constituted
according to the same, and called one, but understood as a triad, as it were
also the mind unbegotten, but the word begotten, and this at a time being
blackened, and being written, and being sent; so also upon the God of all, the
Father as unbegotten mind, and the Son as begotten Word, and this being sent,
according to a time of good pleasure of the paternal will, not as a pronounced
word begotten at a time and season toward need, but as hypostatic, and being
this very thing, just as he has also been begotten. For once begotten from the
Father, he is a hypostasis, having an undissolved standing, by which things he
is also naturally to be. For hypostasis according to etymology is interpreted
hypostasis. For as mind the Father, according to the cause of the begotten,
has the Son as begotten upon himself. Wherefore also the evangelist instead of
"under" has written "toward," saying: "In the beginning was the Word, and the
Word was toward God, and the Word was God." Therefore "under" and "toward" are
equivalent, and turning back according to one signification, being attached
according to preposition of the name. =28.924= The Son then is a hypostasis,
as having standing under the Father as some one, and not being without the
Father, but always being toward the Father; and neither having another will
besides the Father, nor indeed another power, nor indeed another energy, as
neither another essence, but all things whatsoever the Father has, the Son
himself also having, he is consubstantial with the Father and with the Spirit.

This one according to an ancient and true paternal counsel, as angel of the
Father's great counsel, wonderful counselor, and of old, as Word of the
Father, having spoken in many parts and in many ways to the fathers in the
prophets, in these last days has spoken to us having become incarnate and
become man, who, he says, also made the ages; not having committed his own
providence to certain gods who work wonders, as the Greeks say, but the Son
himself by the good pleasure of the Father and the cooperation of the Holy
Spirit having taken up all the providence of the things that are. Since also
showing the common of the same and one providence and energy, he says: "My
Father works until now, and I work." For neither does the Father and Son work
according to another and another providence, but according to one and the same
essential energy of the godhead. "Who being" (as the herald also says again)
"the radiance of the glory, and the character of his hypostasis" (and it is
clear, of the Father's also), and bearing "all things by the word of his
power, having made purification of our sins, sat down at the right of the
majesty on high." And the discourse turning back again is gathered into the
same thought, looking round, that the radiance, as an all-sun ray, as from an
all-sun disk has the illumination undistant, and a character is some showing
of the hypostasis, through which it is also characterized. For as a character
being impressed according to a ring, the same impression, which is also
interpreted as a seal-imprint, has nothing lacking in the things according to
essence; so the Son bears the same character of the Father. For the character
being impressed does not vary, but remains invariable, according to the same
character in the ring, being impressed both in wax and in gold according to
the royal denarius; and thus the divine character of the Son of the Father's
hypostasis is an invariable seal, and bearing all things by the word of his
power, according to his own character, he received into himself the as-it-were
waxen flesh, impressing it in himself (for this is neither impossible, nor
indeed unbelievable); that according to a time of paternal good pleasure, as
character (since he is also character) he might impress in himself the
incarnation, bearing this as a waxing, or a gilding; since also the whole
mystery of such an unspeakable and uninterpreted incarnation is hinted. For it
still remains unspeakable and uninterpreted because of the growing; since we
also dogmatize according to growth, and =28.925= not according to the at-once
of the seal. For upon the seal, the impression can be referred at once both
before many and in many waxes; but upon the divine incarnation not at once,
but according to growth the perfect is dogmatized. And this again would also
be able to be thought, as if someone should have fineness of mind, and be able
to enter according to the depth of the psychic body being sown, and to see
unspeakably and unutterably, how there, when the generative seed falls in, it
immediately runs together upon some portion, but little by little and
according to increase it takes the growth. For neither is this spoken upon the
things according to us, except that by imagination it becomes as a hint
unutterably and unspeakably; but according to growth then the divine
incarnation of the Word comes to be, the divine character not growing, nor
indeed growing together; but the ensouled flesh increasing according to the
whole fullness, according to which a man is appropriated and is carried in the
womb. For this reason, nine-month by the law of human gestation, and from
there the incarnation of Christ is dogmatized as God-hypostatic, and in no way
man-hypostatic; since according to the divine character the human form
received its own standing, according to a union indeed as of spirit toward
soul. For thus Christ is both two and not one, and one and not two; and he is
two and not one according to the two natures, and one and not two according to
the one hypostasis. Wherefore we also say two wills, and not two who will, and
two energies and not two who energize. But one who wills according to the one
hypostasis, and two wills and two energies, according to the property-account
of the natures.

In these things then proclaiming the divine mystery of the incarnation and
becoming-man, we cast away the blasphemies of the heretics, according to which
also leaving them sunk, we pass over to the things historically related by the
evangelist concerning the Annunciation of the Theotokos and Mother of God; and
as it has arrangement, we shall hear more at length. We note then, that
according to a time of good pleasure of the paternal will upon the completion
of the seventy weeks of Daniel the prophet, after Jerusalem was built, when
the captivity of Judah returned from Babylon, through Salathiel and Jesus the
son of Josedek, who were then high priests, according to the completion of the
four hundred ninety years, according to the course of his priesthood,
Zacharias entered into the temple of God to burn incense; and an angel of the
Lord appeared to him on the right of the altar, and he is evangelized
concerning John his son, that Elizabeth his wife will conceive, and will bear
the Baptist and forerunner of Christ. And when the conception had come to be,
in the sixth month of the carrying in the womb of Elizabeth, the archangel
Gabriel is sent forth from God, into Nazareth a city of Galilee, toward the
Virgin Mary. And the angel having entered toward her, was greeting her saying:
=28.928= "Hail, full of grace, the Lord is with you." And here again we
contemplate, that the voice of the angel, being interpreted according to a
pronunciation of a spirit of his sounding-forth, was not itself the hypostasis
of the Son, nor did it itself become flesh; but according to that voice,
another property of the Word and God according to hypostasis at the same time
visited in the womb of the Virgin. And they blaspheme who say that the voice
itself of the archangel was the hypostasis of God the Word. Wherefore we also
marked the place, casting away the condemnation of the blasphemy. The
hypostasis of the Word and Son of God then being other besides the voice of
the archangel, at once, as the hearing of the Virgin had been opened
beforehand through the archangelic voice, the divine hypostasis of the Son
entered into her. As the Virgin herself does not know; but the one who entered
knows how he entered. Wherefore also, as not knowing the outcome of the
mystery, she was at once troubled at the word of the angel who said, "Hail,
full of grace, the Lord is with you," and she was considering of what sort
this greeting might be; whence also the archangel taking up said toward her:
"Do not fear, Mary; for you have found grace with God. And behold, you will
conceive in the womb, and you will bear a son, and you will call his name
Jesus. This one will be great, and will be called Son of the Highest, and the
Lord God will give him the throne of David his father, and he will reign over
the house of Jacob unto the ages, and of his kingdom there will be no end."
But toward these things also the Virgin stood at a loss, and looking toward
nature, and considering according to Joseph, in return for which she was
betrothed to him, she answers toward the angel, and says: "How will this be to
me, since I do not know a man?" And since, she says, O angel, I do not know a
man, and you through the demonstrative address, behold, say, "Behold you will
conceive," as according to present time; how it will be, I do not know. But
say, O angel, and interpret, "how will this be?" And the angel answering said
to her: "The Holy Spirit will come upon you, and the power of the Highest will
overshadow you." This, he says, is the manner of the conception, thus you will
conceive in the womb, the Holy Spirit coming upon you, and the power of the
Highest overshadowing you. "Wherefore, he says, also the holy thing being born
will be called Son of the Highest." And she still being at a loss, yet praying
for herself, says: "Let it be to me according to your word." And the angel
departed from her. For the evangelist marks this, that the angel departed from
her, that he may not leave a starting-point to those who wish to say, that the
angel himself having dwelt in the Virgin became incarnate, nor that his word
according to a pronunciation of sounding-forth energized the becoming-man; but
that when the angel had departed the Holy Spirit came upon the Virgin, and the
power of the Highest overshadowed her; and thus afterward the saving
conception was completed.

Concerning which it is needful again according to what is necessary to attend,
and to examine, how it was said toward the Virgin: "The Holy Spirit will come
upon you, and the power of the Highest will overshadow you." Is it then just
as =28.929= Christ also was saying toward the apostles, "But you sit in the
city of Jerusalem, until you are clothed with power from on high," the Holy
Spirit having come upon you? For the promise is equal, and can be taken from
the equal manner, since here also power from the Highest was to come upon the
apostles and a presence of the Holy Spirit. What then? Is it as toward the
apostles the Spirit and the power of the Highest has been promised, so also
toward the Virgin? And if thus just as also into the apostles, both the power
of the Highest and the coming of the Spirit came to be, and in the apostles an
incarnation did not follow; it is clear, that neither in the Virgin was the
power incarnate, nor did the Spirit become man, even if the Son himself
according to a common account of theology is called power of the Highest. But
just as in the apostles the power came to be toward being able against every
opposing power, and the coming of the Spirit according to all the things
essentially belonging to him, energizing in all signs, and wonders, and
powers, toward confirmation and persuasion of the promise of Christ; so also
upon the Virgin the coming of the Spirit came to be, in all the things
essentially belonging to him according to the thearchy, producing grace,
toward her rejoicing in all things. Since also for this she was named full of
grace, because of her being filled in all graces that are through the Spirit;
and that the power of the Highest overshadows her, and she has it in every
time according to the conception, and I have believed that also after the
conception. For neither toward a season, as I am persuaded, did this come to
be in the Virgin, but in every season, both then, and now, and unto the
continuous, the Virgin having the power of the Highest overshadowing her, and
the coming of the Holy Spirit coming upon her; that she may remain full of
grace. For thus we think concerning this: All things were full of grace for
her through the Spirit and the power of the Highest; and not, as the heretics
say, that some energy came to be from the Spirit in the Virgin, for the
incarnation to be completed. For even if without seed, we do not so say, that
according to the division of the gifts of the Spirit dividing privately, as he
wills, flesh was formed, besides the appropriating hypostasis of the Son of
God, may it not be! for to think thus is of Paul of Samosata, and of the
remaining heretics; but thus we say according to the correctness of the
orthodox dogmas, that the Virgin was filled with power and Spirit. Toward a
sanctification indeed of her flesh, and toward being able to bear the saving
conception, thus consequently the divine hypostasis of the Word, having
tabernacled in her, became incarnate and became man; not by power and energy,
but by hypostasis, according to the whole fullness of the godhead, the very
first-god and super-original hypostasis of the Son of God became incarnate and
became man.

For it was shown beforehand, that not hypostasis, but just as the essence and
the authority, and kingdom, and all the other things, which have been set
forth according to theology, each =28.932= is not called hypostasis, but
around the hypostasis being interpreted privately according to essence; so
also the energy and the power is referred essentially around the hypostasis;
and commonly indeed upon each of the three holy thearchic hypostases, but
particularly also upon the one of the Son of God that became incarnate and
became man. The Holy Spirit then came upon the Virgin, and sanctified her, as
also in the Psalms the Spirit says: "The Highest has sanctified his
tabernacle," and the power of the Highest overshadowed, empowering her, and
producing shadows of a divine manifestation, that being typed from there she
might be able to see, as far as she is able to see, the formless God being
carried in the womb in her. For it is not to be supposed, that according to a
simple property of nature the Virgin was able to see the God who tabernacled
in her (for this neither the ensouled, both rational and intelligent
incarnation itself was able to see); but according to what is possible, as the
power of the Highest itself was overshadowing, and as it were producing
shadows toward seeing the God who tabernacles. And just as we ourselves having
a soul tabernacling in our flesh, are not able to see it as the soul is, but
as it were shadows of it and types from its powers and its energies; such the
Virgin, and the rational and intelligent soul itself, was able to see, and now
also is able from the powers and energies given in to her. And according to
this account it analogizes rather, that God made man, and according to the
image of God he made him. For according to the image and likeness of such an
incarnation and becoming-man God made man; that, bearing in himself the
according-to-image and likeness, he may not have the things upon such an
incarnation and becoming-man as doubtful; for of incarnation and becoming-man;
and indeed we proclaim a name by joint-expression, and we announce not only
incarnation, but also becoming-man; that it may not seem to the godless
heretics that we dogmatize an unsouled and mindless and irrational
incarnation, but through saying incarnation and becoming-man, signifying both
that we believe the flesh, and the rational and intelligent soul. Wherefore
also those who say one nature of God the Word incarnate, and do not add
ensouled, both rational and intelligent, we do not altogether accept, except
as they resolve and in dogmas ascribe the interpretation of the saying; for it
was also formerly said unfeignedly by some, but was later maltreated, the
heretics dealing wickedly according to the saying.

The Virgin then having been sanctified by the coming of the Holy Spirit, and
having been empowered from the power of the Highest, carries in the womb the
Son of God according to his appropriating hypostasis, according to his one
nature of the godhead; and she bears this one having become incarnate and
become man according to the property of the two natures; dogmatized from two
and in two unconfusedly, unchangeably, indivisibly; not a God-bearing man, but
a flesh-bearing God. For let not the heretics, as to say again, having
understood these things thus, but as it has been said, the gestation in the
Virgin having come to be according to an energy of Spirit and power of the
Highest, have dogmatized the one from the =28.933= Virgin a God-bearing man; a
borne-about divine power and energy of the Spirit, who energized against
Pharaoh and all the sons of Israel. Whence they also take up the voices from
the chief, speaking in the Acts concerning Christ, he himself saying rightly,
but these taking them crookedly and wickedly, in return for which he also
says: "Jesus the one from Nazareth, whom God anointed with the Holy Spirit;"
saying such a thing, that in the womb of the Virgin God formed a man without
seed, by an energy of the Spirit, and anointed him power of the Highest, as
also oil of gladness beyond his fellows, it has been written. And the
Scripture called this one Son of the Highest, as also it has been said in the
Psalms, God saying toward many: "I said, You are gods, and sons of the Highest
all." Which such things, refuting the mind of the heretics' dogmas, and being
eager to set right through the present proclamation, we say, that Christ is
not a God-bearing man, but a flesh-bearing God, in return for which also being
God, he wore a man according to the whole fullness of humanity; perfect God
and perfect man, being one according to one hypostasis, and from two and in
two the natures. For this man, whom we also say that he wore, this one God
even the Father by his good pleasure, and by the cooperation of the Holy
Spirit according to the one self-working of the Son, anointed with the Holy
Spirit, having furnished him all energy, and girded him with power of the
Highest, toward being able to bear the assault of every opposing power, and to
energize all healing in the people; since he also accepted to be tempted by
Satan, and endured all humiliation and poverty in his flesh; just as also
concerning this the great sacred-herald cries saying: "For the impossible of
the law, in which it was weak through the flesh, God having sent his own Son
in likeness of flesh of sin, and concerning sin, condemned the sin in the
flesh." Therefore the Son of God, having come to be in flesh, himself is said
having become man, just as also in other places he says: "But when the
fullness of the time came, God sent forth his Son, come to be of a woman, come
to be under law, that he might redeem those under law." And from here it is to
be noted, that first the Son is sent from the Father, and then comes to be of
a woman, not first comes to be, and from then is sent; but first is sent, and
then comes to be of a woman. And with these apostolic things the prophets
agree, saying him a sent Son. And first in Isaiah hear the sent one himself
saying: "I am first, and I am unto the age, and my hand founded the earth, and
my right hand made the heaven firm;" and going out to the things according to
a time of energy, he joins saying: "And now the Lord has sent =28.936= me, and
his Spirit;" having clearly shown through these himself, that the God himself
who made the heaven and the earth says: "And now the Lord has sent me, and his
Spirit." And through Zechariah saying: "Rejoice and be glad, daughter of Zion,
for I come and I will tabernacle in the midst of you, and many nations will
flee for refuge upon the Lord in that day; and you will know, that the Lord
has sent me toward you." From here also the one who became incarnate and
became man himself confesses himself saying: I was sent from the Father; and,
The Father has sent me, witnessing the same things in many sayings. Therefore
he was not sent as man, but as God, and Lord, Son from the Lord the Father, as
also the, "The Lord rained from the Lord," is laid down as from the Lord; and
the, "The Lord said to my Lord." And he takes also in these the throne of
David, of his father genealogized bodily; "And he will reign over the house of
Jacob unto the ages, and of his kingdom there will be no end."

And understand a discourse and dogma being woven together with this
interpretation. For the heretics seek, shaking all things, and they ask
pretending: If this followed the incarnation of Christ; that is, his having
taken the throne of David his father, and having reigned over the house of
Jacob unto the ages. Answering toward whom, we say that one must attend with
observation, that, the angel having said, "And the Lord will give him the
throne of David," he did not mark, upon which to fix or to confirm; but he
says absolutely, "and he will give." And from here one must resolve the
difficulty from the things completed according to energy; that the "he will
give" has been said, upon which to transfer this throne, as also Christ
himself interpreting was saying toward the Jews: "For this reason the kingdom
will be taken from you, and will be given to a nation producing its fruits."
Therefore Christ having taken the throne of David, transferred it and gave it
to the holy kings of Christians, to turn these back upon the house of Jacob;
and having handed it over to reproach and to plunder, he reigns in Christians
unto the ages, and of his kingdom there will be no end; for it will not be
left to another people, according to the visions of Daniel the prophet; but
they announce him to be Christ king, him lasting until his second presence,
according to which coming, he remains king unto the ages, and of his kingdom
there will be no end. For if having been born from the Virgin, and having
completed all the things according to the economy of the death and of the
cross, having risen he says: "All authority has been given to me in heaven and
upon earth;" already from there he reigned, and is announced king of
Christians; as also at the beginnings of the prayers being called together
with one another, they cry out saying: Come, let us worship, and let us fall
down to him, Christ our king. And coming =28.937= according to his great and
manifest second presence, he himself remains king unto the ages, and of his
kingdom there will be no end. And since he himself is king who was born from
the Virgin, and he himself is Lord God. Through him also the one who bore him
is dogmatized queen, and Lady and Theotokos, properly and truly.

And it is fitted from here to say as looking toward her, and toward him who
was born from her, her flesh-bearing Son: Now "the queen stood at your right
in gilded clothing, wrapped, embroidered." For even as woman the queen was
named, and Lady, and Mother of God, according to her womanly form, and now as
queen standing at the right of her all-king Son, in gilded clothing of
incorruption, wrapped, embroidered, she is sacredly spoken; in return for
which she does not stand according to a spiritual simplicity, and as it were
unfleshly and bodiless, but wrapped indeed according to her all-holy flesh
with incorruption and immortality, and embroidered according to her
all-holiest bones that support her flesh. For also from her fleshes and from
her bones, as from the ancient Adam, the new Adam, having formed for himself
face to face the as-it-were rib incarnation, bears her unto the continuous.
And from here the new Eve, being named Mother of life, remains wrapped,
embroidered, unto a first-fruits of immortal life of all the living. Let us
say then also again and again and always and through all, as looking toward
him the king Lord and God, and toward her the queen Lady and Theotokos, by a
movement of contemplation of the seeing eye according to our mind: "The queen
stood at your right in gilded clothing, wrapped, embroidered." And indeed
hear, daughter of David and of Abraham, and incline your ear to our petition,
and do not forget your people, nor us the house of your Father. For we are
your fathers according to the analogy, and you our daughter according to the
birth from us, and it is fitting for you as having become Mother of God, both
queen and Lady, and mistress, through him who was born from you, king and
Lord, and God, and master, to remember, standing upon the one fearful to us,
but delightful to you, and furnishing all graces; in return for which also you
were named full of grace, as having been shown full of all joy, through the
coming of the Holy Spirit upon you. Wherefore also the rich of the people,
having been enriched with such good and spiritual contemplations, petitioning
your face, cry concerning you: Remember us, all-holy Virgin, and having
remained virgin after the birth, and give back to us instead of these small
words the great gifts from the wealth of your charisms, full of grace; for
which, and as of underlying and constitutive differing voices, =28.940=
especially to you toward encomium, if there is any virtue, and if there is any
praise, a hymn is brought to you from us and from all creation, to the one
full of grace, to the Lady, to the Mother of God, and ark of the sanctuary.

Behold then also from now and from the beginning of the day as a first-fruits
of encomium, the archangel wove upon you according to a namesake encomium
crying: "Hail, full of grace, the Lord is with you." All the generations bless
you, and first all the tetrarchies of the angels according to heaven, and of
those upon earth, lifting hierarchical hands, they bless you the one blessed
in the heavens, and being called blessed upon earth. "Blessed are you among
women, and blessed is the fruit of your womb." Such things the first hierarchy
of the thrones, both cherubim and seraphim of those called burners, or a
multitude of knowledge of God and king to come to be, blessing say: Blessed
are you among women, and blessed your womb that bore God, and breasts, which
he himself having become an infant sucked. The second hierarchy of the
lordships and powers, and authorities looking up toward you cry, as having
learned from the higher and first-initiated hierarchy of them: Blessed are you
among women, and blessed your womb, which contained God, and breasts which
nourished God become a child. Such things also the third hierarchy of the
principalities, both angels and archangels, having been commanded and
entrusted from the God who sent, as through one archangel Gabriel to cry out
the most manifest and most comprehensive hymn, always cries: "Hail, full of
grace, the Lord is with you." From these also we who according to the
hierarchy upon earth bear the exaltations of God in the throat, having learned
or borrowing the voices, we exalt with a great and piercing voice saying:
"Hail, full of grace, the Lord is with you." Intercede, Lady, and mistress,
both queen and Mother of God, for us; because from us are both you and the
flesh-bearing God of us who was born from you; to whom belongs the glory and
the magnificence, all honor and worship and thanksgiving, with his
beginningless Father, and the holy, and good, and life-giving Spirit, now and
always, and unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
