* On the Denial of Peter

On the denial of Peter, and on the cross, and how Joseph became a type
of Christ, a homily.
=59.613=
αʹ. It is necessary in the present season both to pay the debt of
Joseph and to proclaim the call of the cross. Today Joseph is crowned,
and the cross is planted; today Peter also denies, and the thief is
received; =59.614= today the law also is fulfilled, and Christ the foretold is
doxologized; today, in the strict sense, is fulfilled the saying
plainly spoken about the death of the Lord, In the snare of himself the
sinner was caught. And who is the sinner? The devil, the inventor of
sin. And what is the snare constructed by him? The cross, =59.615= and
nothing else; for he himself stirred up children of the Jews to hew
the cross, supposing he was outwitting the unoutwittable, and casting
death around the one free among the dead; not perceiving, the
stranger, that the cross blunts the point of sin, and will crush the
head of his pride, and will stop the mouth of his blasphemy, and will
pluck off the wings of his opposition, and will show him empty and
impassable to desolate places.
Today also the oracle of the prophet David is fulfilled, A testimony
in Joseph; that is, the Lord was pre-delineated in Joseph, what he was
about to stand upon at the last of the days. For many things in Joseph
are found similar to the Master; as, for instance, I say: Joseph was
plotted against by his own kinsmen ... Joseph stripped off the
variegated coat; the Lord, the one woven from above, the soldiers took
and divided. For they divided my garments among themselves, he says,
and upon my clothing they cast a lot. Joseph was slandered to his
father; likewise they slandered the Lord to the Father, the Jews
saying he was the son of the carpenter, and again the parasites of the
Jews, the children of heretics, dogmatizing him to be of another
substance. Joseph was sold to the Ishmaelites; the Lord by Judas to
the Pharisees. Joseph, having been cast into an empty pit, came up
uninjured; the Lord, having been buried in a new monument, rose on the
third day. But why do I speak at length? Joseph accused the Egyptian
woman; the Lord made public the synagogue. Joseph was recognized by
his brothers; the Lord after the resurrection appeared to his
disciples. Joseph, imprisoned with two eunuchs, declared to them their
dreams; the one of them became a devourer of birds, and the other
shall be restored to his own homeland; the Lord, crucified with two
robbers, sent the unbelieving one away to the foods of Gehenna, and
established the believing one as chief citizen of paradise ... and the
Lord, the four-horsed chariot of the evangelists, was made known to
the Father; Joseph was shown to be a feeder of beasts, and was
proclaimed as king; the Lord was crucified as man, and was glorified
as God. But hear also another prophecy stretching toward the cross;
Going up on high he led captivity captive, and gave gifts to men. What
is the height? who is the one going up? what is the captivity? what
are the gifts to men? The height is the honored cross, on account of
the mystery ungraspable; the one going up is the Master Christ; For he
who came down, he is the same also who went up. What is it, He led
captivity captive? That is, those held beforehand by sin the Lord came
and refashioned through grace, giving gifts to men. What gifts? The
forgiveness of sins, the gift of adoption, the inheritance of the
kingdom, the proclamation of immortality, and the pledge of the seat
at the right hand. And who is the witness of this? The Lord himself,
saying, When I am lifted up, I will draw all to myself; that is, when
I am crucified, then all will believe in me. You heard just now
another more manifest testimony stretching toward the cross, Isaiah
the prophet crying out and saying, As a sheep he was led to
slaughter, and as a lamb before the one shearing him he was silent.
What is the sheep? who is the lamb? what is the slaughter? what is the
shearing? who are the slaughterers and shearers? Sheep and lamb is the
Master Christ, typologically; sheep, on account of the fleece of
baptism; lamb, on account of the sacrifice in flesh; the slaughter is
the side healing side with its side. Who are the slaughterers and
shearers? Sadducees and Pharisees; for first they were sheared along
by the blasphemy, and then they conspired with the envy. For as among
men the one being sheared both remains without pain and is found
beautiful in form; so also Christ, receiving the blow, accepted it as
a shearing, not enduring pain, and showing the beauty of the
resurrection. Concerning him it is written, Fair in beauty beyond the
sons of men. And Jeremiah also sang in harmony with Isaiah, saying, I
came as an innocent lamb to be sacrificed. He was necessarily named a
lamb, as not having revolted about the passion, as not having kicked
about the slaughter. But even if the Jews slaughtered and we bought,
theirs is the accusation, ours the right deed; theirs the blow, ours
the freedom; theirs the vengeance, ours the blessing. The light
arrives, Christ is remembered. Today is fulfilled also the prophetic
oracle of David, fitting the present cross, They gave gall for my
food, and for my thirst they watered me with vinegar. Bring to mind
then, beloved, how the Lord, hanging on the cross, cried out saying, I
thirst; and immediately one ran, and having filled a sponge with
vinegar and gall, brought it to the Lord. And why did the Lord put
forward thirst, he who prepared the sharp rock to become a pool?
Because what was written had need also to be fulfilled, They gave gall
for my food, and for my thirst they watered me with vinegar. Such are
the dregs of the Jewish vine; For from the vine of Sodom is their
vine, and their vinebranch from Gomorrah. For their dregs are vinegar
and bitterness; but our vintage is sweetness and immortality. Wherefore
also the prophet, urging, said, Taste and see that the Lord is sweet.
βʹ. What then remains to be sought? The Lord, having called his
disciples to him, said to them face to face, In this night you will
all be scandalized in me. For it is written, I will strike the
shepherd, and the sheep of the flock will be scattered. Do you see how
all things are witnessed, how all things are firm, how nothing is
idle, how nothing unworked? All of you will be scandalized in me. For
it is written, I will strike the shepherd, and the sheep of the flock
will be scattered. O the exactness of the Holy Spirit! For even if the
prophet cried out, nevertheless the Spirit was heralding the thing
beforehand. He did not say, I will strike the shepherd, and the sheep
will perish; but he said, And they will be scattered; that is, For a
little they will be scandalized; but after not much they will hold
fast the faith, calling the Lord shepherd; since the shepherd himself,
the good one, who left the ninety-nine sheep and came to the one gone
astray; that is, who left the heavenly powers and came to Adam gone
astray. The apostles also were named sheep, as yielding to the saving
staff, and growing green with the Lordly body, and being penned into
the fields of the kingdom of the heavens. For as in the present, when
a wolf has come into the flock and seized a sheep, the remaining sheep
are scattered, leap away, and flee; but if they only hear the shepherd
whistling, again they run, and join with one another; so also the
disciples of the Lord, beholding the cross as a wolf, fled, and leaped
away; but when they heard the resurrection of the Master, immediately
they ran near, and recognized, and were not estranged from the fold of
the heavens.
These things therefore, the Lord saying to his own disciples that All
of you will be scandalized this night in me, Peter, speaking
allegorically, as wishing to oppose him and to be found more
Master-loving than the remaining disciples, or rather to say it more
truly, to make public his own denial, said to the Master, What do you
say, Master? that we all be scandalized on your account? and have you
counted me together with all? so little do you trust my steadfastness?
and I also will deny you, who walked upon the sea? who was thought
worthy of the transfiguration on the mountain? who drives away a herd
of demons? who heals every passion? did not I recognize you before
all, and cry out, You are the Christ, the Son of the living God?
Wherefore also, having received my testimony, you blessed me, and
named me rock of the Church, saying, You are Peter, and upon this rock
I will build my Church, and the gates of Hades will not prevail
against her. I therefore shall deny you, the leader of the apostles,
the pillar of the Church? Wherefore also, trusting in myself, I cry,
Even if all be scandalized, I will not be scandalized. And the Lord
said to him, What do you say and contradict, Peter? do you withstand
God with words? do you find me lying? do you not beseech to be made
firm, but promise not to be shaken? has not this your heat prepared
you for much worse? I will show this fall of yours also in walking on
the sea, if I had not more quickly stretched out my hand. Did you not
also sin against me on the mountain of the transfiguration, wishing to
forge three tents, and arranging with your friends me the Master? did
you not beseech me not to go up to Jerusalem, not bearing the threat
of the Jews? I, Peter, was not willing to make you public, but you
have inscribed the foreknowing eye of my Godhead. Behold, I say to you
what you are about to do, that from your own deeds you may know who
you are. Amen, amen I say to you, before the rooster cry, you will
deny me thrice. And Peter, again caught in his understanding, not
perceiving the changeableness of the human habit, answered the Lord,
saying, Why do you frighten me? I know to despise death, and I will
not deny you. And the Lord said to Peter, Well indeed do I command,
but after not much you will know; for the season is at the doors, that
both your command and my foresight be made manifest.
What then is the end of the word? for the evening is at hand. For the
Lord, having been seized, and led into the court of the high priest,
and all the disciples having been scattered, Peter alone followed into
the court of the high priest, as being about to do the things brought
together. And the Lord, having been secured more within, Peter sat in
the outer court, bold, unbending, as having borne the victory. What
then? While he sat for a long time, neither beholding a sword being
sharpened, nor whetters laid before him, nor a confiscation being
promised, nor an exile being written up; a little maidservant, bought
with silver, lowly, having come near to Peter, says, What do you here,
man? where is the power of your Teacher? behold, he is shut up
within; behold, he is about to be given over to a cross. And Peter,
having spat out from his memory the boldness, answered saying, Why do
you buzz around me, girl? I do not know the man. O the things! the one
saying, Even if it be needful for me to die with you, now denies.
This denial therefore having first happened, Peter, being troubled,
leaped out of the court as from fire, running outside of the porch.
And what? Again another maidservant met him. O the things! nowhere a
soldier, nowhere a sword, but a lowly girl met Peter; opposing him,
saying, Why are you troubled, man? where do you hasten? why are you
anxious? did I not always see you walking about with the Galilean? why
then, having gone out of the porch, do you run? And Peter answered no
longer simply, but with an oath says, I do not know this man. Do you
see the forewarning of God, and the slip of man? Then, the second
denial having been fulfilled, the third denial contains thus, After a
little the bystanders, having come near, said to Peter, Truly also you
are one of them; for even your speech makes you manifest. And Peter
began to curse and to swear, that I do not know the man. Where is the
promise of Peter made without deliberation? the one formerly saying,
You are the Christ, the Son of the living God, here says, I do not
know this man? Perhaps Peter, being awake about this word, not
willingly; he did not say, I do not know the God Word; but he said, I
do not know this man.
He stumbled about the thing seen, not about the thing understood.
Wherefore also the Lord, knowing beforehand, said, Everyone who says
blasphemy against the Son of man, it will be forgiven him. What then
Peter? I do not know this man; and immediately the rooster cried; the
unwritten accuser of Peter, the one crying before the Lordly decision,
the advocate, the one making public the denial, and the one showing
the foreknowledge, and the one recording the tears of repentance.
=59.618= γʹ. And let these things have been said sufficiently; but let
us exercise the word of almsgiving, encouraging your love, and saying
as it is said in the Gospel, Make for yourselves friends from the
mammon of unrighteousness, that, when you fail, they may receive you
into the everlasting tents. He said well, Everlasting. For here even
if you have a brilliant house, it perishes altogether, being corrupted
by time; or rather even before the corruption by time, death, having
fallen upon it, casts you out of this brilliant dwelling; but often
also before death, difficulties of certain affairs, and assaults of
slanderers, and plots, prepared you to fall out of it. But there none
of these things is to be hinted at, neither corruption, nor death, nor
a fall, nor the pursuit of slanderers, nor anything else; but the
dwelling is unmoved and immortal. Wherefore he called them everlasting.
Make for yourselves friends from the mammon of unrighteousness. Do you
see how great the love of men of the Master, how great the goodness
and gentleness? for he did not simply place this addition, but since
for many rich men wealth has been gathered together from seizure and
covetousness, he says, badly indeed, you ought not to have gathered
wealth in this way; but since you gathered, stand away from seizure
and covetousness, and use the money for the needful.
I do not say, that seizing you should show mercy, but that, standing
away from covetousness, you should spend the wealth on almsgiving and
love of men. For if one should not cease from seizure, neither will he
work almsgiving; but even if he should cast myriads of money into the
hands of those in need, seizing and coveting the things of others, he
is reckoned by God equal to murderers. Wherefore it is needful, having
first stood away from covetousness, thus to have mercy on those in
need. For great is the power of almsgiving. But let no one take the
continuity of the reminder as an accusation of those hearing. For also
in those contests of the runners the spectators stir up those whom
they see to have become near the prize, and to have many hopes of the
victory. And I therefore, since I always see you receiving the words
concerning almsgiving with much readiness, therefore I also myself
more continually move the exhortation concerning these things. The
poor are physicians of our souls, benefactors and protectors; for you
do not give so much as you receive. You give money, and you receive
the kingdom of the heavens; you loose poverty, and you reconcile to
yourself the Master. Do you see that the exchange is not equal? These
things on earth, those in heaven; these things perish, those remain;
these things are corrupted, those are superior to all loss. Wherefore
also before the doors of the houses of prayer our fathers stood the
poor, that this sight of the poor might raise to remembrance of
almsgiving even the most slothful and most inhuman. For when a chorus
of old men stands having bent down, clothed in rags, parched, dirty,
having staffs, being able to be supported with difficulty, but often
also having the eyes dug out, and maimed in body; who is so adamantine
as to withstand, and to remain before all these things without
yielding, both the age, and the weakness, and the maiming, and the
poverty, and the lowliness, and all things simply bending him toward
sympathy? Wherefore for these things they stand before our doors,
drawing those entering to love of men through the sight more powerful
than every word. For as fountains are considered to be in the courts
of the houses of prayer, that those about to pray to God, having first
washed their hands, so raise them to prayer; so also the fathers stood
the poor instead of springs and fountains before the doors, that, as
with water we wash our hands, so, having first cleansed the soul with
love of men, thus we pray. For not as the nature of water washes the
stains of the body, so does the power of almsgiving cleanse the filth
of the soul. Therefore as you do not dare, having entered with
unwashed hands, to pray, though that accusation is less; so neither
come ever to prayer without almsgiving. And yet, often having clean
hands, unless we first wash them with water, we do not raise them to
prayer; =59.619= so much is habit. Therefore let us do this also
concerning almsgiving; even if we are conscious to ourselves of no
great fault, nevertheless let us cleanse the conscience through
almsgiving.
Many terrible things you have drawn from the marketplace; an enemy
provoked; a judge compelled you to do some of the things not fitting;
you often uttered words unseemly; a friend besought you to do some of
the things having sin; I will add many other things, such as it is
likely for a man being to be rubbed about, turning in a marketplace,
sitting by the tribunals, doing the things of the city; for the sake
of all these you enter to ask God forgiveness, and to make defense.
Cast down then money into the hands of the poor, and wipe away those
stains, that thus with boldness you may call upon the one being able
to forgive you these sins. If you establish yourself in habit never to
step upon these sacred thresholds without almsgiving, never either
willingly or unwillingly will you fail of this beautiful work; for such
is habit. And as nothing whatever would happen, you endure to pray
with unwashed hands, since you once came into habit; so also concerning
almsgiving you will place such a law for yourself, and both willingly
and unwillingly you will fulfill it each day, being drawn by habit.
Prayer is fire, especially when it is sent up from a watchful and
awakened soul; but this fire needs also oil, =59.620= that it touch
those heavenly vaults; and the oil of this fire is nothing else than
almsgiving. Pour out then the oil abundantly, that, rejoicing in the
right deed, with more boldness and greater readiness you may perform
the prayers. For as those conscious to themselves of nothing good
would not be able to pray with boldness; so those having accomplished
something, and coming to prayer after that righteousness, rejoicing in
the memory of the right deed, with more readiness make the supplication.
That therefore the prayer may become more powerful for us according to
this, our understanding watching in the petitions from the memory of
the right deeds, let us come to the prayers with almsgiving, and let
us remember with exactness all the things said; and before all other
things keep continually that image, in which I said the poor stand
before the doors of the houses of prayer, fulfilling on the soul that
need which in the body the fountain. For if we are continually mindful
of this, continually washing the reason, we shall be able both to
perform clean prayers, and to draw from God much boldness, and to
attain the kingdom of the heavens, by the grace and love of men of our
Lord Jesus Christ, through whom and with whom to the Father be the
glory, together with the Holy Spirit, now and always, and unto the
ages of the ages. Amen.1
