* On the Bill of Divorce

On the saying: A wife is bound by the law as long as her husband lives; but if
he fall asleep, she is free to be married to whom she wishes, only in the
Lord. But she is more blessed if she remain so.
=51.217=
αʹ. Concerning marriage recently the blessed Paul was laying down the law for
us, and the rights of marriage, writing thus to the Corinthians and saying:
Now concerning the things of which you wrote to me, it is good for a man not
to touch a woman; but because of fornications let each man have his own wife,
and let each woman have her own husband. For this reason we too spent the
whole discourse on these words. Therefore it is necessary today also to
converse with you again concerning the same subject, since today also the
same Paul converses concerning the same things. For you heard him crying out
and saying:
A wife is bound by the law as long as her husband lives; but if her husband
fall asleep, she is free to be married to whom she wishes, only in the Lord.
But she is more blessed if she remain so, according to my judgment; and I
think that I too have the Spirit of God. Let us follow him then today also,
and let us converse concerning this subject; for following Paul, through him
we shall altogether follow Christ, since this man too, not following himself
but him, wrote all things. For a well-ordered marriage is no ordinary matter,
just as it becomes a cause of countless misfortunes to those who do not use it
as they ought. For just as the wife is a helper, so often she also becomes a
plotter. Just as marriage is a harbor, so also it is a shipwreck, not from its
own nature, but from the mind of those who have used it badly. For the one who
accomplishes it according to the fitting laws finds in the house and in his
own wife a certain consola =51.218= tion and deliverance from the marketplace
and from all the evils everywhere; but the one who handles the matter simply
and as it happens, even if he enjoy much calm in the marketplace, will see
reefs and rocks when he enters the house. Since therefore the danger for us is
not about ordinary things, it is necessary to attend with accuracy to what is
said, and for the one who is about to take a wife to do this according to the
laws of Paul, or rather according to the laws of Christ. I know indeed that to
many what is said seems new and paradoxical; yet I will not be silent because
of this, but having first read the law to you, I will thus try to resolve the
seeming contradiction of laws. What then is the law which Paul laid down for
us? A wife, he says, is bound by the law. Therefore she must not be split off
while the husband lives, nor bring in another bridegroom, nor enter upon a
second marriage. And see how with accuracy he uses even the very nature of the
words. For he did not say, Let her dwell with the husband as long as he lives;
but what? A wife is bound by the law as long as her husband lives; so that
even if he give a bill of divorce, and she leave the house, even if she go
away to another, she is bound by the law, and such a one is an adulteress. If
then the husband wishes to cast out the wife, or the wife to leave the
husband, let him remember this saying, and let him think that Paul is present
and pursuing her, crying out and saying, A wife is bound by the law. For just
as runaway slaves, even if they leave the master's house, have the chain
dragging after them; so also wives, even if they leave their husbands, have
the law condemning them in place of a =51.219=


chain, accusing of adultery, accusing those who take them, and saying: The
husband is still living, and what is happening is adultery. For a wife is
bound by the law as long as her husband lives. And whoever marries her who is
put away commits adultery. And when, he says, will it be permitted her to
enter upon a second marriage? When? When she is released from the chain, when
the husband dies. Indicating this then, he did not add, that If her husband
dies, she is free to be married to whom she wishes, but, If he fall asleep,
all but consoling the woman in widowhood, and persuading her to remain upon
the former, and not to bring in a second bridegroom. Your husband did not die,
but he sleeps. Who does not wait for one who is sleeping? For this reason he
says, But if he fall asleep, she is free to be married to whom she wishes; he
did not say, Let her marry, lest he seem to force and compel; neither did he
forbid the one who wishes to enter upon a second marriage, nor did he exhort
the one who does not wish, but he read the law saying, She is free to be
married to whom she wishes. And saying that she has become free after the
death of the husband, he showed that before this she was a slave, while that
man lived; and being a slave and subject to the law, even if she receive a
bill of divorce ten thousand times, she is caught by the law of adultery. For
it is permitted to slaves to change living masters; but it is not permitted to
a wife to change husbands while the husband lives; since the matter is
adultery. Do not read me the laws laid down among those outside, which command
to give a bill of divorce and to withdraw. For God is not going to judge you
according to these laws on that day, but according to those which he himself
laid down. And even the laws of those outside have not laid this down simply,
nor as a primary thing, but they too punish the matter; so that from this also
it is shown that they are displeased toward this sin. At any rate they cast
out the one who becomes the cause of the divorce naked and destitute of money,
and from whichever side the pretext of the dissolution comes, they punish this
one also with the loss of substance; they would not therefore, having done
this, have been praising what happens.

βʹ. What then of Moses too? He too was doing this for the same reason. But do
you hear Christ saying, that Unless your righteousness abound more than that
of the scribes and Pharisees, you shall not enter into the kingdom of heaven.
Hear him again saying, He who puts away his wife except for the cause of
fornication makes her commit adultery, and he who marries her who is put away
commits adultery. For this the only-begotten Son of God came, for this he took
the form of a slave, for this he poured out the precious blood, he dissolved
death, he quenched sin, he gave more abundant the grace of the Spirit, that he
might lead you to a greater philosophy. And besides, Moses too did not
legislate this as a primary thing, but being compelled to condescend to the
weakness of those for whom the law was made. For since they were ready for
murders, and filled the houses with kindred blood, and spared neither their
own nor strangers; lest they slaughter indoors the wives they found
displeasing, he commanded them to cast them out, taking away a greater evil,
the readiness for slaughter. For that some were murderers, hear the prophets
themselves saying: Building Zion in blood, and Jerusalem in injustices; and
again, They mix bloods upon bloods; =51.220= and again, Your hands are full of
blood. And that they raged not only against strangers, but also against their
own, the Prophet making this clear also said: And they sacrificed their sons
and their daughters to the demons. And those who did not spare their children
would not have spared wives. Lest then this happen, he permitted this. For
this reason also Christ, when the Jews asked and said, How did Moses permit to
give a bill of divorce? showing that he was not writing these things as
legislating against him, says somehow thus: Moses toward the


hardness of your heart said it, but from the beginning it was not so; but he
who made them from the beginning made them male and female.

If this were a good thing, he says, he would not have made one man and one
woman, but having made the one Adam, he would have made two women, if indeed
he wished to cast the one out and bring the other in; but now through the
manner of the creation he introduced the law which I write now. What law is
this? That the wife allotted from the beginning, this one he have always; this
law is older than that one, and by as much as Adam is older than Moses. So
that I am not innovating now, nor introducing foreign doctrines, but things
older and more ancient than Moses. And it is worthwhile also to hear the very
law of Moses, which he laid down concerning this. If anyone take a wife, he
says, and dwell with her, and it shall be, if she do not find favor before
him, because he found in her a matter of unseemliness, he shall write her a
bill of divorce, and give it into her hands. See: he did not say, Let him
write, and let him give; but what? He shall write her a bill of divorce, and
give it into her hands. And there is a great difference between this and that;
for to say, Let him write, and let him give, is of one commanding and
ordering; but to say, He shall write a bill of divorce, and give it into her
hands, is of one reporting what has happened, not introducing the law from
himself. If then anyone, he says, cast out the wife, and send her from his
house, and she go and become another man's, and the last husband also hate
her, and write her a bill of divorce, and give it into her hands, and send her
out of his house, or the husband die who took her to himself as wife, the
former husband who sent her away shall not be able to turn back and take her
to himself as wife. Then showing that he does not praise what has happened,
nor consider it a marriage, but condescends because of their weakness, having
said, The former husband shall not be able to take her to himself as wife, he
added, After she has been defiled, indicating through the manner of the
expression that the second marriage, happening while the former husband lives,
is rather a defilement than a marriage. For this reason he did not say, After
she has been married. Do you see that he speaks in harmony with Christ? Then
he also added the cause, Because it is an abomination before God. And Moses
thus; but the prophet Malachi shows this very thing far more clearly than
Moses, or rather not Malachi, but God through Malachi, saying thus: Is it
worthy to look upon your sacrifice? or to receive it as acceptable from your
hands? then when he said, For what reason, he says, have you left the wife of
=51.221= your youth? and showing how great an evil it is, and depriving of all
pardon the one who has done this, he increases the accusation through what
follows, adding thus and saying: And she is your partner, and the wife of your
covenant, and the remnant of your spirit, and no other made her. See how many
just claims he sets down; first that of age, The wife of your youth; then the
necessary, And she is your partner; then the manner of the creation, Remnant
of your spirit.

γʹ. In addition to all these, what was greater than all these, the dignity of
the one who made. For this is the, No other made. You cannot say, he says,
that God made you, but that woman not God, but some other inferior to him; but
one and the same brought both into being; so that even if nothing else, at
least respecting this, keep the love toward her. For if for slaves often who
are at strife with one another this has become a cause, I mean the need for
both to serve one and the same master, much more must this happen in our case,
when both of us have the same as creator and master. Did you see how even in
the Old


the commandments were already receiving beginnings and preludes of the new
philosophy? For since they had been nourished in the law for a long time, and
ought to travel toward the more perfect commandments, and the things of their
way of life were already meeting them toward the end, with the fitting time
the prophet now leads them to this philosophy. Let us then obey this good law,
and let us deliver ourselves from all shame, and let us neither cast out our
own, nor receive those cast out by others. For with what face will you look
upon the husband of the woman? with what eyes the friends of that man, the
servants? For if, when the one who dwelt with her has died, someone taking
that man's wife, then seeing the image alone set up, felt something and was
troubled, as seeing the husband of her who dwells with him still living, what
life will he live? how will he enter the house? with what mind, with what eyes
will he see that man's wife as his own? Or rather one could not justly call
such a woman either that man's or his own; for the adulteress is no one's
wife. For she both trampled the covenants toward that man, and did not come to
you according to the fitting laws.

Of what madness would it not be, to bring into the house a matter full of so
many evils? For is there a scarcity of women? For what reason, when there are
many whom it is permitted to take according to the fitting laws and with a
clean conscience, do we run to those who are forbidden, overturning the
houses, and introducing civil wars, and preparing enmity for ourselves from
every side, opening the mouths of countless accusers, and putting our own life
to shame, and, what is hardest of all, gathering for ourselves inescapable
punishment on the day of judgment? For what shall we say then to the one who
is about to judge us, when bringing the law into the midst and reading it he
should say: I commanded not to take a woman who has been put away, saying that
the matter is adultery. How then did you da =51.222= re to come to a forbidden
marriage? What shall we say, and what shall we answer? For we must not put
forward there the laws laid down by those outside, but it is necessary, silent
and bound, to be led away into the fire of Gehenna with the adulterers and
those who have wronged others' marriages; for both the one who put away
without cause, that of fornication, and the one who marries her who has been
cast out, while the husband lives, are punished alike with her who was cast
out. Therefore I exhort, and I beg and I entreat, that husbands neither cast
out wives, nor wives leave husbands, but hear Paul saying: A wife is bound by
the law as long as her husband lives; but if the husband fall asleep, she is
free to be married to whom she wishes, only in the Lord. For what pardon could
those have who, when Paul even permits a second marriage after the death of
the one who dwelt with her, and grants such license, dare to do this before
the death? what defense would they obtain, either these who take the wives
while the husbands live, or those who go away to the public prostitutes? For
that too is another species of adultery, for the one who has a wife within to
have dealings with prostitute women. For just as the wife who has a husband,
even if she give herself to a slave or to some free man who does not have a
wife, is caught by the laws of adultery; so also the husband, even if he sin
with a public prostitute, even if with another woman who does not have a
husband, having a wife, the matter is judged adultery. Let us then flee this
manner of adultery also. For what shall we have to say, what to put forward,
daring such things? what plausible pretext shall we furnish? The desire of
nature? But the allotted wife stands by, depriving us of this defense. For
this marriage was introduced, that you may not fornicate; or rather not the
wife only, but also many others who have shared the same nature with us
deprive us


of this pardon. For when your fellow-slave, having the same body, possessing
the same desire, driven by the same necessity, sees no other woman, but
remains loving his own alone, what defense will you have, putting forward the
desire?

And why do I speak of those who have wives? Consider for me those who live
continually in virginity, and have not had dealings with marriage at all, and
have displayed much self-control. When then others are self-controlled without
marriage, what pardon will you obtain fornicating after marriage? Let both men
and women hear these things, and widows, and those who have married; for Paul
converses with all, and this law that says: A wife is bound by the law as long
as her husband lives; but if he fall asleep, she is free to be married to whom
she wishes, only in the Lord. And to those who have husbands, and to those who
do not have them, and to those who are widows, and to those who bring in a
second bridegroom, and to all simply this word is useful. For she who has a
husband will not choose, while that man lives, to be another's, having heard
that while he lives she is bound; and she who has lost him again, if she wish
to enter upon a second marriage, does not do this simply, nor as it happens,
but according to the laws laid down by Paul, who said, She is free to be
married to whom she wishes, only in the Lord, that is, with self-control, with
gravity. But if she choose to keep the covenants toward the one who has
departed, she will hear of the crowns laid up for her, and she will receive
greater eagerness. For she is more blessed, he says, if she remain so.

δʹ. Do you see how the word is useful to all, both condescending to the
weakness of those, and not depriving these of their proper praises? For what
he did in the case of marriage and virginity, this he did also in the case of
the former marriage and the second. For just as there he did not shut out
marriage, lest he weigh down the weaker, nor did he impose necessity, lest he
deprive those who wish to remain virgins of the crowns laid up, but he showed
that marriage is good, and made clear that virginity is better; so here again
he sets down other degrees for us, showing that the state of widowhood is
greater and higher, and that of second marriages second and lower, both
anointing the stronger who do not wish to leap over, and not letting the
weaker fall. For having said, But she is more blessed if she remain so, lest
you think the law is human, hearing, According to my judgment, he added: And I
think that I too have the Spirit of God. You cannot then say that the judgment
is human, but the sentence is of the grace of the Spirit, and the law is
divine. Let us not then think these things are of Paul speaking, but of the
Paraclete laying down the law for us. And if he says, I think, he does not say
it as not having, but moderating and contracting himself. That she is more
blessed, then, he said; but how more blessed, he did not still add, having
given a sufficient proof in bringing the sentence from the Spirit. But if you
wish also to examine by reasonings, you will find here a great abundance of
proofs; and you will see the widow being more blessed, not according to the
age to come only, but also according to the present life. And this very thing
Paul knows especially, which he also hinted concerning the virgins, saying:
For exhorting and counseling to choose virginity, he spoke somehow thus: I
think that it is good for a man so to be because of the present necessity; and
again, And if the virgin marry, she has not sinned; calling virgin here not
the one who has dedicated herself, but the one inexperienced of marriage only,
and not become liable to the promise of perpetual virginity. But such will
have tribulation in the flesh; but I spare you. For with this one and bare
word he left it to the conscience of the hearers to gather all, the pangs, the


rearing of children, the care, the illnesses, the untimely deaths, the
hatreds, the contentions, the being enslaved to countless minds, the being
liable for others' evils, the receiving countless griefs while having one
soul. And from all these evils she is delivered who has chosen continence, and
with the deliverance from the painful things she has a great reward laid up
according to the life to come. Knowing all these things then, let us be eager
to be content with the former marriages. And if we also prepare to enter upon
second ones, with the fitting manner and form, according to the laws of God.

For this reason he said, She is free to be married to whom she wishes; and he
added, Only in the Lord, at once both giving license, and walling in the
license, and granting authority, and again setting bounds and laws to this
authority from every side; as, that the woman not bring into the house
polluted and corrupted men, or those from the stage, or those devoted to
fornication; but with gravity, with self-control, with piety, that all things
may be to the glory of God. =51.224= For since many women often, when the
first husbands have died, having first committed adultery, thus brought in the
later ones, and invented other polluted ways, for this reason he added, Only
in the Lord; that the second marriage may have none of these things; for thus
it will be able to be delivered from accusations. For it is better than all
things to wait for the one who has died, and to keep the covenants toward that
man, and to choose continence, and to remain with the children left behind,
and to draw more goodwill from God. But if someone wish to bring in a second
bridegroom, with self-control, with gravity, according to the fitting laws;
for this too is permitted, and only fornication and adultery are forbidden.
Let us then flee this, both those who have wives, and those who do not; and
let us not put our life to shame, nor live a ridiculous life, nor defile the
body, nor bring an evil conscience into the mind. For how will you be able to
come into church after the dealings with the prostitutes? how to stretch the
hands to heaven, with which you were embracing the prostitute? how to move the
tongue, and to call with this mouth, with which you kissed the prostitute?
with what eyes will you see the more grave of your friends? And why do I speak
of the friends? For even if there be no one who knows, you will be compelled
before all to blush and be ashamed of yourself, and more than all to loathe
your own body. For if this were not so, for what reason do you run to the bath
after that sin? is it not because you think yourself more unclean than all
mire? What other greater proof do you seek of the uncleanness of the things
that have happened, or what vote do you expect God to bring, when you who have
transgressed have such a judgment concerning the things that have happened?
That they think themselves unclean, then, I greatly praise and accept; but
that they do not come to the fitting manner of the purifications, for this I
accuse and blame. For if the filth were bodily, reasonably you would wipe
yourself with the purifications of the baths; but having defiled the soul and
made it unclean, seek such a purification as will be able to wipe away her
stain. And what is the bath of such a sin? Warm springs of tears, groanings
coming up from below from the heart, continual contrition, intense prayers,
almsgivings, and abundant almsgivings, the condemning of the things that have
happened, the no longer attempting the same deeds; thus the nature of sin is
wiped away, thus the filth of the soul is cleansed; so that if we do not do
such things, even if we go through all the springs of the rivers, we shall not
be able to take away even a small part of this sin.


The better thing then is no longer to take experience of this abominable sin.
But if someone has been tripped up sometime, let him apply these medicines,
having first promised no longer to fall into the same. Since if while sinning
we condemn the things already done, and again attempt the same, there will be
no benefit to us from the purifications. For the one who washes, and again
rolls in the same mire, and the one who pulls down again what he built, and
builds again, that he may pull down, gains nothing more, than to do
superfluous things and to toil. Let us too then, that we may not spend life in
vain nor at random, cleanse the former sins, and let us spend all the
remaining life in self-control and seemliness =51.225= and the rest of virtue;
that having God also gracious, we may obtain the kingdom of heaven, by the
grace and philanthropy of our Lord =51.226= Jesus Christ, to whom be the glory
unto the ages of ages. Amen.
