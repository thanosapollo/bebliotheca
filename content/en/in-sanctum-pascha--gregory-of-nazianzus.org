7. Mind then and sense, thus already distinguished from one another, stood
within their own bounds =36.632= and bore in themselves the magnificence of
the creating Word, silent praisers of the great workmanship, and far-sounding
heralds. But there was not yet a blend of both, nor any mixture of the
opposites, a mark of greater wisdom and of the lavishness concerning the
natures; nor was the whole wealth of goodness known. This then the craftsman
Word wishing to display, and one living being out of both, I mean of invisible
and of visible nature, he creates man; and taking the body from the matter
already in existence, and putting in from himself a breath (which the word
knows as intellectual soul and image of God), he sets him upon the earth as a
kind of other world, great in small, another angel, a mixed worshipper,
overseer of the visible creation, initiate of the intelligible, king of things
upon earth, ruled from above, earthly and heavenly, temporal and immortal,
visible and intelligible, midway between greatness and lowliness, the same
spirit and flesh; spirit because of the grace, flesh because of the pride; the
one, that he may remain and glorify the benefactor; the other, that he may
suffer, and suffering be reminded, and be trained, ambitious for the
greatness; a living being managed here, and transferred elsewhere, and the end
of the mystery, deified by the inclination toward God. For to this, I think,
the moderate light of truth here leads, both to see and to suffer a brightness
of God worthy of him who both bound together, and will loose, and will bind
together again more highly.

8. This one he set in paradise, whatever this paradise was, honoring him with
self-determination, so that the good might belong to the one who chose no less
than to the one who supplied the seeds, a farmer of immortal plants, perhaps
of divine thoughts, both the simpler and the more perfect, naked in simplicity
and in the artless life, and without any covering and defense; for such it was
fitting that the one from the beginning should be; and he gives a law, matter
for the self-determination. And the law was a command, of which plants he was
to partake, and of which he was not to touch. And this was the tree of
knowledge, neither planted badly from the beginning, nor forbidden enviously
(let not the God-fighters send their tongues there, nor let them imitate the
serpent); but good when partaken of in due season (for the plant was
contemplation, as my contemplation has it, which it is safe for those more
perfect in habit alone to step upon, =36.633= but not good for those still
simpler, and greedier in desire; just as neither is perfect food profitable
for those still tender and needing milk. But when by the envy of the devil,
and the spite of the woman, which she both suffered, as being more tender, and
which she brought on, as being more persuasive (alas for my weakness! for the
forefather's is mine), he forgot the command that had been given, and was

9. And having been trained first by many things in place of the many sins
which the root of wickedness sprouted, according to different causes and
times, by word, law, prophets, benefactions, threats, blows, waters, burnings,
wars, victories, defeats, signs from heaven, signs from air, from earth, from
sea, of men, of cities, of nations, by unhoped-for changes, by which the thing
aimed at was that wickedness be worn away; at last he needs a stronger
medicine for the more terrible sicknesses, mutual murders, adulteries,
perjuries, lusts for men, the last of all evils and the first, idolatries, and
the transfer of worship from the maker to the creatures. These things, since
they needed the greater help, also obtain the greater. And this was the Word
of God himself, the one before the ages, the invisible, the incomprehensible,
the bodiless, the beginning from the beginning, the light from the light, the
fountain of life and of immortality, the impress of the archetype, the unmoved
seal, the unchanging image, the definition and word of the Father, he comes to
his own image, and wears flesh because of the flesh, and is mixed with an
intellectual soul because of my soul, cleansing like by like. and he becomes
all things, except sin, a man; conceived from the Virgin, who had been
purified beforehand in both soul and flesh by the Spirit (for it was necessary
that birth also be honored, and virginity be honored more), and coming forth
God with the assumption, one from two opposites, flesh and spirit, of which
the one deified, the other was deified. O the new mixture! O the paradoxical
blending! He who is, becomes; and the =36.636= uncreated is created; and the
uncontained is contained, through a mediating intellectual soul mediating
between Godhead and the thickness of flesh; and he who enriches, is poor; for
he is poor of my flesh, that I may be rich of his Godhead. And the full is
emptied; for he is emptied of his own glory for a little, that I may partake
of that fullness. What is the wealth of goodness? What is this mystery
concerning me? I partook of the image, and I did not keep it; he partakes of
my flesh, that he may both save the image and immortalize the flesh. He shares
a second communion, far more paradoxical than the first; by as much as then he
imparted the better, now he partakes of the worse. This is more Godlike than
the former; this is higher to those who have mind.

10. But what is this to us, perhaps someone of those very feast-loving and
more fervent would say? Spur the colt around the turning-post; philosophize
for us the things of the feast, and those on which we sit in session today.
This then I will also do, even if I began a little from above, the discourse
and the longing having so constrained me. And perhaps it is not worse for the
lovers of learning and of beauty to treat briefly of the very name of the
Pascha. For this might not be a poor episode for the ears. This Pascha, the
great and venerable, is called Phaska among the Hebrews, according to their
speech; and the speech means the crossing; historically, because of the flight
and migration from Egypt toward the land of Canaan; but spiritually, because
of the progress and ascent from the things below toward the things above and
the land of promise. And what we have found happening in many places of
Scripture, some of the names being altered from the more unclear toward the
clearer, or from the more rustic toward the more seemly, this we have observed
here too. For some, thinking this to be the name of the saving passion, then
hellenizing the speech, according to that of phi toward pi, and of kappa
toward chi

alteration, they have named the day Pascha. And custom, taking over the
speech, made it stronger =36.637=, the hearing of the many running to it, as
to a more pious word.

11. That the whole law is a shadow of the things to come and the intelligible,
the divine Apostle declared before us. And the God who spoke before this to
Moses, when he was legislating about these things: For see, he says, you shall
make all things according to the type shown to you on the mountain; showing
the things seen as a kind of sketch and pre-delineation of the invisible. And
I am persuaded that none of these things has been arranged at random, nor
unreasonably, nor in a low way, nor unworthily of the legislation of God and
of the ministry of Moses; even if it is hard to find for each of the shadows
each contemplation, going down into the fine points, as many things as have
been legislated concerning the tent itself, and measures, and matter, and the
Levites and ministers who lift these things, and as many as concerning
sacrifices, and purifications, and heave-offerings; and that they are
contemplatable only for those according to Moses in virtue, or as near as
possible to that man's training. Since even on the mountain itself God appears
to men, in one respect himself descending from his own height, in another
raising us from the lowliness below, so that the uncontained may be contained
moderately at least by mortal nature, and as much as is safe. For it is not
otherwise possible to come into a conception of God, the thickness of a
material body and of a bound mind not being helped. Then, therefore, not all
appear to have been deemed worthy of the same rank and standing; but one of
this, another of that, toward the measure, I think, of each one's own
purification; and some altogether driven away, and allowed only to hear the
voice from above, as many as are beastlike in manner, and unworthy of divine
mysteries.

12. Yet we, going a middle way between those altogether thicker in mind, and
those too contemplative and lifted up, so that we may neither remain wholly
idle and unmoved, nor be more curious than we ought, and fallen away from the
things set before us and alien (for the one is somehow Jewish and low, the
other dream-interpreting, and both alike condemned); thus we will speak about
these things, according to what is reachable for us, and not very outlandish,
nor ridiculous to the many. For we hold that, since we who had fallen =36.640=
from sin from the beginning, and had been stolen through pleasure as far as
idolatry and the unlawful bloods, needed to be called back again, and to be
brought up to the ancient state, through the bowels of mercy of God our
Father, who did not bear that so great a work of his own hand, man, should be
lost. How then to be remade, and what to become? That the violent of the
healing be rejected, as neither able to persuade, and able to strike besides,
because of the swelling from time, and that we be managed toward correction by
the gentle and loving-kind of the therapy. For neither would a crooked shoot
bear a sudden bending-across and the force of a straightening hand (it would
be struck sooner than set upright), nor a hot and over-aged horse the tyranny
of a bit, without some flattery and clicking of the tongue. For this reason a
law is given us for help, as a kind of partition, midway between God and
idols, leading us away from the latter, and leading us back toward the former.
And he concedes something small from the beginning, that he may take the
greater. He concedes the sacrifices for a time, that he may establish God;
then, when it is the season, he may also dissolve the sacrifices, wisely
exchanging us by the gradual withdrawals, and transferring us toward the
Gospel already trained toward obedience.

13. Thus then and for these reasons the written law came in, gathering us unto
Christ, and this is the account of the sacrifices, as my account has it. But
so that you may not be ignorant of the depth of the wisdom, and the wealth of
his unsearchable judgments, he did not leave even these wholly unholy, nor
unprofitable, nor going only as far as bare blood; but the great and
unsacrificed victim, if I may so speak, as far as concerns the first nature,

has been mixed into the legal sacrifices, and not of a small part of the
inhabited world, nor for a little, but a purification of the whole world and
everlasting. For this reason a sheep is taken, because of the innocence, and
the garment of the ancient nakedness. For such is the victim for us, a garment
of incorruption, both being so and being called so. And perfect, not because
of the Godhead alone, of which nothing is more perfect, but =36.641= also
because of the assumption that was anointed with Godhead, and became what the
anointer is, and I dare to say, co-divine. And male, as being offered for
Adam, or rather the more firm of the firm, of the first who fell under sin,
and because it bears in itself nothing female, nor unmanly; but even bursting
by force from virgin and maternal bonds, according to much authority, and born
male from the prophetess, as Isaiah evangelizes. And of a year, as sun of
righteousness, either setting out from there, or circumscribed by the thing
seen, and turning back upon itself; and being blessed as a crown of kindness,
and equal to itself and like from every side; and not only this, but also as
giving life to the circle of the virtues, being mixed gently and blended with
one another, by a law of friendship and order. And unblemished and
unadulterated, as healing of blemishes, and of the diminutions and pollutions
from wickedness. For even if he took up our sins, and bore the diseases, yet
he himself suffered nothing of the things worthy of healing. For he was
tempted in all things according to our likeness, yet without sin. For he who
persecuted the light shining in the darkness did not overtake it.

15. From here the holy night, opponent of this poured-out night of the present
life, according to which the firstborn darkness is loosed, and all things come
into light, and order, and form, and the former disorder takes a world. From
here we flee Egypt, the sullen and pursuing sin, and Pharaoh the invisible
tyrant, and the bitter taskmasters, being refashioned toward the world above;
and we are freed from the clay and the brickmaking, and of the chaffy and
unstable constitution of this little flesh, which among most is not even held
by as much as chaff-like reasonings. From here the lamb is slain, and deed and
word are sealed with the precious blood, or rather habit and activity, the
doorposts of our doors, I mean of the motions and doctrines of the mind, being
opened and closed well from contemplation, since there is a measure also of
graspings. From here the last and heaviest plague upon the pursuers, and truly
worthy of night; and Egypt mourns the

firstborn of its own reasonings and deeds (which is also called by Scripture
Chaldean seed being taken away, and Babylonian infants dashed against the rock
and destroyed), and all things are full of shouting and cry for the Egyptians;
and their destroyer will then withdraw from us, in reverence and fear of the
anointing. From here a seven-day lifting of the leaven (for this is most
mystical of numbers, and coordinate with this world), of the old and sour
wickedness (not of the bread-making and life-giving); so that we may not
provision ourselves with any Egyptian dough, and remnant of Pharisaic and
godless teaching.

16. And let them mourn; but for us the lamb will be eaten; toward evening,
because the passion of Christ is at the completion of the ages; since he also
shares the mystery with the disciples in the evening, loosing the darkness of
sin. And not boiled, but roasted; so that the word may have nothing
uncontemplated for us, nor watery =36.645=, nor easily dissolved, but may be
wholly compact, and firm, and tested by the purifying fire, and free of every
material thing, and without superfluity, and let us be helped by the good
coals, which kindle and purify our thinking part, from him who came to cast
fire upon the earth, the consumer of the wretched habits, and who hastens the
kindling. As much then as is fleshly of the word and nourishing, with the
innards and the hidden things of the mind, will be eaten and consumed, and
given up into spiritual digestion, as far as head and feet, both the first
contemplations concerning Godhead, and the last thoughts of the incarnation.
And we will not carry out anything, nor will we leave it over until morning;
because neither is there a carrying-out to those outside of most of our
mysteries, nor is there any purification beyond this night, and the delay is
not praiseworthy for those who partake of the Word. For just as it is good,
and dear to God, that anger not spend the day, but be dissolved before the
sun, both temporally and anagogically (for it is not safe for the sun of
righteousness to set upon us while we are angry); so also that such eating not
spend the night, nor be put off to the next day. And as much as is bony and
inedible, and hard for us to contemplate, will not even be broken, being badly
divided and understood (for I forbear to say that neither, according to the
history, was that of Jesus broken, even though death was being hastened by the
crucifiers, because of the Sabbath); nor will it be thrown away and swept
aside; so that the holy things may not be given to the dogs and evil tearers
of the Word, just as neither to the swine the bright and pearl-like of the
Word; but it will be consumed by fire, by the Spirit that also searches all
things and knows, the whole-burnt-offerings being refined and saved, not
destroyed down waters, nor sown; as the head of the calf by Moses, which was
improvised for Israel, for a reproach of the hardness.

17. And it is worth not running past the manner of the eating either, because
neither did the law, even as far as this, labor over the contemplation in the
letter. For we will consume the victim in haste, also eating unleavened bread
upon bitter herbs, and having the loins girded, and wearing the sandals, and
staff-bearing in an elder's way. In haste, so that we may not suffer what that
Lot is forbidden by the =36.648= command; let us not look around, let us not
stand in all the surrounding country; let us be saved away to the mountain,
lest we be taken along with the Sodomitic and foreign fire, nor be fixed into
a pillar of salt, from the turning back toward the worse, which delay works.
And upon bitter herbs, because of the bitterness of the life according to God,
and the steepness, especially for those beginning, and higher than pleasures.
For even if the new yoke is kind, and the burden light, as you hear; yet this
is because of the hope and the recompense, which is far more abundant than the
hardship here; since otherwise, who would not say that the Gospel is much more
laborious and harder than the legal ordinances? For while the

law forbids the ends of the sins, we are accused even of the causes, almost
as deeds. You shall not commit adultery, says the law; but you, you shall not
even desire, inflaming the passion from a curious and laborious looking. You
shall not murder, that one; but you, you shall not even strike in return, but
you will even yield yourself to the one who strikes. How much more
philosophical are these than those! You shall not swear falsely, that one; but
you, you shall not even swear at the start, not a small thing, not a greater,
as the oath giving birth to the false swearing. You shall not join house to
house, that one, and field to field, oppressing the poor; but you, you will
put away even what was justly acquired, readily, and will strip yourself for
the poor, so that you may lift the cross lightly, and be rich in the things
not seen.

18. And the loin, for the irrational, let it be loose and unbound; for they do
not even have reason, the one that rules the pleasures; I do not yet say that
even those know a bound of the natural motion; but for you let the desiring
and neighing part also be held back by a belt and by temperance (as the divine
Scripture says, mocking the shame of the passion), so that you may eat the
Pascha purely, having put to death the members upon the earth, and imitating
the belt of John, the desert-dweller, and forerunner, and great herald of the
truth. I know also another belt, I mean the soldierly and manly, according to
which Euzonoi of Syria and certain Monozonoi are named; according to which
also God, speaking to Job, says: Nay, but gird your loin as a man, and give a
manly answer; which also the divine David boasts to have been girded with
power from God, and he introduces God himself =36.649= clothed with power and
girded, namely against the ungodly; unless it is dear to someone that the
excess of the power, and as it were drawn up, be so indicated, according to
which he also puts on light as a garment. For who will withstand the
uncontainable of his power and of the light? I seek, what is common to loin
and truth? And what does the holy Paul mean in saying: Stand therefore, having
girded your loin in truth? Perhaps as the contemplative tightening the
desiring, and not letting it be carried elsewhere? For what is disposed
erotically toward something does not wish to have the same power toward the
other pleasures.

19. And as for the sandals, let the one who is about to touch the holy and
God-trodden earth take them off, as also that Moses upon the mountain, so that
he may bear nothing dead, nor anything midway between God and men. And so also
if some disciple is sent upon the Gospel, philosophically and without
superfluity; who besides being without bronze, and without staff, and
single-coated, must still also go barefoot, so that the feet of those who
evangelize peace may appear beautiful, and every other good. But let the one
fleeing Egypt and the Egyptian things be shod, both for the rest of safety and
against the scorpions and the serpents, which Egypt also rears many of, so as
not to be harmed by those who watch the heel, which we were commanded to
tread. And concerning the staff I hold thus, and concerning the riddle about
it. I know the one that props, and the pastoral and teaching one, which turns
the rational sheep. But for you now the law commands the one that props, lest
you perhaps buckle in reasoning, hearing blood of God, and passion, and death,
lest you perhaps be carried about godlessly, as an advocate of God; but
without shame and without doubt, eat the body, drink the blood, if you are
desirous of the life, neither disbelieving the words about flesh, nor being
harmed by those about the passion. Stand braced, firm, planted, being shaken
in nothing by the opposing things, nor swept aside by words of plausibility.
Stand upon your own height, set your feet in the courts of Jerusalem, plant
upon the rock, so that your steps according to God may not be shaken.

20. What do you say? Has it seemed good thus, to go out of Egypt, the iron
furnace, to leave the polytheism there, and to be led by Moses, and of

his legislation and generalship? I introduce something also of the things not
mine, or rather also very much mine, if you contemplate spiritually. Use gold
and silver vessels from the Egyptians; travel with these; be provisioned from
the others', or rather from your own; a wage of the slavery and of the
brickmaking is owed you; be clever yourself also about the demand; strip well.
Be it so, you have been worn down here, fighting with the clay, with this

memorial will perish with a sound. What then? Do you go out unpaid and without
wage? What then? Will you leave to the Egyptians and to the opposing powers
what they acquired badly, and will spend worse? It is not theirs; they
plundered, they snatched of him who said: Mine is the silver, and mine is the
gold, and I will give it to whom I wish. Yesterday it was theirs; for it was
conceded. Today the Master brings it to you and gives it, to one who will use
it well and savingly. Let us acquire friends for ourselves from the mammon of
unrighteousness, so that when we fail, we may be received in the season of
judgment.

21. If you are some Rachel, or Leah, a patriarchal and great soul, steal also
the idols, whichever you find, of your father, not that you may keep them, but
that you may make them vanish; but if a wise Israelite, transfer them toward
the land of promise; and about these let the pursuer be in pain, and let him
know, having been outwitted, that he tyrannized and enslaved the better in
vain. If you do thus, and thus go out of Egypt, I know well, you will be led
by a pillar of fire and of cloud by night and by day, a desert will be tamed,
a sea will be cut for you, Pharaoh will be baptized, bread will rain, a rock
will spring, Amalek will be warred down; not by weapons only, but also by
warring hands of the righteous, forming at once a prayer, and the unconquered
trophy of a cross; a river will be cut off, a sun will stand, a moon will be
held, walls will be brought down, and without engines, hornets will run
before, making a way for Israel, and holding back the foreigners; and the rest
as many as after these and with these have been recorded, so that I may not
stretch the discourse long, will be given you from God. Such a feast you keep
today; such a birthday-feast of the one born, and a funeral-feast of the one
who suffered, he hosts for you; such is the mystery of the Pascha for you.
These things the law sketched; these things Christ perfected, the dissolver of
the letter, the perfecter of the Spirit, who by what he suffered, teaching the
suffering, by what he was glorified, grants the being glorified with him.

22. It is possible then to examine a matter and a doctrine, overlooked by the
many, but by me even very much examined. For to whom was the blood for us, and
concerning what was it poured, the great and celebrated of God, and high
priest, and victim? For we were held by the evil one, sold under sin, and
having taken in exchange the pleasure of the wickedness. And if the ransom
becomes of no other than of the one who holds, I seek to whom this was brought
in, and through what cause? If to the evil one, alas for the outrage; if the
robber receives not from God only, but even God himself as a ransom, and so
monstrous a wage of his own tyranny, for whom it was also just to spare us;
but if to the Father, first how? For we were not held by him. And second, what
is the reason, that blood of the Only-begotten should delight a Father, who
did not even accept Isaac when being offered by the father, but exchanged the
sacrifice, giving a ram in place of the rational victim? Or is it clear that
the Father receives, not having asked, nor having needed, but because of the
economy, and that man had to be sanctified by the human of God; so that he
himself may snatch us out, having overpowered the tyrant by force, and may
lead us back to himself through the Son who mediated, and who managed this
unto the honor of the Father, to whom he appears yielding all things. The
things of Christ then are such, and let the more be revered in silence. And
the bronze serpent is hung against

the biting serpents, not as a type of him who suffered for us, but as an
antitype; and it saves those who look toward it, not because it is believed to
live, but because it has been put to death, and puts to death with it the
powers under it, having been destroyed, as it was worthy. And what is the
fitting funeral-speech for it from us? Where, death, is your sting? Where,
Hades, is your victory? You have been struck by the cross, you have been put
to death by the life-giving. Breathless, dead, unmoved, inactive, and, if you
keep the shape of a serpent, being pilloried on high.

23. And we will partake of the Pascha, now =36.656= still typically, even if
more nakedly than the old (for the legal Pascha, I dare even to say, was a
dimmer type of a type); but a little later, more perfectly and more purely,
when the Word drinks it new with us in the kingdom of the Father, revealing
and teaching what he now moderately indicated. For what is now being made
known is always new. And what the drinking is and the enjoyment, for us to
learn, for him to teach, and to communicate the word to his own disciples. For
teaching is food, even of the one who feeds. But come, let us also partake of
the law evangelically, not in writing; perfectly, not imperfectly; eternally,
not temporally. Let us make our head not the Jerusalem below, but the
metropolis above; not the one now being trodden by armies, but the one being
glorified by angels. Let us sacrifice, not young calves, nor lambs putting
forth horns and hooves, among which there is much that is dead and without
sense; but let us sacrifice to God a sacrifice of praise, upon the altar
above, with the choir-standing above. Let us part the first veil, let us
approach the second, let us peep into the Holies of holies. I say the greater,
let us sacrifice ourselves to God; or rather, let us sacrifice according to
each day and every motion. Let us receive all things for the Word, let us
imitate the passion by passions, let us honor the blood by blood, let us go up
upon the cross readily. Sweet are the nails, even if very painful. For to
suffer with Christ, and for Christ, is more to be chosen than to live in
luxury with others.

24. If you are Simon of Cyrene, take up the cross, and follow. If you are
crucified with him as a robber, as a well-minded one recognize God; if that
one also was reckoned with the lawless because of you and your sin, you become
lawful because of him. Worship the one hanged because of you, even while
hanging; gain something even from the wickedness; buy salvation with the
death; enter into paradise with Jesus, so as to learn from what you have
fallen; Contemplate the beauties there; leave the murmurer to die outside,
with the blasphemy. And if you are Joseph the one from Arimathea, ask for the
body from the crucifier; let the purification of the world become yours. And
if you are Nicodemus, the night-time God-fearer, bury him with myrrhs. And if
you are some Mary, or the other Mary, or Salome, or Joanna, weep at dawn. See
first the stone lifted, and perhaps also the angels, and Jesus himself. Speak
something; hear a voice. If you hear =36.657= Do not touch me, stand far,
revere the Word, but do not be grieved. For he knows to whom he may appear
first. Inaugurate the resurrection; help Eve, the first who fell, the first to
greet Christ, and to make him known to the disciples. Become Peter, or John;
hasten upon the tomb, running against, running with, competing the fair
contest. And if you are anticipated in speed, win by the zeal, not having
peeped into the memorial, but having come to be inside. And if as Thomas you
are left behind, the disciples being gathered, to whom Christ appears, when
you see, do not disbelieve; and if you disbelieve, believe those who speak;
and if not even these, be certified by the marks of the nails. If he goes down
into Hades, go down with him. Know also the mysteries of Christ there, what is
the economy of the double descent, what is the reason; whether he simply saves
all having appeared, or there also those who believe.

25. And if he goes up into heavens, go up with him; be with the angels who
escort, or those who receive. Command the gates to be lifted, to become
higher, so that they may receive him higher from the suffering. Answer those
at a loss because of the body, and the symbols of the Passion, with whom, not
having gone down, he comes up together, and who for this reason inquire: Who
is this king of glory? that it is the Lord strong and mighty in all things
which he has always done, and does, and in the present war and trophy
concerning the humanity; and give to the double of the question a double
answer. And if they wonder, saying, according to the dramatizing of Isaiah:
Who is this who has come from Edom, and from the earthy things? or, How are
the garments red of the bloodless and bodiless, as of one treading a winevat,
and having trodden a full vat? put forward the beauty of the robe of the body
that suffered, beautified by the Passion, and made bright by the Godhead, than
which nothing is more loveable, nor more fair.

26. To these things what do the slanderers say to us, the bitter accountants
of the Godhead, the accusers of the things praised, the dark ones concerning
the light, the uneducated concerning the wisdom, for whom Christ died as a
gift, the ungrateful creatures, the fashionings of the evil one? Do you charge
God with this, the benefaction? For this is he small, because he is lowly
because of you? because the good Shepherd came upon the wandering one, who
lays down the soul for the sheep, upon the mountains and the hills, upon which
=36.660= you were sacrificing, and he found it wandering, and having found it
he took it up upon the shoulders, upon which also the wood, and having taken
it he brought it back upon the life above, and having brought it up he
numbered it with those who remain? because he lit a lamp, his own flesh, and
swept the house, cleansing the world of the sin, and sought the drachma, the
royal image buried with the passions, and he calls together the powers dear to
him upon the finding of the drachma, and makes them partners of the gladness,
whom he had also made initiates of the economy, because the exceeding-bright
light follows the forerunning lamp, and the Word the voice, and the bridegroom
the bridesman, who prepares for the Lord a peculiar people, and who
pre-purifies toward the Spirit through the water? Do you charge God with these
things? For these things do you suppose him worse, because he girds himself
with a towel, and washes the feet of the disciples, and shows the best way of
exaltation, the lowliness? because he is made low because of the soul bent
down to the ground, so that he may also raise up together that which nods
downward by the sin? And how do you not accuse that, that he also eats with
tax-collectors, and among tax-collectors, and makes tax-collectors disciples,
so that he himself may also gain something? What is this? The salvation of the
sinners. Unless someone should also blame the physician, because he bends down
upon the sufferings, and endures stenches, so that he may give health to those
who are sick; and the one who stoops to a pit because of loving-kindness, so
that he may save up the beast that has fallen in, according to the law.

27. He was sent, but as man; for he was double; since he also grew weary, and
hungered, and thirsted, and was in agony, and wept, by a law of nature. And if
also as God, what is this? Consider the good pleasure of the Father to be a
sending, upon whom he refers his own things, both honoring a timeless
beginning, and so as not to seem to be against God. Since he is also said to
have been handed over, but it is also written that he has handed himself over;
and to have been raised by the Father and taken up, but also to have raised
himself, and to have gone up again; those of the good pleasure, these of the
authority. But you speak the things that lessen, and you run past the things
that exalt; and that he suffered, you reckon; but that willingly, you do not
add. What things the Word suffers even now! By some he is honored as God and
is co-anointed; by others he is dishonored as flesh and is separated. With
whom should he be more angry? =36.661= or rather, whom should he forgive,
those who join badly, or those who cut? For those too ought to divide, and
these to join; the ones in the number, the others in the Godhead. Do you
stumble at the flesh? this also the Jews. Or do you also call him a Samaritan
(and I will be silent about what follows)? Do you disbelieve

the Godhead? this not even the demons. O you more unbelieving even than
demons, and more ungrateful than Jews! Those thought the title of the Son a
voice of equal honor; these knew the God who was driving them; for they were
persuaded from what they suffered. But you, you neither receive the equality,
nor confess the Godhead. It would have been better for you to have been
circumcised and to be demon-possessed, if I may say something even laughably,
than to be in uncircumcision and health disposed wickedly and godlessly. But
let the war toward those either be dissolved, they having come to their senses
at last at least if they should wish, or be postponed, if they do not wish,
but have it as they have it. And at any rate we will fear nothing, for the
Trinity, contending with the Trinity.

28. But now it is necessary for us thus to sum up the discourse: We have come
to be, that we may be treated well; we have been treated well, since we have
come to be. We were entrusted with paradise, that we might live in luxury. We
received a command, that we might be of good repute having kept it; God not
being ignorant of what would be, but legislating the self-determination. We
were deceived, since we were envied; we have fallen out, since we
transgressed; we have fasted, since we did not fast, having been overcome by
the tree of knowledge. For the command was ancient, and contemporaneous with
us, being a kind of training of soul, and a sobering of luxury; which we were
reasonably ordered, so that what we have thrown away by not keeping, we may
receive back by keeping. We needed God incarnate and put to death, that we
might live;

we were put to death with him,

that

we might be cleansed;

we were raised with him,

since

we were put to death with him; we were glorified with him, since we were
raised with him.

29. Many then are the wonders of that time; God being crucified, a sun being
darkened, and again being kindled (for the creatures also had to suffer with
the Creator); a veil being split, blood and water being poured from the side;
the one, as of a man, the other, as above man; earth being shaken, rocks being
split over the rock, dead being raised unto faith of the last and common
resurrection; the =36.664= signs at the tomb, those after the tomb, which who
would hymn worthily? But nothing is like the wonder of my salvation; a few
drops of blood remaking a whole world, and they become just as sap to milk for
all men, binding us into one and gathering us.

30. But, O Pascha, the great and holy, and purification of the whole world!
For I will speak with you as with a living thing. O Word of God, and light,
and life, and wisdom, and power! For I rejoice in all your names. O offspring
of the great Mind, and impulse, and impress! O Word being thought, and man
being contemplated, who bear all things having bound them up by the word of
your power! now may you have this discourse, not as a firstfruit, but perhaps
as a completion of our fruit-bearing, the same a thank-offering and a
supplication that we suffer nothing evil outside the necessary and holy
things, with which we have lived; and may you stop for the body the tyranny
against us (you see how great, Lord, and how it bends), or your vote, if we
should be taken down by you. And if we should be dissolved worthily of the
longing, and should be received in the heavenly tents, perhaps there too we
will sacrifice acceptable things to you upon your holy altar, O Father, and
Word, and Holy Spirit; because to you belongs all glory, honor, and might,
unto the ages of the ages.
