OF GREGORY BISHOP OF NYSSA AGAINST THE WRITINGS OF APOLLINARIUS
REFUTATION
A good prelude for our discourse would be the Lord's voice commanding us to
beware of the false prophets, who come, he says, to you in sheep's clothing,
but inwardly they are ravening wolves; from their fruits you will know them.
If then the fruit distinguishes both the true sheep and the destroyer of the
sheep who in a gentle figure slips unguarded into the company of the flock and
brings into the open the hostile mouth hidden under the gentleness, it is
fitting to seek the fruits, both the good and the worse, through which the
figure of the deceit is uncovered; for he says that From their fruits you will
know them. Of every teaching then the good fruit, at least according to my
judgment, is the addition of those being saved in the church, and the
destructive and poisonous is the dissolution of those standing together. If
then someone should increase the flock through words and should stretch the
vine into all the sides of the house and should plant round the master's table
those being recast from the wild-olive error into olive shoots and should put
the mystic rods into the sweet and drinkable stream of the teaching, through
which the flocks are multiplied so that the possession of Laban is lessened,
and the part of Jacob thrives and is multiplied by the 3,=1.132= marked
offspring if someone should show such a fruit of teaching (for fruit is, as
has been said, the increase of the truth), he is truly a prophet according to
the divine aim, interpreting for the Spirit; but if someone should pluck the
branches from the vine and should empty the divine table, uprooting the
planting, and should plot against the spiritual watering-place so that the
sheep no longer conceive according to the rods of the patriarch nor multiply
the flock with marked offspring, but are driven from the nourishing pasture,
that is from the paternal traditions, and lodge outside the fold and are
scattered toward alien pastures when such is the fruit of the teaching, the
wolfish figure hidden in the sheep's hide would become evident.
Therefore let us examine what the teaching of Apollinarius the Syrian has
sprouted for us, an addition of the flocks or a lessening, a gathering of the
scattered or a dissolution of those standing together, an advocacy or an
overthrow of the ancestral dogmas. If then the zeal of the one named looks
toward the better, he is altogether a sheep and not a wolf; but if the reverse
Beware of the false prophets, says the Lord, let not the mouth sharpened with
the teeth of innovation for the maiming and disappearance of the body that
draws near, and tearing the healthy body of the church of God, escape notice.
And so that what is said may not seem to be abuse, we will set forth one of
the discourses circulated by him, whose title is this: Demonstration, he says,
concerning the divine incarnation according to the likeness of man. And
perhaps someone, having stripped the sense of the title through exactness,
would not need through the writing itself to refute the absurdity of the
dogma. 3,=1.133= Demonstration, he says, concerning the divine incarnation
according to the likeness of man. And it would be better to set beside it the
divine voices for the refutation of this new coining of names. The Word, he
says, became flesh and The glory tabernacled in our land and God was
manifested in flesh, so that through each of the things said we might learn
that the divine, being always unchangeable in substance and unalterable, comes
to be in the changeable and altering nature, that by its own


unchangeableness it might heal our turning toward evil. But this man says that
God has not been manifested in flesh, which is the same as the Word not having
become flesh, the one who in the likeness of man and in figure associated with
the life of men through the servant's person, but he fashions in the discourse
some divine incarnation, I do not know what he means by the word, whether a
turning of the Godhead, altered from its simple and uncompounded nature into a
fleshy resistance, or, the divine substance remaining in itself, that some
other divine incarnation appeared as a boundary of the nature of men and of
God, being neither man nor God, but sharing somehow in both, which by being
incarnation has kinship toward the human, and by being divine is better than
according to man. But indeed it cannot be God; for the divine is simple in
nature and uncompounded; and from what the simplicity is, from this equally
the Godhead too is altogether separated. And again it is not man; for the one
constituted from intelligent soul and body is called man; and to what the two
are not co-conceived, how will the name of man be fitted? For we say body of a
man and soul of a man, as long as each of these is contemplated on its own;
but the running-together of these two both is and is called man. What then is
the divine 3,=1.134= incarnation that is neither man nor God, which the title
of the discourse fabricates, it is not possible to find from the things
examined.
Demonstration, he says, concerning the divine incarnation according to the
likeness of man. What is this again that is shown, the according to the
likeness of man? That the divine incarnation is constituted similarly to the
human. When is this? Is it at the last of the days? And where is the mystery
of the virginity? For the Lord is not incarnate according to the likeness of
man, as the writer says, but by divine power and Holy Spirit, as the gospel
says. But before the ages? And how is what is likened to what is not? For man
is last of the things according to creation, but the Lord is king before ages.
According to the likeness of what man then does that divine incarnation come
to be according to the pre-eternal? According to that of Adam? But he was not
yet. But according to some other man? And who is this man provided for before
Adam, so that according to the pattern laid down the incarnation of God might
be likened? For the like comes to be altogether to what is, not like to what
is not. So that two absurdities appear according to him in the discourse; for
either the thing molded is found older than the molder or the divine being
incarnate is likened to what is not; for at the beginning the Godhead was, but
Adam was not. Therefore if the divine nature is incarnate according to the
likeness of man, the divine incarnation is likened to what is not; and what is
likened to what is not would not itself altogether be. But he says that that
incarnation has come to be in some other manner besides the human. And what is
the likeness of things of another nature? If then neither according to the
pre-eternal is it possible for the divine incarnation to come to be according
to the likeness of man, nor at the last of the times, when the economy of the
Lord is according to man, according to which in 3,=1.135= flesh God was
manifested, the incarnation comes to be according to the likeness of man (for
the mystery according to the virginity does not admit such a thought), he
would have made this title of the discourse in vain according to both
thoughts. That then the title is unconsidered and supported on no standing
thought, I think has been moderately made clear through the things said to
those who listen with attention. And it would be time also to set forth for
examination the discourse itself that is shown by this writing. And I will
write the first things in my own diction, running over the sense in a few
words, in which it is without danger to pass by something of the things
written unexamined.
Only, he says, it was good that the pious faith be thought; for the unexamined
faith did not profit Eve; so that it was fitting that that of the Christians
also be examined, lest it somehow escape notice having fallen in with the
opinions of the Greeks or of the Jews


opinions. Having prefaced these things through more words he brings on the
discourse saying that Among the unbelievers and the heresies it has been
rumored beforehand that it is not possible for God to become man and to
associate with passions, which is also introduced among the heresies under a
figure of faith, they saying Christ a God-inspired man because of the birth
from woman and because of the passions. Which heresies then he knows that say
Christ a God-inspired man, he himself would know and whoever has been trained
in his things; but we, having visited many places and having associated in
zeal both with those sharing the dogma and with those standing off concerning
the things inquired according to the Word, have not yet heard anyone breaking
out such a voice concerning the mystery, that Christ was a God-inspired man.
Therefore the one about to correct the 3,=1.136= deceived assumptions
concerning the dogma, and to introduce instead the more pious, it was fitting
not to fabricate for himself the things that are not and to fight toward the
non-existent, but to arm himself toward the things said by those who set
themselves opposite. For neither do the skilled among physicians introduce
their art toward passions that are not, but they stand through the science
toward the disease that has already come to be. Let him then either show the
one who has said that God has not been manifested in flesh, but that Christ is
a God-inspired man, and then we will also receive his discourse as not having
been made in vain, or until such a disease is not found, all will confess that
it is vain to wrestle with the things that are not. But not without cause does
he fashion for himself such a necessity of the writing; but so that he might
take some path and sequence toward the construction of the dogma according to
himself, he hypothesized what was not said as having been said, so that by
seeming to contend toward these things he might construct through the
fashioned contradiction that the divine is mortal. For the whole aim of the
writing for him looks toward this, that the Godhead of the only-begotten Son
is mortal and that he did not receive the passion in the human, but that the
impassible and unalterable nature was altered toward a sharing of passion.
Whence toward what the discourse looks as useful for him, those initiated in
his mysteries would know; but the things which the sequence shows even to the
very infants are such as not even the most godless of the Greeks would say.
For if the Godhead itself of the only-begotten has died, there died together
with it altogether also the life and the truth and the righteousness and the
goodness and the light and the power; for these according to different
approaches the only-begotten God both is and is called. And being simple and
without parts and uncompounded, 3,=1.137= whatever he may be called, he is
whole, not this in a part and the other in a part so that, he being one, all
things are thought in him and, he not being, all things are cancelled
together. If then the divine was made to disappear by death, all the things
contemplated conjunctly about his Godhead disappeared together by death. But
indeed Christ is not simply power, but power of God and wisdom of God. When
then these were quenched together with the Godhead of the Son through death,
there was not with the Father wisdom of God, nor power, nor life, nor anything
else of the things named toward the better
nothing; for all these are believed to be of God <and all> the things of the
Father to be in him, which, the Son being, are, and, him not being, altogether
are not, because all the things of the Father are confessed to be in him. If
then the power was quenched in death, and Christ is power of God, what power
again was called back from what is not, the one that was having disappeared by
death, and another not having been left? For that the power of the Father is
in the Son is confessed even by those fighting us. If then the power is one,
and this, having been overpowered by death, passed over into disappearance in
the time of the economy of the passion, what other power besides him is
fabricated that calls this power back from death? For if they should say this,
that the one died, but the other remained immortal, they will no longer
confess that the power of the Father is in the Son and thus from the sequence
they will strike out the voices of the Lord himself as not having truthfully,
which testify that all the things


of the Father are of the Son. For the one who has all the things of the Father
also has altogether the immortality of the Father in himself; and immortality,
as long as it is this which it is called, has itself unmixedly and without
communion toward death. If then they say the Godhead of the Son is mortal,
3,=1.138= they construct that he does not have in himself the immortality of
the Father. But indeed the one who says he has the whole Father in himself
speaks truly. Therefore the one who constructs this one as mortal, in whom all
the eternity of the Father is contemplated, speaks falsely. Since then
according to the account of those who are pious the power of Father and Son is
one (for we see the Son in the Father and the Father in the Son), it is
clearly shown that the passible by nature receives death, and what is
unreceptive of passion worked impassibility in the passible.
But let us go forward toward the next of the discourse. And I will speak again
in my own voice, gathering the sense in brief. For he says that to name Christ
a God-inspired man is contrary to the apostolic teachings, and alien from the
synods; and that Paul and Photinus and Marcellus began such a distortion. Then
like the terrible among contestants, as if having grappled with the
interlocutor and having found the author of the saying, he says this: How will
you say that a man from earth has come down from heaven, the man witnessed,
and a son of man the one proclaimed God and Son of God? Again in these things
the construction of the preceding things is a beginning of another absurdity;
for so that he may show a God who has died, he admits nothing at all of the
earthly nature to be contemplated about him, since the passion according to
death seems altogether to have kinship with the earth. There is not, he says,
from earth a man, the man who came down from heaven. A man however, even if he
has come down from heaven; for the Lord does not deny such a designation in
the gospels. How then in the things said does he save the sequence? If the man
is not from earth, but comes down to us from heaven, 3,=1.139= how is the one
coming down from heaven called son of man? For he will altogether grant that,
just as on earth we preconceive the fathers of the sons, so also upon the man
according to him who has come from heaven, since he is called son of man, he
is not named from a father in vain, but that there is of him according to the
human in heaven another man precontemplated as father. For if he is supported
on this saying that has said No one has gone up into heaven except the one who
came down from heaven, the son of man, and through this splits him off from
kinship toward the earthly man, saying a son of man who has come from heaven;
he will altogether grant some other such father in heaven of the one who came
down to us, so that even in the heavenly life tribes of men and peoples and
families and all things, as many as there are according to the life here, are
suspected. If then what has come from heaven is son of man, and what has been
born from Mary of the seed of David according to flesh has been named from
above son of man, falsely according to them is he called Son of God, neither
the heavenly nor the earthly being appropriated toward the divine. But
gathering to himself the things said toward the heading he concludes the
discourse in this: And if both from heaven son of man and from woman Son of
God, how is he not the same God and man? But I say that to believe the same to
be man and God is also of everyone who honors the right account, yet not
according to the thought of the writer; for neither is the divine earthly nor
the heavenly human, as he thinks; but from above the power of the Most High
through the Holy Spirit was overshadowed in the human nature, that is was
formed in it, and from the undefiled virgin the portion of the flesh was made
to appear together and thus what was born in her was named Son of the Most
High; the divine 3,=1.140= power appropriating the near kinship toward the
Most High, and the flesh the human kinship.


But God, he says, by the spirit that was incarnate, and man by the flesh
assumed by God. What again is an incarnation of the spirit apart from the
union toward our flesh? And from where was man assumed, if not from the first
man, whose first genesis we heard Moses genealogizing from earth, not from
heaven? For God, having taken dust from the earth, molded the man; and we have
learned from no one that another constitution of man from heaven was put
together. Then he brings on to the things said that The mystery was manifested
in flesh, saying this well, this is our account, and that The Word became
flesh according to the union, and these too are words of the healthy voice;
for the one who has said that the Word has been united to the flesh says
nothing else than the running-together of the two. But the flesh, he says, is
not soulless; for it has been said to campaign against the spirit and to
campaign against the law of the mind. Excellent for the fairness! He does not
mold soulless flesh around God. Therefore let us ask, if the flesh was assumed
by God the Word as ensouled, as the writer says; and we also call the bodies
of the irrational ensouled; the one who attaches to the Word human flesh and
this ensouled joins nothing else than the whole man; for there is no other
property besides the intelligent nature of the human soul, because in all the
other things there is communion for us toward the irrational; the desiring,
the spirited, the appetite according to food, the power toward growth,
satiety, sleep, digestion, 3,=1.141= alteration, the putting-out of what has
become useless, all are managed equally in us and in the irrational through
some psychic power. Therefore the one who says that the one assumed is man,
and giving this as ensouled, has witnessed to him nothing else than also an
intelligible power, which is proper to the human soul, from the very words of
the apostle which he set beside for us; for the one who has said that The
minding of the flesh is enmity toward God (for it is not subject to the law of
God) says the clear properties of the choosing and thinking power; for to have
itself persuasively or resistingly toward the law is proper to choice, and the
very name of minding one would not separate from the activity according to
thinking. And that to think is the same as to understand not even one of those
altogether infantile would contradict; and the one campaigning against and
taking captive, how would he be without the activity of understanding? For to
be moved toward the base things is not a proof for the evil that there is not
a mind in those, but they are outside of a good thought, yet they understand
nonetheless; since even that serpent, whom we were taught by the scriptures as
leader and inventor of vice, the God-inspired Word says is not unintelligent,
witnessing to this one more prudence than the others.
Therefore from the very things through which he thinks to establish his own
account, the voices set beside for us refute the impiety of the dogma
according to him. For not in these only does the apostle set the flesh against
the spirit, that is the wicked choice against the more decent life, but also
to the Corinthians he says, reproaching the passible in them, that You are
fleshy. Were then at that time, according to which the discourse came to be
toward them, according to the three-part cutting of men of Apollinarius, the
men without share of the activity according to mind? Or altogether from the
measureless disposition toward the flesh 3,=1.142= were they named fleshy by
Paul, taking the designation from what was in excess? And the context of the
things written shows such an account; For where among you, he says, is zeal
and strife, are you not fleshly? And to be zealous and to strive is of the
activity according to mind. And the things next of the writing contain a
demonstration, as it were, through more testimonies of man being constituted
from three, from flesh and soul and mind, which are not far from the account
according to us. For it is the same to say that man is from intelligent soul
and body and, having numbered the mind separately on its own, to divide in
three


the contemplation about man; even if the account gives many holds in this to
the heretics; for it is possible, if someone should number the intelligent on
its own, to say another circumscription of the spirited and again another of
the desiring. And perhaps someone, enumerating also the remaining movements of
the soul according to the account, would show man some many-cleft and
many-compounded instead of the three-part.
But let these things be left, so that we may not, going through each, stretch
the refutation into measurelessness, and let us go forward toward the next of
the discourse. For having constructed through many things the three-natured or
three-part or however one should wish to name the human compound, he remembers
also certain synodical dogmas, both of those gathered against Paul of Samosata
saying From heaven the Lord has been deified; and of what was uttered
according to Nicaea, whose wording he set beside as said in these words in
this manner the Having come down from heaven and having been incarnate and
having become man. And having said these things as having preconstructed the
account about to be said he thus strips the thought of his own will and says,
as if attaching it to the things demonstrated, 3,=1.143= thus according to the
wording: The spirit then, that is the mind, Christ having God with soul and
body is reasonably called man from heaven. What common thing do the things
said have toward the preceding? Where did the synod against the Samosatene
dogmatize this? But I leave the things older than our memory. What such thing
did he show in the dogmas according to Nicaea? The faith says the one who came
down from the heavens and was incarnate, so that the flesh is not thought
before the descent, but the one incarnate and become man is second after the
descent? This is proclaimed by all in churches and this is our account, or
rather the account of the church. Where then in the things said is it, that
the spirit, that is the mind, Christ having God with soul and body is called
man from heaven? For whether you examine the account upon the wording alone or
also the thought of the things said, in neither will what was said be found.
For the wording is so far from the wording as, to speak psalmically, easts are
far from wests; and the thought of the things said, what common thing does it
show toward the things shown through that? This man says that Christ incarnate
in the human flesh has in himself God the spirit, that is the mind; and the
one who commands not to say Christ a God-inspired man, having God in himself
as another in another, himself constructs this before the others. But the
voice of the synod says the one who came down from heaven. Let then the many
and simple understand the descent from the heavens toward us; for we will
differ nothing toward such an assumption, even if the mind shows a higher
thought, not saying that the divine comes down locally, being everywhere and
having grasped beings round about, but indicating the condescension toward the
low of our nature; yet however it may be dear to the one wishing to receive
the descent, the one incarnate 3,=1.144= again the account of the synod says,
saying this well too; for how would it have interpreted more fittingly the
birth from woman? For not, inasmuch as he is God, himself by himself was he
born from woman. For the one who is before creation does not receive being
itself by having been born through flesh; but having made a way beforehand by
the Holy Spirit for the entrance of his own power and having needed nothing of
the material starting-point toward the constitution of his own tent, according
to what has been said about wisdom He built himself a house, having made man
of the dust from the virgin, through which he was mixed toward the human.
Where then in these things does the account say what seems good to
Apollinarius, <that> the spirit, that is the mind, Christ having God with soul
and body is reasonably called man from heaven? For if it is permitted him by
authority to drag the divine oracles toward what seems good and, according to
the interpreters of dreams, to fit to the letter whatever thought he should
wish, these and such things to the divine words he may attach


thoughts; but if the apostle says that old-wives' myths and the profane
new-voices must be pushed away in every manner, it is in no way to be conceded
to him to fit to one another the unsharing and to glue the incompatible. And
rather he himself through the things with which he contends for the things
said would fight with us toward the overthrow of his dogma; for he says that
Paul also calls the first Adam a soul being with a body, having said this well
and on behalf of the truth. Did then the first man, the one remembered by Paul
unto a living soul, have some bestial and irrational soul? And indeed the
history witnesses to him not a little thinking grace; for the 3,=1.145=
animals were brought to him by God, but he was master of the finding of the
names, thinking out the fitting and suitable for each. But also the
disobedience and the co-disposition about the things not due and the shame
upon the things done and the finding of a suitable defense upon the charges,
all these become a proof of the activity according to mind. Why then was the
intelligent of the soul upon Adam passed over in silence by Paul? Or is it
clear that the remaining is co-named with the part, as by To you all flesh
will come David signifies through the flesh the whole human; and Jacob
sojourning with the Egyptians in seventy-five souls is not historicized by the
scripture at that time as mindless nor fleshless.
But the writer says that the second man from heaven is called spiritual and he
makes this a sign of the man mixed with God being mindless. And yet the
refutation of the absurdity is ready from the very things which he set beside.
For the one who has said As the heavenly, such also the heavenly withdraws far
from such a suspicion. For if those who have believed the one who has come
from heavens are themselves also called heavenly by Paul, those who have
transferred, as he says, the citizenship into heavens, and as that heavenly
one is, such also these heavenly ones the apostle says they are, and no one of
those who have received the faith is mindless; there is every necessity that
this likening comparison become a proof that according to the likeness of men
the mind is in that one also. For As, he says, the heavenly, such also the
heavenly; so that they will confess of necessity either the mind in all or in
none. For just as the properties of the earthy are contemplated in those from
him, so it is necessary according to the declaration of the apostle that the
one who has been tried in all things of our life according to likeness
3,=1.146= without sin and the mind is not sin have himself fittingly toward
all our nature. For thus the apostolic is made true, that we are according to
that one, if it should be confessed that that one too has come to be according
to us, so that from having come to be as we are he may make us as that one is.
Again then let us remember the things said by him: Inasmuch as Paul also calls
the former Adam a soul being with a body and not without a body, giving the
designation to the whole, even if even it itself according to a
taking-together of the spirit is called soul on its own. Saying these things
he confesses the three to be called according to a taking-together by the name
of soul as the signification both of the body and of the spirit being included
in the same. But the last, he says, Paul calls a life-making spirit. What then
in that one he showed through what was in excess to have mastered the
designation against the remaining things signified, this altogether has the
equal power upon this one too. For if Adam, since he sinned, is called soul,
the man mixed into God, since He did not do sin nor was deceit found in his
mouth, the whole is named spirit. But he does not admit such a thought; for he
says that one earthy from earth, because the body molded from the dust was
ensouled. Was the mind, which he names spirit, not thrown down together with
the molding according to Adam? Therefore in what is the likeness toward God
for him? And what is the outflow from the divine inbreathing, if not the


mind should be believed to be these things? And this one he says is called
from heaven for this reason, because the heavenly spirit was incarnate. What
scripture says these 3,=1.147= things? To which of the saints does he refer
the account, that a spirit was incarnate? Not thus did we hear from the
gospels, not thus were we taught from the great voice of the apostle, but that
The Word became flesh, the proclamation says and the gospel history says that
the Spirit came down in the form of a dove; but an incarnation of spirit none
of those who speak mysteries by the Spirit has said. The glory tabernacled in
our land; and The truth sprang up from the earth; and God was manifested in
flesh; and Righteousness looked down from heaven; and many other such things.
But a spirit being incarnate the God-inspired scripture does not know. And the
man Christ, he says, preexists not as the spirit, that is God, being other
besides him, but as the Lord being a divine spirit in the nature of the
God-man.
These are the words of the writer upon the wording. And since somehow by the
slackness of the interpretive power the thought is not very evident in
clarity, I will first strip from the gloom of the wording the thought lying in
it, and then thus I will set forth the account for examination. The man
Christ, he says, preexists. He co-conceives with the Word who is in the
beginning the man who appeared as preexisting before the manifestation and he
works out the thought through the sequence. For he says that the spirit is not
other besides him, constructing through this that the Godhead itself of the
Son is from the beginning man, which is shown more clearly through the next
account; for he says that the Lord was a divine spirit in the nature of the
God-man. He made one the nature of God and of man and named the fusion of two
men. 3,=1.148= The thought then of the things said is this, that what was
manifested through flesh from the virgin is preconceived of beings not only
according to the eternity of the Godhead, as we all believe, but also
according to the flesh itself. For he brings on to the things said toward a
testimony of such an assumption the Before Abraham came to be I am; and the
voice of John the He was first of me; and One Lord, through whom are all
things; and He is before all; and some voice of Zechariah, which as having
been dragged forcedly toward this aim we will pass by. I then, if the
construction of the absurd escaped notice through some wrapping of thoughts,
would well have been eager about the refutation of the hidden things; but
since he cries the impiety expressly, I do not know what more would come to be
from our discourses. If the flesh is pre-eternal, if before Abraham came to be
it belonged to the one from Mary, then the virgin is older than Nahor; then
Mary also is before Adam.
And yet why do I say this? Older, as it seems, than the constitution of beings
itself and more ancient than the ages themselves. For if he was incarnate in
