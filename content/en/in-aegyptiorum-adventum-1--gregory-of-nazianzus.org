ORATION XXXIV.
On those who came from Egypt.
A. I will address those from Egypt, for it is just; since they have also
eagerly assembled, having overcome envy by zeal, of this Egypt, which a river
enriches, raining out of the earth, and flooding in season, so that I too may
imitate in some small way those who are elegant about these things; and which
my Christ enriches, formerly being a fugitive into Egypt, but now being
supplied from Egypt; then, from Herod's child-murder, but now, from the
fathers' love of children; Christ, the new food of those who hunger well, the
greater grain-distribution of all that is recorded and believed; the bread
that comes down from heaven, and gives life to the world, the indestructible
and undissolved, concerning whom even now I seem to hear the Father saying:
Out of Egypt I called my Son. B. For from you the word has sounded forth to
all men, being healthily believed and preached; and you are the best
fruit-givers of all, especially of those now rightly believing, as far as I
know, who am not only a lover of such food, but also a supplier, and not only
at home, but already also beyond the borders. For you feed bodily peoples and
cities, as far as your lovingkindness reaches; and you feed spiritually, not
this or that people, nor this or that city circumscribed by small bounds, even
if they think themselves exceeding-bright, but almost the whole inhabited
world. And you heal, not a famine of bread, nor a thirst of water, which both
to suffer famine is no great thing, and not to suffer famine is easy; but a
famine of =36.244= hearing the word of the Lord, which both to suffer is most
pitiable, and to heal now is most laborious; since lawlessness has been
multiplied, and I find few the genuine healers of this. C. Such was Joseph
your grain-measurer, and to say the same also ours, who both knew how to
foresee famine by surpassing wisdom, and to heal it by words of stewardship,
healing with the fair and fat of the cattle the ugly and unnourished. And
Joseph, whichever you wish to take, either the lover of immortality, and
maker, and namesake; or his successor in throne, and word, and grey hair, the
new Peter for us, not less in virtue than in the calling; by whom the middle
was cut out and crushed, even if it still twitches small and weak things, as
the tail when the serpent has been cut through; of whom the one, having ended
life in a good old age upon many contests and wrestlings, looks down from
above, I well know, now upon our things, and stretches a hand to those who
toil for the good, and so much the more, as he is free of the bonds; and the
other hastens to the same ending, or dissolution, upon the same contests, and
already draws near those above, and is still with the flesh so much as to help
the word the last things, and to become of the way with more abundant
provisions. D. Of these you are the nurslings and offspring of the great ones,
and doctors, and contestants of the truth, and victors; whom neither time, nor
ruler, nor word, nor envy, nor fear, nor accuser, nor slanderer, nor one
warring openly, nor one plotting secretly, nor one seeming to be ours, nor a
stranger, nor gold, the hidden tyrant, by which now most things are tossed
about and diced, nor flatteries, nor threats, nor exiles both long and
numbered (for they did not become capturable by confiscation, because of the
great wealth, possessionlessness), nor anything else of things absent, or
present, or expected, lifted up, nor persuaded to become worse, and to betray
anything of the Trinity, and to be penalized of Godhead. On the contrary they
were strengthened by the dangers, and have become more ambitious toward piety.
For such is suffering for Christ: it adds to the affection, and becomes as it
were an earnest of the contests next for the high-souled. =36.245= Such, O
Egypt, are your present narratives and wonders. E. But you used to praise to
me the Mendesian goats, and the Memphite Apis, a fatted and much-fleshed calf,
and the rites of Isis, and the tearings of Osiris, and your solemn Serapis, a
wood worshipped with myth, and time, and the mindlessness of those who
worship, as some unknown and heavenly matter, except matter, even if it is
helped by the lie; and the things still more shameful than these, multiform
fabrications of certain beasts and creeping things, of all which Christ has
prevailed, and the heralds of Christ; others in other ways each having shone
according to their own times; and indeed also the Fathers now mentioned; from
whom you are known more, O admirable one, today, than all together of all,
both of old and new narratives. F. For these things I embrace and greet you,
best of peoples, and most Christ-loving, and hottest in piety, and worthy of
those who lead; for I have nothing greater than this to say, nor that by which
I would more welcome you; and I welcome you, with few things by tongue, but
with the many by movements of mind. My people; for I name mine the one of like
mind and like doctrine, and worshipper from the same Fathers, and of the same
Trinity. My people; for mine, even if it does not seem so to those who envy;
and, that those who suffer this may be struck more, behold I give right hands
of fellowship before so many witnesses, both seen and unseen; and I thrust
away an old slander by a new kindness. My people; for mine, even if the
smallest claims the greatest. For such is the grace of the Spirit: it makes
those of like mind of equal honor. My people; for mine, even if from afar;
because we are joined divinely, and in another manner than that which the
thicknesses are. For bodies are joined by place, but souls are fitted together
by Spirit. My people, who formerly philosophized the suffering for Christ, but
now the not doing, if you obey me, but to set as sufficient gain the power of
doing, and to consider that you bring in a service to Christ, as then the
endurance, so in the present the gentleness. A people, against whom the Lord
has been arrayed to do good, as to do evil to the adversaries. A people, whom
the Lord chose for himself out of all whom he called. A people, who has been
painted upon the hands of the Lord, to whom, For you are my will, says the
Lord; and, Your gates are carved work; and whatever later things to those
being saved. A people, and do not wonder at me for the insatiability, if
=36.248= I turn you about often; for I revel in you by the continuity of the
calling, as those who fill themselves insatiably of certain spectacles or
hearings. G. But, O people of God, and ours, beautiful also was your
yesterday's assembly, which you set up upon the sea, and if any other was a
grace of eyes, when I saw the sea tree-covered, and hidden by a handmade
cloud, and beauty of ships, and speed, as if arrayed for a procession, and a
moderate breeze standing at the stern, as if purposely escorting, and sending
along to the city a city of the sea; but more beautiful the one now seen and
more magnificent. For you did not mix yourselves bringing yourselves to the
many, nor did you measure piety by multitude, nor did you endure rather to
become a crowded demos, than a people of God purified by the word; but, as
much as is fitting, having brought in to Caesar the things of Caesar, you
assigned to God the things of God; to the one the tax, to the other the fear;
and having fed the people with your things, you have come also yourselves to
be fed by us. For we too distribute grain, and a grain-distribution perhaps
not worse than yours. Come, eat my bread, and drink wine which I have mixed
for you; I call you with wisdom to my table. For I praise you of the
good-mindedness, and I run toward the eagerness, because you arrived at us as
at your own harbor, having run to the like, and you honored the kindred of the
faith, and you judged it of the absurd things, that those who insult the
things above should be of one mind with one another and be fitted together,
and think that the rottenness of each is set right by the common of the
combination, as of ropes those strengthened by the plaiting, but that you
yourselves should not come together, nor be bound with those of like judgment,
with whom it is more fitting; for we ourselves gather even Godhead. And that
you may know, not having run to us in vain, nor as to strangers and aliens,
but having put in among your own, and having been well guided by the Spirit,
we will philosophize briefly about God to you. And recognize your own things,
as those who discern what is their own by the marks of the weapons. H. I know
two highest differences in the things that are, both mastery and slavery; not
those which among us either tyranny has cut, or poverty has separated, but
those which nature has distinguished, if it is dear to anyone to call it so.
For the first is also above nature. And of these, the one is both productive,
and originating, and unmoved; and the other is made, and under hand, and
changing. And still more briefly =36.249= to speak, the one is above time, and
the other under time. And the one is called God, and stands in three greatest,
cause, and maker, and perfecter; I mean the Father, and the Son, and the Holy
Spirit. Which are neither so separated from one another as to be cut by
nature; nor so narrowed as to be circumscribed into one person; for the one is
of the Arian madness, and the other of the Sabellian godlessness; but it is
more unitary than the things altogether divided, and more abundant than the
things perfectly monadic. And the other is both with us, and is called
creation, even if some things rise above others, according to the proportion
of nearness to God. I. And these things being so, let whoever has the heart
toward the Lord go with us, and let us worship the one Godhead in the three,
bringing no name of lowliness to the unapproachable glory, but bearing the
exaltations of the one God in the three always in the throat. For of which it
is not even possible to speak properly of greatness of nature, because of the
infinite and undefined, how shall we introduce lowliness to this? But whoever
is alienly disposed toward God, and for this cuts the one substance above all
the things that are into inequality of natures, it is a wonder if he will not
be cut by the sword, and his portion be set with the unbelievers, reaping an
evil fruit of an evil opinion, both now and later. J. For concerning the
Father, what need even to speak, of whom even the thing conceded by all is to
spare, they having been preoccupied by the natural conceptions? Even if he
bore the first things of the dishonor, having been first cut into Good and
Maker by the ancient innovation. And concerning the Son, and the Holy Spirit,
consider how we will discourse simply and briefly. If anyone has to say of
these anything changeable or alterable, or measured by time, or place, or
power, or energy, or not naturally good, or not self-moved, or not
self-determined, or a minister, or a hymn-singer, or fearing, or being set
free, or not numbered together; let him show this, and we will be content,
being glorified with the solemnity of fellow-servants, even if we are
penalized of God. But if =36.252= all things whatever the Father has are of
the Son, except the cause; and all things whatever of the Son are also of the
Spirit, except the sonship, and whatever things are said bodily about him
because of my man, and my salvation, that having taken what is mine, he may
gift his own through the new mixing; cease babbling at last at least, O
sophists of empty words, themselves falling. And why do you die, house of
Israel, that from the Scripture I may mourn you? K. For I for my part
reverence the titles of the Word, being so many, and so high and great, which
even demons respected; and I reverence the equal honor of the Spirit; and I
fear the defined threat to those who blaspheme against it. And blasphemy is
not the theology, but the alienating from the Godhead. And it must be observed
here, that the thing blasphemed was the Lord; and the thing avenged, the Holy
Spirit, clearly as Lord. I cannot bear to be unenlightened after the
enlightenment, counterfeiting anything of the three, into whom I have been
baptized, and truly to be buried in the water, being perfected not into
regeneration, but into death. L. I dare to utter something, O Trinity; and
pardon for the mindlessness; for the danger is about a soul. I too am an image
of God, of the glory above, even if I have been set below. I am not persuaded
to be saved by the equal-honored. If the Holy Spirit is not God, let him first
be made God, and so let him deify me the equal-honored. But now what is the
deceit of the grace, or rather, of those giving the grace, so as to believe
into God, and to go up godless? to confess one thing, and to be taught
another? what thefts and deceits of the words, carrying through another
question and confession into another? Woe of the brightness, if after the bath
I have been blackened, if I see those not yet purified brighter, if I am diced
by the evil opinion of the baptizer, if I seek a better Spirit, and do not
find it! Give me a second bath, and think badly about the first. Why do you
envy me of the perfect regeneration? why do you make me, being a temple of the
Spirit as of God, a dwelling of a creature? why do you honor the one of my
things, and dishonor the other, badly judging Godhead, that you may cut the
gift for me, or rather, me myself by the gift? Either honor the whole, O new
theologian, =36.253= or dishonor the whole, that, even if you are impious, you
may at least be consistent with yourself, not judging unequally a nature of
equal honor. M. And the sum of the word: Glorify with the cherubim, gathering
the three holinesses into one lordship, and showing so much of the first
substance as the wings open to the laborious. Be enlightened with David,
saying toward the light: In your light we shall see light, as if in the Spirit
the Son; than which what would become more far-shining? Thunder with John, the
son of thunder, sounding nothing low about God, nor from earth, but high and
lifted (knowing both the one who is in the beginning, and is toward God, and
God Word, as God, and true God from true Father, but not a good
fellow-servant, honored by the title of son only; and the other Paraclete,
that is another of the one speaking; and the Word was of God); and when you
read, I and the Father are one, mirroring the conjunction of the substance;
and when, We will come to him, and will make an abode with him, considering
the divided of the hypostases; and when, the name of Father, and of Son, and
of Holy Spirit, the three properties. N. Be inspired with Luke, conversing
with the Acts of the apostles. Why are you ranked with Ananias and Sapphira
the new embezzlers (if indeed the theft of one's own is truly new), and
embezzling these things, not silver, nor any other of the cheap and small,
such as a golden tongue, or a plain one, or a didrachm, as once a greedy
soldier, but stealing Godhead itself, and lying, not to a man, but to God,
which you have heard? What? Do you not even reverence the authority of the
Spirit, blowing upon whom he wills, and when, and how much? He sojourns with
those about Cornelius before the baptism, with others after the baptism
through the apostles. So that from both sides, both from the things upon which
he comes as master, but not as slave, and from the things for which he is
sought toward the perfecting, the Godhead of the Spirit is testified. O.
Theologize with Paul, the one taken up to third heaven; sometimes numbering
together the three hypostases, and this interchangeably, not having kept to
the orders, numbering before, numbering in =36.256=, numbering under the same.
For what? That he may show the equal honor of the nature. And sometimes
mentioning the three; and sometimes some, or one, as the lacking altogether
following; and sometimes giving the energy of God to the Spirit, as differing
in nothing; and sometimes, instead of the Spirit, bringing on Christ; and when
he divides the hypostases, saying, one God, from whom are all things, and we
into him; and one Lord Jesus Christ, through whom are all things, and we
through him. And when he gathers the one Godhead; that from him, and through
him, and into him are all things, that is through the Holy Spirit, as is shown
in many places of the Scripture. To him the glory unto the ages of the ages.
Amen.
