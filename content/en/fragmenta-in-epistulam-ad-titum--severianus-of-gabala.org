* Fragments on the Epistle to Titus
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Tit 1:12-13a Since the poet who said Cretans are always liars, evil beasts,
idle bellies, taking hold of them said that they badly show a tomb of Zeus who
is not, and Paul used the same verse, lest he be thought to run with the poet
and to witness immortality to the so-called Zeus, he added: this testimony is
true. That one lied, advocating for Zeus, but I speak truly saying that the
Cretans are always liars and idle bellies. This testimony is true, not that
one. Tit 1:13b Since often praising he teaches gentleness, but he found the
Cretans having need as of a goad of rebuke, for this reason he said that
rebuke sharply. Tit 1:15-16 All things indeed are clean to the clean. God made
all things clean; and if they are unclean, the accusation touches the one who
created. And this is: they confess to know God, but by works they deny. Tit
2:3 He showed that he calls the women into a lot; for he would not have said
priestly. Tit 2:7 He took the [word] of indifference upon the mind, and that
of gravity upon the work, that he may not only keep from a corruptible work
but also from a thought.
