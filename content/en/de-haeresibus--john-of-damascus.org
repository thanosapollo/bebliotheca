* On Heresies
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

On heresies in brief, one hundred, whence they began and from where they have
come. Of all heresies the mothers and prototypes are four: 1 such as
Barbarism, 2 Scythism, 3 Hellenism, 4 Judaism, from which all the others
sprang up.

1 Barbarism, which by itself lasted from the days of Adam for ten generations
of Noah. And it has been called Barbarism from the fact that the men of that
time did not have some leader or one agreement, but that each one followed
himself and a law for himself came about according to the preference of his
own will.

2 Scythism, from the days of Noah and afterward until the building of the
tower and of Babylon and after the time of the tower for a few years, that is
until Peleg and Reu, who, having turned toward the climate of Europe to the
part of Scythia, were also assigned to their own nations from the age of Terah
and beyond, from whom the Thracians have come.

3 Hellenism, beginning from the times of Serug with idolatry, and as each one
at that time followed according to some superstition toward what was more
political and toward customs and ordinances, the races of men having begun to
be ranged with idols, whom they then followed, they deified, and at first
drawing in colors and imaging those then honored among them, either sorcerers
or some who had done something in life seeming worthy of memory through
prowess or strength of bodies, then from the times of Terah, father of
Abraham, having also introduced the error of idolatry through statues, having
honored their own forefathers through likenesses and having fashioned those
who had died before them from the ceramic art first, then imitating with each
art, builders having hewn stone, silversmiths and goldsmiths having wrought
from their own material, and so also carpenters and those in succession. And
Egyptians together with Babylonians and Phrygians and Phoenicians became the
first introducers of this religion with statue-making and mysteries; from whom
the first things were transferred to the Greeks from the age of Cecrops and in
succession, and afterward and much later they proclaimed as gods those around
Cronus and Rhea, Zeus and those in succession and Apollo. And they have been
called Hellenes from a certain Hellen, a man of those who dwelt in Hellas; but
as others say, from the olive at Athens. And the Ionians have become leaders
of these, as accuracy has it, from Javan, one man of those who built the
tower, when the tongues of all were divided, for which cause all have also
been called Meropes because of the divided speech. And later Hellenism was set
into heresies further down in time, I mean of Pythagoreans and Stoics and
Platonists and Epicureans; and for the rest there was a character of godliness
together with the law according to nature living as a citizen, separating
itself from these nations, from the foundation of the world until now, being
in the middle of Barbarism and Scythism and Hellenism, until the godliness of
Abraham was joined. 4 And afterward Judaism, having received the character
through circumcision from the times of Abraham and in Moses, seventh from
Abraham, having been written through the law given by God, and from Judah,
fourth son of Jacob who was surnamed Israel, and through David the prophet who
reigned from the tribe of the same Judah having been allotted the completion
of the name of Judaism. Clearly concerning these four heresies the apostle
cutting off said: "For in Christ Jesus there is neither

barbarian, nor Scythian, nor Greek, nor Jew, but a new creation." Differences
of Greeks.

5 Pythagoreans or Peripatetics: Pythagoras dogmatized the monad and providence
and the forbidding of sacrifice to the so-called gods, and not to partake of
living things, and to abstain from wine. And he distinguished at the same time
the things from the moon and above as immortal, and those below as mortal; and
transmigrations of souls from bodies into bodies as far as animals and beasts,
and he taught to practice silence at the same time for a time of five years,
and last naming himself a god.

6 Platonists: that god and matter and form exist and the world generated and
perishable, but the soul ungenerated and immortal and divine. And that there
are three parts of it, rational, spirited, appetitive; and that women become
common to all, and that no one has his own wife, but those who wish consort
with those who are willing. And likewise transmigration of souls into bodies
as far as beasts, and together he also dogmatized many gods from the one.

7 Stoics, dogmatizing that the all is body and thinking this sensible world to
be god, and some declared that it has its nature from the substance of fire.
And they define god as mind, and as soul of the whole vault of heaven and
earth that is; and his body the all, as I said, and the luminaries as eyes,
and that the flesh is destroyed and the soul of all is transmigrated from
bodies into body. 8 Epicureans supposed atoms and indivisible bodies, both
homoeomerous and infinite, to be the beginning, and dogmatized that the end of
happiness is pleasure and that neither god nor providence administers affairs.
9 Samaritanism and the Samaritans from this. This is from Jews, before
heresies were established among Greeks and before their doctrines were
constituted, but after the religion of the Greeks existed and in the middle of
Judaism, having taken a pretext from the times of Nabucho donosor and the
captivity of the Jews; being settlers from Assyrians into Judaea and having
received the pentateuch of Moses, the king having sent to them from Babylon
through a priest called Esdras, having all things as Jews, except for
abhorring the nations and not even touching certain things and except for
denying resurrection of the dead and the other prophecies after Moses. Four
nations of Samaritans: 10 Gorothenes, who keep the feasts at other times than
the Jebusites. 11 Jebusites differing from the Gorothenes for the same cause
of the feasts. 12 Essenes, who oppose neither, but feast together with those
who happen by indifferently.

13 Dositheans, living by the same customs as Samaritans, using circumcision
and sabbath and the rest and the pentateuch; and more than the others keeping
abstinence from living things, likewise also in the rest, and living in fasts
most continually. And they also have virginity, and some of them are
continent; others also believe resurrection of the dead, which is foreign to
Samaritans. Seven heresies of Jews. 14 Scribes, who were lawyers and repeaters
of traditions of the elders among them, keeping by more excessive will-worship
customs which they have not learned through the law, but fashioned for
themselves observances of a justification of the legislation.

15 Pharisees, who are interpreted separated; who live the highest and
supposedly more approved than the others, among whom also resurrection of the
dead, which is also among the scribes, and the assent concerning angels and
holy spirit, that it is; and a different way of life, and continence up to a
time and virginity, and fasting twice in the sabbath,


purification of xestai and of platters and of cups as also the scribes, and
tithes and firstfruits and continual prayers and will-worshipping shapes of
dress both through the cloak and the dalmatics or colobia and the widening of
phylacteries, that is signs of the purple and fringes and little pomegranates
on the wings of the cloak, which these happened to be signs of the continence
among them until a time; who also introduced genesis and fate. 16 Sadducees,
who are interpreted most just, who were from a race of Samaritans together and
of a priest named Zadok, denying resurrection of the dead and not receiving
angel nor spirit, but being in all things Jews.

17 Hemerobaptists, who were in all things Jews, but said that no one attains
eternal life, unless he should be baptized each day.

18 Ossenes, who are interpreted most daring. And these were fulfilling all
things according to law, but also used other writings after the law, and
rejected the most of the later prophets. 19 Nasaraeans, who are interpreted
rebels, who forbid all flesh-eating, and do not partake of living things at
all, and as far as Moses and Joshua son of Nun using the holy names of
patriarchs in the pentateuch and believing, I mean Abraham and Isaac and Jacob
and those above and Moses himself and Aaron and Joshua. Andthey dogmatize that
the writings of the pentateuch are not of Moses, and they affirm others
besides them.

20 Herodians were Jews in all things, but Christthey expected Herod and
rendered to him the prize and name of Christ. This is the first tome
containing against these twenty heresies; and in it the hypothesis concerning
the sojourn of Christ and the confession of the truth. These things are in the
second tome of the first book, in which concerning thirteen heresies, the
heresies among Christians.

21 Simonians, those from Simon the magus of the time of Peter the apostle, of
the village Gitthon of Samaria. This man started from Samaritans, having put
on the name of Christ only. And he taught shameful mixing of defilement,
indifference of bodies. And he rejects the resurrection and says that the
world is not of God. And an image of himself as of Zeus and of the harlot with
him named Helen, as a type of Athena, he handed over to his own disciples for
worship. And he said himself to be the father to Samaritans, and the Christ to
Jews.

22 Menandrians, those who began from this Simon through a certain Menander,
but differing toward the Simonians in something, who said that the world had
been generated from angels.

23 Saturnilians, who in Syria strengthened the shame-work of the Simonians,
and proclaiming other things besides the Simonians for more excessive
astonishment, having had the beginning from Saturnilus, and they themselves
according to Menander saying that the world had been generated by angels, but
of seven only, through the mind of the father.

24 Basilidians, celebrants of the same shame-work, from Basilides, who
together with Saturnilus was also a disciple to Simonians and Menandrians and
thinking the like things, but differing in something; for he says there are
365 heavens and he sets angelic names upon these. Wherefore also the year is
of so many days, and the name Abrasax is 365, and this he says is a holy name.

25 Nicolaitans, from Nicolas who was appointed by the apostles over the needs,
who through jealousy of his own wife taught to accomplish the shame-work
together with the others


to his own disciples, and introducing to the world concerning Caulacau and
Protonicus and other barbaric names.

26 Gnostics, who succeeded to the heresies, and more than all these working
the shamefulness madly, and in Egypt called Stratiotics and Phibionites, and
in the upper parts Socratites, and among others Zacchaeans. And others call
them Coldians, and others call them Borborites. These boast of Barbelo and
Bero.

27 Carpocratians, those from a certain Carpocrates of those in Asia. This man
taught to accomplish every shame-work and every practice of sin. And unless
one, he says, pass through all things and accomplish the will of all the
daemons and angels, he will not be able to go beyond the highest heaven nor to
pass the principalities nor the powers. And he said that Jesus had received a
rational soul, and knowing the things above announced them here, and if anyone
should do things like Jesus, he is according to him, and he forbade the law
together with the resurrection of the dead, as the heresies from Simon and
until now. Of this man Marcellina came to be in Rome. And having made an image
in secret of Jesus and Paul and Homer and Pythagoras, he burned incense to
these and worshipped.

28 Cerinthians, who are also Merinthians. These from Cerinthus and Merinthus,
certain Jews boasting circumcision, said that the world had been generated by
angels, and that Jesus according to progress had been called Christ.

29 Nazoraeans, who confess Christ Jesus son of God, but living in all things
according to law.

30 Ebionites, near to the aforesaid Cerinthians and Nazoraeans, and in
something the heresy of the Sampsaeans and Elkesaeans was joined, who say that
Christ was created in heaven and the holy spirit, and that Christ having
sojourned in Adam and [according to time putting off Adam himself and] again
according to time putting him on, they say he accomplished the same in his
incarnate presence. And being Jews they use evangelical things, but they abhor
flesh-eating, they have the water instead of God, and that Christ as a man in
his incarnate presence put on, as I said. They are baptized continually in the
waters both of summer and of winter, for purification supposedly as the
Samaritans.

31 Valentinians, who forbid resurrection of flesh, and set aside the old
covenant, and reading prophets and receiving whatever other things can be
tropologized into likeness of their own heresy. They introduce certain other
mythologies, saying namings of thirty aeons, male-female together, generated
from the father of the all, whom they also think gods and aeons. And that
Christ brought the flesh from heaven and passed through Mary as through a
pipe.

32 Secundians, to whom Epiphanes and Isidore are joined, using the same
syzygies, having thought the like things as Valentinus, but relating other
things besides him in some measure. And they add the things of the shame-work,
teaching. And they themselves also forbid the flesh.

33 Ptolemaeans, being themselves also disciples of Valentinus, to whom Flora
is joined; and these too say the same things concerning the syzygies just as
Valentinians and Secundians, but they also differ in something. This is also
the recapitulation of the second tome of the first book of the thirteen
heresies. These things are also in the third tome of the first book, in which
there are thirteen heresies.

34 Marcosians. A certain Mark has been a fellow-student of Colorbasus, two
principles and


this man also introducing. And he sets aside resurrection of the dead, and by
certain fantasies through cups changing from incantation into a dark-blue
color and a purple he initiated the deceived women. And he himself also wishes
all things to be led by the twenty-four elements similarly to Valentinus.

35 Colorbasaeans. And this Colorbasus likewise relating the same things, but
differing in something toward other heresies, I mean toward those around Mark
and Valentinus, taught the projections and ogdoads otherwise.

36 Heracleonites; and these appear to follow the mythology of the ogdoads,
otherwise than Mark and Ptolemaeus and Valentinus and the others. But also
toward the end they redeem those dying among them similarly to Mark through
oil, opobalsam and water, saying certain invocations in Hebrew words upon the
head of the one supposedly redeemed.

37 Ophites, who glorify the serpent and think this to be Christ, and having by
nature a serpent, the creeping thing, in a certain chest.

38 Cainites together with the other heresies likewise that set aside the law
and the one who spoke in the law think the same things. Denying resurrection
of flesh, they glorify Cain saying that he is of the stronger power. And
together they also deify Judas, and together those around Korah and Dathan and
Abiram, but also the Sodomites themselves.

39 Sethians. These again glorify Seth saying that he is from the mother above
who repented over those around Cain whom she had projected, then after Cain
was cast out and Abel was killed, having come together with the father above
she made Seth a pure seed, from whom thereafter the whole race of men was
brought down. And they themselves also principalities and powers and whatever
the others dogmatize.

40 Archontics. These again refer40 the all to many rulers and say that the
things that come to be have been generated from these; and they are also
caught in a certain shamefulness. And they set aside resurrection of flesh and
reject the old covenant. And they use old and new covenant, handling each
expression toward their own mind. 41 Cerdonians, from Cerdon who succeeded to
Heracleon in the partnership of the error, and added to the deceit; who having
migrated from Syria to Rome set forth his own preaching in the times of bishop
Hyginus. And this man preaches two principles opposed to one another, and that
Christ is not generated; and likewise he sets aside resurrection of the dead
and the old covenant.

42 Marcionists. Marcion starting from Pontus was son of a bishop, and having
corrupted a virgin he fled because he had been driven out by his own father
from the church. This man having gone up to Rome, then having asked repentance
of those leading the church at that time and not having obtained it, was
lifted up against the faith and dogmatized three principles, good and just and
base, and that the new covenant is alien to the old and to the one who spoke
in it. Both this man and the Marcionists from him set aside resurrection of
flesh, they give baptism not only one, but also two and three after falling,
and on behalf of the dead and of catechumens others among them are baptized.
And among them even women are permitted without fear to give the bath.

43 Lucianists. A certain Lucian, not the one now in the times of Constantine,
but more ancient, dogmatized all things according to Marcion, and besides
these he also dogmatizes certain other things besides Marcion supposedly.

44 Apelleans. And this Apelles the like things to Marcion and Lucian


dogmatizes, he reviles the whole creation and the one who has made it. But not
similarly to these did he set forth three principles, but one principle and
one God being highest and unnameable, and that this one has made another. And
this one, he says, who came to be, having been found wicked, by his own
baseness made the world.

45 Severians. A certain Severus again riding upon Apelles rejects wine and
mythologizing that the vine has grown from the dragon-formed Satan and earth,
when they came together with one another. And he declines woman saying that
she exists of a left power. And he brings in certain namings of rulers and
apocryphal books. And similarly to the others he sets aside the resurrection
of the flesh and the old covenant.

46 Tatianists. This Tatian flourished together with Justin the most holy
martyr and philosopher, and after the death of the holy Justin he was
corrupted toward the doctrines of Marcion and dogmatized equal things to him,
having added also other things besides that man. And he was said to start from
Mesopotamia. These are the chapters of the three tomes of the first book
concerning the forty-six heresies. Recapitulation of the second book, fourth
tome, in which there are 18 heresies.

47 Encratites, who being a fragment of Tatian reject marriage, saying that
this is of Satan, and forbidding all eating of living things.

48 Those of the Cataphrygians and Montanists and Ascodrugites, who receive old
and new covenant, but introducing other prophets, boasting a certain Montanus
and Priscilla.

49 Pepuzians, who are also Quintillians, to whom Artotyrites are joined, two
heresies. They are from the Cataphrygians themselves, but they dogmatize other
things besides those, deifying a certain deserted city Pepuza in the middle of
Galatia and Cappadocia and Phrygia and thinking this Jerusalem (and there is
also another Pepuza), and assigning to women the ruling and the priesthood.
And they initiate certain things, piercing a young boy with bronze needles as
those of the Cataphrygians, and having mixed flour with his blood and made
bread they partake of an offering. And they mythologize that to Quintilla or
Priscilla Christ has been revealed there in Pepuza in the form of a female.
And they use old and new covenant likewise, transforming them according to
their own mind.

50 Quartodecimans. These accomplishing the pascha on one day of the year, on
whatever day the fourteenth of the moon should fall, whether on sabbath or on
the Lord's day, both fasting that day and at the same time keeping vigil they
feast.

51 Alogi, called so by us, who set aside the gospel according to John and his
Apocalypse because they do not receive the God Word who came from the Father,
being always.

52 Adamiani, from a certain living man called Adam, have the doctrine of
mockery rather than of truth. And something of this sort happens: Naked they
gather as from the mother, men and women to the same place, and thus they
accomplish the readings and the prayers and whatever else, supposedly both
living as monks and being continent and not receiving marriage they think
their own church paradise. 53 Sampsaeans and Elkesaeans, still until now
inhabiting Arabia lying above the Dead Sea; who having been deceived from a
certain Elxai, a false prophet, of whose kindred there still until now existed
Marthus and Martha, women worshipped by the heresy as goddesses, having all
things similarly of the Ebionites.


54 Theodotians, those from Theodotus the cobbler from Byzantium. This man
having become extreme in Hellenic education, and together with others of those
in the days of the persecution then, having fallen alone while those others
bore witness through God. Since therefore this man having fled was reproached,
he devised to say that Christ is a mere man, because of the charge, that he
denied God.

55 Melchizedekians. These honoring Melchizedek, saying that he is some power
and not a mere man and having dared to refer all things to his name and to
speak.

56 Bardesanists. This Bardesanes started from Mesopotamia; at first existing
of the true faith and distinguished in philosophy, but having declined from
the truth he dogmatized similarly to Valentinus except certain things, in
which he differs toward Valentinus.

57 Noetians. This Noetus was from Smyrna of Asia. And having been carried on a
chariot together with others he says Christ is son-father; he taught that the
same is father and son and holy spirit. And he said himself to be Moses and
his own brother Aaron.

58 Valesians. These, as we have supposed, are those who inhabit Bakatha, a
mother-village of Arabian Philadelphia, who castrate those who happen by and
are hosted among them; and the most even among themselves are eunuchs, having
been cut. And they teach certain other things full of heresy, rebelling from
the law and the prophets and introducing certain other shame-works.

59 Cathari, these together with Novatus the Roman having been joined, and they
reject the twice-married altogether and do not receive repentance. 60
Angelics. These have altogether died out, boasting that they have an angelic
rank or because they have been called to angels.

61 Apostolics, who are also Apotactics. And these around Pisidia, receiving
only apotactics. And they are near to the Encratites, but they think other
things besides them.

62 Sabellians, who glorify the like things as Noetus except for not saying
that the father suffered, saying the Word is uttered and again poured back. 63
Origenists, those from a certain Origen; and these 63 are shame-doers, doing
unspeakable things and delivering their own bodies to corruption. 64 Other
Origenists, of Origen who is also Adamantius thecomposer, who reject the
resurrection of the dead, and introducing Christ as a creature and the holy
spirit, and allegorizing paradise and heavens and all the other things, and
talking nonsense that the kingdom of Christ has sometime ceased and that the
angels will likewise be made to cease, and they tell marvels that Christ will
be reigned over together with the devil and that Christ was crucified to
daemons. These of the fourth tome, and of the second book, 18 heresies. These
things are in the fifth tome, and of the second book, five heresies. 65
Paulianists, from Paul of Samosata. This Paul all but affirms that Christ is
non-existent, having shaped him as an uttered word, and that he is from Mary
and until now, having the things said concerning him in the divine scriptures
as pre-announced, but not being, but from Mary and until now from the
incarnate presence having the existence.

66 Manichaeans, who are also Aconites. These disciples of Manes the Persian,
saying Christ in appearance, but worshipping sun and moon and praying to stars
and powers and daemons, introducing two principles, both wicked and good,
always existing; and that Christ has appeared and suffered in seeming,
blaspheming the old covenant and the God who spoke in it, defining that not
the whole world, but a part, has been generated from God.


67 Hieracites. These from Hieracas of Leontopolis of Egypt, a certain exegete,
setting aside resurrection of flesh, and using old and new covenant,
forbidding marriage altogether, and receiving monks and virgins and the
continent and widows, saying that children not yet come to age do not share in
the kingdom because they have not contended. 68 Meletians, who in Egypt are a
schism, not a heresy, not praying together with those who have fallen in the
persecutions, but now joined to Arians.

69 Arians, who are also Ariomanites and Diatomites, who say the son of God is
a creature and the holy spirit a creature of a creature, affirming that the
savior received flesh only from Mary, but not also a soul. These of the fifth
tome, of the second book, five heresies. These things are in the first tome of
the third book, seven heresies. 70 Audians, a schism and a rebelling, yet not
a heresy. These possess a well-ordered conduct and life, and they are in all
things having the faith as the catholic church, the most dwelling in
monasteries and not praying together with all. And they use especially the
apocrypha, blaming to satiety the bishops among us as rich, and others for
other things. And they make the pascha in a peculiar way with Jews. And they
have something private and contentious, interpreting "according to the image"
most dryly.

71 Photinians. This Photinus starting from Sirmium thought the like things as
Paul of Samosata, but differed toward him in something. And this man also
affirms Christ from Mary and until now.

72 Marcellians, those from Marcellus from Ancyra of Galatia. This man having
thought at the beginning similarly to Sabellius was rumored; and having come
to defense often and having defended in writing he was accused by others of
remaining in the same things. And perhaps having changed his mind he maybe
corrected himself, or his disciples. Certain orthodox in the middle have made
a defense over his words.

73 Semi-Arians; who confess Christ a creature, but by irony saying he is a
creature not as one of the creatures. But, they say, we call him son, and
because of not attaching passion to the father through having begotten we call
him created. Likewise also concerning the holy spirit they define it a
creature altogether, throwing out the homoousion of the son, and they wish to
say homoiousion. And others from them have also thrown out the homoiousion.

74 Pneumatomachi. These concerning Christ speak well in something, but they
blaspheme the holy spirit, defining it created and not being from the godhead,
rather that it has been created catachrestically through activity, saying it
is a sanctifying power only.

75 Aerians. This Aerius started from Pontus; and still until now this is a
temptation to the life. And he became a presbyter of the bishop Eustathius who
was slandered among Arians; and since Aerius himself was not appointed bishop,
he dogmatized many things against the church, being in faith a most complete
Arian, but he dogmatizes more excessively. One must not, he says, offer for
those who have fallen asleep, and he forbids to fast on fourth-day and the day
before sabbath and the forty days and pascha; he denounces renunciation, he
uses all kinds of flesh-eatings and foods without fear. And if anyone of his
should wish to fast, not on appointed days, he says, but when you wish; for
you are not, he says, under law. And he says that a bishop differs in nothing
from a presbyter.

76 Aetians, those from Aetius the Cilician who became a deacon under George
the


bishop of the Arians of Alexandria, who are alsoAnomoeans called, and among
some Eunomians through a certain Eunomius who became a disciple of Aetius;
with whom Eudoxius also was, but supposedly through fear toward the emperor
Constantine he separated himself, and he exiled Aetius only. Yet Eudoxius
remained arianizing, yet not according to Aetius. These Anomoeans and the
Aetians altogether alienate Christand the holy spirit from God the Father,
affirming him created, and they say he does not even have some likeness. From
Aristotelian and geometrical syllogisms they wish to present God, and that
Christ supposedly cannot be from God through such ways. And the Eunomians from
them rebaptize all who come to them, and not only, but also those from Arians,
turning the feet of the baptized above according to the head, as a much-sung
account has it. And they say that to have fallen in something, fornication or
another sin, is nothing terrible; for God seeks nothing except that one be in
this their supposed faith only. These likewise are of the sixth tome, and of
the third book, seven heresies. These things are in the seventh tome, and of
the third book, four heresies.

77 Dimoerites, who are also Apollinarians, who do not confess the presence or
incarnation of Christ as perfect, as some dared to say sometime that the body
is consubstantial with the godhead, and some sometime, that he also received a
soul, and they denied this, and some leaning on the saying "the Word became
flesh" denied that he received flesh from created flesh, that is from Mary;
and they said contentiously only that the Word had become flesh, and later
considering, what I do not know to say, they say he did not receive a mind.

78 Antidicomarianites, who say that the holy Mary the ever-virgin after
bearing the savior was joined to Joseph.

79 Collyridians, who on a certain day of the year appointed offer a certain
cake in the name of the same Mary, whom we have named Collyridians. 80
Massalians, who are interpreted Euchetes. And to these are also joined of the
heresies practiced from Greeks these so-called Euphemites and Martyrians and
Sataniani. This is the recapitulation of the seventh tome. Chapters of the
impious doctrine of the Massalians taken up from their book. That Satan
cohabits with the man in-hypostatically and in all things lords over him. That
Satan and the daemons hold the mind of men, and the nature of men is
communicative of the spirits of wickedness. That Satan and the holy spirit
cohabit in the man and that not even the apostles were clean of the energy
being energized and that not even baptism perfects the man nor the partaking

And it is brought down from Ishmael who was born from Hagar to Abraham;
wherefore they are named Hagarenesand Ishmaelites. And they call them Saracens
as empty from Sarah because of Hagar having said to the angel: Sarah sent me
away empty. These then having idolized and worshipped the dawn star and
Aphrodite, whom they also named Chabar in their own tongue, which signifies
great. Until then the times of Heraclius they idolized openly, from which time
and until now a false prophet sprang up for them named Mamed, who having
happened upon the old and new covenant, likewise having consorted with an
Arian supposedly monk constituted a heresy of his own. And on a pretext of the
seeming of godliness having taken the nation to himself, from heaven


he noise-spreads that a writing was brought down upon him by God. And having
engraved certain compositions in the book from him, worthy of laughter, he
thus hands over the worship to them. He says there is one God maker of the
all, neither having been begotten nor having begotten. He says that Christ is
Word of God and his spirit, but created and a slave, and that from Mary, the
sister of Moses and Aaron, he was born without seed.

For the Word, he says, of God and the spirit entered into Mary, and she bore
Jesus being a prophet and a slave of God. And that the Jews having broken the
law wished to crucify him and having seized they crucified his shadow, but
Christ himself was not crucified, he says, nor did he die; for God took him to
himself into heaven because he loves him. And this he also says, that, Christ
having gone up into the heavens, God asked him saying: O Jesus, did you say
that I am son of God and God? And Jesus answered, he says: Be merciful to me,
Lord; you know that I did not say nor am I proud to be your slave; but the men
the transgressors wrote that I said this word, and they lied against me, and
they are deceived. And God answered, he says, to him: I know that you did not
say this word.

And telling many other marvels in such a composition worthy of laughter, he
snorts that this was brought down toward him from God. And we saying: And who
is the one witnessing that God has given him a writing, or who of the prophets
foretold that such a prophet arises, and being at a loss toward them, as Moses
received the law of God according to the Sinai mountain at the sights of all
the people, God having appeared in cloud and fire and gloom and storm, and
that all the prophets from Moses and in succession proclaimed beforehand
concerning the presence of Christ and that Christ is God and son of God coming
incarnate and to be crucified dying and to be raised and that this is judge of
living and dead, and we saying, how did your prophet not come thus, others
witnessing concerning him, but not even while you were present did God, as to
Moses with all the people seeing, the mountain smoking, give the law, and to
that one furnish the writing, which you say, in order that you also may have
the firm thing, they answer, that God does whatever he wishes. This we also,
we say, know, but we ask how the writing came down to your prophet. And they
answer, that, while he sleeps, the writing came down upon him. And we say the
laughable thing toward them, that thereafter, since sleeping he received the
writing and did not sense the energy, the thing of the popular proverb has
been fulfilled in him. Again we asking: How, he having commanded you in your
writing to do or receive nothing without witnesses, did you not ask him, that
first you yourself show through witnesses that you are a prophet and that you
came out from God, and which writing witnesses concerning you, they are silent
being ashamed.

Toward whom we reasonably say: Since it is not permitted to you to marry a
woman without witnesses nor to buy nor to acquire, nor do you yourselves
accept to have donkeys or a beast unwitnessed, you have both women and
possessions and donkeys and the rest through witnesses, but faith and writing
alone you have unwitnessed; for the one who handed this over to you has the
firm thing from nowhere nor is some pre-witness of that man known, but he even
received this sleeping. And they call us associators, because, he says, we
introduce an associate to God saying that Christ is son of God and God. Toward
whom we say, that the prophets and the scripture have handed this over; and
you, as you insist, receive the prophets. If then we badly say that Christ is
son of God, those men taught and


handed over to us. And some of them say, that we having allegorized the
prophets have added such things, and others say, that the Hebrews hating us
deceived us as having written from the prophets, in order that we may perish.
And again we say toward them: You saying that Christ is Word of God and
spirit, how do you revile us as associators? For the Word and the spirit is
inseparable from that in which it has been born; if then it is in God as his
Word, it is clear that it is also God. But if it is outside of God, God
according to you is without word and without breath. Therefore fleeing to
associate God you have cut him. For it was better for you to say that he has
an associate, than to cut him and as a stone or wood or some of the unfeeling
things to intro duce. So that you speaking falsely call us associators; but we
name you cutters of God. And they slander us as idolaters worshipping the
cross, which they also abhor. And we say toward them: How then do you rub
yourselves on a stone according to your Chabathan and love the stone kissing
it? And some of them say, that upon it Abraham had intercourse with Hagar, and
others, that upon it he tied the camel being about to sacrifice Isaac. And we
answer toward them: The scripture saying that it was a wooded mountain and
woods, from which also Abraham having split for the whole-burnt-offering laid
upon Isaac, and that with the servants he left the donkeys. From where then
for you the talking nonsense? For neither do wooded woods lie there nor do
donkeys travel through. They are ashamed, yet they say the stone to be of
Abraham. Then we say: Let it be of Abraham, as you talk nonsense; this then
kissing, because only Abraham upon it had intercourse with a woman or because
he tied the camel, you are not ashamed, but you correct us, that we worship
the cross of Christ, through which the strength of daemons and the deceit of
the devil has been dissolved. And this, which they say a stone, is a head of
Aphrodite, whom they worshipped, whom they also named Chabar, upon which even
until now a shadow of a carving appears to those who perceive accurately. This
Mamed having composed many nonsenses, as has been said, set an appellation
upon each of these, as the writing "of the woman" and in it he legislates to
take four women openly and concubines, if he can, a thousand, as many as his
hand may hold subject from the four women. And whichever he should wish to
send away, which he should wish, and to bring another, having legislated from
such a cause. Mamed had a fellow-laborer named Zeid.

This man had a beautiful woman, of whom Mamed was enamored. While they were
sitting then Mamed says: So-and-so, God commanded me to take your woman.

And he answered: You are an apostle; do as God said to you; take my woman. Or
rather, that we may speak from above, he said toward him: God commanded me,
that you send away your woman. And he sent her away. And after other days he
says: That I also take her, God commanded. Then having taken and having
committed adultery with her he set such a law: Let the one who wishes send
away his woman. And if after the sending away he turn back upon her, let
another marry her. For it is not permitted to take her, unless she be married
by another. And if also a brother send away, let his brother who wishes marry
her. And in this same writing he commands such things: Work the earth, which
God gave you, and make it beautiful, and do this and thus, that I may not say
all things as that man shameful. Again a writing of the camel of God,
concerning which he says, that there was a camel from God and it drank the
whole river and did not pass between two mountains because of not being
contained. A people then, he says, was in the place, and on the one day he
himself drank the water, and the camel on the next. And drinking the water it
nourished them furnishing the milk instead of the water. Those men then rose
up, he says, being wicked and they killed the


camel; and there existed an offspring of it, a small camel, which, he says,
the mother having been killed cried out toward God, and he took it to himself.
Toward whom we say: From where that camel; And they say, that from God. And we
say: Was another camel brought together with this? And they say: No. From
where then, we say, did it beget? For we see your camel fatherless and
motherless and without genealogy, and having begotten it suffered an evil.

But neither does the one who brought it together appear, and the small camel
was taken up. Your prophet then, to whom, as you say, God spoke, why did he
not learn concerning the camel, where it grazes and who are milking this? Or
even it itself sometime having met evils as the mother was killed or it
entered into paradise a forerunner of you, from which the river will be for
you, which you talk nonsense, of the milk? For you say three rivers flow for
you in paradise: of water, of wine and of milk. If the forerunner camel of you
is outside of paradise, it is clear that it was dried up by hunger and thirst
or others enjoy its milk, and in vain your prophet snorts as having spoken
with God; for the mystery of the camel was not revealed to him. But if it is
in paradise, again it drinks the water, and you are dried in waterlessness in
the middle of the luxury of paradise. And if you should desire wine from the
river going by, water not being present (for the camel drank down the whole),
drinking unmixed you are burned and you rave with drunkenness and you sleep;
and being heavy-headed both after sleep and having revelled from wine of the
pleasant things you forget paradise. How then did your prophet not think these
things, lest they happen to you in the paradise of luxury, nor has he been
concerned concerning the camel, where it now lives? But neither did you ask
him, as he declared to you concerning the three rivers dreaming. But we
clearly promise your wondrous camel to have run before you into souls of
donkeys, where you also are about to live as beastlike. And there is the outer
darkness and unending punishment, a sounding fire, an unsleeping worm and
tartarean daemons.

Again Mamed says: the writing "of the table" and he says, that Christ asked a
table from God, and it was given to him. For God, he says, said to him, that I
have given you and yours an incorruptible table. Again he says a writing "of a
little ox" and certain other words worthy of laughter, which because of the
multitude I think it necessary to run past. Having legislated these to be
circumcised together with women and having ordered neither to keep sabbath nor
to be baptized, having handed over to eat some of the things forbidden in the
law, and to abstain from others; and wine-drinking he forbade altogether.
