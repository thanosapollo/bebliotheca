ORATION 11. Of the same, to Gregory of Nyssa, the brother of the great Basil,
who arrived after the ordination.

1. There is no exchange among things that exist for a faithful friend, nor any
scale of his beauty. A faithful friend is a strong shelter, and a fortified
palace. A faithful friend is a living treasure. A faithful friend is above
gold and much precious stone. A faithful friend is a garden enclosed, a
fountain sealed, opened and partaken of in due season. A faithful friend is a
haven of refreshment. And if he also excel in understanding, how great then?
And if he is also supreme in education, and in education of every kind, both
ours I mean and that which was once ours, how much more splendid? And if he is
also a son of light, or a man of God, or one drawing near to God, or a man of
the better desires, or worthy to be named with some such names as Scripture
uses to honor those who are godly and lofty and of the portion above, this
already is a gift of God, and clearly beyond our desert. And if he also comes
to us from a friend, and this one equal in virtue and in our friendship, still
more delightful and gracious, and more fragrant than the myrrh that adorns
both a priest's beard and the hem of a garment. =35.833=

2. Are these things then sufficient, and has the discourse depicted the man
for you in due measure? Or must we, like the careful among painters, apply the
colors many times, in order that we may present to you a more complete
painting of the discourse? Come then, we will paint him for you more
completely and more clearly. Who among lawgivers is most illustrious? Moses.
Who among priests most holy? Aaron. They were brothers no less in piety than
in body; or rather, the one is God to Pharaoh, and leader and lawgiver of
Israel, going within the cloud, and both spectator and mystagogue of divine
mysteries, and craftsman of the true tabernacle, which the Lord pitched, and
not man; and both alike are priests. For Moses, it says, and Aaron among his
priests; the one a ruler of rulers and a priest of priests, using Aaron as a
tongue, while himself becoming for him the things toward God; the other
immediately after him, but far before the others in dignity and in nearness to
God. Both tormenting Egypt, cutting the sea, leading Israel through, baptizing
the enemies, drawing bread from above, water unbelievable in the desert, the
one giving it forth, the other sweetening it; both warring down Amalek by the
holy stretching out of hands, and by a type of a greater mystery; both toward
the land of promise, both guiding and hastening. Is anything more recognizable
than the image? Has not the painter-discourse clearly portrayed for you the
one who is my namesake and of one soul with me?

3. Of these, the one anointed us, and brought us who were hiding into the
midst; I do not know what he suffered, or how he was moved unworthily of the
Spirit in him; for even if the word is harsher, still it shall be said;
friendship will bear all things, both suffering and hearing. The other has
come to exhort, and to reconcile, and to tame us toward the Spirit. It is a
great thing that even now he has come, for me (and how is it not the greatest?
I who have been set over you for the whole of life); but I blame him that he
is later than the need. How after the defeat and the onslaught is the
alliance, O best of friends and of allies? And after the storm the helmsman,
and after the scar the medicine? Was it that as a lover of your brother you
were ashamed of the tyranny? Or did you yourself as a ruler take the
disobedience ill? Which of the two brothers do you accuse, and which do you
release from blame? I will speak something to you of the words of Job, myself
also in pain, and to a friend, even if not such a one, nor upon like
sufferings. =35.836= To which do you attach yourself? Or whom do you intend to
help? Is it not the one who has much strength? Not the one who has much wisdom
and knowledge? For this I see many of the present judges suffering, who would
more readily grant the greatest things to the lofty than the least to the
humble. This then you yourself would know; for it is not lawful for me to
declare anything unseemly about you, I who set you as the bound and canon of
every good; and at the same time I have been admonished by Scripture not to be
swift to judgment. But I am ready to give an account, both to you and to
everyone who wishes through friendship, of my disobedience, as some
would name it, or of my forethought, as I persuade myself, and of my caution,
that you may know you do not have a friend altogether absurd and ignorant;
but one able in some things even to discern better than the many, and bold
where it is right to be bold, but fearful where there is fear, and of those
things which for those who have sense it is more fearful not even to fear.

4. What then seems good, and what is better? Shall we now render the accounts
to you, do you command this, and do you not reject the time? (Which is a
festival, and not a court of judgment.) Or shall we put this off to another
time and assembly? For the discourse is longer than the present occasion. But
what shall we speak to you worthy of the festival, lest we ourselves dismiss
you fasting, and that though we are the hosts? Let us purify ourselves,
brethren, for the martyrs; or rather, by that by which they too were purified
through blood and the truth. Let us be freed from every defilement of flesh
and spirit; let us wash, let us become clean; let us also present our bodies
and our souls, a living sacrifice, holy, well-pleasing to God, this our
reasonable worship and intercession. For nothing is so precious to the Pure as
purity or purification. Let us contend for the athletes; let us conquer for
the conquerors; let us bear witness to the truth for the martyrs. Let us grant
this to their contests, that we too become crowned, and heirs of the same
glory, both that which exists for them from us and that laid up in the
heavens, of which the things seen are memorials and certain small impressions.
=35.837= Let us contend against the principalities, against the powers,
against the unseen persecutors and tyrants, against the world-rulers of the
darkness of this age, against the spiritual things of wickedness in the
heavenly places, and around the heavenly things against the inward war in
ourselves that is in the passions, against the daily uprisings of the things
that fall upon us from without.

5. Let us bear anger as a beast, and the tongue as a cutting sword, and let us
quench pleasure as fire. Let us set doors to our hearing, well opening and
closing; and let us chasten the eye; let us discipline a raging touch, and a
tearing taste, lest death come up through our windows; for so I think the
senses are called; and let us laugh down the excess of laughter. Let us not
bend the knee to Baal through need, nor through fear worship the golden image.
Let us fear one thing only, to fear anything more than God, and to insult the
image through wickedness. In all things let us take up the shield of faith,
and let us escape all the darts of the evil one. And this too is a terrible
war, and this a great array, and this a great trophy. If we have thus come
together, or are running together, truly the festival is according to Christ,
truly we have honored the martyrs, or shall honor them, truly we dance the
victory-songs. But if we shall gratify the pleasures of the belly, and revel
in things of a season, and bring in the things that are emptied, and take
these as places of drunkenness, not of temperance, and as times of business
and affairs, but not of ascent or deification, if I may dare so to speak, of
which the martyrs are mediators, first I do not even recognize the season. For
what is the chaff to the wheat? And what is the softening of the flesh to the
wrestlings of martyrs? Those things belong to the theaters, these to my
assemblies; those to the unchaste, these to the temperate; those to the
flesh-lovers, these to those being loosed from the body. Then I wish to say
something even bolder, but I spare the blasphemy out of reverence for the day;
only the martyrs do not demand these things of us; for so to speak is more
moderate. =35.840=

6. Let us not then celebrate the holy things in an unholy way, brethren, nor
the lofty things in a lowly way, nor the precious things without honor, nor,
to speak in a word, the things of the Spirit in an earthy way. The Jew also
keeps festival, but according to the letter; the Greek also feasts, but as it
pleases the demons. But for us, as all things are spiritual, deed, motion,
will, word, even as far as gait and garment, even as far as a nod, the Word
reaching into all things and ordering the man according to God; so also is the
keeping of festival, and the being made bright. For I do not forbid
relaxation, but I chasten excess. If we thus come together, and thus keep
festival, it is a great thing that we ourselves too shall attain the same
contests, so to speak, and inherit the same glory; for the things which
neither eye has seen, nor ear heard, nor human mind ever portrayed, fashioning
blessedness at will, these we think are laid up for those purified through
blood, and who have imitated the sacrifice of Christ; but at least we shall
see the brightness of the holy martyrs, for this too is no small thing, as my
discourse has it, and we shall enter into the joy of the same Lord, and by the
light of the blessed and princely Trinity, I well know, we shall be illumined
more clearly and more purely, in whom we have believed, and whom we worship,
and whom we confess before God and men, fearing nothing, being ashamed of
nothing, neither the enemies without, nor the false christs among ourselves
and enemies of the Spirit. And may we confess until the last breath with much
boldness the good deposit of the holy Fathers, of those nearer to Christ, and
of the first faith, the confession that has been our companion from childhood,
which we first uttered, and with which may we last depart, carrying away from
here, if nothing else, piety.

7. And the God of peace, who reconciled us to himself through the cross, we
who had been made enemies through sin; who preached peace to those near and
those far, both those under the law and those outside the law; =35.841= the
Father of love, Love itself; for these things he rejoices to be called before
others, that he may legislate brotherly love even by the names, who gave the
new commandment in so loving one another as we have also been loved; who
granted both to tyrannize well, and to be tyrannized through fear, and to draw
back with reason, and to take heart again through the Word; who both equips
the great flocks, and magnifies the small through grace, himself, according to
the abundance of his own kindness, may he comfort us with much comfort, and
lead us onward, shepherding together and preserving the flock; and may he
equip you for every good work, and persuade you to keep festival spiritually
to the martyrs, and deem you worthy of the luxury from there, where is the
dwelling of all who rejoice, and having appeared in righteousness may he fill
you with his own glory, shown in Christ Jesus our Lord, to whom be the glory
and the might, the honor and the worship unto the ages of ages. Amen.
