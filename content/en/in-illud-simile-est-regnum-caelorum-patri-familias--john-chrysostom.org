* On the Saying, The Kingdom of Heaven Is Like a Householder
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Catechetical discourse on the saying of the Gospel: "The kingdom of heaven is
like a man who is a householder, who went out early in the morning to hire
laborers for his vineyard," and what follows.

=59.577=

1. I see mixed in with the flock of the pious some strange sheep, bearing on
their foreheads the new seal of the heavenly shepherd; and I wish to find
grass of teaching suited both to those who have already tasted of =59.578=
grace, and to those who are about to discern the herb of the Spirit; so that
the pasture of the same catechesis may become for those already enlightened a
reminder of the gifts of God, and for those about to be enlightened a greater
exhortation toward =59.579= baptism. For the word must be mixed for both,
because the God-loving people itself is mixed of both initiated men and men
being initiated.

I have found, then, from where I shall feast you also, all you ancient
household-born of Christ, and how I shall exhort our newly bought servants
toward the freedom of the calling; I will announce to my brothers masterly
sayings able both to make more eager those who long ago took possession of the
royal vineyard, and to wake those who have only just peered into it toward the
like zeal. The kingdom of heaven is like a man who is a householder, who went
out early in the morning to hire laborers for his vineyard. The Master names
himself a householder; and he calls laborers summoned about the third and
sixth and ninth and eleventh hour all the pious, those who in different ages
received baptism; the vineyard he calls the saving commandments, which have
little toil but much gain. The kingdom of heaven is like a man who is a
householder, who went out early in the morning to hire laborers for his
vineyard. And having agreed with them, that is with the laborers, for a
denarius a day, he sent them into his vineyard. I am the householder; for just
as the householder builds a house as he wishes, so I arrange the whole world
as I will. I, the sun of righteousness, having put on the cloud of humanity,
came down together with humanity, and I go about seeking laborers for my
vineyard. I do not need men as vinedressers; for I have angels and archangels
doing what I wish; but I long to see my earthly servants lodging in my
possessions. I run about, and I look round everywhere whom I may find, whom I
may hire, not that I may grow rich by the toils of others, but that I may
enrich those who obey me. I gathered Peter and John and the other disciples,
the unhesitating planters of the inhabited world; I agreed with them for a
denarius a day; I hired out their whole life, and I promised them, for all the
work together, to give as wage the kingdom of heaven; but these alone do not
suffice to satisfy my soul. I cannot bear to see so few farmers farming my
field; I am cramped in my eyes, seeing so many only; still I thirst for other
tillers, still I long for other hired men, still I seek other initiates. Wide
and great is the paradise of my commandments; it holds all who have believed
in me; receiving all who love virtue it becomes still broader.

What then shall I do? From where shall I fill my enclosures? From where shall
I exhort more enrolled men? Let me go out again to their crossroads, let me go
out toward


those who wander the willing wandering, let me go out toward the sluggish, let
me go out toward the poor, I who am rich, that I may make the poor rich? Let
me go out again toward the gathering of the laborers? I will do what is owed;
I will call the poor again also. Mine is to exhort, theirs to be persuaded;
mine is to will to give a wage, theirs is to will to receive; if they obey me,
they will do a favor both to me and to themselves; but if they disobey me,
they will harm themselves; for I will not lack farmers able to dig around and
trench around and prune the vines; my vineyard cannot remain lying fallow or
fruitless. And going out about the third hour, he saw others idle in the
marketplace, and to them he said: Go you also into my vineyard.

And where were you at dawn? Where were you the days before? How did you not
seek me who had come in search of you? How did you not rise early toward what
is profitable for you? Are you so ignorant of the rays of piety that you
reckon the day night? And while the light of truth is shining, why do you have
heavy heads and doze? Now at least be awakened, now at least watch, now at
least turn, now at least come to your senses. Go you also into my vineyard.
See how I love you; I did not confine the preaching to the dawn, I did not
wall off the approach from those who come second, I did not enclo =59.580= se
your turning with the first calling; I granted you every hour toward
repentance; I embrace every age. Even if infants are brought to me, I receive
them rejoicing; for I am of infants creator, and father and mother, and nurse
and food; even if children still lisping take refuge, I am pleased; even if
younger men draw near, I am the more gladdened; even if full-grown men run to
me, I receive them; all who run to me with faith I embrace as my brothers. Go
you also into the vineyard. I do not reckon the loss of the hours that have
passed, if I see eagerness being weighed against sluggishness; I will not
lessen the wages of the day, if you are eager to overtake those who arrived
before you. Leave the vineyard of the Jews, the ungrateful, the thankless,
that knows how to cultivate only vinegar; abandon the vine transplanted from
Egypt, that still carries about the bitterness of Egyptian idolatry, and has
learned to sprout a cluster of bitterness for the planter, and move over to my
vineyard: And whatever is just, I will give you. I am not unjust; I am just by
nature; I am not greedy, I who set forth laws against greed; I am not
ungrateful toward grateful servants, I am not stingy or small-souled toward
those who are great-souled toward me; I will give you wages that surpass the
toils; I will supply gifts that conquer your sweats.

Again he went out about the sixth and ninth hour, and did likewise. I leave no
time unused; I net those who are willing, and I do not grow weary calling
those who are weary in sins toward righteousness. I went out once, and having
hunted I returned; I went out a second time, and having taken them alive I
came back; I went out a third time, and having grown rich I came up again; I
went out a fourth time about the ninth hour, and having taken some I am here.
The more continual I make the course, the more warmly I kindle the longing;
however many I bring to myself, so many others I long for; and having found
those, I seek others; for I will that all men be saved and come to the
knowledge of truth.

2. And about the eleventh hour, going out, he found others standing idle. I do
not cease stripping the devil, so long as this life is present; I will not
cease plundering death, the terrible thief of humanity; I cannot bear that
those who have become mortal from their own judgment should not be changed
toward immortality, even to their last breath. For this reason I do not even
of the eleventh hour


make light; for this reason I sit at the gates of Hades, that I may be able to
transfer some before death toward life. And about the eleventh hour, going
out, he found others standing idle, and he says to them: Why have you stood
here the whole day idle? Have you no church? Have you no prophets? Have you no
apostles? Have you no martyrs? Have you no divine Scriptures? No spiritual
work at all? Why then have you stood here the whole day idle? Do you not see
the sun accomplishing its appointed course? Do you not see the moon serving
the one who created it? Do you not see creation serving the maker? Do you not
see each of the elements working the work for which it came to be? Why then do
you alone insult nature by idleness? Why do you imitate dead pillars? Have you
no eyes, that seeing the beauty of the world you may hymn the world-maker?
Have you no mouth, that you may move your tongues toward hymnody? Have you no
hands, that you may work almsgiving? Have you no feet, that you may run
courses profitable to the soul?

Why have you stood here the whole day idle? Until when do you turn aside from
my lordship? Why have you stood here the whole day idle? And they say to him:
No one hired us. Such are those who allege excuses in sins, those who put off
the present into the future, those who say, Let it go for now, and when God
commands me, then I am baptized; grant me this Pascha, and in the future I am
enlightened. Such are those who say, No one hired us. But what does he say to
them who by kindness conquers the delays of those who postpone the grace? Go
you also into the vineyard. He did not refute their falsehood =59.581=; he did
not pillory the irony of their wandering manners; he did not say to the
sluggish, Why do you say that No one hired us? Why do you stitch falsehood
onto sluggishness as well, no one hired you? Did the prophets not exhort you?
Did the apostles not urge you? Did I not come through myself to you? Did I not
call often, and you did not obey? Did you not see me hiring your companions,
and making agreements with them? Why then did you not wish to imitate those
who were persuaded by me, but remained until old age as spectators? Of these
things the Savior said nothing, but Go you also into my vineyard. I receive
even old men who wish to labor; I receive even gray hairs made bright by
piety, I receive even trembling limbs sinewed by faith; I am glad to see old
men holding both staffs and crosses together.

Go you also into my vineyard. You offered youth to the devil, lend even old
age to me; you granted strength to the enemy, give even the useless age to me;
you served the tyrant the whole time as he wished, give the remaining time to
me, the lawful king. Become faithful before you become dead; receive before
death the robe of immortality; bury yourselves before the end in the
incorruptible grave-clothes of grace; run, make haste to be found within the
vineyard before evening; contend, that the setting sun may leave you inside.
If before the setting of the sun you number yourselves with my lovers, you
will see the glory of my divinity, as lovers of light; if you come out of the
vineyard with those who have been called, I will recompense you who have not
toiled as those who have toiled; if the night of death overtake you standing
outside the court, I no longer bear to have you as friends; for a dead man
does not believe, a dead man does not confess, a dead man does not work and is
not entrusted with a mystery, a dead man does not work temperance, a dead man
cannot demand a wage, an unsealed dead man is condemned even before the
judgment; the living and healthy will praise the Lord; such a dead man lives
even after death, such a dead man blooms again after burial. Go you also into
the vineyard.


While you still have a little remaining term of life, do business; do not wait
for the necessity of sickness to take the crown of your own judgment. Do not
wish, at the time of death, to change the provisions of immortality; in life
traffic for life; in health and sobriety receive the talent, that you may gain
the working also. Go you also into the vineyard; and whatever is just, you
will receive, And beyond what is just, you will receive; only repent, only
come near, only be with me. You will not blame my exhortation; for no one who
has believed in me has gone away ungrateful, nor has he who served me remained
still poor. Thus the householder of this house filled this vineyard.

And when evening had come. Already the present life having flowed away as a
day, already the long night of death having arrived, already the resurrection
of the dead having risen, like some dawn that has no evening as successor, the
lord of the vineyard says to his steward: Call the laborers.

Call all those from Adam; call Abel who was sacrificed through sacrifice; call
Enoch who was well-pleasing and was translated; call Noah, the good helmsman
of the shipwrecked nature; call most faithful Abraham, who was a lover of God
rather than a lover of children, who campaigned against his own inward parts,
who kept the faith; call Joseph the worker of temperance, call the patriarchs,
call the prophets; call Elijah who drove well and became charioteer of fiery
chariots; call the apostles, the luminaries of the New Covenant; call the
martyrs, and those who imitated my death on their behalf; call Stephen who was
stoned for me; call Barlaam who for the guarding of the vineyard had his right
hand burned; call all who have toiled well; call those who have cultivated my
possessions well, And give them the wage.

Open my treasures, open my storehouses, open the bright palaces of my kingdom;
bring forth the royal crowns, bring forth the divine prizes; spare none of my
=59.582= treasures. For these did not spare their own souls for me. Crown the
heads of those who have contended, as I wished; put the prizes into the hands
that have toiled. Let no one go out from here uncrowned, let no one return
unrewarded, let no one be deprived of the recompenses; let all grow rich, let
all dance, and let them see for what I hired them; let them see of what goods
those who obey me obtain; let them see of what gifts those who are called and
do not wish to come deprive themselves. Give them the wage, beginning from the
last even to the first, gladdening the last first with the gifts; for these
especially have need of my goodness. Those indeed, having begun the toils
straightway from early morning together with the day, have the day itself and
the hours and the virtues and the contests and the covenants as advocates; but
these last, being destitute of all these, look only toward my love of mankind.
To these last then first hand the gifts, and then you will hand the
recompenses to the first.

And those who came about the eleventh hour received one denarius. And turning
this about gladly in their own hands they said: We were in danger of
disbelieving the good things; for they knew they had worked nothing worthy of
what they received. And seeing the gift with their eyes, and holding the grace
in their palms, they danced, they kept festival at the good things beyond
hope. But the first coming supposed that they would receive more, and they
also received one denarius. And having received they murmured against the
householder, saying: These last. The master brought this in by the word, not
that they might envy those honored (for they rejoice, they are not indignant
when all


the holy are being saved), but that he might show his own love of mankind to
be such as to beget envy even in the unenvious, if it were possible.

3. And having received they murmured against the householder, saying, These
last, these who have grown old, these who have scarcely come to know your
lordship, who have scarcely bent the knees toward your worship, who have
scarcely with trembling fingers marked the sign of your saving cross on their
foreheads, who have not borne baskets of temptations, who have not held
mattocks of calamities; These last have worked one hour. They only saw your
vineyard, they shared the work with us by the eyes alone, they showed their
faces, they accomplished no deeds; they have become witnesses of the toils,
not sharers; And you have made them equal to us who have borne the burden of
the day and the heat? We were burned by pleasures, but we were not quenched by
virtues; we were persecuted for you, we endured countless toils, that we might
widen your substance; and we go from here having received wages like those who
have not grown weary like us; the toil is unlike, and the gain similar; the
labor is unequal, and the glory a match.

These last have worked one hour. That a harlot woman might weep near you for
one hour, and loose the hair which she had plaited badly before this, and kiss
your undefiled feet with temperate lips, and offer you the heavenly one an
earthly myrrh, you gave her the advocacy of a daughter and a virgin. That a
thief crucified for one hour might take refuge with you on the cross and cry,
Remember me, Lord, when you come in your kingdom, for this voice alone you
opened paradise to him. That Paul the persecutor, the enemy, might repent for
one hour, he has been shown forth an Evangelist. These last have worked one
hour, and you have made them equal to us who have borne the burden of the day
and the heat. What then does the man-loving Lord say to those so disposed?
Answering one of them he said: Friend, I do you no injustice. O unsurpassable
love of mankind! The Master is judged by the servant on behalf of the
servants; the judge apologizes to the accuser on behalf of those being judged.
Friend, I do you no injustice.

Judge me according to reason, friend, and do not be pained unjustly; converse
with me lawfully, and do not be vexed unlawfully; say, what injustice were you
yourself done, when your fellow-servant was honored? say, what harm did you
yourself suffer, when your brother still reigns together? Did you not receive
that for which you toiled? Did you not receive the complete wage of the day
=59.583= whole? Did you not get back what you longed for? What else then do
you seek besides? Did you not agree with me for a denarius? Remind yourself of
my promises, on which I hired you at the beginning. If I have done anything
contrary to what was agreed, you are justly indignant; but if I have fulfilled
the covenants, you accuse in vain the one who cannot be accused. Did you not
agree with me for a denarius? Have I lessened any of your gifts? Have I taken
yours and given them to others? Have I assigned your glory to others? Have I
not portioned out my own substance to whom I wished? Why then do you murmur,
harmed as a slave, honored as a free man? Why then do you murmur, you who have
been so benefited? Why do you envy your brothers, having an ungrudging master?
Take what is yours, and go. Be content with what is present; do not wish to
reign alone, having been called with many; the kingdom of heaven is enough
both for you and for all who are worthy of it. But I will to give to this last
as also to you. Who will stand against my will? God is the one who justifies,
and who is the one who condemns? God is the one who supplies, who is the one
who calls to account? God is the one who enriches, who is the one able to
strip? The judge is an advocate, who is the accuser? The judge is a
fellow-pleader, what adversary will be found? But I will to give to this last
as also to you. I cannot bear to leave my statues naked of glory; I cannot
bear my image


going in poverty to be overlooked; I cannot bear not to have these also reign
together with me, for whose sake I came down from the heavens. I will to give
to this last as also to you. He came last, I know it too; but he brought me
faith that apologizes for the lateness that has come about; he came last, but
he showed a longing such as I long for; he wandered the time past as a sheep,
but the sheep has come to know the shepherd. This one was mine even when he
roamed. He came last, but he passed through my font, just as you also. He came
last, but he enjoyed my table, just as you also; he came last, but he saw all
the comeliness of my vineyard; he saw the plant of the tree of life; he kissed
the thorns which I wore as a crown for him; he embraced the cross which I
endured for him. I cannot then fail to honor with all eagerness and honor the
one who has so longed for me, who has so believed in me. As you believed, he
believed; as you were well supplied, he was well supplied; as you worshiped,
he worshiped; as you reigned, he reigned; as you toiled, he did not toil, but
I by grace filled up what was lacking. Or is it not lawful for me to do what I
will with my own? For I am not spending yours, am I? I supply my own. For I am
not being generous with yours, am I? I have authority in my own. For I did not
appoint you steward of my judgment, did I? I took you as a hired man from the
beginning. For I did not appoint you lord of my authority, did I? As a lover
of mankind I made you a partner of my own things. If your eye is evil, it
cannot see what eye has not seen and ear has not heard. There is need of an
unclouded gaze for the inspection of the mysteries; there is need of a pure
soul for the understanding of the pure.

You have seen how good our Master is; you have seen how those who come at
different times, but show one faith and eagerness and way of life, he has
deemed worthy of like honors and gifts; you have seen how he has made even the
last equal to the first through the abyss of his compassions. Be drawn then
toward one so loving of mankind; he has hired you also now; he says also to
you: Go you also into the vineyard. God is the one who hires, the wage is
heavenly, the work is saving, the vineyard is ready, the door of the vineyard
is the font. Divine grace standing at the door of the font, like a bright
doorkeeper, cries to all who enter through it: This is the gate of the Lord;
the newly enlightened shall pass through it. We do not envy you as having come
last, and sharing the same glory with us; but we dance with you as brothers,
and we rejoice together, reckoning your salvation our own glory. Long then for
the one who has called; =59.584= take faith as an unerring guide of the good
passage into the vineyard; for faith is a good conductor of the road to the
font, faith is an anchor of storm-tossed nature, faith is a calm harbor of
humanity, faith is a far-shining mirror of the unseen, faith is an accurate
teacher of the consubstantial Trinity, faith has prepared us to say those
saving words: "I believe in one God, Father Almighty." I believe, I do not
search; I believe, I do not pursue the incomprehensible; I believe, I do not
measure the immeasurable. If I believe, I am enlightened in the soul; if I am
overcurious, I darken my reasonings; if I believe well, I am lifted toward
heaven; if I seek overcuriously, I am carried down toward the deep. I believe
in one and only true God and Father Almighty. I deny the gods that have been
led astray by the Greeks; for polytheism does not dwell in heaven. I hymn one
and only God; I hate Arianism; I do not say, There was once when the Father
was not; but I proclaim God always God, the same always Father, being Father
thus as he himself knows. I believe in our Lord Jesus Christ, his Son the
only-begotten, who was begotten of him before all the ages, as he alone who
was begotten knows; begotten, not made; begotten, not


created; begotten, not fashioned; having shone timelessly from the paternal
substance, not having come in from outside from somewhere, not a substituted
or bastard son, but genuine radiance of the genuine Father, unvarying impress
of an invisible form. I know Christ as Son of God the Father as God; I know
Christ as son of his handmaid and mother, appearing as man; I know Christ
consubstantial with men, as man from man; I know Christ invisible and seen; I
know Christ intangible, and handled by Thomas; I know Christ feeding thousands
by a word, and walking upon the waves, and commanding the demons, and raising
the dead, and doing countless wonders by the power of his own divinity; I know
Christ hungering, and thirsting, and growing weary, and being in agony, and
sweating by the nature of humanity; I know Christ as God impassible; I know
Christ having died, and having received the passion in the flesh; I know
Christ having endured the end according to what was possible; I know Christ
having raised the temple of his body.

I recognize the difference of his two natures; I do not divide the one Son
into two sons. I believe also in the Holy Spirit, the Spirit of truth, who
proceeds from the Father, who completes the Trinity; to him I trust my
sanctification, to him I trust the resurrection from the dead.

4. Meditate these things always with soul and tongue; cry these things
continually with mind and mouth. You have the apostolic definition of faith
and of a God-loving way of life; for without faith the works are dead. I speak
humanly because of the weakness of your flesh; For as you presented your
members as weapons of unrighteousness to sin, so now present your members as
weapons of righteousness to God. Let the fornicator become temperate, let the
unjust become just, the robber ready to share, the greedy a lover of the poor,
let him who was inhuman and a lover of gold before this now appear a lover of
Christ, the blasphemer a hymn-speaker, let him who runs to theaters run
henceforth to the courts of God, let him who is mad for the courses of horses
give leisure to the apostles, whose course is saving to those who imitate
them. As being about to be named brides of Christ, so clothe your souls and
your bodies.

Let your gazes be images of the calm according to the soul; let your faces be
clear marks of the piety within; let your mouths send forth the fragrance of
psalms and hymns, orderly, temperate, fitting a virgin about to be married;
let the souls be clean, white, that they may receive the genuine dye of the
=59.585= royal purple. And when you have reached the bridechamber of the
Spirit, when you have run into the bridal chamber of grace, when you have come
near the font that is both fearful and longed for, fall before the king as
captives; all alike throw yourselves upon the knees, and stretching the hands
toward heaven, where our king of all sits royally, and directing the eyes
toward the unsleeping eye, use such words toward the lover of mankind: Open
your vineyard to us, Lord, even if we have arrived about the eleventh hour; do
not be angry with us as having been slow, Master, nor shut the doors of your
love of mankind before our faces; we have condemned our former idleness, we
have condemned the sluggishness of the day that went before, we were harmed by
disobeying, we were damaged by sleeping, we were shipwrecked while taking
pleasure, we were stripped while playing and being played with, we were
destroyed while being entertained by vanities. We have scarcely watched, we
have scarcely become sober, we have scarcely found what is profitable, we have
scarcely fled the war; we have utterly renounced the evil one, we have
renounced giving leisure together to the destructive evils that are also
pleasant; we are henceforth arrayed with the Savior, with the only God.
Receive the


bill of our confession, that fearing the penalty we may remain in what we have
also agreed; receive the symbol of the covenants toward you, that being
ashamed of the symbol we may establish our manner unturned.

Pity your servants, the captives, and naked, and dead; give us back the
ancient kinship; clothe those whom the devil stripped; anoint us with the oil
of your holy anointing; that we may not be harmed if we are bitten again by
the serpent. We wish to work your commandments, we wish to be found within the
vineyard, we wish as drawing near you to be named laborers; we have lived all
our days =59.586= badly. We wish to live the remaining ones well. Would you
forgive as fornicators. Forgive. Would you treat us as tax-collectors; would
you show love of mankind as to thieves; would you save as persecutors; save,
as you will; have mercy on those repenting, toward whom you were
long-suffering while they sinned; have mercy on those falling before you, whom
you pitied even when they were kicking; have mercy on those entreating, whom
you bore when they were insulting. Open to us who are storm-tossed the harbor
of the font; open to us who have been wounded the painless infirmary; open to
those grown old the griefless furnace; open to those who owe death the
unperilous tomb; open to us the life-bearing impress of your three-day death;
open to us the virgin womb of regeneration; open to us the incorruptible
mother of adoption; open to us the gate of immortality; open to us the door of
the heavens; because you said, Knock, and it shall be opened to you. What you
have said, fulfill in truth; what you have spoken, accomplish as one who does
not lie. We believe that you can do all things; we believe that you can wash
away the filth of our sins; we believe that you can through Spirit and water
regenerate those who have grown old in sins; we believe that you can work the
strange childbirth; we believe that you can bring up from the waters women and
infants and youths and men and old men newly formed as newborn infants; we
believe that you can drown our sins in the waters, just as you flooded Pharaoh
and all his power; we believe that you can find a way of salvation, as once
you made a way in the midst of the sea for the Israelites; we believe that you
can do so many things as you also will.

Saying these things toward the Lord, remain, that the Savior may say also to
you: Do you believe that I can do this? According to your faith be it unto
you. To him be the glory unto the ages of ages. Amen.
