ORATION XII.
To his own father, when he entrusted him with the care of the Church of
Nazianzus.
A. I opened my mouth, and I drew in the Spirit, and I give all that is mine,
and myself, to the Spirit, action and speech, inaction and silence; only let
him hold me, and lead me, and move both hand and mind and tongue toward what
is right and what he wills; and let him lead them back again from what they
must leave, and from what it is better to leave. I am a divine instrument, a
rational instrument, an instrument tuned and struck by the Spirit, the fair
craftsman. Yesterday he was working silence? I made silence my study. Today he
strikes the mind? I will sound the word, and I will make speech my study. I am
neither so talkative as to desire to speak when I am being moved to silence;
nor so silent and untaught as, when it is time for speech, to set a watch upon
my lips; but I both shut and open my door for Mind, and Word, and Spirit, the
one kindred nature and Godhead.
B. I will speak then, since I am commanded this, and I will speak both to this
good shepherd and to you, the holy flock, what I think it better for me to say
and for you to hear today. Why have you asked for one to shepherd with you?
For the speech will begin from you, O dear and honored head of mine, worthy of
that of Aaron, down which that spiritual and priestly ointment drips as far as
beard and clothing. Why is it that, still able to strengthen and guide many,
and indeed guiding them in the power of the Spirit, you set a staff and a prop
under your spiritual works? Is it because you know and have heard that even
with that great Aaron Eleazar and Ithamar, the sons of Aaron =35.845=, were
anointed? For Nadab and Abihu I will willingly pass over, for fear of speaking
ill; and Moses, still living, appoints Joshua in his own place as lawgiver and
general for those pressing on to the land of promise? And as for Aaron and Hur
supporting the hands of Moses on the mountain, that Amalek might be warred
down by the cross, sketched and figured from afar, I think I will willingly
pass it by, as not very proper or fitting for us; for Moses did not choose
these as fellow lawgivers, but as helpers of prayer, and supports for the toil
of his hands.
C. But what ails you? What is worn? The body? I am ready to support it; but I
have both supported and been supported, like that Jacob, by fatherly
blessings. But the spirit? Who is stronger, and more fervent, and now most of
all, as the things of the flesh recede and give way, as what blocks and
opposes a light, and hinders the radiance? For these for the most part love to
war against one another and sit opposed; and the body is in health when the
soul is weary, while the soul thrives and looks upward when pleasures recede
and wither away together with the body. But I have also otherwise admired your
old-fashioned and noble spirit, how you did not even fear this, which belongs
so much to the present times, that your spirit should be thought a pretext,
and that we should seem to the many to take these things carnally, while
pretending spiritually. For the many have made the office be thought a great
and lordly thing, with a wonderful kind of enjoyment, even if someone presides
over and leads a flock still narrower than this, and one that brings more
hardships than pleasures. This then belongs to your simplicity or your love of
children, by which you neither admit anything wicked yourself, nor readily
suppose it of others; (for what is slow to move toward vice is slow to suspect
evil;) but it remains for me, as a second duty, to speak briefly to this
people, whether yours or now also mine.
D. We have been tyrannized, O friends and brothers; for we will call you to
our aid now, even if not then; we have been =35.848= tyrannized by a father's
old age and, to speak moderately, by a friend's kindness. So help me, whoever
is able, and give a hand to one pressed and torn between longing and Spirit;
the one proposes flights, and mountains, and deserts, and quiet of soul and
body, and that the mind withdraw into itself, and be gathered from the senses,
so as to converse with God without stain, and to be lit purely by the rays of
the Spirit, with none of the things below and turbid mixed in, nor falling
across the divine light, until we come to the source of the radiances here,
and come to rest from longing and desire, when the mirrors are dissolved by
the truth. But the other wills to bring me into the midst, and to bear fruit
for the common good, and that this too is to be helped, to help one another,
and to publish the illumination, and to bring to God a people for his own
possession, a holy nation, a royal priesthood, an image cleansed in many. For
it is better and more, as a garden is better than a plant, and a whole heaven
with its beauties than a single star, and a body than a member, so also, for
God, a whole Church brought to completeness is better than one man succeeding;
and one must look not only to one's own, but also to that of others. Since
Christ also thus, though he could remain in his own honor and Godhead, not
only emptied himself as far as the form of a slave, but also endured a cross,
despising shame, that in his own sufferings he might consume sin, and kill
death by death. Those then are the fabrications of desire, these the teachings
of the Spirit. And standing in the middle of longing and Spirit, and not
knowing to which I shall grant the more, I will share with you also what I
think I have found most fair and most safe, that you may test it with me and
take part in the counsel.
E. It seemed to me strongest and least dangerous to turn to some middle way of
longing and of fear, and to give one part to desire, the other to the Spirit;
and this would come about if I neither fled the ministry altogether, so as to
set aside the grace, for that is unsafe, nor took up a burden greater than
myself, for it is heavy; and the one belongs to another's person, the other to
another's power; or rather both belong to madness. But it belongs to piety and
to safety at once to measure the ministry by the power; and as with food, to
accept what is according to power, and to send away what is beyond power; for
thus health comes to the body =35.849=, and safety to the soul, by moderating
in both. For this reason I now accept to share the care with the good father,
like a nestling not useless, flying near beside a great and high-soaring
eagle; and after this I will give my wing to the Spirit to carry where he
wills, and as he wills; and no one shall force me, nor lead me away elsewhere,
when I am taking counsel with him. For a father's toils inherited are sweet,
and this flock is more familiar than a strange and foreign one; and I would
add that it is also more precious to God, unless affection deceives me, and
habit steals the sense; yet it is not more useful than that the willing should
rule the willing, nor safer; since it is not of our law to lead by force, nor
by compulsion, but willingly. For this would not even hold another government
together, since what is held by force loves to take its freedom when it seizes
a time; but what most of all preserves ours, which is not a rule but a
training, is the willing. For the mystery of piety belongs to those who will
it, not to those who are tyrannized.
F. This is the speech from me to you, O men, spoken simply and with all
goodwill, and this is the mystery of my mind. And may that win which is going
to benefit both you and us, as the Spirit leads our affairs (for the speech
comes back again to the same point), to whom we have given ourselves, and the
head anointed with the oil of perfection, in the Father almighty, and the
only-begotten Word, and the Holy Spirit who is also God. For how long shall we
cover the lamp with the bushel, and begrudge others the perfect Godhead, when
it ought already to be set upon the lampstand and shine to all Churches and
souls, and to the whole fullness of the inhabited world, no longer
conjectured, nor sketched by the mind, but also spoken out plainly? Which is
indeed the most perfect demonstration of theology to those deemed worthy of
this grace in Christ Jesus himself our Lord, to whom be the glory, honor,
power unto the ages. Amen.
