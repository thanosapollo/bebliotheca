OF GREGORY, BISHOP OF NYSSA
AN ENCOMIUM ON SAINT STEPHEN THE PROTOMARTYR

How fair is the succession of good things, how sweet the succession of
gladness. For behold, we receive a feast from a feast from a feast, and grace
in exchange for grace. Yesterday the Master of the universe feasted us, today
the imitator of the Master. How this one, or how that one? That one putting on
the man for our sake, this one putting off the man for his sake. That one
entering the cave of life for us, this one going out from the cave for him.
That one being swaddled for us, this one being stoned for him. That one
destroying death, this one treading upon death as it lies. But let us too,
brethren, run together with the word toward the spectacle, as the great
athlete contends against the evil adversary of human life, stripping in the
stadium of confession. For truly, according to Paul's voice, Stephen became a
spectacle to the world and to angels and to men, 6 first binding on the crown
of martyrdom, first making a way of entrance for the choir of martyrs, first
standing against sin unto blood. And it seems to me that the whole army of the
supercelestial ones, and all the myriads of angels, both of those who minister
and of those who stand by, and whatever of the honorable we hear of in the
upper allotment, in principalities and powers and thrones and authorities and
dominions, and the whole heavenly assembly, then made a spectacle of the
athlete locked in combat with the antagonist.

For the human life had been spread out as a kind of stadium for those who
contend, and both rushed against each other: both the evil adversary of human
life, trained from the fall of the first-formed until the times of Stephen in
victory against men, and the great contestant of the faith, judging the onset
of the adversary as nothing. And the weapons of both against each other were,
for the inventor of death, the threat toward death, for the disciple of life,
the confession of the faith. For who would not have marveled at this novel
kind of contest, when the truth was being decided by life and death, and death
became the proof of the truth? For the herald of the life that was still
hidden and unknown announced the preaching to men by deed: for the readiness 8
to leave this life was a proof to those who judge rightly that he had
exchanged the more honorable life for the one being left. But it would be good
to paint the contest accurately with the word as on a tablet, so that along
the way, through the sequence of the events, the order of the wonders might be
displayed to us. Just now the violent breath from above, having scattered all
the aerial and deceptive power of the demons, filled the house of the
apostles, and the Spirit, divided fire-like into tongues according to the
number of those who received the grace, came to be in each. And already, at
the paradox of the wonder concerning voice and tongue, astonishment and
confusion had seized those from every nation sojourning in Jerusalem, when all
who had been split into varied and different kinds of tongues had suddenly
become of one tongue with the disciples, when the power of speaking was added
to the apostles not from previous instruction and some practice, but from the
inspiration of the Spirit, this grace having been suddenly improvised for
them. For it was necessary that those who dissolved the common speech at the
earthly tower-building should come again into one speech at the spiritual
building of the Church. And for this reason well 10 from here the economy of
the Holy


Spirit of grace begins, so as to divide the common benefaction of men into
every difference of human speech, lest the preaching of piety, shut up in one
tongue only, remain idle and inoperative for those of other tongues.

Already then, when the Pharisees both disbelieved even their own ears and were
constructing that those struck by the wonderworking had gone astray, as if new
wine had produced such a derangement in them, and when Peter with one cast of
the word had netted three thousand souls for Christ, and when the Church,
always thereafter by the addition of those being saved, was increasing into a
multitude, and when the one lame from birth had again opened the Beautiful
Gate of the temple for those being saved, sitting at which he, by the wonder
of his own healing, led those lame in soul toward the faith: when then many
were streaming together toward the preaching of the faith, and the need was
seeking a greater many-handedness of those ministering to the grace, then
Stephen, great in wisdom and in grace, is called by the Spirit to the aid of
the apostles. And let no one suppose that by the name of ministry he is second
to the apostolic dignity; since Paul too knows himself as a minister of the
mysteries of Christ, and the Lord of the universe, who was administering human
salvation through flesh, was not ashamed of the name of ministry, saying that
he was in 12 the midst as one who serves, he who works the divisions of
ministries, as the apostle says. And just as fire, laying hold of suitable
matter, raises the flame on high and makes the brightness more conspicuous, so
the Holy Spirit, having come to be in the greatness of nature of Stephen, made
the rays of grace more manifest. Therefore all were looking toward him, as
many as had some share of knowledge and education. And those who seemed to
have more than the rest, gathering in groups, packed together through one
another as into certain phalanxes, thus tried to withstand Stephen's assault.
But he was equally invincible to all, mixing both with many of his own rank
and with lesser ones. In this, then, what appeared were Alexandrians and
Libertines and Cyrenians and men from everywhere grappling with the contestant
of the truth; but what was understood through what appeared was the father of
the lie, raising himself through human faces against the truth spoken in
Stephen. But the truth was trophy-bearing against the lie, the champion having
well put to flight all the forerunners of the deceit. But the minister of the
truth was being plotted against by the enemy of the truth, as not revealing
the truth that is, but making a truth that is not. Why do you contrive these
things against the 14 herald? I would say to the devil: if you have any power,
harm the truth in Stephen; but if that is higher than your devices, why do you
occupy your malice about the vessel of the truth and destroy the container,
leaving what is contained, as dogs do, gaping around the stones with which
they are struck, not touching the one who throws? Since then the falsehood was
convicted through the most true things, and [the evil one] was no longer able
to find any other forerunner of the deceit, all looking toward the
manifestation of the truth, he recalls his own wrestling-hold and, dividing
his operation into both the accusers and the judges and being in both, he led
the ones to speak falsehoods against Stephen, and the others to receive the
slander with anger. And having variously sown himself among the Jews, he
becomes all things to Stephen: accuser, judge, executioner, and the rest of
death's service, not knowing with what a fall he would be brought down after
such a vote against Stephen. For just as those experienced among athletes,
getting under the body of those wrestling against them, prepare a more
grievous


fall for them by a certain technical twist 16, so also the great Stephen,
having been laid to the earth, works the grievous crushing of the adversary.

For from this comes the apostles' course upon the inhabited world; this is the
beginning of the word's going about everywhere. For if the people of the Jews
had not gone mad against the apostles by the bloodshed against that man,
perhaps the grace of the gospel would have been shut up in Jerusalem alone.
But as it is, having been persecuted by the Jews, they were scattered, one
here and another there, among the nations throughout the inhabited world,
driving the devil out from everywhere through the teaching of the mysteries.
Thus Samaria receives the word; thus as a by-work of the road the eunuch's
salvation comes about through Philip; thus the great vessel of election, Paul,
armed by the devil with rage and threat, turns the darts upon the armorer
himself, driving him beyond all the inhabited world, so as to leave no place
unentered by the faith of Christ. From this, Egyptians, Syrians, Parthians and
Mesopotamians, [Italians and] Galatians, Illyrians, Macedonians, and the
nations from everywhere, the word running through, brings to the faith. Do you
see Stephen's athletic science? With how many falls was the adversary crushed,
who thought through the slander to become superior to the one wrestling
against him?

18 But let us return again to the stadium. With what words do the slanderers
stir up the people? He does not cease, he says, speaking words against the
holy place [this] and the law; for we have heard him saying that this Jesus
the Nazarene will destroy this place and change the customs which Moses
delivered to us. Such then is the accusation of the devil's orators. But what
is the folly of those who hear? At what do they grow harsh against the one
accused? What wickedness did they detect in what was said? For the things
brought forward against him were of the things that would come to pass later.
For they said he had said that the place would be destroyed and the ordinances
of Moses would be transferred. What then in these things is the wrong, whether
he speaks truly or falsely? For if he speaks falsely, the distressing thing
will not come to pass; but if he speaks truly, how does the word do wrong in
announcing the outcome beforehand? For the things that happen will altogether
come to pass both if men are silent and if they are not. And the murder of the
one who announced it beforehand: what correction of the distressing things
does it bring? Then: Jesus the Nazarene is accused, but the vote of punishment
is carried against Stephen. And yet if the one who does wrong moves the anger,
and the wrong is the alteration of the place and of the customs, and the
accuser says these things come about not from Stephen but from Jesus, the
court ought altogether to have been moved against the 20 one accused. O the
unjust vote of those who hear. Since Jesus, he says, will alter the laws, let
Stephen be stoned. But how will Jesus destroy the law, he who is so disposed
toward the law, who legislated so many things toward the confirmation of the
ancient things, who said: I came not to destroy the law, but to fulfill, who
confirmed the law about not murdering through not even being angry at the
start for the disciples, who cast out adultery together with desire, who
through commanding not to avenge the one who previously harmed constructed
even not beginning unjust hands, who through the sharing of what one has
banished the passion according to greed? How were these things neither
remembered nor examined together with the judgment? I would wish now that the
council of those murderous judges were present to me, and to learn concerning
the places over which they grow harsh: where is that two-named temple, where
the unbelievable magnitudes of the stones, where that gold almost equal in
weight to the rest of the material about the temple, where the lawful
sacrifices, the ram, the calf, the lamb, the heifer, the dove, the turtledove,
the scapegoat? If they condemn Stephen to death for this, that none of those
gloomy 22 things may come to fulfillment, let them show how much they saved
for themselves through the bloodshed. If


none of these things is, for which they brought that vote, for the sake of
what was the murder? Let them say.

But let us see in the contests that follow how he who is being buried by the
snowstorms of stones defends himself against the murderers, what kind of shots
he sends back against those who send the shots of the stones. Let the children
of the Jews learn the weapons of the Christians, which the great Stephen used
for defense against those who harm, and has made the deed a law for life. For
they, with a certain beastlike and savage rage, standing in a circle around
the holy one, all threw toward one mark, making every weapon that came to hand
against Stephen. But he, like some priest according to the spiritual law,
performing a holy sacrifice, offering not another's body but his own, and
sprinkling the blood instead of a libation, through himself propitiated God,
whom he saw in the heavenly sanctuaries, on behalf of those who were sinning,
repaying their bloodshed with benefaction, crying in the ears of the murderers
[and saying]: Lord, do not set this sin against them. And he was blotting out
their sin through prayer, which the murderers were writing with their own
lawless hands; but they were even provoked toward the prayer and did not
earlier cease throwing, until the great 24 Stephen, as if being poured about
with tender flowers or some light dew, was laid down into the sweet and
blessed sleep. But the victory outran the contests, and before the struggles
we see the crowned one. For before beholding the contest, we were led by the
word to the end of the contests. For I think it was necessary not to run past
those things in the word through which especially the martyr's virtue was
displayed. What the council of the murderers was like, and how equally in all
the rage had been raised toward the murder; how great was the unison of those
gathered toward the evil; what each one's look was like, what the form, what
the passion about the teeth, as the divine Scripture notes, that they were
sawn through in their hearts and gnashed their teeth at him. And he, standing
in the midst of such and so many, and raising himself against all the opposing
power that was being worked in the murderers, surpassed them all by the
greatness of his mind, setting against rage long-suffering, against threats
disdain, against the fear of death contempt of life, against hatred love,
against hostility well-doing, against slander the manifestation of the truth.
For the contestant of the truth was not shown a victor through one manner, but
having divided himself through the 26 varied virtue toward every kind of
wickedness then being worked in the Jews, he both grappled with all and
surpassed them all. Thus I hear also in the gymnastic contests that those who
have the advantage in strength, often stripping for the whole gymnastic
stadium, carry the prizes against all the adversaries. Such was he who led the
way in the stadium of the martyrs, having set himself against all the power of
the adversary, and he is shown brilliant in the victory against all: the
falsely named wisdom that wrestled against him through Libertines and
Cyrenians and the wise from Alexander's city he contended down through the
true wisdom, fear through boldness of speech, threat through disdain,
bitterness through well-doing, falsehood through the truth. They looked toward
the murder and already armed their hands with the stones, and by the look and
the panting and the clenching of the teeth they marked the bitterness; but he
saw them as brothers and greeted them as fathers. For men, he says, brothers
and fathers, hear. They were plausibly fabricating the slander; but for him
the council of the murderers was a school of the truth. He did not cut short
the word by fear, nor did he become palsied toward the hope of the dangers,
nor did he look toward death, but having his soul on the height and
overlooking all things at his feet as children raving in vain


he trained them with the word, using the things believed 28 by those very men
as a proof that they had gone astray concerning the doctrines.

Abraham is brought forward by the word, and all the history concerning him is
brought before the eyes through a few things, then the succession of the
saints thereafter. Moses after these being born, reared, educated, initiated
on the mountain, scourging Egypt, saving the Israelite people, announcing
beforehand the mystery concerning the Lord. Which especially moves the council
and reheats the disease, that even Moses, about whom they pretended to be
especially zealous, was shown an advocate of the doctrine. Upon which,
standing up, they bring the end worthy both of their own bitterness and of
Stephen's desire. For he, going out from nature and before going out from the
body, sees with pure eyes heavenly gates parting for him and what is within
the sanctuaries appearing through, both the divine glory itself and the
radiance of the glory; and of the paternal glory no character is sketched
through the word, but the radiance is beheld by the athlete in the form that
was seen by men, appearing as it was containable for human nature. He then,
having come outside human nature and having been transformed toward the
angelic grace, so as to be even a wonder to the murderers themselves, the form
about the face having been changed 30 toward the dignity of angels, both saw
the unseen things and cried out the grace that had been seen by him. But they
held their ears and did not receive the narrative of the vision, having done
this at least well in all things; for neither was a profane hearing worthy to
receive narratives of a divine manifestation. He then shared the grace with
those present, putting forward in common by the narrative those things of
which he had been counted worthy in private, saying, I behold the heavens
opened and the Son of man standing at the right hand of God. But they, crying
with a great voice and holding their ears, rushed upon him with one mind. Well
did the history concerning him add the cry to the deeds, that it might show
the kinship of their purpose with the Sodomites. For their lawlessness too was
named a cry by the one who judges. For a cry, he says, of Sodom and Gomorrah
has gone up to me. These then also cried out, that their cry against Stephen
might be heard. Yet the athlete was not ignorant that he was being benefited
through the bitterness of those murdering. For, crowned by the circle of those
stoning around him, he thus received what was happening as a victory-crown
being plaited 32 in the hands of the opponents. Therefore he also defends
himself against the murderers with blessing, not thinking it right that the
deed upon him be divided toward opposite outcomes, so as to bring life to him,
but destruction to the opponents, but that the enemies too, as joint-causes of
good things for him, might not fall away from those things of which he himself
had been counted worthy. Thus he who sees Christ knows how to be disposed
toward those who make war. For when he saw the lawgiver of long-suffering, he
was reminded of the laws that command to love the enemies and to do good to
those who hate and to pray for those who make war. But the athlete is not
glorified from human praises. For neither did the aim of his contest look
toward glory from men, but having surpassed the whole world by the greatness
of the achievement and having run alongside the measures of human power, he
cast every encomiastic power behind himself.

Therefore let him have the prizes against every human word, and let him
contend together with us by the history concerning himself toward the
salvation of souls. For just as among bodily athletes those who have ceased
from the contests train the young in the athletic exercises, teaching them by
a certain technical twist to slip out of the holds of those wrestling against
them, so I think we too must be trained through the great Stephen toward
piety, that through him we may flee the holds of those who fight against the
Spirit. For those who rage against the glory of the Spirit say that Stephen is
an advocate of their 34 absurdity, who, gazing intently into heaven


saw the glory of God and Jesus standing at the right hand of God. They say
then, for the overturning of the doctrines of piety, that if the Spirit must
altogether be numbered with Father and Son, how did Stephen not see in the
vision the Spirit also with the Son? How then will Stephen stretch a hand to
those being tripped by such words? How will he set upright by the athletic art
those who are buckling? Or is the help from this very place, which overturns
the unbelief of the one wrestling against us.

Do you seek, O fighter against the Spirit, when the glory of the Father was
seen and the Son having the standing at the right hand, where is the Spirit?
If the Spirit were in you, you would not have run past the word concerning him
that was set before you, just as those blind in their sight pass by, not
knowing the gold lying before their feet. But now at least hear, if indeed you
do not hold your hearing as the Jews do. How did Stephen see the superheavenly
glory? Who spread open for him the gates of the heavens? Was the achievement
of human power? Did some one of the angels bring up to that height the nature
that lies below? These things 36 are not so; for the history concerning him
does not say thus, that Stephen, being great in power or having become full of
angelic help, saw what he saw. But what does it say? But Stephen, being full
of the Holy Spirit, saw the glory of God and the only-begotten [Son] of God.
For it is not possible, as the prophet says, for the light to be seen unless
it is beheld in light. For in your light, he says, we shall see light. (It is
not possible that the vision of the light come about except in light; for how
is it possible to look at the sun having come to be outside the rays?) Since
then in the light of the Father, that is, in the Holy Spirit who proceeds from
there, the only-begotten light is beheld, therefore, having been previously
shone upon by the glory of the Spirit, he came into comprehension of the glory
of the Father and the Son. Since, how shall we say that the evangelical
declaration is true, that no one has ever seen God? And how shall we say that
the apostle does not cry things contrary to what has been historically told,
when he says, neither has any of men seen, nor is he able to see? For if by
human nature and power the glory of the Father and of the Son became
containable, then altogether false is he who declared that the [divine]
spectacle is uncontainable for men. But indeed it is necessary both that that
one not speak falsely and that the history speak truly. Therefore the malice
of those who fight against the Spirit has been clearly detected, that like
things are beheld 38 by the like, as Scripture has testified. For Stephen does
not see the divine remaining in human nature and power, but having been mixed
toward the grace of the Holy Spirit he was raised through that to the
comprehension of God.

Therefore if without the Spirit it is not possible either to see the Lord
Jesus, as the apostle says, or to comprehend the paternal glory, it has been
clearly shown that where the Spirit is, there also the Son is beheld and the
paternal glory is grasped. But another weapon of impiety is put forward to us
from the things historically told by those who fight against Christ. For they
say that the inferiority of the Only-begotten is signified through these
things; for they make his standing beside the glory of the Father a proof that
he is subject to the authority of the Father. What then of Paul, I would say
to them? And what before him of the prophet David, both setting forth the
glory of the Only-begotten by the teaching of the Spirit? For David says: The
Lord said to my Lord, Sit at my right hand; and the apostle says that the Lord
has sat at the right hand of the throne of God. If then standing is of
diminution, sitting is altogether a sign of equal honor; either then let them
set aside the magnificent testimonies through which the height of the dignity
is marked 40, or let them also receive this one piously. For equal upon each
of the things said is the grace of the Spirit as teacher. For Stephen, being
full of the Holy Spirit, both saw what he saw and spoke what he saw. And David
in the Spirit calls him Lord, as the gospel says; and Paul, as he himself
says, speaks the mysteries in the Spirit. If then the teacher is one, having
no


disagreement toward himself, and the teacher is the Spirit of truth, who has
come to be in those borne by God, how would anyone suspect some disagreement
of the doctrines? But sitting, he says, indicates one meaning, and standing
another, according to what is understood at first hand. I say so too. But it
is not pious to reckon upon the bodiless nature also what the emphasis of the
words shows upon bodies. For in a man sitting sketches the position of the
body upon the haunch, so that the tension of the joint may not always grow
weary, holding up the weight of the body upon itself. And conversely standing
interprets the man as upright upon the knees, not resting upon the haunches
through sitting. But upon the surpassing nature both sitting and standing are
pure of such thoughts, each equally separated from the emphasis understood at
first hand 42. For we shall take neither the standing of the bodiless upon a
joint nor the seating of the unshaped upon haunches, but through either voice
we have piously understood the steadfastness in every good and the
unchangeableness in every. For he who says that the divine stands and he who
says that it sits do not differ at all from one another in the otherness of
the words concerning the meaning, the one dogmatizing that the divine has
planted itself firmly, the other that it has been seated unchangeably in the
good. And just as the prophet David and the apostle Paul, each setting forth
in the words according to himself the sitting of the Only-begotten, did not
grant one to understand that while the Father stands the Son is in a seat,
although they mentioned only the seating of the Son, in the same manner also
in Stephen's case, having heard the standing of the Son, you would no longer
reasonably suspect concerning the sitting of the Father. For as in Paul and
David it has been agreed that the Father sits through the Son's sitting at the
right hand, although the word taught nothing beforehand concerning the Father,
in the same manner also in Stephen's case the standing of the Son jointly
indicates the equal also upon the glory of the Father.

For thus 44 the word of the image would be preserved, if everything whatsoever
that is understood and beheld in this were believed the same also in the
archetype. And as in the good the good, and in the light the light, and in all
things the original beauty is characterized through its own in the image, so
also in the sitting of the Son, whatever this name grants one to understand,
the sitting of the Father also is grasped, and in the standing the standing,
so that the word of the image may not fall, being alienated in the exchange of
the properties of the archetype.

Let these things, brethren, have been contemplated by us in passing toward the
question at hand, the vision of Stephen having brought such a contemplation
into the word. And may it come about that we become not spectators only of
Stephen's contest, but also partakers of the grace, filled with the Holy
Spirit, for the overthrow of the adversaries, for the glory of our Lord Jesus
Christ, to whom be the glory and the might unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
