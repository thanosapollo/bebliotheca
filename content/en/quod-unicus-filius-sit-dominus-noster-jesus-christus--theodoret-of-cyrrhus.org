* That our Lord Jesus Christ is one Son
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

That even after the incarnation our Lord Jesus Christ is one Son. Those who
have composed slanders against us say that we divide the one Lord Jesus Christ
into two sons. But we are so far from thinking these things that we even
indict for impiety those who dare to say them. For we have been taught by the
divine Scripture to worship one Son, our Lord Jesus Christ, the only-begotten
Son of God, God the Word who became man; for we confess the same one both as
God before the ages and as man who in the last days became man for the
salvation of men; and he became man not by a change of the Godhead, but by the
assumption of the humanity. For he has the nature of the Godhead unchangeable
and unalterable, just as the Father who begot him before the ages, and
whatever one may think concerning the essence of the Father, this he will
certainly find also concerning that of the Only-begotten. For he has been
begotten from that essence. And the Lord, teaching this, said to Philip: "He
who has seen me has seen the Father." And again elsewhere: "All things that
the Father has are mine." And in another place: "I and the Father are one."
And one may find countless other things signifying the identity of essence.
Therefore he did not become God, but he was; "for in the beginning was the
Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God." But he was not man; he
became man, and he became so by assuming what is ours; for this the blessed
Paul said: "who, being in the form of God, did not count equality with God a
thing to be grasped, but emptied himself, taking the form of a servant." And
again: "For surely he does not take hold of angels, but he takes hold of the
seed of Abraham." And again: "Since then the children have shared in flesh and
blood, he himself likewise partook of the same." Thus he was both passible and
impassible, both mortal and immortal: passible and mortal as man, impassible
and immortal as God. For as God he raised his own flesh that had died; for the
saying is his: "Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up";
and as man he was passible and mortal until the passion; for after the
resurrection, even as man he has impassibility and immortality and
incorruption; and he sends forth God-befitting flashes; not having been
changed according to the flesh into the nature of Godhead, but preserving the
characters of the humanity, =83.1436= nor having a body that is
uncircumscribed (for this is proper to the divine nature alone), but remaining
in the former circumscription. For even after the resurrection he taught this
and said to the disciples: "See my hands and my feet, that it is I myself;
handle me and see, that a spirit does not have flesh and bones as you see me
having." Seen thus, he was taken up into heaven; thus he promised to come
again; thus both those who have believed and those who crucified him will see
him; "for they shall look," it says, "on him whom they pierced." We therefore
worship one Son, but we contemplate in him each nature as perfect, both the
one that assumed and the one that was assumed; both that from God and that
from David. For this reason he is also named Son of the living God and son of
David, each nature drawing the fitting appellation; and for this reason the
divine Scripture names him both God and man, and the blessed Paul cries: "One
God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus, who gave
himself a ransom for all."

But having named him man here, elsewhere he addresses him as God. "Looking
for," he says, "the blessed hope and appearing of the glory of our great God
and Savior Jesus Christ." And in another place he sets both together: "from
whom," he says, "is Christ according to the flesh, who is God over all, unto
the ages. Amen." And he has called the same one both from the Jews, according
to the flesh, and God over all, as God. Thus the prophet Isaiah, having said:
"A man in affliction, and knowing how to bear sickness; this one bears our
iniquities and is in pain for us"; added a little later: "Who shall declare
his generation?" And this is not human but divine. Thus through Micah God
says: "And you, Bethlehem, land of Judah, are by no means least among the
rulers of Judah; for from you shall come forth a ruler who shall shepherd my
people Israel. And his goings forth are from the beginning, from the days of
eternity." And by saying, "For from you shall come forth for me a ruler," he
indicated the economy of the incarnation; and by adding, "His goings forth are
from the beginning, from the days of eternity," he hinted at the Godhead
begotten from the Father before the ages. Having been taught these things by
the divine Scripture, and having found the teachers of the Church who shone in
various times thinking these things, we are eager to keep inviolate the
inheritance which we received; and we worship one Son of God, as one God the
Father, and one Holy Spirit; yet we know the difference of flesh and Godhead.
And just as we say that those who divide the one Lord Jesus Christ into two
sons step outside =83.1437= the road worn by the holy apostles, so also we say
that those who say that the Godhead of the Only-begotten and the humanity have
become one nature incline toward the other precipice.

We think these things, we proclaim these things, we contend for these dogmas.
And that those who say we advocate two sons slander us, the testimony of the
facts cries out; for we teach those who approach the all-holy baptism the
faith set forth at Nicaea; and performing the mystery of regeneration, we
baptize the believers into the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the
Holy Spirit, offering each appellation in the singular, and moreover,
continually ministering in the churches, we are accustomed to glorify the
Father, and the Son, and the Holy Spirit; not sons, but Son. If then we say
two sons, who is the one glorified by us, and who is the one remaining without
honor? For we have not gone so far into madness as to say two sons, yet deem
the other worthy of no honor. From this too, then, the slander has become
clear; for we worship one only-begotten Son, God the Word who became man; and
we call the holy Virgin Theotokos, since she has borne Emmanuel, which is
interpreted, God with us. And the prophet who foretold concerning Emmanuel a
little later again wrote such things concerning him, that "A child has been
born to us, a son also has been given to us, whose government is upon his
shoulder, and his name is called, Angel of great counsel, Wonderful,
Counsellor, Mighty God, Potentate, Prince of peace, Father of the age to
come." And if the infant born of the Virgin has been named Mighty God, then
reasonably the one who bore him has been named Theotokos; for the one who bore
has shared in the honor of the one who was born, and the Virgin is both
mother, as of a man, of the Master Christ, and again a servant, as of Master
and Maker and God. Because of this difference of expressions he is also called
by the divine Paul both without father and without mother and without
genealogy, having neither beginning of days nor end of life. For he is without
father according to the humanity; for as man he was born of a mother alone;
but he is without mother as God; for from the Father alone he was begotten
from eternity; and again without genealogy as God, but he is also given a
genealogy as man. "The book," it says, "of the generation of Jesus Christ, son
of David, son of Abraham." And the divine Luke also gives him a genealogy.

Thus again, as God, he had no beginning of days, for he was begotten before
the ages; nor indeed does he have an end of life, for he has an immortal and
impassible nature. But as man he both had a beginning of days; for he was born
under Augustus Caesar; and he had an end of life; for he was crucified under
Tiberius Caesar. But now, as I said, he has the human nature also immortal,
and as he was taken up he will come again =83.1440= according to the voice of
the angels. "For this Jesus," they said, "who was taken up from you into
heaven, shall so come in like manner as you have seen him going into heaven."
The divine prophets brought this teaching to us, and so did the choir of the
holy apostles, and so did the saints who shone in the East and in the West:
that renowned Ignatius, who received the high priesthood through the right
hand of the great Peter, and for the confession of Christ became food for wild
beasts; the great Eustathius, who presided over the assembled synod and for
his fervent zeal for piety dwelt in exile. Meletius the excellent proclaimed
this with equal sufferings; for he too was thrice driven from the flock for
the apostolic dogmas; Flavian who adorned that throne proclaimed this; the
wonderful Ephrem, the instrument of divine grace, using the Syrian tongue,
left this benefit written down for us. The all-praised Cyprian, who governed
Carthage and all Libya and who accepted death by fire for Christ, and Damasus,
who steered great Rome, and Ambrose, who adorned the throne of Milan,
continued both preaching and writing these things in the Roman tongue. The
great luminaries of the Alexandrians, Alexander and Athanasius, endured the
dangers sung of by all.

The great teachers of the imperial city also brought this pasture to the
flocks, Gregory the brilliant advocate of piety, John the teacher of the
inhabited world, Atticus who received their presidency and teaching. With
these Basil the most radiant lamp of piety, and Gregory who boasted the same
parents as he, and Amphilochius who through him received the gift of the high
priesthood, brought the teaching not only to the men of their own time, but
also left the benefit to us through their writings. But the day will fail me
as I enumerate Polycarp and Irenaeus, and Methodius, and Hippolytus, and the
other teachers of the Church. We say then in short that we follow the divine
oracles and all these saints. For through the grace of the Spirit they plunged
into the depth of the God-inspired Scripture, and they themselves knew its
mind, and made it clear to those who wished to learn. For the difference of
tongues did not produce a difference of dogmas; for they were springs of the
grace of the divine Spirit, receiving the stream from one fountain.
