* Homily on the Ascension of Christ
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of our father among the saints Epiphanius, bishop of Cyprus, on the
ascension of our Lord Jesus Christ.
Blessed be God.
The ornament of the body is the head set upon the body; and the ornament of
the feasts is today's feast. For what is it? he says. We celebrate
Christ's ascension with the flesh, as shown to be the fulfillment of the
Lord's feasts. For just as a head of shining form, so also today's
feast beams forth the beauty of divine grace; but the many, not knowing
its greatness, think it the lesser. And if you wish to learn, stretch
your mind toward me with patience. Only cooperate with me through
prayers, I beg; and still the leaden breezes; and calm the waves of idle
talk; and grant me an ear at leisure. For according to the wise Solomon,
the words of the wise are heard in quiet.
First, then, =43.480= is the dread and wondrous feast of Christ's birth
according to the flesh. For how is it not wondrous, the descent of God
from the heavens toward us, or rather his condescension? because the
Master of all deigned to unite to himself the form of a servant, and he
who contains all things deigned to have a poor mother. The second feast
is that of the Theophany, having a far greater contemplation than the
first. For in the first feast a star pointed out the God Word who had
been born; but in the second feast John cried out, Behold the Lamb of God
who takes away the sin of the world. And the Father, crying from heaven,
confirmed the testimony concerning the one being baptized: This is my
beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased. But not even then was there the
complete enjoyment of soundness, since the mortal body had not yet
received immortality through the resurrection. The third feast, the
resurrection of the saving passion, gives life to those who through the
baptism of his blood flash forth with radiances, and are born again
through water and the Holy Spirit. Therefore he also raised up the fallen
world, and deemed Adam, who of old had fallen, worthy of eternal life.
Through a tree Adam harvested death; through the tree of the cross he
grants life to the world. And this feast was shown more glorious than
those that went before, since in it death, having put death to death
through death, also brought immortal life to those who had died. But even
this did not have the fullness of joy, holding the risen one upon the
earth. And the feast of Pentecost too had a great joy beyond speech, in
which the Holy Spirit was given to the apostles; but today, on the day of
the Ascension, all things through all things have been filled with
gladness. Having opened the light-bearing heavens, and having made the
most subtle air into a stair, and having whirled up from earth into the
heavenly vaults the one from earth, and having stirred all the natures of
the angels into unceasing joy, he showed us a strange sight: our flesh
raised upon a royal throne.
Where are those who busy themselves with the hippodromes, and love the
swiftness of charioteers? Come, see a paradoxical hippodrome: the Creator
of creation riding in a human chariot, not driving in disorder upon the
earth, but in a novel way drawing up aetherial courses, and hastening to
reach the heavenly turning-post, and mightily overthrowing Belial who
runs alongside. =43.481= But the children of the Jews, the envious of
the truth, having heard that Christ was taken up into the heavens,
wishing to dim the paradoxical wonder of the Sun of righteousness, bring
in Elijah, saying: Why do you boast greatly, Christians, that Christ was
taken up


as you say? For Elijah too, being a man, having been deemed worthy of
like glory, was taken up into the heavens.
To whom we shall speak with boldness: Why, O Jews, do you plunder the
books as the blind, and not see with accuracy the things written in them?
Why do you read, and not recognize? For what does the divine Scripture
say? And Elijah was taken up in a whirlwind as into heaven; but the "as
into heaven" has an ambiguous sense; whereas "into heaven" shows the
truth clearly. And that neither Elijah nor any other went up into heaven,
except only the only-begotten Son of God who came down from heaven, he
himself, fully assuring all concerning himself, says: No one has gone up
into heaven except he who came down from heaven, the Son of man, who is
in heaven; the good shepherd, who left the ninety-nine sheep of the
angels upon the heavenly mountains, and having found the wandering sheep,
and bearing it on the shoulders of loving-kindness, and having brought it
up to the heavenly harbor, offering a gift to his own Begetter, speaks: I
have found, Father, the wandering sheep, which the deceiving serpent
deceived with guileful devices, and showed it the ways of wickedness, and
with the mud of polytheism defiled the purity of the knowledge of God.
Seeing it therefore drowning in the mire of life, I swiftly snatched it
up with the right hand of my Godhead; and with the bowels of mercies I
washed it in the streams of the Jordan, and having anointed it with the
fragrance of my Holy Spirit, I have now come through the resurrection,
bringing you a gift worthy of your Godhead, the rational sheep.
Today the devil bewails his own defeat, seeing our body being led up into
the heavens; today sin, through the ascension of Christ Jesus, has been
dissolved as smoke; today the devil laments, saying, What shall I do,
wretched that I am? Having snatched from me all those from the ages as a
swift-flying hawk, he has left me desolate, and having clipped my wings
from every side he has dashed me down to the earth. The son of Mary has
deceived me. I did not know that God was hidden in a human body. I saw
him wearing a human body, and thinking him a mere man, I stirred the Jews
against him; and what I did not dare to accomplish through myself, these
things I worked through the Jews. I invented a myriad =43.484= of
slanders; I devised every kind of outrage against him; I gave him to
drink with a reed and a sponge; I crowned his head with thorns; I
dishonored him with blows, I nailed him to a cross with nails; having at
last subjected him to death, as if I had conquered, I celebrated with the
Jews.
But seeing that he was raised on the third day, as had been foretold,
receiving as it were a sword suddenly against the heart I fell; and
seeing that he eats and drinks, I was preparing yet another cross. But as
I was pondering these things with myself, I see him going up with glory
into heaven, from where I fell with shame. Therefore also the Psalmist
from many years cried out, saying: All you nations, clap your hands;
shout to God with a voice of exultation, for the Lord most high, a
fearful king, is great over all the earth; and again: God has gone up
with a shout, the Lord with the voice of a trumpet. I want to pursue him,
and I am carried down as lead; I wish to seize him, and I am invisibly
forced back by the elbows. What shall I do, miserable one? He has driven
me round from every side. From the heavens he slung me down to earth as a
very small stone. And having fallen on the earth, I wished to hide in the
waters; and finding me hidden in the streams of the Jordan, as a dragon,
he seized me and scourged me. I want at least to hold the earth, and all,
as keepers of the Master's pasture, cry out the voice against me: The
earth is the Lord's and the fullness thereof, the world and all who dwell
in it. The earthquake showed that the earth is the Lord's. For the earth,
not bearing the name of the Lord, was shaken with unbearable trembling,
because of the disorder of her


children, being scourged, opening rifts like a mouth against the
elements, she cried out
the magnitude of the hidden pain; and she begged that the sinners be
spared, as a mother for her children.
Who shall speak the mighty acts of the Lord? who shall make all his
praises heard? The earth is the Lord's and the fullness thereof. For
Christ united in himself the flesh that was formed from earth, and sat in
the heavens. The devil comes, wishing to defile through fornication. But
as a watchman having the eye of the soul unsleeping, do not allow the
robber to tear the eye of the mind, that you may say to him with
confidence: I do not become a betrayer of the Master's property, devil.
For the earth is the Lord's, and the Church of Christ, which is always
farmed by the heavenly rain, and bearing fruit ever-green to Christ
thirty [and sixty] and a hundred. The sower went out, it says, to sow;
and some fell upon the path, the Greek deceit. And the birds came, the
sharp-hungering plunderers of the demons, and ate it up. Another fell
upon the rocky places, I mean the hard-natured choice of the Jews, and
immediately there sprang up =43.485= a plant of rashness [and] of
lawlessness. For such is the manner of the Jews. For being given the law
in the wilderness they said to Moses: All that God has said, we will do
and we will hear. And those who promised these things worshiped a
lifeless calf, and preferred the dead thing to the living God. Another
fell upon the thorns, the rough-spiked and blasphemous-tongued heresies,
which daily choke the seed of piety. Another fell upon the good earth,
the Church of Christ, the fertile, the one bearing fruit thirty and sixty
and a hundred. Thirty the yoke of temperance; sixty of angelic-formed
virginity; a hundred of divine righteousness, and purity, and of the
valor of the martyrs. For the heralds of piety, announcing to all the
coming of the Lord at the last, said:
Thus he will come in the manner you saw him being taken up. For two men
appeared in white clothing. Two, that the truth might be believed; men,
that they might establish the disciples in manliness; clothed in white,
that they might make bright the minds of those who hear. What then did
those who appeared proclaim? Men of Israel, why do you stand looking into
heaven? why do you gaze into heaven? Rather, beholding the maker of
heaven, glorify the greatness of the one being taken up, and marvel at
the condescension of his loving-kindness, because having united the
earthly man to himself, he showed him a citizen of heaven, and
established him as a dweller of heaven; oh! where has he led up the one
lying below.
Why do you stand looking into heaven? Thus he will come. Thus. How?
Wearing our body, putting forward the side wounded by the spear; that the
Jews may look on him whom they pierced; and that we may be deemed worthy
to see his face with boldness, and obtain the promised goods; by the
grace and loving-kindness of our Lord Jesus Christ; with whom to the
Father and the Holy Spirit be glory, power, honor, now and always, and
unto the ages of ages. Amen.
