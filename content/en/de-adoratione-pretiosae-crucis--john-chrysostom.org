* On the Veneration of the Precious Cross
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

On the veneration of the precious and life-giving cross, in the middle week
of the fasts. =52.835= αʹ. The yearly day has come to us, the all-venerable
and light-bearing middle week of the holy fasts, bringing the thrice-blessed
and life-giving cross of our Savior Jesus Christ, and setting it forth for
veneration, and sanctifying those who venerate it with a sincere heart and
holy lips, and showing them more eager and more vigorous for the further
course of the stadium of the holy fasts. Today therefore the venerable day
of the precious cross has been established, and come, friends, let us
embrace it with fear and longing. For shedding forth the rays of the
resurrection of Christ it illumines all, and sanctifies with its gifts;
wherefore let us greet it, exulting in soul. Today there is joy in heaven,
and on earth, because the light-bearing and life-giving cross of Christ is
made manifest to the world, through which demons are put to flight, and
diseases flee away, and gloomy darkness is driven off, and all the ends of
the earth are illumined. Today the Church of Christ is shown to be another
paradise, having set forth in the midst the all-holy wood of the precious
cross, and making the cross the procession of the passion of Christ, and the
hastening-on of his resurrection. Today the prophetic oracle has been
fulfilled, which says, We worship in the place where stood the feet of our
God. Rejoice therefore and exult, O Church of Christ, and bring forward your
children, purified by the temperance of the passions, by the fast, =52.836=
and flashing with the God-luminous virtues, and dance the unutterable dance.
For as long ago in the desert those who had been bitten, looking upon the
bronze serpent, were rescued from death; so now those who have run through
the middle course of the stadium of the fast, touching this, show the
intelligible serpent dead, and are themselves made deathless, and being
shown partakers of the sufferings of Christ through temperance, are shown to
be made partakers also of his resurrection. And not only this, but running
on further in youthful fashion, they offer victory-offerings to God. For the
cross of the Lord has been established a trophy-bearing and victorious
weapon; the unconquerable weapon of kings, the horn of the Church, the
feller of enemies, and the salvation of the faithful. And truly blessed and
thrice-blessed are those thought worthy to embrace it with holy lips and a
pure mouth. For truly in deed they fulfill the saying of the Lord: If anyone
wills to come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and
follow me. And see how he makes the word without compulsion. For he did not
say, Whether you will, or not, this you must suffer, but how? If anyone
wills to come after me; I do not compel, I do not force, but I make each one
master of his own choice; wherefore I also say: If anyone wills to come
after me. For I call to a good thing, not to an evil and burdensome thing,
not to punishment and retribution, but to a heavenly kingdom and a heavenly
life. For the very nature of the things is sufficient to attract. =52.837=
If anyone wills to come after me, whether man, or woman, or ruler, or ruled,
and wills to be saved, let him come by this way. And three things are said,
to deny oneself, to take up the cross, and to follow. But let us first see
what it is to deny oneself. Let us learn what it is to deny another, and
then we shall know what it is to deny oneself. What then is it to deny
another? He who denies another, for instance a brother, or a friend, or


a neighbor, or anyone at all, even if he should see this one scourged, or
bound, or suffering anything whatever, does not stand forth, does not help,
does not bend toward him, does not suffer anything toward him; for he has
become alien to him. Thus then God wills us to be unsparing of our body,
that even if some scourge us for his sake, or punish, or drive, or do
anything else, we spare not ourselves. For this is to deny; that is, let him
have nothing toward himself, but let him give himself over to the dangers,
to the contests, and let him be disposed as if another were suffering. And
he did not say, let him deny himself, but, Let him deny, by this small
addition showing the great hyperbole. And take up his cross. Do you see how
the King of the heavens armed the soldier following him? He gave no shield,
no helmet, no bow, no breastplate, no greave, none of such things at all,
but what is stronger than all of these, the safety from the cross, the
symbol of the victory against the demons. This is a sword, this a shield,
this a breastplate, this a helmet, this a greave, this a sure fortress, this
a harbor, this a refuge, this a crown, this a prize, this a treasure of all
the good things, both of those now and of those to be hereafter. For as one
taking a strong weapon gives it also to his own soldiers, so also Christ.
Behold, he says, my cross, how many things it accomplished; do you also such
things, and accomplish such things as you will. And yet elsewhere he also
promised greater than these, saying: He who believes in me, the works that I
do, he also shall do, and greater than these shall he do. What then is the
saying itself, Take up his cross, and follow me? Is it that each should
carry the wood? By no means; what virtue is this? But that we be arrayed
against the dangers, carrying about our blood in our souls, ready for
slaughter and death daily, doing all things so as not yet to expect our life
to last until evening, as surely about to die. Which also the apostle Paul
said: I die daily. Has not one death been given to you by nature? It is
permitted, if you will, to die ten thousand times for your Master. βʹ. For
such is grace; it leads the poverty among friends out into great wealth,
because of the munificence of the Master, marking the crowns not only by the
outcome of the things, but also by the purpose of those contending. What is,
Take up? Be so eager, he says, to be slaughtered and crucified as that one
carrying it upon the shoulders; so let him reckon himself near to death. All
are struck with amazement at such a man; for we do not so fear those fenced
with myriad human weapons, and fortified with so much courage, as this man
for freedom. For nothing so makes one escape death as to despise death. And
lest anyone should think this alone to be enough, to be ready for death (for
there are also robbers, and such enchanters, and all are man-killers), for
this reason he added: And follow me. I seek, he says, not the brave only,
nor the one undismayed at the end of life, but the gentle and temperate and
modest, and full of all virtue. Do you see the ministration of the cross
prevailing so much, not upon the Master alone, but also upon the servants?
This made Peter chief; this made Paul so great. Wherefore also he said: Who
shall =52.838= separate us from the love of Christ? tribulation, or
distress, or danger, or sword? This also made the fasting ones brave against
not meats only, but passions. Let us wrap ourselves around, beloved, with
the power of the cross, and armed for the further course, let us go on more
eagerly to the fast, and let us despise all the pleasant things of life. For
wealth, and glory, and power, and love, and such things, are pleasant for
this reason, that we are soul-cherishing, and are nailed to the present
life. But when this is despised, no account of those things holds for us.
For fair is the present life,


fair and sweet; for it is a gift of God; but when the life to come appears,
then it is rightly despised. Let us not therefore be ashamed of the
venerable symbols of our salvation, nor let us thrust away the great chief
of goods, through which we live and are, giving ourselves over to luxury and
revelry and gluttony, but let us carry about the cross of Christ as a crown,
that is, the mortification of the passions. For all things concerning us are
accomplished through it; if one must be born again, the cross comes in; if
one must be fed with that mystical food, if ordained, or do anything else
whatever, everywhere the symbol of the cross is present to us. Wherefore
also upon houses, and upon walls, and upon windows, and upon foreheads, and
upon the mind we inscribe it with great earnestness. For this is the symbol
of the salvation for us and of the common freedom, and of the forbearance of
the Master. Wherefore it is called a seal, because all the deposits of God,
as many as we receive, we seal with this, as with some royal signet and
ring, and no evil thing any longer dares to come near. If we deposit with
some people a protection, or if we bring one to the dignity of the
priesthood, after myriad prayers, and calling the grace of the Spirit come,
we seal it with this, as shutting up the gift given in a sure treasury. So
also it is taken up in the liturgies. For the cross of Christ is a sword and
wood, on which Christ, slain by the deicide Jews, loosed the works of the
devil, and cast down the devil himself, nailing our sins upon it. For this
reason it is taken up in the liturgies, as a kind of royal sword and whip,
torturing and driving away every daemonic onset and satanic assault.
Wherefore the divine hieromystics, like some bodyguards, setting forth the
royal whip, through it drive away every opposing and antagonistic phalanx,
distributing the divine and royal gifts to the initiated, and honoring with
them Christ the King. γʹ. When therefore you seal, conceive the whole
economy of the cross, and quench anger, and all the other passions. When you
seal, fill the forehead with much boldness, the breast, the eyes, and make
every member represent a sacrifice well-pleasing to God. For this is the
rational worship. This sign also opened the shut doors of our forefathers,
this quenched poisonous drugs, this loosed the power of hemlock, this healed
the bites of venomous beasts. For if it opened the gates of Hades, and
spread out the arches of the heavens, and renewed the entrance of paradise,
and cut out the sinews of the devil, what wonder if it prevails over
poisonous drugs? Engrave therefore this upon your mind, and embrace the
salvation of our souls. For this cross saved the inhabited world, drove out
error, brought back truth, made earth heaven, wrought men angels. Through it
the demons are no longer terrible, but easily despised, nor is death death,
but sleep and waking. Through it the passions of the flesh warring against
those who fast =52.839= wither away. If therefore anyone of the Jews should
say to you: Do you worship the Crucified? say with a bright and great voice,
and with a rejoicing face: Yes, and I worship, and I shall not cease
worshipping; and if he laugh, weep that he is mad, and does not understand
what he utters; and give thanks to the Master Christ, that he has benefited
us with such things as one cannot even learn without the revelation from
above. Wherefore this man also laughs, because the psychical man does not
receive the things of the Spirit. But what does the frenzied and ungrateful
and disobedient Jew say? If then Christ is God, as you say, and Son of God,
and has come for the salvation of men, why was he crucified with such a
shameful


death? To him we shall say a few things selected from the prophets: It was
necessary for Christ to be crucified, O lawless and ungrateful Jew, because
the law and the prophets thus proclaimed, that mankind be saved through
Christ. For first Moses says, You shall see your life hanging before your
eyes, and you shall not believe your life. And Isaiah: As a sheep he was led
to slaughter, and as a lamb before the one shearing it is silent. And
Jeremiah, Come and let us cast wood into his bread. And, They gave his honor
into the field of the potter, as the Lord appointed me. And that God is the
crucified Christ, hear Esdras saying: Blessed is the Lord who spread forth
his hands, and saved Jerusalem before our eyes. And because the serpents
killed the people, Moses, hanging a serpent upon wood at the command of God,
said: Attend to this, and you shall not die. And again Jeremiah says, as
from the face of those crucifying him: Come and let us cut him off from the
land of the living. And again Christ through the same prophet: I was as a
harmless lamb led to be sacrificed, and knew it not. And again David as from
the person of Christ: They dug my hands and my feet, and divided my garments
among themselves, and upon my clothing they cast lots. And again, I spread
forth my hands the whole day to a people disobedient and contradicting, who
walked not in a good way, but after their sins. And that the cross of Christ
and its type is venerable and to be worshipped, the prophets also teach; for
David saying: You gave to those who fear you a sign, to flee from the face
of the bow; and again: Make with me a sign for good, and let those who hate
me see it, and be ashamed. And God also through Ezekiel the prophet says:
Give the sign upon the foreheads of those who groan and are grieved in all
their iniquities; and go through, and smite, and do not pity; elder and
youth, and women and suckling infants blot out; but upon those having my
sign do not touch. And Solomon says, Bless the wood through which
righteousness comes to be. And Isaiah, whence it was, and what the woods of
the cross, says: In cypress and pine and cedar, together to glorify the holy
place. And Moses also, casting wood into Marah, sweetened the bitter waters
as a type of the cross of Christ, who sweetened the world from the
bitterness of the demons. And the rod of Moses which split the rock was a
type of the cross of Christ, who split the hearts of men, and cast the grace
of the Holy Spirit into them. δʹ. And this is truly the way of life, and all
who hold it live; but those who leave it will have death and punishment
without end. For the law and the prophets preach the holy Trinity; and truly
this is the one proclaimed by the prophets and by the law, Jesus Christ the
Lord, who comes in the name of the Lord, and there is no other, except he
who was born in Bethlehem of Judaea of the Virgin and Mother of God Mary in
the days of Caesar Augustus. And whoever does not receive h52.840 im, and
does not believe in him, is an apostate of God, and anathema to him.
Wherefore Isaiah also cries concerning you who have gone astray, saying, My
people has been led captive because they knew not the Lord. For from the
time when Christ came, the power of your cross was undone, O Jews, and the
priesthood was made void, and you were scattered by the Romans. For Isaiah
says concerning those who crucified Christ: Because of our sins God turned
away his face from us, and our remnant in sins. For if he himself were not
Christ born of Mary, the elect of God, and his true Son, proclaimed by the
law and by the prophets, the nation of you Jews would have been glorified,
and exalted, and would have reigned, as having killed the anti-god and
deceiver.


But from the cross, and Christ having been crucified upon it by your
fathers, from that time until today it is unto perdition and unto dishonor,
and God was more angered with you than for the captivity in Babylon. For
there after sixty years God had mercy and recalled you; here he utterly
thrust you away; and your father and prophet and patriarch Jacob was found a
liar, and all your lawful things fell, and you were driven out of your
country of Judaea, and scattered in places, and are unto contempt and unto
derision in all the inhabited world, from the east even to the ends of the
earth. But the things of us Christians flower every day, and grow, and are
strengthened, and into all the inhabited world the proclamation of our pious
faith ran, and Christ reigns in us, and we worship his precious and
life-giving cross, and hold it as a treasure most precious. For truly
brighter and more venerable than every royal crown is the cross of the Lord;
and what do I say, royal crown? brighter than the very rays of the sun. And
the thing of old was a condemnation of an evil life and of corrupted deeds;
but now a symbol of divine gift, a sign of spiritual nobility, an inviolate
treasure, an irremovable gift, a ground of sanctification. This also we set
forth upon bed and upon table, and everywhere wherever we may be. For as
many of the soldiers without weapons neither make breakfast nor sleep; so
now instead of a sword let us hang it upon the bed, instead of a bar let us
draw it upon the door, instead of a wall let us throw it around the whole
house; the things within and the things without let us fence with it. For
this abolished death, opened the heavens, purified earth, led up our nature
to the royal throne, overthrew the tyranny of the devil. This form is
twofold; one of matter, or gold, or pearls, or precious stones, which is
also often taken away by barbarians or thieves; the other immaterial; for
its subsistence is not of matter, but from faith is the essence, from the
disposition of the doer the matter. This keeps those sleeping, this secures
those waking, this saves those in danger; through this war is dissolved, and
peace is established. I hymn you therefore, the long-suffering and
unresentful mystery of the economy concerning me, Lord; I worship your
precious and life-giving cross, Master; I embrace the passions, I love the
nails, and the piercings of the members I greet; the reed and the spear and
the sponge I love beyond all; as a royal diadem I put on the crown of
thorns, and as with clear stones I am adorned with the spittings, as with
the brightest ornament I am honored by the blows. And I confess you the true
God, the one of the holy Trinity, Christ Jesus, who for me condescended to
the passion, and was wrapped in linen, and mocked by the lawless Jews, at
last buried, and risen on the third day, and coming again to judge the
living and the dead. For yours is the glory and the power, together with the
Father and your all-holy and life-giving Spirit, now and always, and unto
the ages of ages. Amen.
