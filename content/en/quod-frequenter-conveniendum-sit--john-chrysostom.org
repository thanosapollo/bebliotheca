* That One Must Assemble Frequently
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of our father among the saints John, archbishop of Constantinople, the
Golden-mouthed, a homily delivered in the martyr-shrine at the old rock, few
having gathered because of the winter; on the need to assemble continually,
and not to despair of salvation while being in sins, but to display
repentance.

=63.461= What is this? When the whole city ought to have been present today,
not even the smallest part of the assembly has met us? Perhaps the mud is the
cause, and the rain? Away with that: not the mud, but the slothful and
collapsed mind. For what pardon could those who were left behind have, when
the martyrs even despised life itself, and they for the sake of being present
did not even dare the mud? How shall I call you who are present blessed? how
shall I count those who were left behind wretched, both for the absence and
for the cause of the absence? For it is quite clear that, nailed to worldly
cares, and kindling more fiercely in themselves the love of money that ruins
everything, they were left behind from this fair feast. But even if they are
not present, it is necessary to speak also toward those who are absent; for it
is quite clear that through you who are present they will hear these things:
until when the madness for money? until when the furnace that is never
quenched comes upon all things and burns them up? do you not know that this
flame begets that unquenchable fire? that this wasting begets that venomous
worm? But if you despise Gehenna, and these words do not shake your mind
because the punishment is still to come, then let even the present things
persuade you. Did you not know what fruit the love of money displayed a little
while ago? are the memorials not before your eyes? is the proof of so great a
catastrophe not still ringing in your ears? The whole city is filled for us
with the remnants of that shipwreck; and wherever you go, as when a great ship
has been sunk, one has the plank, another the oar, another the sail, another
some part of the cargo, having saved them and carrying them about; so also of
the earthquake that was brought upon us from there a little while ago, some
having divided the house, some the fields, some the slaves, some the silver,
some the gold, have made the sight of the disaster a broad one, and have
prepared the memorials of the shock to be scattered everywhere. And he, having
spent sleepless nights, and having endured ten thousand toils and dangers, and
having gathered so many sins from such greed, has become houseless, cityless,
a fugitive toward exile, and is in want of necessary food; and each day he
sees the danger concerning the last things hanging over him, imagining swords
and executioners and pits, living a life more grievous than ten thousand
deaths; but others revel in his possessions, and those who before this
flattered him now plot against him. Are these things then not =63.462= enough
to bring even the exceedingly insensible to sobriety? But after so great a
blow, after so great a storm, after so great a catastrophe, after so great and
such a change, the one appearing before the eyes, the one that happened not
even thirty days ago, do you rave at these things again? and what pardon would
you have, or what defense? And you not only rave, but you do not even come,
that you may learn this very thing.

For as toward those present I converse with those who are absent, stupefied
with much despondency, because they do not become better either by the fear of
the things to come, or by the experience of the present things; but seizing,
defrauding, like worms in some dunghill, caught and buried in the rubbish of
these cares, they do not even once


in the week take care to be present, that they may learn at least this very
thing, where they are. For just as those possessed by phrenitis could not
themselves see in what state they are, but need physicians who will deliver
them from the madness; so also those possessed by the grievous madness for
money need other teachers, that they may learn at least that they are raving.
For this reason especially I need them and exhort and beseech them to meet us,
and to receive the medicines from the word. I do not have a sharpened iron,
but I have a word sharper than iron; there is no fire, nor a biting drug, but
there are words hotter than fire, bringing on a painless healing.

βʹ. For what reason then do you leap away, tell me, and do not even display
the same forethought concerning the soul as the sparing you have concerning
the flesh? For when that is in a bad state you both spend money, and if it is
necessary to borrow, you consume everything, having mortgaged it all; and to
physicians, even if they wish to cut, even if to burn, you offer the body with
much eagerness to do whatever they wish; but when the soul has springs of
worms, you do not come to hear, tell me, a word that cleanses the
putrefaction; and this when you neither have money to pay down, nor so great a
pain to endure; but you have given yourself over to utter destruction? and how
are these things worthy of pardon? For if I were saying, Let the greedy man,
the robber, the fornicator, the adulterer not come to church, and I were
driving and chasing away all those in sins; even then there would be no
defense; for one ought to come after having been cleansed; but now I do not
even say this, but even if you fornicate, even if you commit adultery, even if
you rob, even if you defraud, come to church, that you may =63.463= learn no
longer to do such things; I drag all and draw them, and having spread the nets
of the word from every side, I desire to catch alive here not those who are
healthy, but also those who are sick.

These things I say each day: come and be healed with me; for I too, while
healing, have need of medicines; for I am a man and am subject to the same
passions of nature as you, and I need words that bridle the disorderly bond;
nor do I live an untroubled life, quiet and undisturbed, but I myself also
have tumults of desire and disturbances of waves. And what need is there to
speak of me and so-and-so, when Paul who touched the heavens himself also
needed much healing? And this very thing he made clear to us, that he needed
it, and that he himself did not live an untroubled life, but had many
wrestlings; wherefore he also said: I buffet my body and bring it into
slavery, lest having preached to others, I myself become disqualified. And he
was buffeting that which was rising up, and bringing into slavery that which
wished to throw off the reins; wherefore he also exhorted others saying: Let
him who thinks he stands look lest he fall.

But if Paul did not enjoy a calm, but like those borne on the open sea, saw
many waves being raised from every side; who will dare to say that he does not
need correction and healing and continual watchfulness? Come then and be
healed with me the teacher. But if you are healthy, even for this reason come,
that you may become healthier; for the word both delivers those who have
diseases from the infirmity, and makes those who do not have them more secure,
being both corrective of the things that have happened, and preventive of the
things that have not yet occurred. But if you do not have this sin, you have
another; For who will boast that he has a pure heart? or who will speak boldly
that he is clean from sin? Do not then, because you have sinned, be ashamed to
be present, but for this very reason come. For no one says: Since I have a
wound, I do not seek the physician, nor do I even accept a medicine; but for
this very reason especially it is necessary to seek both those who heal and
the power of the medicines. For we too know how to pardon, inasmuch as we
ourselves also are liable to other sins. For this reason also God did not give
us angels as teachers, nor having brought Gabriel


down from above did he set him over his flocks, but taking from the flock
itself he makes shepherds, from the sheep themselves the leader of the flock,
that he may be forbearing toward those ruled, and considering his own
weakness, may not be lifted up against those shepherded, but may have a bridle
and a ground of humility, the necessity of his own conscience. And that what
has been said is not a guess, hear Paul writing these things and
philosophizing; for writing to the Hebrews, he spoke thus: For every high
priest taken from among men is appointed on behalf of men, to offer gifts and
sacrifices, being able to deal gently with those who are ignorant and going
astray, since he himself also is clothed with weakness; and because of this he
ought, as for the people, so also for himself to offer for sins. Did you see
how most excellently he has told us the reason why not angels nor archangels,
but men, have been set over the Churches, that they may be able to suffer
together with those of the same race, having the consciousness of their own
sins as a greatest school of humility? For he himself also, he says, is
clothed with weakness, and because of this he ought, as for the people, so
also for himself to offer for sins. This then also happens now; for standing
at this sacred table, and offering up the fearful sacrifice, just as we ask
that there be forgiveness for the trespasses of the =63.464= people, so also
for our own we exhort and beg and beseech and we offer up the sacrifice for
all ......

γʹ. Do not bring before me the greedy man, and the slave of the belly, and the
one who has betrayed the nobility to the wickedness of nature, but this man
and the men after that one's pattern, who keep the archetype, and do not spoil
the royal stamp, and then you will know what a man is. For this man too was a
man, and was born of a woman, and was fed with milk, and dwelt on earth, and
breathed air, and all things were common to him with us, the things of nature
common; but since the things of choice became exceptional, through these he
also shone forth with this grace. And yet money was not present to him, nor
distinction of race, nor greatness of fatherland, nor cleverness of words, nor
a sharpened tongue, nor herds of slaves, nor a swarm of eunuchs, nor a
gold-roofed house, nor silken garments, nor a table having luxury, nor any
other of the things that seem to many to be enviable, but the last poverty;
for he did not even have plenty of necessary food, but of a widow woman, and
that a Sidonian, he became a beggar, stretching out a hand, and asking for
bread; and a cave was his dwelling, and a skin his garment, and the earth his
table, and ancestors both obscure and nameless, and a cheap fatherland, and a
rustic life.

But nevertheless none of these things became a hindrance to the man toward
good repute, but he was even wealthier than all kings, and wiser than all
philosophers and orators, and more distinguished than those who have diadems,
and more well-born than those who possess royal cities; for he had the
inhabited world as fatherland, or rather even this was a small city to him,
and Paul cries saying: They went about in sheepskins, in goatskins; of whom
the world was not worthy. For he belonged to the city above, whose craftsman
and maker is God. For this reason I brought before you a poor and rustic man
in good repute, that you may have no pretext. For if I had brought a rich and
wise man, you could have said, that Poverty and ignorance hinder me from
becoming such; but now you have no such pretext to flee to; for that man too
was poor, poorer than all men, and he dwelt in the desert, and from ancestors
obscure and from fatherland and from all the other things. But nevertheless he
shone above the sun both then and now, and as much earth as the sun looks
upon, so much does his glory go upon, although he came to be so long a time
ago; but the multitude of time did not quench his memory; and very reasonably.
For such is virtue, an immortal thing, and


shining above the sun. But, as I said before (for one must not forget the
subject, having been carried out into the sea of the praises of the prophet),
this man also shows the things said concerning Moses. For since it was likely
that, lifted up by the achievements, he would not be very forbearing toward
the many, see what God does: He lets him be stripped of the grace from him, so
that his weakness may be shown; or rather first it is necessary to show how he
was not very forbearing. For when he shut the heaven, and made the earth
barren, and there was a famine, the bitterest famine of all, and waters were
consumed, and every herb was dried up, and rivers failed, and the face of the
earth was fearful, threatening death, and the famine coming upon all things
=63.465= was feeding on them, bringing on the most pitiable death; not even so
did he yield, but having gone away he sat on a mountain-peak, handing over the
people of the Jews to this grievous wasting.

But the loving God, and the unspeakable goodness, did not bear this austerity;
but since he himself did not wish to exhort him from himself to loose the
terrible thing, and he himself again did not wish to do this without that man,
see what happens: first he communicates it to him, and then he lets go the
rain. Did you see God's goodness and loving-kindness in this also, and honor
toward the saint? For he did not let go the reins of the rains before he spoke
with him and told him what was about to be, and sent him as a herald of the
coming plenty. For since through him the gloomy things had come about, he
wished him also to become a messenger of the good things, and having brought
him down from the mountain, he sent him announcing everywhere in that land the
presence of the rains and the loosing of the famine. But nevertheless, when
the rain had been brought down, and in a brief turn of time plenty had
returned, having brought the priests of Baal, the four hundred, and the four
hundred and eighty, he slaughtered them, and let loose torrents of blood.

δʹ. What then have these things to do with the present subject? Do not be
troubled, beloved; for I shall now walk toward the very end of the things
said. For when he had brought down that torch, and had won the victory, and
had set up a splendid trophy, and had worked the slaughter of the priests, he
went away rejoicing, and thinking great things of what had happened. The wife
of Ahab sent to him, a cheap and contemptible little harlot, saying: Thus may
the gods do to me, and thus may they add, if I do not make your soul tomorrow
as one of these priests. And he feared, and fled a journey of forty days. What
then? that heaven-high soul, the one who looked down on the whole world, who
despised so great a famine, and rose up against a king so tyrannical, who shut
the heaven and opened it, who now brought down rain, now a torch, who trampled
the necessities of nature, the unfrightened one, the one bold everywhere; this
man all at once, after so many achievements, after such freedom of speech,
could not bear the threat through words of one girl who played the harlot, but
thereafter becomes a fugitive and cityless, and runs away to the desert, and
withdraws a journey of forty days. What then was the cause? God stripped him
of the grace, and the weakness of nature was shown; he showed the prophet, and
he showed also the man, that they might learn that the things that happened
then also were a work of grace. And he did these things both making him
forbearing toward the many, and cutting under and checking the madness that
comes from achievements. For that he thought great things of himself and
imagined them, and rose up against all the others, I will furnish you this man
himself as witness.

For when God came to him, and asked the cause of his spending time there,
saying: What are you doing here, Elijah? and, What are you doing here? he
asked, not wishing to learn, but


wishing to uncover for us the secrets of his mind; since also when Christ
asked the Canaanite woman, it was not that he might learn, but that he might
teach us the treasure of faith stored up in her; he asks him then why he had
arrived so great a journey, and leaving cities and peoples, embraces the
desert, not that he himself might learn (for how could he who knows all
things?), but that he might teach us through the =63.466= answer of that man
the secrets of his mind, and that reasonably he let him be shaken by fear, and
fall into cowardice; he let him, he did not push him; he allowed, he did not
operate, but he only stripped him, and he was convicted; when then he said,
What are you doing here, Elijah? let us hear what he says: They have torn down
your altars, and they have killed your prophets; and I have been left alone,
and they seek my soul. Do you see that he thought all had perished, and that
he alone had been left, and that after him there was no other; which little by
little could lead him into madness. For this reason then God, taking away this
deceived reasoning of his, says: I have left to myself seven thousand men, who
have not bent a knee to Baal. Since then also when he brought on the drought,
he remained stretching the punishment, and after these things he imagined
great things of himself, inasmuch as he alone was such on the earth; he lets
him perceive his own weakness, teaching the multitude of those who were saved,
through both of these things bringing down his mind, and persuading him to be
moderate everywhere, and to be forbearing, and to mix loving-kindness with the
zeal.

εʹ. But if you wish to see these same things also in the New, again we shall
try to bring before you the chiefs and towers and bulwarks, and the heads of
those in the New. As Moses and Elijah, so Paul and Peter are shown in the New.
For just as each of these, one fearing the threat of one Egyptian, the other
of one harlot, were put to flight; so also Peter the foundation, the base, the
pillar, after ten thousand commands, after so many wonders, after such
philosophy, feared the threat not of a queen, nor of a man, but of a
doorkeeper girl; and it was much more grievous than the former. For those,
having feared, only fled, but this man also fell a most grievous fall, which
all know; for when he was stripped of the grace, his weakness too was shown,
having become desolate of the care of God. And God let him fall, since he was
about to make this man too a ruler of the whole inhabited world; that
remembering his own falls, he might pardon those who were tripped up in the
things that happen thereafter.

And that what has been said is not a guess, hear Christ himself saying: Simon,
Simon, how many times Satan has sought to sift you as the wheat; and I have
prayed concerning you, that your faith may not fail. And you, when you have
turned, strengthen your brothers. Give me this return, he says, of the help
concerning you; for if you had not enjoyed my providence, you would not have
been sufficient by yourself to bear that one's assault. Considering then your
own things, become also forbearing toward the others; for the Strengthen is
this, of one hinting: strengthen those who are shaken, condescending,
stretching out a hand, displaying much loving-kindness. And Paul, being bolder
than every lion, the unfrightened soul, suffered this same thing. And see how
he shows himself continually needing the medicines of humility; for writing to
the Corinthians he says: For I do not wish you to be ignorant, brothers,
concerning our affliction which happened to us in Asia, that we were weighed
down exceedingly beyond power; but we ourselves in ourselves have had the
sentence of death =63.467=, that we may not be trusting in ourselves, but in
God who raises the dead, who delivered us from so great deaths. And we have
hoped that he will also deliver.


We were given up, he says, we did not expect to live, but we reckoned in
ourselves that we would die altogether; for this is, We ourselves in ourselves
have had the sentence of death; this is what the affairs answered us, that we
shall perish altogether. For what reason did God allow us to come as far as so
many dangers? That we may not be trusting in ourselves, he says, but in God
who raises the dead. And what he means is such: That we may not think great
things, that we may not be lifted up by the achievements. Which also going on
he says more clearly, writing again thus: for when he spoke of the snatching
into heaven, the entrance into paradise, the communion of those unspeakable
words, he added: And by the excess of the revelations, that I may not be
lifted up, there was given me a thorn in the flesh, an angel of Satan, that he
may buffet me; hinting here at those who afflict and insult, and throw him
into prison; for each of these he calls Satan. And I was allowed, he says, to
endure temptations, that my madness might be checked, and I might learn to be
moderate. For this reason also having besought God, I did not obtain the
request; but I remained continually plotted against, having the affliction as
a medicine of humility. And the life before this too, the one before the
conversion, was enough to bring him to sobriety. For he who sat at the feet of
Gamaliel, who lived according to the exactness of the ancestral law, the
zealot of the paternal traditions, who unrolled the prophets each day, who was
reared with the law, this man, when Christ came and worked wonders, and
discoursed these things, and set forth and interpreted the things from the
Scriptures, did not receive him, but even when he had been crucified and
=63.468= risen he persecuted, and with ten thousand hands he struck that one's
disciple, and he made war on the Churches, and was more savage than every
wolf; and until a light shone from above, and a voice was brought drawing him
toward the truth, he was not able from himself to see what was needed.
Wherefore he himself also continually turns these things over in the letters,
bowing down and covering himself over the things that have already happened;
and writing to Timothy he said: I give thanks to Christ who strengthened me,
that he counted me faithful, putting me into ministry, the one who was
formerly a blasphemer and a persecutor and an insolent man; but I was shown
mercy, because I did it ignorantly in unbelief. And again, For this reason I
was shown mercy, that in me first God might display all the long-suffering,
toward a pattern of those who are about to believe upon him unto eternal life.
And writing to the Corinthians he said: Who am not sufficient to be called an
apostle, because I persecuted the Church.

ςʹ. And all these things happened, and God let them be naked of his own grace,
so that, when they have been taught their own weakness, and how a man is
nothing when he has no share of the impulse from above, having gone up onto
the judicial throne, and having become leaders of the people and rulers, they
may display much loving-kindness, much condescension, much pardon toward those
ruled. Wherefore also writing he said to the Galatians: Brothers, even if a
man is overtaken in some trespass, you who are spiritual restore such a one,
looking to yourself, lest you also be tempted. Knowing these things then let
us both draw those who throw off the reins, and become forbearing toward those
who sin, and let us display much loving-kindness together with the laws of
God; that we ourselves also may enjoy much pardon in the things in which we
sin, by the grace and loving-kindness of our Lord Jesus Christ, through whom
and with whom to the Father be the glory, together with the Holy Spirit, now
and always, and unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
