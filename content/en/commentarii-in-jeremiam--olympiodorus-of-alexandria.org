* Commentaries on Jeremiah

=93.628= Chapter 1. "Who dwelt in Anathoth." Anathoth from fifty thousand of
Jerusalem. =93.629= "I know you." According to the reasons of foreknowledge.
"To root out." To root out vice. "What do you see? And I said: A rod of
nut-wood." These things from an interval, when he says: And the word of the
Lord came to me saying; for not in sequence, but he was giving [him] to
understand the things said. For this wood is hard, and heavy, and striking.
For what reason was I seeing these things? Because the deeds and the sights
were more vivid, and struck the hearer more. For the rod is a symbol of a
blow. A word, that the nut is productive of watchfulness; and that the wood is
also easily broken, and it signifies the more moderate wrath; as God is
watchful over those who sin, but punishing more moderately. "From the face of
the North." Because of the Babylonian, he says. For Babylon is more northerly
as toward Jerusalem. And toward the sense, the devil is taken as the face of
the hard North wind. "A cauldron kindled underneath." According to the letter,
Jerusalem, and the rulers in her; and toward contemplation, the conscience
that proves itself. "And you, gird yourself." Instead of, play the man, go
with confidence. And toward the sense, the temperate man girds his loin. For
they say the seeds are in the loin. For it has been said concerning Levi: For
he was still in the loin of your father. "Do not fear, for I am with you."
Having fenced [him] about with the angelic powers. Chapter 2. "I remembered
the mercy of your youth." Holy Israel to the Lord, to the Saviour Christ; the
true Israel, the mind that sees God, whom he also looks on gladly as a
firstfruit of products. Israel then is called at one time the people of the
Jews, and at another the more clear-sighted, and also the patriarch himself
and Christ according to the flesh from Israel. According to mercy, he says,
and love I anointed you as a prophet; for the gifts are not from achievements.
"And they went after the vain things." Of the idols and of the manners of
vice. Into Carmel." This is a mountain of Palestine. And it is interpreted
knowledge of circumcision; through which we cut off the good from the
worthless. "Pass over to the islands of Chettiim." Some say Cyprians, and some
nations of Indians. Kedar is a descendant of Ishmael; and through this the
nations of the Saracens are signified. And it is interpreted darkening; and
they say that also an earthenware thing is indicated through Kedar. "Lions
roared upon him." Lions toward the letter the Babylonians, and toward the
sense untamed demons. "Because of not being inhabited;" Because of not having
good inhabitants. =93.632= "To drink water of Geon." Geon is the Nile, and
water =93.632= of the Egyptians, their polytheistic error. "To drink water of
rivers." Rivers of Assyrians the many-formed manners of vice, and upon either,
both can be understood. "For from of old you have broken your yoke." For from
the beginning stiff-necked, always resisting the Holy Spirit; and he calls the
legislation a yoke. "There I will be poured out in my fornication." For vice,
having taken a beginning, is poured out to infinity. "How have you been turned
into bitterness?" For evils are not according to nature, but according to
turning aside; the alien vine, she who by choice alienated herself from God.
"How will you say, I have not been defiled?" Of extreme madness for the sinner
to deny. "See your ways in the polyandrion." A polyandrion is the tomb; either
then they were doing in the tombs some things pleasing to demons, or that they
themselves also were whitened tombs. Pursuing falsehood in hypocrisy of the
truth. "Late her voice was howling, she widened her ways upon waters of a
desert." Female dogs howl, calling the males to coupling; this then, he says,
she has suffered in darkness of soul, calling the unclean demons. She widened
her ways under waters of a desert. Waters of a desert, the many-formed and
fruitless


ways of vice; they were being filled with operations of unclean spirits.
Having given herself over by choice. For no one will heal him who does not
wish to be healed. "In her humiliation they will find her." Because of her
being attached to them. "I will play the man." I suffice for myself for help,
I do not need God, or he says this, that toward that in which she was being
admonished, she was playing the man unto disobedience. "Have I become a desert
to Israel?" Have I not granted them, he says, all things toward life and
benefit. "What good will you still practice in your ways to seek love?" A soul
standing away from the true God, not enduring to be godless, procures for
herself gods that are not, procuring consolation for herself from there. Not
so then, he says, that is, it is not possible for there to be consolation for
the soul from the falsely named gods. "I did not find them in diggings, but
upon every oak." Not even secretly, he says, but on high, that is, they sin
openly. "And you said, I am innocent." When you ought to confess the sins, and
you are shameless, denying them? "Behold I am judged with you in your saying,
I have not sinned." If through another prophet he commands one to speak the
sins, so that one may be justified, for one speaks [looses] the fault through
confession, absurd a =93.633= gain is he who says: I am innocent, let his
wrath be turned away from me; for he adds, Behold I am judged with you, in
your saying, I have not sinned; everywhere the one who has faulted must heal
the sin by confession. "For you despised repeating your ways." Thus you held
[them] in contempt, and you do the evils without fear, so as even to add
others to those that have gone before. "Your hands upon your head." This is a
mourning figure. Chapter 3. "And you fornicated among many shepherds." Either
with false teachers, or also with the unclean demons, [he says]. "Did you not
call me as a house, and a father, and a leader." Even if you, he says, did not
call upon me, as a covering, and a father, and a leader of your virginity, yet
I will not let the evils remain, either saving you by grace, or by
chastisements. "And I said after she fornicated all these things, Return to
me; and she did not return." He calls her uncompounded toward herself, uneven,
and discordant, because honoring God with the lips, she was lying in the
heart, or also her who loosed the covenants toward God, or also the unstable
one, not remaining in the same things. I loosed the just things toward her,
having delivered [her] to the Babylonians. "Go, and read these words toward
the North." Because of their hearts having been chilled, and not having
spiritual warmth; for the North is a cold wind. "And I will not fix my face
upon us." I will not use wrath against us persistently. "And I will take you
one from a city, and two from a family." Man is composed of two, of soul and
body; when then he saves the property of the soul, and has not wholly become
flesh, the one is understood as two; but when having wholly declined into the
evil things he becomes entirely fleshly, he is justly called one. He says then
that both those who have sinned wholly, and those who have not yet suffered
this, I call through grace unto salvation; or thus: One from a city was
called, Paul from the tribe of Benjamin, and two from a family, the apostles
called in pairs. "And I will bring you into Sion." Into Sion, the Church. "I
will give you shepherds." Those who lead the Churches. "They will call
Jerusalem the throne of the Lord." Because the Holy Spirit rests in the
Church; and he hints in these sayings also at the Jerusalem above. "I will
appoint you for children." Either the adoption has been promised [alt. is
announced] to the prophet, or also to all the people being saved. "Sons,
return, returning." You who are about to [come] toward return. =93.636= "Truly
the hills were unto falsehood." The divinations in the mountains, or also the
bulk of sin. "Their sheep." Sheep, those things about which they were
disposed. "We slept in our shame." He calls rest sleep; thinking then, he
says, to rest upon the works of sin, we were being filled with shame. Chapter
4. "If Israel is not turned." If he does not repent upon a whole heart. "And
he swear."


He does not command to swear, but if after all someone swears, he wishes them
not to make the idols their oaths. And he weaves, As the Lord lives, into the
oath, so that swearing they may at the same time both doxologize and give
thanks. For it is not unjustly, or without judgment, that one must bring
forward the name of God. For good conduct becomes a cause of salvation also
for others. "Be circumcised to your God." For he wishes vice, not the flesh,
to be circumcised. "Announce in Judah. He prophesies the evangelical
preaching. "Let us enter into the cities that have walls." Into the Churches,
whose gates of Hades will not prevail. "Flee into Sion." Flee into Sion, into
the catholic Church; for also all the Churches happen to be one; or also into
the godly polity. "A lion went up from his fold." Figuratively he signifies
the presence of the devil; and according to history, of the Babylonian. "The
priests will be astonished." At the unexpected disasters. "And the prophets
will wonder." The false prophets. "A spirit of wandering in the desert." By
that of the onset of the Babylonians, or of Romans, or by that of the
consummation, those formerly deceived, whether Jews, or the remaining men,
through their own desolation will know the former error. "A spirit of filling
will come to me." Since a spirit of wandering deceived the desert souls, he
promises to give a Holy Spirit turning the souls that fill his Churches.
"Until when will they exist?" For also those who reason vain things labor
toward finding what is sought. "Because a voice of one going up from Dan will
come to me." Of him who announces that the capture will happen from Dan, and
as far as Beersheba, by Babylonians, or Romans. And Dan is beyond the Jordan,
and the mountain of Ephraim is Samaria. And some say that also from the tribe
of Dan the adversary will come. "My skins were torn, my tents were
distressed." Figuratively [he says] the soul, and the distributive powers of
the soul; and the prophet's inward parts also are troubled from sympathy. "The
tent has been distressed," either the temple, or also toward =93.637= the end
all the earth, which we inhabit as a tent, being sojourners. Again either he
says the temple, or the coverings of vice, all things being made manifest
toward the end. "Until when shall I see those fleeing, hearing a voice of
trumpets?" Of the Babylonians', or Romans', or also toward the end of the
diabolical preaching. Either the perceptible enemies, or also those not able
to stand against the devil, he says. Either of the enemies, or also of the
strange preachings. "And into the heaven, and his lights were not." He
pre-announces the darkness that happened upon the cross, or also the taking
away of the luminaries toward the end. Or he signifies that no one is doing
just things, or also that the earth is restored unto nothing because of the
apostasy. "I saw the mountains, and behold they were trembling." Either the
things upon the cross, he says, when the rocks were split, or the things
concerning the universal consummation, or the opposing powers being shaken,
when Christ was loosing the prison of Hades. "I looked, and behold there was
no man, and all the birds of the heaven were terrified." He says the universal
consummation, which also happened at the flood, because of sin. Or the
perceptible things when the darkness was happening, or also the holy powers,
shuddering at the complete apostasy of men. "I saw, and behold Carmel was a
desert." Carmel is interpreted knowledge of circumcision. When then, he says,
the apostasy is happening, they do not know to cut off the worthless things.
And you can understand these things also upon the onset of the Babylonians, or
also of the Romans. "From a voice of a horseman, and of a stretched bow."
Either of the perceptible, or also he signifies the continual battle of the
invisible enemies. "They slipped into the caves." Into their robber manners,
standing away from God. And the cave being self-carved also signifies the
self-determining of the soul. "And they hid in the groves." Into the dark
deeds. "And they went up upon the rocks." Into the fruitless and uneven
practices; and take these also perceptibly according to history. "And I will
not make a consummation." The preludes of the


consummation having sketched, he added this. "If she wrap herself in scarlet."
The animal-slayings according to the law. To the ark, and to the lampstand,
and to the rest he signifies the piety with hypocrisy. "They seek your soul."
The invisible demons, not able to help. And these also, pretending to love,
plot against the very soul of those loved. "Upon those slain." Perceptible, or
also those who have lost the soul itself through apostasy. =93.640= Chapter 5.
"If there is one doing judgment, and seeking faith." Toward the saints who
were left behind he says that because those upon the earth did not keep the
faith toward Christ, the end will be set upon [them]; and he also signifies
the scarcity of the saints of those in the captivity. "As the Lord lives, they
say, therefore they do not swear in falsehoods." Against me, he says, and no
longer to swear by the idols of the falsely named gods. But they denied the
faith toward Christ. Therefore Jews were captured by Romans, no longer
idol-worshipping, but having denied their own Saviour. "Lord, your eyes are
unto faith." Either that God seeks faith, or that he judges faithfully and
truly, or that what he says he brings into work. "They are poor." Not having
perfect knowledge. "Therefore a lion from the thicket struck them." The
perceptible, or also the intelligible enemy because of the strong and savage.
"And a wolf as far as the houses utterly destroyed them." The perceptible; and
toward the sense, the psychic. For those who go out from the manners of piety
are hunted by the said beasts. Those who have thrust away the good shepherd, a
wolf seizes, or a varied onset of the enemies, or also the many-formed vice.
"To which of these shall I be gracious to you?" Which sin shall I forgive, and
which not? "I filled them, and they were committing adultery." For he was the
one who filled them in the desert before flesh, before becoming man, and after
flesh, through words, through wonders, and the five, and the seven loaves.
"Leave behind her stays." Leave a hope of repentance; leave the prophets at
the onset of Babylonians; leave the apostles at that of Romans. "My words in
your mouth fire." Consuming of those who disobey. A nation whose voice of his
tongue he will not hear." Because of being other-tongued. "And they will eat
the harvest, and your sons." They will use the slaughter against them
insatiably [alt. blow]. "Him who gives us early rain." The perceptible because
of the fruits, and the intelligible in the Old and New Testament. "Your sins
stood the good things away." They stood the good things away, so that the
worthless things might be brought on. "For ungodly men were found in my
people." The false prophets, the false apostles, the heresiarchs, the unclean
demons confirmed the things of the false prophets. Chapter 6. "Be strong, sons
of Benjamin." The Benjaminites are recorded as being ambidextrous. He calls
then unto the spiritual war those able through =93.641= the weapons of
righteousness to war, of the right and of the left. Such was Paul from the
tribe of Benjamin. "And in Thecue." Do not envy, he says, but correct your
neighbors, you who are able to war. "And your height will be taken away." The
temple, the legal worship. Or also the spiritual adornment of the soul.
"Shepherds will come into her." The enemies, perceptible rulers, or also
intelligible. "And they will shepherd each with his own hand." Not with the
hand of God, but each according to his own operations. "Woe to us." Those left
behind in the captivity, and sinners at the consummation will send forth this
voice. "For the day has declined." He calls the present life a day, and the
citizenship in it a shadow. "Rise, and let us go up against her by night." Of
Chrysostom. [alt. of Olympiodorus.] Night and midday he says the indifferent
time of the onset of the enemies. "As a pit cools its water, so her vice
cools." For because of lawlessness the love of the many will grow cold. For
they were not boiling in the spirit, nor serving the Lord. "With toil and a
scourge you will be educated." Since by word you were not persuaded, be
persuaded through the scourge, so that my soul may not stand away from you.
"The


remnant of Israel." Those who were not able, not even through a scourge, to be
healed. "Behold their ears are uncircumcised." Not turning away the
unprofitable hearings. Note what circumcision God seeks. "Saying, Peace,
peace." They were not letting them perceive the scourges, promising them
peace. "And not even so ... And they did not know their dishonor." For those
educated neither by word nor by scourge remain in the fall. "Therefore they
will fall ... and in the time of their visitation they will perish." Even if I
call them back, he says, from the perceptible enemies, and visit them,
remaining [alt. they remaining] in the sins they will be delivered to eternal
punishment. "Stand upon the ways." Stand upon the ways, and see both of life
and of death; and the life of each is also called a way. Paths of the Lord his
commandments. "Hear the voice of the trumpet." Those who preached the
prophetic, or also the evangelical word. Of a trumpet, of the teaching.
"Behold a people comes from the north." Of the Babylonian, or also of the
adversary. "They will hold a bow and a spear." A bow for those far, a spear
for those near. For those approach =93.644= ing they strike with works, and
those from afar with words. "Do not go out into a field." Beside the history,
understand a field as the worldly affairs. "And do not walk in the ways." The
many-ways of vice; for there is one way, the Lord himself. "For a sword of the
enemies sojourns round about." The death-bearing sin, for the ungodly walk in
a circle. "Bronze and iron." Bronze and iron unmixed with one another, not
having love. "The bellows failed from fire." A teaching word or also an
educating scourge. "Lead failed." Lead mixed with silver cleanses its vice;
this then he says, that every cleansing manner was brought upon them, and they
were not cleansed. Chapter 7. "Hear a word of the Lord, all Judea." After the
history it is fitting to understand that Judea is every soul drawing near to
piety. For Judah is interpreted confessing. "And you burn incense to Baal."
They were calling every idol Baal. And Baal is interpreted the higher evils.
And the head of sin is idolatry. "Is my house a cave of robbers?" The cave is
without a door, and set before everyone for entrance. He blames them then as
entering the holy things indifferently. "All the seed of Ephraim." The royal
tribe ruling the twelve tribes. "Their sons gather wood." They sin according
to power. "They kindle fire." Fire has been named sin in many places of
Scripture. Therefore it becomes a cause of eternal fire for those who sin. "To
make chabones." They perform foreign rites. And he says chabones are certain
cakes. "And eat meats." You eat them, he says; for I do not need sacrifices.
"Days and dawn." He signifies the unceasing of the prophetic exhortation.
"Faith failed from their mouth." He accuses their unbelief, or also
false-speaking. Chapter 8. "In that time they will open." Josiah the godly
king did this, having burned the remains of those who idol-worshipped. "And
they were overpowered in their choice." For man is self-determining. "There is
not a man who repents." Do not say, he says, There is no repentance, for there
is. "As a sweaty horse in his neighing." A horse runs in a long course [alt.
long-coursing], and neighs, signifying the toil; and a horse while running
leaves off, but these work vice unturnably [alt. unturningly] =93.645= "And
the asida in the heaven knew her time." Aquila rendered the heron; and the
asida is an Indian kind of bird. Turtledove and swallow, sparrows of the
field, kept times. "In vain a false rush became for scribes." The rush, he
says, that is the lot of the scribes, which they obtained unto teaching,
became vain, because of practicing a certain private wisdom, and
misinterpreting the divine ordinances. For this the [text] shows: What wisdom
is in them? "What wisdom is in them?" For there is no wisdom in such. And only
a surplus of vice ending [alt. toward] unto folly. "There is not


a grape in the vines." For he gave the vineyard out to other farmers. "And
upon what do we sit?" For they were not farming the things according to God.
And what more of safety could there be, when God who furnishes all safety to
all betrays [it]? When they ought to say Let us flee upon God, they still lie
below. He who masters nature, and makes trees useless, not much more the
walls? "Let us enter into the fortified cities." Toward history they later
fled into Egypt; and toward the sense he exhorts to flee into the Churches.
"And let us be thrown aside there." According to: I chose to be thrown aside
in the house of God. "We were gathered unto peace, and there were no good
things." We thought, he says, through the law to be reconciled to God, and not
having understood his power, we did not find good things. "Therefore behold I
send out serpents unto you." The perceptible enemies, or also intelligible.
"For which there is no charming, and they will bite." For since those bitten
feign piety, and are not persuaded by exhortation, thinking they are pious;
thus bitter are the nets of knavery. "With pain of your heart being at a
loss." For it is not possible not to be pricked by conscience. "Summer passed,
harvest went by." In a time of fair weather, he says, when it was a time of
repentance, we did not repent. "Is there not resin in Gilead?" The mountain
Gilead has many cedars, from which the resin [is] made for many passions. And
the city Gilead, being a city of the Jordan, was fashioning idols; they did
not then have the healing prophets, because they were sinning thus. "Why did a
healing of the daughter of my people not go up?" The wounds happen to be
hollow. Why then, he says, having those who heal, did they not bring up a
healing? that is, did they not heal their own wounds, did they not close them
up? Chapter 9. "Who will give water to my head?" The prophet asks =93.648= to
receive water upon the head, so that springs of tears may become for him, and
he may worthily weep those wounded from sin; and their trespasses are
numbered, having happened also in the times of the prophet, and having been
dared by them also against the Master Christ. And he speaks also against
hatred of brothers, and against those who lend at interest; and he also
foretells the complete desolation of Jerusalem. "Of the daughter of my
people." Of the synagogue of the Jews. "And they stretched their tongue as a
bow." Not even a little slackening, but unyieldingly sinning through the
tongue, just as indeed also washing the Saviour with insults. "He will mock."
He trips up by deceit. "They did not hear a voice of existence." They did not
wish to hear the voice of the Lord that grants them existence forever, which
is immortality. "From birds of the heaven, and as far as cattle." Both the
high, he says, according to virtue, and the beastlike declined into the evil;
and he also signifies the complete desolation of the place. "It was kindled as
a desert because of it not being traveled." Because of having no one, he says,
traveling in the ways of the Lord it became a desert. "And I will send the
sword unto them." The abrupt vengeance. "And send to the wise women." The
Churches, or the souls of the saints, whom he calls wise; for these lament
those slain by sin, as Paul says: And I will mourn many of those who have
sinned before, and have not repented. "Let your eyes bring down tears." The
tear of repentance. "We abandoned the land." The knowledge of piety. "And we
threw away our tents." We ourselves have become causes of the captivity for
ourselves. "For death went up through your windows." Wholly through the
teachers who seem to interpret the hidden things. For the windows make
manifest the things within of each one through the senses; and this perhaps
happened according to history, through the enemies; windows are the inventions
of error. "But in this ... let him boast to understand and to know." The word
exhorts to boast in nothing except in understanding and knowing the Lord, and
that the will of God is mercy, and judgment, and righteousness. "Upon Egypt,
and upon Idumaea."


Egyptians are called Barbarians in the divine Scripture. And Idumaeans, who
are also Edomites, fornicators, and profane, as from Esau; and the Moabites
and Ammonites through the manner are commanded not to enter into a Church of
God. He characterized then the manner of the Jews through these nations,
having called them by these names. =93.649= "And upon everyone shaven around."
Those who beautify what appears. And he means those who have a form of piety,
but have denied its power. Chapter 10. "And from the signs of the heaven do
not fear, because." Seeing, he says, the solar cycles, and moon waning and
waxing, and relation and distance of stars, and their appearances and hidings
according to time, and their well-fitted motion, do not fear them as gods, but
him who made these things. "Who set the inhabited world upright in his
wisdom." In the Son; for in him all things were created. "Every man was made
foolish from knowledge, was put to shame ... because he cast false things." He
who does not have the knowledge of this theology is a fool. "Vain are works
... in the time of their visitation they will perish." When God visits the
earth, in the presence of Christ, clearly. "He gathered from without his
substance that dwells." O knowledge of God, that dwells in the elect, from his
wonders and his makings your substance is grasped. And the Church also from
without, that is from the nations, was gathered. "And they made his place
desolate." Not letting [them] be nourished in the faith and the good
teachings; either having taken [them] away utterly, or having compelled [them]
to idol-worship. Chapter 11. "Thus says the Lord: Cursed is the man who will
not hear the words." God curses, but those who transgress the divine laws draw
the terrible things upon themselves. "To give them a land flowing milk and
honey." Toward history, Palestine; and toward the sense, the Church; giving
milk to drink to the newly planted, and honey to those able to taste the more
perfect lessons. "As this day. And I answered, and said, So be it, Lord." The
prophet foresees the theophanic day of the Saviour, according to which these
things have been given to the faithful, which also praying to be present, he
says, So be it, Lord. "A bond was found in men of Judah." An agreement of all
unto the worse. "What is the beloved?" Instead of, How is the beloved? "A fair
olive tree, well-shaded in appearance." When having chosen the patriarchs he
planted. "Unto a voice of her circumcision, fire was kindled upon her, great
is the affliction upon you." When, he says, the Synagogue ought to have been
well-worked, then she made herself worthy of fire; and she ought to have been
well-worked in the presence of Christ. "Lord, make known to me, and I will
know." He asks to receive a divine =93.652= revelation both concerning the
things about himself, and those upon the Master Christ. "And I as a harmless
lamb." For as a sheep the Lord was led to slaughter, according to Isaiah. He
did not resist. "For he will not strive, he says, nor cry out, nor will his
voice be heard outside." "Let us throw wood." Toward the prophet, a certain
deadly herb, so that eating he may be in pain. And to the precious cross they
fixed the bread of life. "And let us wipe him out from earth of living, and
his name shall no longer be remembered." See how they wish to make him even
nameless, wishing there not even to be some memory of him. For not only
living, but also having died, sinners are weighed down by the just. You see
Herod, how having taken John away, he had fear in himself as of one living? so
much is virtue, so much vice, thus that is cowardly and unmanly, [alt.
cowardly and unmanly,] thus this is manly and speaking with freedom. "Toward
you I revealed my just claim." For being insulted he was not insulting in
return, suffering he was not threatening; but he was delivering to him who
judges justly, according to the sacred apostle. "Upon those dwelling in
Anathoth." The citizens of the prophet, who were also plotting against him.
Chapter 12. "You are just, Lord, because I will make a defense toward you.
Yet." I know, he says, that you are just; and a word of


defense upon your judgments I seek. Or also thus: I must first make a defense
to you, Lord, that you are just, then seek the cause of the things in doubt.
"Why is it that a way of ungodly men is made prosperous?" You know that I
speak not envying, but asking. "You planted them." Instead of, you created
them, as maker of all, and they were not cut out while sinning. "You are near
their mouth, and far from their kidneys." You know, he says, that as far as
