HOMILY XII.
On the Meeting of our Lord Jesus Christ.
=77.1040= The assembly is very great, and the hearer is eager (for we see the
Church full); but the mystagogue is not =77.1041= rich. Yet he who gives to
man both mouth and tongue will again supply us with good thoughts. For he
himself says somewhere: Open your mouth wide, and I will fill it. And since
you have all come together eagerly, the brightness of the Master's feast
having gathered you to this with bright lamps, let us keep festival brightly,
and let us take hold of the contemplation of the things fulfilled today in a
manner befitting God, gathering for ourselves from every side what is sure for
faith and love of God. A little while ago we saw Emmanuel lying as an infant
in a manger, wrapped in swaddling clothes in human fashion, yet hymned in a
God-befitting way by the host of the holy angels; and they were announcing the
good news of his birth to the shepherds.
For God the Father has assigned, as a special honor, the firstfruits of the
proclamations concerning him to those in heaven. And we see him also today
yielding to the laws given through Moses, or rather God the lawgiver, as man,
subject to his own decrees. And for what cause, the most wise Paul teaches,
saying: When we were children, we were enslaved under the elements of the
world. But when the fullness of time came, God sent forth his Son, born of a
woman, born under the law, that he might redeem those under the law. Therefore
Christ bought out from the curse of the law those who were under the law, yet
had not also kept it. And how did he buy them out? By fulfilling it; and also
in another way, that he might loose the charges of the transgression in Adam,
presenting himself on our behalf to God the Father as obedient and tractable
toward anything whatsoever. For it is written that, as through the
disobedience of the one man the many were made sinners, so also through the
obedience of the one the many will be made righteous. He therefore bowed his
neck to the law with us, and this he did by way of economy; for it was fitting
for him to fulfill all righteousness. For since he altogether took the form of
a servant, for this reason, as one numbered among those under the yoke because
of the humanity, he once also paid the didrachma to those who collect tribute,
although by nature he was free and a fellow-contributor, as Son. When
therefore you see him keeping the law, do not be scandalized, nor yet bring
the free one down among household slaves; rather consider the depth of the
economy. When therefore the eighth day had come, on which it is customary for
circumcision in the flesh to be performed, at least according to what seemed
good to the law, he receives the name, that is, Jesus.
And this is interpreted Salvation of a people. For so God the Father willed
that his own Son should be named, who was born according to the flesh of a
woman; then especially there came to be salvation of a people, and not of one
only, but of many, or rather of every nation and of all that is under heaven.
At the same time, therefore, the =77.1044= matter of circumcision was being
performed, and he was receiving the name. And what is the riddle, or of what
mysteries will what is being performed be indicative for us? Come, let us look
again, having searched it out. The blessed Paul said: Circumcision is nothing,
and uncircumcision is nothing. But it is likely that some will say to this:
Did God of all then command, through all-wise Moses, that what is nothing
should be kept, and having hung punishment over those who transgress it?
Yes, I would say. For so far as it concerns the nature of the thing, that is,
what is fulfilled concerning the flesh, it is nothing at all; yet it is in
labor with a type of mystery that is not unseemly, or rather it has the
manifestation of the truth hidden within it. For on the eighth day Christ rose
from the dead, and has given us the circumcision in the Spirit. For God
commanded the holy apostles: Go, make disciples of all the nations, baptizing
them in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. And we
say that the circumcision in the Spirit is performed especially at the time of
holy baptism, when Christ also makes us partakers of the Holy Spirit. And of
this again that ancient Joshua was a type, the commander after Moses. For he
brought the sons of Israel across the Jordan, then sitting down at once he
circumcised them with knives of stone.
Therefore when we cross the Jordan, then Christ circumcises us by the power of
the Holy Spirit, not cleansing flesh, but rather shearing away the defilement
in souls. On the eighth day, therefore, Christ is circumcised, and receives
the name, as I said. For then, then we have been saved through him. And they
bring him up to Jerusalem to present him to the Lord. O great and wondrous
economy! O depth of the wealth and wisdom and knowledge of God! He who is in
the bosom of the Father, the Son who sits with him and is co-eternal, he
through whom all things are offered in a manner befitting God, yet
when he has come to be in the measures of humanity, he also offers a
sacrifice, he who is overlaid with gold by the worships from all and is
glorified together with his own Begetter. And what then has he also brought as
firstborn and male? A pair of turtledoves, or two young pigeons, according to
what was prescribed in the law. But what does the turtledove wish to signify,
and what also the other, that is the dove? Come, come, let us look closely.
The one, then, is the most talkative among the birds of the field, and the
other is gentle and meek; and such the Savior of all has become toward us,
having meekness to the utmost, and after the manner of a turtledove flooding
what is under heaven, and filling his own vineyard with his own fair voice,
that is, us who believe in him; for it is written in the Song of Songs that
The voice of the turtledove was heard in our land; for the divine and
evangelical saving proclamation has spoken to us. Turtledoves, then, and doves
were being offered, when he was presented to the Lord, and one could see at
one and the same time the =77.1045= truth together with the types. Christ
therefore has offered himself as a fragrance of sweet smell, that through
himself and in himself he might bring us near to God the Father, and so loose
the turning-away that had come upon us, both because of the transgression in
Adam and because of the sin that tyrannized over us all. For we were those who
of old were crying: Look upon me, and have mercy on me. And what could there
be of equal weight toward gladness with learning that God has saved what is
under heaven, the Son mediating, because he became as we are? For it is
written that There is one God and one mediator of God and men, the man Christ
Jesus, who gave himself as a ransom for us; for he willingly descended to our
poverty, that he might make us rich, as we gain what is his.
See him therefore being brought to the Father as one of those according to us,
and following the shadows of the law, still also sacrificing according to
custom, even if these things were done through the mother. Was he then unknown
to all in Jerusalem, and known to none of those there? And yet how is this
true? For God the Father was proclaiming beforehand through the prophets from
of old that the Son would in due seasons become manifest, that he might save
the lost and give light to those in darkness. He said then through one of the
prophets: My righteousness draws near quickly, and my mercy to be revealed,
and my salvation shall burn as a lamp. And mercy and righteousness is Christ.
For we have been shown mercy through him and have been justified, having
washed off the filth of wickedness, through faith, that is, that which is in
him. And what a leading lamp is to those in night and darkness, this also
Christ has become to those in mist and intelligible darkness, putting the
divine light into them; for this reason the blessed prophets also were asking
to become partakers of such grace, saying: Show us, Lord, your mercy, and give
us your salvation.
Christ, then, was being brought into the temple, being still a small infant
and at the breast; but the blessed Simeon, honored with prophetic grace,
receives him into his arms; and being filled with the highest gladness, he
blessed God, saying: Now you release your servant, Master, according to your
word, in peace, because my eyes have seen your salvation, which you prepared
before the face of all the peoples: a light for revelation of the nations, and
glory of your people Israel. For the mystery of Christ was prepared beforehand
even before the foundation of the world itself; but it has been manifested in
the last times of the age, and it has become light to those in darkness, and
to those who have gone astray, and to those who have fallen under the devil's
hand. And these were those who were serving the creation rather than the
Creator, and worshipping also the first-evil dragon, and attaching the glory
of God to the unclean multitude of demons; but they have been called by God
the Father to the knowledge of the Son, who is the =77.1048= true light. He
said then through the voice of Isaiah: I will give them a sign, and I will
receive them in, because I will redeem them, and they shall be multiplied,
inasmuch as those who were going astray were many, and they have been called
through Christ. And many again are, as also formerly, those who have been
received in and redeemed, having received a token of peace from God the
Father, the intimacy and grace through faith in Christ.
And the divine disciples were sown among peoples. And what from this? Those
who were far from God have according to disposition become near; to these also
the divine Paul writes, saying: But now you who once were far off have become
near in the blood of Christ. And having become near, they make Christ their
boast. For God the Father said again concerning them: And I will strengthen
them in the Lord their God, and in his name they shall boast, says the Lord.
This also the blessed Psalmist teaches, saying, as speaking toward the Savior
of all: Lord, they shall walk in the light of your face, and in your name they
shall rejoice all the day, and in your righteousness they shall be exalted,
because you are the boast of their strength. And we find the prophet Jeremiah
crying out to God: Lord, my strength and my help, and my refuge in the day of
my evils; to you nations shall come from the end of the earth, and they shall
say: How our fathers acquired false idols, and there is nothing profitable in
them. Christ therefore has become a light for revelation of the nations, but
also for the glory of Israel. For even if some of them have become insolent
and disobedient, and having a mind full of senselessness, yet the remnant has
been saved, and has been glorified through Christ. And the firstfruits of
these have been the divine disciples, whose brightness of good fame flashes
around all that is under heaven; and glory of Israel also in another way,
because he came forth from them according to the flesh, although God over all,
and blessed unto the ages. Amen. And Simeon blesses the holy Virgin, as having
served the divine will, and having ministered to the birth, and as not having
submitted to the laws of humanity. For being a virgin she gave birth, and not
from a man, but when the Holy Spirit came upon her.
What then does the prophet Simeon say concerning Christ? Behold, this one is
set for the fall and rising of many in Israel, and for a sign spoken against.
For Emmanuel has been set by God the Father as a foundation of Zion, being a
chosen stone, a cornerstone, precious. But those who believed on him were not
put to shame; but as many as were unbelieving and ignorant, and were not able
to see the mystery concerning them, having fallen were crushed. For God the
Father said again somewhere: Behold, I lay in Zion a stone of stumbling, and a
rock of offense, and he who believes on him shall not be put to shame; and on
whomsoever it shall fall, =77.1049= it will winnow him. But the prophet was
securing those from Israel, saying: And sanctify him, and he shall be your
light; and if you have trusted in him, he shall be to you for sanctification,
and you shall not meet him as a stone of stumbling, nor as a rock of falling.
Therefore since Israel did not sanctify Emmanuel who is Lord and God, nor yet
was willing to trust in him, they stumble [perhaps: he falls] as upon a stone
through unbelief; he was crushed and has fallen; but many rose, that is, those
who received the faith in him. For they were transplanted from legal worship
into the spiritual, and from having a slave spirit in themselves, they have
become rich in that which sets free, that is, the Holy; they have become
partakers of the divine nature, they have been counted worthy of adoption, and
they are in hopes of obtaining even the city above itself, that is, the
kingdom of the heavens.
And he names the precious cross a sign spoken against, as also the most wise
Paul writes: to Jews a stumbling-block, but to nations foolishness; and again,
The word of the cross is to those who are perishing foolishness, but to us who
are being saved the power of God unto salvation. The sign, then, is spoken
against, seeming to be foolishness among those who are perishing, but to those
who have known its power, salvation and life. Simeon said then to the holy
Virgin, that And a sword shall pass through your own soul also; perhaps
meaning by sword the grief she had over Christ, seeing him crucified whom she
had borne, and not knowing at all that he would also be stronger than death,
and would rise from the dead.
And do not wonder if the Virgin did not know, when we shall find even the holy
apostles themselves of little faith concerning this. And indeed the blessed
Thomas, unless he put his hands into his side after the resurrection and also
felt the places of the nails, would not consent to believe, when his other
disciples were saying that Christ has been raised, and has made himself
manifest to them. The most wise evangelist therefore benefits us, teaching all
the things which the Son, having become flesh, endured for us and on our
behalf, and not thinking it unworthy to take the poverty that is according to
us, that we may glorify him as Redeemer, as Lord, as Savior and God, because
to him and with him to God the Father belongs the glory and the might, with
the Holy Spirit, unto the ages of ages. Amen.
