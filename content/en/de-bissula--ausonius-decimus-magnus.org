:CUSTOM_ID: h1

You prevail at last and you break into the covered things of my muses, which the obscurity of the initiations veiled, although not profane, dearest Paulus, for although I do not think you of that crowd which Horace keeps from the entrance, yet each has his own rites, nor is it the same for Ceres as for Liber, even under the same worshipers, little poems which I had played upon my foster-daughter, rude and unformed for the solace of a domestic song, when without fear they were hidden and enjoyed a secret security, you have forced to bring forth to the light things growing dark. You have of course desired a spoil of my shame, or to have it shown by an unwilling man how much right you had in me. Have you not gone beyond the stubbornness of Alexander of Macedon, who, when he could not loose the straps of the fateful yoke, cut them and penetrated the cave of the Pythia on a day it was not lawful to be open. Use therefore as your own, with equal right, but with unequal confidence: for yours can not fear the people; I blush at mine even within myself, farewell.

** PREFACE
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h2-h1
:END:

As you wished, Paulus, you have all the verses of Bissula,
which we played for the favor of a little Suebian girl,
fostering leisure more than studying glory,
you a troublesome demander read troublesome songs,
for you, what you have kneaded must be eaten: so the old word commands,
that the smith himself may wear the fetters which he himself made.

** TO THE READER OF THIS LITTLE BOOK
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h3-h1
:END:

About to read a thin little book of uncombed song,
put down the eyebrow,
weigh serious poems with contracted wrinkles:
we follow Thymele.
Bissula will be sung in this sketch, not Erasinus:
I warn, drink beforehand,
I write nothing for the fasting; if anyone after cups
will have read mine, this one will be wise.
but this one will be more wise, if he will sleep and think these
dreams sent to himself.

** WHERE BISSULA WAS BORN AND HOW SHE CAME INTO THE HANDS OF HER MASTER
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h4-h1
:END:

Bissula, brought forth by stock and hearth beyond the chilly Rhine,
Bissula knowing of the nascent Danube,
captured by a hand, but sent by a hand she lords in the
delights of him whose warlike prey she was.
lacking a mother, needing a nurse, who does not know a mistress's
command, who herself rules the house of the master,

who has felt no reproaches of fortune and fatherland,
immediately free in an untried slavery,
so changed by Latin goods that a German
face would remain, blue as to eyes, yellow as to hair.
now the tongue, now the form makes the girl ambiguous:
this proclaims her born of the Rhine, this of Latium.

** ON THE SAME BISSULA
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h5-h1
:END:

Delight, blandishments, play, love, pleasure,
a barbarian, but a foster-child who would conquer Latin dolls,
Bissula, a rustic little name of a tender girl,
a little bristly to those not used, but charming to the master.

* Section 5

** TO THE PAINTER ON THE IMAGE OF BISSULA
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h6-h1
:END:

Bissula imitable neither by waxes nor by any dye
does not lend a natural grace to a feigned art.
sandyx and white lead, simulate other girls:
the hand does not know this temper of the face, therefore come, painter,
confound purple roses and mix lilies,
and whatever color of air will be from those, let that itself be of the face.
