Homily 17.
On Barlaam the Martyr.
Formerly the deaths of the saints were adorned with laments and tears. Joseph
wept bitterly over Jacob when he had died; the Jews too mourned Moses' end not
a little; and they have honored Samuel with many tears. But now we leap at the
endings of the holy. For after the Cross the nature of grievous things has
been changed. No longer do we escort the deaths of the saints with dirges, but
we dance upon their tombs with godly choirs. For death is sleep to the
righteous. Or rather, a departure toward a better life. Hence martyrs leap as
they are slain. For the longing for the more blessed life makes the pain of
slaughter dead. The martyr does not look at the dangers but at the crowns; he
does not shudder at the blows but counts the prizes; he does not see the
executioners scourging below, but he pictures the angels acclaiming from
above; he does not mark what is passing in the dangers, but what is eternal in
the rewards. Even among us they already reap a bright pledge, applauded with
the godly praises of all, and from their tombs drawing countless crowds as
with a net.
This is what has been done today for the noble Barlaam. For the war-trumpet of
the martyr has sounded, and it has gathered the soldiers of piety, as you see.
The athlete of Christ who lies here has been proclaimed, and he has set the
Church's assembly on wing. And as the Master of the faithful said: "He who
believes in me, even if he dies, shall live." The noble Barlaam has died, and
he holds festivals; he has been spent in the tomb, and he calls us to a feast.
Now is the time for us to cry out: "Where is the wise? Where the scribe? Where
the disputant of this age?" A rustic is today our unconquered teacher of
piety, =31.485= whom a tyrant dragged as easy prey, but after the trial knew
as a soldier who could not be taken; whom he mocked as speaking in broken
words, but shuddered at as daring with an angel's youth. For his manner did
not go barbarous with the instrument of his tongue, nor was his thought seen
limping with his syllables; but he was a second Paul, speaking with Paul:
"Even if unskilled in speech, yet not in knowledge." The scourging
executioners grew numb, but the martyr was found more at his prime; the hands
of those who scraped were unstrung, but the mind of the one being scraped was
not bent. The scourges loosed the joinings of the sinews, but the tension of
the faith was drawn tighter still. Sides dug open were spent, but the
philosophy of his spirit was in flower. The greater part of his flesh had been
deadened, but he was in his prime as if he had not yet begun the contests. For
when the love of piety has first taken the soul, every form of wars is
laughable to it, and all who scrape it down for the sake of the beloved
delight it rather than strike it. The apostles' longing bears me witness, once
making the scourges from the Jews a delight to them. For they went, it says,
rejoicing from the presence of the council, because they had been counted
worthy to be dishonored for his name. Such also is the soldier hymned among us
today: counting the punishments as gladness, thinking he was being struck with
some roses by the scourges; eluding the outrages of impiety as arrows;
reckoning the judge's wrath a shadow of smoke; laughing at ranks of guards
grown savage; dancing at the dangers as at crowns; gladdened by the blows as
by honors; leaping at the fiercer penalties as at brighter prizes; spitting at
swords when they were bared; receiving the executioners' hands as softer than
wax; embracing the penal wood as saving; taking pleasure in prison locks as in
meadows; delighting in devices of torture as in the variety of flowers;
possessing a right hand more taut than fire. This was the last engine brought
against him by those who warred on him.
For having kindled an altar for the libation of the demons, they bring the
martyr and set him beside it, and having commanded him to hold his right hand
upturned over the altar =31.488=, they used the hand as a bronze altar,
wickedly setting burning frankincense upon it. For they hoped that the hand,
thrown in the wrestling by the force of the fire, would of necessity quickly
let the frankincense fall upon the altar. Alas for the intricate trickery of
the impious! Since, they said, we have not bent his resolve with countless
wounds, let us at least bend in flame the contentious wrestler's hand. Since
we have not shaken the soul with varied engines, let us at least bring the
right hand to the fire and make it waver.
But the wretches did not even have the profit of this hope. For the flame ate
through the hand; yet the hand remained, bearing the flame as ash. It did not
give its back to the warring fire as runaways do, but stood unmoved, winning
glory against the flame, and granting the martyr to speak the Prophet's words:
"Blessed is the Lord my God, who teaches my hands for array and my fingers for
war." For fire was grappling with a hand, and the marks of defeat were found
on the fire. A bout was joined between flame and a martyr's right hand, but
the victory of the wrestling was some new thing of the right hand: the flame
falling out through the midst of the hand, the hand still stretched for the
bout. O hand more contentious than fire! O hand that never learned to be bent
by fire! O fire taught to be defeated by a hand! Iron yields to the tyranny of
fire, being softened; bronze gives way to the fire's dominion; even the
stubbornness of stones knows how to be conquered by it. But the tension of
fire that forces all things did not, though it burned, bend a martyr's
stretched right hand. The martyr might well cry out on this to the Master:
"You have held my right hand, and in your counsel you have guided me, and with
glory you have taken me." What shall I call you, O noble soldier of Christ?
Shall I call you a statue? I have much lessened you in endurance. For fire
softens a statue that receives it; but it did not even persuade your right
hand to be seen moving. If I name you iron, I find even such an image falling
short of your manhood. For you alone persuaded flame not to force a hand; you
alone acquired a right hand as an altar; you alone with a burning right hand
struck the faces of the demons, and then with a hand turned to charcoal you
melted their heads together; and now, treading with this hand turned to ash,
you blind their armies. But why do I lessen the =31.489= champion with
childish lispings? Let us then yield the hymn to him to more magnificent
tongues; let us call the louder trumpets of the teachers upon him. Rise now
for me, O splendid painters of athletic achievements; magnify with your arts
the truncated image of the commander. The crowned one drawn more dimly by me,
make him shine round with the colors of your wisdom. Let me go away conquered
by your painting of the martyr's feats; I rejoice today at being defeated in
such a victory of your strength. Let me see the wrestling of the hand toward
the fire drawn more exactly by you; let me see the wrestler painted more
brightly on your image. Let demons weep, struck even now by the martyr's feats
among you. Let the hand be shown to them again, burning and winning. Let
Christ too, the president of the bouts, be written on the panel; to whom be
the glory unto the ages of ages. Amen.
