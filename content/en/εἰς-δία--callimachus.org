#+BEGIN_QUOTE
To Zeus edited by Ulrich von Wilamowitz-Moellendorff
#+END_QUOTE

At the libations of Zeus, what else would it be better to sing than the god
himself, always great, always lord, driver-away of the Pelagonians, judge for
the sons of Heaven? How then shall we sing him, as Dictaean or as Lycaean? The
heart is much in doubt, since the birth is disputed. Zeus, some say you were
born in the Idaean mountains, Zeus, others in Arcadia. Which of them, Father,
lied? /Cretans are always liars/; for the Cretans even fashioned a tomb for
you, O lord; but you did not die, for you are forever. But Rhea bore you in
Parrhasia, where the mountain was most thickly screened with bushes (whence
the place is holy, and neither any creeping thing that has need of Eileithyia
nor any woman comes near it, but the Apidaneans call it the ancient childbed
of Rhea); there, when your mother had laid you down from her great bosom, she
at once sought a stream of water with which she might wash away the stains of
birth and bathe your body. But Ladon was not yet flowing great, nor
Erymanthus, whitest of rivers, and all Arcadia was still waterless; yet it was
destined to be called very well-watered thereafter. For at that time, when
Rhea loosed her girdle, in truth the wet Iaon lifted many oaks above it, and
Melas carried many wagons, and many reptiles made their lairs above the Carion
though it was wet, and a man went on foot over Crathis and stony Metope
thirsty; and the much water lay beneath his feet. And lady Rhea, held by
helplessness, said: "Dear Earth, you too give birth; and your birth-pangs are
light." She spoke, and the goddess stretching her great arm high struck the
mountain with her staff; and it split wide apart for her, and poured forth a
great stream. There she washed your body, lord, and swaddled you, and gave you
to Neda to carry into a Cretan hiding-place, that you might be reared in
secret, the eldest of the Nymphs who then midwifed her, the first generation
after Styx and Philyra. Nor did the goddess pay empty thanks, but she named
that stream Neda. It somewhere, in great part, along the city of the Caucones,
which is called Lepreum, mingles with Nereus, and the grandsons of the
Lycaonian bear drink its most ancient water. When the Nymph, carrying you
toward Cnossos, left Thenae, Father Zeus (and Thenae was near Cnossos), then,
O daemon, your navel fell away; whence the Cydonians afterwards call that
ground Omphalian. Zeus, the companions of the Corybantes took you in their
arms, the Dictaean Meliae, and Adrasteia laid you to sleep in a golden cradle,
and you sucked the rich udder of the goat Amaltheia, and besides you ate the
sweet honeycomb. For suddenly there came to be the works of the Panacrian bee
in the Idaean mountains, which they call Panacra. And the Curetes danced the
war-dance densely around you, beating their armor, so that Cronus might hear
with his ears the clang of the shield and not you as a child.

Fairly you grew, and fairly you were reared, heavenly Zeus, and swiftly you
came to manhood, and quickly your down came. But still being a child you
devised all things complete; therefore even your brothers, though born
earlier, did not grudge you to have heaven as your allotted house. The old
singers were not altogether true; they said that the sons of Cronus divided
the dwellings by lot in three parts. Who would draw lots for Olympus and for
Hades, unless he were quite a fool? For it is fitting to cast lots on equal
terms; but these are as far apart as can be. May I lie in things that would
persuade the ear of one who hears. Lots did not make you king of the gods, but
the works of your hands, and your might and your strength, which you set even
near your chariot. And you made the far-preeminent messenger of birds of your
signs; may you show them favorable to my friends. And you chose what is best
of men in their prime: not those skilled in ships, not the shield-wielding
man, not the singer; but those things you left there for the lesser blessed
ones, for others to care for other things, and you chose the city-rulers
themselves, under whose hand is the land-worker, the one skilled in the spear,
the rower, all things. What is not under the strength of the one who rules?
For instance we call bronzesmiths of Hephaestus, warriors of Ares, hunters of
Chitone Artemis, and those who know well the ways of the lyre of Phoebus;
/kings are from Zeus/; on earth nothing is more divine than lords. Therefore
you also chose them as your own portion. You gave them cities to guard, and
you yourself sat on the heights in the cities, watching those who with crooked
judgments guide the people and those who again set them straight; and you cast
among them flowing wealth, and prosperity enough; to all, but not quite
equally. And it is fitting to judge by our ruler; for he has gone far and wide
beyond. In the evening that one completes what he may have thought of in the
morning; in the evening the greatest things, and the lesser as soon as he
thinks of them. But others accomplish some things in a year, some not in one,
and of some you yourself cut off the fulfillment utterly, and broke their
desire. Hail greatly, son of Cronus, most high, giver of good things, giver of
safety. But who could sing your deeds? There has not been, there will not be;
who will sing even the deeds of Zeus? Hail, Father, hail again; and give both
excellence and wealth. Neither does prosperity know how to increase men
without excellence, nor excellence without wealth; give both excellence and
prosperity.
