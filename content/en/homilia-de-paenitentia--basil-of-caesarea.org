HOMILY ON REPENTANCE.
The most honorable brother, not bearing the arrogance of those who dare to do
away with repentance, laid a second charge upon us, seeing this proud thing,
and stretching out a temperate right hand to the brethren toward repentance.
And the word is most useful to men. For none of men is without sin. For it is
witnessed of one that he did no sin. We shall speak then concerning repentance
from both Old and New. For these are the treasures of the Church. And we
commend repentance, not urging on a brother's sin (for we do not wish the one
who has sinned to go on into sins in hope of repentance), but hastening to
raise up the one who has fallen. For the despair introduced by the adversaries
urges the one who has fallen once to be rolled up together with the sins; but
the expectation of repentance urges the one who has fallen to rise and to sin
no longer. And who are we, legislating for God? God wishes to forgive; and who
is the one who hinders? Because we say not what those dare, but what God
commands. "Does not he who falls rise again?" They speak against God. He who
falls does not rise. He did not persuade you with a bare word; he will
persuade you through a clear example. For what is so unwashed as scarlet? and
what among us is white as snow, or as clean wool? He who fashioned these
things says that If your sins become as the unwashed colors, if you should
wash, it changes over into the purity of snow. But this forgiveness is not
after baptism. For he said, he says, "Wash yourselves, become clean." And this
especially, if you had asked it of us, we would have been able to show from
Scripture that after baptism there is pardon. For one must not pick out the
easy things and contradict, but stand up against the very things that seem
strong in the opposing argument. For so long as the opposing things are not
taken away from the soul, the one who is sick is incurable.
But since the salty things have no room to be sweetened, and we too now speak
from the Old: let us say what they object against the things said from the
Old. For they say that before the Passion of the Savior, before the holy blood
was poured out for us, if you read to us from the Old concerning repentance,
we say that in the Old it has been granted; but in the New, because of the
Passion, there will be no pardon. But if we show from the New that even after
baptism and participation of the Spirit someone who has sinned is called back
through repentance to the gift, it is clear that there is repentance =31.1477=
for those who have fallen even after baptism; and the things in the Old will
be valid on behalf of repentance. And I speak as to those who attend; hence I
also take hold of heavier things, that, having become of good hope, we may
not, having fallen once, remain in the fall. You were not persuaded by him
when he set it forth through an example; be persuaded by the oath of God. God
says: "I live, says the Lord." And this is an oath befitting God. For since he
had none greater than himself, and he swears because of the unbelief of those
who do away with repentance, "I live, says the Lord," [and let no one
frighten,] that "I do not wish the death of the sinner, but that he should
turn and live." The one who lives wishes to make live. Is God not even
trustworthy when he swears? You are persuaded neither by word, nor by example,
nor by oath? Be persuaded by deeds. Nineveh was not overthrown; for it
repented. The indignation of the prophet, that it was not overthrown; a gourd
sprang up. He makes a defense to you, that you may remember that he is good.
And I fear lest you hear from him: "Is your eye evil, because I am good?" Who
is recorded more impious than Ahab? I speak as to those who read. Besides the
other plunderings he seized a vineyard, having murdered the master; he went
down to inherit; he went down rejoicing. The one who grieves met him, the
prophet who convicts; not as an enemy, but as one sparing he declared not his
own sentence, but that of God.
After the sentence he put on sackcloth, and he puts off the mastery. For it is
written that the prophet himself, the one sent upon the sentence, heard from
God that Since Ahab made petition, fearing the threat, the things I said shall
not be in his days. I am compelled to desire a good medicine. David the holy,
and holy even after the sin, of the ten commandments in one matter
transgressed three. Since we speak to you, let us say also more clearly. There
was adultery, and murder, and desire of another's wife. The righteous man
sinned, and God made him judge of his own sin. For since we do not see a beam
in our own eye, but a speck in another's, he brings in his own case to him as
another's, and says that "There were two men in one city, the one rich, the
other poor. The rich man had many; but the poor man had one ewe lamb, which
also slept in his bosom (and this is a mystery of a wife), ate with him, and
drank from his cup. A stranger, he says, came to the rich man;" and well that
"stranger;" for David did not have custom in these things. "A stranger
therefore came to him. Having left, he says, to sacrifice for the stranger
from the things he had, the one ewe lamb, he says, of the poor man he seized
and sacrifices." However far the strong man may fall, he has a swift rising.
David is indignant; for David was David even having fallen. "The Lord lives,
the one who did this is worthy of death." He declared against himself. When he
had declared, the prophet made plain who the man is. Straightway he has come
upon the medicine; he saw the wound; he fled to the physician. "I have
sinned," he says, and straightway the healing. "And the Lord has put away your
sin =31.1480=." For what do you wish, that one sin be reckoned to the holy, or
much righteousness? He did a sin, but he also did many righteousnesses. What
is just with a just God, to remember the few, or to forget the many? Let there
be a scale, and with what we measure, it shall be measured to us. If the sins
are more, let him be a sinner; but if the righteous deeds are more, let him be
righteous. One and one. For all things among us are named from the greater
part. And there is a whiter than the white, and another sweeter than the sweet
for comparison. So also we are righteous as men, not as sin is righteousness.
Hence the Son of God is called righteousness; but we are righteous by
participation of righteousness. And Moses spoke something with the tongue, but
he was not taken in the history. And Abraham is found faithful, and there is a
place where he disbelieved. I do not accuse the righteous, but I glorify God.
I do not urge that we should sin. Enoch pleased God, says the Scripture, after
begetting the son. If after these things he pleased, it is possible for one
not being pleasing to please. But let us come to the New, still having more
from the Old. For even Manasseh, who set four-faced idols in the temple of
God, that from wherever one should enter he might worship the idols, having
repented was called back from the captivity. Let us come to the New. For from
that from which they most wish to shut out repentance, from it most of all
repentance is proclaimed. Let us ask the Savior, Why did you come? and he
answers: "I did not come to save the righteous, but sinners," he says, "to
repentance."
Let us examine him, What do you bear upon your shoulders? A lost sheep, he
says. Over whom does joy come to be in the heavens? He says, Over a certain
sinner repenting. Angels rejoice, and do you begrudge? God receives with joy,
and do you hinder? Let us flee more vehemently to a good God. Two, he says,
there were, both sons (you cannot tell me that the repentance here is that
from the Greeks, but both sons, having divided equally). The younger spends
what he had; being righteous, he falls away. And having spent all, he came to
repentance, and he hastens toward the father, and he rehearses, as he is about
to meet the father, what he might say. "I have sinned," he said, "against you,
and before heaven." These things he planned; they did not escape the good
father. He came to make defense; the one who is ashamed met him. Only will it,
and he himself =31.1481= comes to meet beforehand. He begins to speak, and the
father takes him up. Consider the bowels of compassion, and do not cut off the
hope. He received him, and says: Sacrifice a calf, and bring out a robe, and
this an ancient one, and a ring on the hand. All these things are
misinterpreted, but they show in short the good urging of the one who
received, not toward the things I wish, but toward the things he says. And if
you are indignant that he receives after the feeding of swine, after the
spending of all, the brother also was indignant, but nevertheless the one who
is ashamed received him. And a fig tree being cultivated, and in three years
not giving fruit, was left, and was not cut out, in hope of giving. The master
grants freely, and will you speak against? For is someone seizing things of
his own for himself? And if God grants freely, who is the one who contradicts?
Since let us be taught to pray: "Forgive us our debts, because we also have
forgiven." You forgive, God does not forgive? He made you physician of
yourself. With the measure you measure back, it shall be measured back to you.
You forgive, he forgives you. But you the wicked one are kind, the good one
not much more? Jesus with sinners, the physician with the sick.
Pharisees are indignant, the fathers of those now indignant; the merciful one
does not turn away. He entered into a Pharisee's house having been called; a
sinful woman entered in upon him not having been called. She saw the sin, she
came to the righteousness; she saw the disease, she went away where the
physician was. She came not to the face, for she did not have boldness; she
did not take hold of his hand, for she did not dare; but upon the feet, which
she washed with the tears, she put forth the hair then only well, the hair
that had often appeared badly she then well laid bare. There has been sketched
for you the fleeing to the feet of Jesus. He went up to the Father, but he
says: "I am with you." Whenever you will, flee taking courage, wet feet, he is
near. And he came being present, and he went away being present. These things
from the New are kindred to those in the Old. What does John proclaim? A
baptism of repentance. And he teaches in parables, not once, not twice, that
being taught many times you may learn. Peter denies three times, and he lies
in the foundation. Paul from persecutors a herald. But here there is a
question. But what does he himself say? "Jesus Christ came into the world to
save sinners, of whom I am first." He makes plain his own defect, that he may
show the greatness of the grace. But Peter having spoken and been blessed,
having said that "You are the Son of God" the Most High, and having heard that
"You are a rock," was praised. For even if a rock, not as Christ is a rock, as
Peter is a rock; for Christ is truly an unshaken rock; but Peter through
=31.1484= the rock. For Jesus grants freely his own dignities, not being
emptied, but having the things he gives. He is light. "You are the light of
the world." He is a priest; he makes priests. He is a sheep. "Behold I send
you as sheep in the midst of wolves." He is a rock; he makes a rock, and he
grants freely his own things to the servants. For this is a sign of a rich
man, to have and to grant freely. But the rich among us, when they grant
freely, lessen the things they have. For they are not rich, for we possess
things that are another's. But he who is rich in this way, however many things
he may grant freely, remains so much being rich; as an ever-flowing spring.
However much you may take, the stream is not lessened. And not as a spring;
for nothing of the things among us is a worthy example of that nature. But not
having something worthy, we use the lesser things, that somehow we may know
the unknown things. Three times Peter denies, not that Peter may fall, but
that you too may have consolation. He wept; the tears bent our merciful one.
For the eagerness was good. Everywhere he alone confesses. "The spirit indeed
is eager; but the flesh is weak." He who fashioned the fashioning knows, and
he bears the weaknesses, forgiving the trespasses. "Remember, he says, Lord,
that we are dust." For what is our righteousness? Abraham was truly great. He
was great in this way, because he is earth and ashes. For he who knows how
much is between God and men, knows himself. Hear also other beatitudes.
"Blessed are they whose lawlessnesses have been forgiven, and whose sins have
been covered." God blesses, and do you hinder? Is it that the Scriptures speak
as we wish, or as it has been written ought we to follow? And these things are
against us.
Contention is not good, cutting off hope. But since we promise pardon after
baptism (for this is the most cohesive thing toward the malice), the Apostle
writes in the former Letter to the Corinthians that "Fornication is wholly
named among you, and such fornication as is not even named among the nations."
You heard the greatness of the evil, hear also the greatness of the sentence.
"When you are gathered, he says, and my spirit in the power of Christ, to
deliver such a one to Satan for destruction of the flesh, that the spirit may
be saved." He disciplines, for he does not cast off as an enemy. In the former
he has delivered to Satan; but in the second he writes nothing contrary to the
former. "To confirm love toward him, lest by the excess of grief such a one
perish." The Apostle loosed even the unlooseable things. Then he says to
deliver such a one to Satan; after these things writing concerning the same
and forgiving, he says: "That we may not be taken advantage of by Satan." For
if he takes one of us, he has also =31.1485= taken advantage of us. A sheep
goes out from a flock, it becomes food of wolves. Bring it in, that it may
flee the plot; just as the good shepherds, even if the sheep is taken, at
least hurry to take the skin. If it is a good thing to snatch away the skin of
a dead one, is it not worthy to take a soul from a wolf's mouth? And in war a
champion is wounded, and not because he was wounded, for this is he not
crowned; but he received many wounds having accomplished much. Does God reckon
the one as evil, and not reckon the many good things? How many martyrs, having
first denied, the second time called back the defeat? But having denied not in
the mind, but with the tongue. For the flesh is weak. Denying he falls away,
confessing again he is called back. For God does not keep the evils and set
aside the goods. And someone being tortured often yielded, not having borne,
not the eagerness, but the pain. But having been brought down, and having
received relief, he is called back. Shall the small word be kept for him, and
the tortures not be numbered? But God is judge, not the daring of men. Do you
dare to legislate for God, who knows the weakness, and supplies the help, and
will give the victory? How many did not from a good life become martyrs? Then
because desire turned, shall they be deprived of the labors? We are not
angels, but men; and we fall and we are raised, and often in the same hour.
"And star differs from star in glory." Why? For if there is some measure of
righteousness, all ought to have the equal; but if some shine as the sun, and
some gleam as the moon, and some shine according to the difference of the
stars, it is clear that another excels; and the one who is lessened, it is
clear that he has small sins.
And if the forgiveness is of one who has sinned nothing, the difference was in
the variety. But since it is necessary to show those who have fallen being
saved after baptism, I speak fearing, lest you stand away contrary to my wish;
for I very much wish those who receive baptism not to sin. But if some should
ever fall, not wishing it, I do not wish what those who do away with
repentance wish, not wishing us to die together with the cross; but what the
Apostle, both to be buried with him, and no longer to live to sin, this I do
wish. "And I do not set aside the grace of God," the Apostle writing to the
Galatians says that they received Spirit. Do you seek something clearer than
this? But he adds: "Are you so foolish, having begun in Spirit, are you now
being completed in flesh?" Therefore they have received Spirit. And again:
"This only I wish to learn from you: did you receive the Spirit from works of
law, or from hearing of faith? You were running well." See how many
advantages. Hear the sins. "Now you are being completed in flesh, you have
fallen away from Christ." You saw them having the Spirit; you heard that "You
have been abolished =31.1488= from Christ;" and likewise also, "You have
fallen away from the grace." What after these things does he add, giving place
of renewal? "My little children, whom I again travail." He had begotten once;
but the one who has begotten once does not refuse also to beget a second time
unto salvation. If someone will work malice in all the other things, this
itself is godless. But men, having received the Spirit, running well, having
endured so many things, having been in danger, having endured in vain, having
fallen away and been abolished, are born again, that they may again take on
the form of God. Does a contradiction concerning repentance still remain for
you? Who fashioned us? God. He made a body for us, he made a soul for us. Does
he overlook? Who has given us a medicine of life? Why readings? That we may
stand away from sins. Why are we watered? That we may bear fruit. What do we
pray? That our sins may be forgiven. Let us fulfill the word. For whom is
there not repentance? But the one who works malice in hope of repentance has
the manner of malice, and has been deprived of repentance. There is hope even
after the sin, there is healing even after the wound, but the scar remains.
Blessed is not even to hesitate; a second beatitude is the healing after the
blow. And to God the fashioner from the beginning, and who comes together with
the weakness, and who keeps the choice, and who grants freely repentance,
through the one who brings the good things to us, Jesus Christ our Lord, be
the glory and the power unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
