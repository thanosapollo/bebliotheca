Of Origen, an exhortation to martyrdom.

1 "Those weaned from milk, those withdrawn from the breast, expect affliction
upon affliction, expect hope upon hope, yet a little yet a little, through
contempt of lips, through another tongue." And you therefore according to
Isaiah, as no longer fleshly nor "babes in Christ," having advanced "in" your
intelligible "age," most God-fearing Ambrose and most pious Protoctetus, and
no longer "having need of milk" but of "solid food," hear as "weaned from
milk" and "withdrawn from the breast" in what way as to athletes who have been
weaned not a simple affliction but some "affliction upon affliction" is
prophesied. And he who does not thrust away the affliction "upon affliction,"
but as a noble athlete receives it, immediately receives also "hope upon
hope," of which after not much of the affliction "upon affliction" he will
enjoy; for such is the "yet a little yet a little." 2 But even if they should
hold us cheap and make little of us, calling us either impious or fools, those
who are foreign to the dialect according to the holy scriptures, let us be
reminded that the hope "upon hope," which "yet a little" will be rendered to
us, will be rendered "through contempt of lips, through another tongue." And
who would not receive "affliction upon affliction," in order that he may
immediately receive also "hope upon hope," having reckoned with Paul "that the
sufferings of the present time are not worthy," with which we as it were buy
the blessedness, "in comparison with the glory about to be revealed unto us"
from God; and especially since "the momentary lightness of our affliction,"
"light" both being and being called so for those not weighed down by the
things standing round, by how much more exceeding it becomes, by so much
greater and more abundant "an eternal weight of glory it works for us," if at
the time of the things that afflict and as it were wish to press our souls we
turn our ruling faculty away from the painful things and look not at the
present painful things but at the things stored up through endurance in these
for those who have contended "lawfully" in Christ by the grace of God, who
multiplies the benefactions and beyond the worth of the labors of the one who
contended grants so many things as it would be fitting for God, who does not
give in a niggardly way, the munificent one and the one who knowledgeably
magnifies his own graces, to give to those who as far as power goes through
having despised the earthen vessel have displayed the love toward him with the
whole soul. 3 And I think God is loved with the whole soul by those who tear
it away and set it apart through much eagerness to have communion with God not
only from the earthly body but also from every body; for whom not even with
any distraction and dragging about does the putting off of "the body of
humiliation" come to be, whenever a season gives through the supposed death to
strip off the body "of death" and to be heard having prayed apostolically and
said: "Wretched man that I am; who will deliver me from the body of this
death?" For who of those groaning "in the tent" because of being burdened by
the corruptible body will not also give thanks, having first said: "Who will
deliver me from the body of this death?" seeing that having been delivered
through the conversation from "the body of death" he will holily utter the:
"Thanks be to God through Christ Jesus our Lord." But if to anyone such a
thing appears hard, he has not "thirsted" "toward God" the strong "the living"
nor has he longed toward God "in the manner that the deer longs upon the
springs of the waters" nor has he said: "When then shall I come and appear to
the face of God?" nor has he reckoned in himself the things which the prophet,
having reasoned them, when it is said:


"Where is your God?" to him, "each day" poured out upon himself "his soul,"
rebuking it still becoming very sorrowful according to weakness and being
troubled, and saying: "that I will pass through in a place of a wonderful tent
as far as the house of God, in a voice of exultation and confession of a sound
of one keeping festival." 4 I was praying then that you, throughout the whole
present contest remembering the much reward stored up in the heavens for those
persecuted and reproached "for the sake of righteousness" and for the sake of
the son of man, would rejoice and exult and leap, just as the apostles once
rejoiced having been counted worthy "to be dishonored for his name." And if
ever you should perceive a shrinking about your soul, let the mind of Christ
in us say to it, wishing as far as lies in it to confound even him: "Why are
you very sorrowful, soul? And why do you trouble me? Hope upon God, that I
will confess to him;" and again. Would that the soul might in no way at all be
troubled, but even at the very judgment seats and at the bare swords being
brought against the neck might be guarded by the peace of God that surpasses
"every mind" and might be calm, reckoning that those who go abroad "from the
body" are at home with the Lord himself of all; but if we are not so great as
always to keep the untroubled state, at least let the trouble of the soul not
be poured out nor appear to those outside, in order that we may have room for
a defense toward God, saying to him: "My God, toward myself my soul was
troubled." And the word exhorts us to remember also that which has been said
in Isaiah in this manner: "Do not fear the reproach of men, and by their
contempt do not be defeated." For it is also absurd, when God is clearly
present at the movement of heaven and of the things in it and at the things on
earth and sea accomplished by his divine art in the generations and
constitutions and nourishments and growths of all kinds of animals and plants,
to shut the eyes and not look at God, but to turn the eyes toward fear of
those who after a little will have died and will be handed over to the
judgment according to their desert. 5 To Abraham then once it was said by God:
"Go out from your land," but to us perhaps after a little it will be said: Go
out from the whole earth; which it is good to obey, in order that he may
quickly show us the heavens, in which is the so-called kingdom of the heavens.
Concerning many virtues then it is possible to see the life filled with
contests and those who contend. For others also besides those from the portion
of God will seem to have contended concerning self-control, and some to have
died with courage keeping the purpose for the common master, and those clever
about the words being examined to have taken care of prudence, and those who
have proposed to live justly to have given themselves to justice. And there
campaigns against each virtue either "the mind of the flesh" <or> also the
many of the things outside; but concerning piety alone "the chosen race, the
kingly priesthood, the holy nation, the people for a possession" contends; the
remaining men not even as far as seeming showing that, if there are things
that contend against the pious, they propose to die for piety, preferring
death with piety to living with impiety. And each of those who wish to be
heard as being from the chosen race is persuaded by God saying at every
season, even when those who on the pretext of being polytheists are atheists
plot against him, the: "There shall not be for you other gods besides me" and
the: "The name of other gods you shall not remember in your hearts, nor shall
it be named from your mouth." Wherefore "with the heart" God "is believed unto
righteousness" by such, "and with the mouth is confessed" by such "unto
salvation," as they grasp that they will neither be justified if they do not
so believe God with their heart so disposed, nor will they be in salvation if
the word is not also such as the disposition is. For they deceive themselves
who think it sufficient toward attaining an end in Christ that "for with the
heart it is believed unto righteousness," even if there is not added the: "but
with the mouth it is confessed unto salvation." And it is possible to say that
it is more


to honor "with the lips" while having the heart "far" from God, than to honor
him with the "heart," the mouth not confessing "unto salvation." 6 And if
indeed the one who says: "You shall not make for yourself an idol nor a
likeness of anything" and the things following, as of things differing from
one another, the: "You shall not worship them" and the: "nor shall you serve
them," says these things, perhaps the one disposed toward the idols serves
them; but the one not so disposed but through cowardice, which he names
accommodation, hypocritically pretending them in order to seem to be pious
similarly to the many does not serve but worships the idols. And I would say
that those who at a judgment seat disown Christianity, or before a judgment
seat, do not serve but worship the idols, taking the name of the Lord God, the
"God," upon vain and lifeless matter. And in this way the people that was
profaned "unto the daughters of Moab" were worshipping and not serving idols.
For it has been written in these very words that "they called them to the
sacrifices of their idols, and the people ate of their sacrifices, and they
worshipped their idols and were initiated to Beelphegor." And observe that it
has not been said: and they served their idols, for it was not even possible
after so many signs and wonders in one turn of a season to be persuaded by the
women with whom they were fornicating toward thinking the idols gods. And
perhaps thus also according to the calf-making in Exodus "they worshipped,"
not serving the calf, which they beheld coming into being. The standing
temptation then must be thought by us to have become a proof and examination
of the love toward the divine. "For the Lord your God tests you," as it has
been written in Deuteronomy, "to know if you love the Lord your God from your
whole heart and from your whole soul." But you, being tested, "will go behind
the Lord your God and will fear him and will keep his commandments," and
especially the: "There shall not be for you other gods besides me," "and you
will hear his voice and will be added to him," as he takes you from the places
here and adds you to himself unto "the," as the apostle named it, "increase of
God" in him. 7 But if indeed "every" wicked "word" is "an abomination to the
Lord" your God, how great an "abomination" must the wicked word of denial be
thought to be, and the wicked speech of proclamation of another god, and the
wicked oath according to the fortune of men, a thing without subsistence? When
this is put forward to us we must remember the one who taught the: "But I say
to you not to swear at all." For if indeed the one who swears by heaven
transgresses the throne of God, and the one who swears by earth acts impiously
by deifying the so-called "footstool of the feet of God," and the one who
swears unto Jerusalem sins, although it is a city of a great king, and the one
who swears by his own head goes wrong; how great a sin must swearing by
someone's fortune be thought to be? And then we must remember also the:
concerning every idle word you will give "an account in the day of judgment."
For what other word is so idle as the oath in denial? But it is likely that he
will wish to outwit us through whatever the enemy can toward worshipping "the
sun or the moon or all the world of the things from heaven." But we will say
that the word of God "did not command" these things. For the creatures are in
no way to be worshipped when the one who created is present and sufficient and
arriving at the prayer of all. And not even the sun himself would wish to be
worshipped by those from the portion of God, and likely not even by anyone
else; but imitating the one who said: "Why do you call me good? No one is good
except one, God the Father," he would as it were say to the one wishing to
worship him: Why do you call me God? There is one true God; and why do you
worship me? "For the Lord your God you shall worship and him alone you shall
serve." I too am generated; why do you wish to worship the one who worships?
For I too worship and serve God and the Father and, being persuaded by his
commands, "have been subjected to vanity because of the one who subjected in
hope" and I will be freed "from the slavery of corruption," I myself now


bound in a corruptible body "unto the freedom of the glory of the children of
God." 8 It is possible to expect also some prophet of impiety, perhaps not one
but more, who will speak to us as a "word" of the Lord, "which the Lord did
not command," or as a word of wisdom that which is foreign to wisdom, in order
that he may kill us by the word of his mouth. But we even then, when the
sinner stands opposite us, let us say: "But I as one deaf was not hearing and
as one speechless not opening his mouth; and I became as a man not hearing."
For the deafness toward the impious words is good, whenever we despair of the
correction of those who say the worse things. 9 And it is useful for us to
think in the seasons of those who call us toward the contrary things, what
God, wishing to teach, says the: "I am the Lord your God, jealous." And I
think that in the way a bridegroom turning the bride toward living solemnly,
as she gathers her whole self toward the bridegroom and in every way guards
herself from being poured out toward anyone else besides the bridegroom, even
if he is wise, would display jealousy, using such a display as a drug toward
the bride; thus the one who oracles, and especially if he appears "to be the
firstborn of all creation," says to the bride soul that God is jealous,
standing those who hear off from all fornication toward the demons and the
ones thought to be gods; and as God so jealous he says concerning those who
have in any way fornicated behind other gods: "They made me jealous over a
no-god, they provoked me in their idols; and I will make them jealous over a
no-nation, over a senseless nation I will provoke them; because a fire has
been kindled from my wrath, it will burn as far as lowest Hades." 10 And even
if not for himself as wise and impassible the bridegroom turns the betrothed
away from every defilement, yet at least for her sake, seeing her defilement
and the abomination, he will do all the things able to heal and turn her, as
applying to a self-determining nature words that turn away from fornicating.
What defilement then would you think comes to be worse for a soul, proclaiming
in any way another god and not confessing the one who is truly one and only
Lord? And I think that, just as "the one joined to the prostitute is one
body," so the one who confesses someone, and especially in a season of torture
and examination of the faith, is mixed and united to the one confessed; but
the one who denies, by the denial itself in the manner of a sword cutting
through, suffers the division from that one whom he denies, being separated
from that one whom he denied. Wherefore attend, lest perhaps as a thing
following and necessary to happen to the one who confesses, the being
confessed, and to the one who denies, the being denied, it has been said:
"Whoever confesses in me before men, I too will confess in him before my
Father who is in the heavens." And the Word-itself and the Truth-itself would
say both to the one who has confessed and to the one who has denied: "With
what measure you measure it will be measured back to you." You then who have
measured with the measure of confession toward me and have filled the measure
of the confession will receive a measure of confession from me "shaken pressed
overflowing," which will be given "into your bosom"; and you also who have
measured with the measure of denial and have denied me will receive
analogously to the measure of the denial a measure of denial from me
concerning you. 11 And how a measure of confession is filled or is not filled
but falls short, we would thus contemplate. If throughout the whole time of
the examination and of the temptation we would not give place to the devil in
our hearts, who wishes to defile us with wicked reasonings of denial or of
double-mindedness or of some persuasiveness calling toward the things hostile
to martyrdom and to perfection, and in addition to this if we would not even
defile ourselves with a word foreign to the confession and if we would bear
all the reproach from the opponents and mockery and laughter and ill-speaking
and pity, with which they seem to pity us, taking us to be led astray and
fools and calling us fallen, and further if we would not be dragged about
being distracted even by the affection concerning the children or their mother
or some of those


thought to be dearest in life, an affection toward possession or toward this
living, but having turned away from all these things we would become wholly of
God and of the life with him and beside him as about to have communion with
his only-begotten and with his partners, then we would say that we have filled
the measure of the confession; but if we should fall short even in one thing,
we have not filled but have defiled the measure of the confession and have
mixed something foreign into it. Wherefore we will need the things which those
need who have built upon the foundation "wood" or "hay" or "stubble." 12 And
this too we must know, that we received the so-called covenants of God upon
contracts which we made toward him, undertaking to live according to
Christianity. And in our contracts toward God was the whole constitution
according to the gospel, saying: "If anyone wishes to come behind me, let him
deny himself and take up his cross, and let him follow me. For whoever wishes
to save his soul will lose it; but whoever loses his soul for my sake, this
one will save it." And often we have become more inspired hearing also the:
"For what will a man be benefited, if he gains the whole world, but is damaged
in his soul? Or what will a man give as an exchange of his soul? For the son
of man is about to come in the glory of his Father with his angels, and then
he will render to each according to his deed." And that one must deny himself
and take up his own cross and follow Jesus not only Matthew, whose wording we
have set beside, recorded but also Luke and Mark. For hear Luke saying: "And
he said to all: If anyone wishes to come behind me, let him deny himself and
take up his cross and follow me. For whoever wishes to save his soul will lose
it; but whoever loses his soul for my sake, this one will save it. For what is
a man benefited having gained the whole world, but having lost or been damaged
himself?" And of Mark: "And having called the crowd with his disciples he said
to them: If anyone wishes to follow behind me, let him deny himself and take
up his cross and follow me. For whoever wishes to save his own soul will lose
it; but whoever loses it for the sake of the gospel will save it. And what
will it benefit the man, if he gains the whole world and is damaged in his
soul? For what will a man give as an exchange of his soul?" Long ago then we
ought to deny ourselves and say: "I live no longer I;" and now too let it
appear, if having taken up our cross we have followed Jesus; which has come to
be, if "Christ lives" in us. If we wish to save our soul, in order that we may
receive it back better than soul, let us also lose it by martyrdom. For if we
lose it for the sake of Christ, casting it upon him in the death through him,
we will procure for it the true salvation; but if the contrary, we will hear
that nothing is benefited the one who has gained the whole sensible world
through his own destruction or through having been damaged himself. And once
someone has lost his own soul or been damaged in it, even if he gains the
whole world, he will not be able to give it "as an exchange of the" perishing
"soul." For that which has been fashioned "according to the image of God" is
more honorable than all bodies. One alone has been able to give an exchange of
our soul that was formerly perishing, the one who bought us with his own
"precious blood." 13 And according to certain deeper words Isaiah says: "I
gave Egypt as your exchange and Ethiopia and Syene in your place; from which
you have become honorable before me," but the accurate word concerning this
and the remaining things, if being lovers of learning in Christ and wishing
already at some time to go beyond learning "through a mirror" and "in a
riddle" you hasten toward the one who called, as soon as ever you will know
"face to face" as friends of the Father in the heavens and of the teacher. For
the friends learn in form and not through riddles or with a wisdom bare of
voices and words and symbols and types, applying themselves to the nature of
the intelligibles and to the beauty of the truth. If


you believe that Paul was caught up "into a third heaven" and "was caught up
into the paradise and heard unspeakable words, which it is not lawful for a
man to speak," you will consequently know that of the unspeakable words then
revealed to Paul, after which he has come down from the third heaven, you will
immediately know more and greater things, after the knowledge not about to
come down, if taking up the cross you follow behind Jesus, whom we have as "a
great high priest who has passed through the heavens." And you yourselves, not
standing off from those behind him, will pass through the heavens, going
beyond not only earth and the mysteries about earth but also heavens and the
things about them. For there are stored away in God much greater spectacles
than these, which no nature of those in a body, unless it has first been
released from every body, is able to contain. For I am persuaded that than the
things which sun and moon and the choir of the stars saw, but also of holy
angels, whom God made "spirit" and "a flame of fire," God stores up and keeps
with himself much greater things, in order that he may manifest them, whenever
all "the creation" is freed "from the slavery of the enemy unto the freedom of
the glory of the children of God." 14 And so someone going beyond, having
something more than many martyrs, the love of learning in Christ, will more
quickly go up. And to you, holy Ambrose, looking with examination at an
evangelical voice it is possible to see how then perhaps either no one of
those ever or very few will attain a pouring of an exceptional and more
abundant blessing; in which sort you too will be, if you should swim through
the contest without hesitation. And the words are thus: Peter once said to the
savior: "Behold we have left all things and have followed you; what then will
there be for us? And Jesus said to them" (clearly to the apostles): "Amen I
say to you that you who have followed me in the regeneration, whenever God
sits upon a throne of his glory, you yourselves also will sit upon twelve
thrones judging the twelve tribes of Israel. And everyone who has left
brothers or sisters or parents or children or fields or houses for the sake of
my name will receive many times more and will inherit eternal life." And for
the sake of these sayings I would have prayed, having acquired so many things
upon earth as you have, or even more than these, to become a martyr in Christ
God, in order that I may receive "many times more" or, as Mark says, "a
hundredfold;" <which> are much more than a few, of the sort we will leave
behind, if we are called upon martyrdom, being multiplied a hundredfold. And
for the sake of this if I bear witness, I was wishing also to leave children
behind with fields and houses, in order that also beside God and Father of our
Lord Jesus Christ, "from whom every family in heavens and upon earth is
named," I may be styled father of children many times more and holier, or,
that I may speak definitely, a hundredfold. And if there are fathers,
concerning whom it has been said to Abraham: "And you will go away to your
fathers with peace, nourished in a good old age," someone would say (I do not
know if speaking truly): perhaps those are fathers who once bore witness and
left children, in place of whom they have become fathers of fathers of the
patriarch Abraham and of other such patriarchs; for it is likely that those
who have left children and have borne witness become fathers not of infants
but of fathers. 15 But if someone as it were zealous for the greater gifts and
blessing rich martyrs and fathers who are martyrs who will beget "a
hundredfold" children and will receive hundredfold fields and houses inquires,
if reasonably those beside the poor martyrs in life will have the possession
in intelligibles multiplied, it must be said to him that, just as those who
have endured tortures and toils have displayed a more splendid virtue in the
martyrdom than those not examined in these, so those who in addition to loving
the body and loving life have also cut through and broken the so many worldly
bonds, having used a great love toward God and having truly taken up the
living word of God and active and sharper "than every two-edged sword," have
been able, having cut through the so many bonds and having made wings for
themselves as


an eagle to return "into the house of the one set over" themselves. It is
worthy then just as for those not examined in tortures and toils to yield
first places toward those who in joint-racks and various instruments of
twisting and fire have displayed the endurance, so also for us the poor, even
if we bear witness, the word suggests to stand aside from first places to you
who through the love in Christ toward God trample the deceitful glory that
reaches the many and the so great possession and the affection toward
children. 16 And at the same time also observe the solemnity of the scripture
promising a multiplying and a hundredfold of brothers and children and parents
and fields and houses; but a wife has not been numbered together with these.
For it has not been said: everyone who has left brothers or sisters or parents
or children or fields or houses or a wife for the sake of my name will receive
many times more; "for in the resurrection of the dead they neither marry nor
are given in marriage, but they are as the angels who are in the heavens." 17
What then Jesus said to the people setting them upon the holy land, these
things the scripture would say also to us now. And the wording is thus: "Now
fear the Lord and serve him in uprightness and in righteousness." And it will
say to us being persuaded toward idolatry also the things following that are
thus: "Put away the foreign gods, whom your fathers served in the beyond of
the river and in Egypt, and serve the Lord." At the beginnings then when you
were about to be catechized it would reasonably have been said to you: "And if
it does not please you to serve the Lord, choose for yourselves today whom you
will serve, whether the gods of your fathers of those in the beyond of the
river or the gods of the Amorites, in whom you dwell upon the land." And the
catechist would have said to you: "But I and my house will serve the Lord,
because he is holy." But now these things have no room to be said to you; for
then you said: "May it not be for us to leave the Lord, so as to serve other
gods. The Lord our God himself is God, who led us and our fathers up from
Egypt, and guarded us in all the way in which we went in it." But also in the
contracts concerning piety long ago you answered to those who were catechizing
you this: "And we will serve the Lord; for this one is our God." If then the
one who has transgressed the contracts toward men is some unsparing one and
foreign to salvation, what must be said concerning those who through denying
set at naught the contracts which they placed toward God and run back upon
Satan, whom being baptized they renounced? For toward such a thing must be
said the words spoken by Eli to the sons: "If a man sinning sins against a
man, they will also pray concerning him; but if he sins against the Lord, who
will pray concerning him?" 18 A great theater is assembled over you contending
and being called upon martyrdom; as if we were saying that such a thing comes
to be over certain supposed paradoxical athletes †contending together upon the
spectacle of the contest of countless numbers. And you will say not less than
Paul, whenever you contend: "We have become a theater to the world and to
angels and to men." The whole world then and all the angels right and left and
all men, both those from the portion of God and those from the remaining, will
hear us contending the contest concerning Christianity. And either the angels
in the heavens will be gladdened over us, and "rivers will clap a hand
together, and the mountains will exult," "and all the trees of the plain will
clap with the branches;" or, which may it not be, also below powers that
rejoice at evils will be gladdened. And it is not at all absurd to see from
the things written to Isaiah the things that will be said by those in Hades to
those who have been defeated and have fallen from the heavenly martyrdom, in
order that we may still shudder at the impiety in denying. For I think such
things will be said toward the one who has denied: "Hades below has been
embittered meeting you; all the giants who ruled the earth have been raised up
with you, who raised from their thrones all kings of nations. All will answer
and will say to you." And what will the defeated powers say


to the defeated and those captured by the devil to those captured in denial,
what then are these? "And you too were captured just as we also, and among us
you were reckoned." And if someone even happening upon a great and glorious
hope in God is defeated in God by cowardice or by toils being applied, he will
hear: "Your glory has gone down into Hades, your much gladness; they will
strew decay under you, and your covering is a worm." And if someone also shone
in the churches often as "the morning star" appearing to them, his good works
having shone "before men," and after these things contending the great contest
has lost the crown of such a throne, he will hear: "How has the morning star
that rises early fallen from heaven? He was crushed upon the earth." And this
too will be said toward him who has been made like the devil through the
denial: "You will be thrown in the mountains as a dead man abominated with
many who have died, pierced through with swords, going down into Hades. In the
manner a garment stained in blood will not be clean, so neither will you be
clean." For how will he be clean who has been defiled with blood and murder by
the abominable stumble of denial and mixed with so great an evil? Let us now
show that we have heard the: "The one who loves son or daughter above me is
not worthy of me," let us take heed, lest perhaps even a hesitation concerning
whether at some time one must deny or confess come to be in us; in order that
the word of Elijah may not be said also toward us, being thus: "Until when
will you limp upon both hams? If the Lord is God, go behind him." 19 And it is
likely that we will also be reproached by neighbors and sneered at by some who
encircle us and move the head at us as upon senseless ones. But when these
things happen let us say toward God: "You have set us as a reproach to our
neighbors, a sneering and a laughter to those around us. You have set us for a
parable among the nations, a moving of head among the peoples. The whole day

good and beneficial hatred. And just as for the sake of the soul being guarded
