On fasting; on the fifth Sunday of the fasts.
=62.731=
The time has come, brethren, for us through fasting to reap the fruits of
repentance. For toward the gathering of fruits summer and autumn are
suitable, but toward sowing winter and spring; but toward fasting and
prayer every time is suitable. For the bridegroom shall be taken from
them, and then they shall fast. If then, besides the remaining days, this
work has escaped us, yet behold now is an acceptable time for us to work
the field. And I call fasting a field, since it has room for us all toward
the labor and the sowing; it does not thrust away a woman, nor yet an old
man, nor a younger man, nor little children; it opens the doors to all,
and receives all, that it may save all, as it did the Ninevites.
Good is the seed of fasting, because it both uproots our sins as thorns,
and raises up and makes righteousness blossom as a flower; good is the
labor of fasting, because it lightens the soul from the weight of sins,
and grants the light burden of the commandments of Christ. For take my
yoke upon you; for my yoke is easy, and my burden is light. The yoke is
the faith laid upon all; the burden, the doings of the commandments, borne
on the shoulders of the soul in works, made manifest in the heavens.
Through fasting the lawgiver Moses became glorious; remaining forty days
and nights without food, he saw God speaking to his face, a cloud
overshadowing, and through it God speaking to him the things that were
profitable, a pillar of fire shining by night as a guide of the camp of
Israel, the gates of heaven being opened, angelic bread coming down upon
earth, man partaking of the manna of God, a rock being split by a rod and
bringing forth waters, a sea bringing forth a strange food, the quail, and
feeding the people. Many other signs also Moses saw because he attended to
fasting.
Fasting is the overthrow of death. The Savior said: This kind of demons
comes out by nothing except by prayer and fasting. And nothing is worse
than the demons; the demon is a mountain hard to move; the demon is a
wound more grievous than all evils, a laughingstock in the marketplaces,
humiliation in houses, confusion in churches, a devouring of flesh,
weakness of the soul, daily pain, an obstacle of the mind, one hindrance
of works. Great is the spite of the demon. But fasting and prayer prevail
over all these.
David, praying and singing psalms, drove the demon from Saul the king; For
the Spirit of God departed from Saul, and he was choked by an evil spirit
of a demon; and as David sang, the king was refreshed. And that king David
fasted, hear him writing in the Psalms: My knees have grown weak from
fasting, and my flesh has been changed because of oil. If Adam had fasted
from the tree, he would not have lost para =62.732= dise; if Eve had not
eaten, deceived by the serpent, she would not have brought death upon the
world; if Esau had inclined toward fasting, he would not have lost the
birthright, through one eating he lost glory, and became a slave of his
brother. Do you wish to see the strength of fasting? run to the Ninevites
who used it for a common achievement. For when God wished because of their
sins to overthrow Nineveh, he sends Jonah the prophet, and says to them:
Yet three days, and Nineveh shall be overthrown. And the love for mankind
of God trumpets what he is about to do, and to the debtors of this he
foretells death, not that they may flee from the city, but that they may
repent in the city, that they may be aroused and become more earnest
toward the word. He did not say: Fast, and I save you; he did not make
clear: Repent, and you shall be shown mercy; but he hid the love for
mankind, and proclaimed the severity, lest anyone should say: He compelled
them to fast or to repent, and to worship God. And what is from necessity
is not well-accepted; For not from grief, nor from necessity; for God
loves a cheerful giver. Yet three days, and Nineveh shall be overthrown.
Short is the threat, an unjust appointed time at the heels.
What have you come to do? from what bosoms to leap? shall I set a strong
army against him who speaks? who is the one answering back to God? the
speaker is without falsehood. But if it is God who threatens, they say
among themselves: What flight from the evil shall we find? in what war
shall we draw up? who will become champion of our souls? who will prevail
upon God to cease the wrath against all? what intercessor shall we send to
God to reconcile him to peace? We all happen to be sinners; being enemies
of God we are not heard. What shall we do? where shall we flee from the
face of the Lord? Come, let us run to fasting, O men of Nineveh, says the
king, and let us strew ashes under ourselves, and let the sheep and the
oxen not graze, nor drink; and let us see if God will have mercy on us.
These things the Ninevites reasoned, and having made fasting as a most
sharp arrow, and having prepared prayer as a sweet-smelling incense,
bringing down tears of repentance as rivers of waters, and each turning
back from their evil way, they prevailed upon God to reconcile them to
peace. The three days of the fast went up into the heavens, drew near to
the Master, received from him salvation. He granted life to all, to men
and to cattle. Repentance shone forth, and fasting. See that you do not
despise the creature, but come, keep watch, and make toward him a fruit of
piety. Let us therefore pray all with one accord, that we too may be
delivered from the wrath that is upon us, and may obtain the kingdom of
the heavens, by the grace and love for mankind of our Lord Jesus Christ,
to whom be the glory and the power, unto the ages of ages. Amen.
