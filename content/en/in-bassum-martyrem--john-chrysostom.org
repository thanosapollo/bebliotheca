* On Bassus the Martyr
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

On the commemoration of Saint Bassus. It was delivered concerning the terrors,
and on the text, Learn from me, that I am meek and lowly in heart.
=50.719=
1. We ought indeed, when so great a fountain of words is customarily poured
out upon you, and now that through our priests and teachers who have gone
before a rich spiritual table has been set before you, to hold back the meager
droplets of our own words, and especially as we are held by the long-standing
weakness of the body, and by the feebleness of the voice, and moreover by
successive cares, Christ-loving ones; but since you also bid us to speak,
yourselves held by an insatiable longing for the divine hearing, come, by the
grace of the Savior, let us set before your kindness, in brief, what is
fitting and suited to the season. For this, I think, is appropriate both for
us who speak and for you who hear, as we furnish ourselves by reasoning with
what is better and profitable.
For the holy and much-hymned martyr and bishop Bassus, who today gathers this
festival for us, has his prizes from his own contests, in no way needing the
contribution of our encomia, while he himself customarily makes intercessions
for us to the Master, as one who has also acquired boldness toward Him through
the wrestling-ground of martyrdom, and has received from Him the crown of
incorruption, which Christ has also prepared beforehand for the faithful.
For abundant, =50.720= according to the divine Apostle, are the gifts of the
Master, who of old has also dispensed the bounties of the martyrs to those who
call upon Him genuinely, and especially now, when the yearly remembrance of
the terrors awaits us shortly, and the humane wrath of that fearful threat
calls us to doxology. For we truly beheld wrath flashing with loving-kindness,
when we were girded about with the fear of earthquakes, when we knew the
creation shaking, when the ground was violently shaken, yet the Savior did not
withdraw His own compassions, when we expected the bitter end, when we
suddenly thought our houses tombs, when, held by the shaking, we found neither
place nor manner of refuge, when, having reached the middle of the day, we did
not hope to see the evening, when the sword hung from above, and from below
well-doing with prayer took courage, when the peoples cried out with one
voice, Have mercy. And the Master was bent by compassions; for moving it with
His glance, with His hand He braced the trembling creation.
Why do I not say it briefly? when, if the Lord of Sabaoth had not helped us,
our soul had almost sojourned in Hades. For whom does the greatness of the
Savior's loving-kindness not astound? =50.721= Whom do the things that
happened then not move to thoughts of thanksgiving, and not only those then,
but also those after them, and those that met us a little while ago? He shook
the supports of the earth, He made the foundations of the houses totter, the
roofs were tossed like merchant-ships in the waves of the sea; He showed only
a judicial eye, and we all were shaken as if standing in waters. Great was the
fear, and the things of loving-kindness were many times greater than the fear.
For shaking the creation He did not overthrow it, convulsing the whole He did
not raze it to the ground, He did not strip the earth of the beauty of its
creatures; He shook the roofs only for our reminder, and He did not even bring
us a taste of destruction. So great is the sea of His compassion toward us.
And the shaking of the earth's bases too was as a guardian, as one who loves
compassion. For He saw those who set Him at naught and provoke Him, He saw
those who love plunder, He saw those joining house to house, and bringing
field near to field, that we might take something from our neighbor; He saw
those not showing mercy to orphans, and not attending to the judgment of
widows; He saw teachers doing the opposite of what they say; He saw disciples
insulting the good order of the Church with theatrical disorders, He saw those
living in wickedness and envy, He saw those joining deceit to envy, He saw
storms of hypocrisy sinking the simpler like boats, He saw those murdering by
purpose, He saw those doing injustice as far as we are able, He saw love
shipwrecked, but deceit sailing fair, in the sea of life, He saw those leaping
away from the truth and running to the lie, and to speak it in a word, He saw
those serving mammon rather than the Lord, and He shook the earth for us as a
tutor for children, and He showed the bowels of an affectionate mother, who
often, wishing to make her nursing child, crying in a disorderly way, depart
from the habit, shakes that little cot violently, not that she may strike, but
that she may frighten the infant; so also the Master of all shakes the
inhabited world which He bears in His hand, not that He may overthrow it, but
that He may turn those who are disorderly toward salvation.
2. And let no one blame us, because we likened the Lord to an affectionate
mother, when the Lord Himself likened Himself to a bird in the Gospels,
saying: Jerusalem, Jerusalem, who kills the prophets and stones those sent to
her, how often I wished to gather your children, as a hen gathers her
nestlings, and you would not? The Lover of mankind therefore moved the
soulless earth, that He might stay the ensouled from disorderly motion; He
threatened death of the flesh, that we might guard against the destruction of
the soul. Did you see, Christ-loving one, how great is the compassion of the
Creator toward us? Did you see a threat flashing with loving-kindness? Did you
see compassions anticipating indignation? Did you see a sentence overcome by
goodness? And nothing strange; He Himself is even now our meek and loving
Master, who customarily cares for our salvation, who cries out plainly in the
Gospels, as was just now read to us: Come, learn from me, that I am meek and
lowly in heart.
O how great is the condescension of the Fashioner =50.722=, and the one
fashioned is not ashamed! Come, learn from me: when did a Master come to
exhort fallen servants? Such is Christ to us: He shows compassions, when He
ought to punish the sinner, when He ought to wipe out the race of those who
have provoked Him, He sends forth gentle words to those liable: Come, learn
from me, that I am meek and lowly in heart. God is lowly, and man is high; the
judge is gentle, and the condemned is proud; the Craftsman makes a poor voice,
and the clay speaks as a king. Come, learn from me, that I am meek and lowly
in heart. The former things did not persuade, the things after these and those
a little while ago did not put us to shame, how both then and now, having
moved the creation, He again established it, having shown mercy; Come then,
learn from me, that I am meek and lowly in heart. He has not a scourge that
strikes, but one of a healing nature. Come, see unspeakable loving-kindness.
Who does not love a master who does not strike? Who does not wonder at a judge
who requests of the condemned? The lowliness of the words surely astounds you:
I am Creator, and I love my work; I am Fashioner, and I spare the one I
fashioned. If I use my dignities, I do not rescue fallen humanity, and when it
is sick incurably, if I do not plaster it with gentle medicines, it is not
strengthened; if I do not cheer it, it dies, if I only threaten, it perishes.
For this reason I will go around it as it lies, with plasters of goodness; I
stoop much, sharing the suffering, that I may raise it when it has fallen. The
one who stands, if he does not bring his hand down together, does not raise
from the fall the one who has been thrown.
Come, learn from me, that I am meek and lowly in heart. I do not make a
display of words; I have given you a trial of deeds. That I am meek and lowly
in heart, consider from what I became. Look at my form, and think of my
dignity, and worship my condescension for your sake; reckon from where I have
come, where I converse with you; having heaven as a throne, I stand on earth
speaking to you; I am glorified above, and as long-suffering I am not angry,
Because I am meek and lowly in heart. If I, the Son of the King, were not
meek, I would not have chosen a slave as mother; if I, the Creator of unseen
and seen substance, were not meek, I would not have sojourned among you; if I
were not meek, I, the Father of the ages, would not have endured
swaddling-clothes; if I were not lowly, I who possess the wealth of all
creation would not have accepted the poverty of a manger; if I were not meek,
I who am not even seen by the Cherubim would not have dwelt with irrational
creatures; if I were not meek, I who spit light upon the blind would not have
been spat upon by lawless lips; if I were not meek, I who free slaves would
not have endured the slavish slap; if I were not meek, I would not have given
my back =50.723= to scourges for captives. And why do I not say the greater?
If I were not meek, when others owed the suffering, I who owed nothing would
not have paid the debt of death for them. And I paid it that I might also
dissolve the penalty of condemnation for those held down in Hades; for I am
not only King of the living, but also Master of the dead. For this reason I
came each of the two roads of the economy. I appeared as man, I also became
dead for a little, that I might distribute to all, even those under the earth,
the gift of my incorruption.
Come, learn from me, that I am meek and lowly in heart. The lowliness is of
will, not of nature; I have an unapproachable essence, but a mind that walks
toward all. Come, learn from me, that I am meek, and lowly in heart, not in
dignity; I am moderate in will, not in strength; through strength I am fearful
to angels, through will I am lowly to men; Come, learn from me, that I am
meek, and lowly in heart. I do not speak from what I am, but I converse from
the things by which I show mercy. I love kindness more than authority. I who
say these things to you am King; there is greatness of power with me, but I do
not astound your weakness with the power present to me. I do not say, Come,
learn from me that I am Master, the Lord of creation, who looks upon the
earth, and makes it tremble, who measured heaven with a span, and holds the
earth in a handful; but, Come, learn from me, that I am meek, and lowly in
heart. So meek am I: you sinned, and I was scourged; so willingly am I lowly:
I, the Master, came to free those in bondage, and those who serve slapped me,
the Liberator, and crucified me together, and besides this, interceding to my
Father for them, I said: Father, forgive them; for they do not know what they
do. Come then, learn from me, that I am meek and lowly in heart; come, I
entreat, begging, I am not ashamed to entreat, I choose to exhort my own
servants, that I may not be compelled to punish; come, learn from me the
meekness before you see my fearfulness; come now, I heal, after a little I
take account; now I spare, after a little I give sentence; now I am a lover of
mankind, after a little I shall appear as the just Judge. Come, learn, that I
am meek and lowly in heart. Either honor the meekness, or revere the power.
Come, anticipate my face in confession; for the things of such moderation from
me have been appointed for a season.
3. The present life alone is a display of my long-suffering; a time will come
when the door of this my kindness will be shut; a time will come when a
dripping tear of sinners will not help; a time will come when trumpets
sounding from every side declare my second coming, when angels running through
the whole earth present the myriads of the dead to judgment, when a tribunal
is set for me, and I mount among the powers above, when principalities and
authorities escort me as I come, when the luminaries of my kingdom light up
the whole world, when books of what each has lived are opened, when there is
exactness of laws, and an exact ledger of demons, when the one judged =50.724=
stands, having only his works standing by him, when reasonings accuse, and
conscience convicts, when wicked spirits attend to the words of the Judge,
when the furnace of the Judge awaits the sentence, when the word, Have mercy,
is no longer of use to the one who cries it. Come then, before I shut my doors
of compassions, before the festival of life comes to be dissolved, before the
theater of life passes; for the appointed time of the end is at the doors.
Come before I begin to judge; for if I have sat to judge, I no longer grant
favor. For these reasons I made evident the example of the foolish virgins,
whose lamps of life, not having oil of righteousness, were quenched, and how
the doors of the bridechamber were shut against them, and how to those
knocking I answered from within, I do not know you, hinting at the voice
toward sinners in the judgment.
If therefore, brethren, we have been taught from the Savior's words the
Master's goodness, let us not despise a Judge speaking loving things before
the judgment, let us not lose the time of repentance; let us clothe our own
souls with good deeds and almsgiving; let each of us already traffic in the
things suited to eternal life, let us withdraw ourselves from grievous deeds.
For if we keep the faith unshaken in good works, the creation also will be
unshaken with us. Let us adorn our souls with temperance, and let us firmly
acquire the priceless pearl of the pure faith; before the time of our life is
quenched, before the fashion of the world, and the flower of glory, and all
the delights of the whole are corrupted, let us serve the unbribable Judge.
For He Himself is the one who says: As I live, says the Lord, I do not will
the death of the sinner, as that he should turn and live. If He desired to
punish the sinner, He would be silent; He wishes to have mercy, therefore He
exhorts; He spares, therefore He urges; He frightens you by telling
beforehand, that He may forestall the experience of the danger. For God, when
threatening, wishes to save, but when silent He chooses to punish. We have the
lesson from the facts. He threatened against the Ninevites, and He spared; He
was silent against the Sodomites, and He punished; He has prepared crowns, if
we do not bring tortures upon ourselves. He wishes Gehenna to be idle, He
wishes to shut the prison of darkness, He wishes all His wrath to be kept for
the devil alone, He wishes to sit as Judge, not that He may punish some, but
that He may crown all.
Having then such a Master, let us seize a voice full of goodness, let us obey
Him who says: Come, learn from me, that I am meek and lowly in heart, that we
may also be counted worthy to hear that longed-for and blessed voice which
says: Come, you blessed of my Father, inherit the kingdom prepared for you; of
which may we all come into enjoy =50.725= ment, by the grace and
loving-kindness of our Lord Jesus Christ, with whom to the Father be glory
=50.726= together with the Holy Spirit unto the ages of ages, Amen.
