* On the Image of God and According to Likeness

OF THE SAME GREGORY OF NYSSA CONCERNING WHAT IT IS TO BE ACCORDING TO THE
IMAGE OF GOD AND ACCORDING TO LIKENESS

Those who wish to contemplate without error the God-created beauty of their
own face cannot otherwise behold as in a mirror their own image and the
character of the face except through some most pure mirror, in which, drawing
near, they see clearly within it and through it a certain counterpart
character according to the image and according to the likeness of themselves.
Therefore we too, having gazed as in a certain mirror toward the divine ray of
the noetic sun, let us from there clearly learn the shadowing and the form and
the image of that which is according to the image and according to the
likeness of our nature. For there is, there is, as it seems to me at least, a
certain fearful and hard-to-interpret constitution of man, imaging in itself
many and hidden mysteries of God. And just as the nature of the eye readily
contemplates the things outside itself, but cannot contemplate itself, of what
sort it is: so to every eye of human thought the reason of our fashioning has
been established as hard to behold and hard to grasp.

For when the Creator had completed the simple and most noetic cosmos of the
invisible powers, and after it also this material and visible one, composed of
four elements, then he says, Let us make man according to our image and
according to likeness, and he makes a living being, as it were a mixed cosmos
akin to the two cosmoi, composed of an incorporeal and immortal and
incorruptible soul, and of a material and visible four-elemented body. Which
having come to be, Scripture says again, that And God made man, according to
the image of God he made him; and it calls God both the Father and the Son and
the Holy Spirit. Many and diverse opinions, then, have been uttered about this
by the interpreters. For some said that the ruling and authoritative character
of man is that according to the image of God and likeness; others the noetic
and invisible of the soul. Others the incorruptible and sinless, when Adam
came to be =44.1329=; others have called it a prophecy concerning baptism.

Last of all, as it were to the untimely birth, it was given also to me to say
some things about this; and first of all it is worth seeking this: why ever
did God not rather name the noetic and immaterial and heavenly and God-near
angels according to his image and according to likeness? For they too have the
ruling and authoritative over all the earth, and over man himself, or rather
beyond man. Likewise also the incorruptible, and the immaterial, and the
invisible, and the pure, and all other praises you might speak concerning
Adam, are superabundantly in the noetic and heavenly choirs of the
incorporeals. Therefore something deeper than the things set before us is
intimated by that according to the image of man. For man does not possess one
image and likeness of God, but also a second, and a third, imaging as in a
certain mirror and typical sketch, not natural, the mystery of the
trihypostatic Godhead; and not only this, but also clearly pre-writing the
incarnation of the one of the holy Trinity, God the Word. And perhaps that
according to the image is the bare soul of the Godhead, while that according
to likeness of the Word's enfleshment is the composite of our soul and body.

Rather let us run back to the very beginning of the account, seeking from
there as from some deepest spring first this: why ever did God not, after the
likeness of the remaining rational beings, that is the angels, or again
according to equality with the ensouled animals, make similar in manner our
ancestral and chief hypostases, I mean of Adam and of Eve, and of the son who
came forth from them? But Adam he established without cause and unbegotten,
and his second man the son as begotten; and Eve neither begottenly, nor again
without cause, but by taking, that is processively, having come forth from the
substance of the uncaused Adam, being given substance* ineffably. And is it
not the case that these three heads of the first-born of all humanity,
consubstantial hypostases, have come to be, as it seems to Methodius,
typically according to a certain image of the holy and consubstantial Trinity?
Adam the uncaused and unbegotten having the type and image of the uncaused and
cause of all, almighty God and Father; his begotten son pre-writing the image
of the begotten Son and Word of God; and the processive Eve signifying the
processive hypostasis of the Holy Spirit?

Wherefore God did not breathe into her a breath of life, because she is a type
of the breath and life of the Holy Spirit, and because she was going to
receive through the Holy Spirit God who truly is the breath and life of all.
Whence one may see and marvel that the unbegotten Adam had no other unbegotten
or uncaused like him among men, just as neither did the processive Eve, as
being true types of the unbegotten Father and of the Holy Spirit; but the
begotten son had all men, being begotten sons, as like brothers =44.1332=; as
being according to the image and typical likeness of Christ the begotten Son,
who became man firstborn without seed among many brothers. And if not so, nor
according to this according to the image, why ever did not four, or two, or
more hypostases of the forefathers come to be, having their hypostatic
properties altered? I mean the unbegotten and the begotten and the processive,
but three and only three? Therefore you have according to the image and
according to typical likeness a triad in monad in three hypostases; it remains
for you next to learn also a monad in triad. And how you will be able to
discern this well, hear one of the wise exhorting you and saying: If you wish
to know God, first know yourself; from your own composition, from your own
constitution, he says, from the things within yourself. Enter, slipping under
into yourself, look through as in a certain mirror in the soul of yourself,
distinguish its constitution, and you will see yourself being according to the
image and likeness of God. Nameless and unknown is your noetic and immortal
essence of the soul, according to a typical image of the nameless and
unknowable and immortal God. For neither the essence of God, nor of a rational
soul, has anyone of the men who have come to be from the ages known. The soul
is life-giving and constitutive and providential of the four-elemented nature
of the body, according to the image of God the provider of all four-ended
creation and of the creation above.

Wherefore neither have we known the place of God where he dwells, but we only
believe that he is everywhere, nor indeed do we know the place of the soul
where it dwells in the body; but we only know that it is in the whole body and
is at work. Your soul has also something else according to the image of God, I
mean that it is of another essence than all created nature. And the most
paradoxical of all of that according to the image in us is this, that neither
the reasons of God's existence, nor indeed the essence of our soul, how it has
been naturally brought into existence and arrives, can human mind grasp.
Whence those who have dogmatized badly about grasping this have thus stumbled:
some who say that the souls are sent down from heaven into bodies; others say
they come into existence together with the body, being created by God; others
say that man, as being according to the image of the Creator, is generative
also of the soul together with the seed; others say the soul is born from both
parts, of the male and of the female, as when some stone and iron striking
together generate fire by the kindling. And some say that at the very
instantaneous moment of the conception of the body the soul also is
established; others on the fortieth =44.1333= day after conception, bringing a
certain, as they think, legal confirmation about this; and some imagined it
consubstantial with the angels, some below, some in the air, some as a certain
divine essence pervading the whole. Wherefore also, being united to a body and
being as according to the image of God, and giving life to this, it remains
impassible of the bodily passions and corruptions of the body, neither seen,
nor interpreted, nor grasped by nature, nor by form, nor by shape, nor by
figure, nor by quality, nor by quantity, nor by existence, nor by
constitution, nor by beauty. Whence also Methodius says in the Symposium that
the soul has an impossible and indescribable beauty, and for this reason is as
it were envied and loved by the opposing spirits, as wearing a form better
than those noetic ones themselves. And all the incomprehensibility and
unclarity and obscurity about it intimates nothing other than that it is
properly and truly an image of the incomprehensible God.

Whence, being ignorant of all the things about it, we confirm its existence
from its activities alone in the body; just as we also confirm God's existence
from his workings in the visible creation. But come, let us go to the most
timely of that according to the image and according to likeness, so that
according to the promises we may show the monadic of the Godhead in Trinity.
And what is this? It is clear that it is again our soul, and its noetic word,
and the mind, which the Apostle named spirit, when he charges us to be holy in
soul and body and spirit. For again the soul is unbegotten and uncaused, as a
type of the unbegotten and uncaused God and Father; but its noetic word is not
unbegotten, but is begotten from it ineffably and invisibly and
uninterpretably and impassibly. And the mind is neither uncaused nor
unbegotten, but processive, running through all and surveying all things, and
invisibly feeling about according to the image and likeness of the all-holy
and processive Spirit, concerning whom it has been said that The Spirit
searches all things, even the depths of God. The soul is not processive so
long as it is in the body, since if it were processive, we would then be dying
every hour. Our word is not unbegotten, since we would then be some irrational
and beastlike beings. And the more paradoxical of these paradoxes is this,
that we have a soul that is simple, and likewise a mind monadic and
incomposite; but having a double word, the same, we keep the begetting both
one and undivided.

For the word is begotten in the heart with a certain incomprehensible and
incorporeal begetting, and remains within unknown, and is begotten a second
begetting, bodily, through the lips, and then is made known to all; and it is
not separated from the soul that begot it, so that through the word of our two
begettings we may clearly learn the two begettings of God the Word according
to image and likeness. =44.1336= For he has been begotten invisibly and
uninterpretably and incomprehensibly from the Father before the ages. And he
was unknown as in a certain soul toward the Father, until, as from a certain
heart, that of the holy Virgin, he was begotten according to flesh
incorruptibly and without seed, and appeared to the world, not being separated
from the paternal hidden essence of God the begetter. So that it has been
shown in the monadic of our immortal and noetic psychic essence in image,
three certain hypostatic properties: unbegottenness of soul, and begetting of
word, and procession of spirit, that is of the mind. And I take courage and
dare, and I say that according to this invisible triadic contemplation of the
soul, the divine Apostle said that man is according to the image of the
invisible God.

And if these things are not so, why did it not rather become two-parted or
four-parted by God, but our soul has a certain tripartition and only this
unconfused, according to the image of the holy and like-essential
[consubstantial] and life-giving Trinity, so that, if it is lawful to say,
even in man, and especially in the just man, the whole fullness of the Godhead
dwells typically, but not naturally, sketching somewhat dimly the God in
Trinity? Whence also the wise outside have defined our soul again as
tripartite in another manner, saying it has the desiring and the reasoning and
the spirited, so that through the desiring it may be joined to the love of
God, through the reasoning it may receive the knowledge and wisdom from him,
and through the spirited it may array itself against the spirits of
wickedness, and in these three again it draws that according to the image of
God. For in three manners the God in Trinity administers and governs three
parts, I mean the heavenly and the earthly and the under-earth, by his
creative power and the providential and the judicial; for everything that God
accomplishes, he works it according to one of these three manners, either
creating, or providing, or disciplining.

And of the creative of God that according to the image is the desiring of the
soul; for desire leads to action; and of his providential again a symbol is
the reasoning of the soul; and of the disciplinary the spirited has been
established as a sign. And perhaps desire is proper to the soul. For before
all speech infants, having become ensouled, immediately reach for the breast
and for sleep; and the reasoning it is clear is proper to the rational; and
the spirited is naturally constituted about the mind, which also is disturbed
in the one who is angry contrary to nature. If then someone should wish to
learn how man is according to the image and likeness of God, let him feel
about these things, let him enter into these and such inner shrines of
thoughts, and let him survey the constitution of his own nature, that of the
noetic soul; let him learn all its parts finely and thinly, the parts of the
parts, the reasons, the manners and processions, the unions, the distinctions,
its single-formed, its tripartite, its unitary, its dual, its triadic, how it
is also one, and is contemplated in three, according to the image and likeness
of God a monad in triad, and a triad in monad typically recognized. For that
it is also one in essence, and not one in the contemplation of its own parts,
=44.1337= he made clear who said, I will sing with the spirit, I will sing
also with the mind; I will pray with the spirit, I will pray also with the
mind. Rather more clearly some of men teach us this typical according to the
image Trinity in us, from some cause often having a soul at times, but not
having mind or word; others again being well supplied with soul and word, but
wholly lacking mind; others again having mind and soul, but deprived of word.
Whence also the nature of infants, being born and coming from darkness into
light, immediately shows that it has the soul as a type of God and Father,
being noetic in potency, and having in itself the word and the mind; and
according to progress, as the body advances and grows, it secondarily
manifests the word, and this not all at once nor suddenly, but lisping
beforehand, and as it were sketching and pre-announcing the begetting of the
Word through flesh; and the presence of the mind is shown, when the infant
advances to a perfect man. But what, he says, do these things contribute to
the present subject of man?

And very much, O man: through these we learn the manner of God's manifestation
and showing-forth in the world, as in a certain body, and how according to
progress our nature came to know the mystery of the Trinity. For man was
conceived by the seed of the evil one as in a certain womb of error, sitting
in darkness and in the shadow of death; then coming forth into the light of
the knowledge of God as an infant at the beginning, through the guiding of the
law he was shown ensouled, having come to know the Father and God having in
himself, just as also the soul, the Word in essence and the Spirit. But man
not having room, through much weakness of judgment and infancy, for the
manifestation of the word and of the mind, lest through piety he should slip
into polytheism; then as time advanced he was being made wise thereafter, as
an infant growing, our nature of the world, being taught as from a certain
soul by God and Father to recognize dimly, as through certain unclear
lispings, of the prophetic teachings the trial and utterance and appearing of
the essential and hidden Word of the Father. After which lispings, I mean
Mosaic and prophetic riddles of the word, when the perfect Word of God had
come forth clearly and articulately, as from certain lips of the virginal
womb, the human nature of the world thereafter recognizes with these and
through this its complete fullness, the triadic, as having received as a
certain mind the Holy Spirit, not having taken up residence in it transitively
from without, but from the things within itself, that is, of the soul
=44.1340= and of the word, that is of the Father and of the Son, having been
manifested to it, not creatively, nor as introduced from outside, nor as of
another kind, but essentially from its innate and connatural hypostatic
existence the soul bringing forth its essential word, and putting forth the
connatural spirit of the mind, and as a certain body the incorporeal having
the members connatural with it that are themselves incorporeal, through which
as types she who is beyond form and shape is characterized and formed and
constituted, as having a certain bodily breath, the spirit of the mind, and as
life bearing the word co-living with her; of which, according to deprivation,
the rational and noetic soul according to the image and likeness of God can
neither be nor be known; so that through these things in her we may learn and
be taught that neither Father nor Son pre-existed the all-holy Spirit; but
just as in the soul, at once a rational soul, at once with it the word in it,
and at once, and together in it the spirit that is vital and constitutive and
completing; so at once Father, at once God the Word with the Father, at once
Son with the Father, together the Spirit with Son and Father.

And if you separate and deprive the word from the soul, your soul thereafter
is without word; so that through this according to the image of God you may
learn that if you deny God the Word, saying he is not with God and Father, you
thereafter proclaim God without word and beastlike; and if you separate the
Spirit from God, you thereafter say God is some dead thing and not living; if
you wish to philosophize concerning that according to the image and likeness
of God, philosophize thus, not from the things outside, but from the things
within you recognize the hidden God from the triad in you, know the Trinity
through enhypostatic things; for this is more firm and more trustworthy than
every other legal and scriptural testimony. For through this cause alone God
constructed such a living being, since the mystery of the holy Trinity was
going to be preached in the world, as hard to interpret and incomprehensible;
so that you who are according to the image and likeness of God may have in
yourself the image and likeness and the types and the examples of the holy
Trinity; so that looking to the image of the psychic constitution you may no
longer hesitate in the triadic mystery; no longer say and busy yourself
saying: If God is Trinity, how is he a monad? and if he is one, as it is said,
how Trinity? and if the Word is Son, how is it possible for an offspring to be
co-beginningless with the begetter? and if the Spirit is from the Father, how
is it not begotten, but processive? or which did the Father bring forth first,
the Son or the Spirit?

And if both at once, would there then be a brother-godhead and a twin-bearing
in the Trinity? and how shall we also discern, in the case of the incorporeal
and unmoved and unchanging, the difference of begetting and procession? and
how is it also possible for the offspring to be of like glory with the
begetter? and did the Father then beget willing =44.1341= or not willing? And
who is the witness that the Father and the Son and the Spirit are of one
essence? and if God and Father is a perfect hypostasis, and if the Word of God
is a perfect hypostasis, and if again the Spirit of God is a perfect
hypostasis, who does not say that also the mind of God is not another divine
hypostasis of God, and the arm another God, and another hypostasis of God the
finger? likewise also the right hand and all the remaining so-called members
of God in the divine Scripture? Therefore, lest you say and reason these
things, into which the heretics having stumbled and reasoned fell, God made
you according to the image and likeness of his own triadic existence, a
certain typical triad recognized as a consubstantial monad, looking well into
which, well as in a certain mirror and type you will find all the things
piously preached concerning God; I mean the triadic of the hypostases, the
monadic of the nature, the contemporaneous, the indivisible, the
incomprehensible, the unshaped, the unseeable, the unbegotten, the begotten,
the processive, the creative, the providential, the judicial, the unfeelable,
the incorporeal, the incorruptible, the indestructible, the immortal, the
eternal, the uninterpretable, the most beautiful, and to speak briefly, of all
the things piously said in the Godhead, you will find the types and the images
and the shadowings sketched in your soul.

And for this reason it has been said by God, Let us make man according to our
image, and according to likeness. But these things the heretics and the
unbelievers now have not known, nor considered. For if they had well
considered that according to the image and likeness of man, they would not
have hesitated about the triadic mystery, they would not have subjected the
above-nature to natural demonstrations. They would not have been darkened
saying it is impossible for God to be a trihypostatic Trinity. If Arius had
learned that according to the image of himself, he would not have taught the
Word of other essence from the Father. If Macedonius had piously considered
that according to the image, he would not have said the Holy Spirit is a
creature; but having been blinded they have suffered the same as those who
have the pearl within in their own bosom, but are ignorant of it, and
wandering seek it in the abyss. See then that not only in things typically
does our soul possess that according to the image of God, typically I say, not
of equal nature. And how is this? I will at once make clear what is said. We
believe God the Father to be uncircumscribed, likewise also the Son and the
Holy Spirit; wherefore as unbounded they have also their own appellations
borne-together and bound-to-one-another. For when one names Father, it is
clear that he has indicated a showing of some Son of his. For how will he even
be called Father if a Son is not known? Likewise also when one says Spirit, he
has indicated God; for God is Spirit, as Scripture says. Then come from this
holy Trinity to its image, I mean the triad existing within us, and you will
see also its three appellations =44.1344= existing borne-together and united.

For when you say a rational and noetic soul, it is clear that you have also
signified word and mind; and when you name word, altogether you have also
indicated the rational soul that is generative of it. Likewise also when you
say mind, in every way you have indicated both the soul and the word. For of
what will mind even be if not of soul and word? For this reason, just as they
have the appellation borne-together and bound-to-one-another, so they have
also the essential energy common and indivisible. For one and like is the
energy of Father and Son and Holy Spirit, one strength, and one power, one
will, one judgment. For whatever the Father would do, inseparable and
co-worker is also the Son; and whatever the Son, or the all-holy Spirit, would
accomplish, the Father cooperates altogether indivisibly; for neither does the
Son without the Father of himself by himself do anything, nor the Father
altogether apart from the Son and the Spirit, nor again does the Spirit
without the Son and Father work anything. Come then again from the prototypes
to the type according to the image of God and according to likeness, that of
our soul, and you will see one and like energy in us. For neither does the
soul without word accomplish anything, nor the word without soul, nor indeed
does the mind again by itself apart from the soul and the word work anything,
because of their connatural and congenital and bound-to-one-another common
power and energy, that according to the image and likeness of God. And if you
say to me that the soul energizes nothing by itself apart from the body, this
too we have already said before, that also in this being according to the
image and according to likeness it shows its invisible powers through visible
matter; except that also being separated from the body the soul that is pure
according to nature and essence, then especially being found more
clear-sighted and simple and undisturbed and more luminous, can more truly be
named and be according to the image and according to likeness of God. And if,
as is likely, the one of the contrary side mocks what has been said, as our
not having shown three properly completed hypostases in the soul according to
equality with the all-holy Trinity, let the foolish one learn that the soul is
according to a certain typical image, and not according to equality with the
holy Trinity; except that also in the Godhead, if it is not fearful to say,
the Father has a local division and an inscribed distance from one another
from the Son, and the Holy Spirit from him; do not run past, in your likeness
according to the image toward God, this either, I mean that through the bodily
begetting of the word from the lips the virtue and the wisdom and the prudence
and the power and the depth and the knowledge of your soul and of the mind is
made known to all and published; this example also declaring to you that it
exists according to the image and likeness of God. For through the begetting
according to flesh of God the Word there was manifested in the world the
virtue and the power and the knowledge and the wisdom and all the remaining
good things of the Father and of the Holy Spirit. =44.1345=

For see how many and of what sort are the achievements of the word, as a type
of God the Word, in the world, and how through word all things are created and
constituted; by word angels were established; by word they honor the Creator;
by word the visible things came to be; by word the creation is illumined; by
word the things that are were known; by word alone they were made manifest; by
word the ends were shown; by word we honor the Creator; by word the knowledge
of God has been preached; through word we have come to know God; through word
all things are constituted; and just as the speechless infant is without mind
to men until the begetting of the word through the lips, so through the
begetting according to flesh of God the Word all the irrationality of our
nature passed away, God the Word having shown clearly to the creation the
Father and the Holy Spirit. Whence as God-cut our soul is not even sent into
service after the release from the body, as the angels are sent. Since those
are ministering spirits; but the souls of the saints especially are according
to the image of God masterly spirits. For if man was made a little lower than
the angels after the disobedience, yet through the hypostatic union of God the
Word in him something greater has come to be; for he who was once according to
the image of God has now become the together-with-God; and he who before was
partaking of the image of God has become imparting of his own image; to him be
the glory unto the ages of ages. Amen.
