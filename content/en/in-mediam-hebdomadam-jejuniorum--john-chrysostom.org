On the middle week of the fasts.
=59.701=
Blessed be God who has granted to the contestants of the fast also to
behold the middle prize of self-control. For behold, we have stood at
the doors of the middle wrestling-grounds of the fast; behold, the
middle of the contest lies before us. Come then, taking courage in
Christ the president of the games, let us dare also the second
wrestlings of the fast. We have already, by the Lord's grace, reached
the middle of the ladder that leads up into the heavens; let us consume
also of our sins not only the half, but the whole. We have now come
near the prizes; let us therefore remain in the wrestling-grounds. For
already the gifts of the wrestling-grounds of self-control shine, the
crowns of the athletic sweats gleam, from heaven the prizes of the
contests are set forth, which Christ the president of the games awards
to the victory =59.702=.50 of those who wrestle. From heaven he nerves
us toward the vigor of the fast, and from heaven he promises to give
gifts to the athletes of the fast. Let us not then grow faint toward
the contests, that we may overtake the city of those crowned for the
fast. For those nourished by the fast go about heaven, heaven embraces
the imitators of the angels, since the citizenship of those who fast is
heavenly. For fasting transfers men from earth to the heavens, leads
them up into the rank of the angels, makes the journey from earth to
the heavens easy, and presents them before the heavenly king himself,
supplying the enjoyment of the immortal goods. For strange, brethren,
is the wonder of the contest among us. For children are anointed
together with men, women =59.703= contend with men, the weak nature of
females is made manly toward the contest.
The wrestling-ground of the fast is free to those who wish. For Christ
sits as a generous president of the games. Let us hasten also toward
the middle contest of the fast; let no one put this spiritual contest
to shame; let no one desert these spiritual contests. For the enemy has
been left of his boldness, the greater part of the battle has been
taken away, he has been conquered by the judgment of God, he has been
enslaved having been defeated, he has been driven out through fasting,
he has been thrown to the ground, mocked by infants, receiving from
them the epitaph of shame as a reproach. Let no one then falter,
beloved, having come about the middle of the fast; let no one grow
faint of the middle wrestling-grounds; for our nobility is his
downfall. If anyone contends sluggishly, he strengthens that one; if
any of you contends weakly, he nerves that one. Let us therefore stand
toward the contest nobly, that the adversary may fall the more. Let us
flee the desire of foods; let us endure for a little, that we may
receive immortal grace. For Adam ate, and was condemned to death;
Elijah fasted, and was entrusted with the keys of heaven; Noah drank,
and his mind was changed; Enoch practiced ascesis, and was translated
by angels; Pharaoh was filled, and was drowned in the sea with his
chariots; Moses fasted, and went down upon the sea, becoming a builder
of newer walls from the wet substance of the waters, and preparing the
people to travel a strange land, he led them through the sea with a
plastered foot.
Eve partook of a lawless eating, and all from her are delivered over to
death; the Master born of Mary abstained from lawful eating, and freed
all from the condemnation of death. The tree in paradise is a taste of
death. For on whatever day, says God, you eat of it, you shall die by
death. Flight from death is the tree of the cross; for from there
immortal life sprang =59.704= forth. From the cross the thief passed
over to paradise, exchanging life in place of death. The fruit of the
envy of the plant in paradise is the eating; and the destruction of
envy is the partaking from the fruit of the cross. If Adam had fasted,
he would not have been condemned to death, he would not have become an
exile from the toilless delight, he would not have been subjected to
corruption. For the divine Scripture says that God created man for
incorruption; but by the envy of the devil death entered into the
world. But Christ came, Our Peace, and having dissolved the middle wall
of the partition, the enmity in his flesh, he nailed the bond of sin to
the cross, having cancelled the handwriting of death against us. And
Paul cries: Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law, having become
a curse for us. Let us therefore, according to our power, recompense
the benefactor, beloved; with small obols let us serve him who is rich
in mercy.
Our Master was crucified for us; but let us feed the poor man created
according to his image; that one conversed with death, that he might
free us from death; let us through him with small obols snatch our
kinsmen from deadly famine. For he himself, he himself it is, who
through the poor receives what is offered. Do not then, O man, pass by
the poor man stretching out his hand, looking to the Master who
receives through that one; but let the hand of the poor man become for
you a censer set forth in the marketplace. For it has, instead of fire,
the coals of famine that melt. Let us therefore cast upon it, instead
of incense, two obols, and let us fill heaven with the fragrance, that
from the heavens we may hear: Inasmuch as you did it to one of these
little ones, you did it to me, as Christ cries; to him be the glory and
the power unto the ages of ages.
