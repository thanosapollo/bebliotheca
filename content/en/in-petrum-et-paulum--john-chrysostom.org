* On Peter and Paul

On the chiefs of the apostles Peter and Paul, and their most glorious
martyrdom.
=59.491=
1. I see a rivalry of heaven and earth because of the present festival of the
memory of the apostles, the heavenly powers praising with honorable voices the
teaching of their toil, as through them the mystery of the economy was made
known to those powers, as Paul cries, That now unto the principalities and the
authorities in the heavenly places might be made known through the Church the
manifold wisdom of God; while men upon earth are straining to show a worthy
praise through honor toward the chiefs, as having returned to salvation
through them. For what is greater than Peter? And what is equal to Paul? who
by deed and by word surpassed all the creation in heaven and on earth; who
were bound up with the clay of the body, and were found better than angels.
What then shall we say to the teachers of the upper and the lower creation?
For I find no word worthy to praise those who have praised our race, who went
around all the earth and sea, and tore up the roots of sins, and cast the
seeds of piety in the hearts of disobedient men.
Peter the instructor of the apostles, =59.492= Paul the evangelizer of the
inhabited world, and fellow-partaker of the powers above; Peter the bridle of
the ungrateful Jews, Paul the consolation of the nations. And see for me the
surpassing wisdom of the Master. For he chose Peter from fishermen, and Paul
from tentmakers; and this too he did for our benefit; for the glory of the
Jews was being sunk. Therefore the Lord entrusted it to this fisherman, saying
to him: Go to the sea, and cast a hook; and taking the first fish that comes
up, open its mouth, and you will find a stater. By sea he means the unstable
mind of the lawless Jews; by hook, the skilled word of teaching; fish
signifies the law. And to open its mouth means to interpret it; and to find a
stater in it signifies to examine it spiritually. And to Paul as tentmaker he
entrusted the nations, that through law and grace he might clothe the naked
with the scarlet garment, dyed from water and the Master's blood. Thus the
Master crying to him, Go, he says, for I will send you far away to the
nations. O blessed pair entrusted with the souls of the whole world! Peter the
beginning of orthodoxy, the =59.493= great hierophant of the Church, the
indispensable counselor of Christians, the treasure of the powers above, the
apostle honored by the Master. Paul the great herald of the truth, the boast
of the inhabited world, the man in heaven and angel on earth, the glory of the
Church, the eagle flying on high, the lyre of the Spirit, the swallow and
cicada, the Master's instrument, the wakeful servant of Christ. Paul and
Peter, the yoked oxen of the Church, who traded a fair merchandise for the
inhabited world, who took up the cross instead of a yoke, and instead of a
rider the Savior, instead of yoke-straps the covenants of the Scriptures,
instead of a goad the grace of the Holy Spirit. Paul and Peter, who brighten
the Church each day, the Master's treasuries, the inns of the inhabited world,
the vessels of the Spirit, the interpreters of the Holy Trinity, the guides of
the economy of the divine Word. Peter, my spiritual love; Paul, the vessel of
election, my staff. Peter, the temple of God; Paul, the mouth of Christ, the
lyre of the Spirit, the three-cubit man, and touching the heavens; who is
circumscribed in a place, and circumscribes the whole world for the Master;
who from Jerusalem and round about as far as Illyricum has fully preached the
Gospel of Christ; the swift runner, the eagle flying up to heaven, filled with
divine grace, who is testified by the Lord to bear his name before all the
inhabited world, who passed through the third heaven, and entered into
paradise, and went up even to the Master's incomprehensible throne, and heard
the unspeakable mysteries, which it is not lawful for a man to utter.
And what shall we say also to Peter? the sweet spectacle of the Church, the
radiance of the inhabited world, the most chaste dove, the instructor of the
apostles, the fervent apostle? he who is boiling with the Spirit, the angel
and man, filled with grace, the solid rock of the faith, the aged mind of the
Church, Bar-Jonah, who heard blessed and son of a dove from the Master's mouth
because of his purity, who received the keys of the kingdom of the heavens
from the Lord himself. You plundered the ranks of the angels. And why do I say
many things? The Lord himself praises you, saying: You are the light of the
world; richer than kings, more powerful than the wealthy, stronger than
soldiers, wiser than the wise and the philosophers, more eloquent than
rhetors, Having nothing, and possessing all things. You are the endurance of
the martyrs, the orthodoxy of the patriarchs, the ascesis of those who live as
monks, the crown-wearers of the virgins, the peacemakers of those in the same
yoke, the bridles of rapacious rich men, the chastisers of the unrestrained,
the shelter of kings, the walls of Christians, the adversaries of the
barbarians, who put a muzzle on the heretics, who put to death the irrational
passions of bodies, who drove out the legions of demons, who tore down the
altars of the Greeks, who inherited the things above and the things below;
having received the keys of the things above, and having authority to loose
and bind the sins of those below. O the wonder of the unlearned man! O the
wisdom of the unlettered! Peter, who in his shadow was making the paralyzed
firm, and loosing death; Paul, who in his garments was driving out diseases,
and putting demons to flight; who have the Mother of the Lord in their midst,
and are better than a righteous spirit; Paul the unceasing swallow of the
Church; Peter who continually, like a nightingale, plays the lyre without
pause for the inhabited world; the pillars of the Church, the great luminaries
of the inhabited world, better than one another, and better than all creation
together. Hail, Peter, the rock of the faith; hail, Paul, the boast of the
Church; hail, Peter, the foundation of orthodoxy; hail, Paul, the care of the
Churches; hail, Peter, the ornament of the inhabited world; hail, Paul, the
entrance of paradise; =59.494= hail, Peter, the one who leads by the hand to
the kingdom of the heavens; hail, Paul, fair harbor of those storm-tossed;
hail, Peter, who was counted worthy of many praises by the Lord; hail, Paul,
who is the steersman of the many gifts; hail, Peter, fervent and boiling with
the Holy Spirit; hail, Paul, the strenuous runner; who illumined the whole
world under the sun with the holy preaching, who endured countless hardships
for the Church, shut up in prisons, abominated by Jews, dragged by barbarians,
tortured by kings, not permitted to breathe easily, and not consenting to
cease from teaching; who were unable to move a limb of the body, because of
the weight of the bonds, and through letters were loosing all the inhabited
world bound by sin.
You put to flight the phalanxes of the demons; through you the grace of the
Holy Spirit has been spread over the inhabited world; you folded up the mist
of error, you cast down the insolence of the demons; you destroyed the altars
of the Greeks; you made all falsely named worship into ashes; from the wheat
you discarded the tares as refuse and threw them out; and your daily teaching
showed the offspring of the Church to be pure.
2. What thanksgiving then shall we render to you who have toiled so much for
us? I remember you, Peter, and I am struck with amazement; I recall you, Paul,
and being beside myself I am constrained by tears. For what I should say, or
what I should speak, contemplating your afflictions, I do not know. How many
prisons did you sanctify, how many jails did you illumine? how many dungeons
did you fill with your teachings? how many chains did you make splendid? how
many tortures did you endure? how many places did you sanctify with your
footsteps? how many insults did you disregard? how did you bear Christ? how
did you make the Churches bright with the preaching?
Blessed is the instrument of your tongue; your members have been mixed with
blood for the Church; without fail in all things you imitated Christ. For into
all the earth went forth your sound, and to the ends of the inhabited world
your words. Christ will keep unbreakable the Church betrothed by you. No one
before you, nor after you, has appeared such. For who, holding the teaching
office, dared to say anything apart from your tradition? You made all men
teachers, you fashioned all as theologians, you illumined all who were
darkened. Your endurance conquered our cripplings; you who yourselves prayed
to be anathema, that you might gain the world.
What then shall we render to you for all the good things which you have
rendered to us? Today are the memorials of your contest; today is the festival
of your witness concerning Christ; today we all, rejoicing, honor your holy
relics. Hail, Peter, who partook of the wood of the cross, and did not wish to
be crucified in an upright posture after the likeness of the Teacher, as our
Master was; but rather head downward, as making on earth the journey toward
heaven. O blessed nails, which passed through those holy members, longed for
by me, and better to me than heavenly glory. He who with boldness delivered
his soul into the hands of the Master, who served the Lord without
distraction, and the Church betrothed to him with all eagerness, the
Master-loving and well-serving servant of the Lord, boiling with the Spirit,
the faithful apostle of the Master of all. And Paul the thrice-blessed had his
head cut off by the sword, the indescribable man. What sword passed through
that throat, the Master's instrument, marveled at by heaven, and still
shuddered at by earth? what place received your blood, which was seen
milk-like on the tunic of him who impaled you; which made his barbarous soul
sweeter than honey, and prepared him to become faithful together with his
companions. May that sword be to me instead of a crown. Who served the Lord
thirty-five years with all eagerness; and having finished the course for
piety, he rested at about sixty-eight years.
We answer =59.495= our sins with your blessed voices. Rejoice in the Lord
always; pray without ceasing for us; fulfill your promises. For you cry out,
blessed Peter, saying thus, that I will be diligent after my departure to make
remembrance among you. The blessed Peter then, the apostle of Christ, is from
Bethsaida of Galilee, where our Lord Jesus Christ worked most of his miracles,
from which city Philip also came; and the blessed Paul from Tarsus of Cilicia.
And both have fallen asleep in the great city of ancient Rome, =59.496= in the
month of June, on the twenty-ninth, being slain under Nero the most lawless of
kings, and having arrived after very many labors to the Teacher and Lord of
all.
We ought then to show all eagerness, beloved, begging Christ the King of all,
our God, that he may count us worthy by his loving-kindness to abide in their
traditions and teachings, that we may receive mercy before his judgment seat;
through whom and with whom to God the Father be glory, together with the
all-holy and good and life-giving Spirit, now and always, unto the ages of
ages. Amen.
