Of our father among the saints John Chrysostom, the surviving parts of the
exposition on the Proverbs of Solomon.

CHAPTER 1.
To know wisdom and instruction. [cod. f. 1 b.] He receives the wisdom of God
from him, and instruction, learning through it the mysteries of the Word; and
those who have come to know the true and heavenly wisdom will easily
understand the wise words spoken by it; and there is also a human wisdom,
being a certain experience concerning crafts and the useful things of life. To
receive turns of words. [cod. f. 2 b.] For the things spoken conversely in
parables by the Holy Spirit become easy to know for those who have a faithful
heart toward God; for they understand the true righteousness proclaimed
through Christ. And to make judgment straight. [cod. f. 3 a.] And one makes
judgment straight when he has obtained divine grace before the rest; for a
man's steps are made straight by God; since he is mortal, how would he
understand his own ways? That he may give craft to the guileless. [cod. f. 4
a.] What do you say? Do you wish to make the guileless a crafty man? But let
us see whom he calls guileless: the simple, the one without malice, the one
who has not yet had experience of evil; for it suits that one most of all to
be wise, for he also calls understanding craft; for this one will stand
=64.661=
away from senseless reasonings; or since the one inexperienced in malice
falls, from simplicity, under the crafts of the malicious, they must, while
guarding against the craft of the wicked, become harmless among themselves,
but toward those who plot against them prudent as the serpents; For in malice,
he says, be infants, but in mind become perfect; to the guileless, to those
who approach God with a simple and guileless heart. To a young child
perception and thought. [cod. f. 4 b.] Whoever have become young as infant
children, and have obtained regeneration through Christ, receive perception
and a good thought, as the Lord says: Unless you turn and become, and so on.
For when a wise man has heard these things, he will be wiser. [ibid.] For
every man who listens to the divine words, and does them, being wise, becomes
wiser, being steered and guarded by the Holy Spirit; for the intelligent man,
he says, will acquire steering, so that, clearly, he is sunk by none of the
unexpected things; for a steersman too, when he has taken on skill, will be
more fit for the steering; he therefore who has become intelligent, he says,
will add what is lacking both in practice and in contemplation, so as to make
straight the voyage concerning these things; for he who sails knowledgeably
will arrive at the harbor of the divine will, having suffered shipwreck in
none of the virtues; but he who is not such will suffer shipwreck either
concerning faith, or concerning hope, or concerning love, or some one of the
virtues; and he who has suffered shipwreck concerning these things would
reasonably say: I came into the depths of the sea, and a storm drowned me; and
one must always say: May I be delivered from those who hate me, and from the
deep waters. Steering is the complete science of the soul concerning the
unstable nature of human affairs, how it is fitting for it to make the
crossing; for in many places we find this life named by Scripture waters and
seas. And he will understand a parable and a dark word. [cod. f. 5 a.] The
spiritual is compared with spiritual things; but the soulish man does not
receive the things of the Spirit of God; for it is folly to him, not



knowing that it is spiritually discerned; for as many things as the Holy
Spirit spoke through the prophets by parable and darkly, these become easy to
know for those who are partakers and sharers of the Holy Spirit, being taught
by the Spirit itself; for an unsealed man, unsteered, cannot come to know the
divine Scriptures. Piety toward God is the beginning of perception. [cod. f. 6
a.] Piety toward God is a beginning, and as it were a fountain and cause of
the divine perception according to the inner man, through which we see the
true light, and hear the hidden oracles, =64.664= and are fed with the bread
of
life, and take in the fragrance of Christ, and touch the word of this life; or
that when piety is present to us, the bodily senses also work what is due; and
then they are with us, when the eye does not see evil things, or the mouth
utter them. Hear, son, the laws of your father, and so on. [cod. f. 6. a.]
That is, of God and Father of all; the ordinances of a mother, of the wisdom
that regenerated you, which becomes as a mother and nurse of us, nursing us
and bringing us up in the words and commandments of God. For you will receive
a crown of graces. [ibid.] For if you keep in a faithful heart the law of your
father, and guard the commandments of your mother, you will receive a crown of
graces on your head, and a golden collar someday in the resurrection of the
righteous, being glorified and crowned by Christ in the heavenly and
incorruptible kingdom, if you contend in a manner worthy of the crown; for no
athlete is crowned unless he contend with self-control and according to the
law. Let not impious men lead you astray. [cod. f. 7. a.] He speaks of the
mysteries that happened in the case of Christ through the Jews, and how Judas
acquired a common purse with the Jews, bursting in the middle. Let us seize
his costly possession, and let us fill our houses with spoils. [cod. f. 8. a.]
The possession of a righteous man is wisdom and prudence, which the impious
seize, persuading the righteous man to do some of the things forbidden, so
that the mind, blinded by sin, may fall away from these holy possessions;
those too would say this who said: This is the heir; come, let us kill him,
and let us hold his inheritance; and the demons strip those whom they conquer,
taking from them the divine panoply, the helmet of salvation, and the
breastplate, and the sword of the spirit, which is the word of God; and their
houses would be the churches of those who do evil, which they wish to fill
with spoils. Do not go on a way with them. [cod. f. 8. b.] He did not say, Do
not act, but do not even go; and if you also start, turn aside at once; for
they run toward murder; and if you are caught in the snare, although the thing
was not of necessity but of will, you store up destruction for yourself, by
desire of more, not having seen the hidden ruin in the snare; he has therefore
suffered, he says, the same thing justly as the birds, which having wings and
being able to fly, turn about on the earth; and you, having reason, and being
able to be high, if you do not wish it, will not perish unjustly; or he calls
a net the many-branched nature of the punishments, to which the unjust are
subjected. Having become impious they hated perception. [cod. f. 9. b.] And if
it has no accuser, piety toward God is the beginning of perception. When you
call upon me, and so on. =64.665= [cod. f. 10. a.] He threatened those who are
unbelieving and disobedient to his name; so also David: He who dwells in the
heavens will laugh them to scorn; for it will be, when you call, and I will
not listen. And through Isaiah: If you stretch out your hands toward me, I
will turn my face away from you. They will eat the fruits of their own way.
[cod. f. 11. a.] They will have the honors from themselves, and they will eat
the toils of their fruits; for is the terrible thing for them from me? They
themselves



are the cause; they will take their fill, he says, of their own desire.

CHAPTER 2.
Son, let not an evil counsel seize you. And a divine covenant forgotten. [cod.
f. 13. b.] He calls the devil an evil counsel, which counsel, having seized
Adam, left him of the new and unaging learning of God. And he calls a
forgotten divine covenant that which Christ in the Gospels promised to Adam
and Noah, Abraham and Moses, that he will render it to as many as conquer the
devil, which even angels desire in the kingdom of the heavens, when it comes.

CHAPTER 3.
Do not craft evils against your friend, who dwells beside you and trusts in
you. [cod. f. 19. b.] Do not betray the one who has taken heart in you; for
this is of a beastly nature. Do not love enmity toward a man in vain. [cod. f.
20. a.] Do not become an enemy without cause; is there then a without cause?
for there is one through piety. Therefore Paul also says: If possible, as far
as it depends on you, being at peace with all men?

CHAPTER 4.
Who taught me. [cod. f. 21. a.] Do you see how he did not think it unworthy to
hear from his mother the things to be done? for there is not, there is not, in
the things to be done, anything wicked to hear from parents, unless some
should be distorted. Let our word rest fixed into your heart. For there is no
profit from the teacher, when the one being taught does not attend; and he
said well, Let it rest fixed. Let it rest, he says, let it be bound, let it
not withdraw, nor leap away; or let it be propped, show it a prop of the soul.
Love her. [cod. f. 21 a.] Since loves also are loosed, love her, he says, with
immortal loves, that she may keep you unharmed; for it is not possible without
a hot desire to accomplish anything noble. Speak my words. That many ways of
life may come to be for you. [cod. f. 21 b.] For hearing is useless when the
word has not been received; and the many ways are the virtues, which lead to
one way, him who said: I am the way; or as one might say, All things will be
easy for you and ready and =64.668= passable; so that from wherever you wish,
you
may go, through wealth, through poverty, through glory and dishonor; for as if
someone being unstartled, or should take wings from nature, will suspect no
way, even if it is rough, even if desert, even if some robber or housebreaker
lies in wait, so he who has been lightened by the wing of wisdom will be able
to go about everywhere with ease. For if you go, your steps will not be shut
in. [cod. f. 22 a.] If you run, he says, having come to be in a habit of
virtue, you will not endure a shutting in, you will have no hindrance, that
is, an end of the practice, which is not even in wisdom, by being young, and
not taking satiety of progress; for on the highways it often happens that,
having gone a great part, one meets a ravine that cuts off our journey
forward; or also meets someone, and is taught that we are not traveling well,
then turns back; but in virtue there is no hindrance; and how is it possible
not to grow weary while running, wearing so toilsome a body? since even the
tension of walking alone, when hurried, becomes a cause of pain; he therefore
who runs the way of the virtues does not take satiety, because he is always


drawing near to what is longed for. The ways of the impious are dark. [cod. f.
23 a.] Darkness is always a trial and a danger. But still in dangers the light
does not stand off, until it loose the night of the trials. With all keeping
keep your heart. [cod. f. 23 b.] Instead of, with much safety and attention,
or instead of, at every time; according to, From the watch of morning until
night, make the mind safe, so as not to give entrance to the enemy; or instead
of, both from deeds, and from words, and from thoughts; for the enemy knows
how to steal away not through actions only, but also through words; for from
such keeping life will accrue to you. Remove from you a crooked mouth, and put
unjust lips far away from you. [cod. f. 23 b.] He means the distorted words,
the ones not straight, the ones not true; and unjust lips are those of
slanderers, which he advises to put far away, and not to attend at all to
those who speak, but to stop the ears. For that one too, not having a hearer,
will be silent.

CHAPTER 5.
For honey drops from the lips of a harlot woman. [cod. f. 24 b.] For with
empty and deceitful and vain words promising honey, she tries to deprive even
of the small things. Who for a time makes your throat sleek. Who, conversing
toward pleasures of heart, and yielding to desire of flesh, seems for an hour
to sweeten the man. Afterward however you will find it more bitter than gall.
=64.669= [cod. f. 25 a.] For the last things of that man become worse than the
first, coming into judgment, which is as a sharp sword searching out even the
hidden things of his heart. For the feet of folly, and so on. [ibid.] For the
foolish, both running with the error and taking part with it, being entangled
by it, are led down after death into Hades; her whom he called a harlot above,
he now called folly. This one therefore, he says, leads down living into Hades
those righteous who use her; but the unjust, already dead; those who use her,
he says, are led down after death toward Hades; and Hades is named from seeing
unpleasantly all the punishments there, those who go down; those who even
after death go down into Hades David prays, saying: Let them go down into
Hades living, for wickedness is in their sojournings. Make your way far from
her. [cod. f. 15 b.] Do not keep company with the dogmas of the heresy, nor
come near to doorways, that is, to the variety of the ordinances which she has
procured for herself. The calling it a fountain shows the abundance of the
thing; and the granting the enjoyment to him alone makes the pleasure evident.
He calls another's embraces the wicked reasonings, in which one must not
linger as holding the soul together, which also do not escape God who knows
the heart. And each is bound with the cords of his own sins. [cod. f. 28 b.]
Even if God does not see, he says, yet giving you a time of repentance, he
himself does not punish, but you throw yourself into the nets of sin; you
prepare the bonds for yourself; blame no one then, not God, not man; the evils
are from us. He said well they hunt, because the punishments of the sins are
not brought on always; and not every sin is called a cord, but that which is
plaited with a blameworthy deed and a censurable purpose; for he who has had a
certain desire, but has not yoked to it the working of the sin, possesses a
simple passion. Do not then, he says, escape notice twisting a rope against
yourself by the habit of sin, and being led as prey toward the practice of it.
Let us therefore blame no one; the evils are from us; let even the



Manichaeans be ashamed at these things; and they are not simply bound, but are
tightened, and before being brought to the judge, he has given punishment from
the conscience. This one dies with the uninstructed; and from the multitude of
his own fatness he was cast out. [cod. f. 28 b.] For having been hunted by
lawlessness, and having become uninstructed, he will suffer the same things as
those; for he too who has his portion with murderers is a murderer. And see
how he makes death appear bitter, having said with whom he perishes; for it is
dreadful even to depart with a wicked opinion; for law =64.672= lessness
multiplied them, he says, which he called fatness; from which he also became
flesh, from the works of the flesh, and outside the life in which he was being
saved. And he perished through folly, not through desire; for he had a lawful
enjoyment of desire, his own wife; so that no one should blame nature, but his
own lack of measure; and this is not of nature.

CHAPTER 6.
Be, not being loosened. [cod. f. 29 a.] Be not falling back, nor letting the
tones of endurance slacken; but raising up the body pledged by you to God, and
pressing it toward the rendering of the commandments set before it. Go to the
ant, O sluggard. [cod. f. 29 b.] It is a great accusation, that a man should
go to the irrational animal to be taught virtue; for he could have named a
greater animal as well, but by the cheapness of the example he wishes to put
the idle man to shame; and see in what he bids him emulate: in the toil, in
the sweat; use just toils. And he said well, not even having one who compels;
for when a table of abundant summer is present to it, when one cannot suspect
famine; yet it does not look to the present, but takes thought for the future;
and the present prosperity does not make it more careless, as we often, living
in plenty, do not take perception of the future terrors. See then you too,
lest ever, when the gathering has been marked off for you in the present, the
winter stand round you, the bitter shutting in, and you suffer the thing of
the virgins. [cod. f. 30 a.] Go to the ant, that is, to the nation set at
nought by the rulers of this age, but possessing hope toward Christ; and
imitate its good way of life, and become wiser. There being no husbandry,
instead of, possessing no hope in the world; nor being deceived by the sight
of the lawless deeds practiced in life; nor being forced by anyone, but
willingly and from a purpose of heart believing God, it prepares through good
works the eternal life. Go to the bee. [cod. f. 30 b.] Run to the church, and
learn the works of the light that come to be in it, both how solemnly it makes
its working; and displaying its way of life as temperate and pure, whose toils
both kings and private men, whose commandments rich and poor take for their
own salvation, although it is weak in strength, although it is cheap in the
world; but believing in Christ it was brought near; for there is present to it
a rich and costly table, at the time when it stores it up; yet it does not
look to the present, but makes much forethought of the future; in summer it
prepares the food, in the harvest it makes much laying by. See how the animal
takes thought for the future; you too then enjoy safety according to the
present life; but look around lest ever, when winter has come on, your house
be found empty and bare of food. [cod. f. 31 =64.673= a.] See its justice; it
is
not



useful to kings, and useless to private men; perhaps the multitude both of
these and of those is served by its medicine; it toils by nature, not by
honors; be such yourself also, not honoring a face, but the nature of things;
and yet what beauty has it in the body? none; so that the sluggard may not
have refuge in weakness, he took an animal weaker than all, taking away its
defense. And it is desired even by those who do not have it, nor possess it;
each day in fields, in cities, we shall hear all who pass by speaking ten
thousand praises of this race. Poverty comes upon you as a bad traveler, and
want as a good runner. [cod. f. 31. b.] Nothing is harder than voluntary [or
involuntary?] poverty, which comes most quickly to the idle man, beating the
good in the race by speed; and these are poverty and want; poverty, a
deprivation of knowledge; and want, a scarcity of virtues. See the perfection
of the working; are you not taught by the irrational creatures? learn from the
trial of things; pray not to appear more irrational than the irrational.
Therefore flee poverty also. Is working burdensome? but see its end. Is sloth
sweet? but look at the close; let us not learn thoroughly the beginnings of
things, but let us look where they end; he who goes out of the house does not
wish to remain on the way, but even while he is at the beginning of the going
out, he attends to the end; for those things he also makes the beginning, so
as to accomplish the end of the beginning; but you do the contrary. At every
time such a one raises tumults for a city. [cod. f. 32. a.] Wonderful, how
great a strength he has, at every time? for even if he wish to signal some
good thing, the manner of the signaling is full of suspicion; clashing crowds,
and making whole cities unsettled; such a one has no peaceful spirit, nor has
he heard Jesus saying: Blessed are the peacemakers; and through these things
he teaches to give a handle to no one. An eye of an insolent man, an unjust
tongue, hands pouring out just blood. [ibid.] He signifies from a part the
working of the members of the body toward evil; what therefore in bodies is
eye, and tongue, and hands, this in the soul is reasoning, impulse, and
judgment; and he did not list these for us simply, but that we may learn to
discipline them by fleeing the imitation. They pour out just blood, who drink
the blood of the Lord and are defiled by shameful deeds. A heart crafting evil
reasonings. [cod. f. 32. b.] For when the heart is unclean, it has made the
members unclean also; for it poured the venom out. He said well it kindles;
for falsehood is a fire separating those who are united, not only not
benefiting, but even making them enemies; and according to thought, =64.676=
brothers would be those who have the gift of adoption, and are under the same
father Christ; whom unjust and passionate reasonings, having fallen in,
attempt to trouble; kindling wrath toward hatred, and desire toward shameful
workings. And bind them upon your soul, and so on. [cod. f. 33. a.] Instead
of, bind together in the depth of the mind, having first stored the teaching
of the laws; and then, adorning through good works, fasten them upon your
neck. A good companion and conversant: to meditate the oracles of God
continually; and especially when we walk in the marketplace; for then we need
these weapons more, because the eye also wounds, and wrath kindles, and desire
of money from the things seen stirs. For you will meditate in them sitting,
and going, and lying down. A command is a reproof of evils, and instruction of
goods; for through law is knowledge of sin. To keep you from a woman under a
husband, and from slander of a strange tongue. [cod. f. 33. b.] See him making
much account of temperance; for desire is darkness; and from slander, instead
of, not from adultery only, but also from suspicion; for it is often possible
to be clean of the sin, and of the suspicion not



to have been freed; but the commandment accomplishes this too; and the
teaching of the heterodox would also be a woman under a husband, and their
slanderous word, which he advises to guard against. Let not the charms of the
elegant phrasing carry you away. The honor of a harlot is as much as even of
one loaf. He showed the shortness of the pleasure, and the cheapness of its
hire, through the one loaf. [cod. f. 34. a.] He will not be held guiltless.
Nor will he who is clean of the deed, but is defiled by the remaining
associations, be held guiltless; instead of, he will not be clean; for as it
is not possible for those who have opened the eyes not to see, so it is not
possible, a woman being seen, for desire not to be born; why then do you heap
fire upon nature? For he steals that he may fill his own hungry soul. [cod. f.
34. b.] He does not say this releasing the thief from the charge, but by
comparison; for the one has poverty as a defense, but the other is mastered by
folly alone; and the one, if caught, will be subject to loss only, but the
other to death and that a shameful one; and according to contemplation, that
it is no wonder if someone uses one and a second theorem of the outside
wisdom, so as through them to bring the true into light, and to refute the
things that seem firm among them; such was Paul, taking captive every thought
unto Christ, and making the Athenians from their own inscription; but if,
leaving the true wisdom, we love that of the world, we commit adultery
foolishly.

CHAPTER 7.
Put them on your fingers. [cod. f. 35 a.] Fingers signify the more particular
actions; and the hand is set upon the action as a whole; he says then, work
virtue even as far as the fine things; for thus you will have the heart broad
for the reception of the virtues; which Paul advised the Corinthians who did
not have it, saying, Be you also enlarged. [cod. f. 35. b.] Contemplation of
the things that have come to be is our sister, brought forth together with the
bodiless beings, whose forms are prudence, knowledge, instruction. From a
strange woman, from the polytheist teaching; for she teaches pleasant things,
being talkative, pleasure and love of money; If she throw at you words toward
favor; The words that throw toward favor are the passionate reasonings; and he
who is at leisure for the wisdom of God is kept from the wickedness that
begets them. Her house, the breadth of the dogmas; a window, the particular.

CHAPTER 8.
Son, you proclaim wisdom, and so on. [cod. f. 38 a.] Desire wisdom, he says,
and the habit of the mind will obey you; and Upon the heights, either that the
things she says are manifest, or that she makes men high; and the perfect man,
knowing that wisdom made all things, sees both her height, and the lowliness
or stepping upon the paths; but the newly planted, knowing only the height,
ought to call her so that he may see her; so as easily to hear the voice; Only
walk toward her; and beside the gates of potentates, either that she makes
potentates, or that those especially have need of her; or that she sits beside
the introductory words of the teachers; for one who has come to be in her
porches hears her being hymned; and she is said to be hymned sometimes in
goings out, sometimes in comings in, by those going out from vice and going in
to virtue. Then he brings her in conversing, which is enough to draw; I exhort
you, O men; and I send forth my voice to sons of men. Even if the gain is
yours, he says, yet I exhort; and man and sons of men are the same; or men,
the perfect, who are deemed worthy of exhortation;



but sons of men, the more deficient, who for the time are deemed worthy of a
voice. The Lord made countries and uninhabited places, and inhabited heights
of what is under heaven. [cod. f. 41 a.] Do not bring in another craftsman,
even if the uninhabited has no profit; and for this reason he made the heights
to be inhabited, he says, that having learned that the limits and the
extremities are a work of God, you may not suspect concerning the middles; or
heights, the incomprehensible and divine mysteries; just as countries are the
fruit-bearing souls; and uninhabited, the divine thoughts, which it is not
permitted for a man to speak.

CHAPTER 9.
Wisdom built herself a house, and set up seven pillars. [cod. f. 42 b.] The
Son of God being Wisdom, having become man, himself prepared for himself a
house, the flesh from the Virgin; seven pillars, spirit of God, spirit of
wisdom and understanding, spirit of counsel and might, spirit of knowledge and
piety, spirit of fear of God, as Isaiah says. Or also the Church a house, and
the apostles pillars; or he says the wise man, that he is safe, sufficient to
himself and needing no one. The house of wisdom is the Church; pillars, those
who seem to be pillars. She slew her own sacrifices. Sacrifices the divine
thoughts, or the interpretations of the Scriptures; and mixture, the mixing
with the history the anagogy; for the divine things being unmixed cannot be
contained; for this reason dividing the dogmas and the interpretations finely
and variously, she works food for those being taught. [cod. f. 33 a.] She
mixed into a mixing-bowl her own wine. For the divine things being unmixed she
mixed with human voices, that is, with wisdom of men, that we may be able to
contain her. The sacrifices, the blessed prophets, being killed in season by
the unbelievers, as he says, that For your sake we are put to death all the
day. [cod. f. 43 b.] She sent her own prophets and apostles with a high
preaching, that is, with dogmas having nothing low and cast down, calling
forth all the nations into the knowledge of God; and he calls high, Their
sound went out into all the earth. [cod. f. 44 a.] To those in need, to those
who have not yet possessed the power of the Holy Spirit; or to those who have
begun, but are imperfect. Come, come to know the true and straight way; and
Drink wine, and receive Holy Spirit as wine. Leave folly, turn aside from the
ways of ignorance, and come to know the eternal life; Eat, be strengthened by
my words and mysteries. Give occasion to a wise man. [cod. f. 45 a.] Then is
the reproof an occasion, a saving and useful thing; or he means that through
the words; for thus he will be raised toward the heights of the anagogy. Make
known to a righteous man, that is, to the well-disposed make known the ways of
righteousness; and he will add, instead of, he will love you, the sins from
inattention being corrected through the reproof. Counsel of saints is
understanding. Counsel of saints discerning the things to be done and not; for
such is the counsel with understanding. You will live a long time. The long is
set instead of the infinite; or perhaps it may release us from doubt; for the
years of this life are eternal. [cod. =64.681= f. 45 b.] He who does not use
the
wisdom of God shepherds winds, proposing to himself insubstantial beginnings
both of things and of the Scriptures. Ways of his own vineyard, he calls the
legal way of life, leading toward Christ; for Israel is a vine. [cod. f. 46
a.] He ought to farm, but what does he do? He goes through a desert; for the
nature of falsehood is without fruit.



Did you see how he presented the nature of falsehood as without fruit? he who
leans on this possesses only thorns and thistles; and waterless would be that
which does not have divine teaching; and thirsty that which does not gush
drinkable water of the Spirit, which is given to the fruit-bearing fountains
by Jesus; and unfruitfulness, vice and ignorance. Touch hidden breads
pleasantly. [cod. f. 46 b.] And what pleasure of stolen things, in which there
is fear, danger, expectation of death? and although it seems sweet, it is not
