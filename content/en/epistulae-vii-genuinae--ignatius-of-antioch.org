THE GENUINE LETTERS
IGNATIUS TO THE EPHESIANS
Ignatius, who is also Theophorus, to her who is blessed in the greatness of
God the Father's fullness, who was foreordained before the ages to be always
unto an abiding, unchanging glory, united and elect in true passion, by the
will of the Father and of Jesus Christ our God, to the church worthy of
blessing, which is in Ephesus of Asia, abundant greeting in Jesus Christ and
in blameless joy.
Having welcomed in God your much-loved name, which you have acquired by a just
nature according to faith and love in Christ Jesus our Savior; being
"imitators" of "God", having rekindled in the blood of God the kindred work,
you have accomplished it perfectly; for having heard that I was bound from
Syria for the common name and hope, hoping by your prayer to attain in Rome to
fighting with beasts, that by attaining it I might be able to be a disciple,
you hastened to see me; since then I have received your multitude in the name
of God in Onesimus, who is beyond description in love, and your bishop in the
flesh, whom I pray that you love according to Jesus Christ and that you all be
like him. For blessed is he who granted you, being worthy, to obtain such a
bishop. Concerning my fellow-servant Burrhus, your deacon according to God,
blessed in all things, I pray that he may remain to your honor and that of the
bishop; and Crocus also, worthy of God and of you, whom I received as an
exemplar of the love from you, has refreshed me in every way; as the Father of
Jesus Christ shall also refresh him, together with Onesimus and Burrhus and
Euplus and Fronto, through whom I have seen you all in love.

May I have joy of you always, if I am worthy. It is fitting then in every way
to glorify Jesus Christ who has glorified you, that being perfectly joined in
one submission, subject to the bishop and the presbytery, you may be
sanctified in all things. I do not command you as being somebody. For even if
I am bound in the Name, I have not yet been perfected in Jesus Christ. For now
I am beginning to be a disciple, and I speak to you as my fellow-learners. For
I needed to be anointed by you with faith, admonition, endurance,
long-suffering. But since love does not let me be silent about you, for this
reason I have taken the lead in exhorting you to run together with the mind of
God. For Jesus Christ, our inseparable life, is the Father's mind, as also the
bishops, appointed throughout the ends, are in the mind of Jesus Christ. Hence
it is fitting for you to run together with the bishop's mind, which you also
do. For your justly famous presbytery, worthy of God, is so attuned to
the bishop as strings to a lyre. Therefore in your concord and harmonious love
Jesus Christ is sung. And you, man by man, become a chorus, that being
harmonious in concord, taking the tone of God in unity, you may sing with one
voice through Jesus Christ to the Father, that he may both hear you and
recognize you by the good things you do as members of his Son. It is useful
then for you to be in blameless unity, that you may always partake of God. For
if I in a short time had such intimacy with your bishop, not human but
spiritual, how much more do I count you blessed who are mixed with him as the
church with Jesus Christ and as Jesus Christ with the Father, that all things
may be harmonious in unity? Let no one be deceived: unless a man is within the
altar, he lacks "the bread of God". For if the prayer of one and of a second
has such power, how much more that of the bishop and of the whole church? He
therefore who does not come to the

same place, this one already is proud and has separated himself. For it is
written: "God resists the proud." Let us then be eager not to resist the
bishop, that we may be subject to God. And the more anyone sees the bishop
silent, the more let him fear him; for everyone whom the master of the house
sends for his own stewardship, we must so receive as the one who sent him.
Therefore it is clear that we must look upon the bishop as the Lord himself.
Onesimus himself then praises exceedingly your good order in God, that you all
live according to truth and that no heresy dwells among you; but you do not
even listen to anyone more than to one speaking about Jesus Christ in truth.
For some are accustomed to bear the Name about with wicked deceit, while doing
other things unworthy of God; whom you must shun as wild beasts. For they are
mad dogs, biting in secret; whom you must guard against as being hard to heal.
There is one physician, both fleshly and spiritual, begotten and unbegotten,
God become in flesh, true life in death, both from Mary and from God, first
passible and then impassible, Jesus Christ our Lord. Let no one then deceive
you, as indeed you are not deceived, being wholly of God. For when no strife
has been fixed among you that can torment you, then you live according to God.
I am your offscouring, and I am purified for you Ephesians, a church renowned
through the ages.

The fleshly cannot do the things of the spirit, nor the spiritual the things
of the flesh, just as faith cannot do the things of unbelief nor unbelief the
things of faith. But the things you do even according to the flesh are
spiritual; for you do all things in Jesus Christ. But I learned that some
passed through from there having evil teaching; whom you did not allow to sow
among you, stopping your ears so as not to receive what was sown by them, as
being stones of the Father's temple, prepared for the building of God the
Father, being carried up to the heights by the engine of Jesus Christ, which
is the cross, using the Holy Spirit as a rope; and your faith is your
windlass, and love the way that carries up to God. You are then all also
traveling companions, God-bearers and temple-bearers, Christ-bearers,
holy-bearers, adorned in all things with the commandments of Jesus Christ; in
whom I also, rejoicing, was counted worthy through what I write to converse
with you and to congratulate you that according to another life you love
nothing except God alone. And for the other people too "pray without ceasing".
For there is in them hope of repentance, that they may attain to God. Permit
them then at least to be discipled by your works. Toward their angers you be
meek, toward their boastings you be humble-minded, toward their blasphemies
you the prayers, toward their error you "steadfast in the faith", toward their
wildness you be gentle, not being eager to imitate them in return. Let us be
found their brothers in gentleness; and let us be eager to be "imitators of
the Lord": who has been more wronged? who more deprived? who more set at
nought? that no plant of the devil may be found in you, but that you may
remain in all purity and sobriety in Jesus Christ, fleshly and spiritually.
The last times. For the rest let us be ashamed, let us fear the long-suffering
of God, lest it become a judgment for us. For either let us fear the coming
wrath, or let us love the present grace, one of the two; only to be found in
Christ Jesus unto the true life. Apart from him let nothing be fitting for
you, in whom I bear about the bonds, the spiritual pearls, in which may it be
granted me to rise by your prayer, of which may I always be a partaker, that I
may be found in the lot of the Ephesian Christians, who also always agreed
with the apostles in the power of Jesus Christ. I know who I am and to whom I
write.

I am a condemned man, you have been shown mercy; I am under danger, you are
established. You are the passage of those being slain unto God,
fellow-initiates of Paul, the sanctified, the

witnessed, worthy of blessing, under whose feet may it be granted me to be
found when I attain to God, who in every letter remembers you in Christ Jesus.
Be eager then to come together more frequently for the thanksgiving of God and
for glory. For when you come together frequently to the same place, the powers
of Satan are destroyed, and his destruction is dissolved in the concord of
your faith. Nothing is better than peace, in which every war of heavenly and
earthly things is abolished. None of this escapes you, if you have faith and
love perfectly toward Jesus Christ, which is the beginning of life and the
end; the beginning is faith, "and the end love". And the two becoming in unity
are God, and all the rest follows toward nobility.

No one professing faith sins, nor does one who has acquired love hate. "The
tree is manifest from its fruit"; so those who profess to be of Christ will be
seen by what they do. For the work is not now of profession, but in the power
of faith if one is found to the end. It is better to be silent and to be, than
speaking not to be. It is good to teach, if the one who speaks does. There is
then one teacher, who "spoke, and it came to be"; and even the things he has
done in silence are worthy of the Father. He who has truly acquired the word
of Jesus is able also to hear his stillness, that he may be perfect, that he
may act through what he speaks and be known through what he is silent. Nothing
escapes the Lord, but even our hidden things are near him. Let us then do all
things as he dwelling in us, that we may be his temples and he in us our God,
which also is and will appear before our face, from the things by which we
justly love him. "Be not deceived, my brothers"; house-destroyers "shall not
inherit the kingdom of God". If then those who do these things according to
the flesh died, how much more if a man corrupts the faith of God by evil
teaching, for which Jesus Christ was crucified? such a one, becoming filthy,
will go into the unquenchable fire, and likewise the one who hears him. For
this reason the Lord received ointment upon his head, that he might breathe
incorruption upon the church. Do not be anointed with the foul odor of the
teaching of the ruler of this age, lest he take you captive from the life that
lies before you. Why then do we not all become prudent, having received
knowledge of God, which is Jesus Christ? Why do we perish foolishly, being
ignorant of the gift which the Lord has truly sent? My spirit is an
offscouring of the cross, which is a "stumbling-block" to those who do not
believe, but to us salvation and eternal life. "Where is the wise? where the
disputer"? where the boasting of those called intelligent? For our God Jesus
the Christ was carried in the womb by Mary according to God's economy "of the
seed" of "David", and of the Holy Spirit; who was born and baptized, that by
the passion he might cleanse the water. And the virginity of Mary and her
childbearing escaped the ruler of this age, likewise also the death of the
Lord: three mysteries of a cry, which were worked in the stillness of God. How
then was he manifested to the ages? A star in heaven shone beyond all the
stars, and its light was unspeakable and its newness furnished astonishment,
and the rest of the stars together with sun and moon became a chorus for the
star, and it was exceeding its light beyond all; and there was disturbance,
whence the newness unlike them. Hence all magic was being dissolved and every
bond of wickedness was vanishing; ignorance was being torn down, the old
kingdom was being destroyed, God being manifested humanly unto newness of
eternal life; and that which had been perfected with God was taking a
beginning. From there all things were being moved together because the
dissolution of death was being planned.
If Jesus Christ should count me worthy in your prayer and it be his will, in
the second booklet which I am about to write to you, I will further make clear
to you the economy which I have begun, unto the new man Jesus Christ, in his
faith and in his love, in his passion and resurrection. Especially if the Lord
reveal to me that you, man by man, all in common in grace, come together by
name in one faith and in Jesus

Christ, who is "according to the flesh" of the race of "David", the son of man
and son of God, so that you obey the bishop and the presbytery with
undistracted mind, breaking one bread, which is the medicine of immortality,
an antidote against dying, but to live in Jesus Christ always. I am your
ransom, and of those you sent to God's honor to Smyrna, from where I also
write to you, giving thanks to the Lord, loving Polycarp as also you. Remember
me, as Jesus Christ also does you. Pray for the church in Syria, from where I
am being led bound to Rome, being last of the believers there, as I was
counted worthy to be found unto the honor of God.

Farewell in God the Father and in Jesus Christ, our common hope.

IGNATIUS TO THE MAGNESIANS
Ignatius, who is also Theophorus, to her who is blessed in the grace of God
the Father in Christ Jesus our Savior, in whom I greet the church which is in
Magnesia on the Maeander and I pray in God the Father and in Jesus Christ
abundant greeting.
Having known the well-ordered character of your love according to God,
rejoicing, I resolved in the faith of Jesus Christ to speak to you. For having
been counted worthy of a most God-befitting name, in the bonds which I bear
about, I sing the churches, in which I pray for a unity of flesh and spirit of
Jesus Christ, who is our life always, and of faith and love, to which nothing
is preferred, and what is more lordly, Jesus and the Father; in whom if we
endure all the insolence of the ruler of this age and escape, we shall attain
to God.

Since then I was counted worthy to see you through Damas your bishop worthy of
God and the worthy presbyters Bassus and Apollonius and my fellow-servant the
deacon Zotion, of whom may I have joy, because he is subject to the bishop as
to the grace of God and to the presbytery as to the law of Jesus Christ. And
it is fitting for you also not to take advantage of the bishop's age, but
according to the power of God the Father to render him all respect, as I have
learned that the holy presbyters also have not taken upon themselves his
apparent youthful station, but as prudent in God yield to him, yet not to him,
but to the Father of Jesus Christ, the bishop of all. Unto the honor then of
him who willed us, it is fitting to obey without any hypocrisy; since a man
does not deceive this bishop who is seen, but cheats the invisible one. And
such a word is not toward flesh, but toward God who knows the hidden things.
It is fitting then not only to be called Christians, but also to be; just as
some also call a man bishop, but do everything without him. Such men do not
seem to me to be of good conscience, because they do not assemble securely
according to the commandment.
Since then things have an end and the two lie before us together, death and
life, and each is about to go "to his own place". For just as there are two
coinages, the one of God, the other of the world, and each of them has its own
stamp lying upon it, the unbelievers that of this world, but the faithful in
love the stamp of God the Father through Jesus Christ, through whom unless we
have dying into his passion of our own choice, his life is not in us. Since
then in the persons written above I have seen the whole multitude in faith and
have loved it, I exhort you, be eager to do all things in the concord of God,
the bishop presiding in the place of God and the presbyters in the place of
the council of the apostles, and the deacons, most sweet to me, entrusted with
the ministry of Jesus Christ, who was with the Father before the ages and
appeared at the end. All of you then, having received a God-like character,
respect one another, and let no one look upon his neighbor according to the
flesh, but in Jesus Christ love one another always.

Let nothing be among you that will be able to divide you, but be united to the
bishop and to those who preside, unto a type and teaching of incorruption. As
therefore the Lord did nothing without the Father, being united, neither
through himself nor through the apostles, so neither do you do anything
without the bishop and the presbyters; nor try to have anything appear
reasonable as private to yourselves, but at the same place: one prayer, one
petition, one mind, one hope in love, in the blameless joy, which is Jesus
Christ, than whom nothing is better. All of you run together as to one temple
of God, as to one altar, to one Jesus Christ, who came forth from one Father
and was with one and has gone to him. Do not be led astray by heterodoxies nor
by old fables, which are unprofitable. For if we still live according to the
law, we confess that we have not received grace. For the most divine prophets
lived according to Christ Jesus. For this reason they also were persecuted,
being inspired by his grace, to persuade fully those who disobey that there is
one God, who manifested himself through Jesus Christ his Son, who is his Word
come forth from silence, who in all things pleased the one who sent him. If
then those who were conversant in old things came to newness of hope, no
longer sabbatizing, but living according to the Lord's day, on which also our
life arose through him and his death, which some deny, through which mystery
we received our faith, and for this reason we endure, that we may be found
disciples of Jesus Christ our only teacher; how shall we be able to live
without him, whose disciples even the prophets were in the Spirit, as they
expected him as teacher? And for this reason he whom they justly awaited,
being present, raised them from the dead.
Let us then not be without sense of his kindness.

For if he should imitate us according as we act, we are no more. For this
reason, having become his disciples, let us learn to live according to
Christianity. For whoever is called by another name besides this is not of
God. Put aside then the evil leaven, grown old and sour, and change to new
leaven, which is Jesus Christ. Be salted in him, lest any among you be
corrupted, since you will be convicted by the smell. It is out of place to
speak Jesus Christ and to judaize. For Christianity did not put its faith in
Judaism, but Judaism in Christianity, into which every tongue that has
believed was gathered to God. But these things, my beloved, not because I knew
that some of you are so, but as one lesser than you I want you to be on guard,
not to fall into the hooks of empty glory, but to be fully persuaded in the
birth and the passion and the resurrection which took place in the time of the
governorship of Pontius Pilate; things done truly and securely by Jesus
Christ, our hope, from which may it be granted to none of you to be turned
aside. May I have joy of you in all things, if I am worthy. For even if I am
bound, I am not equal to one of you who are loosed. I know that you are not
puffed up; for you have Jesus Christ in yourselves; and all the more, when I
praise you, I know that you are respectful, as it is written, that the
"righteous is his own accuser". Be eager then to be confirmed in the decrees
of the Lord and of the apostles, that "whatever you do, you may prosper" in
flesh and spirit, in faith and love, in Son and Father and in Spirit, in
beginning and in end, with your most worthy bishop and the worthily woven
spiritual crown of your presbytery and the deacons according to God. Be
subject to the bishop and to one another, as Jesus Christ to the Father
according to the flesh and the apostles to Christ and to the Father and to the
Spirit, that there may be a unity both fleshly and spiritual. Knowing that you
are full of God, I have exhorted you briefly. Remember me in your prayers,
that I may attain to God, and the church in Syria, from which I am not worthy
to be called; for I need your prayer and love united in God, that the church
in Syria may be counted worthy

to be refreshed as with dew through your church. The Ephesians greet you from
Smyrna, from where I also write to you, being present to the glory of God as
you also are, who have refreshed me in every way together with Polycarp,
bishop of the Smyrnaeans.
And the remaining churches also greet you in the honor of Jesus Christ.
Farewell in the concord of God, possessing an undivided spirit, which is Jesus
Christ.

IGNATIUS TO THE TRALLIANS
Ignatius, who is also Theophorus, to the church beloved of God, the Father of
Jesus Christ, the holy church which is in Tralles of Asia, elect and worthy of
God, at peace in flesh and spirit in the passion of Jesus Christ, our hope in
the resurrection unto him; which I also greet in the fullness in apostolic
character and I pray abundant greeting. I have known you to have a blameless
and undivided mind in endurance, not by use but by nature, as Polybius your
bishop made clear to me, who by the will of God and Jesus Christ came to
Smyrna and so congratulated me bound in Christ Jesus, that I beheld your whole
multitude in him. Having then welcomed the goodwill according to God through
him I glorified, finding you, as I knew, to be imitators of God. For when you
are subject to the bishop as to Jesus Christ, you appear to me to live not
according to man, but according to Jesus Christ who died for us, that
believing in his death you may escape dying.

It is necessary then, as you do, that you do nothing without the bishop, but
be subject also to the presbytery as to the apostles of Jesus Christ our hope,
in whom if we live we shall be found. And the deacons also, being of the
mysteries of Jesus Christ, must in every way please all. For they are not
deacons of foods and drinks, but servants of the church of God. It is
necessary then that they guard against accusations as against fire. Likewise
let all respect the deacons as Jesus Christ, as also the bishop being a type
of the Father, and the presbyters as the council of God and as the band of the
apostles; without these the church is not called. Concerning which I am
persuaded that you are so. For I received the exemplar of your love and have
it with me in your bishop, whose very demeanor is great discipleship, and his
meekness is power; whom I reckon even the godless respect. Loving you I spare
you, though I could write more intensely about this. I did not think myself
such that, being a condemned man, I should command you as an apostle. I think
many things in God, but I measure myself, lest I perish in boasting. For now I
must fear more and not attend to those who puff me up.
For those who speak to me scourge me. For I love to suffer, but I do not know
if I am worthy. For the envy is not apparent to many, but it wars against me
more. I need then meekness, in which the ruler of this age is dissolved. Can I
not write to you the heavenly things? But I fear lest I put harm before you as
being infants; and pardon me, lest not being able to receive them you be
strangled. For even I, not because I am bound and can understand the heavenly
things and the angelic placements and the archontic constitutions, "things
visible and invisible", am I already for this a disciple. For many things are
lacking to us, that we may not lack God. I exhort you then, not I, but the
love of Jesus Christ: use only Christian food, and abstain from alien plant,
which is heresy. They intertwine Jesus Christ with themselves, making
themselves credible, as giving a deadly drug with honeyed wine, which the
ignorant takes gladly, in evil pleasure, his dying.
Guard yourselves then against such men. And this will be for you if you are
not puffed up and are

inseparable from God Jesus Christ and from the bishop and from the ordinances
of the apostles. He who is within the altar is clean; but he who is outside
the altar is not clean; that is, he who does anything without bishop and
presbytery and deacons, this one is not clean in his conscience. Not because I
knew any such thing among you, but I am guarding you in advance, being my
beloved, foreseeing the ambushes of the devil. You then, taking up meekness of
feeling, recreate yourselves in faith, which is the flesh of the Lord, and in
love, which is the blood of Jesus Christ. Let none of you have anything
against his neighbor. Do not give occasions to the nations, lest through a few
fools the multitude in God be blasphemed. For woe, through whom "my name" is
"blasphemed" among some in vanity. Become deaf then, when anyone speaks to you
apart from Jesus Christ, who was of the race of David, of Mary, who was truly
born, both ate and drank, was truly persecuted under Pontius Pilate, was truly
crucified and died, the heavenly and earthly and under-earthly looking on; who
also was truly raised from the dead, his Father having raised him, who in like
manner will also so raise us who believe in him, his Father, in Christ Jesus,
without whom we do not have the true life. But if, as some who are atheists,
that is unbelievers, say, that he suffered in appearance, they themselves
being the appearance, why am I bound, and why do I also pray to fight with
beasts? I die then for nothing. Then I also lie against the Lord.

Flee then the evil offshoots that beget death-bearing fruit, of which if
anyone tastes, he dies at once; for these are not the Father's planting. For
if they were, they would appear as branches of the cross and their fruit would
be incorruptible; through which in his passion he calls you, being his
members. A head then cannot be born without members, God promising a unity,
which is himself. I greet you from Smyrna together with the churches of God
present with me, who have refreshed me in every way, in flesh and in spirit.
My bonds exhort you, which I bear about for the sake of Jesus Christ, asking
to attain to God; remain in your concord and in the prayer with one another.
For it is fitting for you, each one, and especially the presbyters, to refresh
the bishop unto the honor of the Father of Jesus Christ and of the apostles.

I pray that you hear me in love, lest I be a testimony against you by having
written. And pray also concerning me, who need the love from you in the mercy
of God, that I may be counted worthy to attain the lot which I am surrounded
with, lest I be found unapproved.

The love of the Smyrnaeans and of the Ephesians greets you. Remember in your
prayers the church in Syria, from which I am not even worthy to be named,
being last of those there. Farewell in Jesus Christ, being subject to the
bishop as to the commandment, likewise also to the presbytery. And you, man by
man, love one another in an undivided heart. My spirit is dedicated for you
not only now, but also when I attain to God. For I am still under danger; but
the Father is faithful in Jesus Christ to fulfill my request and yours, in
whom may you be found blameless.

IGNATIUS TO THE ROMANS
Ignatius, who is also Theophorus, to the church that has been shown mercy in
the majesty of the Most High Father and of Jesus Christ his only Son, beloved
and enlightened in the will of him who willed all things that are, according
to the faith and love of Jesus Christ our God, which also presides in the
place of the region of the Romans, worthy of God,

worthy of honor, worthy of blessing, worthy of praise, worthy of success,
worthy of holiness, and presiding in love, named from Christ, named from the
Father, which I also greet in the name of Jesus Christ, Son of the Father; to
those united in flesh and spirit to every commandment of his, filled with the
grace of God undividedly and filtered from every alien color, abundant
blameless greeting in Jesus Christ our God.
Since praying to God I have attained to see your faces worthy of God, as I was
asking even more to receive; for being bound in Christ Jesus I hope to greet
you, if it be the will that I be counted worthy to be unto the end. For the
beginning is well managed, if I attain grace to receive my lot unhindered. For
I fear your love, lest it wrong me. For for you it is easy to do what you
want; but for me it is hard to attain to God, if you do not spare me. For I do
not want you to please men, but to please God, as indeed you do please him.
For neither shall I ever have such a time to attain to God, nor you, if you
are silent, have a better work to be inscribed upon. For if you are silent
from me, I shall become God's; but if you are in love with my flesh, I shall
again be a runner. Grant me nothing more than to be poured out as a libation
to God, while an altar is still ready, that becoming a chorus in love you may
sing to the Father in Christ Jesus, that God has counted the bishop of Syria
worthy to be found unto the west, having sent for him from the east. It is
good to set from the world toward God, that I may rise unto him. You never
envied anyone; you taught others.

And I want those things also to be firm which you command when you disciple.
Only ask for me power, both from within and from without, that I may not only
speak but also will, that I may not only be called a Christian but also be
found one. For if I am found, I can also be called one, and then be faithful,
when I am not apparent to the world. Nothing that appears is good. For our God
Jesus Christ, being in the Father, appears more. Christianity is not a work of
persuasion, but of greatness, when it is hated by the world. For I write to
all the churches and I command all that I die willingly for God, if you do not
hinder me. I exhort you, do not become an untimely goodwill to me. Let me be
food of beasts, through which it is possible to attain to God. I am God's
wheat, and I am ground by the teeth of beasts, that I may be found pure bread
of Christ. Rather flatter the beasts, that they may become my tomb and leave
nothing of my body, lest when I have fallen asleep I become a burden to
anyone. Then I shall be a disciple truly of Jesus Christ, when the world will
not even see my body. Entreat Christ for me, that through these instruments I
may be found a sacrifice to God. I do not command you as Peter and Paul. They
were apostles, I a condemned man; they free, but I until now a slave. But if I
suffer, I shall become a freedman of Jesus Christ and I shall rise in him
free. Now I am learning, being bound, to desire nothing. From Syria to Rome I
fight with beasts, by land and sea, night and day, bound to ten leopards,
which is a military company; who even when well treated become worse. But in
their wrongs I am discipled more, "but I am not thereby justified".

May I have joy of the beasts prepared for me, and I pray that they may be
found ready for me; I will even flatter them to devour me promptly, not as
they have not touched some, being cowardly. And even if they themselves,
unwilling, do not want it, I will force them. Pardon me; I know what is
profitable for me. Now I am beginning to be a disciple. Let nothing of things
visible and invisible envy me, that I may attain to Jesus Christ. Fire and
cross and packings of beasts, cuttings, divisions, scatterings of bones,
chopping of limbs, grindings of the whole body, evil punishments of the devil,
let them come upon me, only that I may attain to Jesus Christ. Nothing of the
pleasant things of the

world will profit me, nor the kingdoms of this age. It is good for me to die
unto Christ Jesus, rather than to reign over the ends of the earth. I seek him
who died for us; I want him who rose for us. The birth-pangs are upon me.
Pardon me, brothers; do not hinder me from living, do not wish me to die; do
not give to the world him who wants to be God's, nor deceive him with matter;
let me receive pure light; arriving there I shall be a man. Permit me to be an
imitator of the passion of my God. If anyone has him in himself, let him
understand what I want, and let him sympathize with me, knowing the things
that constrain me. The ruler of this age wants to snatch me and to corrupt my
mind toward my God. Let none then of you who are present help him; rather
become mine, that is, God's. Do not speak Jesus Christ and desire the world.
Let envy not dwell in you. Not even if I myself when present exhort you, be
persuaded by me; rather be persuaded by these things which I write to you. For
I write to you living, longing to die. My desire has been crucified, and there
is not in me a fire that loves matter; but water living and speaking in me,
saying to me from within: Come to the Father. I do not take pleasure in food
of corruption nor in the pleasures of this life. I want the bread of God,
which is the flesh of Jesus Christ, "of the seed of David", and I want drink,
his blood, which is love incorruptible. I no longer want to live according to
men. And this will be, if you want it. Wish it, that you also may be wished
for. Through a few letters I ask you; believe me; and Jesus Christ will make
these things clear to you, that I speak truly; the mouth without lie, in which
the Father spoke truly. Ask concerning me, that I may attain. I have not
written to you according to flesh, but according to the mind of God. If I
suffer, you wanted it; if I am rejected, you hated me. Remember in your prayer
the church in Syria, which has God as shepherd in my place. Jesus Christ alone
will oversee it, and your love. But I am ashamed to be named from them; for I
am not even worthy, being last of them and an untimely birth, but I have been
shown mercy to be someone, if I attain to God. My spirit greets you, and the
love of the churches that received me in the name of Jesus Christ, not as one
passing through. For even those not belonging to me on the way according to
the flesh went before me city by city. And I write these things to you from
Smyrna through the Ephesians who are worthy of blessing. And Crocus, the name
dear to me, is also with me together with many others. Concerning those who
went before me from Syria to Rome to the glory of God I believe you have come
to know, to whom also make clear that I am near. For all are worthy of God and
of you; whom it is fitting for you to refresh in every way. And I wrote these
things to you on the ninth before the Kalends of September. Farewell unto the
end in the endurance of Jesus Christ.

IGNATIUS TO THE PHILADELPHIANS
Ignatius, who is also Theophorus, to the church of God the Father and of the
Lord Jesus Christ which is in Philadelphia of Asia, shown mercy and
established in the concord of God and rejoicing in the passion of our Lord
undividedly and fully persuaded in his resurrection in all mercy, which I
greet in the blood of Jesus Christ, which is joy eternal and abiding,
especially if they are in one with the bishop and the presbyters and deacons
with him, approved in the mind of Jesus Christ, whom according to his own will
he has established in firmness by his Holy Spirit. Which bishop I have known
to have obtained the ministry that belongs to the common, not from himself nor
through men nor according to empty glory, but in the love of God the Father
and of the Lord Jesus Christ; whose fairness I have been struck by, who being
silent can do more than those who speak empty things. For he is attuned to the
commandments as a lyre to strings. Therefore

my soul blesses his mind toward God, recognizing it to be virtuous and
perfect, his unmovedness and his freedom from anger in all the fairness of the
living God.
Children then of the light of truth, flee division and the evil teachings; but
where the shepherd is, there follow as sheep. For many wolves, credible, by
evil pleasure take captive the God-runners; but in your unity they will have
no place. Abstain from the evil plants, which Jesus Christ does not cultivate,
because they are not the Father's planting. Not that I found a division among
you, but a filtering. For as many as are of God and of Jesus Christ, these are
with the bishop; and as many as repent and come to the unity of the church,
these also shall be of God, that they may be living according to Jesus Christ.
"Be not deceived, my brothers;" if anyone follows a schismatic, "he does not
inherit the kingdom of God"; if anyone walks in an alien mind, this one does
not consent to the passion. Be eager then to use one eucharist; for there is
one flesh of our Lord Jesus Christ and one cup unto unity of his blood, one
altar, as one bishop together with the presbytery and deacons, my
fellow-servants; that whatever you do you may do according to God. My
brothers, I am greatly poured out in loving you, and overjoyed I make you
secure; yet not I, but Jesus Christ, in whom being bound I fear more, as still
being unfinished; but your prayer will finish me unto God, that I may attain
in the lot in which I was shown mercy, having fled to the gospel as to the
flesh of Jesus and to the apostles as to the presbytery of the church. And let
us love the prophets also, because they also proclaimed unto the gospel and
hoped in him and awaited him, in whom also having believed they were saved,
being in the unity of Jesus Christ, saints worthy of love and of wonder,
witnessed by Jesus Christ and numbered together in the gospel of the common
hope. But if anyone interprets Judaism to you, do not hear him. For it is
better to hear Christianity from a man who has circumcision, than Judaism from
an uncircumcised man. But if both do not speak about Jesus Christ, these are
to me pillars and tombs of the dead, on which only names of men are written.
Flee then the evil crafts and ambushes of the ruler of this age, lest being
oppressed by his mind you grow weak in love; but all of you come to the same
place in an undivided heart. And I give thanks to my God that I have a good
conscience among you, and no one has it to boast, either secretly or openly,
that I burdened anyone in small or in great. And I pray for all in whom I
spoke, that they may not acquire it as a testimony against themselves. For
even if some wanted to deceive me according to the flesh, yet the spirit is
not deceived, being from God. For it "knows where it comes from and where it
goes", and it convicts the hidden things. I cried out being among you, I spoke
with a great voice, with the voice of God: Give heed to the bishop and to the
presbytery and deacons.

But those who suspected me of saying these things as having known beforehand
the division of some: but my witness is he in whom I am bound, that I did not
know it from human flesh. But the spirit was proclaiming, saying these things:
Do nothing without the bishop, keep your flesh as a temple of God, love the
unity, flee the divisions, become imitators of Jesus Christ, as also he of his
Father. I then was doing my own as a man perfectly joined unto unity. But
where there is division and anger, God does not dwell. To all then who repent
the Lord grants forgiveness, if they repent unto the unity of God and the
council of the bishop. I believe in the grace of Jesus Christ, who will loose
from you every bond. And I exhort you to do nothing according to factiousness,
but according to the learning of Christ. For I heard some saying that, if I do
not find it in the archives, I do not believe it in the gospel; and when I
said to them that it is written, they answered me that it is in question. But
for me the archives are Jesus Christ, the untouched archives his cross

and his death and his resurrection and the faith that is through him, in which
I want to be justified in your prayer. The priests also are good, but better
is the high priest who has been entrusted with the holy of holies, who alone
has been entrusted with the hidden things of God; he being the door of the
Father, through which Abraham and Isaac and Jacob and the prophets and the
apostles and the church enter. All these things unto the unity of God. But the
gospel has something exceptional, the presence of the Savior, our Lord Jesus
Christ, his passion and the resurrection. For the beloved prophets proclaimed
unto him; but the gospel is the completion of incorruption. All things
together are good, if you believe in love. Since according to your prayer and
according to the compassion you have in Christ Jesus, it was announced to me
that the church in Antioch of Syria is at peace, it is fitting for you as a
church of God to elect a deacon to go there as God's embassy, to congratulate
them on having come to the same place and to glorify the Name. Blessed in
Jesus Christ is he who shall be counted worthy of such a ministry, and you
shall be glorified. For you who are willing it is not impossible for the name
of God, as also the nearest churches sent bishops, and others presbyters and
deacons. Concerning Philo the deacon from Cilicia, a man who has been
witnessed, who even now serves me in the word of God together with Rheus
Agathopus, an elect man, who follows me from Syria having said farewell to
life, who also bear witness to you, I also give thanks to God for you, that
you received them, as the Lord also will you; but those who dishonored them,
may they be redeemed in the grace of Jesus Christ.
The love of the brothers in Troas greets you, from where I also write to you
through Burrhus, sent together with me from the Ephesians and Smyrnaeans for a
word of honor. The Lord Jesus Christ will honor them, in whom they hope in
flesh, soul, spirit, faith, love, concord. Farewell in Christ Jesus, our
common hope.

IGNATIUS TO THE SMYRNAEANS
Ignatius, who is also Theophorus, to the church of God the Father and of the
beloved Jesus Christ, shown mercy in every gift, filled in faith and love,
lacking in no gift, most God-befitting and holy-bearing, which is in Smyrna of
Asia, abundant greeting in blameless spirit and word of God. I glorify Jesus
Christ the God who has so made you wise; for I understood you to be perfectly
joined in unmoved faith, as if nailed to the cross of the Lord Jesus Christ in
flesh and in spirit and established in love in the blood of Christ, fully
persuaded unto our Lord, truly being of the race of "David according to the
flesh", Son of God according to the will and power of God, truly born of a
virgin, baptized by John, that all righteousness might be fulfilled by him;
truly under Pontius Pilate and Herod the tetrarch nailed for us in flesh, from
whose fruit we are from his God-blessed passion, that he might "raise a
standard" unto the ages through the resurrection unto his saints and faithful,
whether among Jews or among nations, in one body of his church.

For all these things he suffered for us, that we might be saved; and he truly
suffered, as also he truly raised himself, not as some unbelievers say, that
he suffered in appearance, they themselves being the appearance; and according
as they think, it shall also happen to them, being incorporeal and demonic.
For I also after the resurrection know him in flesh and believe him to be. And
when he came to those about Peter, he said to them: "Take, handle me and see,
that I am not a bodiless demon." And immediately they touched him and
believed, being mixed with his flesh and the spirit. For this reason they also

despised death, and they were found above death. And after the resurrection he
ate with them and drank with them as one in flesh, although spiritually united
to the Father. And I exhort you in these things, beloved, knowing that you
also are so. But I guard you in advance from the beasts in human form, whom
you must not only not receive, but if possible not even meet, but only pray
for them, if somehow they may repent, which is hard. And of this Jesus Christ
has authority, our true life. For if these things were done by our Lord in
appearance, I too am bound in appearance. And why have I also given myself
over to death, to fire, to sword, to beasts? But near a sword near God, among
beasts among God; only in the name of Jesus Christ. Unto suffering with him I
endure all things, he strengthening me who became the perfect man. Whom some,
not knowing, deny, or rather have been denied by him, being advocates of death
rather than of the truth; whom neither the prophecies persuaded nor the law of
Moses, nor even until now the gospel, nor our own sufferings man by man. For
they think the same also about us. For what does it profit me if someone
praises me but blasphemes my Lord, not confessing him as flesh-bearing? and he
who does not say this has denied him perfectly, being a corpse-bearer. And
their names, being unbelieving, I have not thought good to write. But may it
not even be granted me to remember them, until they repent unto the passion,
which is our resurrection. Let no one be deceived; even the heavenly things
and the glory of the angels and the rulers visible and invisible, if they do
not believe in the blood of Christ, for them too there is judgment. He who can
receive it, "let him receive it". Let place puff no one up; for the whole is
faith and love, to which nothing is preferred. But take note of those who hold
heterodox opinions about the grace of Jesus Christ which has come to us, how
they are contrary to the mind of God.

They care nothing about love, not about a widow, not about an orphan, not
about one who is afflicted, not about one bound or loosed, not about one
hungry or thirsty. They abstain from eucharist and prayer, because they do not
confess the eucharist to be the flesh of our Savior Jesus Christ, which
suffered for our sins, which the Father in his kindness raised. Those then who
contradict the gift of God die in their disputing. And it would have been
profitable for them to love, that they might also rise. It is fitting then to
abstain from such men and not to speak about them either privately or in
common, but to give heed to the prophets, and especially to the gospel, in
which the passion has been made clear to us and the resurrection has been
completed. And flee the divisions as the beginning of evils. All of you follow
the bishop, as Jesus Christ the Father, and the presbytery as the apostles;
and respect the deacons as God's commandment. Let no one do anything belonging
to the church without the bishop. Let that eucharist be considered secure
which is under the bishop, or him to whom he may entrust it. Wherever the
bishop appears, there let the multitude be, just as wherever Christ Jesus is,
there is the catholic church. It is not permitted without the bishop either to
baptize or to hold a love-feast; but whatever he approves, this is also
well-pleasing to God, that everything that is done may be safe and secure. It
is reasonable then to sober up and, while we still have time, to repent unto
God. It is good to know God and bishop. He who honors the bishop is honored by
God; he who does anything secretly from the bishop serves the devil. Let all
things then abound to you in grace; for you are worthy. You have refreshed me
in every way, and Jesus Christ you. You loved me absent and present. May God
repay you, through whom if you endure all things you shall attain to him.

Philo and Rheus Agathopus, who followed me in a word of God, you did well in
receiving as deacons of Christ God; who also give thanks to the Lord

for you, that you refreshed them in every way. Nothing of yours shall perish.
My spirit is your ransom, and my bonds, which you did not treat with pride nor
were ashamed of. Neither shall the perfect faith, Jesus Christ, be ashamed of
you. Your prayer went forth upon the church in Antioch of Syria, from where
being bound with most God-befitting bonds I greet all, not being worthy to be
from there, being last of them; but by will I was counted worthy, not from
conscience, but from the grace of God, which I pray may be given me perfect,
that in your prayer I may attain to God. In order then that your work may be
perfect both on earth and in heaven, it is fitting unto the honor of God that
your church elect a God-ambassador, to go as far as Syria to congratulate
them, that they are at peace and have received their own greatness and their
own corporate body has been restored to them. It seemed to me then a worthy
thing to send one of yours with a letter, that he may glorify with them the
fair weather that has come to them according to God, and that it was already
attaining a harbor by your prayer. Being perfect, think also perfect things.
For God is ready to grant to you who want to do well.

The love of the brothers in Troas greets you, from where I also write to you
through Burrhus, whom you sent with me together with the Ephesians, your
brothers, who has refreshed me in every way; and would that all imitated him,
being an exemplar of God's ministry. Grace will repay him in every way. I
greet the bishop worthy of God and the God-befitting presbytery and my
fellow-servant deacons and each man by man and all in common in the name of
Jesus Christ and in his flesh and blood, in passion and resurrection, in
fleshly and spiritual unity of God and of you. Grace to you, mercy, peace,
endurance always. I greet the households of my brothers with wives and
children, and the virgins called widows.
Farewell to me in the power of the Spirit. Philo, who is with me, greets you.
I greet the household of Tavia, whom I pray to be established in faith and
love, fleshly and spiritual. I greet Alce, the name dear to me, and Daphnus
the incomparable and Eutecnus and all by name. Farewell in the grace of God.

IGNATIUS TO POLYCARP
Ignatius, who is also Theophorus, to Polycarp, bishop of the church of the
Smyrnaeans, or rather overseen by God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ,
abundant greeting.
Welcoming your mind in God, established as upon an unmoved rock, I glorify
exceedingly, having been counted worthy of your blameless face, of which may I
have joy in God. I exhort you in the grace with which you are clothed, to add
to your course and to exhort all, that they may be saved. Vindicate your place
with all care, fleshly and spiritual; take thought for the unity, than which
nothing is better. Bear all, as also the Lord you; endure all in love, as
indeed you do. Be at leisure for unceasing prayers; ask for more understanding
than you have; watch, possessing a sleepless spirit. Call upon them man by man
in a God-like character; bear the diseases of all as a perfect athlete; where
there is more toil, much gain. If you love good disciples, you have no grace;
rather subject the more pestilent in meekness. Not every wound is healed by
the same plaster. Stop the inflammations with fomentations. Become prudent as
a serpent in all things and innocent always as the dove. For this reason you
are fleshly and spiritual, that you may flatter the things that appear before
your face; but ask that the invisible things be manifested to you, that you
may lack nothing and abound in every gift. The season demands you, as pilots
winds and as a storm-tossed man a harbor, unto attaining to God. Be sober as
God's athlete; the prize is incorruption and life

eternal, about which you also are persuaded. In all things I am your ransom,
and my bonds, which you loved. Let those who seem to be credible and who teach
otherwise not strike you with panic. Stand firm as an anvil when struck. It
belongs to a great athlete to be beaten and to conquer. And especially for
God's sake we must endure all things, that he also may endure us. Become more
diligent than you are. Learn the seasons. Await him who is above season, the
timeless, the invisible, who for us is visible, the intangible, the
impassible, who for us is passible, who in every way endured for us. Let
widows not be neglected; after the Lord you be their caretaker. Let nothing be
done without your mind, nor do you anything without God, which indeed you do
not; be steady. Let gatherings be more frequent; seek all by name.
Do not treat slaves and slave-women with pride; but let them not be puffed up
either, but let them serve more to the glory of God, that they may attain a
better freedom from God. Let them not desire to be set free from the common
fund, lest they be found slaves of desire. Flee evil crafts, or rather make
speech about these things. Speak to my sisters, to love the Lord and to be
content with their companions in flesh and spirit. Likewise charge my brothers
also in the name of Jesus Christ to love their companions as "the Lord the
church". If anyone is able to remain in purity unto the honor of the Lord's
flesh, let him remain without boasting. If he boasts, he has perished, and if
it become known except to the bishop, he is corrupted. And it is fitting for
those who marry and those who are given in marriage to make the union with
the mind of the bishop, that the marriage may be according to the Lord and not
according to desire. Let all things be done unto the honor of God. Give heed
to the bishop, that God also may to you. I am the ransom of those subject to
the bishop,
presbyters, deacons; and with them may it be granted me to have my portion in
God. Labor together with one another, contend together, run together, suffer
together, sleep together, rise together, as God's stewards and assessors and
servants. Please him for whom you campaign, from whom also you receive the
wages; let none of you be found a deserter. Let your baptism remain as
weapons, faith as a helmet, love as a spear, endurance as full armor; let your
deposits be your works, that you may receive your accepted sums as worthy. Be
long-suffering then with one another in meekness, as God with you. May I have
joy of you always. Since the church in Antioch of Syria is at peace, as was
made clear to me, through your prayer, I also became more cheerful in God's
freedom from care, if through suffering I attain to God, that I may be found
in your resurrection a disciple. It is fitting, Polycarp most blessed of God,
to hold a most God-befitting council and to elect someone whom you have as
greatly beloved and unslothful, who will be able to be called a God-runner; to
count this one worthy to go into Syria and glorify your unslothful love unto
the glory of God. A Christian has no authority over himself, but is at leisure
for God. This work is God's and yours, when you complete it.

For I believe in the grace, that you are ready for well-doing that belongs to
God. Knowing the intensity of your truth, I have exhorted you through a few
letters. Since then I was not able to write to all the churches because I am
suddenly sailing from Troas to Neapolis, as the will commands, you will write
to the churches in front, as possessing the mind of God, that they also may do
the same, some able to send on foot, and others letters through those sent by
you, that you may be glorified by an eternal work, as being worthy. I greet
all by name, and the wife of Epitropus with her whole household and the
children. I greet Attalus my beloved. I greet the one who is about to be
counted worthy to go into Syria. Grace shall be with him always and with
Polycarp who sends him. I pray that you fare well always in God

our Jesus Christ, in whom may you remain in the unity of God and of the
episcopate. I greet Alce, the name dear to me. Farewell in the Lord.

TO GNATIUS, MARY FROM CASSOBOLA.

Mary, a proselyte of Jesus Christ, to Ignatius Theophorus, most blessed bishop
of the apostolic church at Antioch, beloved in God the Father and Jesus,
greeting and to fare well. We always pray for you the joy and health in him.
Since, O admirable one, among us also Christ has been made known to be Son of
the living God and in later times to have become man through the virgin Mary
of the seed of David and Abraham according to the voices about him spoken
beforehand by him through the chorus of the prophets, for this reason we
exhort, asking that there be sent to us from your understanding Maris our
companion, bishop of our Neapolis by the Zarbos, and Eulogius, presbyter of
Cassobola, that we may not be deserted of the patrons of the divine word, as
somewhere also Moses says: Let the Lord God look out a man who shall lead this
people, and the congregation of the Lord shall not be as sheep who have no
shepherd. And concerning the men written above being young, fear nothing, O
blessed one. For I want you to know that they think little of flesh and take
no account of its passions in themselves, flashing with the gray of priesthood
in fresh youth. And stir up your reasoning through his Spirit given you by God
through Christ, and you will know that Samuel while a little child was called
the Seer, and being enrolled in the chorus of the prophets he convicts the old
man Eli of lawlessness, because he had preferred stricken sons to God the
cause of all and had left unpunished those playing at the priesthood and
acting wantonly toward the people. And Daniel the wise, being young, judged
certain crude old men, showing them to be accursed and not elders, and that
being Jews by race they were Canaanites by manner. And Jeremiah, declining
because of youth the prophecy being put into his hands by God, hears: Do not
say that I am younger, because to all to whom I send you, you shall go, and
according to all that I command you, you shall speak, because I am with you.
And Solomon the wise, being twelve years old, understood the great question of
the women's ignorance concerning their own children, so that all the people
were astonished at such wisdom of the child and feared him not as a boy but as
a perfect man. And the riddles of the queen of the Ethiopians, having a flood
like the streams of the Nile, he so solved that she who was so wise was beside
herself. And Josiah the God-beloved, still speaking almost inarticulate
sounds, convicts those possessed by the evil spirit, how they are
false-speakers and people-deceivers, and he uncovers the deceit of demons and
puts to shame the gods that are not and slaughters those consecrated to them
with impunity, and he overturns their altars and defiles their sanctuaries
with dead remains and tears down precincts and cuts down the groves and
smashes the pillars and digs up the tombs of the impious, that not even a sign
of the wicked may still exist; so zealous was he of piety and a punisher of
the impious, still stammering with the tongue. And David the prophet together
with being king, the root of salvation according to the flesh, is anointed as
a boy by Samuel to be king; for he himself says somewhere that I was small
among my brothers and younger in my father's house. And time will fail me if I
should want to track down all who in youth were well-pleasing to God and were
entrusted by God with prophecy and priesthood and kingship. And for a reminder
the things said are also sufficient.

But I entreat you, lest I seem to you superfluous and showy; for not teaching
you, but reminding my father in God, I have set forth these words; for I know
my own measures and I do not stretch myself alongside such as you. I greet
your holy clergy and your Christ-loving people shepherded under your care. All
the faithful among us address you; pray that I be in health according to God,
O blessed shepherd.
