On the harlot and on the Pharisee.
=61.709=
Today the Pharisee invited the Master to a meal, mixing honor with insult; but
the harlot entertained him with faith. For while he was in the house of Simon,
behold a woman, a harlot. A mad figure, a harlot woman in the city, the war
within the walls, the dart not of iron, the birdlime of the young, the
whetstone of desire, the waking of the passions, the tavern-trade of the body,
the unprofitable commerce, the loss-making business, which procures death both
for the seller and for the buyer, the net of youth, the uncovered snare.
For the eye of a harlot is a snare of sinners; the storehouse of
licentiousness, she who sells herself and leads the buyers into slavery, the
many-headed lioness, the stench of the city, the plague that creeps upon every
sense, the hook of the eyes, the death that is carried about, the marketplace
of loss, the desire of destruction, the smell of death, the much-putrid
gangrene. Behold a woman in the city, the plague of the inhabitants, the
tyranny of marriages, the overthrow of modesty, the battle of man and wife,
the wasteful table, the emptying of purses, the outflow of money, the
destruction of labors, the winepress of the heirs, which the treaders,
treading, danced their own stripping. Behold a woman in the city, who was a
sinner. But that sinner ran to righteousness, and as thorns in fire she
consumed the sin
=61.710= s. The thicket has become a paradise, the lioness a lamb, the stench
myrrh, destruction a treasure, the crow a dove, the harlot a virgin, sister of
Christ, bride of the Word. For standing behind Jesus, the disciple of the
woman with the issue of blood, she wet the feet with tears, and wiped them
with her hair, and with her lips she kissed the footprints, and anointed them
with costly myrrh.
All the weapons of sin she brought to the king, asking for peace; and she
brought the full armor of evils to the feet of the Master. And since formerly
with forced tears she had melted many into passion, now with true tears having
moved the Master she loosed herself; and she who had plaited her hair with
evil artifice, and had piled it up unseemly into a tower, wiped the feet of
Christ with her hair as with linen, not whitening the Master, but sanctifying
herself. For she was confessing through the hairs how many she had hooked
through them and fished unto death. And she anointed with costly myrrh. Why?
Because formerly, being perfumed, she had mired many; likewise having used the
spices badly, she used to recast her lovers into scent-tracking dogs. But now
having used the myrrh well, and having brought the myrrh to the True One, she
washed away the satanic unguents. Then as the Lord received her doing these
things, Simon was whispering, becoming an accountant of the Master's goodness.
The Master says to him: Simon, who =61.711= invited me for honor, and
dishonored me, as I did not expect; There were two debtors to a certain
lender, you and she, not creditors of me nor debtors, but debt-owers, being
doubly liable, because having received both the natural loan and the principal
of the law, you fell away from the benefactor in both. The one owed five
hundred denarii, - you the Pharisee, who have the heavier and more numerous
sins of the soul; Since the tax-collectors and the harlots go before you into
the kingdom of the heavens; - but the other fifty, the liable manner of the
harlot.
And they themselves not having anything to repay through achievements of good
deeds, he freely granted to both, having received faith and having torn the
bond. Tell, who will love him more? Then the Pharisee, I suppose, that he to
whom he granted the more. And the liable one was becoming a judge of himself,
and found the Master a praiser, saying: Rightly =61.712= you have judged. Hear
then: Do you see this woman who owed less than you, and who has brought more
guest-gifts and presents? do you see this woman who became the destruction of
many? the living tomb; the death that is carried about? I entered your house;
water, the cheap thing set before all, you did not bring upon my feet; but she
brought springs of tears from her eyes to my feet.
You did not give me a kiss; but she spread her lips under my feet. You did not
anoint my head with oil, as one shown mercy by me; but she anointed the body
with myrrh, and with faith she anointed my divinity. For this reason I say to
you, her many sins have been forgiven; that what was said in the psalms might
be fulfilled, "You have forgiven the iniquities of your people," and what was
proclaimed by John, "Behold the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the
world." To him be the glory unto the ages. Amen.
