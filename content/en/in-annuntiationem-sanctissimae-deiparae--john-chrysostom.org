On the Annunciation of the all-holy Theotokos.
=60.755=
Let us keep today a feast of royal mysteries; the whole creation, gathered
together, rejoices. Heaven by a star was leading those who hastened to the
festival; earth through the shepherds was running to the worship. Isaiah was
walking with the swift step of prophecy, that he might show the virgin to the
festival. Jeremiah, though declining, yet pointing out the Master himself of
this very festival, saying: This is our God. Zechariah was running down,
bringing joy to those keeping festival, making known the command of the
Master's coming: Rejoice, he says, daughter of Zion; for behold, your king
comes to you meek, mounted on a beast of burden and a young colt. Mary and
Joseph set forth in the manger, as on a display, a pearl of great price. The
magi, seeing and being astonished at the beauty of the nature, announced him
to Herod as a king worthy of the name.
But he, hearing, and hiding by deceit a death-bearing worship, was eager to
see the pearl; yet since he was unworthy, he failed of what he longed for. And
he, as one mocked, was considering =60.756= what he should do. And angels of
bodiless natures, dancing in the festival, were saying: Glory to God in the
highest, and on earth peace. O the strange and paradoxical wonder! The subject
of the feast conquers every tongue that would speak the praises of Mary, to
whom the angel, being sent, cried: Hail, full of grace, the Lord is with you.
But she, rising up, watched the speaker with a sidelong eye; and at the sight
of his appearance she was astonished, yet she was weighing the words in her
own mind, what manner of greeting this might be, saying: O stranger, you
astonish me, speaking with a strange voice. Either you do not know the custom
of those who greet, or you are testing me, whether I am easily taken. The Hail
is common to all who greet; but Full of grace is strange, especially to those
who profess godliness in a life of dignity. Therefore, threatening him with a
stern face and an angry look, she drove the speaker away: Go, go, man, from my
doorway; you have come from the very opening; you do not please me in what you
think. You want to lead me captive, as Eve, the mother of our race. You will
not conquer the beauty of my face, nor the conscience I keep toward my poor
betrothed =60.757=. Before the old man comes, flee from my house; leave what
is here, lest the elder accuse you; for he is jealous. It is better for you to
withdraw; for if he sees you speaking and promising me such things, things
that human nature cannot contain, you will bring distress upon yourself, and
upon me grief and tears. But he kept speaking without relenting: Hail, full of
grace.
But the virgin, seeing the persistence of the one present, opened her hearing,
wishing to learn more exactly the speaker's earnestness, and having heard
Hail, full of grace, she waited for what came after. And he adds: The Lord is
with you. And as soon as the virgin heard The Lord is with you, she quickly
called the speaker further in. You have brought the Lord with you, she says;
come further in, and quickly tell why you have come. And the angel: Behold,
you shall conceive in the womb, and bear a son, and you shall call his name
Emmanuel; this one shall be great, God mighty, Prince of peace, Ruler, Father
of the age to come. And the virgin: Not yet the marriage, not yet the bridal
chamber; and already the birth? You do not know what you are doing. If as a
stranger you need something, take it and go, and do not unseasonably prophesy
what you do not know. Yet even if I shall conceive a son, it will surely be of
Joseph. And the angel: Do not be deceived; this one shall be great.
And the virgin: What sort of great one? My betrothed is poor, and I am very
needy; I have no possessions, we lack money, we are not distinguished in
lineage, and after this we are being dragged to a census. Surely the collector
will exact the didrachma; and you say, He shall be great? Cease, cease
promising such things. And the angel: You do not believe the lesser? Receive
the greater, even if you do not wish it: He shall be called Son of the Most
High. And the virgin: Come out of my house at once, lest Joseph hear and
report it to the priests, and he who is deprived of marriage cut off your neck
with an adze, since you speak near me and in the doorway of my cell. Do you
promise to bring me a bridegroom from heaven? And what nature in heaven is
desirous of marriage? All the heavenly spirits are bodiless; how then shall
the bodiless be mixed with bodies? And the angel: From body and from the
bodiless he shall be called Son of the Most High. And the virgin: Whence then,
as you say, shall he who is to be born of me have a father? And the angel: For
this, virgin, is the exceedingly paradoxical thing: that you shall not know a
man, and you shall bear him of whom I speak to you, and the child shall not be
found an orphan; and he shall come forth from your womb, and he shall not
press aside the gate, and you shall not lose being a virgin, and you shall
nurse what is born of you, and you shall carry the infant in your arms, and
you shall see him seated upon the Cherubim. To this one born of you the Lord
God shall give the throne of David his father, and he shall reign over the
house of Jacob forever, and of his kingdom there shall be no end. And the
virgin: As you did not intend, you have fallen in speaking. Your own mouth
accuses you, and your lips have become your refutation; for a little while ago
you were saying, He who is born shall be called Son of the Most High; and now
you say, Son of David; what is this word? When you wish, you raise my mind to
the heavens; and when you wish again, you bring it down to the earth. Which
saying shall I believe? The first, or the second? For you say, The Lord shall
give him the throne of David his father, and of his kingdom there shall be no
end.
None of the kings has been immortal, none has escaped the common end; death
removes all, as a master; the worms call all to account; none of the kings has
escaped the gate of Hades; it holds all, it guards all in tombs; none has
leapt over its nets, none has escaped its snares; not Saul, who first
inaugurated the diadem of the kingdom; not David, who was manifested by the
horn; not Samuel, the father of kings, who by oil made known the worthy and
thrust away the unworthy; not Solomon the most wise, who by a sword exposed
the harlot's lies; not Hezekiah, who by tears overturned the sentence of
death. And how shall my son reign forever? And the angel: For this, Mary, is
the strange mystery: that all have died, and your son shall conquer death,
shall raise the dead, and shall raise those who have died, shall open =60.758=
tombs, shall crush the bars of Tartarus, and shall raise many bodies of those
who have fallen asleep. For the Immortal, born of the Immortal, shall put
death to death by his own death. Therefore do not disbelieve any longer, lest
you come under accusation. For he of whom I speak to you has already leapt
into your womb through your hearing, performing the dedication of your belly.
And the virgin: That I may agree to these things, first give me assurance: how
is he who is to be born of me, as you say, both Son of the Most High and son
of David, and to exist without a father? And how have you proclaimed the two
fathers? And the angel: First learn, and then contradict; when I say to you,
Son of the Most High, I have announced him who was begotten before the ages
without passion from the Father; and when I say to you, Son of David, I have
said it because you are of the father David. Having therefore received the
assurance, keep my words; for I too am a servant of him whom you are about to
bear. Having said these things to the virgin, the angel left her. And she was
reasoning with herself, saying: Shall I announce it to Joseph, or rather hide
the mystery? If he who came has lied thus far, I stir up against myself the
tongue of the old man.
And as the virgin was considering these things, the womb, swelling, was
advancing, and the infant was dancing within. And Mary was considering this,
and having thrown countless coverings over her belly, she wished to escape
Joseph's notice; but she could not hide a city set upon a mountain. The
showing of the womb pricked Joseph, and with a sidelong eye he was weighing
the virgin's belly. And he wanted to insult her, and he was forced to be
silent; he wanted to insult her, as one who had sold the treasures of
virginity; and her former life hindered him. So then he was tormenting his own
soul with afflictions, saying: Woe is me, what shall I do? The face of a
virgin, the bearing of a virgin, the eyes of a virgin, the laughter of a
virgin; and the womb not of a virgin, but of a mother; the speech of a virgin;
woe is me, woe is me; I can no longer bear to look. Shall I then make the
matter public, shall I convict Mary? For the shame is common. Shall I cry out
with a shout, lest I be called father of the seedless child?
There is no accuser of the girl, since the one being carried in the womb has
no one who sowed the child. Perhaps both to be silent and to speak is
dangerous for me. What then shall I do? Shall I call and ask, or show before
asking? But the Scripture bears witness; rather I will convict. Did she not do
it? Having therefore called her with a stern face, he sat as judge of the
Judge in the womb. Tell me, he says, with exactness what has happened to you;
do not hide from me what has befallen you; no one present is a hearer of the
words; I know how to keep mysteries; no one learns the matter, only give me
assurance. Whence is this that is seen in you? Show me the father of this, and
I will free you from the fault; for you shall be deemed worthy of pardon, as a
woman overcome in desire. And the virgin answered him: If you seek the father
of this one, you will never find him; and if you suppose him an orphan, you
have failed of this also. And Joseph: You have not been able to lie at all,
Mary; for how can he not be an orphan and not have a father? And the virgin:
And if you seek who, Joseph (for you do not know), if I tell you the truth, I
am not believed. You see a womb, and you do not see the Lord hidden; you
consider a belly, and you do not reckon him who was begotten of the Father
from the womb before the morning star, and who lodges in me.
If I tell you what the angel said to me, you will say, You bear witness to
yourself, and your witness is not true. And Joseph: Bring those who heard,
give those who were present, lead truthful witnesses. And the virgin said: If
you seek these things, you will never find them; for one, sent through one to
one, spoke through her alone in private; no one was present; since no one was
going to be father of the one being born. But as the virgin was not believed,
she, suspecting then, came to another thought, Mary saying: The belly betrays
me, my child accuses me, leaping for joy in the womb; why then do I stir
Joseph against myself still more? I will choke my lips with silence, and I
will plead invisibly against the angel: Where are you now, you who spoke Hail,
full of grace? In what places do you lodge? Tell me, that I may come there,
and hang upon your neck, until you come and give Joseph assurance whose son it
is who dances through the chamber of my womb. I cannot bear the old man
looking askance, murmuring; and in what place you linger I do not know; come,
and plead my cause. As Mary was saying these things, the angel quickly comes
to Joseph =60.759=, and having given him assurance he prevented him from
putting the virgin away, saying to Joseph: Do not fear to take Mary your wife.
I speak to you not of the communion of marriage, but of the economy of divine
mysteries. But you are not a father? Do not seek a father on earth, nor a
mother in the heavens. Nature has been set free from all orphanhood; for he
alone exists from the One alone, in solitude, before the ages. Now he alone
from her alone, as he alone knows. Do not lose heart concerning Mary, do not
grieve what is of Mary; do not seek the how, lest the infant do you wrong; do
not be curious about the vessel, lest you lose the treasure within.
Undefiled is the vessel, spotless the treasure, unclouded the alabaster,
seedless the birth, unwedded the maiden, =60.760= unchambered the virgin,
unhewn the rock, closed the garden, the fountain sealed, unsown the field,
unplanted the vine-shoot, inconceivable the birth. But if you disbelieve me
who speak, wait for the time of the birth. And if Mary weeps or groans, as the
other women do, he who is born of Mary is a man, and not God. And Joseph said:
If these things are so, as you have foretold, O archangel, Why did the nations
rage, and the peoples imagine vain things? And the angel said: If you wish to
learn, be taught quickly; since the Lord said to him who is born, You are my
Son, today I have begotten you; for to him belongs glory unto the ages of
ages. Amen.
