* On Glorying in Tribulations

:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

On the apostolic saying that says, "Not only so, but we also glory in
tribulations, knowing that tribulation works patience," and what follows.
=51.155=
αʹ. It is laborious for the husbandman to yoke oxen, and to draw a plow, and
to cut a furrow, and to cast seed, and to endure winter, and to bear frost,
and to drive a trench around, and to beat back the flood of waters coming upon
the seeds, and to make the banks of rivers higher, and through the midst of
the field to cut deeper furrows; but these laborious things, which have toil,
become light and most easy, when in his hopes the farmer sees the standing
grain waving, and the sickle sharpened, and the threshing-floor filled with
sheaves, and the ripe fruit being brought home with much gladness. So also a
helmsman dares wild waves, and often scorns storm and a raging sea, and
unstable winds, and knows how to bear seagoing swells and long voyages, when
he reckons up the cargoes, and the harbors of trade, and sees the boundless
wealth being born from these. So also a soldier bears wounds, and receives
clouds of darts, and endures famine, and frost, and the long campaigns, and
the dangers in the battle-line, reckoning from that quarter the trophies and
the victories and the crowns. But for what reason did I mention these things?
Or what do I want with these examples? I want to bring in from this a
consolation for you toward the hearing, and an encouragement toward the sweats
on behalf of virtue. For if each of the things sai =51.156= d counts the labor
light because of the hope of the things to come, and this although, even if
one of them should be able to attain them, they are dissolved together with
the present life, much more must you endure the hearing of the spiritual
teaching, and bear nobly both the battle-array and the sweats on behalf of
eternal life. For to those men the things of the temporal hope are unclear,
and often, having come to be in expectation only of the good things, they have
thus ended their life, having taken their fill of the hopes, but not having
drawn near to the works themselves, and indeed on this account having endured
the more grievous things. What I mean is this: After those many toils and
sweats the husbandman sometimes, in the very sharpening of the sickle and
preparing for the harvest, when rust has come down, or a multitude of locusts,
or rain beyond measure has occurred, or some other blow has been brought on
from the unevenness of the airs, has gone home with empty hands, having
endured all the toil, but having fallen away from the fruit of the hope. And
the helmsman likewise, rejoicing in the multitude of the cargoes, and having
spread the sails with much pleasure, and having crossed a great sea, often in
the very mouth of the harbor, either a reef having struck, or having dashed
against some underwater rock and crag, or having been struck by some other
such unexpected circumstance, has lost all the freight, and has scarcely saved
his own body naked after those myriad dangers =51.157= . So also the soldier,
having often arrayed himself in countless wars, and having beaten back the
adversaries, and having prevailed over the enemies, and being in the very
expectation of the victory, having exchanged his life has gone away, having
enjoyed nothing at all good from the toils and the dangers. But our things are
not such; for they have hopes eternal, unmoved and firm, and not dissolved
together with this temporal life, but toward that undefiled and blessed

that everlasting life, and not only not plotted against by unevenness
of airs and by the circumstance of unexpected affairs, but not even dissolved
by death itself. And from the hopes themselves one might see even in the very
things that befall their fruit shining, and the recompense being much and
great. Therefore the blessed Paul cried out, saying: Not only so, but we also
glory in tribulations. I beg you, let us not run past what has been said
simply; but since, I know not how, the word has again brought us into the
harbor of the good helmsman Paul, let us take our fill of the saying, which is
brief, but trains us in much philosophy. What then is what has been said, and
what did he hint to us in saying this, Not only so, but we also glory in
tribulations? Let us, if you please, lead the word of teaching a little higher
up, and we shall see much clearness, and the power of the thoughts being born
for us from there. But let the body make no one soft; but let the desire of
the spiritual hearing become in place of dew. For our discourse is about
tribulation and desire of eternal goods, and patience, and the recompense that
comes from these for those who have not given way. What then is the "Not
only"? For he who has said this shows that many other goods also have been
accomplished for us beforehand; and with those he adds this too, that which is
from the tribulation; therefore he himself also said: Not only so, but we also
glory in tribulations. And that what has been said may become clearer, bear
with us a brief hour, while we make the teaching longer in the word. For when
the divine
preaching had been announced by the apostles, and they went about everywhere
in the world sowing the word of piety, and tearing up error from the root, and
overthrowing the ancestral laws of the impious, and driving out all
lawlessness, and cleansing the earth, and commanding them to leap away from
idols and temples and altars and the festivals from those places and the
rites, and to acknowledge the one and only God of all, and to wait for the
hopes to come, and they discoursed concerning Father and Son and Holy Spirit,
and philosophized concerning resurrection, and discoursed concerning the
kingdom of the heavens; on account of these things a grievous war was kindled,
and of all wars the most tyrannical, and everything was full of uproar and
confusion and faction, and all cities, and every people, and house, and
inhabited and uninhabited, since indeed the ancient customs were being shaken,
and the prejudice that had held for so long was being struck, and new
doctrines were being introduced, such as no one had ever heard; toward these
things kin =51.158= gs grew savage, and rulers were displeased, and private
men were troubled, and marketplaces were in uproar, and courts were angry, and
swords were bared, and arms were made ready, and laws were indignant. From
there punishments and chastisements and threats and all things that seem to be
dreadful among men were stirred together. As a raging sea travailing with
grievous wrecks, the affairs of the world were in no better state, a father
then disowning a child on account of piety, and a bride being split from a
mother-in-law, and brothers being divided, and masters being angry against the
household slaves, and nature almost raising faction against itself, and not
only a civil but also a kindred war going through every house. For the word,
entering like a sword, and cutting the diseased from the healthy, made faction
and contention to be much everywhere, and prepared countless burdens and
fights to be raised from every side against those who believed. From this some
were led away to prisons, some to courts, some on the road that leads to
death; and of some the properties were confiscated, and others often fell away
both from fatherland and from life itself, and from every side denser than
snowflakes


the evils stood around; fights within, fears without, from
friends, from strangers, from those themselves bound to one another by nature.
βʹ. Seeing these things, then, the blessed Paul, the trainer of the world, the
teacher of the heavenly doctrines, since the dreadful things were in hand and
being done before the eyes, but the good things were in hopes only and
promises, the kingdom of the heavens, I mean, and resurrection, and the lot of
those goods that surpass all mind and word; but furnaces and frying-pans and
swords and punishments and all kinds of chastisements and deaths not in hopes,
but in experience; and those who were about to contend toward these things had
been newly adopted into the faith from altars and idols and luxury and
wantonness and drunkenness, having not yet practiced thinking anything high
concerning the everlasting life, but rather clinging to the present things;
and it was likely that many of these would be faint-hearted and dissolve, and
give way being besieged each day; see what the sharer of the unspeakable
things does, and attend to the wisdom of Paul. He discourses with them
continually concerning the things to come, and brings the prizes before their
sight, and shows the crowns, anointing them and encouraging them with the
hopes of the eternal goods. And what does he say? For I reckon that the
sufferings of the present time are not worthy to be compared with the glory
that is about to be revealed to us. For what do you tell me, he says, of
wounds and altars and executioners and punishments and famines and
confiscations and poverty and bonds and chains? Set down all you will of the
things that seem dreadful among men, and you say nothing worthy of those
prizes and the crowns and the recompenses. For the one set is dissolved
together with the present life, but the other in the boundless age has no end;
and the one passes by, being temporal, but the other remains continually,
coexisting unaging. Which same thing elsewhere, hinting at this very thing, he
said: =51.159= For the momentary lightness of the tribulation; cutting under
the quality by the quantity, and comforting the burdensome by the time. For
since the things then happening were by nature heavy and burdensome, he cuts
under their burdensomeness by the temporal, saying: For the momentary
lightness of our tribulation works for us an eternal weight of glory far
beyond all measure, while we look not at the things that are seen, but at the
things that are not seen. For the things that are seen are temporal; but the
things that are not seen are eternal. And again, leading them up into the
thought of the greatness of the goods then, he brings in the creation itself
in pain and groaning together over the present wretched things, and greatly
desiring the things to come, as being altogether good, saying thus: For the
creation groans together and travails together until now. For what reason does
it groan? For what reason does it travail? Expecting those goods to come and
desiring the change toward the better. For even the creation itself, he says,
shall be set free from the bondage of corruption into the freedom of the glory
of the children of God. And when you hear that it groans and travails, do not
think it is rational, but learn the manner of Scripture. For when God wishes
to announce something great and good to men through the prophets, he makes
even the lifeless things themselves perceive the greatness of the wonders that
come to be; not because we say the creation perceives, but that he may be able
to set forth the greatness of the wonders from the things that befall men.
This indeed we too are accustomed to say, when something unexpected happens,
[that the city itself grew gloomy, the very ground became sullen;] and of
fearful men also who have a beastlike rage one might say this same thing: that
he shook the very foundations, the very stones trembled at him; not because
the stones truly trembled at him, but that they may be able to set forth the
excess of his beastlike heart and the rage. For this reason also the admirable
prophet David


announcing the goods that happened to the Jews, and the
pleasure that came to them from the freedom of Egypt, said: When Israel went
out of Egypt, the house of Jacob from a barbarian people, Judah became his
sanctuary, Israel his dominion; the sea saw and fled, the Jordan was turned
back; the mountains skipped like rams, and the hills like lambs of sheep, from
the face of the Lord. And this no one has heard as having happened anywhere.
For the sea, and the Jordan, was turned back by the command of God; but the
mountains and the hills did not skip; but, as I said before, wishing to set
forth the excess of the pleasure, and the relief that had been accomplished
for them from the ill-treatment of the Egyptians, he said that even the
lifeless things themselves skipped and leaped at the good things that had
befallen them; as when he wishes to announce something grievous also, born
from our sin, he says: The wine shall mourn, the vine also shall mourn; and
elsewhere: The ways of Sion mourn; but he says that the unfeeling things also
weep: For the wall of the daughter of Sion, bring down tears, he says; and he
says that the earth itself, and Judah, mourn and are drunk with grief, not as
if the elements perceive these things, but, as I said before, each of the
proph =51.160= ets wishing to set forth the excess of the goods furnished to
us from God, and of the punishments brought upon us from our wickedness;
therefore indeed the blessed Paul also himself brings in the creation groaning
and in pain, that he may be able to set forth the greatness of the gifts of
God that will succeed us afterward.
γʹ. But all these things, he says, are in hopes; and the faint-hearted and
wretched man, even now released from idolatry, and not knowing how to
philosophize concerning the things to come, is not much set right by these
words, but seeks to find some consolation also in the present time. For this
reason this wise teacher also, who knows all things, does not encourage only
from the goods to come, but also anoints from the present good things. And
first he tells beforehand the goods that have been accomplished for the world,
which have come to be not in hopes and expectation, but in experience and
enjoyment, which is also the greatest and clearest proof of the things hoped
for and to come, and having stretched out the word much concerning faith, and
having mentioned the patriarch Abraham, and that nature forbade that he should
be a father, yet he hoped and expected, and was persuaded that it would be,
therefore it also came to be, and from there leading them up to this, that one
must never fall down into the weakness of reasonings, but by the greatness of
faith be set upright and rise up and think high things; after these things he
says also the greatness of the things accomplished. And what is this? That the
Son,
he says, the only-begotten, the genuine, the beloved, this one God gave for us
the thankless servants, and those weighed down with countless sins, and
pressed by so many loads of transgressions, he not only released from the
sins, but also made them righteous, having commanded nothing burdensome, nor
laborious, nor grievous, but having sought from us faith only, and he made
them righteous and fashioned them holy, and showed them sons of God, and
appointed them heirs of a kingdom, and made them joint-heirs of the
Only-begotten, and promised resurrection, and incorruption of bodies, and a
lot with angels, surpassing all word and mind, and the sojourn in the heavens,
and converse with him, and from here already he poured out the grace of the
Holy Spirit, and released us from the hold of the devil, and delivered us from
the demons, and dissolved sin, and made the curse vanish, and shattered the
gates of Hades, and opened paradise, not an angel, not an archangel, but he
sent the Only-begotten himself for our salvation, as


he says through
the prophet: Not an ambassador, nor an angel, but the Lord himself saved us.
Are these things not more splendid than countless crowns? that we were
sanctified? that we were justified? that it was through faith? that the
only-begotten Son of God came down from heaven for us? that the Father gave
his beloved for us? that we received Holy Spirit? that it was with all ease?
that we enjoyed unspeakable grace and gift? Having said these things, then,
and having made all clear through brief =51.161= words, he again ended the
word upon the hope. For having said, Being justified therefore by faith, we
have peace toward God through our Lord Jesus Christ, through whom also we have
had the access by faith into this grace in which we stand, he added: And we
glory upon the hope of the glory of God. Since then he said the things that
have come to be, and the things to come; for to be justified, and that the Son
was slain for us, and that through him we were brought to the Father, and that
we enjoyed grace, and gift, and that we were released from sins, and that we
have peace toward God, and that we partook of Holy Spirit, these belong to
things that have come to be; but the things to come were that unspeakable
glory; which itself also he added, saying: In which we stand, and we glory
upon hope of the glory of God; but the hope, as I said before, is not very
sufficient to set upright and raise the faint-hearted hearer; see what he does
again, and behold the vigor of Paul, and the philosophic mind. For from the
very things that seem to grieve and disturb and trouble the hearer, from these
very things he weaves the crowns of consolation and of boasting. For when he
had enumerated all these things, he then adds saying: I do not say only these
things which I have said, he says, such as that we were sanctified, that we
were justified, that through the Only-begotten, that we enjoyed grace, that
peace, that gift, that remission of sins, that communion of Holy Spirit, that
with all ease, that even without toils, and without sweats, that by faith
only, that he sent the only-begotten Son, that he gave the one and promised
the other, unspeakable glory, incorruption, resurrection of bodies, an angelic
lot, the life with Christ, the sojourn in the heavens; for all these he set
forth in saying, And we glory upon hope of the glory of God. He does not then
say only these things that have come to be and that will be, but even the very
things that seem to be grievous among men, the courts I mean, the
leadings-away, the deaths, the threats, the famines, the tortures, the
frying-pans, the furnaces, the plunder, the wars, the sieges, the fights, the
factions, the contentions, and these he sets in the rank of gift and of
achievements. For one must not only rejoice and be glad upon those things that
have been said, but one must also glory in these, as when he says: Now I
rejoice in my sufferings for you, and fill up the things lacking of the
tribulations of Christ in my flesh. Did you see a vigorous soul, and a high
mind, and an unoverturned purpose, not only adorning itself upon the crowns,
but also taking pleasure in the contests? not rejoicing upon the recompenses,
but also exulting in the wrestlings? not being gladdened upon the returns, but
also glorying in the pancration itself? Do not tell me the kingdom of the
heavens, nor those undefiled crowns, nor the prizes, but the present things
themselves, which have tribulation and toil and much hardship; bring these
into the midst, and I shall be able to show that one must glory upon these
even more greatly. For in the contests outside, the wres =51.162= tlings have
the toil, but the crown the pleasure; but here it is not so, but even before
the crowns the wrestlings themselves bear much boasting. And that you may
learn that these things have this manner, reckon up each of the saints of


each generation, as he says: Take, brethren, the example of suffering
and of patience, the prophets, who spoke the word in the name of the Lord. But
indeed this one also who has now proposed this contest to us, and has
assembled the present spiritual theater, I mean Paul, after enumerating those
countless hardships of each of the saints, which it is not even easy now to go
through according to the word, adds saying: They went about in sheepskins, in
goatskins, being in want, being afflicted, being maltreated, of whom the world
was not worthy, and upon all these rejoicing. And this same thing one might
see, when after the prison and the insults, having been scourged they were
driven away, what does he say? They therefore went rejoicing from the face of
the council, because they had been counted worthy to be dishonored for the
name of Christ.
δʹ. But indeed these things have happened in our time also; and if anyone
should wish, let him consider what I say, what sort of things occurred in the
time of the persecutions. A tender virgin, unwedded, having a body softer than
wax, entered; then having been nailed to the wood from every side, she was
pierced, her sides being scraped, and she ran down with blood, and as if
rather being married and sitting in a bridal chamber, so she bore the things
happening with goodwill on account of the kingdom of the heavens, being
crowned in the very contests. Consider how great it was to see a tyrant with
armies and sharpened swords and so many arms being conquered by one girl. Do
you see that even the tribulation itself has a most great boasting? And you
yourselves bear witness to what is said. For the martyrs not yet having
received the recompenses, nor the prizes and the crowns, but having been
dissolved into dust and ash, we run together with all eagerness to their
honors, and we assemble a spiritual theater, and we proclaim these, and we
crown them both for the wounds and the blood, for the tortures and the blows,
for those tribulations and the straits; so even the tribulations themselves
have a boast even before the recompense. Consider how great Paul was then,
dwelling in the prisons, and being led to courts, how notable, how splendid,
and how conspicuous he appeared among all, but most of all among those by whom
he was warred against, and plotted against. And why do I say notable among
men, when even to the demons he was then more fearful, when he was being
scourged? And when he was being bound, when he was being shipwrecked, then he
worked the greatest signs, then he more greatly prevailed over the opposing
powers. Knowing well, then, the gain that accrues to the soul from these
tribulations, he said: When I am weak, then I am strong; then he adds:
Therefore I take pleasure in weaknesses, in insults, in necessities, in
persecutions, =51.163= in sufferings, that the power of Christ may tabernacle
upon me. Therefore also toward certain who were spending time in Corinth, and
addressing himself to these who were high-minded about themselves, and
condemning the rest, writing out the character of the Epistle, he had
necessity from this to compose for us the image of his own achievements, and
this not from the signs, not from the wonders, not from the honor, not from
the ease, but from the prisons, and the courts, and the famine, and the cold,
and the wars, and the plots, saying thus to them: Are they ministers of
Christ? I speak as beside myself, I more; and showing the "I more," and the
superiority, he says: In labors more abundantly, in stripes above measure, in
prisons more abundantly, in deaths often, and what follows; If I must glory, I
will glory the things of my weakness. Do you see him glorying more greatly in
these things, than adorning himself upon splendid crowns, and for this reason
saying: Not only so, but we also glory in tribulations? And what is the "Not
only"? Not only, he says, do we not lose heart being afflicted and


hard pressed, but as advancing toward a greater honor and glory, we glory
rather upon the grievous things that befall. Then, since he said that there is
a most great glory and a boast from the tribulations and an adornment; and it
is quite clear that the glory also has pleasure; for where there is pleasure,
there is altogether also glory, and where such glory, there is altogether also
pleasure; since then he showed that to be afflicted is splendid and
conspicuous and makes one adorn oneself, he says another most great
achievement of it, and some most great and paradoxical fruit. And what this
is, let us see. Knowing therefore, he says, that tribulation works patience,
and patience proof, and proof hope, and hope does not put to shame. What is:
Knowing that tribulation works patience? This has the greatest fruit, this
makes the one afflicted stronger. For just as of trees those that are reared
in the shade and stand in windless places, flourishing in appearance, become
softer and spongy, quickly harmed by every assault of winds; but those that
stand on the higher ridges of mountains, and are fanned by many and great
winds, and bear continual unevenness of airs, and are shaken by a most
grievous storm, and are struck with much snow, stand rather stronger than all
iron; and bodies likewise that are wrapped in many and various pleasures, and
adorned with soft garments, and that have used continual baths and unguents,
and that live in luxury with many kinds of foods beyond =51.164= need, become
altogether useless toward the sweats and toils on behalf of piety, being
liable to a most great chastisement; so indeed also souls, those that pursue
the life without hardship, and are full of ease, and prefer to be pleasantly
disposed toward the present things, and the painless life, over being
afflicted for the kingdom according to all the saints, being set rather softer
than all wax and weaker, lie forth as food of eternal fire; but those given
over to dangers and toils and hardships of the tribulation through God, and
wrapped in these, are rather firmer and nobler than iron itself or adamant
from continually suffering ill, being set unconquerable to the things that
come on, and having taken on a certain habit of patience and of courage
unconquerable. And just as those who have first boarded a ship are seasick and
dizzy, being troubled and disturbed, held by nausea and vertigo; but those who
have crossed many and long seas, and have dared countless waves, and have
endured continual wrecks, lay hold of such a voyage with confidence; so indeed
also the soul that has endured many trials, and has borne great tribulations,
is set thereafter in a practice of toils and a habit of endurance, not being
panic-struck, nor easily scared, nor being troubled by the grievous things
that fall upon it, but from the continual exercise of the things that befall,
and the frequent practice of the things that come to be, bearing with much
ease all the dreadful things that come on. This then the wise master-builder
of the heavenly polity making clear, said: Not only so, but we also glory in
tribulations; because before the kingdom and the heavenly crowns we harvest
from this a most great reward, from being continually afflicted our soul
becoming more enduring, and the reasonings being made stronger. Knowing all
these things, then, beloved, let us bear nobly the grievous things that fall
upon us, both because it seems good to God, and for our own profit, and let us
not be distressed, nor give way at the bringing-on of the trials, but even
standing with all courage, let us give thanks to God continually for all the
benefactions accomplished toward us, that we may both enjoy the present goods,
and attain the gifts to come, by the grace and compassions and loving-kindness
of our Lord Jesus Christ, with whom to the Father be glory and the might,
together with the holy and


life-giving Spirit, now and always, and
unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
