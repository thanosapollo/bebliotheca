On the Transfiguration.
=61.721=
The rising sun stretches its own rays into all the world; and the natal rising
of the Master Christ according to the flesh sent forth his disciples, like so
many rays, into the whole inhabited world. Therefore today also, after the
manner of luminaries, Peter and James and John brighten this sacred assembly:
the netters of the unbelieving, and fishermen of those who had formerly gone
astray, bringing them to the knowledge of God; who use the cross in place of a
rod, and in place of a well-rope the threefold confession, drawing up the
rational fish from the depth of wickedness; who share with the Teacher in the
unspeakable mysteries, and have become eyewitnesses and earwitnesses of things
unutterable and unseen. Peter, called Rock because of his faith, and bearing
upon himself the building of the Church. For you are, he says, Peter, and upon
this rock I will build my Church, and the gates of Hades shall not prevail
against it. But let us see, if you will, for what reason and of what kind the
chief obtained this prize. The Savior once asked the disciples what opinion
they held concerning him. While the others were still silent, this noble one
sprang forward and said: he did not say, You came to be, you were created;
but, You are, according to the prophet who says, But you are the same, and
your years shall not fail; and because of this confession he receives the keys
of the heavens. For whatever, he says, you bind on earth shall be bound in the
heavens; and whatever you loose on earth shall be loosed in the heavens.
O strange dignity, exceeding nature! Walking on earth he sent forth laws to
the heavenly powers, and they kept them. And below he dictated what seemed
good, while from above God confirmed the vote of what was said. That Joseph
was once counted blessed, having been entrusted with the granaries of Pharaoh.
How is Peter not enviable and thrice-blessed, being entrusted with the
treasures of the heavens themselves? But I do not count him so blessed because
he received the authority to bind, nor because even shadows stretching from
his garments drove away diseases, nor am I astonished that he raised Tabitha
from the dead; for these, for the most part, are the gifts of the Spirit who
works; but I count that Peter blessed for the true confession, he who first of
all until then had stepped forward: You are the Christ, the Son of the living
God. And he confesses what is manifest, You are the Christ; this is the
declaration of coexistence with the Father. But let us see, if you will, also
the distinctive advantages of the thrice-blessed John; not from those things
in which he shared divine poverty with Peter; for he was with the one who
said, Silver and gold I have none, and by silence he showed his agreement; but
because, having fallen upon the Master's breast, from there he forged that
sacred and loud-voiced trumpet, through which what heaven did not say, angels
did not conceive, none of the other evangelists grasped, he alone made
manifest with a daring and faithful tongue. In the beginning was the Word, and
the Word was with God, and the Word was God. For what reason, then, and for
what =61.722= cause, though he was first among the other evangelists, did he
write the Gospel last? It is necessary to inquire into this also. Matthew
composed the evangelical wonders in the language of the Hebrews; Mark, having
become a disciple of Peter, wrote the things that came to pass through the
Master Christ, I mean the unmingled birth, the blameless manner of life, the
strange signs, the Jewish envy, the saving passion, the death that gives us
life, the resurrection unto incorruption, the ascension to the Father's bosom.
And likewise Luke also, being a disciple of Paul, wore the same road as those
before him. John, then, alone, like some fisherman sitting at a distance, and
having become a spectator of others fishing, when he saw that the chief point
of knowledge had been left out, takes in hand the fishing-rod of the Gospel,
and from it hunts the fish that was sought, and sells this without a price to
the faithful, crying, In the beginning was the Word. Not from the time when
Mary, he says, the supposed mother, did Christ take a beginning; not from when
Joseph was named his father does he have existence; nor from Moses, nor from
Abraham, the father of all before the law; but, In the beginning was the Word.
Nothing is more ancient than beginning, nothing older than beginning; every
creature begins, and is under a beginning; but the Son is the beginning of all
things, and of all he is beginning as Maker. Everything taken along with the
beginning is younger; but beginning alone holds the privileges of eternity.
But let us look at just James; for even if the other James obtained the
distinctive surname of the Just, the one styled Just, yet he will not grudge
such an honor to his fellow-apostle. The son of Zebedee also, then, is rich in
many evangelical achievements; but for the present it is enough for a word of
praise that he gladly had his head cut off for love of the Master.
For Herod, as Luke relates in the Acts, puts him to death. These are the
athletes of piety, these the ministers of the evangelical proclamations. But
when one crowned victor returns from Olympic contests, peoples and cities run
together to see him, and this though he is crowned with leaves of laurel; but
we have beheld not one but three contestants, contestants for the Trinity, who
contended and were crowned for the Trinity, come to us with a lesser
concourse. And yet every tongue dares the immeasurable, trying to go through
the praises of one of these, and the mind grows dizzy, forced to measure a sea
of virtues with a cup; yet we have attempted it, showing to the fathers the
fruit of gratitude. Two days ago the birthday festival of Christ the King of
all was completed; rightly, therefore, these splendid contests were
accomplished in honor of the Master's birth by the pentathlon contestants.
This is accustomed to happen also among those outside; for the rulers in the
cities honor royal birthdays; but there the victor harvested the pleasure of a
shadow and of passing joy; but here the prizes for the crowned are immortal,
the crowns everlasting, the joy with angels, the brightness with the saints in
the resurrection.
These =61.723= privileges are laid up for these contests; by whose
intercessions may we too be kept safe, and may this =61.724= good shepherd be
granted to us for many decades of years in Christ, to whom be the glory unto
the ages. Amen.
