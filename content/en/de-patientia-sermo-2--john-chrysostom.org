On patience.
=60.729=
Thirsty travelers imagine the absent spring in their mind, and by longing for
it they hurry with a sharp movement, so as to find the remedy of thirst, and
to cool the flame burning within through the streams. In the same way you
also, beloved, thirsting for the word of truth, come with eagerness toward the
true spring, our Lord Jesus Christ. For he himself is the one who says: If
anyone thirsts, let him come to me, and drink; and again he himself says:
Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for righteousness. Longing for this
spring the blessed David sang, saying: As the hart longs for the springs of
waters, so my soul longs for you, O God. But lest we seem to remain outside
the Scriptures through these prefaces, come then, let us take hold of the
words; for thus the discourse will be shown to us more brilliant, and the
Church is shown to us bright. But let us return to the matter at hand. What
then was it that we just now heard the blessed David saying: Waiting I waited
for the Lord, and he attended to me, and he heard my petition?

Patience has appeared to us as a great trophy; for we have found all the
saints crowned with patience, and having this as a teacher =60.730= within, we
know the blessed Abraham, who became a hundred years old and had not yet
begotten a child, but by this man's patience the fruit was already harvested
before his eyes. The tree cast off the leaves, and the root was weighed down;
the flesh grew old, and his mind was in its prime; but nonetheless by patience
he bore Isaac as fruit. See for me the blessed Job, the athlete of the world,
who wrestled and tore apart the power of the devil; how many times did the
devil wrestle, and he did not throw the athlete? He emptied the quiver of
arrows, he brought on the engines, and he did not shake the tower; he raised
the waves, and he did not sink the ship; he broke the branches, and he did not
pull up the root; he raised the great waves, and he did not shake the rock.
Did you see the patience of a righteous man? did you see the contest of an
athlete? did you see a rock made firm? did you see the soldier drawn up? did
you see the tower standing? did you see the righteous man glorified and the
devil fleeing with shame? See for me, if you please, also the crown of his
patience. For after the captivity of the oxen, and the disappearance of the
sheep, and the fall of the house, and the disaster of the children, for he saw
them born at different times, but all together going under the =60.731= end of
life; after so great a consumption then the wife also comes upon him saying:
How long will you endure, saying: Behold I wait a little time, expecting the
hope of my salvation? for behold your memorial has vanished from the earth,
your sons and your daughters, the pangs and toils of my womb, which I labored
in vain with hardships; and you yourself sit upon the rottenness of worms,
passing the night in the open air, and I a wanderer and a servant, going from
place to place and from house to house, waiting for when the sun will set,
that I may rest from the toils and from the pains that surround me, which now
hold me. But say some word against the Lord, and die. But he, looking at her,
said the famous voice sung among all: If we received the good things from the
hand of the Lord, shall we not bear the evil? Did you see a sea of patience?
did you see teaching that does not fall?

The barrenness of the things that came upon him did not make his patience
slack, but though so many things came on like wave after wave, the ship did
not become underwater. Did you see the patience of a righteous man? did you
see a crown being plaited for the righteous man through patience? See for me
also the poor Lazarus, or rather one more well-provided than rich men, how
this man too through patience rests in the bosom of Abraham. For this Lazarus
sat by the gate of the rich man, wasting away with hunger; and that rich man
each day filling his belly with various foods; but Lazarus did not even have
bread to fill his belly; this one fed parasites, and this one was licked by
dogs; this one had crowned mixing-bowls of wine, and that one soothed his
wounds; this one had a gold-roofed house and Lacedaemonian marbles and golden
helmets, and Lazarus had no hut; this one was clothed in silks and hare-skins,
and Lazarus did not have a rag. But see for me the end of patience. So the
rich man died; for the death of the rich man is death; for the death of
sinners is evil; and Lazarus also died, or rather was transferred. This one
was drawn by angels, this one lived as a citizen in heaven, and that one was
burned in Gehenna; this one enjoyed the waters to excess, and that one was
burned in Gehenna, he longed for a drop; this one rested in the bosom of
Abraham, and that one was stung by the sleepless worm.

And see, if you please, the patience of the blessed Lazarus. He did not say in
himself, that I living in virtue am poor, and this one living in injustice is
rich; servants stood by this one offering unfailing service; this one is
blessed by men, and this one was counted wretched; cooks served this one with
varied art, bakers, chief bakers; baths in the city, and baths throughout the
country, and gardens and paradises. This one each day enjoyed honor to excess;
the earth brought forth to him without fail, and this one did not share with
those in need; the trees, weighed down with fruit, looked toward the earth,
and that one, by the weight of evils, did not look up to heaven; but Lazarus
sat alone, thrusting away by patience the storm of poverty. Did you see the
patience of a righteous man? did you see the cruelty of a rich man? did you
see the endurance of a poor man? did you see the inhumanity of the rich man?
did you see what a wage patience has made to bloom? See for me also the three
children spending time in the furnace, and treading the furnace as thin air by
patience, how these young men dared so great a torment, and did not reckon the
threat of the flame, nor how many bitter tortures were about to come upon
them? but they were so high in the mind of patience, as to say: And if not, be
it known to you, O king, that we do not serve your gods, and we do not worship
the golden image which you have set up. But nonetheless they dared his
tortures, and they walked in the furnace and hymned God, as in a meadow and a
paradise or some grass-bearing place; and as one living in luxury, so they
walked, and said: Blessed are you, O Lord the God of our fathers, and
praiseworthy and glorified is your name unto the ages. And see patience
sprouting, bringing on humility next: For you are righteous in all things
which you have done to us. And even if they had committed sins, the power of
the =60.732= flame washed them all away; but nonetheless by patience they
said: For you are righteous in all things which you have done to us. Did you
see the patience of righteous men? They did not reckon that their bodies were
about to have converse with fire, but they were found more solid than adamant,
and so brave that they did not stand in one place in the furnace. And walking
they blessed God and glorified him.

And not they alone only, but they also called all creation with themselves,
the sun and the moon, the stars of heaven, sea and rivers, springs and lakes
and the earth, the mountains, the hills, the glens, the

plains, the nights, the days. And see for me the wonder: they hymned and
glorified God in order. Read me the making of the world, you will find in it:
In the beginning God made the heaven and the earth. Then next he brings on the
creation. Thus also the children, knowing the law and knowing God, spoke thus:
Bless, O heavens, the Lord, stars of heaven, the Lord, all waters above the
heaven, the Lord, sun and moon, the Lord. And see for me the wonder last of
all: Bless, Ananias, Azarias, Misael, the Lord. For as in the making of the
world the lawgiver, writing the history, said after the creation had been
completed, Let us make man according to our own image and according to our
likeness; so also these, having made the filling-up of the hymnody last of
all, say next, Bless; Ananias, Azarias, Misael, the Lord.

Did you see the patience of righteous men? did you see a crown being plaited?
did you see the strength of the brave young men? did you see zeal more
vehement than fire? did you see a mouth that shares the diet of the angels?
did you see a tongue that serves together with the angels? did you see the
patience of athletes? They did not cower toward death, their face was not
changed through the punishment that was about to be for them, but bright and
glad with the bloom they said: If not, be it known to you, O king, that we do
not serve your gods, and we do not worship the golden image which you have set
up. Did you see how everywhere patience becomes the procurer of the kingdom of
the heavens? See for me, if you please, also the blessed Daniel who was thrown
into a den of lions, and taught the lions to fast. For the beasts did not dare
to taste of the prophetic flesh, because he had been nourished by patience.
This blessed Daniel then, having entered into the den of the lions, did not
cower, but prayed to God; by patience he too changed the beastliness into
gentleness, and the nature of wildness was changed into the gentleness of a
sheep, and Daniel was standing as a shepherd with lambs. Did you see nature
being changed? And the tyrant was put to shame, and it was possible to see a
strange and paradoxical thing: the lions became gentler than the king.

For those, having received the food, and having fallen into so great a hunger,
did not dare to taste of prophetic flesh, and though the table was set before
them, and the ration, they denied their nature because of the patience of the
prophet, and the attack of the beasts did him no injustice, because he hoped
in the Lord; for even the nature of wild things knows to reverence the image
of God. Therefore David, knowing clearly that he who hopes in the Lord,
nothing fearful is before his eyes, says: And you will trample on lion and
dragon; and again the Lord: Behold, I have given you the authority to tread
upon serpents and scorpions and upon all the power of the Enemy; and again
David, For even if I walk in the midst of the shadow of death, I will not fear
evils, for you are with me. See for me, if you please, also the blessed
Elijah, who shut the heaven by the word for three years and six months, how
this man too remained by patience, eating no bread, nor being filled with
water; but he walked the desert and went about the glens through fear of
Jezebel, and so great a man was fed by a raven through patience. By the river
Chorazin he sat, persevering in patience, and being fed by a raven; and yet
the raven is unclean by the law. Let the Jew stand forth. How was your prophet
fed by an unclean thing? how is this, tell me? Moses said the raven was
unclean; but your prophet received his food from an unclean thing. For was
there not another bird? was there not in the country a turtledove, not a dove,
that he was fed by a raven? =60.733= Interpret for me, O Jew, the inquiry of
the bird. Did not Moses legislate concerning the raven, and place him among
the unclean? how then the prophet, whom you expect to come, who mounted upon a
fiery chariot, and the

heaven having shut for three years and six months, how this one who by prayer
raised the dead, was fed by a raven? for could not the one who shut the heaven
bring down loaves for himself? But that he might show you that the Old
overturned the things of the law. And this man too lived, having contended
through long-suffering and patience, and we have found all the saints having
contended through patience. But behold for me, if you please, also the blessed
David who became last of men and of his brothers, but later reigned over his
brothers; and he threw down so great an enemy with a stone, and having
overturned his phalanx, and having torn his power as a spider's web; and of
this man who had thus contended, thus he was warred upon by Saul. And yet
David was doing him good, and when he sang the demon fled, and often when
David found him ready for slaughter, yet again by forbearance he overlooks
him; and often when Saul came upon him so as to kill him, again David gave the
whole to God.

And see for me also the blessed Joseph, the athlete of chastity, how this man
too philosophized by patience; for first being plotted against by his brothers
because of the dreams. And see envy of brothers. If the dreams are true, what
device, how will the things foreseen not come out altogether? but if the
visions of the dreams are false, why do you envy the one who is mistaken? And
see the wonder, beloved: He saw himself worshipped by sun and moon and eleven
stars. His brothers heard this, or rather the disciples of the devil, who had
been taught by their father the devil envy and murder, their sister
iniquities, which Paul also yoked together, saying: Full of envy, murder. They
heard the kingdom that was about to succeed them, and they were pricked to
kill him.

But the blessed Joseph endured the contest, and when his brothers had gone to
feed sheep in Shechem, his father said: Behold, are not your brothers feeding
in Shechem? Come, I will send you, and see if your brothers are well and the
sheep, and announce to me. And as he was going, they saw him coming from far
off, and said to one another: See, that dreamer is coming; come, let us kill
him, and let us see what his dreams will be. Did you see a counsel of those
wanting to work fratricide? Again the devil became counselor to these, and
they learned the lessons more sharply. The brother came to them, and they
stripped him of his tunic; but he did not become naked of patience. For even
if he put off the covering of the flesh, yet he did not put off his chastity
and his hope toward God, which also brought up his cause. They slaughtered a
kid, they filled his tunic with blood, and they brought it to the father
saying: Know whose this tunic is. The father recognized the many-colored tunic
of the son, he began to mourn and to wail, and lamenting he said: An evil
beast has eaten Joseph. For truly they who did this were beasts and evil, or
rather even wilder than the beasts, inasmuch as they took counsel to kill
their own brother and wanted to work fratricide. But nonetheless the blessed
Joseph contended by patience; he was thrown in the pit, and he endured this.
But tell me, O polluted ones, why did you cast so great an inhumanity upon
your own brother? was the stripping of the tunic not enough for you, but you
also threw him in the pit? did you not reckon that God in heaven sees all
things? did your body not shudder? did your heart not cower, having dared to
do this? do you not know that the first commandment of the law is, You shall
not murder, and this you were eager to do among the first things, especially
toward a brother? Then having made a counsel they tried to sell this one into
slavery.

The Ishmaelites went down into Egypt; they saw them, and they brought him up
from the pit, and they sell their own king, as of a slave who would never
reign. They went down into Egypt. And see how temptation suc =60.734= ceeded
temptation. An Egyptian woman was added to him; with harmful speech she tried
to plunder the righteous man, saying: Sleep with me, and I will grant you
honors and gifts; and let my household servants be under your hands; I will
fill you with various possessions, and I will set my whole house under your
hands, and I myself will become your handmaid; only be persuaded by me,
Joseph, come down to our wound, quench the flame burning within, cool my heart
that is despondent with passion, come down to my wretched soul. But he
answering said: O woman, I have never yet become a slave; but through envy and
jealousy I came down here into slavery.

You shall not defile my chastity, you shall not plunder my nobility; I do not
betray my mind, I do not cast myself into the mouth of death, I do not destroy
the dignity of my kin. I am a descendant of Abraham who spoke with God with
boldness, to whom God speaking said: Surely blessing I will bless you, and
multiplying I will multiply your seed, as the stars of heaven, and as the sand
by the lip of the sea. Isaac happens to be my grandfather, that one who
offered himself readily for sacrifice; my father is Jacob who wrestled with an
angel, that one who saw the heavenly ladder established from the earth unto
heaven, and the angels of God going up and going down. I am his son; I am not
a runaway nor implacable; I was not sold here as one condemned, but through
envy and jealousy I am here. And when she was not able by such words to trip
him toward adultery, nor to make his chastity slack by much speaking, she
caught him by the garment; but he, having put off his garments, went out
naked. Did you see patience? The chaste athlete went out saying, that Through
the serpent we put on, through chastity we put off; through disobedience we
received the covering, through the commandment of God that says, You shall not
commit adultery, we put this off. And he went out naked, or rather clothed
with Christ. The sea then foamed, raging, wanting to raise the great waves, to
sink the ship with all hands, and it did not have the strength, but in so
great a storm he sailed as in a calm. How many things it moved, and it did not
harm this one! The ship trembled, and it did not receive the cargo. How many
things it troubled, and it did not plunder the treasure! Then looking around,
and finding her desire from nowhere, she begins then to slander, and says to
her own husband: You brought in here a Hebrew servant to mock me.

That one heard, and was kindled toward anger, and he put him in prison. For
the lamp was fitting for those in darkness, and a giver of grain for those
wasting away in famine. Then see for me also another wrestling-place here. For
the deprivation of the father was not enough for him, and the plot of the
brothers, and the alienation from the land, but also a woman's uprising, and
the shutting in of the prison. Then, beloved, he lay in prison. The chief
cupbearer and the chief baker were thrown into his place. Both saw dreams, and
their faces were gloomy. Joseph saw them, and said to them: Why are your faces
gloomy? And they said: We have seen a dream, and there is no one who judges.
He says to them: Tell me each of you what he saw. What do you say, Joseph? did
you then see a dream? Their end did not come out. You saw yourself worshipped
by sun and moon, and behold you were sold as a slave; you saw the sheaves of
your brothers turning about, and worshipping your sheaf, and behold you have
been shut in the prison.

Why then do you also inquire into the disaster of the brothers? why are you so
mad? I am not mad, he says; I know the crown of my patience, I know the gain
of these

disasters, I know what kind of flower will appear; For no one is crowned,
unless he contend lawfully; and again the blessed David, Waiting I waited for
the Lord, and he attended to me. Did you see the patience of an athlete, did
you see endurance, did you see an unenslaved mind, did you see an athlete who
does not fall, did you see a crowned one of chastity, did you see the ship of
purity, did you see the rudder of the young, did you see the bit of those in
their prime, did you see the helmsman of pleasures, did you see his contest,
did you see his athletic struggle? =60.735= As many of you then as are young,
take up the reasoning; for nothing is stronger than patience. For patience is
the root of all good things, mother of piety, branch of gladness, unfading
fruit, unconquerable tower, unstormed harbor. Did you see what a good wage
patience is?

But let us pass over, if you please, also to the blessed Susanna; she too
contended by patience; and let us see also in her, in a woman's body, a manly
soul, and a woman's chastity, whose memory remains immortal with victory. For
this blessed Susanna too contended in the public theater, where God and angels
and principalities and powers also watched her contest. For I wish to set
forth her patience in the word. This blessed Susanna then entered into the
garden according to custom to walk; and the two lawless elders were hidden in
the garden. And she was as a lamb left behind and walking in the garden; and
the two elders as wolves clothed, how they might make this one a prey of
beasts. And they found her alone, and they ran upon her; but she did not
cower, nor did their attack terrify her, but she stood unmoved in chastity;
and they said to her: We are in desire of you, be persuaded by us; we are the
elders of the =60.736= people who seem to govern the people, themselves making
themselves underwater; the law is subject to us, or rather the law has
condemned you, saying, You shall not desire the wife of your neighbor; but if
you are not persuaded by us, we will accuse you, that a young man was with
you. And she, having heard the word, groaned saying: It is strait for me on
every side. Death is choice for me for the sake of chastity, rather than a
disordered marriage. And they, having fallen from the prey, accused her before
the people; but she was silent, having seen the gain of patience. They
condemned this woman for chastity, they laid their hands upon her, she was
going to die.

The people saw the condemnation; but God who knows all things beforehand
raised up the Holy Spirit upon Daniel, and he convicted hidden thoughts, the
incontinence of the elders, and the chastity of Susanna. These were put to
shame, and she was glorified; these in dishonor, and she in glory. You know
what a wage patience is. As many of you then as have the image of patience, be
zealous for this longing; For he who has endured unto the end, this one will
be saved in Christ Jesus our Lord; to whom be the glory and the power unto the
ages of the ages. Amen.
