* Question to Cyril
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

A petition submitted to the holy Cyril, faithful high priest, genuine servant
of God, to holy Cyril archbishop of Alexandria from the brotherhood.
It would indeed have been well for us remaining quiet, even if perhaps
accomplishing something of the things being pursued, yet no less even
departing from there, we think ourselves to be benefited through your sight
alone, and most of all we shall gain, being taught piety from your very mouth.
For to read, even when stationed far from the land, the books and the labors
of your holiness is both good and beneficial and carrying those who attend
toward what is ahead; but to be watered with the teaching from the very lips
that pour forth divine grace and spiritual sweetness is both more secure and
more life-giving. We have come therefore again because of the desire toward
you and toward piety, both our own progress and the correction of others. For
having received formerly from your holy hands the book that solves the inquiry
concerning the right doctrines among us, we carried away no small profit. For
the word has become for us a confirmation, and for others a correction; and
few are those who have not come to the healthy teaching, whose mouths have
been stopped. But those of the country of Abilene, stretching themselves badly
toward one another on account of certain dogmatic questions, have driven to
such a pitch of madness as even to define depositions and anathemas against
one another and to bring persecutions against one another and to undergo
plunderings and neither to yield to the bishops nor to the fathers there, nor
indeed to those among us, nor to give way to any others of the saints, that
they have been deemed worthy of a greater measure of knowledge, so masterfully
has the satanic disorder taken them captive far from the divine peace. In
addition to these again some of the Egyptians also, having been taught not to
think rightly concerning God, suffering the same madness as those, are brought
against one another with the equal pursuits of greed. 548 But both parties,
pricked from God, have come to the saints in Palestine, and have brought to
these the inquiries being sought. And we, having measured our own power,
judged that we ought not ourselves to define concerning these things, nor
indeed to be sufficient to give an account concerning such examinations. The
aforementioned then, having learned that we have boldness toward your
holiness, earnestly entreated that through our smallness the word of your
teaching receive an end of the fight against one another.
The holy fathers among us having therefore consented, we of our own will
received the entreaty, granting a favor rather to ourselves than to those, by
both enjoying the very sight and being gladdened together with one present and
with a living word and not with a letter sent through others and kindling the
desire still more. We therefore seized the arrival, rejoicing not moderately,
that both through us the least they may lay down the fight they are joining
against one another, and through your healthy teaching the peace of Christ may
as quickly as possible be awarded both to them and to us; whether we go away
because of beloved quiet, or remain because of love and gathering together and
the progress from these things and send the things that will be written more
opportunely. But you yourself, most holy father, do not refuse to look upon us
genuinely, preserving a father's bowels even toward us. For neither, as I
think, shall we need even an apology, sharing boldness with reverence through
God's grace and your kindness, which we also demand to be widened for us
ourselves equally with our genuineness, and this with prudence. For thus it
would come about both that we for now obtain the reciprocal things and that
your holiness, doing the fitting things even toward us, be well-minded, which
the Savior of all may accept still more, even being brought down toward us the
least according to his imitation, not by constraint of force, but by
genuineness of love, and offering this gift to him. And the things being
sought are those set out below.
[The chapters were contained in the petition, but in order that we not write
these twice, we have yoked each chapter together in the interpretation.]
