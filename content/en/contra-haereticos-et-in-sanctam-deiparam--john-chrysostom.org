Against the heretics, and on the holy Theotokos.
=59.709=
1. Again, by the boldness of the Holy Spirit, tearing apart the chains of
ignorance, I take the teacher's throne, not ashamed of my poverty; for being
lovers of the poor, you pity me through the munificence of love. For being
poor I stretch myself toward the rich, not putting forward the disease of
arrogance, but filling up the desire of your God-loving soul. For you take
delight in my words, since I too am gladdened by your ways. I do not know my
poverty, and my stammering voice, when I behold this fair and beloved theater.
For I know that you do not come together as judges =59.710= of my words, or as
spectators of contests, but that as fathers you may lend your strength to the
child. For I speak with the tongue, but you speak this with the heart to God:
that a word be given to us in the opening of the mouth; for being lovers of
children and fond of offspring, you count our labors as your own crowns.
Therefore I do not hesitate to speak; for the longing I have for you undoes my
hesitation, and your holy prayer turns my ignorance into virtuous teaching.
But let these things be said by way of preface. And I today, having girt the
loins of =59.711= truth, and put on the breastplate of righteousness, and shod
my feet with the preparation of the gospel of peace, begin, having taken up
the contest against the heretics. I lay hold of the contests, not joining
words to words, nor with rhetorical cleverness dissolving the chains of their
propositions, but by the voice that has been read through completely, tearing
apart the ingenious problems of the heretics like a spider's web. And this is
the apostolic reading: O the depth of the riches and wisdom and knowledge of
God! How unsearchable are his judgments, and untraceable his ways! What do you
say, O Paul? Does the wealth of God lie in the depth, and is it known in the
height? Yes, he says, both in the depth and in the height: in the depth
because of the humanity, in the height because of the divinity. For thus,
revealing the economy through the prophet, he said to Ahaz: Ask for yourself a
sign from the Lord your God, in the depth or in the height: in the depth,
because of the economy, in the height, because of the theology. For a deep
sign is to be born of a Virgin, and a lofty proclamation also is to be
begotten God of God. Therefore, O man, neither by prying into the height do
you grasp it, nor by busying yourself with the depth do you find it. For the
generations of Christ are sacred: one is according to nature, the other
contrary to nature. For contrary to nature, in later times, he was born of the
Virgin; according to nature, before the ages, he was born of the Father, as
the One who begot knows. And the generation above is without deceit. For truly
God was begotten of God, truly from the Virgin God was born as man among men:
above, only from only, Only-Begotten; and below, only from only,
Only-Begotten. And just as in the generation above it is impious to suppose a
mother, so also in the case of the Virgin it is blasphemous to suppose a
father. The Father begot without flux, the Virgin bore without corruption. For
God did not undergo flux in begetting, for he begot in a manner befitting God;
nor did the Virgin undergo corruption in bearing, for she bore spiritually. O
new wonder! O sacred and unspeakable mystery! For I dare to say what, in
saying, I do not err: that even above a virgin nature begot, for the essence
of the Godhead is pure and incorrupt, and below an incorrupt Virgin bore the
Incorrupt; for You will not give your Holy One to see corruption. When nature
walks in order, then seek the things according to nature, O man; but when God
commands something, then nature denies its own order, and does those things
which the law of God wills.
What I mean is this: God commanded the rock in the wilderness to gush forth
water, and it gushed, not what it had in its bosom, but what the command
deposited. As therefore the rock by the nod of God became a spring, and by the
rush of waters bore a torrent, no vein lying beneath, not borrowing from a
river, but the dry and veinless and waterless suddenly became mother of
rivers, pouring forth unsparingly water which it did not have: so also the
unwedded Virgin, by the overshadowing of the Holy Spirit, bore our Lord
according to the flesh, not moved by the order of nature, but coming to birth
by the power of the Most High. For she did not bear by borrowing from the law
of nature, but being empowered by the Holy Spirit she so bore, as the grace of
God willed. For the Holy Spirit, he says, will come upon you, and the power of
the Most High will overshadow you; therefore also the holy thing that is born
will be called Son of God. And if you wish to know how nature denies its own
order, that contrary to nature it may serve the will of God, ask Aaron's rod:
Speak, dry rod, how did you sprout leaves and fruit in an instant, contrary to
nature? Moses did not set you in the earth, he did not water you with rains,
time did not nourish the roots, you did not see summer or spring, you did not
enjoy winter or autumn; how then did you give leaves and flowers and fruit?
And the rod says: I was not enslaved to turns of the airs or changes of
seasons, but serving the divine word I trampled nature, and contrary to nature
I gave fruit, that both according to nature and contrary to nature we might
serve the will of the master-craftsman. As therefore the rod contrary to
nature gave fruit, O man, that Judaea might believe: so also the living rod,
the royal Virgin, without the laws of marriage bore, that the world might be
saved.
2. But if you say, What likeness has a sprouting rod to a Virgin who bears?
learn that it preserves the likeness altogether. =59.712= For the rod was a
type of the Virgin; that was the image, this the truth; and the example that
went before is this demonstration. For just as that rod gave fruit contrary to
nature, so here too contrary to nature the Virgin sprouted the flower of
piety. For a rod, he says, shall come forth from the root of Jesse, and a
flower shall rise up from it. Does Paul then marvel in vain at the wealth of
God, that is, the incarnate economy of Christ? Alas for the unbelief of the
heretics! Alas for their frenzy and God-fighting tongue! Paul, the citizen of
the heavens, the spectator of paradise, the ardent lover of Christ, the hearer
of unspeakable mysteries, when he inquired into the economy, shuddered and
grew dizzy; and you, interpreting the Godhead, do not shudder? Paul says the
judgments of God are unsearchable; and you search the nature of the Father as
if it were easy to hunt down? It is time to call you, having grown downcast,
to turn back upon the things spoken unspeakably by you. You do not know how
the Virgin bore, and you want to know how God begot? You are ignorant of how
the generation of Christ according to the flesh in the days of Herod came
about, and you dare to interpret, and attempt to examine, that which was
begotten from the Father without beginning and beyond interpretation and
sacredly and unspeakably, before all thought and times and ages, without
mediation? If Thomas, having touched the crucified body of Christ, did not
remain uninstructed, and that though he was an apostle: of what punishments
are they worthy who do not touch the body of Christ with the hand, but with
the tongue search out the eternal nature of his Godhead? For Thomas even the
body which he saw, he touched; but these, the Godhead which they did not see,
they have searched out as if they had seen it. Tell me, O terrible and
ingenious orator Eunomius, you who distinguish the nature of the Godhead in
three ways, who fashion gods a little lesser again: why do you call Christ a
creature, and not an offspring? So that, he says, I may not attach a notion of
flux to the uncreated nature of God; for the one who begets must of necessity
undergo flux. Then it follows that the one who creates must also undergo toil.
For the one who creates does not create without labor. If then, fleeing the
notion of flux, you refuse to call the Father Father, do not call him Creator
either, lest you cast upon him a suspicion of toils and sweats. But, he says,
we ought not to take concerning God the same opinion we have concerning men.
For men,
when they create, create with toil and sweat; but God, when he creates,
creates without toil; for he is uncompounded in nature. Since then, O wise and
learned one, the creating God creates without toil, can he not beget without
flux? But when he creates, God creates without toil, and when he begets, he
undergoes flux like a man? And remaining impassible when he fashions, he falls
into a human passion when he begets? I shudder, and I fear, and I tremble at
the blasphemous, the unbridled, the unrestrained tongue of the heretics,
considering that they believe the Virgin bore incorruptibly, and they doubt
that God begot without flux. The incorrupt womb of the Virgin even after
bearing remained intact, O man, and the nature of the Father in begetting did
not escape the flux? But the Virgin even after bearing remained a virgin, not
deprived of her virginity by the bearing; and the Father after begetting could
not remain the same, the essence remaining without flux and unaltered? And
where there is bulk of body, harmony of members, composition of bones, order
of sinews, multitude of veins, bonds of joints, the infant came forth as he
knew, having woven himself unspeakably in the virginal womb; but where there
is no body, no member, no joints, no sinews, no bones, no veins, but an
intangible and incorporeal and invisible nature, you introduce flux and think
of diminution? And he came forth from the womb, and the womb remained intact.
For he who without seed ensouled for himself a temple in the Virgin, himself
also kept the womb intact, as God. For the works of God are not enslaved to
the nature of things, but follow the command of God the Word. For sometimes
God creates according to nature, that you may know the good order of nature;
and sometimes he fashions contrary to nature, that you may learn the power of
the one who works. For neither do all things come about according to nature,
lest nature be deified, nor all things contrary to nature, lest the things
done contrary to nature be thought a disorder of nature. But God forms
according to nature, and fashions also contrary to nature, that you may learn
that nature, holding the rank of a handmaid =59.713=, is moved according to
nature, and runs contrary to nature, fulfilling the command of God.
3. Tell me, you who inquire how Christ, coming forth from the intact womb, did
not destroy the seal of virginity, how from the virgin Adam the virgin Eve
came forth. For the Theotokos Mary, not knowing a man, bore a man; the
first-formed Adam, not knowing a woman, sprouted a woman. But for a virgin to
beget, though it is paradoxical, still has a reason, inasmuch as a belly and a
womb and a natural path have been prefigured for those who are born; but in
the case of Adam all is strange. For who has seen a human being born from a
rib? When did a rib contribute fruit-bearing? God cast sleep upon Adam, that
he might not be pained by the taking away of the rib, but nonetheless his
bonds slept; for the sinews are the bonds of nature. And Daniel is a witness,
saying, And I saw the vision, and I fell; and the joints of my loins were
undone. A rib then, secured by the law of nature, interwoven with bonds of
sinews, woven with flesh, united with veins: how did he take it out? how did
he pull it away? how did the rib come out, and Adam remain whole? How? The one
who undid it knows; the one who undid it also bound it. For if one bound and
another undid, there would be a question; but if the one who fitted it undid
it, and the one who formed the rib from earth took the rib away, he alone
knows how he undid it, who also alone understands how he composed the harmony
of the members. As therefore even after the taking away of the rib Adam
remained whole, so also after the coming forth of the infant the Virgin
remained incorrupt. For neither was the harmony of Adam's members injured when
the rib was taken, nor was the seal of virginity outraged when the infant came
forth. For just as among us, if someone wishes to undo another's bond, he
destroys it and cuts it up through inexperience, but the one who bound undoes
it without labor, for he knows the harmony of what he bound: so also God who
fitted, who compressed, who knotted the body, both knew the joints of the
sinews, and how he joined rib to rib, and joint, and member; and from what he
had bound together, skillfully and all-wisely he lifted a rib, and did not
diminish Adam. The Only-Begotten Son of God formed him in the Virgin, and
neither in forming did he undo the virginity, nor in coming forth through the
womb did he corrupt the womb. Tell me then, O wise and learned one, which is
easier: for a virgin to bear, and after bearing to remain a virgin again; or
for a rod to be ensouled into an animal, and from an animal to become a rod
again? How was Moses' rod changed into a serpent? How did the lifeless take a
soul? How did the unmoving creep upon the earth? And how again from a serpent
did it become a rod, recognizing its own nature? For at one time from a rod a
serpent, at another from a serpent a rod came to be. As therefore Moses' rod,
O man, was according to nature a rod, but according to economy a serpent;
=59.714= so also Mary was according to nature a virgin, but according to
economy became a mother, that the magi of the Chaldeans might believe, just
as, that Egypt might fear, the rod became a serpent. Therefore, that the world
might be saved, did you not want the Virgin to appear? Then when earthquakes
happen, walls are divided and joined; but when Christ is born, was a womb not
opened, and again woven together? And in earthquakes walls are so divided as
even to threaten death; and so united that neither form nor trace of the
division appears; but in that incorrupt generation, did you not want the womb
to be undone for the honor of the one being born, and to come together and be
united and recover the form of virginity by the power of the one who works?
And do not say to me: Whence did flesh and blood come into being, no seed
being present? For it is possible to learn from things among us that which is
above us. And call for me as images of what is said the sons of the painters,
who often paint plants and trees and meadows and rivers, and when they do not
have nature cooperating, they force by art, by the mixing of colors, to
present the images of things. For they make a river, not borrowing a stream
from rivers; they make a man, borrowing nothing from human nature. As
therefore the painters through colors paint both a man and a spring and a
river, taking nothing from a spring or a river or a man: in the same way also
the Holy Spirit in the Virgin, not with colors but by will, constructed an
ensouled man, not borrowing seed from human nature for the constitution of the
one being formed. God, taking dust from the earth, not with human hands (for
the nature of the Godhead is uncompounded) formed Adam as he willed; but the
Holy Spirit did not take dust, did not allow seed to be deposited by a natural
law, but only shone forth, and fixed bones, and stretched sinews, and nose and
ears, both eyes and tongue, hands and feet, soul and mind and reasoning and
word he constituted, even if the children of the heretics do not wish it. For
just as those who stoop into a mirror see an image of themselves rising up to
the sight from the formless material, an image often laughing, and weeping,
both moving and going about in many ways, and imitating the shapes of the
living form: so also the Holy Spirit shone in a pure mirror, the spotless body
of the Virgin, created a perfect man not by the law of nature, not by time,
not by human seed, but by sight alone, and by an intelligible and holy power
moved the Virgin to birth-pangs, weaving the infant unspeakably in her, as a
garment, for the salvation of men. These things being so, let us for what has
been said send up glory to the God who became man for us, because to him
belongs the glory, and to the Father and to the Holy Spirit, unto the ages of
ages. Amen.
