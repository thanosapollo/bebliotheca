* Fragments on the Epistle to the Galatians

When the preaching had been received among the Gentiles, some of those from
the circumcision went out and were persuading the Gentiles to judaize, and the
first inquiry about this appears to have arisen in Antioch: the apostles wrote
that they were not to be subject to the law, but to abstain from what is
sacrificed to idols and from blood and from what is strangled and from
fornication; for so it seemed good to the Holy Spirit. This disease took hold
of the Galatians, who wished with one accord to judaize. They were persuaded
by those who were perverting them, saying that the rest of the apostles do not
hinder those who wish to keep the law. 299 Gal 1:4 He shows that Moses did not
die for the world, nor any other of the prophets. One must not, then, be
overcome by the one who did not save, but by the one who died for us. Gal
1:6-7 Unto another gospel which is not another, he says; for our gospel and
that of the apostles wants you to be outside the law. Gal 1:8-10 Let it be an
angel, but why also we? When we preached, we preached by the power of the Holy
Spirit; and the Holy Spirit does not repent. If then we say other things, we
have abandoned the things of the Spirit. Let even the herald then be anathema,
setting aside the preaching. Contrary to what you received. It is not that I
preached and you did not receive, but both I preached and you received. I do
not repent of having preached, and you keep what you have received. For am I
now persuading men, or God? Or do I seek to please men? If I were still
pleasing men, I would not be a servant of Christ. Being about to remind them
of his own gospel, he takes care in advance to make himself secure, lest they
think that he is flattering them and being led along; for he rebukes as he
admonishes. And by saying if still, he made clear that he once was pleasing
men. Whom was he pleasing? Those from whom he received letters to harm the
faithful in Damascus. If then, he says, I were still pleasing the Jews, I
would not have believed in Christ. Gal 1:13 What need was there of his manner
of life in Judaism, except that he may show that he is not a slave by
prejudice but by truth; for he did not run under grace because he hated the
law (he was defending it), but having found what is perfect he withdrew from
the law. Now he shows that he has something more than those around Peter; for
they did not show zeal for Judaism while disobeying Christianity, as I did.
300 Do not then suppose that I came into grace because I hated the law (for I
was defending the law), but I found what is perfect, and I do not set the law
on a level with the perfect grace. Gal 1:16 And he says this not slandering
the apostles, but that he did not fall in with mortal flesh. Gal 2:16 For if
from works no one of those who bear the toil is justified, but from faith,
where there is both ease and the greatness of righteousness, how is it not
necessary, he says, to leave the law and be content with grace? He says that
one is not justified from works of law, not from commandments; for the
commandment justifies when it is heard, but the work was what is hard to
accomplish. Gal 2:18 The word is addressed to Peter: you destroyed these
things by a decree which was sent from you to the Antiochenes and to all the
inhabited world. It is unseemly, then, to teach others these things, of whose
care you withdrew and which you destroyed by your own decree. Gal 2:19- The
law has the working and the enjoyment of bodily things: eat this and do not
eat that, keep sabbath, circumcision. Grace does not want you to be approved
through these. I was put to death, then, he says, to these. What need have I
of law? I no longer live, but Christ lives in me. What I was living for
myself, I was under law. But since Christ lives in me, who is no longer under
law but in the heavens at the right hand of the Father, I do not set aside the
one who lives in me. And what I now live in the flesh, I live in faith. For I
believe in the Son of God. And if the Son of God in whom I believe is at the
right hand of the Father, I have my citizenship in the heavens; but the law
had its observances among the things on earth. I am not, then, subject to law.
301 Gal 3:19-22 The law weighed more heavily, and for this reason it has been
named a ministry of death. How then was it added for the sake of the
transgressions, he says? In a brief word it is of this sort: before the law
even the mind of men, together with the flesh, was enslaved to sin and was
content with the evil; once the law had been given, the mind was educated, but
the flesh was still held. Since, then, the one who does not know that sin is
sin pursues impiety as piety and attends to it more eagerly, whereas the one
who knows that it is evil will at least gain this, the consciousness that he
transgresses, and he desires to obey God. For this reason a law was given that
was able to give life, that is, if a law had been given; it was not given, he
says, against the promises of God, nor was it able to prevail through
everything. Gal 3:23 Even if the law accomplished nothing else, yet it
furnished the knowledge of God. And the knowledge of God made one ready for
the reception of grace. Gal 3:27-28 Not the keeping of the law, he says, makes
the Jews who believed better than those from the nations, but the power of
baptism, furnishing the putting on of Christ, makes the grace common to all
together, barbarians, Greeks, Jews, female, male. And he did not simply mix in
the female and the male (for as far as one point he was seeking by the word
toward the matter at hand), but if baptism gathers the differing male and
female into one grace, how much more Greek and Jew. Gal 4:8-10 Now he wishes
to show that after grace has come, even if someone seems according to the law
to keep sabbath and new moons and feasts, he is as it were serving as idols
the elements which he worshiped when he was a Gentile, being enslaved in
another way. And he does not keep the law; for it has ceased. And by calling
them poor elements he did not accuse them as evil, but as unable to enrich and
to suffice for piety. 302 Gal 4:12 Do not think, he says, that I had some
other grief toward you and for this reason I rebuke; for the passion of the
pain continually forces him to change the form of his speech, now to censure,
now to exhort, at one time to accuse, at another to lament. Here then he
teaches that what he writes, he writes out of affection (for nothing, he says,
have I been wronged by you), but also as a healing, because I have been deemed
worthy of very much indeed. Gal 4:15-18 What then was the blessing? That is,
none. Or thus: the keeping of the law, or living by faith? And since he said
that those men do not want you to be zealous, so that they themselves may have
the perfection of the faith by not keeping the law, but want you to be zealous
for them, he added that I say these things not envying them; for I would that
they too were perfect, having used the faith, and it would have been
unhindered for them to be emulated and to imitate us. You then do such things
as make you objects of zeal, that all may imitate you, and do not become such
only when I come, out of respect for me, but even when I am not present
correct yourselves. Gal 4:23-24 Now he used the name, not the force of
allegory. For allegory does not keep step with the things said, but introduces
certain other things that appear from the sequence according to the weave of
the thoughts, such as Let him kiss me with the kisses of his mouth, the verse
in the Song of Songs; for there there is neither kiss nor hair nor belly nor
thighs nor any other such thing, but other things are introduced through other
things, and this is the proper kind of allegory. But here both the history is
confessed, and that it is a type of the future has been shown. The prophecy
through works, then, has now been named allegory. For of the prophecies some
are through words only, as Behold, the virgin shall conceive in the womb, some
through works only, as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, and some
both through works and through words, as what was spoken to 303 Jeremiah: Take
a new girdle and put it on; then bury it by the Euphrates, and when it has
decayed with time and been eaten through, take it, he says, from there. For up
to this the prophecy is through works, but what follows is also through words:
that thus I also put the sons of Israel around me as a girdle and thus I
helped them, and thus I lead them away captive into Babylon, and when they
have decayed and been corrupted I will bring them up from there and restore
them. Now then he named the prophecy through works allegory. Gal 4:25 He did
not simply mention Mount Sinai, but because the Hagarenes, those from Ishmael,
have the desert stretching as far as Mount Sinai. And on Sinai the law was
given, which bears unto slavery, and from Mount Sinai the tablets that had
been given were brought among Israel, and having been transferred many times
from place to place they were at last established in Jerusalem. For this
reason he added: and it corresponds to the present Jerusalem, and she is
enslaved with her children. Where there is promise of earth, and possession
and marriage and human things, slavery also follows; but where there is
release from the cares upon earth and from the bonds, and the citizenship is
in the heavens, from the citizenship itself comes the freedom. Gal 5:17 It is
asked why, drawing them away from the law, he mentions sin. Because the law
was commanding them to live in luxury, and through this desire is provoked
and, being unrestrainable, leads toward sin. For this reason, when there was
mention of the law, he mentions sin also. For even in the epistle to the
Romans, mentioning the law and the correction through the Lord, he introduced,
for those who live according to the flesh cannot please God. And why was
living in luxury and eating conceded? Because abstinence from the confessed
evils would not have gone forward unless eating 304 and drinking had been
yielded to them; for the lawgiver knew that it is impossible for those living
in luxury to master pleasures. But for the time he wished to educate the mind,
that it might know what is good and what is evil and what is pious; for he
foresaw that he was about to come who adds what is lacking to the law, I mean
not walking according to the flesh, as Paul says. Gal 6:7-8 He made clear from
this that many were spending unseasonably, furnishing support to those who
exhort them to keep the law, but furnishing nothing to those who preach Paul's
teaching. Wherefore he says: he who sows unto his flesh; for he who is subject
to law lives in the fleshly things. He then who furnishes support to the one
teaching these things sows unto the flesh only. Gal 6:14-15 Lest he be thought
to claim uncircumcision and not circumcision, and give a pretext of slander to
those who contradict, he most finely added the last, that neither circumcision
avails anything nor uncircumcision, and he said a new creation. Concerning
which elsewhere: if anyone is in Christ, a new creation. If a new creation, he
says, do not come under law; for he who lives according to law lives in the
world, but he who lives in Christ has his citizenship in the heavens. The
world then has been crucified to me and I to the world.
