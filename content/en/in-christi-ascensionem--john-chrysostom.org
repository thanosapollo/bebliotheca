* On the Ascension of Christ
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of our father among the saints John Chrysostom, a discourse on the
ascension of our Lord Jesus Christ.
"Let the heavens be glad and let the earth rejoice, because today having
loosed the middle wall of the partition" Jesus according to Paul, and
having made peace of the enmity between God and our nature, joined the
things above to the things below. Today the Only-begotten has offered to
the Father the firstfruits of the nature which he assumed from us; today
the sacrifice that has taken place on behalf of the inhabited world has
been offered living to God. Today the dragon has been beheaded; today
the serpent has been put to death; today the devil has been cast out of
heaven; today the sin of humanity has been taken away. Today the man who
through the disobedience of Adam was banished from paradise has, through
the second Adam, gone up not into an earthly paradise, "but into heaven
itself," according to the blessed Paul, "to appear now before the face
of God." And who are the witnesses of the Lord's ascension into the
heavens? Twelve disciples. But you doubt, O Jew, and disbelieve the
apostles of Christ and the eyes that beheld the Lord going up into the
heavens in a cloud; you do not believe so many witnesses? Will you not
then be persuaded even by the lawgiver Moses? For what does Moses say:
"Upon the mouth of two witnesses and three every word shall stand."
Wretched and miserable one; Moses the lawgiver says that three witnesses
agreeing about every matter are worthy of belief; and when Luke the
Evangelist produces four times three trustworthy witnesses, you reject
them, O profane and lawless one? Let the prophet Isaiah say, "Your neck
is an iron sinew, and your forehead bronze."
You are not persuaded by our twelve witnesses who became eyewitnesses of
the Lord's ascension into the heavens? Let one alone now stop your
mouth, Daniel the prophet; to whom, even if you act shamelessly ten
thousand times, you cannot speak against. Let Daniel the prophet come,
stopping up your shameless mouth also, just as he once did that of the
dragon, and bursting your belly also, even as he did that of that beast
then. What then did Daniel say, prophesying concerning the Lord's
ascension? Let us hear: "I was watching," he says, "and behold: upon the
clouds of heaven one like a son of man going up" he was. For his second
coming also will be like the former ascension, as the book of the Acts
also indicated, saying: "As the apostles were gazing while the Lord was
going into heaven. And behold: two men stood by them in white clothing,
who also said: Men of Galilee, why do you stand looking into heaven?
This Jesus, who was taken up from you into heaven, shall so come in like
manner as you beheld him going into heaven." Attend carefully, I beg
you, how the prophet clearly made known the hypostases: "Behold," he
says, "upon the clouds of heaven one like a son of man coming; and he
came as far as the Ancient of Days." And let us consider what the end of
the prophecy is: "And to him," he says, "was given the rule and the
honor and the kingdom." Understand wisely to whom he says the rule has
been given: not to the God Word who exists together with the Father from
eternity; for he always has the power, but to the ensouled body united
to him from Mary, which also today goes up into the heavens upon a
cloud. For he is the same, God and perfect man. "To him therefore was
given the rule and the honor and the authority and the kingdom"
according to the economy of the flesh. "And all the peoples, tribes,
tongues shall serve him, because his authority is an everlasting
authority, which shall not pass away nor be destroyed," and "his kingdom
shall not be given to another people."
Let the Jews be ashamed, reading the prophets and not having known the
Christ who was prophesied; but let us worship and glorify him, because
his is the glory and the power unto the ages of ages. Amen.
