Maecenas, you take ship among the tall
ramparts of Caesar's fleet, Liburnian-borne,
and make his danger yours. And we, whose life
is joy if you come through, and grief if not:
are we to sit, as ordered, in a peace
that sours without you, or to take this labor
as hard men take it? We will take it.
Over the Alpine ridges, over Caucasus
that gives no welcome, to the last western bay,
we follow with a steady heart. You ask
what my work adds to yours, I who am
no fighter, and not strong. I go as friend
under the smaller fear. The greater one
belongs to those who stay. A mother bird
dreads the snake more when she has left her unfledged
brood, though sitting by them she could give
no greater help. Gladly I serve this war
and every war, hoping your good grace,
not for more oxen leaning in my plough,
not for herds driven from Calabria
to Lucania before the Dog Star burns,
not for a pale villa on Tusculum
to touch Circe's walls. You have made me rich
enough, and more. I will not scrape together
what a Chremes would hide in the ground,
or a loose-belted spendthrift throw away.

"Happy the man who, far from the world's business,
like the old race of mortals, works his father's
fields with his own oxen, free of usury,
not wakened by the harsh horn as a soldier,
not shrinking from an angry sea, who shuns
the Forum and the proud doors of the great.
So he weds grown vine-shoots to tall poplars,
or in a folded valley watches herds
wander and low, or prunes the useless branch
and grafts a happier one, or stores pressed honey
in clean jars, or shears the weakling sheep.
Or when Autumn lifts over the fields a head
comely with mellow fruit, how he rejoices
to pick the grafted pears and the grape that vies
with purple, gifts for you, Priapus, and you,
father Silvanus, keeper of the bounds.
It is sweet to lie now under an old oak,
now on the clinging grass: meanwhile the waters
slide between high banks, birds complain in the woods,
and leaves murmur against the running springs,
invitation enough to a light sleep.
But when Jove's winter musters rain and snow,
he drives fierce boars with many a hound
this way and that into the waiting nets,
or stretches wide-meshed snares on a smooth pole,
traps for greedy thrushes, and takes with a noose
the frightened hare and the stranger crane,
welcome prizes. Who among these things
does not forget the evil cares of love?
And if a modest wife does her part
for house and sweet children, a Sabine woman,
or the sunburnt wife of a nimble Apulian,
piling the sacred hearth with old wood
against her tired husband's coming, shutting
the happy flock in woven hurdles, draining
the swollen udders, drawing this year's wine
from the sweet jar to set out unbought feasts:
Lucrine shellfish would not please me more,
nor turbot, nor wrasse, though winter, thundering
on eastern waves, should drive them toward this sea;
no African fowl would go down into my belly,
no Ionian grouse, more pleasant than olives
picked from the fattest boughs, or sorrel
that loves the meadows, and mallows wholesome
for a heavy body, or a lamb slain
on the feast of Terminalia, or a kid
snatched from the wolf. Among such meals what joy
to see the fed sheep hurrying home, to see
the weary oxen dragging the turned plough
on a languid neck, and the house-born slaves,
swarm of a rich house, ranged about the shining Lares."
When Alfius the moneylender had said this,
already, already about to be a farmer,
he called in all his money on the Ides,
and looks to put it out again on the Kalends.

If ever a man has broken with impious hand
his father's aged throat, let him eat garlic,
more deadly than hemlock. O hard guts of reapers!
What is this poison raging in my vitals?
Has viper's blood, cooked into these herbs,
deceived me? Or has Canidia handled
the evil feast? When Medea, marveling
at the leader who outshone all the Argonauts,
smeared Jason with this, who was to bind
unknown yokes on the bulls; with this she anointed
the gifts that avenged her on the rival,
and fled on a winged serpent. Never did
so great a heat of the stars settle
on thirsty Apulia, nor did the shirt
burn hotter on the shoulders of Hercules.
But if you ever long for such a thing,
joking Maecenas, I pray your girl may put
her hand against your kiss, and lie
at the far edge of the bed.

As great a discord as fate allotted
to wolves and lambs, such is mine with you,
your flank seared with Spanish ropes, your legs
hard from the shackle. Strut proud on your money:
fortune does not change breed. Do you see,
as you measure the Sacred Way in a toga
six ells wide, how frank indignation turns
the faces of those going this way and that?
"This man, cut with the triumvirs' whips
till the crier sickened of it, ploughs a thousand
acres of Falernian land and wears the Appian
with his cobs, and sits, a great knight,
in the front seats, scorning Otho. What use
to lead so many beaked prows of ships,
heavy with bronze, against pirates and a slave band,
with this, this as military tribune?"

"But O whatever of the gods in heaven
rules earth and the human race, what does this
uproar mean, and why are all faces savage
upon me alone? By your children, if Lucina
came when she was called to true births,
by this empty honor of purple I pray,
by Jupiter who will condemn these things:
why do you look at me as a stepmother looks,
or as at a beast hunted with steel?"
When the boy had made this complaint
with trembling mouth, he stood with his ornaments
torn away, a body not yet grown, such as might
soften the impious hearts of Thracians.
Canidia, her hair bound with short vipers
and her head unkempt, orders fig trees torn
from tombs, orders funeral cypresses,
and eggs smeared with a foul toad's blood,
and the feather of the night screech-owl,
and herbs that Iolcos and Iberia,
rich in poisons, send, and bones snatched
from a starving bitch's mouth, to be burned
in Colchian flames. But Sagana, skirts kilted,
through the whole house sprinkles waters of Avernus,
her rough hair bristling like a sea-urchin
or a Laurentian boar. Veia, checked by no
conscience, was exhausting the ground with hard
mattocks, groaning at the labor, so the buried
boy might die watching a meal changed two
or three times through the long day, his face
jutting as far as bodies hung by the chin
stand out of water: that his sucked marrow
and dry liver might be a cup of love,
once his pupils, fixed on the forbidden food,
had wasted away. Nor was Folia of Ariminum,
of masculine lust, believed wanting, by idle
Naples and every neighboring town, she who
by a Thessalian voice chants down the stars
and tears the moon from the sky. Here Canidia,
savage, gnawing her uncut thumb with a livid
tooth, what did she say, or what leave unsaid?
"O not unfaithful witnesses of my affairs,
Night and Diana, you who rule the silence
when secret rites are done, now, now be present,
now turn wrath and power upon hostile houses.
When the beasts lie in fearful woods, faint
with sweet sleep, let the dogs of the Subura
bark at the old adulterer, that all may laugh,
smeared with nard such as my hands could not
work more perfectly. What has happened?
Why do the dread poisons of barbarian Medea
avail less, with which she fled, avenged
on the proud rival, daughter of great Creon,
when the robe, a gift soaked in corruption,
took the new bride with fire? And yet no herb,
no root hidden in rough places, has escaped me.
Does he sleep on anointed beds in forgetfulness
of all his mistresses? Ah, ah, he walks free
by the spell of a witch who knows more.
Not with ordinary draughts, Varus, O head
that will weep much, will you run back to me,
nor will your mind return, called by Marsian
voices. I will prepare a greater thing,
I will pour you a greater cup in your disdain,
and the sky will sink below the sea,
the earth spread over it, before you cease
to burn with my love as pitch burns with black fire."
At this the boy no longer, as before, tried
to soften the impious women with gentle words,
but doubtful from where to break the silence,
sent forth Thyestean prayers: "Magic poisons
cannot overturn right and wrong, cannot turn
aside the human due. I will hunt you with curses:
a dread execration is expiated by no victim.
No: when I have breathed my last, ordered to perish,
I will meet you as a night Fury, and as a shade
I will go at your faces with hooked nails,
which is the power of the gods of the dead,
and sitting on your restless hearts I will take
sleep away with terror. A crowd, hunting you
street by street with stones from this side and that,
will crush you, filthy old women; afterward
wolves and the birds of the Esquiline will scatter
your unburied limbs, and this spectacle, alas,
will not escape my parents who outlive me."

Why do you harry innocent strangers, you dog,
coward against wolves? Why not turn your empty
threats this way, if you can, and come at me,
who will bite back? For like a Molossian
or a tawny Spartan, a strength dear to shepherds,
I will drive through deep snows, ear raised,
whatever beast goes before; you, when you have
filled the grove with a voice to be feared,
sniff at food thrown down. Beware, beware:
I am fiercest against the wicked, and I lift
horns already prepared, like the rejected
son-in-law of faithless Lycambes, or the bitter
enemy of Bupalus. If anyone comes at me
with a black tooth, shall I weep unavenged,
like a child?

Where, where are you rushing, you wicked men?
Or why are sheathed swords fitted again
to right hands? Has too little Latin blood
been poured on the fields and over Neptune,
not that a Roman might burn the proud citadels
of envious Carthage, or that the unconquered
Briton might come down the Sacred Way in chains,
but that this City, answering the Parthians' prayers,
might perish by her own right hand? This was never
the way of wolves, nor of lions, except against
a different kind. Does blind fury snatch you,
or some sharper force, or guilt? Give your answer.
They are silent, and a white pallor stains
their faces, and stricken minds stand stunned.
So it is: bitter fates drive the Romans,
and the crime of a brother's murder, since
the blood of undeserving Remus flowed
upon the ground, a curse upon his descendants.

To ask, you who have rotted through a long age,
what unstrings my powers, when you have a black
tooth, and old age ploughs your brow with wrinkles,
and a filthy anus gapes between dry buttocks
like a cow's with the flux. But what stirs me
is the chest and the rotting breasts, like a mare's
udders, and the soft belly, and the thin thigh
set on swollen calves. Be happy: let a funeral
and triumphal images lead your cortege,
and let no married woman walk more laden
with rounder pearls. What of it, that Stoic
booklets love to lie among your silk cushions?
Do unlettered sinews stiffen less for that,
or does the member droop less? To call that forth
from a proud groin, you must work it with your mouth.

When, happy Maecenas, shall I drink with you,
glad in Caesar's victory, the Caecuban
stored for festive feasts, under a high roof
(so it please Jove), the lyre sounding a song
mixed with pipes, this Dorian, those barbarian?
As lately, when the Neptunian captain, driven
from the strait, fled with his ships burned,
he who had threatened the City with the chains
he had taken from the faithless slaves he befriended.
A Roman, alas (you who come after will deny it),
bound over to a woman, can bear palisade-stake
and arms as a soldier and serve wrinkled eunuchs,
and among the military standards the sun
looks on the shame of a mosquito-net. At this
the Gauls turned two thousand neighing horses,
singing Caesar, and the sterns of the enemy ships
hide in harbor, driven hard to the left.
Io Triumphe, do you delay the golden chariots
and the unyoked oxen? Io Triumphe, you have not
brought back a leader equal to the Jugurthine war,
nor Africanus, whose valor built a tomb
over Carthage. The enemy, beaten on land and sea,
has changed his cloak for a Punic one of mourning.
Either he will make for Crete, famous
for a hundred cities, on winds not his own,
or he seeks the Syrtes the south wind works,
or he is carried on an uncertain sea.
Bring larger cups here, boy, and Chian wines
or Lesbian, or measure us Caecuban
to check the flowing nausea. It is joy
to loose with sweet Lyaeus the care and fear
for Caesar's fortunes.

The ship puts out under an evil bird,
bearing stinking Mevius. Remember, Auster,
to beat both his flanks with bristling waves;
let black Eurus, on a sea turned upside down,
scatter broken rigging and shattered oars;
let the North wind rise as great as when it snaps
trembling oaks on high mountains; and let no
friendly star appear in the black night
when grim Orion sets; nor let him be carried
on a quieter sea than the Greek band of victors,
when Pallas turned her wrath from burned Ilium
upon the impious raft of Ajax. O how much
sweat awaits your sailors, and a yellow pallor
you, and that unmanly wailing, and prayers
to a Jove who has turned away, when the Ionian
bay, bellowing under a wet south wind,
has broken the keel. And if the rich prey,
stretched on the curved shore, should please the gulls,
a lusty goat and a lamb will be sacrificed
to the Storms.

Pettius, nothing now delights me as before,
to write little verses, struck by a heavy love,
a love that hunts me beyond all others to burn
for soft boys or for girls. This third December,
since I ceased to rave for Inachia, shakes
the honor from the woods. Alas, through the City
(for I am ashamed of so great an evil)
what a story I was, and I repent of the banquets
in which languor and silence proved the lover,
and a sigh drawn from the depth of the side.
"That a poor man's bright talent avails nothing
against cash," I would complain, weeping to you,
as soon as the shameless god, when I was warm,
had moved secrets from their place with a fiercer wine.
"But if free bile should seethe in my heart,
so as to scatter these thankless poultices
to the winds, which ease no bad wound, shame,
driven off, will cease to contend with unequals."
When I had praised these things sternly in your
presence, ordered to go home I was carried
on uncertain foot to posts unfriendly, alas,
to me, and alas, hard thresholds, on which I broke
my loins and my side. Now the love of Lyciscus
holds me, who boasts that he can outdo any
little woman in softness; from which the free
counsels of friends cannot disentangle me,
nor heavy insults, but another fire, either
a bright girl or a slim boy tying back
his long hair.

What do you want, woman most worthy
of black elephants? Why send me gifts,
why tablets, to a young man neither firm
nor thick of nostril? For I alone scent
more keenly whether a polyp or a heavy goat
lies in shaggy armpits than a sharp dog
scents where a boar is hiding. What sweat
on the wrinkled limbs, and how bad a smell
grows everywhere, when with the member slack
she hurries to quiet her untamed rage,
and already the damp chalk will not stay,
nor the color dyed with crocodile dung,
and already with her heat she bursts
the taut bedding and the canopy. Or when
she works my disgust with savage words:
"You go limp less with Inachia than with me;
you can do Inachia three times in a night,
for me you are always soft at one labor.
May she die badly, that Lesbia who, when I
was looking for a bull, pointed you out
as a sluggish thing. When Coan Amyntas
was with me, in whose untamed groin the sinew
holds more constantly than a young tree
clings to the hills. For whom were the fleeces
of wool dyed twice in Tyrian murex being hurried?
For you, of course, so that among the guests
there should not be a man of your years
whom his own woman loved more than you.
O I am not lucky, whom you flee as a lamb
fears fierce wolves, and does fear lions!"

A bristling storm has drawn the sky together,
and rains and snows bring Jove down. Now the sea,
now the woods sound with the Thracian North wind.
Friends, let us snatch the occasion from the day,
and while the knees are green and it is fitting,
let old age be loosed from the clouded brow.
You, bring out wines pressed in my Torquatus'
consulship. Leave the rest unsaid: a god
perhaps will set these things back in their seat
by a kind turn. Now it is joy to be steeped
in Persian nard and to lighten the breast
from dread cares with the Cyllenean lyre,
as the noble Centaur sang to his great pupil:
"Unconquered one, mortal boy born of the goddess
Thetis, the land of Assaracus awaits you,
which the cold streams of little Scamander split,
and the slippery Simois, from which the Fates
have broken your return with a sure thread,
nor will your sea-blue mother carry you home.
There lighten every evil with wine and song,
sweet addresses of unsightly sorrow."

Why soft idleness has poured such forgetfulness
through my inmost senses, as if I had drawn
with a dry throat cups that bring Lethean sleep,
dear Maecenas, you kill me by asking often.
A god, a god forbids me to finish the iambs
I began, a song promised long ago.
Not otherwise they say Anacreon of Teos
burned for Samian Bathyllus, who very often
wept his love on the hollow tortoise-shell
in a simple measure. You yourself burn,
poor man: but if no fairer fire kindled
besieged Ilium, rejoice in your lot;
a freedwoman, and not content with one,
Phryne wastes me.

It was night, and the Moon shone in a clear sky
among the lesser stars, when you, about to offend
the power of the great gods, swore in words
I dictated, clinging with pliant arms more tightly
than a tall oak is bound by ivy, that while
the wolf is a foe to the flock and Orion,
hostile to sailors, troubles the winter sea,
and while the breeze stirs Apollo's unshorn hair,
this love would be mutual, O Neaera
who will suffer much by my manhood. For if
there is anything of a man in Flaccus,
he will not bear your giving constant nights
to a stronger rival, and in anger he will seek
an equal, nor will his constancy, once offended,
yield to beauty, if a sure pain has entered.
And you, whoever you are who are luckier
and now walk proud in my hurt, though you be
rich in flock and much land, and Pactolus
flow for you, and the secrets of Pythagoras
born again not escape you, and you surpass
Nireus in form, alas, alas, you will mourn
loves moved elsewhere, and I in turn will laugh.

Already another age is worn away by civil wars,
and Rome herself falls by her own strength:
she whom neither the neighboring Marsians
could destroy, nor the threatening Etruscan
band of Porsena, nor the rival valor of Capua,
nor fierce Spartacus, nor the Allobrox
unfaithful in revolution, nor wild Germany
with her blue-eyed youth, nor Hannibal,
abominated by our fathers. We, an impious age
of accursed blood, will destroy her, and beasts
will occupy the soil again. A barbarian
victor, alas, will stand on the ashes,
and a horseman will beat the City with sounding
hoof, and the bones of Quirinus that lack
winds and suns (unlawful to see) the insolent
one will scatter. Perhaps you ask, all together
or the better part, what would serve to be free
of these evil labors. Let no counsel be preferred
to this: as the city of the Phocaeans fled
under a curse, and left fields and ancestral
Lares and shrines to be inhabited by boars
and rapacious wolves, let us go wherever
our feet bear us, wherever through the waves
the South wind or violent Africus shall call.
Is this your pleasure? Or has anyone better
to advise? Why do we delay to take ship
under a second omen? But let us swear to this:
"When stones, lifted from the lowest shallows,
swim again, then let return not be a crime;
and let it not irk us to set sails homeward
when the Po has washed the Matine peaks,
or the high Apennine has run out into the sea,
and a strange love has joined new monsters
in lust, so that it please the tiger to couch
with deer, and the dove to play adulteress
with the kite, and the credulous herds not fear
tawny lions, and the smooth goat love the salt sea."
These things, and whatever can cut off a sweet
return, let us go, the whole city under a curse,
or the part better than the unteachable flock;
let the soft and hopeless press their ill-omened
beds. You who have courage, put away womanish
grief, and fly past the Etruscan shores.
The Ocean that wanders round awaits us:
let us seek the blessed fields, the fields
and the rich islands, where the unploughed earth
returns Ceres every year, and the unpruned vine
flowers always, and the shoot of the never-failing
olive buds, and the dark fig adorns its own tree,
honey drips from the hollow oak, and from the high
mountains light water leaps down with a splashing
foot. There the goats come unbidden to the
milking-pails, and the friendly flock brings back
taut udders, nor does the evening bear growl
round the fold, nor the deep ground swell with vipers.
And we, happy, will wonder at more: that watery
Eurus does not scrape the fields with lavish rains,
nor are the rich seeds burned in dry clods,
the king of the heaven-dwellers tempering both.
The pine of Argo with her oarsman did not make
for this place, nor did the unchaste Colchian
set her foot here; Sidonian sailors did not
twist their yardarms hither, nor the toiling
band of Ulysses. No contagions harm the flock,
the burning violence of no star parches the herd.
Jupiter set those shores apart for a pious people,
when he stained the golden time with bronze,
with bronze, then hardened the ages with iron,
from which a happy flight is given to the pious,
with me for prophet.

"Already, already I yield my hands to your
working craft, and as a suppliant I pray
by the kingdoms of Proserpina, by Diana's
powers that must not be moved, and by the books
of songs strong enough to call the stars down
unfixed from the sky, Canidia: spare at last
the sacred voices, and unwind, unwind
the swift wheel. Telephus moved the grandson
of Nereus, against whom he had proudly arrayed
the lines of Mysians and against whom he had
hurled sharp weapons. The Ilian mothers anointed
Hector the man-slayer, given to wild birds
and dogs, after the king fell, leaving the walls,
at the feet, alas, of stubborn Achilles.
The toiling oarsmen of Ulysses stripped
their bristly limbs of the hard hides, Circe
willing; then mind and voice slipped back,
and the known honor into their faces. I have
given you enough and more of punishments,
you much loved by sailors and peddlers.
Youth has fled, and modest color has left
bones clothed in a sallow hide; my hair
is white from your unguents; no leisure
lays me down from labor; night presses day
and day night, nor can I lighten the taut
chest with breath. Therefore I am beaten, wretch,
into believing what I denied: that Sabine
spells can make the breast resound, and a Marsian
dirge split the head. What more do you want?
O sea and earth, I burn, as much as Hercules
smeared with the black blood of Nessus, nor
the Sicilian flame growing green on burning Etna.
You, until I am borne, dry ash, on the injurious
winds, are hot as a workshop of Colchian poisons.
What end, or what pay, awaits me? Speak;
I will pay the ordered penalties in good faith,
ready to expiate, whether you demand a hundred
bullocks, or wish to be sounded on a lying lyre:
'You, chaste, you, honest, will walk the stars,
a golden constellation.' Castor, offended
at the treatment of infamous Helen, and the brother
of great Castor, overcome by prayer, gave back
to the bard the light that had been taken:
and you, for you can, loose me from madness,
O you not stained by a father's filth, nor
a wise old woman to scatter the ninth-day dust
in the graves of the poor. Yours is a hospitable
heart and clean hands, and Pactumeius is yours,
born of your womb, and the midwife washed in your blood
the red cloths, whenever you leap up, a strong
woman in childbed."
"Why do you pour prayers into barred ears?
The rocks that winter Neptune beats with the deep
brine are not deafer to naked sailors. Shall you,
unpunished, have laughed at the Cotytian rites
made public, the sacrament of free Cupid,
and as pontiff of Esquiline witchcraft fill
the City with my name unpunished? What had it
profited to enrich the Paelignian old women,
or to mix a quicker poison? But a slower fate
than you pray for awaits you: a thankless life
must be led by you, wretch, in this, that you
may still be at hand for new labors. The father
of faithless Pelops longs for rest, Tantalus
always in want of the kindly feast; Prometheus
bound to the bird longs for it; Sisyphus longs
to set the stone on the top of the mountain;
but the laws of Jove forbid. You will wish now
to leap from high towers, and in vain you will
knot bonds on your throat, now to open your breast
with a Norican sword, grim with wearying sorrow.
Then I will ride as a horseman on your unfriendly
shoulders, and the earth will yield to my insolence.
Or shall I, who could move waxen images,
as you yourself know, being curious, and tear
the moon from the sky with my voices, who could
raise the cremated dead and mix the cups of desire,
weep at the outcome of an art that does nothing
against you?"
