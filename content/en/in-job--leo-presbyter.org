Of Leontius, presbyter of Constantinople, a discourse on Job. Come then today
too, friend, let us more genuinely handle with the word the inviolable
treasure of the most noble Job: a treasure not imprisoned in a purse, but
fortified in the heavens; a treasure not weighed by a quantity of money, but
guarded by vigor of soul. Therefore one must keep vigil over the soul's
treasure rather than over the money-treasure, where robbers sit in wait and
often keep watch, that they may make the master of the house nod off and
plunder the runaway wealth. But over the soul's treasure robbers do not sit in
wait; rather demons are sent, that they may plunder the right reasoning, that
they may beguile away the unadulterated faith, that they may scatter the
wealth of endurance. For it is not loss of money that makes a man truly
God-loving gloomy, but a destitution of reasonings. Therefore also the Lord,
recording as a great loss the destruction of the soul, said to those who obey
him: What will it profit a man if he gain the whole world and lose his soul?
And again, setting it out expressly to his disciples, he said: Watch and pray,
for at an hour you do not know the thief comes. And who is the thief? The one
who kept watch against Job and found nothing, but shook out his bosom with
empty hands. Concerning the soul's treasure, then, the Lord, exhorting, said:
What will it profit a man if he gain the whole world and lose his soul?
Knowing this clearly, the most noble Job despised all his substance, that he
might not suffer loss of soul; he overlooked oxen, he did not overlook
virtues; he despised sheep, he did not spit on achievements; he stripped off
the grief for the children, he put on the glory of the saints. Therefore also
when the devil saw Job secured on every side and walled more by God-loving
works than by hewn stones, the devil argued with himself, saying: What shall I
do? How shall I plunder Job's wealth? How shall I put him to sleep, a man
being tested in the affairs of life? He is rich and does not blaspheme; so
great a bodyguard, and nowhere a sin; he reigns and does not stumble, but in
all things he reigns. He is in palaces, and I in a wilderness; he is called
righteous and blameless and true, but I "serpent" and "scorpion" and "devil".
What shall I do? I baited Adam, I murdered Abel, I gained Cain, I sent Noah
off with drunkenness, I prepared Ham to be a slave, I made Jacob a fugitive, I
deprived Esau of the birthright; Job I cannot find a way to shake? I have
understood the matter, I know what I shall do; he is helped from above, and
for this reason he is not driven into harm. If the God over all had granted me
to stand with the angels, I would have said to him: "Why is Job blessed in
vain? He worships you for a wage; you guard him, and for this reason he offers
you a small thanksgiving." Arguing these things with himself the devil added
again, saying in himself: And when I was saying these things to the Lord, the
Lord answered, saying to me: "If you think Job great for this reason, because
of the abundance of his money, I give you the authority; harvest his money,
that you may learn that he leans not on money but on achievements." If I had
received this answer from the Lord, and had known securely that he no longer
holds to Job, I would have made known to all those present and to the one
above all what a blasphemer Job is. Listen with understanding. These devices
of the devil according to thought against Job the divine scripture
personified, and

presented the devil as answering the Lord and the Lord the devil. For the sake
of personification, not of truth; for the meaning does not already follow the
wording; the words are set down in one way, the meanings are made clear in
another. Therefore all those present must know clearly that from the time the
devil fell from the heavens he has not seen the face of God, he has not been
counted worthy of the former way of life. For just as an earthly king, having
made a man a tyrant out of his presence, does not receive him before his eyes,
turning away from him because of the tyranny, so also when the devil practiced
rebellion the Lord, having cast him out, no longer counted him worthy of the
vision toward him, not because the Lord hated him, but because that one did
not bend toward conversion. These things being thus known, let us look at what
was read, that we may make clear what was set forth. You heard the historian
Moses in the present evening, which evening I did not wish to call evening but
rather dawn. For what evening ought one to call this, or the sisters of this
evening, in which all those present have arrived as from bed, fasting and
sober, except the clean who are filthy? For these alone stink in a Jewish way.
Therefore the present evening of the fast is again dawn because of the
lightness of the stomach and the sobriety of the soul, but for those it is
night because of the heaviness of the head from wine and the eating of meats.
What then did the historian Moses say? You heard him just now saying: And it
came to pass as this day, and the angels of God came to stand before the Lord,
and the devil came in the midst of them. And it came to pass as this day. What
day? The one that makes known the contests of Job. It came to pass as this
day. What day? The one that set the heavenly archangelic powers present for
the spectacle of the wrestling. For as soon as the Lord saw the devil having
conceived against Job, at once again the Lord was commanding the powers above,
saying: Look down, see an exceptional wrestling; look down, see how the one
from among you who left you and what is yours wrestles madly below with the
one who shares nothing according to essence; look down, see how the one who
fell once falls again; look down, see, that none of you may become as that
one; look down, see Job being strengthened and the devil being unstrung, the
one arrogant in paradise and put to shame on the dunghill. It came to pass as
this day. Truly a day was Job's; for he was noble, of those from the risings
of the sun; day for Job, night for the devil; for he groped and did not find.
It came to pass as this day, and the angels of God came to stand before the
Lord, and the devil came in the midst of them. The slander of the name is
enough for him as a presentation of praises. He was called devil; what he did
he received, what he sought he was named, what he wished he inherited; no
longer angel, the name of the nature, but devil, the watchword of the
intention; angels are angels from announcing, devil is devil from slandering.
It came to pass as this day, and the angels came to stand before the Lord, and
the devil came in the midst of them. He came in the midst, no longer wearing
the face of boldness but that of shame, no longer bringing the royal robe but
the satanic rag. And the devil came in the midst of them, and the Lord said to
the devil: From where have you come? O the paradoxical things. Did the Lord
not know from where the devil had come, he who knows the deep and hidden
things, the tracker of hearts? From where have you come? He was not asking as
one who did not know, but as one wishing to condemn him from his own mouth;
for so he convicted Cain, who was of like manner with him, saying to him:
Where is Abel your brother? He was not asking as one who did not know, he who
hears the voice of the blood, but wishing to condemn him more completely. And
the devil says


he came in the midst of them, and the Lord said to the devil: From where have
you come? And the devil to the Lord: From where have I come? Having gone
around the earth and walked about under heaven I am present. And the Lord to
the devil: Having gone around the earth and walked about it you have come?
What do you say, deceiver? You traveled around the earth? And what were you
doing on the earth? What have I made you, a man or an angel? I have given the
earth to men, the heaven to angels; to each order I allotted its own place.
How have you left heaven and go about the earth? What is the profit, for you
to serve God in heaven or to live as a parasite on men upon the earth? Did you
need someone, that you left the wealth above and steal below? What is better
for you, to be glorified above as an angel or to be bound below with chains as
a runaway? You run around the earth, devil? You loved the earthly things, be
among the earthly. For I toss the man up into heaven; where you were banished
from, there I will plant him. Nevertheless tell me, devil, having gone around
the earth and walked about under heaven have you come? Why are you gloomy,
whom have you struck against, who grieves you? Do you know what you have
suffered? He over whom you expected to reign, he himself is master of you. And
the devil to the Lord, still arrogant as one who has not yet tasted the
defeat: Am I gloomy, Master? Not at all, by no means. I am master of all, I
have prevailed over all, no one among men looks me in the eye. Only do not
become man, and see how I am master of all. And the Lord to the devil: Do you
think to hide it from me, O devil? Have you feared no one? Does no one master
you? I do not set forth many; I mention one only. Have you given heed to my
servant Job, your king but my soldier, that there is not on the earth a man
like him, blameless, righteous, true, God-fearing, abstaining from every evil
deed? If I am wrong in bearing witness, contradict; if I am not speaking the
truth, overturn it; but if I speak just things, how did you say: "No one is
master of me"? And the devil to the Lord: Do you bear witness for Job, Master?
What holy thing has he accomplished? What worthy deed has he pursued? Job is
outside of blessings. Because he does not blaspheme you is he great, because
he does not seize is he honorable, because he feeds strangers is he wonderful?
If, digging and harvesting and toiling, receiving a wage, he were providing
for the poor, it would be reasonable for him to be counted blessed; but if he
gives to the poor from abundance, why is he counted blessed? And why is he
also honored as not greedy? Does Job lack? Does he seek and not find? He
offers you a small thanks; receiving much he offers little. Have you not
fenced about the things outside and the things inside of his house and of all
that is his round about? You have blessed the works of his hands and made his
cattle many upon the earth. Nevertheless send forth your hand and touch all
that he has, if indeed he will not bless you to your face. O the devil's
cunning. The faceless one calls blasphemy a blessing; the unseemly one speaks
solemnly. From that time even now until the present some often call the curse
a blessing, and wishing to curse someone against whom the fight is, they
answer him saying: Go away, man, God will bless you, that is, may you be
cursed from God. What do you say, man? You are going after the devil's voice.
This first voice the devil broke out saying: Nevertheless send forth your hand
and touch all that he has, if indeed he will not bless you to your face. What
is "send forth your hand"? That is, hold back a little your providential
power; do not stand before Job, but let him be grieved a little, suffer loss;
do not provide for him a little, and see how more shamelessly than all he
blasphemes you. Send forth your hand and touch all that he has. And the Lord
to the devil: I will send forth my hand and I will touch all that he has. Have
you begun to confess the defeat, devil? Where are the arrogances of your
rebellion? Are you not the one who says: I will set my throne upon the


clouds and I will be like the Most High? How is it that you do not prevail
over asses and camels, but seek my hand as an ally? But nevertheless I nod to
your request, not yielding to you, but wishing to show my soldier more
radiant. For the one well-pleasing in wealth is also proven in poverty. Wealth
is more burdensome than poverty and more precious toward sin; for what grass
is to fire, this wealth is to the vain-minded. But nevertheless Job, being
such, as he blossomed in wealth will also be crowned in poverty; for necessity
trains poverty, but purpose crowns wealth. Devil, I already foretell to you
that you will harvest all Job's substance, but you shall not dissolve his
courage. Behold, all that is his I give into your hand, but do not touch him
himself, not because the athlete is weak, but because I give the concession
also toward your request. You said that "He worships you because of money";
take the authority over the money only, that you may learn whether he is
master of the money or the money prevails over him. Having received this
authority the devil went out rejoicing from the face of the Lord, he who is
always going out from the face of the Lord. And having gone out he raised a
thousand-surge billow against Job, immediately overturning, burning,
destroying, taking captive, thinking that by the excess of the squall he could
sink the body, the living vessel of Job, not knowing that he is moored inside
the harbor, secured on every side by the anchor of faith. And that the devil
raised varied temptations and storms against Job, you heard just now the
writer saying: While this one was still speaking another messenger came saying
to Job: The yokes of oxen were ploughing and the she-asses were feeding beside
them; and the captors came and took them captive and killed the servants with
the edge of the sword; and I alone was saved and came to tell you. While this
one was still speaking: he multiplies successive messengers, thinking to
unstring the soldier. Do not think it a messenger, but understand the devil
himself; for the devil himself, as cunning and wise unto doing evil,
transforming himself into Job's servants, now as a shepherd, now as a
herdsman, now as the one who feeds the camels, thus frequently going himself
through himself brought him the approaches of the temptations. But the devil
was in these things, and Job stood as some rock holding out against the
violent waves, unbillowed and unshaken. Therefore Job bore nobly the
destruction of the oxen and of all the rest of the substance. But as soon as
he heard the fourth messenger bringing the similarly fashioned death of the
children, at once Job was bent a little, bowed by fatherly compassion, that it
might be known that he was a man. Therefore also as soon as he heard the
fourth messenger saying to him: While your sons and your daughters were eating
and drinking at the house of your son their older brother, suddenly a great
wind came out from the wilderness and touched the four corners of the house
and the house fell on your children and they died, having heard these things
Job at once checked and held the messenger, he looked around at the features,
that he might know who it is that reports and who has dared to bring such a
message. And the devil, being held, turned the features away, wished to run
away, could not bear at all to be held by a righteous man. Therefore also Job,
when he saw him in tumult and turning this way and that and not wishing to
show the face, not yet knowing who he is, growing harsh questioned him: Tell
me, you, the addition of the evils, a great wind came from the wilderness. Are
you not the wind? Are you not the citizen of the wilderness? A great wind came
from the wilderness, the form human and the face faceless. A great wind came
out from the wilderness and touched the four corners of the house. Tell me,
were you inside the house or outside? If you were inside, how were you saved?
If


you were spending time outside, how did you know that the children have died?
Nevertheless tell me, have the seven been melted, the equal-eared golden
pillars of my male offspring? Have the three virgin lamps of my daughters,
equal in light, been quenched? Has the ten-sparrow nest of the rational
nestlings been overthrown? Has the house become a tomb, the table a sepulchre,
the mixing-bowl a press? You have become the reporter of a great misfortune to
me. But nevertheless I rejoice exceedingly, that the seven colts were not
unyoked, nor the three heifers of the seven colts; as they lived, they also
died, they were not separated in their life, nor divided in the death. Great
evils have befallen me. Having lost all the substance I was grieved a little
while the children were still living, as not having any inheritance to leave
them; but when I have also offered the children as a gift to the giver, I am
no longer gloomy, having fulfilled Abraham's sacrifice in deed. And to God be
glory unto the ages of ages. Amen.
