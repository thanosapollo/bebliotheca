ORIGEN'S SELECTIONS ON THE PSALMS =17.105= PSALM 9. Verse 8. He prepared his
throne in judgment. Verse 9. And he himself will judge the inhabited world in
righteousness. He will judge peoples in uprightness. Such a judge, then, is to
be feared, as one flees blameworthy deeds and an impious mind. And you will
understand "inhabited world" as the Church, inhabited by both Father and Son,
according to: I and my Father will come, and we will make a dwelling with him;
and "peoples" as the rest besides the Church. Verse 12. Sing to the Lord, who
dwells in Zion. And otherwise: in every soul that is Zion and a watchtower God
dwells. And he seeks out the bloods of the nations that killed them, and he
remembers them, having raised them. Therefore the saying: Their memorial has
perished with a sound, will be referred to the swords. But he remembers those
of whom he became a refuge. Verse 33. Arise, O Lord my God; let your hand be
lifted up; do not forget your poor to the end. And being distressed by these
impious words, the =17.108= prophet again prays saying these things, asking
God to show by experience that he has no longer forgotten the poor. And he
also reminded of the blasphemy of the impious, saying: Verse 38. You have
heard the desire of the poor, O Lord; to the preparation of their heart attend
with your ear. For they desired that the arm of the sinner be crushed, and to
be freed from his tyranny; which indeed has also come to pass. And the desire
of a good mind is an effective prayer. Which the knower of hearts, having
seen, fulfilled. For they had a heart ready to be saved, and to obtain the
promised kingdom. PSALM 13. Verse 2. The Lord looked down from heaven upon the
sons of men, to see if there is one who understands, or who seeks God.
Whatever is said corporeally about God, who is incorporeal, must be heard in a
manner befitting God. For example, he is said to dwell in the heavens, as
using for a temple the divine powers that are in heaven. For they are called
by the same name as the places in which they are; as also men are styled
earth. As in: Let all the earth worship you, and: All the earth shouts with
gladness. And he dwells in the heavens even while being in men, those who bear
the image of the heavenly. But also as being in all creation by the word of
creation and of providence. This is shown from the fairest parts: both in, Our
Father who is in the heavens; and, God in heaven above. But also in many
places of the divine instruction the appellation of heaven indicates the
intelligible essence, in which it is especially fitting to seek God. For the
heaven of heaven is the Lord's. From this he inspects, as a concomitant, those
of the inferior condition, To see if there is anyone intelligent, seeking God;
so that having found him he might obtain the gifts from him. And this happens
when wickedness together with impiety prevails, so that it is said: There is
no mercy, nor truth, nor knowledge of God upon the earth, and the things upon
these. When men have been thus harmed, how should the good Savior not look
upon them, so that, having found some occasion of understanding, he might
multiply it, and manifest himself to those who seek? For he is manifested to
those who do not disbelieve him. Verse 3. All have turned aside; they have
together become unprofitable. There is none that does kindness, there is not
even one. He prophesies a change of the nations into gentleness and piety.
Therefore he also asks, Will they not, when the Lord has come, cease from the
beastlike life, and come to know the Lord? For Isaiah also says: Those to whom
it was not announced concerning him shall see; those who have not heard shall
understand. Verse 5, . There they feared with fear, where there was no fear;
because the Lord is in the generation of the righteous. For there will be, he
says, a time when those previously mentioned will fear the Lord, among whom
there was not


fear before. =17.109= And "there" will be constituted in the coming
generation, concerning which he treats. PSALM 15. Verse 1. A
monument-inscription of David. The others inscribed Of David; but they
likewise issued monument-inscription, because it contains a prophecy worthy of
eternal memory. For just as the greatest of the athletes competing in the
stadia are deemed worthy of a stele, and trophies are set up of things
accomplished in wars for later memory; so the great trophy of the Savior,
which he raised having overthrown him who has the power of death, was
inscribed from of old in prophetic Scriptures. Therefore Peter also, having
recalled the things in it, added: "Men, brothers, it is permitted to speak
with boldness to you concerning the patriarch David, that he both died and was
buried, and his tomb is among us until this day. Being therefore a prophet,
and knowing that God swore to him with an oath that from the fruit of his
loins he would raise up the Christ according to the flesh to sit upon his
throne; and having foreseen he spoke concerning the resurrection of the
Christ, that he was neither left behind into Hades, nor did his flesh see
corruption. This Jesus God raised up, of whom we all are witnesses." PSALM I .
Verse 4. Because of the words of your lips, I have kept hard ways. Through
these things also the Savior gives us a pattern, not to think highly of
ourselves when we need the help from God. And the paths of the Lord are the
observances of the commandments; or rather the activities according to virtue,
shown smooth and even from being continually trodden by those hastening to
arrive at their end, which is blessedness, and the rendering of the promises.
Let us run then according to them, our steps not slipping, if the Lord should
order them in his paths. He who has obtained this request says: I ran without
lawlessness and I directed my way; and, I ran the way of your commandments,
when you enlarged my heart. PSALM 17. Verse . Pangs of Hades surrounded me;
snares of death anticipated me. And these will also fit the Savior, having
much kinship with, Now my soul is troubled; and, My soul is sorrowful unto
death. Verse 8. And the earth was shaken, and became trembling. And the
foundations of the mountains were troubled, and were shaken, because God was
angry with them. He shows openly the descent of God because of the things said
before, both the death and the pangs of Hades. And when he had descended,
according to Aquila, the earth was moved and shaken. For every hearing, Greek
and barbarian, was filled with the report concerning him. For he called earth
those upon the earth. And of mountains, the opposing powers; which by the
error of the idols were feeding men, lifting themselves against the knowledge
of God. =17.112= The foundations, and the deep reasonings, perceiving the
power of the Lord, were troubled, being vexed. And when God was angry with
them, smoke went up as upon a firebrand; as they were being quenched and had
ceased from the fire with which they were burning men. And this fire he
quenched with his own fire. Inasmuch as God is called a consuming fire in
Moses. And in the Psalms: Fire will go before him, and will flame round about
his enemies. And again: God will come manifestly, our God, and he will not
keep silence. Fire will be kindled before him, and round about him a mighty
tempest. Therefore the Savior also said: I came to cast fire upon the earth.
And what do I wish, if it were already kindled? These things he did having
bent and descended not one heaven only, but all, as many as there ever are;
"to bend" indicating the humiliation of his height, and the emptying of the
divinity. And the hidden and secret character of his economy he indicates by
the gloom and the mist. Then he was taken up upon the cherub, although he did
not descend with it. Whom the divine powers, seeing going up with a body,
having come about him, escorted his ascent, saying: Lift up gates, you rulers
of yours, and be lifted up, eternal gates, and the King of glory will enter.
Which things came to pass when, as it is set down in the Acts of the apostles,
as they were looking he was taken up, and a cloud


received him from their eyes. Verse 12. And he set darkness as his hiding
place; round about him is his tent. Who will see the one in a tent if he has
not come to be inside? So neither God, unless one should enter inside the veil
of his being hard to grasp. For Moses also entered into the gloom, where God
was. For having attended to the creation and providence concerning God, he
knew God to be contained within these. For having opened it a little, he
learned from that which is according to the image the one whose image it is;
and from creation and providence the creator and provider. PSALM 22. Verse 3.
For the sake of his name. Verse 4. For even if I walk in the midst of the
shadow of death, I will not fear evils; because you are with me. From here he
turns the speech toward God. He therefore who sits in the shadow of death is
settled in it, confirmed in wickedness, and needing the light that rises from
mercy; but he who walks passes by; with whom the Lord also journeys. And
concerning the sitting it is said: To those sitting in a region and shadow of
death, light has risen for them. Verse 4. Your rod, and your staff, these have
comforted me. Both refer to scourges, according to: If your sons forsake my
law, and do not walk in my judgments; =17.113= if they do not keep my
ordinances; I will visit their lawlessnesses with a rod, and their sins with a
scourge. But my mercy I will not scatter from them. For the divine mercy has
not been scattered from the one scourged because of sin. For he does not
receive one who sins without scourging; scourging a son whom he receives. And
concerning those whom he does not scourge, it is said: But my feet were almost
shaken; my steps were almost poured out. Because there is no looking up in
their death, and no firmness in their scourge. Therefore pride held them; they
clothed themselves with their own injustice and impiety. If then someone is a
sinful sheep, he is struck with a rod; but if a man, with a staff, according
to the saying of Solomon: He who spares the staff hates his own son. And these
things comfort the one being struck; for he is persuaded that he scourges
every son whom he receives. PSALM 23. Verse 2. And upon rivers he prepared it.
And otherwise, before the advent of Christ God was known in Judea only; but
after it, the earth is the Lord's. But neither was there fullness before this
in all the earth; and most of it was full of wickedness. But after it many of
the nations who have believed might say: From his fullness we all received.
Therefore they also might be called his fullness. But not those empty of the
evangelical way of life. But the sinner also dwells in a desert; and the
inhabited world is he who dwells in the Church, filled with the Holy Trinity;
and he is the Lord's. But not he who has been born of the devil, and has
become that one's portion. Verse 3. Who will go up to the mountain of the
Lord? or who will stand in his holy place? Or also the mountain of the Lord is
the final good and the God Word. For rare is the one able to go up to its
ridge through progress. For he who has been perfected, being no longer able to
go further, stands firm, and is styled a holy place of God. For going behind
the Lord, he goes up; forgetting the things behind, and stretching out to the
things in front, having as an end to stand with God in his holy place; to
which he who evangelizes to Zion must go up and stand, to whom, when Moses had
arrived, God says: But you, stand there with me. But also, He set my feet upon
a rock, as upon another sanctuary, the prophets say. Since the rock was
Christ. But who will obtain these promises has been said more completely in
the fourteenth psalm. =17.116= Verse . This is the generation of those seeking
the Lord, of those seeking the face of the God of Jacob. And yet he said: No
one will see my face and live. For truly no one will see it who is a man, and
has not put off every human thing, and changed into an angel and God, which is
not here. And concerning the angels of the little ones in the Church, the


Savior says that They always see the face of the Father who is in the heavens.
Verse 9. Lift up gates, you rulers of yours, and be lifted up, eternal gates,
and the King of glory will enter. Verse 10. Who is this King of glory? The
Lord of powers, he is the King of glory. And as commander of camps they called
him Lord of powers, which in the Hebrew is said Sabaoth. And this the Seventy
also interpret in the Scriptures as Lord of soldiers and Almighty. And what is
now said, then, indicates the Savior as almighty. Thus says the Lord almighty:
After glory he sent me to you. For the Father being almighty sent the Son
almighty. And John also in the Apocalypse says, These things says the faithful
and true witness, who was and who is and who is coming; having spoken
confessedly concerning the Savior. And some say that the things said twice are
not toward higher gates, but toward the same ones. PSALM 27. Verse 1. To you,
O Lord, I will cry; my God, do not keep silence from me. If someone asks
earthly things from God, he uses a short voice; but if the heavenly, he both
cries and shouts. Hence in the Scriptures the saints cried out to God. And one
cries having entered into his chamber and shut the door. And God does not keep
silence toward the saints. He spoke, at any rate, to Moses, and Aaron, and
Joshua the son of Nun. He spoke also through prophets, when the people was
his. And the things God speaks are not from outside, but the holy things,
those that go up upon our heart, according to: Blessed is the man whose
support is from you, O Lord; goings-up into his heart. And we have another
hearing and a divine sense, according to Solomon, that hears words of God,
united to him. And he to whom God keeps silence is likened to those who go
down into a pit. For we know none of the saints who dug a pit; but he who
needed water dug a well. And Solomon also commands to drink from wells. But
God through the prophet threatens those who drink from pits, saying: They have
forsaken me, a fountain of living water, and have dug for themselves broken
pits. And those go down into a pit who attend to the cold affairs of matter;
where also the dragon, the enemy of men, who is also named cold, dwells. For
of all animals upon the earth, the dragon is colder. =17.117= All sin then is
cold; but the divine things, as it seems, are hot. He who makes his angels
spirits, and his ministers a flame of fire. And, I came to cast fire upon the
earth; and, boiling in the spirit. And that the sinners are beside pits, he
says in Exodus: God killed from the face of Pharaoh, who sits upon the throne,
to the firstborn of the captive who is in the pit. Thus those who go down into
a pit are sinners and captives. But the good God calls even these, and gives
forgiveness. For the Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because of which he
anointed me to evangelize to the poor. He has sent me to proclaim forgiveness
to captives, and recovery of sight to the blind. Verse 2. Hear, O Lord, the
voice of my supplication, when I supplicate toward you. Of those who still
lack some things, of these is the supplication. But when, having understood
the dignity of God, I ask something from God, then I pray. And when I have
become a friend still more, then I give thanks to God. Since Paul also, going
forward from the lesser to the greater, says: I exhort first of all that
supplications, prayers, intercessions, thanksgivings be made. Verse 2. When I
lift my hands toward your holy temple. Hands being lifted toward godly deeds
are stored up in heaven; through which one conquers the intelligible Amalek;
as Moses, when he brought up his hands, the people conquered; but when he let
them down, Amalek. Let us not then bring down the hands, since we will be
conquered. Verse 5. Because they did not understand into the works of the
Lord, and into the works of his hands. For no one is blameless who has not
understood the works of the Lord. For the firmament announces the making of
his hands. For it gives analogously the contemplation of its Creator. Verse 7.
And my flesh blossomed again. And what flesh blossomed again? All flesh shall
see the salvation of God. PSALM L . Verse 1. Do not


vie among those who do evil, nor envy those who do lawlessness. To envy and to
vie differ. For to vie is said of irritating, and moving to jealousy, such as:
They vied me at what is not God; that is, they moved zeal in me, and as it
were irritated me to anger by worshipping idols. But to envy is to wish that
there belong also to oneself what one has thought a good present to the
neighbor. This then he says: Do not irritate another who is doing evil so that
he rise up against you. Or, Do not do such things that the wicked take envy
against you, since he plots against you. For example: If with the mind of
David the young women were saying the hymn, David struck in his ten thousands;
he himself was the one vying among those who do evil, namely Saul. Nor again
imitate or =17.120= bless such people, seeing them flourishing. For having
blossomed for a little they are dried up. For all flesh is grass. What then do
they have that is enviable, who, having blossomed for a little, tomorrow are
thrown into an oven; as the Savior says? For I would not say that the
temporary prosperity of those who do evil is somehow worthy of envy for those
who wish to live worthily. One must rather consider to what sort of end it
will turn out to have chosen to live badly. Do not then envy such people, he
says, nor be provoked toward imitation of those who love sin, even if they
should seem to have the luxuries in the present life. Flee therefore the even
balance, looking to the things that will be; for affairs will not be in these
things continually. Therefore he adds: Because they will quickly be dried up
as grass, and will quickly fall as herbs of greenness. Verse 3. Hope in the
Lord, and do kindness, and dwell in the land, and you will be shepherded on
its wealth. That is, Love to be well approved; thirst for the acquisition of
the sacred gifts; love the things kept for the saints and stored up with God.
And do kindness; as if he were saying to the field: Produce this fruit. So to
you the hearer of the divine lessons: Give, O field, says the Word; do
kindness, which is one of the fruits of the Spirit. The name of kindness here
signifies every manner of virtue. And dwell beside the land; spending time in
the care and farming of the soul; Because whatever a man sows, that he will
also reap. And perhaps he names land the God-inspired Scripture, in which is
the wealth of the saints, that is the promises. And he exhorts to hold
continually to the divine oracles, by love of the good things stored in them
for the saints. Or he has set "dwell" instead of "you will dwell"; and he
means that land clearly which has been promised to the meek; having come to be
in which, you will be shepherded, he says, upon its wealth. And the wealth of
the city above is the abundant supply of the divine gifts. And perhaps he
likens the temporary life to a tent; and he wishes us to use it as a tent in
passing, mastering it, not being mastered by it, and using it up toward what
is beneficial, not toward pleasures. For thus we will be shepherded under a
good Shepherd, harvesting whatever wealth there is of the divine doctrines, so
as to say: The Lord shepherds me, and nothing will fail me. Verse 4. Delight
in the Lord, and he would give you the requests of your heart. Scripture is
accustomed to introduce two men; and the better has almost all the things of
the worse. For there is a food also of the inner man, concerning which it is
said: Man shall not live on bread alone, but on every word going out through
the mouth of God. There is also a drink; For we drink from a spiritual rock
following. And a garment; whence the sinner put on a curse as a cloak, but
=17.121= the righteous put on the Lord Jesus, and bowels of mercies, and the
panoply of God. Thus he who hears only his hortatory words is fed; but he who
gives himself to interpretation of law, narration of prophets, solution of
evangelical parables, clarification of apostolic words, delights in the Lord;
and he eats not toward necessity, nor toward food alone. He teaches us then to
delight in the Lord. For God also, from the beginning wishing us to luxuriate


spiritually, planted the paradise of delight, and granted the torrent of
delight. And no one can luxuriate both in flesh and in spirit. But if he
luxuriated in flesh, as the rich man, he will be deprived of the delight with
Abraham; but if he ate bread of affliction, as the poor man, there he
luxuriates and rests. And otherwise. The Lord is righteousness, truth, wisdom,
sanctification; if then you delight also in the theorems of wisdom, in the
deeds of righteousness, the saying has been fulfilled: Delight in the Lord.
Delight, therefore, is to have indwelling the God Word who gives life to all,
the bread of life; and having him in ourselves, we will receive all the
requests, not simply, but those of the heart. And you will understand what is
said, if you personify each of the members, and see how it asks according to
nature. The eye, at any rate, if it had a voice, would say to you: I ask
light, I ask to see colors suitable to me. The hearing: I ask a tuneful,
pleasant voice. The taste: I ask sweet things, I flee the bitter. The touch: I
ask to touch things smooth, delicate, gentle; not fire, not rough things, nor
things that sting. As then of each of the senses there is a suitable request
and flight; and the eye asks the light, and the smell the fragrant, the
hearing the tuneful, and the rest likewise; so the heart asks the thoughts,
which we will receive from the Lord, if we have nothing alien to his delight
in the hearts. Then we will be able to uncover to him our way. For the God of
all oversees the holy way of life of those who worship him; but he turns away
from that of those who have chosen not to live rightly. Hence he adds: Verse
5. Uncover toward the Lord your way, and hope upon him, and he himself will
do. Verse . And he will bring out your righteousness as light, and your
judgment as midday. Be, he says, walking openly the way that pleases him, that
is, showing to God your own way of life bare and undisputed; and hope upon
him, and he himself will do. In the coming age he will set it forth
manifestly, and as it were will make it conspicuous, not allowing the beauty
of your good life to be hidden. For your righteousness will be clear to all,
shining like midday light. =17.124= Midday indeed is named the very midmost of
the day. And he will judge you to be worthy of light there. Since then
everyone who does base things, as the one who fornicates, hates the light, and
as far as is in him hides what he does, that he may not be convicted; for he
wishes his fornication not to be known; therefore he also hides his way, which
he traveled; but he who does the truth, as the one who is temperate, wishes to
make it manifest, not to men, that he may not have his reward from men, but to
God; therefore it is said: Uncover toward the Lord your way, that is, show him
your blameworthy life, and he himself will heal you from the wounds, and will
make the filthy clean. And if you become fine and good, he will boast in you;
and since, not being vainglorious, you hid your righteousness, he himself will
make it manifest, and thus it will appear, like a sun at midday. For then, he
says, the righteous will shine as the sun. Verse 7. Submit to the Lord, and
supplicate him. Submission then here he calls the withdrawal from evils. For
no one who sins has been submitted to the Lord. Let it be, he says, that I
have already been submitted; what do I do concerning the former things?
Supplicate him, saying: Do not remember our ancient lawlessnesses. So that one
must not first supplicate concerning sin, except having submitted to the Lord,
that is, having stood out from sin. For to ask forgiveness of sins while still
being in sins is altogether unreasonable. Verse 7. Do not vie in the one who
is prospered in his way, in a man doing lawlessness. Do not imitate, he says,
a happiness put together from lawlessness; nor be irritated into wickedness,
even if you see the evil prospering, it being necessary to reckon that this
age belongs to those who do not have another hope. Let them be fortunate in
it, and have the supposed good things. But we look to another age of life; and
our hope is next after this age. It is not possible to have the good things in
this age and


in that one; for if in this one he has them, there being punished he will
hear: You received your good things in your life. Yet the phrase has
necessarily been added: In a man doing lawlessness. For of such people, he
says, one must decline the imitation; and it is exceedingly useful to depart
from vying them. For they prosper, or rather are supposed to have prosperity
in their own affairs, not gaining the thing by votes from above, but from
overreaching and seizing the things of others at times. The prospering, then,
he calls the progress toward the worse. And since many living in
righteousness, when they see themselves in toils, and the sinner prospering in
all things for a time, having become angry, stand off from righteousness, and
this is of extreme folly; therefore the word, =17.125= teaching to be
long-suffering, and to wait for the fruit of the righteousness of God, says.
Verse 8. Cease from anger, and forsake wrath. The example is beneficial, and
most fitting for those submitted to God. For one must, he says, be meek, and
very well settled, and having good order into the mind, and loving continually
the prosperity from long-suffering, and departing from the waves out of anger.
For of the passions some do not fall upon many, but even ordinary people have
thrown them off as they progress. For this polluted passion, anger, burns even
those who seem to be prudent, and troubles them; not some and not others; but
I risk saying all men except the perfect, if somewhere some perfect person is
found. Verse 9. But those who wait for the Lord will inherit land. Since we
are somehow always accustomed to stand off from the wicked, being pushed
toward the better by fear of being punished, and being driven together into
living lawfully by desire of the gifts from God; therefore the psalmist,
having shown the end of those who do evil to be destructive, adds, that Those
who wait for the Lord, that is those who through endurance and patience
accomplish the approved life, will inherit land, which is not a part of this
earth that has come under a curse, which he who works it eats in pains all his
days; but that land, the one prepared for the saints. For their lot is the
fair city of the saints, the holy one and above, whose allotment-giver is God.
And just as the Savior is wisdom, Word, peace and righteousness, so also
patience. For it is written: And now what is my patience? Is it not the Lord?
From whom it is possible to draw and take all things which Christ is said to
be according to the Scriptures. For as by participation in him we become
righteous and wise, and we are at peace; so also by participation in him we
wait. Be still then, waiting and receiving God, not being troubled by the
present things. Verse 11. But the meek will inherit land. Let those from
Valentinus and certain others, who think the Savior says the things not said
in the old Writings, be refuted from here, hearing that Blessed are the meek,
for they will inherit the earth. It had been said also formerly through David
by the same Spirit. And David very economically sets the prizes of the saints
over against the punishments of the lawless. And through these he whets them
toward desire of spiritual manliness, through which it would be possible to
accomplish every manner of virtue. Therefore he says: But the meek will
inherit land, the one above, clearly. For if there is in the heavens according
to Paul a Jerusalem and a mountain Zion, it follows also to understand a land,
which those will inherit =17.128= who have conquered a destructive passion,
anger, which even destroys the prudent. For they are hindered from acting
according to virtue. And meekness and anger are diametrically opposite to one
another. For where there is anger, meekness is in no way; and where there is
meekness, anger is in no way there. Verse 12. The sinner will watch the
righteous, and will gnash his teeth upon him. Verse 13. But the Lord will
laugh him to scorn, because he foresees that his day will come. From here we
learn that, just as darkness and light are by nature contrary, so the sinner
to the righteous. If then you see the righteous then being hated, do not
hesitate to say concerning the one who hates him, that he is a sinner. For
those who think the


worldly things, and having bound their own mind with the tumult of the present
life, are somehow always mad against those who live through the holy and
chosen life, and they consider the lover of righteousness an enemy. Verse 14.
The sinners drew a sword; they stretched their bow, to cast down a poor and
needy man, to slay the upright in heart. Just as there is a panoply of God, so
there is a panoply of the devil, which his soldier has put on; the breastplate
of injustice, the helmet of destruction, the shield of unbelief, the sword of
the wicked spirit, which a sinner draws, whose feet run upon injustice. There
is a preparation of the Gospel, and there is also a sandal most ready into
sin. Here then they drew a sword; because they have sin ready to hand, and are
prepared upon doing it; those who hide in the sheath of sin the sword of the
wicked spirit. An arrow of the righteous is Christ Jesus. You have set me as a
chosen arrow. The word of the sinners is an arrow; it has the poison of sin;
it wounds the one not armed with the shield of faith. They know that they
cannot cast down a rich man who is rich in wisdom, in good works. Therefore
they do not even set upon him at the start. But the whole plot is against the
poor; since a man's own wealth is a ransom of his soul; but a poor man does
