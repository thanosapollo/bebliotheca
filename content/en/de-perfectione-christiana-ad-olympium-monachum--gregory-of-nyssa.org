OF GREGORY BISHOP OF NYSSA TO OLYMPIUS ON PERFECTION

The zeal concerning knowing how one might be perfected through the life
according to virtue, so that through all things the blameless might be
accomplished for your life, is fitting to your purpose. And I would have made
it of all importance in my own life that the examples of the things you are
zealous for be found, so as to furnish by the deeds before the words the
teaching sought by you. For thus the instruction of the good things would have
been trustworthy, the life speaking together with the words. But since this I
pray may come to be sometime, but now I do not yet see myself such as to
exhibit the life instead of the word, lest I should seem altogether some
unprofitable one to you toward the aim and useless, I thought to set forth
that toward which the accurate life must stretch, making the beginning of the
word from here.

Our good Master Jesus Christ having grant 8,=1.174= ed us the communion of the
worshiped name, so that we are named from no other of the things around us,
even if one happens to be rich and well-born, even if he is low-born or poor,
even if he has recognition from certain occupations or dignities, all <and>
such names being idle, there is one lordly calling for those who have believed
in him: to be named Christians. Since then this grace has been confirmed upon
us from above, it would be necessary first to understand the greatness of the
gift, so as to give thanks worthily to the God who has granted such things,
then to show ourselves through the life such as the power of this great name
seeks. The greatness of the gift, then, of which we have been deemed worthy
through being named together with the Master of our life, would become clear
to us if we should recognize the very meaning of the name according to Christ,
so as to understand, when through this utterance we call upon the Lord of all
in the prayers, what thought we take up in our souls, or what, contemplating
through this name, we believe ourselves to invoke him piously. And when we
understand this, then by consequence we shall also clearly learn of what sort
it is fitting that we be shown through zeal concerning the life, using the
name as teacher and guide toward the life. Therefore making the holy Paul a
leader toward these two things 8,=1.175= we shall have most secure guidance
toward the clarity of the things sought. For this man especially of all both
understood accurately what Christ is and of what sort the one named after him
must be he instructed through what he did, having imitated him so clearly that
he showed his own Master formed in himself, the form of his soul having been
changed through the most accurate imitation toward the prototype, so that it
no longer seemed that Paul was the one living and speaking, but Christ himself
living in him; as he says who well perceived his own good things, that Since
you seek a proof of Christ who speaks in me, and that I live yet no longer I,
but Christ lives in me.

This man then also made known to us the meaning of the name according to
Christ, saying that Christ is the power of God and the wisdom of God; but he
also called him peace and light unapproachable, in which God dwells, and
sanctification and redemption, and great high priest and pascha, propitiatory
of souls, radiance of glory, imprint of hypostasis and maker of ages,
spiritual food and drink, rock and water, foundation 8,=1.176= of faith and
head of the corner and image of the invisible God and great God and head of
the body of the church and firstborn of the new creation and firstfruits of
those who have fallen asleep, firstborn from the dead and


firstborn among many brothers and mediator of God and men and only-begotten
Son crowned with glory and honor and Lord of glory and beginning of the things
that are, having said thus concerning him: Who is the beginning, and king of
righteousness besides and king of peace and king of all things, having the
power of the kingdom uncircumscribed, and many other such things, of which the
multitude is not easy to number; which all, composed toward one another, each
of the appellations contributing from itself toward the indication of the
meaning, produce in us a certain emphasis of the signification of the name
according to Christ, indicating to us of the unspeakable majesty as much as we
have room in the soul to understand.

Since then the dignity of kingship lies above every dignity and authority and
power, and by the title of Christ properly and prima 8,=1.177= rily the kingly
power is signified (for the anointing, as we learned in the histories,
preceded the kingship), and in the kingship all the power of the remaining
names is contained, for this reason the one who has understood the things
contained has also understood together the encompassing power of the
particulars, and this is the kingship, of which the indicative name is the
appellation of Christ. Therefore since communion of the greatest and most
divine and first of names has come to us from our good Master, so that those
honored with the appellation of Christ are named Christians, it would be
necessary that all the names interpretive of such an utterance be seen also in
us, so that the calling not be falsely named upon us, but have the testimony
from the life. For being does not come from being called something, but the
underlying nature, of whatever sort it happens to be, is known through the
fitting signification of the name. I mean something of this sort: if one
should grant to a tree or a stone the appellation of a human, will the plant
or the stone then be a human because of the calling? It is not so; but it is
necessary first to be a human, then thus to be named with the appellation of
the nature. For neither upon likenesses do the callings have the proper sense,
as if one should call the statue a human or the imitation a horse; but if
something is going to be named properly and without falsehood, the nature will
altogether show the appellation true. And the matter that has received the
imitation, whatever it happens to be, this also is named, bronze or stone or
some such other 8,=1.178= thing, upon which art has applied the form, shaping
it toward what seemed good.

Therefore those who name themselves from Christ must first become what the
name wills, then thus apply the calling to themselves. And just as if one
should distinguish from the really human the one called homonymously upon the
image, he will make the distinction from the properties (for he will name the
one a rational thinking living being, and the other soulless matter that has
taken on the form through imitation); thus we shall also recognize the
Christian, both the one who really is and the one who seems, through the
properties appearing in the characters. And characters of the real Christian
are all those things which we have understood concerning Christ. Of which as
many as we have room for, we imitate; and as many as nature does not have room
to imitate, we revere and worship. Therefore all the names interpretive of the
signification of Christ must shine upon the life of the Christian, some
through imitation, some through worship, if the man of God is going to be
complete, as the apostle says, nowhere mutilating the completeness through
vice. For just as those who fashion mythical marvels either through words or
through the graphic art, composing certain ox-headed things or hippocentaurs
or dragon-footed things or some other such thing from 8,=1.179= things of
different kinds, do not bring the imitation toward the archetype of nature,
but through this unreasonable invention going outside nature they fashion some
other thing and not a human, shaping the non-existent toward what seemed good,
and one would not call a human that which has been fashioned through this
strange composition,


even if in some part the part of the fabrication happens to resemble the human
body; thus neither would one be accurately named a Christian who has an
irrational head, that is, who does not have in the faith the head of all which
is the Word, even if in the other things he is complete, or who does not show
a body of conduct corresponding to the head of the faith, or who grows
together with the rages of dragons and is made beastlike after the likeness of
these reptiles, or who combines the lust after females of horses with the
human character and becomes some two-natured hippocentaur from rational and
irrational. And it is possible to see many such, either accomplishing the
well-ordered life upon a calf's head, that is, upon the doctrine of idolatry,
as they paint the Minotaur, or fitting to a Christian face a beastlike body in
the life, as they fashion the centaurs or the dragon-footed. In order then
that the Christian might be recognized throughout, as upon a human body, it is
fitting that the faithful imprint in the life the characters of all 8,=1.180=
the good things understood according to Christ. For to be in one thing such as
the name wills, but in others to incline toward the opposites, is nothing
other than to cut himself against himself into the rank of an enemy, being at
faction in himself through virtue and vice, becoming implacable to himself and
irreconcilable in the life.

For what communion has light with darkness? says the apostle. For since the
opposition of light toward darkness is unmixed and unmediated, the one who
holds to both and does not let go of the one of them is himself also of
necessity split together by the opposition of the things standing against one
another, becoming both light and darkness at the same time in the mixed life,
the faith sending in the illumination, but the dark life blackening the
radiance from the word. Since then the communion of light toward darkness is
impossible and irreconcilable, the one who is held by each of the opposites
becomes an enemy to himself, divided in two toward virtue and vice and setting
himself against himself in opposing rank. And just as it is not possible among
two enemies for both to become victors against one another (for the victory of
the one altogether works the death of the opposed), thus also upon this civil
war constituted through the 8,=1.181= mixed life it is not possible for the
better array to conquer, unless the other has been destroyed by utter
destruction. For how will the army of piety become stronger than vice, if the
wicked phalanx of the opponents resists? But if the better is going to
conquer, the opposed will altogether be slain. And thus virtue will have the
victory-prizes against vice, when every thing hostile to it through the
alliance of the reasonings has withdrawn toward the non-existent. And then is
fulfilled what is said from the person of God through the prophecy, that I
will kill and I will make to live. For it is not otherwise possible for the
good in me to live, unless it is made alive by the death of the enemy. And as
long as we are held by the two, touching each of the opposites with either
hand, it is impossible to have the participation of both at the same time. For
of the one who has grasped vice, virtue has slipped from the grip.

Therefore let us take up again the word from the beginning, that there is one
road for the lovers of virtue toward the pure and divine life: to know what
the name of Christ signifies, to which our life also must be conformed, being
rhythmically ordered toward virtue through the emphasis of the remaining
names. Therefore as many words and names as we collected in the proems of the
word from the holy voice of Paul as interpretive of the signification of
Christ, setting these forth we shall make most secure guidance toward the life
according to virtue for the zeal set before us, imitating some, as 8,=1.182=
has been said in what has gone before, and worshiping and revering others. And
let the enumeration of the things said become for us an order. Therefore let
us begin from the first: Christ, he says, is the power of God and the wisdom
of God. Through these first


we learn the thoughts befitting God through the appellation of Christ, through
which the name becomes venerable to us. For since all creation, both as much
as is known by sense and that which lies above sensory understanding, has come
to be through him and in him consists, necessarily toward the definition of
the signification of Christ who made all things, wisdom is plaited together
with power, we understanding this through the yoking of these two utterances,
I mean of power and of wisdom, that these great and unspeakable wonders of
creation would not be, unless wisdom invented their generation, and power
accompanied wisdom toward the completion of the things thought, through which
the thoughts become works.

The meaning of Christ is therefore divided correspondingly into a double
emphasis, into wisdom and into power, so that when we look toward the
greatness of the constitution of the things that are, we may understand his
unspeakable power through the things grasped, and when we rea 8,=1.183= son
how the things that are not came into generation, the many-formed nature in
the things that are having been given substance through a divine nod, then we
may worship the incomprehensible wisdom of the one who thought these things,
whose thoughts are deeds. And it becomes not idle and useless to us toward the
acquisition of the good to believe that Christ is power and wisdom. For what
one invokes praying and toward what one looks with the eye of the soul, this
one draws to oneself through the prayer; and thus both is strengthened in
power toward the inner human, as the apostle says, the one who looks toward
the power (and Christ is the power), and becomes wise, as the proverb says,
the one who invokes wisdom, which again is understood as the Lord. Therefore
the one named together with Christ, who is both power and wisdom, is also
named together with the power, having been empowered against sin, and will
show the wisdom in himself by the choice of the better. And when wisdom and
power are shown in us, the one choosing the beautiful, the other confirming
what has been thought, the perfect of the life is accomplished, plaited
together through both. Thus also having understood Christ as peace we shall
show the calling of the Christian true upon ourselves, if through the peace in
us we exhibit Christ in the life. That one killed the enmity, as the apostle
says. Therefore let us also not make this alive in ourselves, but let us show
it dead in 8,=1.184= our life. Let us never, for the destruction of our souls,
raise up against ourselves through anger and remembrance of evils that which
was well put to death by God for our salvation, working a wicked resurrection
of that which died well. But if we have Christ, who is the peace, let us also
make the enmity dead in ourselves, so that what we believe in that one, this
we may also accomplish in our life. For as that one, having destroyed the
middle wall of the fence, created the two in himself into one new human,
making peace, thus let us also bring into reconciliations not only those
fighting against us from outside, but also those who are at faction in
ourselves, so that no longer the flesh desire against the spirit, and the
spirit against the flesh; but the fleshly mind having been subjected to the
divine law, let us be at peace in ourselves, having been recast into the one
new and peaceful human and having become the two one. For the definition of
peace is the concord of things that have been divided. When then the civil war
has been taken out of our nature, then we also, having made peace in
ourselves, become peace, showing the appellation of Christ true and proper
upon us.

And having understood Christ as true light and unapproachable to falsehood we
learn this, that our life also must be illuminated by the rays of the true
light. And virtues are the 8,=1.185= rays of the sun of righteousness flowing
forth toward our illumination, through which it comes to be that one put off
the works of darkness and walk decently as in day


and having renounced the hidden things of shame, and doing all things in light
also become light himself, so as to shine also for others, which is proper to
light. And if we understand Christ as sanctification, by standing ourselves
away from every profane and unclean deed and thought we shall show ourselves
truly partakers of the name, confessing the power of sanctification by deed
and not by word, by the life.

And having learned that Christ is redemption, the one who gave himself a
ransom for us, we are taught through such an utterance this: to learn that as
having furnished a certain price of each soul he made those ransomed by him
from death through life his own possession of immortality. If then we have
become slaves of the one who redeemed, we shall look altogether toward the one
who is lord, so that we no longer live for ourselves, but for the one who
acquired us through the exchange of life. For we are no longer lords of
ourselves, but the one who bought is master of his own possessions, and we are
his possessions. Therefore the will of the one who is lord will be law of our
life. For just 8,=1.186= as when death was prevailing over us the law of sin
was in force in us, thus since we have become a possession of life, it would
be necessary that our citizenship be adapted toward the one who rules, lest
having been turned aside from the will of life we again desert through sin
toward the wicked tyrant of our souls, I mean death. And the same thought will
make us akin to Christ, whether we hear from Paul that he is pascha or high
priest. For Christ our pascha was truly sacrificed for us; but the priest who
offered the sacrifice to God is not some other besides Christ himself. For He
offered himself, he says, an offering and sacrifice for us. Therefore we learn
this through these things, that the one who looks toward that one who offered
himself as offering and sacrifice and became pascha will also himself present
himself to God a living holy well-pleasing sacrifice, having become a rational
worship; and the manner of the priestly service is Not to be conformed to this
age, but to be transformed by the renewal of his mind, in proving what is the
will of God, the good and well-pleasing and perfect. 8,=1.187= For it is not
possible, while the flesh lives and has not been offered according to the
spiritual law, that the good will of God be shown in it; Because the mind of
the flesh is enmity toward God and is not subjected to the law of God. For it
cannot even, as long as the flesh lives, of which, when it has been offered
through the life-giving sacrifice by putting to death the members upon the
earth, through which the passions are worked, the well-pleasing and perfect
will of God will be accomplished unhindered in the life of those who have
believed. Thus also Christ understood as propitiatory in his own blood teaches
the one who has understood these things that each become a propitiatory for
himself, purifying the soul through the putting to death of the members.

And when Christ is called radiance of glory and imprint of hypostasis, we take
up through these words the thoughts of his worshiped greatness. For the truly
God-inspired and God-taught Paul, who in the depth of the wealth of the wisdom
and knowledge of God searches out the unclear and hidden things of the divine
mysteries, having the tongue weaker than the thought, as far as the hearing of
those receiving his understanding in the mystery had room, through certain
sparks 8,=1.188= indicated the illuminations that came to him from God
concerning the understanding of the unsearchable and untraceable things,
speaking as much as the word was able to serve the thought. For having
understood all things that human power has room for concerning the divine
nature, he declares the account of the transcendent essence unattainable and
ungraspable by human reasonings. Therefore speaking the things contemplated
about it, peace and power and life and righteousness and light and truth and
such things, he determined the account concerning that itself


to be altogether ungraspable, saying that God has neither ever been seen nor
will be seen. For Whom no one of men has seen, he says, nor is able to see.
For this reason seeking how he will name that which cannot be grasped by
reasonings, since he did not find an emphatic name of the interpretation of
the incomprehensible things, he named the transcendent of every good, that
which is neither thought worthily nor spoken, glory and hypostasis. The
essence transcendent of the things that are he therefore left unnamed; but
interpreting the conjunction and unseparatedness of the Son toward the Father,
and that which is contemplated together uncircumscribedly and eternally with
the uncircumscribed and eternal Father, he addresses as radiance of glory and
imprint <of hypostasis>, indicating by the radiance the connatural, and by the
im 8,=1.189= print the equal standing. For neither is any middle thought of a
ray toward the radiating nature, nor is there any lessening of the imprint
toward the hypostasis characterized by it, but both the one who has understood
the radiating nature has altogether understood together also the radiance with
it, and the one who has taken the greatness of the hypostasis in mind
altogether measures also the hypostasis by the appearing imprint. Therefore he
also calls the Lord form of God, not diminishing the Lord by the thought of
form, but indicating the greatness of God through the form, in which the
majesty of the Father is contemplated, nowhere falling beyond its own form nor
being found outside the imprint about it. For there is nothing formless and
unbeautiful about the Father which does not exult in the beauty of the
only-begotten; therefore the Lord says that The one who has seen me has seen
the Father, signifying through this that there is neither any deficiency nor
falling-beyond. But also saying that he bears all things by the word of his
power he looses the perplexity of those who busy themselves with the
unsearchable things, who seeking the account concerning matter nowhere stand
from the busy-ness, saying, whence is matter to the immaterial, and how the
quantified from the unquantified and from the unshaped the shape, and from the
invisible the color and from the unbounded that which is limited by its own
measures? And if there is no 8,=1.190= quality about the simple and
incomposite, whence is matter plaited with the qualities about it? For all
these and such things of the things sought the one who said that the Word
bears all things by the word of his power from non-being into generation
looses. For all things, as many as are about matter and as many as have
obtained the immaterial nature, have one cause of hypostasis, the word of the
unspeakable power. And from these we are taught to look toward him, from whom
is the generation of the things that are. For if from there we were brought
into being and in him we consist, there is every necessity to believe that
nothing is outside the knowledge of the one in whom we are and from whom we
have come to be and into whom we are dissolved. And by this thought the
sinlessness according to life is reasonably accomplished together. For who,
believing that he lives from him and through him and in him, will dare to make
the one who contains each one's life in himself a witness of a discordant
life?

And the divine apostle naming the Lord spiritual food and drink hypothesizes
through the things said that these things are to be understood, that human
nature is not of one form, but the intelligible having been mixed with the
sensible, the nourishment of each of the things contemplated in us is
particular, the sensible eating holding the body together, and the spiritual
food producing in us the well-being of the soul. But just as upon the body the
solid and the moist of the nourishment mixed with one another become
preservative of the nature 8,=1.191= , being mixed through corresponding
digestion to each of the things elementally mixed in us, in the same way
analogously Paul also divides the intelligible food, naming the same thing
eating and drinking, being adapted fittingly toward the need of those who
partake. For to those who are weak and dissolved it becomes bread,
strengthening the heart of a human; and to those who have grown weary through
the hardship toward this life and have for this reason become


thirsty it becomes wine, leading the heart toward gladness. And one must
understand through the things said the power of the word, through which the
soul is nourished, receiving the grace from it correspondingly to the need
according to the riddle of the prophet, who by a place of greenness and water
of rest signifies the consolation that comes from the word to those who have
grown weary. And if looking toward the mystery one should say that the Lord is
named spiritual food and drink properly, neither is this alienated from the
proper emphasis; for truly his flesh is food and his blood is truly drink. But
upon the previously said thought the participation of the word is for all
according to authority, who becomes food and drink offered indiscriminately,
received by those who seek him; but upon the other thought the participation
of such food and drink is no longer unex 8,=1.192= amined and indiscriminate,
the apostle having thus determined that Let each prove himself and thus eat of
the bread and drink of the cup. And the one eating and drinking unworthily
eats and drinks judgment to himself. Toward which the evangelist also seems to
me to look, having securely noted such a thing, when at the time of the
mystical passion that well-ordered counselor, having taken the body of the
Lord with an unstained and pure linen, deposited it in a new and pure tomb; so
that both the commandment of the apostle and the observation of the evangelist
become a law for all of us, of receiving the holy body with a pure conscience;
if there should be some stain from sin, having washed it off with the water of
tears.

But also Christ being called rock will benefit us through this name, in the
firm and unchangeable of the life according to virtue and in holding stoutly
toward the endurance of sufferings and through showing the soul resistant and
unyielding toward every assault of sin; for through these and such things we
also shall be rock, imitating, as is possible, in the changeable nature the
unchangeable and unalterable of the Master. And if the same is named by the
wise master-builder both foundation of faith and head of the corner, neither
will this be shown unprofitable to us toward the contribution of the life
according to virtue. For we are taught through these things that of every good
citizenship and of every good lesson and pursu 8,=1.193= it the Lord is both
beginning and end. For the hope, which we understand instead of the head,
toward which all things zealously pursued according to virtue look, is that
one, thus named by Paul, and the beginning of this high tower-building of life
becomes the faith into him, upon which as upon some foundation we laying down
the beginnings of life and through the daily accomplishments legislating the
pure thoughts and activities, thus the head of all also becomes our head,
adapting itself through the diagonal growing-together to the two walls of our
life, those according to body and soul, being built up through seemliness and
purity. So that if the one of the buildings is lacking, either the seemliness
according to the appearing not being built together with the purity of the
soul, or the virtue of the soul not agreeing with the appearing, Christ would
not become head of this half-finished life, he who adapts himself only to the
double and diagonal building; for it is not possible without the joining of
the two walls for a corner to come to be. Then therefore the beauty of the
chief corner-stone will be set upon our building, when from both sides
according to the straight 8,=1.194= rule of life the double life has been
stretched by the cord of the virtues, harmonious and straight and undistorted
and having in itself nothing crooked or bent.

And Paul naming Christ image of the invisible God, the God over all and great
God (for by these utterances also he proclaims the majesty of the true Master,
saying: Of our great God and Savior


Jesus Christ; and From whom is Christ according to the flesh, who is God over
all blessed unto the ages); saying these things then he teaches us this
through the things said, that the one who always is what he is (and that is
what the one who is knows alone, and of human grasp, even if the one who
thinks the things above always draws near to him through progress, he has
always stood above by the equal measure); this one then who is beyond all
knowledge and grasp, the unspeakable and unutterable and indescribable, in
order that he might make you again 8,=1.195= an image of God, himself also by
love of humanity became image of the invisible God, so that by his own form,
which he took up, he might be formed in you and you again through himself
might be shaped together toward the imprint of the archetypal beauty, so as to
become what you were from the beginning. Therefore if we too are going to
become image of the invisible God, it is fitting that the form of our life be
typed toward the example of life set out for us; and what is this? That living
in flesh we not live according to flesh. For that prototypical image of the
invisible God which sojourned through the virgin was tried in all things
according to the likeness of human nature, but did not receive together the
trial of sin alone. Who did not do sin, nor was deceit found in his mouth.
Therefore just as, if we were being taught the art of painting, the teacher
having set before us some beautified form upon a tablet, it was altogether
necessary that each imitate that one's beauty upon his own painting, so that
the 8,=1.196= tablets of all be beautified according to the example of beauty
set out, in the same way, since each is a painter of his own life, and the
artisan of this creation is the purpose, and the colors toward the working-out
of the image are the virtues, there is no small danger of recarving the
imitation of the prototypical beauty into some ugly and formless face, having
sketched through the filthy colors the character of vice instead of the
Master's form. But as is possible, one <must> take the pure colors of the
virtues mixed with one another according to some artistic mixture toward the
imitation of the beauty, so that we become image of the image, having
impressed the prototypical beauty through active imitation as far as possible,
as Paul did becoming an imitator of Christ through the life according to
virtue. And if it is necessary also to distinguish in the word the particulars
through which the imitation of the image comes to be, one color is humility.
For Learn from me, he says, that I am meek and humble in heart. Another color
is the long-suffering which in some measure appeared upon the image of the
invisible God. Sword and woods and bonds and scourges, cheeks struck, face
spat upon, back given over to blows, an impious tribunal, 8,=1.197= a harsh
sentence, soldiers reveling in the gloomy sentence, in mockeries and ironies
and insults and the blows from a reed, nails and gall and vinegar and all the
most terrible things brought upon him without cause, or rather given in return
for the manifold benefaction. What then is the defense against those doing
these things? Father, forgive them; for they do not know what they do. Was it
not possible for him to hurl the heaven itself down upon them from above, or
to make the insolent vanish by a chasm of earth, or to cast the sea out beyond
its own bounds and make the earth submerged in the depths, or to cast upon
them the Sodomitic rain of fire, or to do some other of the gloomy things by
command? But he bore all these things in meekness and long-suffering, the one
who through himself legislates the long-suffering for your life. Thus also all
the other things about the prototypical image of God are to be seen, toward
which the one who looks and who beautifies his own form clearly according to
that one himself also becomes image of the invisible God, being painted
through endurance.

And the one who has learned that Christ is head of the church, let him think
this before all things, that every head is of like nature and of like essence

And when we hear that he is firstborn of the creation and firstborn from the
dead and firstborn among many brothers, first let us send away the heretical
suppositions, as having no advocacy of their wicked doctrine-making from the
words set forth; and after these things let us also understand the things
contributing to us through these toward the ethical life. For since the
God-fighters say that the only-begotten God the creator of all, the one from
whom and through whom and in whom are all things, is a work of God and a
creature and a made thing, and for this reason they determine that he is
called firstborn of all creation, as being a brother of the creation,
preceding it only by the seniority of time, as Reuben of his own brothers, not
ranked first by nature, but by the seniorities from time; first this must be
said toward those, that it is not possible for the same one to be believed
both only-begotten and firstborn. For neither is the only-begotten understood
with brothers nor without brothers 8,=1.201= the firstborn, but if he is
only-begotten, he does not have brothers, and if he is firstborn of brothers,
he neither is nor is called only-begotten at all. Therefore these names are
irreconcilable and without communion toward one another upon the same one, so
that it is impossible for the same one to be named the two, both only-begotten
and firstborn. But it has been said by the scripture concerning the Word who
is in the beginning, that only-begotten God, and again by Paul that firstborn
of all creation. Therefore it is fitting to distribute to the


criterion of the truth, having accurately distinguished each of these names;
so that the pre-eternal Word is only-begotten, and of all the creation that
came to be afterward in Christ the Word having become flesh became firstborn.
And whatever thought is generated in us, having learned that he is firstborn
from the dead and firstborn among many brothers, that we should also
understand correspondingly upon the firstborn of creation. 8,=1.202= Therefore
he becomes firstborn from the dead, having become the firstfruits of those who
have fallen asleep, so that he might make a way for all flesh toward the
resurrection, and being about to work us through the birth from above which is
through water and spirit into sons of day and sons of light, who formerly were
by nature children of wrath, he himself leads the way of such a birth in the
stream of the Jordan, having drawn the grace of the Spirit upon the
firstfruits of our nature, so that all who are born into life from the
spiritual regeneration may be called brothers of the one born before through
the water and the Spirit. In the same way also understanding him as firstborn
of the creation that came to be in Christ we do not come to be outside the
pious supposition. For since the ancient creation passed away, having been
made useless through sin, necessarily the new creation of life constituted
through regeneration and the resurrection from the dead succeeded the passing
of the things that had vanished, of which the leader of life having led the
way becomes and is named firstborn of the creation. But how one must have
oneself toward those who have been arrayed against, through the few things
said it would easily come to be for the more laborious† to have a sufficient
alliance toward the truth. 8,=1.203= And how these words would appear to
contribute toward the life according to virtue, we shall go through in brief.
Reuben was firstborn of those brought forth after him through generation, but
both the character appearing upon them, having itself fittingly toward the
firstborn, also testified to those born after him the kinship toward him, so
that the brotherhood not be unknown, being testified through the likeness
according to form. Therefore if through the like-mannered regeneration which
is through water and spirit we also have become brothers of the Lord who for
us became firstborn among many brothers, it would be consequent to show
through the characters of the life the near-kinship toward him, the firstborn
of creation being formed in our life. And what character of that one's form
have we learned from the scripture? That which we have often said, that He did
not do sin nor was deceit found in his mouth. Therefore if we are going to be
called brothers of the one who led the way of our birth, the sinlessness of
our life will confirm the kinship toward that one, no filth separating us from
the conjunction toward the purity. But the firstborn is also righteousness and
sanctification and love and redemption and such things. Therefore if our life
also is characterized through such things, 8,=1.204= we shall furnish clear
tokens of our good birth, so that the one who sees these things in our life
may testify to us the brotherhood toward Christ. For he himself is the one who
opened for us the door of the resurrection and for this reason became
firstfruits of those who have fallen asleep. That then we shall all rise in a
twinkling of an eye at the last trumpet, this he showed both by what he did
upon himself and by what he did upon the remaining ones who had been held by
death.

Not however does an equal condition in the life after these things await all
who have risen from the dust of the earth, but They shall go, he says, those
who have done the good things into a resurrection of life, and those who have
practiced the worthless things into a resurrection of judgment. So that if
anyone's life looks toward that fearful condemnation, this one, even if
through the birth from above he happens to be numbered with the brothers of
the Lord, belies the name, denying the near-kinship toward the firstborn in
the form of vice. And the mediator of God and men who through himself joins to


God the human joins that only which would be worthy of the growing-together
toward God. For just as he made his own human akin to himself <in> himself by
the power of the godhead, being a part 8,=1.205= of the common nature, yet not
having fallen under the passions of the nature that call forth into sin (for
He did not do sin, he says, nor was deceit found in his mouth); thus he will
also bring the particulars to the conjunction of the godhead, if they should
bring on nothing unworthy of the growing-together toward the divine. But if
someone should truly be a temple of God, containing in himself no idol and
image of vice, this one will be taken up by the mediator toward participation
of the godhead, having become pure toward the reception of his purity. For
neither will wisdom enter into a malcrafted soul, as the word says, nor does
the one pure in heart see in himself anything other besides God, to whom
having been glued through incorruption he has received within himself all the
good kingdom. And the thing said would become more clear to us, if we should
take together toward the clarity of the things said the utterance of the Lord,
which he made through Mary toward the apostles: I go, he says, to my Father
and your Father, and my God and your God. For these things the mediator says
between the Father and the disowned, the one who through himself reconciled
the enemies of God to the true and only godhead. For since according to the
prophetic word humans were alienated from the life-giving womb through sin and
wandered from the belly in which they were formed, having spoken the false
8,=1.206= instead of the truth, for this reason having taken up the
firstfruits of the common nature through soul and body he made it holy,
preserving it in himself unmixed with and unreceptive of all vice, so that
having dedicated this through incorruption to the Father of incorruption he
might draw together through it all that is akin according to nature to it and
of the same tribe and might receive the disowned into the adoption and the
enemies of God into the participation of his godhead. Therefore just as the
firstfruits of the lump was made akin to the true Father and God through
purity and passionlessness, thus we also the lump through like roads shall be
glued to the Father of incorruption through imitating, as far as may be
possible, the passionless and unalterable of the mediator. For thus we shall
be a crown of the only-begotten God from precious stones, having become honor
and glory through the life. For Paul says that having lessened himself a
little beside the angels through the suffering of death he made those formerly
transferred into a nature of thorn through sin a crown for himself through the
economy according to death, recasting the thorn through the passion into honor
and glory. There is then no small danger, once the one who takes away the sin
of the world has taken up the crown from thorns upon the head, in order that
he might make the crown plaited from honor and glory, that someone be found
being a thistle through the evil life and a thorn, 8,=1.207= then being
inserted in the middle of the Master's crown through the communion of his
body. Toward whom the just voice will altogether say: How did you enter here
not having a wedding garment? How were you plaited, being a thorn, with those
fitted through honor and glory to my crown? What communion has Christ with
Beliar? What portion has a faithful one with an unfaithful? What is common to
the light toward the darkness? In order then that our life may never indict
these words against us, it would be necessary to take care to put away every
thorny deed and word and thought through all our life [of the life], so that
having become honor and glory by the pure and passionless citizenship we may
crown the head of all through ourselves, having become as some treasure and
possession to the Master. For the Lord of glory does not accept to be and to
be called of anything dishonored. Therefore the one alien to all unseemliness
and abomination, according to both the hidden and


the appearing human, makes his own master the one who is and is called Lord of

And he is also beginning; and the beginning of every thing does not have
itself alienly toward the things after itself. For if one should define the
beginning to be life, life altogether also will be the thing understood after
the beginning; and if the beginning is light, light also will be understood
after the beginning. What then do we gain through believing him 8,=1.208= to
be beginning? That we become such as we have believed our beginning to be. For
light is not named a beginning of darkness, nor, life having been ranked into
a beginning, shall we understand death as the continuum of the beginning. But
if someone should not have himself of like nature toward the one who leads,
being joined to the beginning through passionlessness and virtue, the
beginning of the things that are would not become beginning of this one.
Beginning of the dark life is the world-ruler of the darkness, the one who has
the power of death of the death-bearing sin. It is not then possible for the
one ranked under the beginning of darkness through a wretched life to say that
the beginning of every good is beginning of himself; and of the same thought
is held, for those who take the divine utterances toward their own good, that
he is also named king of righteousness and of peace. For the one who according
to the teaching of the prayer prays that the kingdom of God come upon him,
having learned that the true king is king of righteousness and of peace, will
altogether accomplish in his own life both the righteousness and the peace, so
that the king of righteousness and of peace may reign over him. Therefore
every virtue is understood as an army of the king; for through the
righteousness and the peace I think one must understand all the virtues. If
then someone, having deserted from the 8,=1.209= army of God, should enroll
himself in the array of the foreigners, having become a hoplite of the
inventor of vice and having stripped off the breastplate of righteousness and
all the peaceful panoply, how will this one be ranked under the king of peace,
having become a shield-thrower of the truth? For it is clear that the device
of his arming will show the one who is king, showing the one who leads in the
character of the life instead of the image sketched upon the weapons. How
blessed is that one who has been ranked under the divine generalship and
enrolled in the ranks of those numbered in ten thousand ten thousands and
armed against vice through the virtues, which imprint the image of the king
upon the one who has put them on! And why is it necessary to lead the word
further, setting forth to the sequence of the examination all the utterances
by which the name of Christ is interpreted, through which it is possible to be
guided toward the life according to virtue, each name through its own emphasis
cooperating something altogether for us toward the perfection of the life? But
I say it is well to recapitulate the memory of the things mentioned, so that
there may be some hand-leading for us toward the aim of the word, which we set
forth from the beginning seeking, how one might accomplish the perfect in
himself. For I think, if one should always reason this, that he is a partaker
of the worshiped name according 8,=1.210= to the doctrine of the apostles,
being called a Christian, he will necessarily also show upon himself the power
of the other names by which Christ is understood, becoming a partaker of each
calling through the life. I mean something of this sort: three things are
those that characterize the life of the Christian: deed, word, thought. Of
these the thought is more originating than the others. For the mind becomes
beginning of every word, and second after the thinking is the word, uncovering
through the voice the mind that has been impressed in the soul, and deed holds
third rank after the mind and the word, bringing what has been thought into
activity. Therefore when the sequence of life leads us to one of these, it is
well that of every word and deed and thought these divine thoughts, through
which Christ is understood and named, be accurately overseen, lest our deed or
the word or the


thought be carried outside the power of those high names. For as Paul says,
that everything that is not from faith is sin, thus it is possible, having
understood from the consequent, to declare clearly, that everything 8,=1.211=
that does not look to Christ, whether word or deed or thought, looks
altogether toward that which is opposed to Christ. For it is not possible for
the one who has come to be outside light or life not to be altogether in
darkness or in death. If then that which is not worked and spoken and thought
according to Christ has itself fittingly toward that which is opposed to the
good, what is shown through these things would be clear to everyone, that the
one who has come to be outside of that one leaves Christ, through the things
by which he either thinks something or does or speaks. Therefore the divine
utterance of the prophet is true, which says: I reckoned as transgressors all
the sinners of the earth. For as the one who has denied Christ in the
persecutions is a transgressor of the worshiped name, thus also if someone
should deny the truth or the righteousness or the sanctification and the
incorruption, or if he should cast out of the life some other of the things
understood according to virtue in a time of the prevailing of the passions, he
is named a transgressor by the prophecy, through each of these transgressing
in the life the one who is these things. What then must the one deemed worthy
of the great appellation of Christ do? What else than through all things to
distinguish in himself the thoughts and words and the deeds, whether each of
these looks toward Christ or has been alienated from Christ; and great is the
ease of the distinction of such things. For that which through some passion is
either worked or thought or said, this has no concord toward 8,=1.212= Christ,
but bears the character of the opposed one, who instead of mire smearing the
passions upon the pearl of the soul damages the radiance of the precious
stone. And that which is purified of every passionate disposition looks toward
the leader of passionlessness, who is Christ, from whom as from a pure and
incorruptible spring one drawing the thoughts into himself will show such a
likeness to himself toward the prototype, as is that of water toward water,
both the springing water that gushes and that which has come to be from there
in the jar. For one by nature is the purity both that in Christ and that
contemplated in the partaker, but the one gushes, and the one who partakes
draws, transferring upon the life the beauty in the thoughts; so that there is
concord of the hidden human toward the appearing, the seemliness of the life
agreeing with the thoughts moved according to Christ.

This then is, at least according to my judgment, the perfect in the Christian
life, to have the communion of all the names by which the name of Christ is
signified, in soul and in word and in the pursuits of the life, so as to
receive upon himself the entire sanctification according to the blessing of
Paul, in the complete body and the soul and the spirit, being guarded
continuously outside the mixture toward the evil 8,=1.213= . And if someone
should say that the good is hard to accomplish, since only the Lord of
creation is unchangeable, but human nature is changeable and has itself
readily toward the changes; how then is it possible that the firm and unfallen
in the good be accomplished in the changeable nature? Toward such a word then
we say that, that it is not possible for anyone to be crowned who has not
contended lawfully; and there would not be a lawful contest if there were not
the one wrestling against. If then there were not the opponent, neither would
there be the crown; for victory by itself is not, unless there is that which
is defeated. Therefore let us contend toward the very changeable of our
nature, being plaited as with some opponent through the reasonings, becoming
victors not through throwing it down, but through not allowing it to fall. For
neither does the human use the change only toward the evil; for it would have
been impossible for him to come to be in the good, if he had the inclination
from nature only toward the opposite; but now the fairest work of the change
is the increase in the good things, the alteration toward the better always
remaking toward the more divine the one who is well altered. Therefore the


thing that seems to be fearful (and I mean that our nature is changeable) the
word has shown as some wing toward the flight upon the greater things, so that
it is a loss to us not to be able to receive the alteration toward the better.
Let not then the one who sees in the nature the readiness toward change be
grieved, but being altered through all things toward the better and being
transformed from 8,=1.214= glory into glory let him thus be changed, through
the daily increase always becoming better and always being perfected and never
arriving toward the limit of perfection. For this is <the> truly perfection,
never to stand still while increasing toward the better nor to bound the
perfection by some limit.
