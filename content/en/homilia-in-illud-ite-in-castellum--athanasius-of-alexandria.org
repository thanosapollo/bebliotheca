* On that saying, Go into the village
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of the same Athanasius, archbishop of Alexandria, on the saying: "Go into the
village opposite, and you will find a donkey bound and a colt with her; having
loosed them, bring them to me." 1.1 Again the Lord, doing good to us and
stretching out his own grace toward us, at the same time also through his own
teaching stirs up our souls. 1.2 For to those who have toiled in this life and
been distracted in its affairs God has given this age in the heart, so that
men, having come to know, may know that they do not remain through the ages,
but imagine this age. 1.3 Therefore, that they may also take thought for the
age to come, he granted us an innate learning through free will. All men then,
both wise and foolish, rulers and private persons, rich and poor, know that
death will succeed all. 1.4 For this is to imagine this age, that is, for each
to have his own end before his eyes. 1.5 But many, even knowing this, that
death will succeed them, think that after this age they will be as not having
existed. 1.6 Such were those who said: "Short and sorrowful is our life, and
there is no return of our end. Come then," he says, "and let us enjoy the good
things that are; let us fill ourselves with costly wine and ointments." 1.7
And the divine Paul also sets these forth as saying: "Let us eat and drink,
for tomorrow we die." 1.8 If then the wretched were able to know so much, that
"short and sorrowful is" their "life," how did they not more quickly find the
maker of the good things? 1.9 It belongs then to the wise man alone to know
the causes of the things that have come to be. For the wise man knows both how
they came to be and for whose sake they came to be. He knows that God is maker
of all things. =1.10= For he has heard the apostle saying: "Whether you eat or
drink or do anything, do all to the glory of God." =1.11= But those who have
used this life eagerly as in days of youth and have been consumed by their own
wickedness, from beginning to end have not known the works of God. For if they
had made their mind exact concerning these things, they would certainly have
recognized more quickly God the craftsman of them also. =1.12= "For from the
greatness and beauty of created things their maker is seen." He has therefore
used a way that leads men from the created things to the creator and craftsman
of all. 2.1 These things we have added to the discourse, since all have gone
astray as sheep, and that "by the envy of the devil death entered into the
world," that "each was bound with the cords of his own sins." 2.2 For "death
reigned from Adam until Moses even over those who had not sinned in the
likeness of the transgression of Adam." 2.3 But our Lord and Savior Jesus
Christ sojourned, redeeming the captives and giving life to those who had been
put to death. 2.4 And indeed the divine apostle, knowing this, wrote saying:
"For if by the trespass of the one the many died, much more by the righteous
act of the one unto all men unto justification of life." 2.5 Therefore the
living and active Word of God sojourned toward us, the unchanging image, the
only-begotten Son, "through whom the ages came to be, and bearing all things
by the word of his power, having made purification of sins." 2.6 Therefore he
sends two disciples into the village opposite, saying to them: "Go into the
village opposite you, and immediately you will find a donkey bound and a colt
with her; having loosed them, bring them to me." 2.7 Matthew, describing the
church and the things that come from her, named a donkey and a colt. But Luke,
summing up both into one unity, names a colt. 2.8 For thus Luke says in his
own gospel: "He sent two of his disciples, saying: Go into the village
opposite, in which as you enter you will find a colt bound, on which no man
has ever sat; having loosed it, bring it to me." 2.9 You will find then
Matthew everywhere naming two, but Luke summing up the two into one. =2.10= As
also in the case of the blind, Matthew again names two, saying: "And as Jesus
was passing from there, two blind men followed him, crying out and saying:
Have mercy on us, son of David." =2.11= But Luke, summing up the two into one,
says: "And it came to pass as he drew near to Jericho that a certain blind man
sat by the way begging. And hearing a crowd passing by he inquired what this
might be. And they reported to him that Jesus the Nazarene was passing by. And
he cried out saying: Jesus, son of David, have mercy on me." =2.12= Likewise
also in the case of those possessed by demons you will find Matthew naming
two, but Luke, with a more exact mind, having piously said one instead of the
two. =2.13= Matthew then says concerning the two thus: "And when he came to
the other side, into the country of the Gergesenes, two demoniacs met him,
coming out from the tombs, exceedingly fierce." =2.14= But Luke, writing the
two as one, said: "And as he came out upon the land a certain man from the
city met him, who had demons for a considerable time." =2.15= Everywhere then
you will find Matthew speaking of all things in a twofold way, but Luke
discoursing in the singular. =2.16= So that the divine gospels are not in
error (God forbid), nor again did those who are filled from one and the same
Holy Spirit speak things contrary to themselves; for the saints preserve one
and the same power through the holy scriptures. 3.1 And we too have recalled
these things, not falling away from the matter at hand. For the gospel
according to Matthew was read to us, as you heard. 3.2 And the passage of the
scripture is this: "Then Jesus sent two disciples, saying to them: Go into the
village opposite you, and immediately you will find a donkey bound and a colt
with her; having loosed them, bring them to me." 3.3 We have already said
beforehand that this donkey was the church from the nations, and the colt is
our father Adam, whom Satan bound with his own wiles. 3.4 For this donkey was
accounted an unclean animal, neither bringing up nor bringing down rumination.
Which is the church from the nations. 3.5 For before this she was defiled in
her idols and polluted in unclean bloods. So that it was said to her through
Isaiah the prophet: "I committed adultery among many shepherds." 3.6 But the
Son of God, who loves mankind, sojourned toward us "and being found in fashion
as a man he humbled himself, becoming obedient unto death, even death of a
cross." 3.7 For it was not of angels to save a creation of God, but of the
Word of God, of God who came down, "mediator of God and men." For it was owed
also to him who is also called creator. 3.8 He therefore sends the two
disciples into the village opposite to loose the donkey and the colt, "that he
himself might present to himself the church in glory, no longer having spot or
wrinkle" or anything else of the things said before concerning her, "but that
she might be holy and blameless" for Christ. 3.9 For thus also the things born
from her will be "a people of his own possession, zealous of good works." 4.1
The two disciples therefore go away into the village opposite and loose the
donkey and the colt according to the things commanded them by the teacher. 4.2
For he called us back from the village opposite into the heavenly city, the
Jerusalem above. 4.3 And he loosed us from the bonds of the devil. You know
also the prophet Isaiah crying out long before and saying: "I have given you
for a covenant of my race, for a light of nations, to open the eyes of the
blind, to bring out from bonds those who are released, and from a house of
prison those sitting in darkness." 4.4 The disciples therefore go into the
village opposite. And when you hear village, take it as this earthly world;
for the city is the heavenly one, as we said before. 4.5 And Moses also
prefigured "into the village opposite" in Genesis, saying: "God settled Adam
opposite the paradise of delight and commanded the cherubim and the flaming
sword that turns to guard the way of the tree of life." 4.6 It was then of no
other to lead us in, whence we were cast out, than of the Lord himself who set
the cherubim to guard the way of the tree of life. 4.7 For it was of no other
to call us back from the village opposite into the heavenly city than of him
who had pronounced sentence against us. 4.8 Thus then also that concerning the
donkey and the colt will be understood, that it is all humanity. For the
divine scripture uses examples. 4.9 For even if I take the church, I signify
the man; even if I make mention of Adam, yet I confess all humanity together.
=4.10= For this reason our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ sojourned, that he
might turn back what had gone astray and, having sought, find what was lost.
=4.11= For he himself is "the one who looses those who are bound and raises up
those who have been cast down" and calling back those who have fallen and
redeeming the captives. =4.12= And making this known the God-bearing David
also sings in the psalms saying: "Turn, Lord, our captivity as streams in the
south." =4.13= This is the one who also shines upon the darkness, concerning
whom it is written: "Galilee of the nations, the people sitting in darkness,
see a great light." =4.14= For when the presence of the Savior has shone upon
all things, the donkey and the colt are then loosed from the bonds. =4.15= For
he himself also took up our sins, as he says through the prophet: "I am the
one who blots out your sins." 5.1 Let us then also show a good to our own
soul. Let us loose every bond of injustice. For this is the good in the proper
sense, that, in the things in which the teacher himself lived rightly and
well, we may learn that he has handed over to us the things that profit not by
word only, but also by deeds. 5.2 For hear him saying through the prophet:
"The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he has anointed me; he has sent me
to preach good news to the poor, to heal those crushed in heart, to proclaim
release to captives and recovery of sight to the blind." 5.3 For those who
were formerly lords, having bound our feet and hands, took us captive, not
allowing us to advance in the way of truth. 5.4 But the Son of God, who loves
mankind, having sojourned to the human race, set us free. For he sent his own
disciples and loosed us from the bonds of the devil. 5.5 The paradoxical signs
that came to pass from Jesus, then: even if they came to pass perceptibly
according to the history, yet they came to pass for the sake of intelligible
things. The things of the history, then, were accomplished, and the things
signified were realities. 5.6 For if the things signified were not divine
realities, he would not thus have been about to proclaim beforehand his
sojourning through the prophets. 5.7 For Isaiah cries out saying: "Behold,
your king comes to you meek and mounted upon a donkey and a colt, the son of a
beast of burden." 5.8 Upon these paradoxical signs that came to pass from
Jesus it is possible neither to add nor to take away. For he did all things
perfectly and without lack. For he was "mighty in his works and words." 5.9
And indeed the psalmist, making these known, said: "Gird your sword upon your
thigh, O mighty one, in your comeliness and your beauty, and stretch and
prosper and reign." By his great power, then, he set free all humanity. 6.1
And he did these signs, the Lord himself being present with us, that we might
fear from his face. 6.2 And that the perceptible things are symbols of
intelligible things, hear. For the Savior sojourned, that he might first of
all do good to the soul of man. 6.3 But since this was unknown to the many,
through the healings that were seen he showed the hidden salvation of the
soul. 6.4 Indeed, when he granted forgiveness of sins to the paralytic he was
not believed. For the Jews said: "Who can forgive sins except God alone?" 6.5
Then, since having done the unseen and primary thing it escaped notice,
secondarily he healed the body also. For he said to him: "Rise, take up your
bed and walk." 6.6 You see how quickly this man has been loosed from the
bonds, and not only has he been loosed through the outward healing, but he is
also set free of so many sins. 6.7 And further he also showed him as a herald
of the healing, and in general the things that came to pass perceptibly came
to pass for the sake of intelligible things. 6.8 For this reason then the
disciples also are sent into the village opposite and loose the donkey and the
colt according to the things commanded them by the teacher. 6.9 And this
wholly came to pass, that the prophecy of Jacob also might be fulfilled. For
blessing the twelve patriarchs, having come to Judah he prophesies and utters
divine words. =6.10= For hear him saying: "Binding his colt to the vine, and
to the tendril the colt of his donkey." =6.11= And in general you will find
all the things said unto Judah prophesied unto the coming of the Savior. For
the lover of learning, reading little by little, is able to understand the
things said concerning him. =6.12= But that we may not narrate the whole
history, let us hear the apostle saying: "For it is evident that our Lord has
arisen from Judah." 7.1 For this reason then the things that came to pass came
to pass, that they might make the craftsman manifest through his own ministry.
7.2 For who, having seen a ship steered most excellently, does not receive a
notion of a steersman? And a chariot passing well and being borne by skillful
reins, does he not think of the one who drives? If then such things are so in
the case of things in our hands, what would we say in the case of the
paradoxes of the Savior? 7.3 For we see the fearful and paradoxical things of
his works: as the huge waves of the sea and the motions of the winds that dash
against it, and the circuit of the sun and the course of the moon and the
composition of the stars. 7.4 And who was not afraid, when "the veil of the
temple was torn from above to below," when the sun, not bearing what had been
dared, in fear contracted the light, or when "the rocks were split and the
tombs were opened" and those laid in them rose and the enemies were afraid
from his face? Therefore even unwilling they confessed that "truly this was
the Son of God." 7.5 And in general "the greater part of his works is in
hidden things." For "a thousand thousands stood by him praising and ten
thousand times ten thousand minister to him." 7.6 Let us then also recognize
the benefactor. Let us glorify the Father with the Son and Holy Spirit; let us
confess one Godhead. 7.7 Let us cease from sinning. Let us remember the poor.
Let brotherly love remain. Do not forget hospitality. 7.8 For thus living we
shall inherit the kingdom of heaven in Christ Jesus our Lord, to whom be the
glory and the power unto the ages of ages. Amen.
