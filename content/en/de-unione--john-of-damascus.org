On union, of the Damascene.
Union is a communal running-together of things that stand apart. And it comes
to be according to twelve ways: according to blending, according to heaping,
according to nature, according to mixing, according to confusion, according to
fitting, according to juxtaposition, according to coalescence, according to
essence, according to relation, according to composition, according to
hypostasis only. And it is according to blending, as upon liquids: of wine and
waters. According to heaping, as upon dry things: of wheat and barley.
According to kneading, as upon dry and wet: of earth and water. According to
mixing, as upon different flours. According to confusion, as upon meltables:
of gold and silver, of wax and pitch. According to fitting, as upon stones.
According to juxtaposition, as upon boards. According to coalescence, as upon
a lamp coming forth from fire and again being united. According to essence, as
upon individuals, Peter and Paul, or certain horses, or oxen, or other
animals.
According to relation, as upon friends and judgments coming together into one
will. According to composition, as upon soul and body. And according to
hypostasis it is said upon the Godhead and humanity in Christ alone. For the
two natures had one person, and not two, according to the nonsense of
Nestorius. And such a union is also called essential, and true, and not
according to fantasy. And otherwise, union according to hypostasis is the
coming-together of things of different substance into one hypostasis, the
natural property being saved for each after the union.
