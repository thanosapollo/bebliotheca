THE DISCOURSE ON THE TEXT: THE LORD CREATED ME THE BEGINNING OF HIS WAYS
18 If then the Word received a beginning of generation from the time when,
having passed through the mother's womb, he put on the bodily frame, it
follows that he came to be of a woman. But if he was Word and God from of old
with the Father, and we say that all things have come into being through him,
then it is not the One who is, who is also cause of all generated things, that
came to be of a woman. Rather he is by nature God, self-sufficient, infinite,
incomprehensible; but it is the man who came to be of a woman, the one
compacted in the virgin womb by the Holy Spirit. 19 For the temple in the
proper sense, the pure and undefiled, is the human tent about the Word, where
God, having tabernacled, dwelt openly. And we do not say these things from
probabilities. For the Son of God by nature, when he was foretelling the
dissolution and resurrection of the temple, equips us with unambiguous
teaching, saying to the murderous Jews: Destroy this temple, and in three days
I will raise it up. 20 When therefore the Word, having fashioned the man as a
temple, put him on, visiting men in a body, he displayed all manner of wonders
invisibly, and sent out the apostles as heralds of the everlasting kingdom.
21 For Paul did not say "conformed to the Son of God," but "conformed to the
image of his Son," showing that the Son is one thing and his image another.
For the Son, bearing the divine marks of the Father's excellence, is the image
of the Father, since those born like from like appear as true images of their
begetters. But the man whom he put on is an image of the Son, of the kind that
images are also painted in wax from unlike colors; and some are handmade by
convention, while others have come to be by nature and likeness. Moreover the
very ordinance of truth also dictates this. For the bodiless Spirit of Wisdom
is not conformed to bodily men, but the man embodied by the Spirit is the
stamp, who bears members equal in number to all the others, and is clothed
with the same form as each of them.
22 That he means the body is conformed to human bodies, he teaches us more
clearly when writing to the Philippians: Our citizenship, he says, is in
heaven, from which we also await a Savior, the Lord Jesus Christ, who will
transform the body of our humiliation, that it may become conformed to the
body of his glory. And if, transforming the humble body of men, he makes it
conformed to his own body, the slander of the adversaries has been shown stale
from every side.
23 But just as the man, born of the virgin, is said to have come to be of a
woman, so also he is written to have come to be under the law, in that he
sometimes walked according to the legal ordinances. Especially when, while he
was still a child of eight days, the parents were eagerly hastening to
circumcise him, as the evangelist Luke reports. And afterward they brought him
up into the temple to present him to the Lord, performing the purification
offerings, to give a sacrifice according to what is said in the law of the
Lord, a pair of turtledoves, or two young pigeons. If therefore the
purification gifts were offered according to the law on his behalf, and he
bore circumcision on the eighth day, in this way also he writes not
unreasonably that he has come to be under the law. But neither was the Word
subject to the law, as the slanderers suppose, himself being the law, nor did
God need purification sacrifices, cleansing and sanctifying all things at a
single stroke. But even if, having taken up the human instrument from the
virgin, he put it on, and came under the law, being purified according to the
rites of the firstborn, he did not endure the observances as himself needing
their provision, but in order that he might redeem from the slavery of the law
those sold under the sentence of the curse.
24 For the man who died rises on the third day, and answering Mary who was
eager to touch the holy members he cries out: Do not touch me, for I have not
yet ascended to my Father; but go to my brothers, and say to them: I am
ascending to my Father and your Father, and my God and your God. But the words
"I have not yet ascended to my Father" the Word did not utter, nor God who
sets out from heaven and dwells in the bosom of the Father, nor Wisdom who
encompasses all generated things; but the man himself, fitted together from
members of every kind, declared this, the one raised from the dead, who had
not yet after death gone up to the Father, but was reserving for himself the
firstfruits of the ascent.
25 And he clearly names the crucified man himself Lord of glory when writing;
since he also showed him both Lord and Christ, as the apostles, speaking with
one mind to visible Israel, say: Let all the house of Israel therefore know
assuredly that God has made him both Lord and Christ, this Jesus whom you
crucified. Therefore he made the suffering Jesus Lord, and not Wisdom, nor the
Word, who from of old has the power of lordship, but the one who stretched out
his hands aloft upon the cross.
26 For if he is bodiless he neither falls under the touch of hands, nor is
encompassed by perceptible eyes; he does not endure wounding, is not nailed
with nails, does not share in death, is not hidden in earth, is not shut in a
tomb, does not rise from monuments.
27 No one takes my soul from me; I have authority to lay down my soul, and I
have authority to take it again. And if he had the authority over both as God,
he permitted those who were attempting without counsel to destroy the temple,
but more manifestly he was at work raising it. It has been shown from
irrefutable verdicts that he himself through himself raised his own house,
constructing it anew. And the mighty works of the Son must also be ascribed to
the most divine Father. For the Son does not create without the Father,
according to the unbreakable pronouncements of the sacred writings. For this
very reason, at one time the most divine Begetter is written to have raised
Christ from the dead; at another the Son promises that he will raise his own
temple. If then from what has been examined beforehand the divine Spirit of
Christ has been shown impassible, the unholy ones strike needlessly against
the apostolic definitions. For if Paul said that the Lord of glory had been
crucified, looking clearly to the man, it will not on that account be
necessary to attach suffering to the divine. Why then do they join this,
weaving it together, saying that Christ was crucified from weakness?
28 And if indeed it were also fitting to attach a form of weakness to him, one
would say it is consistent to fasten these things to the man, certainly not to
the fullness of deity, or to the dignity of the highest Wisdom, or to the God
who is written as over all according to Paul.
29 This then is the manner of the weakness, through which he is written
according to Paul to have come to death; For he lives from the power of God,
the man clearly dwelling together with the divine Spirit; since the Power of
the Most High who is believed to be in him has also been shown according to
the definitions already given.
30 And if, having entered the virgin womb, he did not diminish the authority,
neither, when the body was fixed to the wood of the cross, is the Spirit
defiled. For the body was crucified aloft, but the divine Spirit of Wisdom
both dwelt within the body, and traversed the heavens, and encompassed all the
earth, and held the abysses, and searching out the souls of each distinguished
them, and did all things together in the usual way as God. For the highest
Wisdom is not confined and contained within bodily bulks, as the materials of
liquids and solids are shut within vessels, and are rather contained than
contain the receptacles. But being a certain divine and inexpressible power,
encompassing the innermost and outermost of the temple she strengthens it, and
from there reaching beyond, she holds all the bulks together by encompassing
them.
31 And if the sun, being a visible body and graspable by sense, though
enduring so many and such outrages everywhere on earth, does not change its
order, nor feel a small or great blow, do we suppose that the bodiless Wisdom
is defiled and changes its nature, if her temple is nailed to a cross, or
endures dissolution, or undergoes wounding, or receives corruption? But the
temple suffers, while the unstained substance stands altogether undefiled in
its dignity.
32 From here I will now also proceed to the things that were done. What then?
Since on account of these things a very great synod arrives at Nicaea, some
two hundred and seventy in number gathered together (for I am not able to
write the exact number because of the crowd of the multitude, since I did not
investigate this with complete eagerness), and when the manner of the faith
was being examined, the writing of Eusebius' blasphemy was put forward as a
clear proof. And when it had been read before all, it immediately procured for
the hearers an immeasurable disaster on account of the deviation, and provided
incurable shame for the one who wrote it. And when the workshop of those
around Eusebius was clearly caught, the lawless writing having been torn up
before the eyes of all together, certain men from a conspiracy, putting
forward the name of peace, silenced all those who were accustomed to speak
best. But the Ariomaniacs, fearing lest perhaps, so great a synod having been
assembled in the same place, they should be ostracized, leaping forward they
anathematize the forbidden doctrine, having subscribed with their own hand to
concordant writings. And having seized the presidential seats by the greatest
possible running about, when they ought to have submitted, they at one time
secretly, at another openly, champion the opinions that had been voted down,
plotting against the proofs in various ways. And wishing to make firm the
tares they had planted, they fear the overseers; and in this way they make war
upon the heralds of piety. But we do not believe that godless men can ever
prevail over the divine. For even if they prevail again, they will again be
defeated, according to the solemn-voiced prophet Isaiah.
