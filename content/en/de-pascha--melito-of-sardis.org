Melito on Pascha
The scripture of the Hebrew Exodus has been read, and the
words of the mystery have been made clear: how the sheep is sacrificed and how
the people is saved. Understand, then, beloved: thus is the mystery of the
Pascha new and old, eternal and temporal, corruptible and incorruptible,
mortal and immortal; old according to the law, new according to the word,
temporal because of the type, eternal because of the grace, corruptible
because of the slaughter of the sheep, incorruptible because of the life of
the Lord, mortal because of the burial in the earth, immortal because of the
resurrection from the dead; old is the law, new is the word, temporal the
type, eternal the grace, corruptible the sheep, incorruptible the Lord, slain
as a lamb, risen as God. For "as a sheep he was led to slaughter," yet he was
not a sheep; and as a silent lamb, yet he was not a lamb; for the type came to
be, and the truth was found. For in place of the lamb God came to be, and in
place of the sheep a man, and in the man Christ, who has contained all things.
So then the slaughter of the sheep and the procession of the Pascha and the
writing of the law have issued into Christ Jesus, for whose sake all things in
the older law came to be, or rather in the new word. For the law became word,
and the old became new, going forth together from Zion and Jerusalem, and the
commandment grace, and the type truth, and the lamb a son, and the sheep a
man, and the man God. For as a son he was born, and as a lamb he was led, and
as a sheep he was slain, and as a man he was buried, he rose from the dead as
God, being by nature God and man. He who is all things: insofar as he judges,
law; insofar as he teaches, word; insofar as he saves, grace; insofar as he
begets, Father; insofar as he is begotten, Son; insofar as he suffers, sheep;
insofar as he is buried, man; insofar as he rises, God. This is Jesus the
Christ, "to whom be the glory unto the ages. Amen." This is the mystery of the
Pascha, as it is written in the law, as it was read a little while ago. I will
narrate the words of the scripture, how God commands Moses in Egypt, when he
wishes to bind Pharaoh under the scourge, and to loose Israel from the scourge
by the hand of Moses. "For behold," he says, "you shall take a lamb without
spot and without blemish, and at evening you shall slaughter it with the sons
of Israel, and by night you shall eat it with haste, and you shall not break a
bone of it. Thus," he says, "you shall do: in one night you shall eat it by
families and clans, your loins girded and the staffs in your hands. For this
is the Pascha of the Lord, an eternal memorial for the sons of Israel. And
taking the blood of the sheep, anoint the doorways of your houses, placing
upon the posts of the entrance the sign of the blood to put the angel to
shame. For behold, I will strike Egypt, and in one night it shall be made
childless from beast to man." Then Moses, having slaughtered the sheep and
completed the mystery by night with the sons of Israel, sealed the doors of
the houses for the guard of the people and for the shaming of the angel. And
when the sheep is slaughtered and the Pascha is eaten and the mystery is
completed and the people rejoices and Israel is sealed, then the angel arrives
to strike Egypt. The uninitiated in the mystery, the one with no share in the
Pascha, the unsealed of the blood, the unguarded of the spirit, the hostile,
the unbelieving: having struck them in one night, he made them childless. For
the angel, having gone around Israel and seen them sealed with the blood of
the sheep, came upon Egypt, and he tamed stiff-necked Pharaoh through
mourning, clothing him not with a grey robe nor with a torn mantle,


but with all Egypt torn, mourning over her first-born. For all Egypt, having
come to be in pains and plagues, in tears and wailings, came to Pharaoh wholly
in mourning, not only in appearance but also in soul, torn not only in the
robes of her covering but also in the breasts of her luxury. And it was a new
spectacle to see, here people beating their breasts, here wailing, and in the
midst Pharaoh in mourning, sitting upon sackcloth and ashes, wrapped in the
palpable darkness as in a mourning garment, girded with all Egypt as with a
tunic of grief. For Egypt was lying about Pharaoh as a wrapping of wailing.
Such a tunic was woven for the tyrant's body; such a robe the angel of
righteousness put upon hard Pharaoh: bitter mourning and palpable darkness and
childlessness. And it was upon her first-born; for swift and insatiable was
the death of the first-born. And it was a new trophy to see upon the dead
falling in a single moment. And the turning of those who lay became food for
death. And a new disaster, if you hear it, you will marvel. For these things
enclosed the Egyptians: a long night and palpable darkness and a groping death
and an angel pressing hard and Hades swallowing their first-born. And you have
something newer and more fearful still to hear. In the palpable darkness the
ungropable death was hidden, and the wretched Egyptians groped the darkness,
but death, searching, groped the first-born of the Egyptians at the angel's
command. If then anyone groped the darkness, he was led out for death. If any
first-born, embracing with his hand a dark body, terrified in his soul, cried
out piteously and fearfully: "Whom does my right hand hold? Whom does my soul
tremble at? Who is this dark one lying about my whole body? If you are a
father, help; if a mother, have compassion; if a brother, speak; if a friend,
stand firm; if an enemy, depart, for I am a first-born." And before the
first-born fell silent, the long silence seized him, addressing him: "You are
my first-born; I, the silence of death, have been allotted to you." And
another first-born, perceiving the capture of the first-born, denied himself,
that he might not die bitterly: "I am not a first-born; I was born as a third
fruit." But the one who could not be deceived laid hold of the first-born; and
he fell face-down, silent. And in a single moment the first-born fruit of the
Egyptians perished. The first-sown, the first-born, the longed-for, the
cherished, was dashed to the ground, not of men only but also of the
irrational animals. And a lowing was heard in the plains of the earth of
beasts mourning over their nurslings; for a heifer with her calf and a mare
with her foal and the rest of the beasts in labor and swollen with milk
mourned bitterly and pitiably over their first-born fruits. And there was
wailing and beating of breasts over the destruction of men, over the dead
first-born. For all Egypt stank from the unburied bodies. And it was a fearful
spectacle to behold, the mothers of the Egyptians with hair loosed, the
fathers with mind loosed, crying out terribly in the Egyptian tongue:
"Wretched, we have been made childless in a single moment of the first-born
fruit." And they were beating their breasts, striking with their hands a
clapping over the dance of the dead. Such a disaster enclosed Egypt, and
suddenly made her childless. But Israel was guarded by the slaughter of the
sheep, and was even illumined together by the poured-out blood, and the death
of the sheep was found a wall for the people. O new and unspeakable mystery:
the slaughter of the sheep was found salvation of Israel, and the death of the
sheep became life of the people, and the blood put the angel to shame. Tell
me, O angel, what put you to shame: the slaughter of the sheep, or the life of
the Lord; the death of the sheep, or the type of the Lord; the blood of the
sheep, or the spirit of the Lord? You are clearly put to shame, having seen
the mystery of the Lord taking place in the sheep, the life of the Lord in the


slaughter of the sheep, the type of the Lord in the death of the sheep. For
this reason you did not strike Israel, but made Egypt alone childless. What is
this new mystery, that Egypt was struck unto destruction, but Israel was
guarded unto salvation? Hear the power of the mystery. Nothing, beloved, is
said and done without a parable and a preliminary model. All things whatsoever
that come to be and are said partake of a parable: what is said of a parable,
and what is done of a prefiguration, so that as what is done is shown through
the prefiguration, so also what is said may be illumined through the parable.
A work does not arise except upon a prior construction. Or is not the thing to
come seen through the typical image? For this reason, then, a preliminary
model of the thing to come is made, either of wax or of clay or of wood, so
that what is to arise, taller in size and stronger in power and beautiful in
form and rich in construction, may be seen through a small and corruptible
model. And when that arises toward which the type was, that which once bore
the image of the thing to come is dissolved as having become useless, yielding
to that which is true by nature the image concerning it. And what was once
precious becomes worthless, when that which is precious by nature has been
manifested. For each thing has its own season: the type its own time, the
material its own time. You make the type of the truth. You desire this because
in it you see the image of the thing to come. You bring the material to the
type. You desire this because of what is to arise in it. You complete the
work, this alone you desire, this alone you love, seeing in it alone the type
and the material and the truth. As then in the corruptible examples, so also
in the incorruptible; as in the earthly, so also in the heavenly. For the
salvation and truth of the Lord was prefigured in the people, and the
teachings of the gospel were proclaimed beforehand by the law. So then the
people became a type of a preliminary model, and the law a writing of a
parable, and the gospel a narration and fulfillment of the law, and the church
a receptacle of the truth. So the type was precious before the truth, and the
parable marvelous before the interpretation. That is, the people was precious
before the church was raised up, and the law marvelous before the gospel was
illumined. But when the church arose and the gospel came to the fore, the type
was emptied, handing over its power to the truth, and the law was fulfilled,
handing over its power to the gospel. In the same way that the type is
emptied, having handed over the image to that which is true by nature, and the
parable is emptied, having been illumined through the interpretation, so also
the law was fulfilled when the gospel was illumined, and the people was
emptied when the church was raised up, and the type was dissolved when the
Lord was manifested, and today those things that were once precious have
become worthless, now that the things precious by nature have been manifested.
For once the slaughter of the sheep was precious, but now it is worthless
because of the life of the Lord; precious the death of the sheep, but now
worthless because of the salvation of the Lord; precious the blood of the
sheep, but now worthless because of the spirit of the Lord; precious the
silent lamb, but now worthless because of the unblemished Son; precious the
temple below, but now worthless because of the Christ above; precious the
Jerusalem below, but now worthless because of the Jerusalem above; precious
the narrow inheritance, but now worthless because of the broad grace. For the
glory of God is not established in one place nor in a short measured lot, but
his grace has been poured out upon all the ends of the inhabited world, and
there the almighty God has tabernacled; through Jesus Christ, to whom be the
glory unto the ages. Amen. You have heard, then, the narrative of the type and
of the corresponding fulfillment; hear also the construction of the mystery.
What is the Pascha? For it has been named from what happened: from suffer
comes suffering. Learn then who the sufferer is, and who the one who suffers
with the sufferer is, and why the Lord is present upon the earth: that, having
clothed himself with the sufferer, he may snatch him up to the


heights of the heavens. "God in the beginning having made the heaven and the
earth" and all things in them, through the Word fashioned man from the earth
and imparted breath to the form. This one he placed in paradise toward the
east in Eden, to take delight there. Having laid down this law for him through
the commandment: "From every tree in the paradise you shall eat for food, but
from the tree of knowing good and evil you shall not eat; on the day you eat,
you shall die by death." But man, being by nature receptive of good and evil,
like a clod of earth for seeds on either side, received the hostile and greedy
counselor, and having touched the tree he transgressed the commandment and
disobeyed God. He was therefore cast out into this world as into a prison of
the condemned. And when this one had become abundant and long-lived, through
the tasting of the tree, and had gone into earth, an inheritance was left by
him to his children. For he left to his children an inheritance not of purity
but of fornication, not of incorruption but of corruption, not of honor but of
dishonor, not of freedom but of slavery, not of a kingdom but of tyranny, not
of life but of death, not of salvation but of destruction. And a new and
fearful destruction of men upon the earth came to be. For these things
happened to them: they were snatched up by tyrannical sin and led into the
regions of the desires, in which they were drained by insatiable pleasures, by
adultery, by fornication, by wantonness, by desire, by love of money, by
murders, by bloods, by a tyranny of wickedness, by a lawless tyranny. For a
father brought a sword against a son, and a son laid hands upon a father, and
an impious one struck nursing breasts, and a brother killed a brother, and a
stranger wronged a stranger, and a friend murdered a friend, and a man
slaughtered a man with a tyrannical right hand. So all became upon the earth,
some man-killers, some brother-killers, some father-killers, some
child-killers. And something more fearful and more novel was found. A certain
mother laid hold of the flesh she had borne, she laid hold of what she had
nursed at her breasts, and she buried the fruit of the womb into the womb, and
the wretched mother became a fearful tomb, having swallowed the child she had
conceived. I speak no further. And many other strange and more fearful and
more wanton things were found among men. Father upon a child's bed, and son
upon a mother's, and brother upon a sister's, and male upon male, and "each
neighed after his neighbor's wife." And over these things sin rejoiced. Being
death's fellow-worker she went ahead into the souls of men and prepared for
him as food the bodies of the dead. And upon every soul sin set a mark, and
those upon whom she set it, these had to die. So all flesh fell under sin and
every body under death, and every soul was driven out of the fleshly house,
and what was taken from earth was dissolved into earth, and what was given
from God was shut up in Hades, and there was a dissolving of the fair joining,
and the fair body was divided. For man was being portioned out by death. For a
new disaster and capture enclosed him. He was dragged captive under the
shadows of death, and the image of the Father lay deserted. For this reason,
then, the mystery of the Pascha has been completed in the body of the Lord.
But first the Lord arranged beforehand his own sufferings in the patriarchs
and in the prophets and in the whole people, sealing them through both law and
prophets. For what is to be in a new and great way is arranged long
beforehand, so that when it comes to be it may obtain faith, having been
prefigured from long ago. Thus also the mystery of the Lord, prefigured from
long ago, but seen today, obtains faith as having been completed, even though
it is thought new by men. For the mystery of the Lord is old and new, old
according to the type, new according to the grace. But if you look to this
type, you will see the


true through the outcome. Therefore if you wish to see the mystery of the
Lord, look to Abel likewise being murdered, to Isaac likewise being bound, to
Joseph likewise being sold, to Moses likewise being exposed, to David likewise
being persecuted, to the prophets likewise suffering on account of the Christ.
And look also to the sheep in the land of Egypt being slaughtered, which
struck Egypt and saved Israel through the blood. And the mystery of the Lord
is also proclaimed through a prophetic voice. For Moses says to the people:
"And you shall see your life hanging before your eyes night and day, and you
shall not believe upon your life." And David said: "Why did the nations rage
and the peoples plot empty things? The kings of the earth stood by and the
rulers were gathered together against the Lord and against his Christ." And
Jeremiah: "I as an innocent lamb being led to be sacrificed. They plotted
evils against me, saying: Come, let us cast wood into his bread and let us
wipe him out from the land of the living, and his name shall not be
remembered." And Isaiah: "As a sheep he was led to slaughter, and as a lamb
silent before the one who shears it, so he does not open his mouth. Who shall
declare his generation?" Many other things also were proclaimed by many
prophets toward the mystery of the Pascha, which is Christ, "to whom be the
glory unto the ages. Amen." This one, having come from the heavens upon the
earth on account of the sufferer, and having clothed himself with that very
one through a virgin womb and come forth a man, received the sufferings of the
sufferer through a body able to suffer, and he dissolved the sufferings of the
flesh, and by the spirit unable to die he killed the man-killing death. For
this one, led as a lamb and slain as a sheep, redeemed us from the worship of
the world as from the land of Egypt, and loosed us from the slavery of the
devil as from the hand of Pharaoh, and sealed our souls with his own spirit
and the members of the body with his own blood. This is the one who clothed
death with shame, and set the devil in mourning as Moses did Pharaoh. This is
the one who struck lawlessness and made injustice childless as Moses did
Egypt. This is the one who delivered us from slavery into freedom, from
darkness into light, from death into life, from tyranny into an eternal
kingdom [and having made us a new priesthood and an eternal peculiar people.]
This is the Pascha of our salvation. This is the one who in many endured many
things. This is the one who was murdered in Abel, and bound in Isaac, and was
a stranger in Jacob, and sold in Joseph, and exposed in Moses, and slain in
the lamb, and persecuted in David, and dishonored in the prophets. This is the
one who was made flesh in a virgin, who was hung upon a tree, who was buried
in the earth, who was raised from the dead, who was taken up into the heights
of the heavens. This is the silent lamb. This is the lamb being murdered. This
is the one born of Mary the fair ewe-lamb. This is the one taken from the
flock and dragged to slaughter and sacrificed at evening and buried by night,
who was not broken upon the tree, not dissolved into earth, having risen from
the dead and having raised man from the burial below. This one has been
murdered. And where has he been murdered? In the midst of Jerusalem. Why?
Because he healed their lame and cleansed their lepers and gave light to their
blind and raised their dead. For this he suffered. Where is it written in the
law and in the prophets: "They repaid me evils for good things and
childlessness to my soul, plotting evils against me, saying: Let us bind the
just one, because he is inconvenient to us." What have you done, O Israel,
this new injustice? You have dishonored the one who honored you. You have
unglorified the one who glorified you. You have denied the one who confessed
you. You have denounced the one who proclaimed you. You have killed the one
who made you live. What have you done, O


Israel? Or is it not written for you: "You shall not pour out innocent blood,
lest you die badly"? I, says Israel, killed the Lord. Why? Because he had to
suffer. You are deceived, O Israel, arguing such sophistries over the
slaughter of the Lord. He had to suffer, but not by you. He had to be
dishonored, but not by you. He had to be judged, but not by you. He had to be
hung, but not by your right hand. This voice, O Israel, you owed to cry out to
God: "O Master, even if your son had to suffer and this is your will, let him
suffer then, but not by me. Let him suffer by foreigners. Let him be judged by
the uncircumcised. Let him be nailed by a tyrannical right hand, but not by
me." But you, O Israel, did not cry out this voice to God, nor did you devote
yourself to the Master, nor were you put to shame by his works. The withered
hand restored to the body did not put you to shame, nor the eyes of the blind
being opened through his hand, nor paralyzed bodies being set upright through
his voice, nor did the newer sign put you to shame, a dead man being raised
from the tomb already of four days. You then, having dismissed these things,
at evening prepared for the slaughter of the Lord sharp nails and false
witnesses and nooses and scourges and vinegar and gall and a sword and
affliction, as against a murderous robber. For you brought upon him both
scourges upon the body and a thorn upon his head, and you bound his fair
hands, which fashioned you from earth, and that fair mouth of his which fed
you life, you fed with gall, and you killed your Lord at the great feast. And
you were rejoicing, but he was hungering. You were drinking wine and eating
bread, but he vinegar and gall. You were bright in face, but he was downcast.
You were exulting, but he was afflicted. You were singing psalms, but he was
being judged. You were giving orders, but he was being nailed. You were
dancing, but he was being buried. You were lying upon a soft bed, but he in a
tomb and a coffin. O lawless Israel, why then have you accomplished this new
injustice, casting your Lord into new sufferings, your Master, the one who
fashioned you, the one who made you, the one who honored you, the one who
called you Israel? But you were not found to be Israel, for you did not see
God, you did not understand the Lord, you did not know, O Israel, that this is
the first-born of God, the one begotten before the morning star, the one who
raised up the light, the one who made the day bright, the one who
distinguished the darkness, the one who fixed the first starting-post, the one
who hung the earth, the one who quenched the abyss, the one who stretched out
the firmament, the one who adorned the cosmos, the one who fitted the stars in
heaven, the one who made the luminaries bright, the one who made the angels in
heaven, the one who fixed the thrones there, the one who fashioned man upon
the earth. This was the one who chose you and guided you from Adam to Noah,
from Noah to Abraham, from Abraham to Isaac and Jacob and the twelve
patriarchs. This was the one who guided you into Egypt and guarded you and
there nourished you. This was the one who led you with light in a pillar, and
sheltered you in a cloud, who cut the Red Sea and led you through and
scattered your enemy. This is the one who gave you manna from heaven, who gave
you drink from the rock, who gave you the law at Horeb, who gave you an
inheritance in the land, who sent you the prophets, who raised up your kings.
This is the one who came to you, who healed your sufferers and raised your
dead. This is the one against whom you acted impiously. This is the one
against whom you did injustice. This is the one whom you killed. This is the
one for whom you took silver, demanding from him the didrachms for his head.
Ungrateful Israel, come, and be judged with me concerning your ingratitude.
How much did you value being guided by him? How much did you value the finding
of your fathers? How much did you value the descent into Egypt and the
nourishment there through the fair Joseph? How much did you value the ten
plagues? How much did you value the nocturnal pillar and


the daytime cloud and the passage through the Red Sea? How much did you value
the gift of manna from heaven and the supplying of water from the rock and the
legislation at Horeb and the inheritance from the land and the gifts there?
How much did you value the sufferers whom he himself, being present, healed?
Value for me the withered hand which he restored to the body. Value for me
those blind from birth whom he led into light through a voice. Value for me
the dead who lay, whom he raised from the tomb of three, even four, days.
Priceless are the gifts from him to you. But you unworthily repaid him,
rendering to him ingratitudes, evils for good things and affliction for joy
and death for life, for which you yourself also ought to have died. Then if a
nation's king is seized by enemies, on his account war is joined, on his
account a wall is broken, on his account a city is plundered, on his account
ransoms are sent, on his account ambassadors are dispatched, that he may be
taken back or sent back to life or buried if dead. But you brought a vote
against your Lord. For the one whom the nations worshipped and the
uncircumcised marveled at and the foreigners glorified, for whom even Pilate
washed his hands, this one you killed at the great feast. Therefore bitter to
you is the feast of unleavened bread, as it is written for you: "You shall eat
unleavened bread with bitter herbs." Bitter to you are the nails which you
sharpened. Bitter to you is the tongue which you provoked. Bitter to you are
the false witnesses whom you set up. Bitter to you are the nooses which you
prepared. Bitter to you are the scourges which you plaited. Bitter to you is
Judas whom you hired. Bitter to you is Herod whom you followed. Bitter to you
is Caiaphas whom you obeyed. Bitter to you is the gall which you prepared.
Bitter to you is the vinegar which you cultivated. Bitter to you is the thorn
which you reaped. Bitter to you are the hands which you made bloody. You
killed your Lord in the midst of Jerusalem. Hear, all you families of the
nations, and see: A new murder has taken place in the midst of Jerusalem, in a
city of the law, in a Hebrew city, in a prophetic city, in a city thought
just. And who has been murdered? And who is the murderer? I am ashamed to say
and I am compelled to speak. For if the murder had happened by night, or if he
had been slain in a desert, it would have been useful to be silent; but now,
in the middle of the street and city, in the midst of the city with all
seeing, an unjust murder of a just man has taken place. And thus he is lifted
up upon a tree, and a title is attached signifying the one murdered. Who is
this? To say it is heavy, and not to say it is more fearful. But hear,
trembling, on whose account the earth trembled: He who hung the earth is hung.
He who fixed the heavens is fixed. He who established all things is
established upon a tree. The Master is outraged. God has been murdered. The
king of Israel has been done away with by an Israelite right hand. O new
murder, O new injustice. The Master has been recast with a naked body and has
not even been thought worthy of a covering, that he might not be seen. For
this reason the luminaries turned away and the day grew dark, so as to hide
the one stripped upon the tree, not darkening the body of the Lord, but the
eyes of these men. For though the people did not tremble, the earth trembled.
Though the people did not fear, the heavens feared. Though the people was not
torn, the angel was torn. Though the people did not wail, "the Lord thundered
from heaven, and the Most High gave his voice." For this reason, O Israel, you
did not tremble at the Lord, you did not fear at the Lord, you did not wail at
the Lord; you wailed over your first-born, you did not tear your garments over
the hung Lord, you tore them over your murdered ones. You abandoned the Lord,
you were not found by him. You dashed the Lord to the ground, you were dashed
to the ground. And you lie dead, but he rose from the dead and went up into
the heights of the heavens. The Lord, having clothed himself with man, and
having suffered on account of the sufferer, and having been bound on account
of the one held, and having been judged on account of the condemned, and
having been buried on account of the buried, rose from the dead and cried out
this voice: "Who is the one being judged against me? Let him stand against
me."


I released the condemned. I made the dead live. I raise the buried. Who is the
one who contradicts me? I, he says, the Christ, I who destroyed death and
triumphed over the enemy and trampled Hades and bound the strong one and
snatched man away into the heights of the heavens, I, he says, the Christ."
"Come then, all you families of men who are mixed in sins, and receive
forgiveness of sins. For I am your forgiveness, I the Pascha of salvation, I
the lamb slain for you, I your ransom, I your life, I your resurrection, I
your light, I your salvation, I your king. I lead you up into the heights of
the heavens. I will show you the Father from the ages. I will raise you
through my right hand." This is the one who made the heaven and the earth, and
fashioned man in the beginning, the one proclaimed through law and prophets,
the one made flesh in a virgin, the one hung upon a tree, the one buried in
the earth, the one raised from the dead, and having gone up into the heights
of the heavens, the one sitting at the right hand of the Father, the one
having authority to judge all things and to save, through whom the Father made
the things from the beginning until the ages. This is "the Alpha and the
Omega." This is "beginning and end," a beginning unspeakable and an end
incomprehensible. "This is the Christ." This is the king. This is Jesus, this
the commander, this the Lord, this the one risen from the dead, this the one
sitting at the right hand of the Father. He bears the Father and is borne by
the Father, "to whom be the glory and the power unto the ages. Amen." Melito,
On Pascha. Peace to the one who wrote and to the one who reads and to those
