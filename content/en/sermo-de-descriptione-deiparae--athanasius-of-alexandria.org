* Sermon on the Enrollment of the Theotokos
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of our father among the saints Athanasius, On the enrollment of holy Mary, and
on Joseph.

Just as the workmen who have been allotted to mine the gold-bearing earth,
when they find a vein putting forth fragments of what is sought, linger there,
there they dig beside it, as a guide of the gain; and they do not go behind,
not changing the place first, until they overtake the lair of what lies before
them: in the same manner we also, the overseers of the ecclesiastical labor,
having the evangelist Luke in our hands, toward the comprehension of the
Master's treasure of the birth from the Virgin, will not first cease from the
search, until we genuinely gather up the wealth that lies before us. Therefore
we will also track the evangelist in order, that we may leave none of the
profitable things behind. For even if the words in the prologues are small,
yet the mystic teachings of the joy are great. And let no one grow weary at
the discourse, having received the things read aloud as small to the hearing.
For as compared with the power of the Master who was born, the things read
aloud are small and exceedingly small, swaddling-clothes, and a manger, and an
unsuitable place. And these indeed are small, but the things set forth are
mystic: a womb uninterpreted, a bosom incomprehensible, a holy-rooted =28.945=
breast, milk of a strange flow, a nipple alien to the natural pasture, the
Mother of the Lord becoming enrolled, the betrothed Joseph embracing a
communion of name and not of deed. You were just now hearing the evangelist
Luke saying: "In those days there went out a decree from Caesar Augustus, that
all the inhabited world should be enrolled. This enrollment first came to be,
Cyrenius being governor of Syria. And all were going to be enrolled, each to
his own city. And Joseph also went up from Galilee, from the city of Nazareth,
into Judaea, to the city of David, which is called Bethlehem, because he was
of the house and lineage of David, to be enrolled with Mary who was betrothed
to him as wife, being with child." Did you see how small the words are, and
how great the mystic teachings? The Lord was being carried in the womb, and a
first enrollment was being legislated: he who has all creation enrolled, and
who said to his own disciples: "Rejoice and be glad, for your names have been
written in the heavens." He permitted his own mother to become enrolled in the
small Roman registers. Who will not speak? Who will not marvel at the
condescension of the Lord? Above free, and below enrolled; above Son, and
below servant; above king, and below a hireling; above rich, and below in
want; above worshipped, and below taxed; above a divine throne, and below a
rustic cave; above the paternal and incomprehensible bosom, and below a small
stable for beasts, and a little manger. Who will not marvel at great things
above, and small swaddling-clothes below? The one who looses was bound; the
one who feeds was fed; the incomprehensible appeared as a child; the one who
lets springs loose was borrowing a breast; the one who bears all things was
being borne uninterpretably; the one who is nowhere left behind, being
uncircumscribed, was in part being circumscribed. O the paradoxical things!
Elizabeth brought forth in a bright little house, and the Virgin gave birth in
the small cave; John upon a bed, and the Lord in a manger. Not simply is the
Lord's birth from a Virgin; not simply is his being wrapped in the
swaddling-clothes; not simply is the drawing of the breast; not simply is the
being laid in a manger; not simply is the being summoned to the enrollment of
Augustus; all things for what reason, and toward what, and riddling.

He is born of a Virgin, that he may set right the first-formed Adam who was
molded of dust from virgin earth. He is wrapped in swaddling-clothes, that he
may loose the cords of our sins, loosing bonds with bonds, according to the
saying that declares: "For each is bound with the cords of his own sins." He
drew the breast, that the milk of grace might gush forth, which he poured from
his own side; for milk =28.948= is nothing other than blood made white. He was
laid in a manger, that he might set right the irrationality of humanity, and
become food of the two animals; of which two animals? of the Gentile and of
the Jewish people, concerning whom it is written: "In the midst of two animals
you shall be known." He accepted the enrollment under Caesar Augustus, that he
might set right the disorder of the inhabited world. For this enrollment has
become a pledge of the good order of the inhabited world. And that the things
said are true, hear in brief. Before the Lord's becoming man, much tyranny
held the race of men; the plot of the earth was governed irrationally; they
made a mockery of the royal dignity; a multitude of kings of many men was
being planted; someone having perhaps a hundred men under himself immediately
usurped the form of kingship. Such was the rivalry of the nations: loving
local rule, and not passing from place to place, unless they had in some
manner armed themselves against one another. While this confusion was being
prolonged, only the Lord accepted to dwell in the virginal palace upon earth;
immediately he stops the many-rule, and plants the monarchy, allotting the
empire of the Romans to govern all, according to the imitation of the heavenly
sojourn. For just as in heaven one kingdom of God prevails, and all the angels
are subject to his will, the demons having thrown off the yoke after the
manner of barbarians: in the same manner also upon the earth, even if there
are many unsubjected nations, yet one kingdom of the Romans prevails. For this
good order then the king who was being carried in the womb commanded the first
enrollment to be assembled under Augustus; whence he also permitted himself to
become enrolled to the empire of the Romans toward the confirmation of the
laws being decreed. For then a king's law prevails, when the king who
legislates first fulfills the law. For the one who legislates and does not
fulfill is a destroyer, rather than a lawgiver. For the aforementioned good
order then, the enrollment under Augustus first came to be, that both the
worship of the law might be transferred, and the tribe of the Jews might be
abolished, so that it might no longer be permitted to the disorderly Jews,
using self-law, to murder the unaccountable, but to follow the laws by the
yoke of necessity. Necessary therefore is the aforementioned enrollment under
Augustus; this enrollment humbled the tribe of the Jews, and highly exalted
the race of the Christians; the Jews no longer daring, in fear of the empire
of the Romans, to use the former madness. Wherefore also after this
enrollment, having seized the Lord, and hastening to hand him over to death,
they did not dare to accomplish it according to what seemed good to them,
=28.949= after the likeness of the prophets, whom they tore apart without
discrimination; but they brought him to Pilate, a ruler of the Romans, who
exacted a petition, they being required to give account of the things said,
angling for a consent. Wherefore also Pilate, the ruler of the Romans, when he
saw the Jews dog-tonguing against the slaying of the Lord, and knowing that
they had handed him over through envy, was questioning the Jews, saying: As
you all know clearly, I find nothing worthy of death in this man, O Jews. Why
do you hasten to defile the empire of the Romans, holding the righteous man by
incontinence, and wishing the unaccountable to be accountable? I find nothing
worthy of death in this man. Rather then let Barabbas be put to death, and let
Christ be released; because that one is a murderer and seditious, but this one
a benefactor and giver of life; since you have also embraced this accusation
against him.

And so, the Jews not consenting, but calling Pilate an enemy of Caesar, who
were saying to Pilate's face, as he was advocating for Christ: "You are not a
friend of Caesar," since you wish the one who says he is a king to be kept
alive. And so Pilate did not permit the Jews to accomplish anything whatsoever
by their own judgment. For if he had permitted the Jews, the Jews would have
put the Lord to death with stones, or would have sawn him with a wooden saw.
And this being done, we would have been deprived of the cross; since the wood
of the cross is a judgment of the Romans. Wherefore also Pilate was
questioning the Jews: "What do you wish that I do to Jesus, who is called
Christ?" And they, taking courage from the custom of the Romans, cried out:
"Away with him, away with him, crucify him;" and immediately Pilate nodded;
for he possessed the nod from above. For this good order then the first
enrollment under Caesar Augustus was being legislated; for if this enrollment
had not been legislated beforehand, the body of the Lord would not have been
handed over to a complete burial; for the children of the Jews, by their
exceeding madness, were going to hand over the body of the Lord either to
fire, or to sink it in the sea. For if they brought the friend Jeremiah down
into a pit of mire; what were they going to accomplish against the body of the
Lord, if they did not have the Roman law boxing them? Wherefore also, the Jews
not being permitted by Pilate to receive the body of the Lord, Joseph of
Arimathea, being a friend of Pilate, having come forward asked from him the
body of the Lord, having offered something incomparable. Necessary therefore
is the enrollment under Caesar Augustus; if =28.952= even the betrothed of the
Virgin, as you were just now hearing, became gloomy, yet the whole world
received a beginning of good order.

What it is, The betrothed of the Virgin became gloomy, hear in brief. When
Joseph the betrothed of the Virgin, as you were just now hearing, on account
of the enrollment of Augustus was arriving at Bethlehem with Mary who was
betrothed to him as wife, being with child, Joseph was ignorant of what was
being mystically wrought in the Virgin; he had not yet received an oracle of
an angel. But the Virgin possessed the security, both in the word and in the
bulk; for as much as the increase of the womb was coming to be, so much the
Virgin was spending a more carefree life; at once both keeping the virginity
undefiled, and bringing the birth unharassed, not so much being wearied as
being lightened. Besides these things Joseph, being in agony, when he saw her
having in the womb, not knowing the treasure within, having become gloomy, was
putting it to the Virgin along the way, saying: What has seemed good to you,
Mary? Are you not the one who as a reverend Virgin was reared in the sacred
precincts? Are you not Mary, who did not accept to look upon a man's face? Are
you not Mary, who was persuaded to be pledged to me contrary to purpose? Are
you not Mary, who prayed to keep the rose of virginity unfading? Are you not
Mary, who loved Susanna, and hated the Egyptian woman? Are you not Mary, who
was recounting Tamar who was forced, and abhorring the disorderly Amnon? Where
is the chamber of your temperance? Where is the spotless bridal-room of your
chastity? Where is your reverent face? I am ashamed, and you speak freely?
Because I am silent about your sin, do you thus hasten to go more closely? Do
you not see the bulk of your belly? Within the infant is panting, and you are
made bright as one untried by trials? Depart, Mary; return to your house; seek
out the stealthy bridegroom; be enrolled to that one. I do not accept
another's outrage; you do not find a defense against the punishment. But the
Virgin to Joseph: Why are you displeased, my betrothed? Why do you vote
against me as disorderly? Why do you look around at the bulk of my womb with
insulting eyes? Why do you compel me to blush? I have become the glory of
women; I have stripped them of the former shame; I have called Eve back; I
have removed the flaming sword; and do you prepare me to be covered? No one
will be able to exact penalties of me; for I bear a common Master. If I bear
witness concerning myself, I will be found as some vainglorious one. Endure a
little, O Joseph, and shepherds will persuade you; magi will fully inform you;
a star will put you to shame; angels will pierce you. Now you doubt, but after
not much you will glorify, when you hear the heavenly host crying with one
voice: "Glory =28.953= to God in the highest, and on earth peace, among men
good will." Then you will fall on your knees, and you will not inquire. Do you
not see the passage of the way, Joseph, and that no man is journeying with us?
Do you not see the sun putting on a brighter robe? Do you not see all creation
leaping? Do you not see the mountains jumping? Do you not see the hills being
gladdened? Do you not see the outpourings of the waters overflowing? Do you
not see the Jordan running around outside? From the signs guess the proof, and
do not, because I am with child, waver, Joseph. I was borrowed, I was not
defiled; I lent the belly, I did not defile the virginity. Do not disbelieve
the things set forth, O Joseph. When I am midwifed, then who I am will be made
known; now I am reproached, then I will be blessed; now you drive me away as a
cause, then you will invite me as a holy one to be near. Do not magnify
yourself, because you seem to keep watch; for I keep you; for the one who
accepted to make my belly a tent, this one is the keeper of all nature. Let us
go then, and let us become enrolled, that both the military legislation and
the royal decreeing may be fulfilled.

In these things Joseph, having become reverent, was thereafter walking a
double way; with the feet making tracks on the earth, and with the reasonings
leaping over the clouds. For what else did Joseph have to accomplish, than to
keep the mind above, since every good giving is given from above. For this
reason thereafter Joseph was being carried along with the Virgin, becoming at
once both a helper and a watcher of the things about to be; not that the
Virgin had need of a helper, or rather the one being carried in the Virgin;
for he himself was and is the helper of all, and toward him the one who cries
has cried out: "My help is from the Lord who made the heaven and the earth."
But that it was necessary as a business of the economy, that Joseph be a
helper, toward the Virgin being rumored as married, toward the devil's being
unaware of what was being constructed, that he might not learn what had come
to be: how God accepted to consort with men. What then does the evangelist
Luke? For it is good thereafter to see also Joseph being fully informed, and
the little house, where the Virgin brought forth, receiving the type of the
Church; where the manger is an altar, and Joseph a day-server, and the
shepherds deacons, priests the angels, high priest the Lord, a throne the
Virgin, mixing-bowls the breasts, a vestment the incarnation, fans the
cherubim, a disk the Holy Spirit, the Father a disk-cover. And that the place,
where the Virgin brought forth the Lord, traced beforehand =28.956= a type of
the Church, you were just now hearing the evangelist Luke saying: "And it came
to pass while they were there, the days were fulfilled for her to bear her
son, the firstborn; and she swaddled him, and laid him in the manger, because
there was no place for them in the lodging." O the paradoxical things! ah, how
small the words, and how great the mystic teachings! "It came to pass while
they were there, the days were fulfilled for her to bear." O the paradoxical
things, the maker of the times was waiting for a defined time! The setter of
bounds of the times was awaiting an appointed term of months, that no one
might suppose a phantasm or a seeming. "It came to pass while they were there,
the days were fulfilled for her to bear her son, the firstborn; and she
swaddled him, and laid him in the manger."

See the mystic bringing-forth of the Virgin; she herself bore, she herself
swaddled. In the case of worldly women another bears, and another swaddles;
but in the case of the Virgin not so; she herself bore, and she herself
swaddled; and she herself an unwearied mother and an untaught midwife, did not
permit anyone with unholy hands to touch the undefiled birth; she herself
through herself tended the one from her, before her and above her; "And she
swaddled him, and laid him in the manger, because there was no place for them
in the lodging." Did you see, O friend, how the cheap lodging, where the
Virgin bore, who once carried in the womb, and did not bear a second time, and
did not endure a suggestion of outrage, sketched beforehand the type of the
Church; even if some, having gone mad, because they heard the evangelist Luke
saying: "And she bore her son, the firstborn," also dogmatized a second
conception of the Virgin, changing incorruption into corruption; saying in
their own dogmas: He would not be called firstborn, if a second had not
followed. And not only do those of the opposite side put forward Luke as a
witness of their own folly, but also the evangelist Matthew announcing the
same things, and still more clearly enjoining; whence also the godless make
his words manifest, saying in their own writings the things which Joseph is
recorded to have received in a vision, the evangelist Matthew saying: "And an
angel of the Lord appeared to Joseph in a dream, saying: Joseph, son of David,
do not fear to take Mary your wife; for that which is begotten in her is of
the Holy Spirit. And Joseph, having arisen, he says, took Mary his wife, and
they went into Egypt; and he did not know her, until she bore her son, the
firstborn." Likewise also =28.957= those of the opposite side enjoin these
words, dogmatizing that the glorious Virgin carried in the womb a second time.
But away with the absurdity! the virginal and glorious treasure remained
altogether unstained; the second heaven was not defiled; the God-containing
vessel of the Most High was not made useless. The Lord was called firstborn
from the Virgin; and he was well called; not because he summoned some second
of like kind; but because he was firstborn, that is, incomparable. For
neither, because the blessed Paul said concerning the Lord's beginningless
generation from the Father, who is firstborn of all creation, did he already
also write a natural brother of the Lord; nor again did the same Paul, having
called the same Lord firstborn from the dead, already also introduce one like
the Lord, who likewise trampled Hades. Wherefore hear understandingly, and
believe in a manner dear to God. The Lord is firstborn from the dead, and from
the Father and from the Virgin, that through all things the unique dignity of
the sonship may be known. And the Master's birth is incomparable; for where
the Mother is a Virgin, how is a birth being interpreted? Likewise also the
evangelist Matthew's saying concerning Joseph, that "He did not know her,
until she bore her son, the firstborn;" for he did not know her truly, until
she bore her son, the firstborn. Until the Virgin was carrying in the womb,
Joseph did not know her; he did not know what was in her; he did not know what
was being transacted; he did not know what was being constructed; for only
when she bore, then he knew; then Joseph knew what the power of the Virgin
was, and what she was counted worthy to become; then he knew that she gave the
breast, and did not undergo the spoiling of the veins; then he knew that she
brought forth, and did not perceive the things of childbirth; then he knew
that the unhewn rock was offering a nipple to the intelligible rock; then
Joseph knew that Isaiah wrote concerning her: "Behold, the virgin shall have
in the womb." On account of the incomparable then Jesus is firstborn. And if
it is necessary to make the word true, the same Lord is also Only-begotten
from the Father; for he alone has been begotten from the alone without
beginning; the same Lord is both Only-begotten and firstborn; Only-begotten
from the Father, Only-begotten from the mother, on account of the definition
of the economy. For he has many brothers, not by nature, but by grace, both in
the Virgin and in the Father. Likewise also in the resurrection the Lord has
brothers; and who are these? Before all the holy apostles, to whom the Lord
said: "I go to my Father, and your Father;" to whom the Lord said: "But you,
praying, say: Our Father who is in the heavens, hallowed be your name," in
Christ Jesus our Lord, to whom be the glory and the power now and always, and
unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
