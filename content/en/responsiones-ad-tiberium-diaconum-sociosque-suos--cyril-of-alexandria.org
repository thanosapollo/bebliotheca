591 That God the Word, being God, works the divine signs through his own
flesh.
SOLUTION. We say that the Only-begotten Word of God became man, not that he
might cast away being God, nor that he might be thought a bare Word, but
rather having become incarnate, and having made his own the body from the holy
Virgin and Theotokos. Therefore he who names Christ signifies neither a bare
Word, nor a common man, or as one of

like us; but the Word from God the Father having become man, as I said, and
having been anointed for a mission. For a man was not deified, as some say,
being united to the Word, but the Word himself, having taken flesh and become
man, remained even so God. Whenever therefore he works the divine signs, do
not, having separated the Word from God apart from his holy flesh, ascribe the
power over the things accomplished to him alone; but rather think piously that
the Only-begotten Word of God, having become man, often also worked through
his own flesh, as having it as his own, not according to confusion or mixture.
And just as one may think in the case of a skilled man, a carpenter perhaps or
a smith, that the soul does the works together with its own body; and no one
would say that they are works of the soul alone, even if it is she who moves
the body toward works, but of both together; 592 so think also in the case of
Christ. For before the incarnation, being still bare and by himself, the Word
worked the things befitting God, but having become man, he worked also through
his own flesh, as I said. Thus he touched the blind, and again raised the
widow's son, stretching out the hand and touching the bier; thus having spat
and made clay he anointed the eyes of the man blind from birth. And if he also
lived spiritually, think again that, raising the things that belong to us into
a spiritual way of life, he himself began the matter in a human way, that he
might become a way and a beginning for the nature of man toward being able to
live, no longer according to the flesh and pleasure-loving, but rather holily
and spiritually. For he has become for us the beginning of every good, and for
this reason he appeared as man, that having freed our nature from the weakness
in Adam, he might show it spiritual as in himself and first.

Likewise how man is according to the image of God.
SOLUTION. But since they say that others also inquire how indeed man ought to
be understood as according to the image of God, and then some altogether
unintelligently say that the image of the body, and the visible form itself,
593 and nothing else, is the likeness toward God; I thought it necessary to
say that they have gone astray, and have a mind unloving of the contemplation
of the truth. For while the Savior says clearly "God is spirit," they say that
the divine nature is body-like, and in such a character as that in which we
also are. Is he then himself also a body, and no longer thought a spirit? For
the forms follow the bodies in every way. But since God is spirit, he is
altogether formless, and beyond type and shape and every circumscription. And
we have been formed toward him according to a first and most proper manner, so
far as it may be thought, according to virtue and sanctification. For the
divine is holy, and source and beginning and generation of every virtue. And
that it would be more fitting thus to understand

that man came to be according to the image of God, the all-wise Paul also will
teach, saying to those in Galatia, "My little children, of whom I am again in

faith toward him; but in those who transgress the faith, the characters do not
shine forth soundly. Therefore they need another spiritual travail and
intelligible regeneration, that, the Holy Spirit having flashed upon them
through sanctification the divine image, they may again be reformed unto
Christ. And it is not unpersuasive to say that the likeness toward God is also
in man according to the ruling power. For 594 there has been given to him to
rule all things upon the earth. And this is a second account of the likeness
toward him. But if being fashioned or having been made according to the image
of the Creator lay in the nature and form of the human body, how would it be
possible for some to lose it? For we have cast away nothing of the things that
belong to us essentially. But since sanctification and righteousness form us
toward God, we say that those who no longer lived according to virtue and as
in sanctification have cast away this holy and exceptional beauty. Wherefore
it is taken up again through sanctification and virtue and the life according
to piety. But if some from very great lightness think the divine nature to be
man-shaped, how did the Savior say to the Jews concerning God and the Father,
"Amen I say to you, you have never heard his voice, nor seen his form"? For if
he were, as I said, man-shaped, how have not the Jews only, but indeed all
men, beheld his form?

595 That the eucharist ought to be accomplished in the catholic churches
alone.
SOLUTION. But the gift, or rather the offering which we accomplish mystically,
must be offered in the holy churches of the orthodox alone, and not elsewhere.
Or those who do this transgress the law openly. And this is to be seen from
the sacred writings. For the law commanded that the sheep be sacrificed
according to the day, or rather the feast, of the Pascha, and it was unto a
type of Christ; but "in one house it shall be eaten," it says, "and you shall
not carry out of its flesh outside." Those therefore carry the gift outside
who do not accomplish it in the one and catholic house of Christ, that is, the
Church; and through another law something of this sort is signified. For it is
written again, "And whoever shall sacrifice a calf or a sheep in the camp, and
shall not bring it to the doors of the tent, that soul shall be utterly
destroyed from its people." Therefore those who sacrifice outside the tent
would be none other than the heretics, and destruction hangs over those who
dare to do this. We believe therefore that the offerings in the churches are
both sanctified and blessed and perfected by Christ.

596 That we are able to curtail the fleshly, or rather natural, pleasures, but
no longer to cut them out entirely.
SOLUTION. To some the all-wise Paul seems to say certain hard things, or
rather hard to understand, according to the voice of the holy apostles. But
that these things are filled with the wisdom from above, it is not possible to
doubt, for Christ speaks in him. He said therefore "that I delight in the law
of God according to the inner man, but I see another law warring against the
law of my mind, and taking me captive in the law of sin," and again "Wretched
man that I am, who will deliver me from this body of death? But thanks be to
God through Jesus Christ our Lord." For the movement of the flesh campaigns
against the mind that looks toward self-control through the fear of God, and
it arrays itself against the impulses toward chastity and leads out against
them terribly. But those who use the watchfulness befitting those who
reverence God rebuke the movement of the flesh, and blunt the sting of sin,
using exercises and labors and the other decent practices. So that it is not
possible to uproot from the flesh its innate desire; but by watchfulness, as I
said, not to allow it to grow bold against the mind is possible, especially
since 597 the Only-begotten Word of God has become man, and has no longer
permitted the savage law of sin that is in our members to play the youth
against us. And the most excellent Paul will teach this clearly, writing, "For
what the law could not do, in that it was weak through the flesh, God, sending
his own Son in the likeness of flesh of sin and for sin, condemned sin in the
flesh, that the ordinance of the law might be fulfilled in us who walk not
according to flesh but according to spirit." We shall therefore prevail over
the innate movements not altogether, nor wholly; for this has been reserved
for the all-blessed life expected according to the age to come; but we are
able to play the man against them, and to rebuke the movements of the flesh,
God working with us, and supplying us the power from on high. And desire is
more vigorous in the slothful, and as it were lording it over their heart; but
it is feeble and easily rebuked and sent out of the mind in those who have the
divine fear. For it is written that "the fear of the Lord is pure," that is,
making pure.

Against those who say, If it was possible for Christ to sin, having worn the
likeness of Adam on account of the flesh.
SOLUTION. But they are altogether unintelligent who have somehow supposed that
598 even Christ himself was able to go astray, because he came to be in the
form that is according to us economically and took the form of a servant and
lived among men upon the earth. For if he departed from being what he was, if
he migrated from being God into being only according to us, let them seek in
him the charges of human weakness. But if he has worn the nature of man for
this reason, that he might show it, as having been weak in Adam, most powerful
in himself and stronger than sin, why do they busy themselves in vain about
what they cannot find? And how have they forgotten him saying, "The ruler of
this world is coming, and in me he will find nothing." For the inventor of sin
accuses all flesh; but the meddling of that one's malice was idle in Christ,
for nothing at all has been found in him. And indeed he said to the Jews,
"Which of you convicts me concerning sin? If I speak truth, why do you not
believe me?" As therefore we were condemned in Adam through the disobedience
and the transgression of the divine commandment, so we have been justified in
Christ through being wholly without transgression and the obedience that is
complete and blameless. And the boast of human nature has come to be in this.
The curse therefore has been stopped, and the mouth of sin has been stopped,
and with it the power of death has been abolished, 599 as having withered away
together with its own root. For if sin has become for us the procurer of all
evils, the justification in Christ will be the taking away of the things that
have befallen, entering through obedience, and possessing complete
blamelessness. So that even if he has worn Adam, as they say, yet he was not

Against those who say, If angels also are according to the image of God.
SOLUTION. But interpreting "According to the image of God" in the case of man,
we were not saying that the form of the body is transformed toward him. For
the divine is bodiless and immaterial and intangible, and beyond quantity and
circumscription, form and shape. But applying the divine imaging to man, we
were saying that according to the quality of morals or of manners, and
according to the spiritual form, which is manifested through the good
appearance of the virtues, we say he has been made according to the likeness
of the Creator himself. For the divine is in every beautiful thing, and is the
very source and root and generation of all virtue, and the good things come
even to us from there. If therefore we are formed toward God according to the
form from the virtues, 600 and this belongs also to the holy angels and
incomparably beyond us; it is not impossible to think that every rational
creation also is formed toward God through sanctification and righteousness
and through every virtue. For if the divine and supercosmic beauty is fitting
for us who are upon the earth, how not much more for the rational powers
above, upon whom God rests? For this reason also the divine Scriptures name
heaven his throne.

Against those who say, How, being bodiless, did the demons mix with women?
SOLUTION. But since they say that some say, How, being bodiless, have the
wicked demons had intercourse with women, and they bore them the giants; it is
necessary also for us to speak toward this cursorily, not stretching ourselves
out with the length of narratives, but setting forth the mind of the matter as
in an epitome. They say therefore that according to the times or seasons still
of old they were divided, I mean both those who came from Cain and those from
Enosh, who on account of his very great righteousness has been named God among
those of that time; "for he hoped," it says, "to call upon the name of the
Lord his God." But those who came from Enosh were cultivators of righteousness
and of every 601 good-working, following the customs of the father; those
however under Cain were bold and accursed and readily practicing every form of
baseness, for such was their father also. So long therefore as the races were
unmixed with one another, there was preserved among those who came from Enosh
the distinction of shining in the best life. But when the sons, it says, of
him who was called god, that is, of Enosh, beheld the daughters from Cain,
whom the Scripture also called daughters of men; then they were corrupted
toward them, and having become subject to shameful desires, they were turned
into those women's manners. Whence God, being angry, prepared the women chosen
