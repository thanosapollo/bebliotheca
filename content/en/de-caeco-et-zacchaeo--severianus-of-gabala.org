* On the Blind Man and Zacchaeus

A homily on the blind man whom Christ healed, and on Zacchaeus, and concerning
judgment, and almsgiving.

Many and various are the teachings of the holy Scriptures, but one is the
grace that shines upon all, and one source of teaching is the Spirit of truth.
And as many things as the law declares, and as many as the prophets teach, as
many as the apostles preach, as many as the Gospel of piety dictates, they
come round as from one flourishing and fruitful spring, the Holy Spirit; and
it is not possible either to say anything solemn, or to think anything right,
or to do anything wonderful, unless the Holy Spirit confirms the things said,
and the things thought, and the things done. For even if someone plumes
himself with human wisdom, and has a soul fortified by human reasonings, yet
shakes off the word of God, he is nothing, and shall be counted as nothing.
For this reason the prophet says: They have rejected the word of the Lord.
What wisdom is there in them? For us, then, the Scriptures of piety are
flowers of the truth; and many are the flowers, but one is the meadow that
nourishes the flowers; many are the lamps of the teaching, but one is the true
luminary; many are the stars, but one is the heaven that bears the stars; many
are the branches, but one is the root; many are the fingers, but one is the
hand that writes through the fingers; many are the strings, one is the lyre of
the Spirit; many teachings, one is the fountain of piety. But I marvel how
this blind man, having read no law, having gone through no prophets, having
met with no Gospels, having been confirmed by no apostles, was calling upon
the Savior of the inhabited world, saying, Jesus, son of David, have mercy on
me. Whence did you learn to call these things, not having learned what you
invoke? You did not read books, being deprived of your eyes; you did not have
leisure for teachings, having one occupation of life, the petition, or rather
the begging; whence then did you know the luminary of the world, whom you
neither =59.600= beheld in the heavens, nor on earth, being deprived of your
eyes? But truly in this man was fulfilled what was said by David: The Lord
makes wise the blind. A great crowd was about Jesus, and the blind man did not
know the luminary of truth, yet he perceived the working; he seized by
disposition what sight did not receive. What, he says, does the crowd want?
They say to him, Jesus the Nazarene is passing by. O the wonder! other things
he is taught, and other things he preaches; he hears Jesus Nazarene, yet he
does not call him Jesus Nazarene, but Jesus son of David. Those who see were
speaking the things from conjecture, but the blind man was proclaiming the
things from truth. Jesus, son of David, have mercy on me. Therefore let each
one, making the voice his own, break forth a confession from the heart, and
let each one say this voice for himself, Son of David, have mercy on me. And
just as, brethren, whenever many go to a mourning, and see a body laid out,
and wept over by its own people, all weep, some sharing in the grief, others
searching out their own affairs; and often a woman weeps and wails, yet not
for the one laid out (for he is a stranger), but for her own who went before,
and the foreign occasion becomes a reminder of her own evils; so let each of
you, brethren, when he learns the things proclaimed by the saints, make the
saying his own, speaking for his own wounds, Son of David, have mercy on me;
and let each one say even now, being aroused from his own evils, one, that he
is blind in understanding, another, that he has a deaf soul; another, that he
has a lame reasoning, another, that he has a leprous mind. For the passions
which Christ healed upon bodies, these also =59.601= come to be upon souls and
need help from above. There is a blind soul, needing to look up again, a blind
soul, that does not see the wonders of the law, a blind soul, that


does not behold the age to come, a blind soul, that sees the body of Christ,
but does not recognize the divinity. And that the soul is blind which is
ignorant of the things concerning God, Isaiah bears witness saying: And the
servants of God were blinded. How were they blinded? For you saw, he says,
many times, and you did not keep watch; the ears were opened, and you did not
hear. And who is blind, but my servants? and who is deaf, but they who lord it
over them? And the Savior, wishing to show a blindness of understanding,
convicts Pharisees who are blind concerning the truth, saying: Let them be;
they are blind guides of the blind; and if a blind man lead a blind man, both
shall fall into a pit. And a soul is deaf that does not obey the law, that
sets aside the Master's voice, deaf not from nature, but from choice. For this
reason the Savior was saying: He who has ears to hear, let him hear; not that
some of those present did not have ears; (for how would they have leisure for
the teaching, not having the organ of hearing?) but he shows clearly that the
appearing ear has been created for hearing, yet the perception of the soul is
not fitted to the hearing of the divine law. There is then no profit of the
outer hearing, when the inner understanding has been made deaf. Someone is a
leper in soul, not the one whose body is darkened, but the one whose soul is
being darkened. For what is leprosy in a body, but a double color? What is
leprosy in a soul? A double reasoning. Woe to the sinner who treads upon two
paths! Whenever someone at one time believes, and at another time disbelieves,
and at one time inclines toward almsgiving, and at another toward inhumanity,
he is a leper in soul, not having a healthy color, but dividing the
understanding; and just as leprosy divides the body, so an evil reasoning
divides the understanding. Let each one therefore cry out for his own wounds,
and call upon Jesus the physician of souls and bodies, as the blind man was
crying: Son of David, have mercy on me. And those going before were rebuking;
but the boldness is not hindered through those who forbid. Faith knows how to
fight against all things, and to conquer all things. Men were rebuking, and
the believer does not shrink back, but follows the Master, knowing that the
shamelessness for piety is a good thing.

βʹ. For if many are shameless for money, is it not most excellent, for the
salvation of the soul, to put on the good shamelessness? Jesus commands him to
be brought. The voice of the one calling upon him with faith stopped the Lord
Jesus; he commanded the one who had already drawn near to him by faith to be
brought to him, and to approach in body. He commanded him to be brought, and
he says, What do you want me to do for you? He does not ask idly; for he was
able even to send the healing to him as he sat, but the Jews who slander the
truth could have said, as in the case of the man born blind, This is not he,
but one like him. He therefore calls the one being benefited into the midst of
the theatre of the ungrateful, that he may first show the maiming of nature,
and then make known the authority of grace. What do you want me to do for you?
He asks that man, but he teaches those present. And he says, Lord, that I may
look up again. See how many pledges he takes from the one receiving the
healing, that he may shut out the slander of those who work evil. He says
then, Go, your faith has saved you. Do you see how what was said to us on the
previous day is shown through the very works? and that the benefits are
unsold, and they are sold for faith? Jesus does not give the benefits for
silver, but he sells them for faith. If you do not put down faith, you do not
receive the benefit; the grace is spread out, but the faith comes forward. And
just as there is one spring, and some draw from the spring with small vessels,
others with larger, and one draws little water, another much, not because the
spring divides the measures, but because each draws according to his own
vessel; so the grace is spread out as a spring, and the purpose of the one who
comes becomes a vessel and instrument of the grace. If then you bring forward
little faith as a small vessel, you draw little grace; but if you bring
forward a great understanding, you draw a great benefit. For this reason the
Savior says, Do you believe


that I am able to do this? And he, Yes, Lord; and the =59.602= Savior,
According to your faith be it unto you. Hence the Apostle exhorts the
Corinthians saying: Be you enlarged, that he may show that to the enlarged
understanding a broader grace enters in; and the prophet, Open your mouth, and
I will fill it. There is no hindrance on the side of the benefit, but on the
side of our sloth. Jesus the luminary of righteousness has risen, the ray of
the intelligible sun is spread everywhere; and one enjoys a lesser grace,
another a greater, not because the grace divides, but because the
understanding submits the measure. For just as there is one sun for all the
earth, and one ray, and one brightness, yet not all the world has been equally
illuminated, but one has the light bright, another less, another greater, and
one narrow, another broader, not because the sun divides to this house the
greater, and to that the lesser, but because the ray takes space according to
the windows which those who built opened, and so is poured in; so, since the
reasonings are windows of our soul, whenever you open broader reasonings, you
receive a broader benefit; whenever you narrow the understanding, you narrow
the grace. Uncover your reasoning, that you may receive the brightness. For
this reason Paul says concerning himself, But we all, with unveiled face,
beholding as in a mirror the glory of the Lord, are being transformed from
glory to glory. One blind man was healed through the history, and the eyes of
many are opened through the teaching. According to your faith be it unto you.
And the blind man, he says, was following, glorifying God, and Jesus does not
hinder him. Here it is necessary to consider, when he healed the demoniac, he
was asked by the one who received the benefit to follow Jesus; for he wished,
he says, to follow. But the Savior hinders and says: Go to your house, and
declare how many things God has done for you. For what reason does he hinder
the one who wished to follow, but does not hinder this one? why does he permit
the following to this one, but does not permit it to that one? Both usefully
to that one, and profitably to this one. He did not allow that one to follow
for another cause, because he was a demoniac; and of the demoniac both the
speech and the tongue had been distorted. He therefore sends him as a herald,
that from his settled state he may proclaim the benefactor. For it was a
strange spectacle to see the one who once was not settled proclaiming settled
words. He therefore makes the very organ of the one benefited a herald of the
one who saved. And see the wonder, see the humility of the Savior, see the
gratitude of the demoniac. The Savior says: Go, and report how many things God
has done for you. He does not say, How many things I have done for you, but in
humility he ascribes it to the name of God. But the demoniac went about
proclaiming how many things God Jesus had done for him. He therefore sends
that one to proclaim, that from the settled state he may show the greatness of
the benefit; but he permits the blind man to follow. Why? Because he was going
up to Jerusalem to accomplish the great mystery, and to take up the economy on
behalf of the world, and a cross was about to follow, and insult, and all the
things newly invented by those who fight against God; for this reason he makes
the blind man follow, that they may have a fresh memory of the miracle, that
the memory of the healing may strengthen the understanding of those who were
about to slip. He makes the blind man follow, lest anyone deny the
wonder-working, since the understanding of the apostles was about to be shaken
as in a storm, and the state of those who saw was about to be tempest-tossed,
seeing the Only-begotten of God upon a cross. Therefore, lest looking toward
the passion they give way, he allows the blind man to follow, that having a
fresh memory of the benefit, they may have an unshaken reasoning of the truth.

γʹ. For Christ does many such things, since he came to take up the economy on
behalf of men. And lest anyone suppose him to suffer through weakness, and not
to endure through love of mankind, before he enters into Jerusalem, he sees a
fig tree. And he went, he says, seeking


fruit from it, and did not find it. And when he did not find it, he rebuked it
saying: Never may fruit come from you unto the age. What did the fig tree sin?
For does it have the fruit-bearing by rational authority? Does not the
creator, when he wills, furnish =59.603= energy to the fruit-bearing? Then
neither was it the season of figs; for the Gospel bears witness to this. And
who among common men, let alone God, demands fruit in an untimely season? Did
you not fix the seasons, O craftsman? Did you not give seasons and times to
the fruit-bearing? Why then do you demand, contrary to the season which you
appointed? And even if it was the season of figs, and the fig tree did not
bear fruit, was it a charge against nature? Does it offer when it wills? Does
it not bring forth fruit-bearings when it is commanded? For what reason then
does he rebuke the fig tree? Because he was about to undergo the passion, lest
they think him to suffer through weakness, he displays the power in the
lifeless thing, that he may show that he was able to dry up all who
contradict, to make vanish all who fight against God. But since he did not
come to judge the world, but to save, as he himself said, he displays the
power upon the lifeless, but he stores up the love of mankind for men. So also
here he was allowing the blind man to follow, that the memory, being young,
might dissolve the scandal, that they might have the wonder-working flashing.
But another wonder succeeded this one; for after the looking-up of the blind
man, another blind in soul looked up. Who is this? The chief tax-collector who
was blind concerning money, who by greed had a blind soul; for nothing so
blinds the soul as money. For this reason Moses says: For gifts blind the eyes
of men, and ruin just words. The chief tax-collector came, and Jesus was
passing by, he who dissolves every sin, who makes every wickedness vanish, who
changes the darkness into light, and brings the crooked things into a straight
way. Zacchaeus was short in stature, but stretched out in desire; short in
body, but great in mind; for he was seeking to see Jesus, and he was not able
because of the shortness of his stature; and going ahead, he procures by a
device what was lacking of the stature; for he runs up upon a sycamore, and he
was seeing Jesus as he passed. But our Lord Jesus Christ, concerning whom Paul
says, "He who knows how to do exceeding abundantly of what we ask or think,"
did not give him so much as he desired, but beyond what he expected. For he
desired only to see, but he deemed him worthy also of the welcome. And he who
saw his understanding (for before he goes up into the fig tree, he knows his
understanding) says to him: Zacchaeus, make haste and come down. A good fowler
is the Savior, bringing a soul down from a tree. Zacchaeus, make haste and
come down. He stretched out to him the rod of the word, he put round him the
birdlime of love, he took him from the wings of the understanding, and he
gathers him under his own Church. And see what he does. And lest you suppose
that it was the saying that did violence, but that the word of God is as a
fowler, the blessed Zechariah says: And the Lord showed me a fowler's vessel,
and he said to me: I will not pass by my people, until, he says, I have fowled
them. Thus it is a truth witnessed, that the word of God is birdlime,
entangling the souls with love as with birdlime; and just as that glue can no
longer separate what is held from what holds it, so also the souls united to
the Savior are glued as with the glue of the fowler. For this reason David,
the good nestling that was caught, was saying: My soul has been glued behind
you; and again the Apostle, But he who is glued to the Lord is one spirit. He
fowls Zacchaeus, and brings him down from the tree, and brings himself into
his tent, which once he himself had darkened with sins, but today the luminary
of piety has shone upon. When therefore the Savior entered, the tax-collector
received him rejoicing. O the great goodness of the Master! the sinless with
sinners, the fountain of righteousness with the matter of injustice; for greed
is matter of injustice. He entered into the house of the tax-collector, not
being insulted by the gloom of greed, but by the brightness of righteousness
making the greed


vanish. And see the wonder: Immediately Zacchaeus, standing before the
doorway, says: Lord, the half of my goods I give to the poor. Have you not yet
been taught, and you obey? have you not yet learned, and you have kept? The
Savior said nothing, taught nothing, did not propose concerning almsgiving,
did not then dictate love of the poor, but he was silent, and quietly shone;
and just as a sun being poured in through the rays into a house =59.604= does
not illuminate by a word, but by the very working shines down; so also the
Savior having entered, by the rays of righteousness banished the course of
injustice; For the light shines in the darkness, as the evangelist says. Lord,
the half of my goods I give to the poor. Well does he divide the greed, he
cuts the injustice. The half of my goods. Every whole is strong, but being cut
it is weak. The half of my goods. O the good voice, conquering nature, or
rather conquering habit! for habit is a second nature. He banished
insatiability, he recognized the truth. The half of my goods I give to the
poor, and if I have wronged anyone of anything or slandered, I restore
fourfold. Here one must attend carefully, that the wealth of Zacchaeus was not
from injustice only, but also from paternal substance; for if it were from
injustice, how could he restore fourfold what he received? For why does he
say, If I have wronged anyone of anything, I restore fourfold? Even if he
lived with lawlessness, yet he also knew the law; for the law commands the
thief to restore the theft fourfold. If anyone, he says, steal a sheep, and it
be known, let him give back four sheep. God multiplies the punishment
fourfold, or rather the loss, that even if the law does not frighten, the loss
may put to shame; for many often, not respecting the law, respect a loss. He
therefore condemns himself, not waiting for the vote of the law, but himself
becoming a law upon himself; For a law is not laid down for a righteous man.
The Savior, having received this voice, says: Today salvation has come to this
house. He justified himself, not because he simply saw Christ, but because he
obeyed the laws of Christ. Today salvation has come to this house, because
this man also is a son of Abraham. Before he did the works of Abraham, he does
not call him a son of Abraham, but he calls him Zacchaeus by the bare name;
but when he fulfilled the works of the patriarch, having promised love of the
poor, and alienation from injustice, then he says, And this man is a son of
Abraham.

δʹ. And we therefore, brethren, even if we are Christians, and are called so,
nothing so shows us to be Christians as the work of the truth. Let us not then
insult the name by the works, but let us honor the faith by the works. We were
called sons of Abraham through faith, let us be called sons of Abraham through
the good works. For as I said before, it is not the nobility of the body that
is sought, but the following of the manners. For this reason Abraham and Isaac
and Jacob and all the patriarchs denied the synagogue, because they did not
inherit the faith of the fathers; Abraham denied his own, because they did not
follow the faith; Isaac also denied them, and Jacob, and all the patriarchs;
for him who has denied God, nature also denies. And what word will bear
witness, that Abraham denied the Jews, lest we seem to be bold as against
enemies, but rather to convict them as having sinned? The blessed Jeremiah
says in the person of the people: Lord, have mercy on us; the mountain and the
city, which our fathers blessed, has become burnt with fire; you, Lord, have
mercy on us, because Abraham has not known us, and Israel has not recognized
us; you, Lord, spare us. Since, he says, those in whom we boast have been
alienated from us, and have denied our race, you have mercy on us, you who
conquer all by love of mankind. Have mercy on us, because Abraham has not
known us. For if he were making our things his own, would he not have
interceded on behalf of his own children? He interceded on behalf of the
Sodomites, and was heard; and interceding on behalf of his own he was not
heard? Abraham has not known us. Abraham denies those who are alien in
manners, but he makes his own, as I said, those who


are alien in race, but his own in piety. For this reason the Scripture,
wishing to show the Jews that of those whose manners one chooses, of these he
also receives the name, says: Your father is an Amorite, and your mother a
Hittite, your sister a Sodomite. See how it constructs the kinship from the
manners. Since she minded the things of the Sodomites, she heard sister of
Sodom, she heard daughter of Canaanites; and thus it was shown not justifying
the race, but honoring the character. But I think that the Canaanite and the
Amorite deny the =59.605= kinship; and whenever he sees those who fight
against God, and those alien to piety approaching him, he denies, and the
Canaanite does not accept to be called father of the ungodly. Why does he
deny? If you press a Canaanite, and say that Your father is a Canaanite, he
will also say: Is this my son who has denied the faith of the fathers? I am
not unfaithful, even if I am a Canaanite; for the Canaanite woman will bear
witness to my faith, crying, Son of David, have mercy on me. Let them not be
called sons of Canaanites; for they did not keep the faith of the Canaanite
woman; let them be called sons of stones and of wood, who say to the stone,
You are my father, and to the wood, that You begot me. But I think that even
the stones deny, and the wood forbid the kinship of the unfaithful. Even a
stone can deny and say: The son of impiety is not my child; the son of
injustice is not my child; for I, even if I am a stone, have heard the
Scripture saying: God is able from these stones to raise up children to
Abraham. Then, Yes, he says, you personify from the person of the stone, and a
stone speaks? Hear the prophet saying, that A stone from a wall shall utter
these things, and a beetle from wood shall speak. Even if you are silent, the
stones shall cry out. What then shall the race of the unfaithful be called, if
they are neither sons of Abraham, nor Canaanites because of the Canaanite
woman (for the brightness of the faith of the Canaanite woman blunted their
unbelief), nor sons of the stones? For the stones also deny the kinship. What
name for the unfaithful? Jeremiah set a name for them: Call them rejected
silver, because the Lord has rejected them. Let us therefore fear also,
brethren. Does the nobility of Christ honor you? Let the just judgment of
Christ frighten you. Yesterday it was being preached concerning almsgiving,
that one must set all things second to almsgiving, that one must prefer the
soul to all things. I marveled also at another ungratefulness, that how those
who do not have children take adopted sons; and one is made a father, another
is made a brother. You adopt the stranger, and you flee the Master? Adopt the
one who gives you back the wage; let Christ become a son to you. The saying,
he says, is fearful. See what you say; lest by pretext of the word you turn
aside from the word, lest you be carried down into unreason. Do you call
Christ a son of a man? But I not only call him son, but also brother, not from
myself, but following his own voices. They say to him once, Your mother and
your brothers seek you. The Savior says, Who is my mother, or who are my
brothers? Whoever does the will of my Father, he is my brother and sister and
mother. And if his mother, then the son of the mother is the one who has
honored righteousness by the name. Let every soul therefore become a mother of
Christ according to disposition. How a mother of Christ? Every soul travails
in herself with Christ; but if she is not transformed by piety, she cannot be
called a mother of Christ. Whenever you receive a word of Christ, and form him
in your understanding; and as in a womb you transform him by the reasoning,
you are called his mother. And that you may learn that in each one Christ is
formed, and a mother of Christ comes to be, I mean of the word of Christ, the
soul of each of us, Paul says: My little children, of whom I travail again,
until Christ be formed in you. Have you worked righteousness? You have formed
Christ in the soul. Have you given almsgiving? You have formed the character
of the truth; not that Christ is formed in each one; for he calls Christ the
word of piety, through which we take up the character of the truth. Have you
given almsgiving? You have formed


Christ. Have you done righteousness? The character of the truth has been
formed. One must therefore attend how one ought to make Christ a brother. Take
him as a partner of your affairs. You do not want to grant him all things?
Give him even the half as =59.606= Zacchaeus. For to him, he says, Zacchaeus
gave, not to the poor. But hear, that He who has mercy on a poor man lends to
God. Giving to the poor, you have given to Christ. Do you not know that he
takes up into himself the things given? do you not know how fearful a tribunal
he has written for you saying: When the Son of man comes in the regeneration,
he shall set the sheep on the right, and the goats on the left; and he shall
say to those on the right, Come, you blessed of my Father, inherit the kingdom
prepared for you, because I hungered, and you gave me to eat?

εʹ. See how the one who borrows through the poor confesses the debt. I
thirsted, and you gave me drink; I was naked, and you clothed me. Therefore in
the appearing form you clothe one who is naked, but in truth you clothe
Christ. Clothe Christ, that he may clothe you in the day of judgment; shelter
him, that he may shelter you in the day of wrath; hear David saying: He
sheltered me in the secret of his tent. There is need of a shelter; that
fearful fire takes hold, the gehenna that cannot be escaped; and if the hand
of Christ does not shelter, the fire will recognize the alien. It knows how to
take hold of aliens, it knows how to honor saints, it respects piety, it takes
hold of impiety. There wealth does not persuade, nor money, nor authority, nor
power; all things stand with equal honor at the judgment-seat of Christ. For
even if there are differences among men, yet toward the Master all things are
of equal honor, even if they have much difference. How great is the difference
of an angel and a man? Great, and not ordinary. How great is the difference of
sun and moon and stars? But they are called together into the law of worship.
How great is the difference of herb and of angels and of living things? Some
are immortal, others mortal; but each in the yoke of slavery recognizes the
mastership. For this reason David also joins the things divided in rank by the
concord, saying, Praise the Lord from the heavens, praise him in the highest,
praise him, all his angels, all his powers, sun and moon. For even if it has
come down in rank, yet it is united in the slavery; and just as, brethren, the
dignities in the world are divided toward themselves, but are united toward
the yoke of slavery (for all bow to the King, all tremble at the authority;
and the difference is about the dignities, but the concord is among the
slaves); so also there all men are equal, and only those who excel are
unequal, those who shine by piety. For this reason Daniel was saying well, I
was considering in a vision of the night, and behold one as Ancient of days
was sitting. Here attend carefully, I ask, how he recounts the judge of the
truth as in a bodily image. For since God is bodiless in nature, he describes
the intelligible things as in an image by the sensible. He says then, As
Ancient of days, not aged by time, but honored by antiquity; His garment was
white as snow, the hair of his head as pure wool; his throne a flame of fire,
his wheels burning fire. A river of fire being drawn before him; a thousand
thousands were standing by him, and ten thousand times ten thousand minister
to him. O fearful tribunal! O memory sufficient to frighten the ungrateful! If
being heard they are so fearful, being seen how much more fearful? And I was
considering in a vision of the night, and behold books were opened, and
thrones were set. Thrones, of whom? Or is it clear that of the holy apostles,
to whom the Savior was saying: When the Son of man comes in his glory, you
also shall sit upon twelve thrones, judging the twelve tribes of Israel. Books
were opened. Boo=59.607=ks, in which the base things have been written, in
which the good things have been written, in which the life of each has been
recorded. And he calls it a book, not because there is skin and letters with
God; but because with you the book is an instrument of memory, he recounts the
memory of God as in a book; for God has no need of a book; for to God memory
is a book.


Not because we men, reading prophets, apostles, Gospels, do not carry about
the memory in the heart without a book, does God need a book; the fountain of
memory does not need a book. There the virtuous works are written, there the
base things are written. Do not fear the prejudgment; even if your wicked
works are written down, they can be wiped out through the good works; just as
again if someone's name is written through piety, but he denies it by his

injustice, pillorying your madness; consider how great a fear, how great a
trembling, how the limbs are shaken, how all the inward parts boil up, when a

shall be able to recount that fear, according to which the Master of all is
about to judge the inhabited world? We say these things, not that we may
frighten, but that we may make sober. Let not money be preferred to Christ;
let not injustice and greed be preferred to God. You have heard the things
said before, that prosperity is as far as the tomb? These things were being
exercised for us yesterday. Why do you wait for the tomb to become an end of
life for you? Wait for the tomb to become an end of life for you, that through
the present you may be ferried across into the coming; love works of
righteousness; it is a tomb, in which we are dissolved, a tomb in which the
earthly vessel vanishes. Let us flee the coming judgment, brethren, I beg.
These things those who suffer must say, and those who suffer must hear; let us
take up a desire of righteousness. See how the prophet cries: Woe is me for
the day of the Lord, because the day of the Lord is near. If a prophet
remembering the day trembles, shall we who are liable to countless sins
remember without fear? Let us put the judge to shame through repentance, let
us wipe out our sins, let us fear that fearful voice which says, Whoever
denies me before men (not the one who denies by word, but the one who denies
by manners, denies), I also will deny him before my Father. Whoever confesses
me, I also will confess him before my Father. If the words being examined are
fearful, the deeds being seen, are they not rather more fearful? Consider,
brother, the judge of the inhabited world sitting, angels standing by,

world, the armies above the heavens, and in all these patriarchs, prophets,
