On the ascension of our Lord Jesus Christ.
=61.711=
The device of sin has been turned back against sin. For the craft of sin was
the cross, and to wrap the sinless body in death; but the cross has become for
the crucified flesh an engine of the ascent into heaven, and what was said
concerning sin has been reversed. For formerly Paul was proclaiming, Sin,
taking occasion through the commandment, deceived me, and through it slew me;
but now the word of righteousness is fitting to the device; for righteousness,
taking occasion through sin, deceived it and slew it. This, striking Paul with
amazement, moved him to that cry, Great is the mystery of godliness. For
resurrection is hidden in the cross, and a birth of immortal life; the King
being brought forth from Hades to men, giving birth to immortality that has
blossomed. Truly a mystery exceeding great. How then is the mystery known? He
was manifested, it says, in flesh. And what is the manifestation? He was
justified in spirit. What is the proof of the righteousness? He was seen by
angels. Angels, it says, recognized the dignity of the one who appeared; they
came and ministered to him. Trustworthy indeed, Paul, are the oracles of the
righteousness, and they honor the power of Christ; yet show to men a sign of
his strength. He was preached, it says, among the nations. A theological word
about him ran, and this though hindered by all; the preaching, being
obstructed, was widened. What then if the thing preached is only as far as
hearing, and having been preached to the nations has become nonsense to those
who heard? He was preached, it says, and was believed on in the world. Then,
showing that even this having been believed is unhindered, He was taken up, it
says, in glory. The strength of the Church was proclaimed, or rather the
regard of the whole inhabited world together. For it is a common help of the
nature of men, the humanity taken up today; for the things of the punishments
after it are more moderate. And consider for me the old punishments of men
from God, and you will find the public benefit of the ascension. From one
transgression Adam, having offended, was cast out; Cain was condemned to a
long trembling of body; heaven rained fire, as a rot of the earth, cauterizing
Sodom; a woman who counterfeited the law of the course was remolded into a
pillar. And what sort of calamities upon peoples, hear Scripture, as it
recounts in a dirge; They tempted, it says, God, and were destroyed by the
serpents. They murmured, it says, and were destroyed by the destroyer. They
fornicated, it says, and there fell in one day twenty-three thousand. The
Lord, it says, gave death upon Israel from morning even until the hour of
breakfast, and seventy thousand men died. Why should I recount to you the
proposals of God to the king at that time? Thus says the Lord, it says: Ask
for yourself three years of famine upon the land, or three months of your
enemies pursuing you, or three days of death in your land. The writers of the
Greeks too make tragedy of the misfortune of ancient times, the excess of
floods, the excess of conflagration, the many years of famines, the public
destruction of earthquakes, the civil outpourings of bloods, waves of
successive wars. There was then no peace of the arrays from heaven and
=61.712= earth, not for Greeks, not for barbarians, not for Jews. For it was
awaiting the mediator who makes peace of the things above and the things
below; concerning whom Paul says: He himself is our peace, who hindered the
enmity in his flesh. How many now are more wanton than the Sodomites? and the
air does not pour fire upon them, as before. For it received the mediator of
peace who goes about, and grew mild toward the race of men. How many are more
wicked than the generation in the time of Noah? and with a flood


these heaven does not wash. How many are more savage of mind than Cain? and
God does not punish them with the punishments of Cain. For when he is provoked
by us, looking toward the firstfruits seated with him on the throne, he is
reconciled; when he is angered at those below who revel outrageously, seeing
the blameless humanity at the right hand, he is prevailed upon; when the race
below that does evil stirs the wrath, the kinsman of the race sitting together
above soothes the anger. Paul, laying this bare for you, says, Christ is at
the right hand of God, and intercedes for us, not as using the voices of one
on trial; for Christ went up into a kingdom; not a private one, but Paul
shaped the very ascension of the firstfruits into an intercession that
vindicates the reconciliations. O the fearful spectacle today for the demons!
for they were seeing a fearful spectacle for them, a nature of man according
to appearance, a traveler of air, ascending higher than they, becoming higher
than heaven. But those indeed were groaning, while angels standing by the
disciples were crying out with great joy; This Jesus who was taken up from you
into heaven will so come, in the manner you beheld him going into heaven. The
mystery of the ascension is confirmed.
For the disciples remained gaping toward heaven and fixed with their gaze; and
as the flesh little by little became higher than their eyes, they were shaken
with thoughts of perplexity. Nowhere, they say, is the body that was being
seen; perhaps the ascension is merely of appearance; perhaps a vision of
fantasy; perhaps we suffered a deception of the gaze; or we did not undergo
deception, but perhaps the body was dissolved into air, and no longer has a
nature visible to those on earth. Seeing into heaven with such perplexity they
heard the angels suddenly: Men of Galilee, why do you stand looking into
heaven? What, it says, does the over-curious sight want for you? why has the
gaze gaped upon the heavens? do you seek him wholly in the heavens? The cloud
carries Jesus in. Or again someone will see him embodied; be confident as
concerning both things as certain. For even if he is now taken up into heaven,
he will also come again, In the manner you beheld him going into heaven.
But let the sinew of the tongue not be stretched for long; let us slacken this
worn-out harp-playing of words; let us give hearing also to the remaining
harps; let many-mouthed praise clap the ascension; let the race of all men
sing today in common, Great is the mystery of godliness; he was seen by
angels, he was believed on in the world, he was taken up in glory. To him be
the glory unto the ages of ages. Amen.
