FROM ORIGEN'S ON THE PROVERBS OF SOLOMON.

=13.17= CH. 1.

Proverbs of Solomon son of David. Son of David the Savior also is called. This
peaceful Savior of ours who came to be from the seed of David, that according
to flesh, reigned in Israel. Wherefore he was also inscribed, fulfilling the
economy on behalf of men: This is the king of the Jews. Jews are Israel, of
whom the peaceful Savior reigns as of those who see clearly, if they be truly
Israel. For not as many as are seed of Abraham are also altogether children,
by not doing the works of Abraham. To know wisdom and instruction. Wisdom is
knowledge of divine and human things, apprehen =13.20= sion; and according to
some, she is a vapor of the power of God, and a pure emanation of the glory of
the Almighty; for nothing dark falls into her; for she is a radiance of
everlasting light, and a spotless mirror of the energy of God, and an image of
his goodness. He who knows the works of wisdom knows these, that is, the
radiance of the everlasting, and the spotless mirror of the energy of God, and
the image of his goodness. And these are nothing other than to know the Son of
the love of God, and the wisdom, Christ. For Christ is God's power and God's
wisdom. And this is not revealed from flesh and blood, but from the heavenly
Father. And he also knows wisdom who knows the science of the divine oracles,
legal and prophetic, both evangelical and apostolic.

He sets the foundation upon the rock, that is, upon the faith of Christ, so as
to hold out against rains, and winds, and spirits, namely the temptations that
come to be from wherever. And if Luke says that he praised the steward of
injustice, he used it in a transferred sense, taking the praise upon the
shrewdness, sharp also through the mind; which seems to be shown also in, And
the serpent was wisest of all the beasts, according to which has also been
said, Become wise as the serpent; as if not inexperienced of the crafts of the
serpent. And he will understand a parable and a dark saying, both sayings of
wise men and riddles. What difference these have toward one another, let us
consider; and first let us see what a parable is, from the evangelical
starting-points we have. For others used the name of parable, and Matthew
thus: On that day, Jesus having gone out of the house was sitting beside the
sea, and all the crowd was standing on the shore, and he spoke to them many
things in parables: Behold, the sower went out to sow. A parable then is a
word as concerning something that has happened, which has not happened
according to the letter, but is able to happen, tropically indicative of
things from a transference of the things said in the parable. For the, The
sower went out, has not happened according to the wording, as we say the
things of history have happened; yet it is possible to happen according to the
letter: Behold, the sower went out, and the rest. I however think that Jesus,
speaking in parables to those outside, to whom, because of being outside, it
had not been given to know the mysteries of the kingdom of God, had gone out
of the house, as Matthew recorded, not without cause and reason having made
the, On that day, Jesus having gone out of the house, was sitting beside the
sea. For it was fitting, toward those who did not have room to enter into the
house, but were outside it, to go out of it. And that some such thing has been
thought by Matthew at the place will be clear to the one who
has observed the things brought on; For having left, he says, the crowds, he
entered into his house, and to the disciples he uncovers the things of the
parable. For observe in these in what manner to the crowds in para =13.21=
bles Jesus was speaking, and without a parable he speaks nothing to them;
which he also does until now; for the crowds do not have room for
interpretation of parables. And attend also to the, Having left the crowds he
entered into his house, very intelligently recorded, since it was not possible
for the crowds to come together with him, but only those differing from the
crowds, who were his disciples, and able to have boldness toward entering into
the house of Jesus. There, not as those who are outside, but having had room
to become inside, coming to the teacher, they ask to learn the inner mind of
the parables. And since they had room to follow him entering into the house,
for this reason answering toward the, Explain to us the parable, he said the
things he said. And Jesus not only goes out of his house, that having become
outside he may speak the parables to those outside, but he also sits beside
the sea, riddling that the crowds and those outside are not far from the waves
of the sea and its salt water. And observe that toward Jesus who had gone out
of the house and was sitting beside the sea many crowds were gathered; but no
longer many crowds, but the few who travel the narrow and afflicted [way], and
who find the way that leads into life, disciples, enter toward him into the
house. Examining then by a word what is fitting to the evangelical, according
to power, and to the will of Jesus who embarked into the boat and speaks in
parables to all the crowd beside the shore, I say, perhaps the Word, not
having planted his feet for the crowds, nor standing, but being in sea-water,
speaks while being carried upon that which, so far as it is possible, is able
to have planted itself upon water; which has been called a boat. And do not
wonder if such thoughts have been brought in more beautifully, so that the one
outside and the crowd would not suppose there to be even some hidden mind, and
one holding dense elegance, stored up in the evangelical disposition; for it
was a work of the grace managing the Word of God. And I try to hear more
deeply also the, The disciples coming said to him: Why do you speak to them in
parables? and the, Having left the crowds he came into his house; and his
disciples came to him saying: Explain to us the parable. For always the
crowds, if they hear a parable, do not even attend to it as needing
explanation; but the disciples, seeing that he speaks in parables to the
crowds, because they are outside, follow Jesus as he leaves the crowds, and
having seen that they cannot learn the things of the parables elsewhere than
by entering into the house of Jesus, they also enter toward him, and coming to
him they say: Explain to us the parable, concerning which they wish to learn.
But such then is the parable.

And next is the dark saying. There are then in the Scriptures some things said
so purposely darkly, that one could not take out even a chance thing in them
without much and great examination, confused both according to the phrasing,
and according to the thought, =13.24= and according to the composition of the
wording; which I think to be what is called in the book a dark saying. I will
set out an example of such things from Psalms, from the 31st psalm, the things
said thus: Because I was silent, my bones grew old, and the things that
follow. And simply, of the thought it is possible to understand the wording,
as we shall show; but of the dark saying, not even such a thing; as also that
in Isaiah: Woe, wings of a land of ships, beyond the rivers of Ethiopia,
sending in the sea hostages and papyrus letters upon the water. But to clarify
each of these and to understand, illuminating by the narration its darkness,
is not of the present time, lest we make many and untimely digressions. And it
is enough only to clarify the thing said from the psalm somehow thus. For so
long as I was crying out hymning God, and through the whole day theologizing,
nothing of mine was growing old, but as much I was being renewed. But when,
having been idle from speaking the things of theology, I was silent, then
oldness sank in as far as my bones, and the things that seemed firmest in me.
And to these followed that your hand became heavy night and day against me,
and because of this from the former visitation your hand that was being made
heavy against me was turned to my misery. And worthily I have suffered these
things, a thorn having been fixed in me, when I was held by cares, or even by
wealth, and pleasures of life. And that some such things have been thought by
the Prophet in these is clear from this being brought on upon them, I made
known my sin, and the things that follow.

After these things one must see what are the sayings of the wise; then what
the riddles; of what wise men the sayings, the wise man who has heard these,
and through having heard them having become wiser than himself. Is it then
only of those wise according to God and his truth, or not of these only, but
also of those of whose craftiness God will catch hold? For the Word has named
these also wise. For why shall the one spoken of understand the sayings of the
blessed wise, but not suffice also toward understanding the words of the
remaining wise, but his mind be less than the mind of the wise of this world,
and not able to follow the things said by them? And perhaps just as it is a
work of the eye not only to see the things that are in order, but also the
things that are otherwise, or as the art in these things wishes; so it is a
work of mind to contemplate both the thoughts that succeed, and those that do
not, if somehow he himself will not be captured by the persuasiveness in
falsehoods, and will rescue those who have been captured, if he understand
also the sayings of the other wise, if anywhere they deal in sophistry, and in
what manner they reason falsely; and how the sophism is solved, and the false
reasoning is refuted. For thus also through his own wise God threatens that he
will set aside the wisdom of the wise that is worthy to perish because of the
falsehood, and the deceitful understanding of those not intelligent according
to him, and thus through his own he will catch hold of the craftiness of the
wise of this age. At once the disciples of the Lord, if they did not
understand the sayings of the =13.25= worldly wise, how were they warring
toward destruction of strongholds, destroying reasonings and every height
lifting itself up against the knowledge of God? And how could one also keep
perfectly the thing said by Peter, Be ready always toward a defense to
everyone who asks you a word concerning the hope in you, not having perfectly
the preparation in word, that he may be ready toward a defense concerning the
hope in him to every Greek and every barbarian, and every wise man, and every
unintelligent, by understanding the sayings of the wise, accusing some,
overturning some, and establishing and proving these? And do not wonder if now
the one truly wise according to God is not found; for most of the exceptional
gifts have failed, so as to be found either not at all or rarely.

It remains to render also what the riddles are. And I think a riddle to be
some narration concerning things as having happened that have not happened,
nor are able to happen, but signifying in concealment some secret thing; as
for example from the Judges: Going they went, the trees, to anoint a king over
themselves. For what three fruit-bearing trees, the fig, the vine, the olive,
not wishing to rule the trees not worthy of their kingdom; and what the thorny
bramble, sending forth fire from itself to devour the cedars of Lebanon, is
for the wise man to see, referring these to the Father, and the Son, and the
Holy Spirit, but the bramble to the adversary. And there is also in the
Kingdoms such a riddle: The thistle that was in Lebanon sent to the Cedar that
was in Lebanon, saying: Give your daughter to my son for a wife, and the
things that follow. And you would also find riddles in the prophets, as in
Ezekiel the, The great eagle, the great-winged, the long in extension, full of
plumage, and the things that follow. So much we have been able to have room for
at present toward the matter at hand; and these were parable and dark saying,
both sayings of wise men and riddles. A parable is a word as concerning
something that has happened, which has not happened according to the letter,
but is able to happen, tropically indicative of riddles, as the, The sower
went out to sow; for it has not happened according to the wording, even if it
is referred to other things. And how would the, I will open my mouth in
parables, fit this? For that which he added has happened: Before their fathers
he did wonders. And according to the letter he broke through the sea, and led
them through; for all things have happened, even if they are symbols of the
tropes of evangelical parables toward which they were referred, being taken.
But another will say that the, I will open my mouth in parables, is not
referred to the things from the Exodus, but to the parables in the Gospel of
Christ saying in the prophet: Attend, my people, to my law, and the things
upon it.

A crown of graces. The compass of the virtues, because of their being bound to
one another. =13.28= A golden collar about your neck. He calls a golden collar
the surrounding radiance and the circular brightness of the divine oracles.
And he calls neck the mind. Let us take his memory from the earth. And those
who said, This is the heir; come, let us kill him, that the inheritance may
become ours, would also say these things. And cast your lot among us. Having
departed from that polity, become with us, sabbatize with us, do the
unleavened with us, and the other observances of the law. For they themselves
are those who share in murder. For God is a giver of goods; but we are causes
of evils, namely of things that work evil. And you would say these things also
concerning the Jews who shared in blood, both of the prophets and of Christ
himself. These are the ways of all who accomplish the lawless things. To
accomplish I think is not simply to do something, but to fill up the measure
of their fathers, and to be liable to every blood from Abel until Zachariah.
Wisdom is hymned in goings-forth. Being silent by a wise man, she is not
hymned; but having obtained a going-forth, she is hymned. For it shall be when
you call upon me. For as Lord we call upon the wisdom who is Christ. He
therefore who calls upon Christ, if he understands who he is, calls upon
wisdom, understanding, sanctification, righteousness, and every virtue; even
if not with lips, yet with deeds. But let us not become such as to call upon
him, and him not to hearken.

CH. 2. If, having received the saying of my commandment, you hide it with
yourself. And one hides it, not doing it upon vainglory, but striving to
escape notice. Then you shall understand fear of the Lord. And see after how
many things we shall understand fear of the Lord; for it too is unclear, as is
evident from the simpler ones; for one can see some fearing the Lord not in
the things in which they ought, and not fearing in the things in which they
should. And give your voice to understanding. And each gives his own voice to
something, either to anger perhaps speaking its [voice], or to grief, or to
fornication. But the blessed man offers his whole voice to the Lord, so that
whatever he may speak may be wisdom. As another [seeks] the silver, so seek
you wisdom, becoming as it were a lover of money in this. Let not an evil
counsel overtake you. Those alien to the Church promise one set of things at
the beginnings, and another according to the ends; for they withdraw from
idolatry from the beginning, and bring [people] to the Creator; then changing,
they set aside the Old Scripture, oppos =13.29= ing the elementary youth. But
with the ecclesiastical teachers the ends are in harmony with the elements at
the beginning.

CH. 3. And let your heart keep my words. After the preceptive and prohibitive
[commands] of the law of God, the words of the wisdom of God announce the
unlying knowledge of the things that are. Such also is the, For out of Zion
shall go forth the law, and a word of the Lord out of Jerusalem. For he who
keeps the laws will take up the wisdom. For sow, he says, for yourselves
righteousness; reap into a season of life. Then next upon the practical is
brought on: Light for yourselves a light of knowledge. Provide beautiful
things before the Lord and men. If you do what is practiced in wisdom, you
shall be a son of God, as has been said: You made all things in wisdom. Honor
the Lord from your just labors. The name of honor the divine Scripture
customarily takes for giving; For honor, he says, widows, those who are truly
widows, that is, Give a share, so that he might not humble the widow, not
having said alms. And the Savior also says: It has been written in the law:
Honor your father and your mother; and the things that follow. And he said
from just labors, that it may not be from seizure and greed. And let no one
hesitate to give; for he will not have a defense after the widow in the holy
Gospel who cast her whole living, and was justified, not beside those who cast
from seizure, but beside those who cast from abundance. And all things are a
giving toward God, works of temperance and of the remaining virtues. For
one must also give firstfruits from every offspring to the Church and to the
poor; and rising from sleep one must give as firstfruit to God the prayer; and
it is fitting to give a firstfruit of the words, psalms. For thus it shall be
said to you: And blessed your storehouses, and your remnants; and he means the
things of the soul. God by wisdom founded the earth. But also the faithful
soul is earth, bearing fruit a hundredfold. And this from the fatness, which
is wisdom, becomes wise, God founding it upon her. For he settles for her the
introduction of the teaching, making a fixing from wisdom, and always
advancing he works her into a heaven, a most pure creature, a dwelling-place
of both angels and intelligent essences. Having taken perception of that which
is, and contemplating the precision of the things, she begins to divide and
dissect them.

CH. 5. Make your way far from her. He calls way the mind journeying toward
virtue; or he also commands to separate virtue from vice.

CH. ʹ. But a woman hunts precious souls of men. And the =13.32= soul hunted by
a woman is precious by nature; for it is not earthy by nature. Or since the
mind and the soul of the manly are precious, nevertheless she hunts even
these.

CH. 8. I was she in whom he rejoiced. The, I was, upon God does not signify
time; for it is temporal, whenever it signifies something of the things under
time, as the, I was in the marketplace. But if she was with God, fitting the
beginningless, she also is everlasting.

CH. 10. She enriches. If every one blessed by the Lord is rich, and those who
walk in the law of the Lord are blessed by the Lord, and prophets walked in
the law of the Lord, these then were rich. In laughter a fool does evil
things. Doing the evil things he is pleased and rejoices. But wisdom bears
prudence to a man, a practical virtue.

CH. 11. He who does not go about with his own house shall inherit winds. The
house is our God in whom we move and are. He therefore who does not walk in
the commandments of God shall be co-heir of the opposing powers. From fruit of
righteousness a tree of life grows. Fruit of righteousness is the end of
virtue. One has as many trees of life as fruits of righteousness. This tree of
life is that planted in the midst of paradise, which Adam is prevented from
touching after the sin, having cast away the seeds of righteousness, from
which the tree of life grows.

CH. 12. He shall be a friend of himself. Origen does not mention this verse at
all; but it was found in some copies; for it lies neither with the Seventy,
nor with others.

CH. I ʹ. Wrath of a king is an angel of death; but a wise man will propitiate
it, if indeed the holy one is passionless; or the wrath must be allegorized.

CH. 25. But in places of the ungodly men are caught. And their places are also
the assemblies of the heretics.

CH. 30. But three things are impossible for me to understand. Impossible is a
theorem being introduced into scientific understanding, and impossible for a
man to contemplate the things above man and to understand, for example the
healing of the crab, even if angels know this; and it is impossible for
something to be understood that in no way exists, nor can come to be, for
example that three is four. It seems then to have been said concerning the
things that do not exist. For an eagle not having left tracks in flying about
the air, analogous to the tracks left =13.33= behind in the sandy earth of
those who have stepped on it, it is impossible to be understood; so also upon
a snake: in easily-yielding earth it is possible for ways to be understood,
but on the rock not. And paths of a ship going through the sea. Perhaps in
these things one must call ship Jesus and his disciples, and sometimes them
alone; not going through the sea; For immediately the boat came to be upon the
land to which they were going, by divine power. But a ship not carrying Jesus
goes through the sea, and this continually, being unstable under the opposing
winds. And since the affairs of life are vanity, of those always going through
the sea, and of no man advancing into everlasting life, it is not possible to
understand their paths. Therefore of the glorious kings of the earth or even
of rulers, no work alien to piety is left after death. For their glory has
been quenched. And they are not recognized, he says, nor ways of a man in
youth, inflamed with the desires because of the instability of manners; and of
the adulteress, likewise because of the shamelessness. Or this is the Church
of the nations, which after having washed through baptism says she has done
nothing out of place. But the just man is not in youth, since, having been
perfected in a little, he fulfilled long times. Such also is every adulterous
woman, or a soul that has alienated herself from the bridegroom, and leaving
the husband, and going after the lovers.

And she goes out to war from one command in good order. As well-ordered the
locust also is praised, obeying the command of the word of God. Such is the
Church, because of being despised by men, compared to a locust as regards
living upon earth; but because of gathering with Jesus and not scattering, but
putting on the panoply of God, that she may stand against the wiles of the
devil, encamping in good order from one command; and she is unkinged by
Pharaoh, but not by God, and the labors of the Egyptians are delivered to her.
Perhaps because of our not being ruled before the kingdom of Christ, the
locust is without a king. For they do not have a leader, as the bees, but each
leads. These are those who by choice are lightened of the earthly things,
journeying an ordered life. Or also locusts, as many as, wishing to fly by the
mind, being unable, run up a little from the earth. But if they should partake
of the practical, they will be led into one practical order. And a he-goat
leading a flock of goats. Third the he-goat according to the Seventy alone
leading a flock of goats, both increasing and multiplying it and leading it to
the pastures on high. For no herd of animals so treads the high places. Such
is the one who leads, raising those in the goat-flock of Christ toward height.
Otherwise the he-goat is a type of Christ, being offered for sin. To this one
being on high follows all the multitude of the godly.
