* Encomium on Saint John Chrysostom

ΙΑʹ. Encomium of our father among the saints John of Damascus on Saint John
Chrysostom.
αʹ. It was needful, O all-golden John, that those attempting to take hold of
your =96.764= encomia, being well endowed with a golden tongue, should put
forth a gold-flowing word. And besides these things your own voice ought to
have been present; for it alone would aim worthily at its own praise, and this
especially now. For you who lived among us on earth before your departure,
forgetfulness of the things achieved was in first speeches an accuser of
itself. For somehow the wise conceal the things of their own achievements,
lest being should slip away into seeming; but since even the stammerings of
children are dear to fathers, and two mites are more beloved to God than the
achievements of the delicate; for he is accustomed to judge these things not
by power but rather by intention; and besides, the exhortation of a God-loving
man is not to be thrust aside; for he is worthy of reverence and is owed very
many thanks from us; I do take hold of the words; yet not wantonly, nor
without restraint, but shrinking with fear, and I offer you the first-fruits
of your own divine and holy teachings. For it will be for me, unfolding such a
subject, if I have come near to worthiness, no ordinary praise; but if I fall
short of it, which it is best not to suffer, yet impossible, pardon for having
yielded to a just defeat; and grant me the fire-breathing grace of the Spirit.
For you yourself also were named a mouth of Christ, bringing forth the worthy
from the unworthy, not one or two somewhere; for this perhaps even one of the
many might do; but whole houses, and peoples, and cities.
βʹ. For beginning from yourself, and having truly made yourself Jerusalem, a
city of the living God for a dwelling-place of the divine Spirit, you went
with the word to the ends of the earth on every side, of the East and of the
Western lot, of the North and of the South, so that upon you most aptly was
spoken the divine fore-utterance of the inspired David: Their sound went out
into all the earth, and their words to the ends of the inhabited world. For
you became a disciple of the very Wisdom itself of Christ, the enhypostatic
power of God and Father, and having tracked him as far as power allowed, and
having become an imitator of God as far as is possible for a man. For you
built, as far as the Gades that are the limits of nature, every form of virtue
upon the deepest foundation of humility, through which alone God was well
pleased to save, and man is saved. For there is no more secure foundation than
this. And that humility is from faith is clear to everyone, I suppose. For how
would anyone submit to the Better, unless he believed that he is better and
most high?
γʹ. And you had become full of words as a servant of the Word, through which
you proclaimed the divine Word, the enhypostatic of God and Father,
consubstantial with the Father through the Spirit, to know clearly a monad
worshipped in Trinity, and a Trinity summed up into a monad, bearing a certain
paradoxical union =96.765= and division. Neither is the unity confused, nor is
the threeness divided at a single stroke; but each is preserved in the other,
in the union of the essence of the hypostases the unity and the
unalterableness of the nature. These things you taught the whole inhabited
world, one Godhead in three hypostases that wholly shared itself with us in
one of its hypostases; and this is the


Son of God and Word, the impassible being united to a passible nature, and as
it were even coming to subsist in these things, and beginning to be, both
being incarnate and becoming dense, and the one simple in nature being
composed by the assumption of a nature, and truly known as of two natures, as
bearing two natures after the union, from which there is one, which things are
proclaimed; each having perfectly according to its own definition and account,
the one without beginning and uncreated, the other begun and created, the one
impassible, invisible, intangible, uncircumscribed, the other passible and
seen, and tangible, and circumscribed; each volitive, and self-determining,
and energetic, himself accomplishing this and that, the one Christ, and Son,
and Lord; the one having ministered organically, the other having operated as
master; each having its own energy and self-determining movement; but the one
himself accomplishing this and that, having worked through both our saving
renewal, in which he compassionately emptied himself.
δʹ. Having learned these things you taught them, and to build upon them the
gold-gleaming and silver-shining beauty of the best works; so that we might
not, drawing near to the testing fire as if we were brushwood, be consumed;
but more and more be purified, being spent as to whatever is counterfeit, and
as pure co-eternal with the pure fire that burns and deifies. Who would give
me a tongue worthy of the praise? Who would set me on the former day, when a
divine fire shone, formed tongue-wise, resting upon each of the apostles in a
unified and many-parted way, so that the one dogma of the faith might be
proclaimed in many-divided tongues, gathering the things standing apart into
one, the many-cleft error having been destroyed, toward which those who of old
built the tower wrongly agreed, and received as the wage of their impiety the
confusing division of the tongue, and through it of the mind? Who would share
with me this tongue of the Spirit, that I might declare the things beyond
nature, the advantages of this Spirit-bearing man? Let an ocean of words come
here, and an abyss of thoughts, but the grace of the Spirit does not yield to
words. For he who wishes to speak the things of the Spirit without the Spirit
chooses to see without light, and has darkness as the guide of his seeing.
Therefore I return again to him who is now being praised, kindling from him as
from a divine lamp the lamp of knowledge, so that he himself may be the
subject of the praise and the supplier of the encomia.
εʹ. Who was so abundant in word and great in understanding, of which the
comparison is incomparable, so that it is unclear in which of the two this man
is more wonderful? Who was so much a type of virtue even when seen, as not to
need a word for instruction? Who poured out snowfalls of words following upon
the works, and taking their power from these, so that one may say also of this
man, of the things which he began both to do and to teach, which the inspired
Luke says concerning Jesus my God, from whom being and well-being have been
allotted? Who went upon both practice and contemplation, having trampled the
pleasures of the flesh as if fleshless, and having considered the divine
things with God? Who orderly set around and subjected the works as members of
a body to faith as to a soul, and animated and made alive the works by faith,
of which the one without the other is useless and unprofitable, even if one
would not miss the mark in giving the precedence to faith? Who so thrust out
gluttony, having enslaved the mistress, and having muzzled her raging with
pious reasoning, becoming her autocrat, not her servant?
ςʹ. And so great was the surplus of continence in him, that it escaped notice
whether, and what, and how much of food and drink was offered to him. For
nature is perishable, and the little flesh is fluid and in motion, and as it
were in need of breath; for to live without respiration is among the
impossibles, and so also the lack of the remaining needs must be filled. For
three things are


emptied: dry, and wet, and breath; of each of which the corresponding
replenishment to constitute this body has been naturally appointed by the
Creator. But the grace of the Spirit does not yield to the limits of nature.
For man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word going out through the
mouth of God. Who was so pure in soul and mind as well as in body, that even a
kind of stupidity toward sexual unions is attested of him? But it was not
truly stupidity; for it is not irrational, nor a weakness of nature, but a
mastery of reason charioteering the irrational, and stretching all desire
toward God, for whom he had also been created, turning away from the
smoothness of pleasure as tinder of fire, and choosing the rough of virtue
that bears toward eternal ease. Thus accustoming himself, advancing from the
small, he became day by day ever more continent than himself, until he put the
passion wholly to sleep, having tamed it by ascetic art and made it subject to
the rein, the habit of custom having driven it in time and step by step into
nature. For toil, having obtained divine help, is accustomed to grant
dispassion.
ζʹ. Who put off love of money before the possessions, and with it the
possessions also, having so longed not to have as another longs to have? The
foundation of the passions, the deprivation of hope, the adversary of faith.
Wherefore also Paul, the God-sounding lyre of the Spirit, the many-voiced
mouth of the apostolic tongues, timely names this a sec =96.769= ond idolatry.
For someone, having let go of fastening the anchor of hope to the divine
providence, leans upon the gathering of money. Setting these things before the
Better, as if he were going to live immortal, and like a sea not being filled,
even if ever so many and greatest rivers of gold flow in, the one now being
praised did not choose these, but having thrust all away, to boast a
fatherland of good fame and conspicuous; I mean that of Antiochus, which has
received the tillers of the East; a race and blood much looked upon, gold,
silver, costly stones, as much of clothing as is soft and renowned, having
yielded to others, and besides also the glory of speech and power.
ηʹ. He goes to Meletius, the president of the Church of the Antiochenes, a man
flourishing with very many divine gifts, resounding in every life and word;
who, having received this one when he was about eighteen years of age, and
having become a lover of the beauty of his heart, as seeing with a foreseeing
eye the outcome of the young man, and having grounded him in the dogmas of
piety, and having sufficiently dignified his character and manner, and having
written beforehand the beauty of the truth, thus through the bath of
regeneration forms in him Christ, the fair one beyond the sons of men, as
shining with the beauty of the Godhead. He was about thirty years of age, and
thus having come at once into the perfection of both bodily and spiritual age,
and having been advanced as both reader and teacher of the divine words, by
the rush of divine love he removes to the desert, wishing to wither the flesh
that was vigorous and bubbling with passions, so that the better might not be
enslaved to the worse. For both desire against each other, and the corruption
of the tent reasonably gives the mastery to the soul; and having consorted
with the neighboring mountains, he is led to a certain old man, Syrian in
dialect, but not a private person in knowledge, philosophizing extreme
continence, and having scoured off this man's hard training in four years, and
having rather easily prevailed over all sweet-suffering, having a word
competing with the toil, longing for obscurity, he became a sojourner of a
certain remote place, boasting a cave as lodging, as a wrestling-ground and
dust-pit of virtue.
There, having undergone how many contests, did he receive in return, according
to the multitude of the pains, the consolation of the Spirit? And how many
ascents having set in his heart did he work by the


Spirit? going from power to power, and through both practice and
contemplation, banishing every Egyptian thought of soul and of body. θʹ.
Another Moses granted to life, he had left the life here of Egypt, and the
things of life, and having become in a manner outside of life, he saw God
shining as in a bush in the roughness of life; and just as the rose from a
thorn, so from the toils of virtue a fragrant plant is accustomed to grow for
a scent of God. Thus loosed from low-lying thoughts, and having put off the
earthly things like sandals in the place of God, he comes to be in his
=96.772= own mind, and sees God as it is possible to see, and again comes into
Egypt, both about to transfer, and about to transfer, many and boundless
multitudes from Egypt and from the bitter tyranny of Pharaoh the world-ruler,
toward the upper land of the promise, through the Red Sea of the divine water
and blood, those who have lived as citizens in the desert of the passions, and
have turned aside Amalek by a stretching of hands raised cross-wise toward the
one who stretched out his hands on the cross for us, and have received from
him the trophy-bearing power. Having then spent a double year in the cave, and
having kept both soul and body sleepless, occupying himself as if fleshless in
the study of the divine oracles, he ostracized all ignorance, having housed
within himself the light of true knowledge.
And if it was necessary even to partake of sleep toward the recovery and
strengthening of nature, still he fulfilled the service of nature, so that for
the double year he was not laid down at all, not by night, not by day. Thus
the things below the belly are put to death, and the powers of the kidneys are
loosened, and the burning of those upon the navel of the belly is quenched
beside, and he becomes without tension toward serving himself. ιʹ. And having
returned again to the fatherland, and having come to be in the rank of
presbyter of the Church, and having paid to this one, as a well-minded child
to the nurse-mother, the costs of rearing, by divine providence he is
transferred to the purple of the cities, and he marries the daughter of the
great high priest. For it is not holy for such a luminary to be hidden under a
bushel, upon whom the timeless and eternal light has rested, but to be set
upon a high and conspicuous lampstand, that as from a distant and most midmost
lookout a trumpet as if gold-wrought might sound around all the ends. Who so
steered and ordered the Church, and displayed a lowly mind, in the high
advantage of the office? Who so charioteered anger and wrath, as to acquire
lawful meekness, abhorring every opposite of virtue, and vindicating the just
from the unjust? Who was so great in love, from which mercy grows? of whose
virtue even until now living pillars bear the tokens.
For every word of his was a subject of mercy, so that those who hearkened to
his words had been driven into a habit of compassion and of sharing. For he
persuaded them to lend their own things to God, giving the corruptible and
flowing to measure back in return the things that stand and are incorruptible,
and to treasure in the heavens the contribution of compassion, which is not
spent. Nor plundered by a thief, to cleanse sins by alms, and injustices in
compassions of the poor; to break bread to the hungry in an evangelical way,
and to give a cup to the thirsty; to wrap the nakedness of the naked, and to
clothe the unclad, and to shelter the roofless under the open sky; to make
visitation of the sick, and to send the foot toward the prison, to harvest
mercy by mercy.
ιαʹ. And who thrust away wrath, and persuaded the flock to thrust it away,
that which shuts the divine bowels of compassions to those who have them? For
as you have done, let it be to you, he says; with the measure you measure, it
shall be measured back. For to the one who has forgiven it shall be forgiven.
And the reverse sequence has been uttered: for it shall not be forgiven to the
one who does not forgive. Who condemned envy, and the evil eye,


having taught also condemnation? To be envied is best; for every good thing is
subject to envy. But the envious thing is self-condemned besides being most
shameful, the most unjust and most vain of all the passions, the adversary of
mercy; if indeed mercy is to be pained at others' evils, but the envious thing
at goods, not for its own sake, but for the one who suffers; and to make the
increase of others' goods an increase of its own wasting, first taking
vengeance on the one who possesses it. And having taught not to judge the
fellow-servant, lest we be judged by just balances of the only judge who
weighs the judgment for us; nor to snatch the dignity of the master. For there
is one judge, who is not accustomed to be judged, as alone free of sin,
mastering, not being mastered. Forgive, he cried in an evangelical way, and
you shall be forgiven.
Who thrust away the grief that leads toward death and taught the grief that is
a procurer of gladness? of which the one, as mourning of sin from God, is
saving; if indeed those skilled in these things have taken sinning to be
missing the mark; but the one that comes upon the deprivation of pleasures,
truly spirit-destroying and utterly without approval. For it is not, it is not
possible for grief to occur, except upon deprivation of desire. Of what sort
then the desire, of such a sort also the grief of missing it; of a good desire
most excellent, but of one otherwise having, otherwise having. And most
excellent of the desires is the one that inclines toward the only most
excellent. And of contrary things the desires also are opposite. And did he
not tone up the slackness of acedia by memory of death? having taught to shake
off that which hamstrings the very power of the soul, and having housed in its
place divine fear, and having urged the people by longing toward prayers, and
that sluggishness be shaken utterly from the souls, and that they keep their
own hearts with all strength, lest death go up upon them as through certain
windows of the senses.
ιβʹ. He taught to search the Scriptures, having become an exegete and
instructor of these, and having searched out with a prophetic spirit the
hidden depths of the Spirit, and having torn the veil of the letter, and
having published the stored beauty; and all these things with an unpuffed and
vainglory-less mind. For he knew, he knew how to trample the empty little
glory, the empty expenditure of toils, that which spoils the crop of the
virtues. For it knows how to bore, as a jar, the soul's receptacle of the
virtues, and taking inflow by the outflow, it sets the receptacle no less
empty, granting profitless sweats, and procuring toil =96.776= without share
of fruit. For he knew that every glory and exaltation befits God, but humility
befits man, becoming a road of exaltation for the best, and the ladders of
Israel, raising a man toward God, and making God a hearth-guest of the heart.
ιγʹ. He humbled himself to all creation on account of the Lord who created,
except only where Christ was being dishonored, and a divine law was being
overlooked and overturned. He refused everything haughty and grim, and
over-browed of eyelid; giving a gentle eye to those who conversed, and a mild
word seasoned with divine salt. The character gracious, and the smile of the
face urbane, not dissolving toward a loose laughter. For knowing that the
vices have been planted beside the virtues, and are somehow next-doored,
obeying the one who commands most excellently, he took heed to himself,
becoming a discerning money-changer; sending away every counterfeit stamp, and
accepting the drachma of the royal image, throwing every form of vice as dust
from his own heart by the rush of the divine Spirit, and being watered and
fattened by the virtues opposite to the vices, and being known as a
fruit-laden olive in the house of God, the Church, ever-green, and bringing to
God a fair fruit, the multitude of those saved through him; or as a certain
palm fenced with thorns of the best rebukes. And


refuting most excellently those who join house to house, and who hurry to
plunder the vineyard of Naboth, as if there were no Elijah to refute. But the
Holy Spirit raised Elijah again, dwelling in the souls of the just. Generation
by generation it raises up prophets, zealots of good works, abhorring whatever
is base and out of place, and housing within and introducing in turn every
lawful and just thing.
ιδʹ. Thus this divine man became a judge of injustice; not judging by this,
far from it; for how? but condemning, and showing this to be rejectable, and
shutting out from the divine precincts those who do the unjust things. For a
widow kept wearing him down, frequently troubling him, her heart worn by the
injustice. She takes hold of this man of God as a certain strong and
indestructible refuge, having deliberated not least securely. For whom else
would she have found to take up her suffering? A ruler? But she who was doing
this insolence ruled the rulers. A king? But by attachment to the member his
judgment was made womanish. For she who was doing the violence was flesh of
his flesh, had been named bone of his. And she who ought to have stopped the
mouths of those who do injustice was hurrying to do injustice.
What are you doing, O lawless little woman? You wear purple, put forward as a
guardian of law, and you trample the law? O the unspeakable wisdom of the one
who said: Do not give the holy things to the dogs, nor cast the pearls before
the swine! For she, having trampled the holy divine law, that is the
much-valued pearl, having turned, tore the poor =96.777= widow, having
plundered this woman's daily livelihood. What are you doing, O insatiable and
ungrateful little woman? having earth and sea bringing gifts, you run down a
destitute widow for the poor possession, whose life it was just for you to buy
in exchange? And confirming the law in word, in deed you make this proclaimed
away? Did you not reverence the father of orphans and judge of widows? But he
who is always fed by the divine words truly heard the God-inspired saying:
Judge the orphan, and justify the widow; and having fire-breathing Elijah as a
guide of zeal, he imitates the boldness of John, saying It is not lawful for
you to have the vineyard of your sister. For do you think you have gone out of
nature, even if you overstepped the dignity by the tyrannical office? For a
rule stamped by law is constituted a kingship, but one founded on lawlessness
is not unjustly named a tyranny. Have you not read the drama of Jezebel? Why
do you enter this divine precinct? Away, far from the divine palaces. The
divine temple is a laver, throwing off the sins. Did not a tax-collector,
having received Jesus as a hearth-guest, scatter well what he had taxed badly?
And you, daughter (for still, you being a member, even if hard to use, I keep
holding you in hope of salvation, but salvation without repentance has been
ranked among the non-existents); give back well what you plundered badly.
ιεʹ. Saying these things, he did not persuade. For she stopped her hearing as
an asp, not heeding the one who was applying the medicine in a God-loving and
wise way. And when he saw the passion altogether incurable, he thrusts out of
the sacred chamber the one not having a wedding garment. Then what? Jezebel
again rises against the prophet, and the herald of the truth flees in no way,
nor is he given over to a guard, as a voice of one crying, but he is condemned
to exile. O the out-of-place mind! Again the charm of the serpent hurries to
exile the man of God from the Church, and it did not miss the desire. For a
little while, the divine glance having long-sufferingly nodded, he is exiled
from the royal city, or rather by the providence of God who triumphed the
manly mind of his own dear servants, and he went about all of God as being of
God in him and with him, being called with Abraham toward a foreign land,
being banished with Moses from the palaces, running off with Elijah toward the
cave


of Horeb, fleeing with Jesus the God into Egypt, so that in the contests the
manliness might be known, and he might reap corresponding prizes. So that in a
manner he might imitate Job, and God will say to him: Or do you think I have
dealt with you otherwise, than that you might be shown just, so that you might
also preach the Gospel to others.
ιςʹ. And if also certain others became helpers of this lawless exile, we honor
them with silence out of reverence for the Fathers, lest we carry off the
=96.780= penalty of Ham, mocking the Fathers. For the narration about this is
of many turns, some of the bishops pouring on the out-of-place thing, and
others denying this altogether, and voting against the empress, who was
threatening the restoration of the Hellenic precincts, already just now having
been taken down, if they should not become of one vote toward the persecution
of the divine man. And let the well-spoken win; for it is not good to compare
Fathers, lest we be set as judges of these by ourselves.
ιζʹ. Whether then thus, or in that way, the president is taken from the
Church, the head of the body, and the flock is widowed of the good shepherd
who in a Christ-imitating way always laid down his own soul for the sheep. And
the Church longed for the herald, the shepherd, the sheep, and the pipe; the
defenders of Christ for the commander, and the trumpet troubling against
intelligible enemies, the widows for the champion; the orphans for the father;
the sick for the one who cares, the strangers for a guide of strangers; the
purple of the cities for the uncounterfeit gold adorning through himself, and
mixing the kingship with the priesthood, and gracing the scepter and the
diadem with the father's bloom; the God-loving ears for the gold-speaking and
gold-mouthed. And if it is not bold to say, Christ went out together, whom he
bore within. O how might I set forth in tragedy the incurable misfortune
without tears?
For not even a stone heart will be able not to weep upon this, since even a
stone struck with a Mosaic rod poured out streams, and a firm heart struck by
the greatness of the tragedy pours out springs of tears river-wise. ιηʹ. But
suddenly the palaces are shattered, and Pharaoh again cries upon the God-seer
for the things toward the warding-off of the scourge; which indeed also
happens. For as soon as John returns by royal command, the shattering ceased.
Thus God knows how to honor those who are his; For I live, says the Lord, but
I will glorify those who glorify me. Again then the flock of Christ is in good
cheer. For he who becomes all things to all is given back to all, and all the
subject of Christ rejoices, and is fattened, and grows in the good earth of
the heart, receiving the words of John as seed of the spirit. But the devil is
wounded, and again as usual rises against the herald, and the powerless one
asks for the manly bulwark of the Church, trying to dissolve, and to plunder
the unsackable wealth; and he receives the authority over the body, as once
over Job, not bearing formerly this man's indignation. And again the just one
is taken up, the bridegroom is ostracized from the chambers, and the Church is
stripped of the bridal adornment, and is shorn of the head, not having this
man. For fire from the throne, as a God-driven thunderbolt, feeds upon the
Church =96.781= together with the senate. And again both wailings and tears, a
multitude desiring to go out together or to dwell in, and death was more
choiceworthy to them than the deprivation of John. But of evils to be fled
prevailing, God conceding, by judgments which he knows.
ιθʹ. For thus again changing, being moved from city from country to country,
having pursued he overtook what he longed for; having reckoned living as good,
and death as gain, having contended the good contest, having kept the faith,
and having finished the course, and


having received the prizes of the contests, the crown of righteousness. But
even having died the beloved one, bride-loving and child-loving, did not leave
his own bride, nor indeed his own children deprived of their proper grace, but
by divine Providence and judgment he is borne correspondingly toward the
palaces, the body toward the things seen, and the spirit toward the unseen;
and he remains a herald of the divine oracles, until Christ shall come again,
with whom he shall be glorified, delighting in his joy and beauty. These, O
Father of Fathers, are my stammerings toward you; but may you watch over us
who are of the same name, and may you give in return intimacy with the
Creator, who is Christ, eternal gladness; to whom be the glory, and the power,
always, now, and ever, and unto the endless ages of ages. Amen.
