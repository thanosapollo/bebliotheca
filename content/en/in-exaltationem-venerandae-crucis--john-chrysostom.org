On the Exaltation of the Precious Cross.
=59.679=
Every change from the worse things to the better works the greatest joy and
gladness for the race of men. For human nature, reaching after the better
things, is naturally always eager for the grasping of what is superior. So
then, sweet to those who sail is the passage from storm to fair weather and
calm; sweet also to travelers is the quiet from toil unto rest; sweet also to
those who grieve is the change from gloom to gladness; sweeter still to the
sick is the recovery from weakness unto health and vigor; sweet also to those
at war is the establishing from enmity into friendship and peace; sweeter
still also is the turning from darkness and night into day. And every action,
so to speak, has a sweeter working, when it produces a benefit for those who
long for it. So then we see also the enjoyment given us by God, furnishing us
gladness by the change.
For wheat is thus sweet; but when it is cast into the earth with toil and
multiplied, it is shown sweeter, being received with all joy as an abundance
of food. For it is one grain in the sowing, thrown into obscurity. So also the
nature of the vine furnishes us a sweeter rest, by the change of the fruit
bringing forth drink from the wood. For tendrils and leaves have a most sharp
and astringent effect, so that it is not even possible for the mouth to touch
them for tasting; but the grape, growing together with tendrils and leaves on
one wood, has so sweet a sensation as to sweeten the condition of bitter and
grievous things, furnishing its own fruit for gladness in the drinking. So
also the stock of the olive has an evergreen leaf, and the tree is always
youthful; but the fruit is borne in season, not appearing from the wood in the
same way as all others, but displaying a certain nature of its own in itself.
For first it is clothed with a blossom like the finest dust, and in the midst
of this it itself springs up like millet; and resembling a cluster it displays
an innumerable multitude, but as it proceeds to growth it is freed from the
multitude as unprofitable, and it is bitter when tasted even to the end, and
without taste when taken by the hands; but becoming through this a guardian of
its own fatness, already by the change of the bitterness it furnishes so sweet
a taste as to be introduced as a seasoning of every luxury, and to become a
mixing for every feast toward the life of men. And so every nature of plants
and of seeds, by the change of the fruits, furnishes gladness to all. Since
then, by the change from the more difficult things upon the better, it grants
so great an enjoyment, come then, having passed over to things more honorable
than these, let us behold of how many goods the cross of Christ has become the
procurer for us, having worked through its own energy so great a change of
goods. For even if the Lord's cross, when heard, is grievous and gloomy, yet
it is full of joy and filled with brightness, having become the cause not of
passion but of impassibility.
And even if it is a stumbling-block to Jews when named, and foolishness also
when proclaimed to the nations, yet to us who believe it is salvation when
remembered. For even if in church, when the cross is read and the passion
through the cross is remembered, the people that believes in the cross is
indignant, sending forth a pitiable voice and a murmuring, it is not because
of the cross, but because of those who crucified and did not believe. For the
cross is the salvation of the Church, the cross the boast of those who have
hoped in it, the cross that which freed us from the evils that went before and
the firstfruits of the goods that have come upon us, the cross the
reconciliation of enemies toward God, and the turning of sinners toward
Christ. For through the cross we were delivered from enmity, and through the
cross we were joined to God in friendship; through the cross we were freed
from the tyranny of the devil, and through the cross we were released from
death and destruction. For this reason the people at first, hearing concerning
the cross, murmured, but then, hearing concerning the resurrection, changed
the murmuring into joy, bringing forth the glory with a pure voice. The cross
changed human nature into an angelic rank, showing it alien to every
corruptible =59.680= practice, and deeming it worthy to dwell in the
incorruptible life.
For he no longer addressed them as men, but even as gods, saying: I said, You
are gods, and all of you sons of the Most High. No longer slaves, but friends
and brothers he named them, saying: I will declare your name to my brothers.
Do you see how great a change the cross has worked? And that you may learn
more exactly the power of the cross, consider what was before the cross, and
what after the cross, and you will find the energy of the cross. Before the
cross the Son was not known; today, the cross being preached, the Son is
named, and the Father is known through the Son; before the cross the devil was
worshipped; now, the cross being preached, the devil has fallen, and demons
are put to flight; before the cross we were occupied with fornications and
wantonness; but now, the cross being preached, we have not only stood away
from fornication, but we have even despised marriages, and having received
virginity, which we did not know, we keep it as our own. The cross was not
being preached, and the devil gathered the Jews against Christ; today the
cross is preached, and the apostles bring Jews near through the faith. The
cross was not being preached, and we were held under death; now the cross is
preached, and as if death were not, we have despised it; and we have longed
for the eternal life. The cross was not being preached, and we were aliens to
paradise; but when the cross appeared, at once a robber was counted worthy of
paradise. O great power of the cross, how great a change it has worked for the
race of men! From so great a darkness it has transferred us into boundless
light, from death it has called us back into eternal life, from corruption it
has renewed us into incorruption. For no longer are the eyes of the heart
covered by the darkness from ignorance, but through the cross they are
illumined with the light of knowledge; no longer are the ears of the deaf
closed by unbelief; for the deaf have heard the word of the Lord, and the
blind have looked up to see the glory of God. These are the achievements of
the cross, these the gifts to us through the cross. For what fair thing has
not been given us from the cross? And what good thing has not been
accomplished for us through the cross?
Through the cross we were taught to be pious, and we came to know the power of
the divine nature; through the cross we are trained in the righteousness of
God, and we pursue the virtue of temperance; through the cross we know one
another, and those who were far off have been joined to Christ, and we have
been counted worthy of the grace of the Holy Spirit; through the cross we have
known the power of love, and we do not refuse to die for one another; through
the cross we have despised all things in the world, and have considered them
to be nothing, reaching after the goods to come, and claiming the unseen as if
they were seen. The cross is preached, and the faith toward God is confessed,
and truth has its citizenship in all the inhabited world; the cross is
preached, and martyrs are shown forth, and the confession toward Christ is
strengthened; the cross is preached, and the resurrection is shown forth, and
life has been manifested, and the kingdom of the heavens is believed, and the
unbelieving believe in the cross, and those who devised the cross against life
obtain salvation. The cross has become the procurer of all these things for
us, and through the cross we have been taught to sing.
What then is more honorable than the cross? And what more profitable than this
to our souls? Let us not then be ashamed, naming the cross, but with all
boldness let us confess it, through which we have been called back into
salvation, and are sent on into eternal life. Do you see how great a
dispensation this cross has worked for the world? It has changed its lawless
practices, and altered the godless doctrines; it no longer has citizenship
under devilish laws, it no longer turns about under death-bearing ordinances.
For God sojourned, and cut out the passions of the maddening sting, legislated
his own things, and set forth the things that are advantageous and profitable;
he ordained doctrines of temperance, and cut out luxury; he set in bounds of
holiness, and fitted a law of chastity, and put lawless fornication out of the
way; he legislated the canons of self-control, and overturned the dominions of
pleasures; by a divine dogma he pulled down the instruments of the desires,
and diminished every =59.681= sin begotten through pleasure.
For since the beginning of fornication is the devising of idols, and the
invention of them is the corruption of life, for this reason he cut out the
root of lawlessness, that he might stop all the streams of impiety. And he
abolished the abominable worships of idolatry, and destroyed the practices of
the unlawful, made useless the rendings of Bacchic lawlessness, and darkened
the fabrications and the adornments of impiety; he cut short the injustices of
deceit, and blocked the streams of destruction; he cleansed the souls, having
rooted out the thorns of impiety, and brought as a grain of wheat the seeds of
godliness, that he might show the souls bearing as fruit the offspring of
righteousness; he opened the floodgates of the spiritual grain-supply, and
filled the rational storehouses of the divine offspring of heavenly wisdom; he
filled those who believe with the sanctifying of the Spirit, and each has
become a vessel of God-inspired holiness, and a river of the divine
comeliness. Of all these things the cross has furnished us the possession, and
of all such great goods we enjoy through the cross. The knowledge of all these
we have received through the cross, and all these things we have been taught
through it. And that you may learn the power of the cross and how much the
energy of the cross is able, learn thoroughly the things that took place upon
the cross, and you will find works of divine power accomplished through it. A
cross is planted against life, and through the cross life has been devised for
the world unto death, and death has been shown dead through it; a cross is
stretched out against the truth, and through the cross the world has been
filled with the truth; a cross is extended against the Master, and the Master,
stretching out his hands through it, has gathered all things to himself.
Christ hangs upon the cross, and the devil has been made dead; Christ is
stretched upon the cross, and a sign of salvation has been given to the world;
Christ is nailed upon the cross, and every soul has been ransomed from bonds;
Christ is fixed upon the cross, and the whole creation has been freed from the
corruption of bondage; Christ has come to rest upon the cross, and a newer
marvel has been shown to the world; for the light of the sun is =59.682=
darkened. A symbol: for the unbelieving, the darkness stored up in the day of
judgment; but for those who believe, the light of day being changed from
darkness. And this a prophetic oracle somewhere also cries, saying: And it
shall be in that day, says the Lord, the sun shall set at midday, and the
light shall grow dark in the day, and I will turn your feasts into mourning,
and all your songs into lamentations. Do you see, beloved, how great a mystery
the prophetic oracle contains?
For here it hints at the affairs of both, I mean of the Jews who are under the
law, and of the nations outside the law who accomplish lawlessness. For those
who feast according to the law will mourn, having completed the feasts, and
instead of songs they will make a beating of the breast concerning Jerusalem.
For Jerusalem will no longer have the worships, nor will a feast be
accomplished in it, all things having received an end after the sojourn of
Christ and his passion and the economy. For this reason then he says: I will
turn your feasts into mourning, and all your songs into lamentations; being
deprived of the things of the law and being delivered into slavery to every
nation, in return for which they did not believe the divine and paradoxical
preaching of the cross. And the nations also, confessing upon their sins, will
mourn a mourning that procures blessedness. For blessed, he says, are those
who mourn, for they shall be comforted. They will therefore mourn over their
vain feasts, and the unlawful songs with which they performed rites to the
unclean demons.
For look with me today, how a Gentile, having repented, who formerly feasted
to the idols, turns the feast into mourning, repenting of the things he did
badly, and says, lamenting, that prophetic oracle: We have wandered in our
shame, and our sins have covered us, because we have been filled with our
impiety. We have known the injustices of our fathers.
So then we also, lamenting well and mourning in the evils written before for
us, let us entwine ourselves with the cross, setting our hopes upon the cross,
that having been trained through the cross, and stretching the mind toward
heaven, having drawn near to Christ our Savior, we may be counted worthy to
become near God in the kingdom of the heavens, in him, Christ our Lord, to
whom be the glory and the power unto the ages of ages. Amen.
