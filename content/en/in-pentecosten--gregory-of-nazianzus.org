ORATION 41.
Of the same, on Pentecost.

1. Let us philosophize briefly about the feast, that we may keep festival
spiritually. For one person has one festival, another another =36.429=
festival; but to the worshipper of the Word, a word, and of words the one most
fitted to the season. And nothing beautiful so delights any lover of beauty as
that the lover of feasts should keep festival spiritually. Let us consider it
thus: The Jew also keeps festival, but according to the letter; for, pursuing
the bodily law, he has not arrived at the spiritual law. The Greek also keeps
festival, but according to the body, and his own gods and daemons; of whom
some are creators of passions according to themselves, and others were honored
out of passions. Therefore their keeping festival too is passionate; that
sinning may be an honor of God, to whom the passion takes refuge as a solemn
boast. We also keep festival, but as it seems good to the Spirit. And it seems
good, either by saying something of the things that ought, or by doing them.
And this is our keeping festival, to treasure up in the soul something of the
things that stand and are held, and not of those that withdraw and are loosed,
and that fawn a little upon the sense, but for the most part both spoil and
harm, at least according to my word. For sufficient unto the body is the evil
thereof. And what need has the flame of more matter, or the beast of more
abundant food, that it may become more ungovernable, and more violent than
reason?

2. For these reasons then one must keep festival spiritually. And the
beginning of the discourse (for we must speak, even if our discourse is a
little more digressive, and we must labor for the lovers of learning, that we
may mix some seasoning, as it were, into the festival): The children of the
Hebrews honor the hebdomad, from the legislation of Moses, as later the
Pythagoreans honor the tetractys, which they have even made an oath; and the
ogdoad and the thirty, those from Simon and Marcion, for whom they even name
certain Aeons equal in number, and honor them. I do not know by what reasons
of analogy, or according to what power of this number, but they honor it. The
evident thing is that in six days God, having both substantiated and formed
the matter, and having ordered it with all kinds of forms and mixtures, and
having made this now visible cosmos, rested on the seventh from the works, as
the name of the Sabbath also shows, meaning rest in Hebrew. And if there is
also some loftier word about these things, let others philosophize. And the
honor among them reaches not in days only, but also into years. That of the
=36.432= days, the Sabbath, shows this which is continuously honored among
them, according to which also the removal of leaven is equal in number; and
that of the years, the seventh year of release. And not in hebdomads only, but
also in hebdomads of hebdomads, likewise both in days and in years. The
hebdomads of days then generate Pentecost, a holy convocation among them; and
those of the years, that named among them the Jubilee, likewise having both a
release of land, and a freedom of slaves, and a reversion of purchased
possessions. For this race consecrates to God firstfruits not of produce only,
nor of firstborn, but already also of days and of years. Thus the number seven
being honored brought in together the honor of Pentecost. For seven being
compounded upon itself generates fifty, one day being short, which we have
taken on from the age to come, being both eighth and first, or rather one and
indissoluble. For the sabbatism of souls here must end there, that a portion
may be given

to the seven, and also to the eight, as already some of those before us have
taken the Solomon saying.

3. And of the honor of seven, the testimonies are many, but a few out of many
will suffice for us. As seven precious Spirits named. For the energies, I
think, of the Spirit, Isaiah loves to call Spirits. And the oracles of the
Lord are purified, according to David, sevenfold. And the righteous, being
delivered six times from necessities, in the seventh is not even struck. And
the sinner is forgiven not seven times only, but also seventy times seven. And
again from the contraries (for the punishment of vice too is praiseworthy),
Cain being avenged seven times, that is, having penalties exacted of the
brother-killing; and Lamech seventy times seven, because he was a murderer
after the law and the condemnation. And the wicked neighbors receiving
sevenfold into their bosom; and the house of Wisdom being supported on seven
pillars; and the stone of Zerubbabel being adorned with so many eyes. And God
being praised seven times a day. And indeed =36.433= also a barren woman
bearing seven, the perfect number, the opposite of her who is incomplete in
children.

4. And if we must also look at the ancient histories, I consider Enoch the
seventh among ancestors, honored by the translation. And I consider Abraham
the twenty-first, glorified with the patriarchate, by addition of a greater
mystery. For the hebdomad being tripled works this number. And one of those
altogether daring might dare even to come to the new Adam, my God and Lord
Jesus Christ, counted seventy-seventh from the old Adam and the one under sin,
according to the genealogy of Luke going backwards. And I consider also the
seven trumpets of Jesus the son of Nave, and so many circuits, and days, and
priests, from which the walls of Jericho are shaken down. And as also the
seventh circuit, and as mystical the threefold breathing of Elijah the
prophet, which breathed life into the son of the widow of Zarephath, and of
the same the equal-numbered flooding upon the pieces of wood, when he consumed
the sacrifice with God-sent fire, and condemned the prophets of shame, who
were not able to do the equal from the challenge. And so also the seventh
looking-out of the cloud commanded to the servant-boy, and Elisha's equal
bending over the child of the Shunammite, which kindled the breath. And of the
same doctrine, I think (to say nothing of the seven-stemmed and seven-lamped
lampstand of the temple), the priest being perfected in seven days, and the
leper being cleansed in seven, and the temple being dedicated in so many. And
in the seventieth year the people being brought back from the captivity, that
what is in units may become this in tens, and the mystery of the hebdomad be
honored in a more perfect number. Why should I speak of things far off? Jesus
himself, the pure perfection, knows to feed in a desert even five thousand
with five loaves, and knows again four thousand with seven. And the leftovers
of the filling, there twelve baskets, here seven hampers; =36.436= neither
without reason, I think, nor unworthy of the Spirit. And you too, collecting
by yourself, would observe many numbers having something deeper than what
appears. And what is most useful for the present season, that according to
these reasons perhaps, or nearest to these, or even some more divine, the
Hebrews honor the Pentecost day, and we also honor it; just as there are also
some other of the Hebrew things, performed typically among them, but restored
mystically for us. Having prefaced so much about the day, let us proceed to
the next of the discourse.

5. We keep Pentecost, and a coming of the Spirit, and an appointed time of
promise, and a fulfillment of hope. And how great the mystery! how great and
venerable! The bodily things of Christ have an end, or rather those of the

bodily sojourn; for I shrink from saying those of the body, until no word
persuades me that it is better to have been stripped of the body. And the
things of the Spirit begin. And what were the things of Christ? Virgin, birth,
manger, swaddling, angels glorifying, shepherds running up, course of a star,
worship and gift-bearing of Magi, Herod's child-murder, Jesus fleeing into
Egypt, returning from Egypt, being circumcised, being baptized, being
witnessed from above, being tempted, being stoned for us, to whom a pattern
had to be given of suffering for the word, being betrayed, being nailed, being
buried, rising, ascending, of which even now he suffers many; from the
Christ-haters, the things of dishonor, and he bears them (for he is
long-suffering); and from the Christ-lovers, the things of honor. And he
defers, as to those the wrath, so to us the kindness; to those perhaps giving
a time of repentance, and of us testing the longing, whether we do not faint
in the afflictions and the contests for piety; as from of old a word of divine
economy, and of his unsearchable judgments, by which he governs our things
wisely. Such then are the things of Christ; and what follows we shall see more
glorious, and may we be seen. And as for the things of the Spirit, let the
Spirit be present with me, and give speech, as much as I also wish; and if not
that much, yet as much as is commensurate with the season. At any rate
=36.437= he will be present as Master, not as slave, nor awaiting a command,
as some think. For he blows where he wills, and upon whom he wills, and when,
and how much. Thus we are inspired both to think and to speak about the
Spirit.

6. Those who bring the Holy Spirit down to a creature are insolent, and wicked
slaves, and worst of the wicked. For it belongs to wicked slaves to set aside
mastery, and to rise against lordship, and to make that which is free a
fellow-slave to themselves. And those who deem him God are God-inspired and
bright in mind. And those who also name him so, if to the well-disposed, are
lofty; if to the lowly, not reserved; entrusting a pearl to clay, and the
sound of thunder to a rotten hearing, and the sun to weaker eyes, and solid
food to those still being given milk; whereas one must lead them little by
little forward, and bring them on by the higher things, granting light to
light, and introducing truth by truth. Therefore we too, leaving aside the
more perfect discourse for now (for it is not yet the time), will converse
with them thus.

7. If you do not confess even that the Holy Spirit is uncreated, O you, nor
timeless, it is clearly the energy of the contrary spirit; for grant to zeal
also to dare a little. But if you are at least so sound as to flee the obvious
impiety, and to set outside of slavery that which also makes us free, consider
what follows yourselves, with the Holy Spirit, and us. For I am persuaded that
you partake of him in some measure, and I will consider together with you
already as with our own. Either give me the mean between slavery and mastery,
that I may set there the dignity of the Spirit; or, fleeing slavery, it is not
unclear where you will rank the thing sought. But you are displeased at the
syllables, and you stumble at the voice, and this becomes for you a stone of
stumbling and a rock of scandal, since Christ also is so to some. The passion
is human. Let us agree with one another spiritually. Let us become lovers of
brothers rather than lovers of self. Grant the power of the Godhead, and we
will give you concession of the voice; confess =36.440= the nature in other
voices, which you reverence more; and we will treat you as weak; even filching
some of the things toward pleasure. For it is shameful, shameful, and
sufficiently irrational, when sound in soul, to be petty about the sound, and
to hide the treasure, as if grudging it to others, or fearing lest you
sanctify even the tongue; but more shameful for us to suffer what we accuse,
and while condemning pettiness, ourselves to be petty about the letters.

8. Confess, O you, the Trinity of one Godhead; or if you will, of one nature;
and we will ask the God-voice for you from the Spirit. For he will give it, I
well know, who gave the first, and the second, and especially if there be some
spiritual cowardice, and not a diabolic persistence, that which fights. Still
more clearly and more briefly let me say: Neither do you call us to account
for the loftier voice (for there is no envy of ascent), nor will we accuse
what is attainable for you so far, until you are also carried by another road
to the same lodging. For we do not seek to conquer, but to take on brothers,
by whose separation we are torn. These things to you, among whom we find also
something vital, who are sound about the Son; whose life we admire, we do not
altogether praise the doctrine; you who have the things of the Spirit, receive
also the Spirit, that you may not only contend, but also lawfully, from which
is also the crown. May this be given you as reward of your way of life, to
confess the Spirit perfectly, and to proclaim with us and before us as much as
is worthy. I dare also something greater on your behalf, to utter the
Apostle's word. So much do I cling to you, and so much do I reverence this
well-ordered robe of yours, and the color of continence, and these sacred
assemblies, and the august virginity and purification, and the all-night
psalmody, and the love of the poor, and of brothers, and of strangers, that I
even accept to be anathema from Christ, and to suffer something as one
condemned; only if you would stand with us, and we would glorify the Trinity
in common. For of the others, what need even to speak, clearly dead (whom it
belongs to Christ alone to raise, who makes the dead live according to his
=36.441= power), who are badly separated in place, bound together in doctrine,
and quarrel with one another as much as squinting eyes, seeing the one thing,
and not by sight but by position being at variance; if indeed one must accuse
them of distortion, and not of blindness. And since I have moderately set
forth the things toward you, come, let us return again to the Spirit; and I
think you too will already follow.

9. The Holy Spirit was always, and is, and will be, neither having begun, nor
about to cease, but always ranged with Father and Son, and numbered with them;
for it was not fitting that ever either Son should be lacking to Father, or
Spirit to Son. For the Godhead would have been inglorious in the greatest
thing, as if having come by afterthought into completion of perfection. He was
always then partaken, not partaking; perfecting, not being perfected; filling,
not being filled; sanctifying, not being sanctified; deifying, not being
deified; always the same with himself, and with those with whom he is ranged;
invisible, timeless, uncontainable, unchangeable, without quality, without
quantity, without form, intangible, self-moving, ever-moving,
self-determining, self-powerful, all-powerful (even if toward the first cause,
as all the things of the Only-begotten, so also those of the Spirit are
referred); life, and life-giving; light, and giver of light; self-good, and
fountain of goodness; Spirit right, princely, Lord, sending, separating,
making a temple for himself, guiding, working as he wills, dividing gifts;
Spirit of adoption, of truth, of wisdom, of understanding, of knowledge, of
piety, of counsel, of might, of fear, of the things enumerated; through whom
the Father is known, and the Son is glorified, and by whom alone he is known,
one ranking, one worship, adoration, power, perfection, sanctification. Why
speak at length? All that the Father has, the Son's, except unbegottenness.
All that the Son has, the Spirit's, except generation. And these do not divide
essence, at least according to my word, but are distinguished around essence.

10. You are in labor with the objections? And I with the course of the
discourse. Honor the day of the Spirit; hold the tongue a little, if possible.
For the discourse is about other tongues; reverence these, or fear them,
=36.444= seen with fire. Today let us dogmatize, tomorrow let us argue
technically; today let us keep festival, tomorrow

let us behave unseemly. These mystically, those theatrically; these for the
churches, those for the marketplaces; these for the sober, those for the
drunk; these of those in earnest, those of those playing against the Spirit.
And since we have put away the alien, come, let us furnish our own.

11. This was at work, first in the angelic and heavenly powers, and as many as
are first after God, and about God. For from nowhere else is their perfection
and illumination, and the difficulty of moving toward vice, or immobility,
except from the Holy Spirit. Then in the Fathers, and in the prophets, of whom
some imagined God, or knew him, and others also foreknew the future, having
the ruling part stamped by the Spirit, and associating with the things to come
as present. For such is the power of the Spirit. Then in the disciples of
Christ (for I forbear to speak of Christ, with whom he was present, not as
working, but as accompanying one of equal honor); and in these threefold,
according as they were able to contain, and according to three times: before
Christ was glorified by the Passion; after being glorified by the
Resurrection; after the Ascent into heaven, or restoration, or whatever one
must say. And the first, that of the diseases, and the cleansing of the
spirits, shows it, having come about not without the Spirit, clearly; and the
breathing after the economy, clearly being a more divine inspiration; and the
present division of the fiery tongues, which we also celebrate. But the first,
dimly; the second, more distinctly; the present, more perfectly, no longer
present in energy, as before, but essentially, as one might say, both
associating and dwelling together. For it was fitting, the Son having
conversed with us bodily, that he too appear bodily; and Christ having
returned to himself, that that one come down to us; coming as Lord, but being
sent as not opposed to God. For such voices no less show the concord, than
they separate natures.

12. Therefore, after Christ, that a Paraclete may not be lacking to us; but
Another, that you may consider the equal honor. For "another" is established
as "another such as =36.445= I". And this is a name of co-lordship, not of
dishonor. For "another" I know to be said not of things alien, but of things
consubstantial. And in tongues, because of kinship with the Word. And fiery, I
inquire whether because of the purification (for our word also knows a
purifying fire, as it is possible to learn from everywhere for those who
wish); or because of the essence. For our God is fire, and a fire consuming
wickedness, even if again you are vexed, being cramped by the consubstantial.
And being divided, because of the difference of gifts; and sitting, because of
the kingly, and the rest among the saints; since also the cherubim are a
throne of God. And in an upper room (unless I seem to someone more
over-curious than is due), because of the ascent of those who would receive,
and the raising from the ground; since also certain upper rooms are roofed
with divine waters, through which God is hymned. And Jesus himself in an upper
room shares the mystery with those being initiated in the higher things, that
that may be shown, that on the one hand God must come down to us, which I know
happened also formerly upon Moses, and on the other we must go up, and thus
there come to be a communion of God with men, the dignity being mixed. But as
long as each remains upon its own, the one of high observation, the other of
lowliness, the goodness is unmixed, and the love of mankind incommunicable;
and a great and impassable gulf in the midst, not only separating the rich man
from Lazarus and from the desired bosom of Abraham, but also the generated and
flowing nature from the ungenerated and standing.

13. This was proclaimed by the prophets, as in, The Spirit of the Lord is upon
me; and, Seven Spirits shall rest upon him; and, The Spirit of the Lord came
down, and led them; and, a Spirit of knowledge filling Bezalel the architect
of the tent; and, a Spirit being provoked; and, a Spirit that took up Elijah
in a chariot, and

was sought from Elisha double; and David being led and established by a good
and princely spirit. And it was promised, by Joel first: And it shall be in
the last days, saying; I will pour out of my Spirit upon all flesh =36.448=
(clearly that which believes), and upon your sons, and upon your daughters,
and the rest; and by Jesus also later, both being glorified and glorifying in
return, as the Father, and by the Father. And the promise, how abundant, to
last through the ages together and remain alongside, whether now with those
worthy according to season, or later with those counted worthy of the things
there, when we keep it entire by our way of life, and do not cast away so much
as we sin.

14. This Spirit co-creates with the Son both the creation and the
resurrection. And let this persuade you: By the Word of the Lord the heavens
were made firm, and by the Spirit of his mouth all their power; and a divine
Spirit that made me; and a Breath of the Almighty that teaches me; and again:
You will send forth your Spirit, and they will be created, and you will renew
the face of the earth. And he creates the spiritual regeneration; and let this
persuade you: that no one can see or receive the kingdom, who has not been
born from above by Spirit, and been cleansed of the former birth, which is a
mystery of night, by a daytime and luminous fashioning, which each fashions
for himself. This Spirit (for he is most wise and most loving of man), if he
takes a shepherd, makes him a psalmist, charming evil spirits, and shows him
king of Israel. If a goatherd scraping sycamore fruit, he makes him a prophet.
Think of David and Amos. If he takes a well-natured youth, he makes him a
judge of elders and beyond age. Daniel bears witness, who conquered lions in a
den. If he finds fishermen, he nets them for Christ, catching a whole world by
the mesh of the word. Take for me Peter, and Andrew, and the sons of thunder,
who thundered the spiritual things. If tax-collectors, he gains them unto
discipleship, and creates merchants of souls. Matthew is witness, yesterday a
tax-collector, and today an evangelist. If hot persecutors, he transfers the
zeal, and makes Pauls instead of Sauls, and as much unto piety as he found
unto vice. This is also a Spirit of meekness; and he is provoked at those who
sin. Therefore let us try him as meek, and not wrathful, confessing the
dignity, and fleeing the blasphemous, and let us not wish to see him angry
unforgivably. This also makes me today a bold herald to you; if then =36.449=
I am to suffer nothing, thanks to God; and if I am to suffer, even so thanks;
the one, that he may spare those who hate us; the other, that he may sanctify
us, having received this reward of the ministry of the Gospel, to be perfected
through blood.

15. They were speaking then in foreign tongues, and not ancestral, and the
wonder was great, a word being spoken by those who had not learned; and the
sign to the unbelievers, not to those who believe, that it may be an accuser
of the unbelievers, as it is written: That in other tongues, and in other
lips, I will speak to this people, and not even so will they listen to me,
says the Lord. But they heard. Hold a little here, and be at a loss, how you
will divide the word. For the expression has something ambiguous, being
divided by the stop. Was it that they heard in their own dialects each, so
that, say, one voice was sounded, but many were heard, the air being struck
thus, and, that I may speak more clearly, the voice becoming voices; or is
They heard to be paused, and speaking in their own voices to be added to what
follows, so that it is, speaking in voices, those own to the hearers, which
happens to be foreign; which I also rather set down. For in that way the
wonder would belong rather to the hearers than to the speakers; but thus to
the speakers; who are also accused of drunkenness, clearly as themselves
working wonders about the voices by the Spirit.

16. Yet even the old division of voices is praiseworthy (when those who were
of one voice badly and godlessly were building the tower, as also some of
those now dare); for the like-mindedness, being dissolved together with the
standing-apart of the voice, loosed the undertaking; but more worthy of praise
is that now being worked as a wonder. For having been poured from one Spirit
into many, it is gathered again into one harmony. And there is a difference of
gifts, needing another gift, toward discernment of the better; since all have
the praiseworthy. And that too would be called good, about which David says:
Drown, O Lord, and divide =36.452= their tongues. Why? Because they loved all
the words of drowning, a deceitful tongue; all but openly accusing the tongues
here, which cut the Godhead. These things then to this extent.

17. And since to those dwelling in Jerusalem, most devout Jews, Parthians, and
Medes, and Elamites, Egyptians, and Libyans, Cretans and Arabians,
Mesopotamians and my own Cappadocians, the tongues were speaking, and to the
Jews from every nation under heaven (if anyone likes to understand it so),
gathered there, it is worth seeing who these were, and of what captivity. For
that into Egypt and Babylon was circumscribed, and long ago had been loosed by
the return. And that under the Romans had not yet happened, but was about to,
being an exacting of the audacity against the Savior. It remains then to take
this as that under Antiochus, not much older than these times. But if someone
does not accept this explanation, as over-curious (for neither was the
captivity ancient, nor spread over much of the inhabited world), and seeks the
more persuasive, perhaps it is better to take that, that often, and under
more, the nation having migrated, as is related by Esdras, some of the tribes
were recovered, and some were left behind; of whom, having been scattered into
more nations, it is likely some were present then, and shared the wonder.

18. And these things have been examined beforehand for the lovers of learning,
perhaps not over-curiously. And whatever else anyone contributes to the
present day, he will have gathered this for us too. But for us the gathering
is already to be dissolved (for the discourse is sufficient), but the festival
never. But we must keep festival, now also bodily, but a little later wholly
spiritually; where we shall also know the reasons of these things more purely
and more clearly, in the Word himself, and God, and our Lord Jesus Christ, the
true feast of those being saved, and rejoicing; with whom the glory and the
reverence to the Father, with the Holy Spirit, now and unto the ages of ages.
Amen.
