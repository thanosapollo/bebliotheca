Prologue of the praises of Constantine.
Pro.1 But I do not come inventing myths, nor snares for the hearing, nor a
fluency of words, to charm ears with a Sirens' voice, nor with golden cups,
with fair flowers of phrases dressed in ornate speech, do I offer drugs of
pleasure to the friends of these things; but obeying the wise I bid men flee
and turn aside from the highways, and I charge them not to be crowded together
with the multitude. Pro.2 I am come, then, to begin among you a newer song of
royal hymns. And though countless others hurry along the same road as I,
shunning the beaten track of men I will walk the untrodden way, which it is
not lawful to tread with unwashed feet. The vulgar, pursuing discourses worn
by the sophisms of youths and greeting a pleasant and popular muse, may charm
mortal ears with mortal tales, having given the judgment over to pleasure; but
the initiates of universal wisdom, being possessed of the knowledge of things
divine and human, setting the choice of the better in the portion of the good,
have preferred and chosen the emperor's god-befitting virtues and god-loving
deeds above the human, leaving to second voices the hymn of the second rank of
goods. Pro.3 For since there is a wisdom of things divine and of things human
even in the emperor's soul, and some of it stretches toward God and some
toward men, let those who stand outside the sacred precincts, the ones fitted
for it, minister the human things, though these too are solemn and
extraordinary and useful for life; for all the emperor's things are fair, yet
they still fall short of the more divine. Pro.4 But let those who have passed
inside holy palaces, into inner shrines and untrodden recesses, shutting the
doors against profane ears, recount the emperor's secret initiations to their
initiates alone; and let those who have cleansed their ears with streams of
piety, and have set the intellect of the soul itself on a soaring wing, dance
around the King of all, celebrating the divine things in silence. Pro.5 And
let oracles of sacred words, uttered not from divination (or rather from
frenzied madness) but by inspirations of divine light, be our teachers of the
rites concerning kingship itself and concerning the Highest King, and
concerning the divine escort around the King of all, and concerning the
imperial pattern among us and the one whose stamp has been counterfeited, and
concerning those who attend upon either rank. Initiated into the god-befitting
rites by these, we shall in this way lay hold of the divine mysteries.

1.1 This is a festival of a great emperor. Let us rejoice in it, we imperial
children, hallowing it with the teachings of sacred words, and the great
Emperor leads the feast for us. And I call great Emperor the one who is truly
great; and I say that this is (the emperor present will not take it amiss, but
will even join in the acclamation of the theology) the One beyond all things,
the Highest of all, the Supreme, the Exceedingly Great, whose thrones of
kingship are the heavenly vaults, and the earth the footstool of his feet. ..
if anyone could worthily conceive him, a light flashing about him with
unspeakable glitterings of rays bars every one from the vision of his deity.
1.2 Heavenly armies go about him, and supercosmic powers escort him,
recognizing their own Master and Lord and King, and infinite multitudes of
angels and companies of archangels and choirs of holy spirits are illumined,
drawing from the glitterings about him as from everlasting fountains of light.
And all lights and the divine and intellectual kinds of bodiless lights,
having been allotted the region beyond heaven, honor the great King with the
highest and god-befitting

hymns, and in the midst a great heaven is thrown about as a dark-blue curtain,
dividing those outside from those within the royal houses. And around this, as
in the forecourts of palaces, sun and moon and the light-bearers in heaven go
about bearing torches, honoring the King beyond all things and by his nod and
word kindling unquenchable lamps of light for those allotted the dark region
outside heaven. 1.3 This great King, Christ, our own emperor, glorious in
victory, also hymns, having perceived very well the cause of his kingship;
god-loving Caesars recognize him as the fountain of all goods, having received
the lesson from their father; hosts of armies, peoples in full multitude
throughout countries and cities, and rulers of nations assembling, revere him,
being trained under a great Savior as teacher; and the whole race of men
together, nations of every kind, tribes and tongues, all in common and each in
part, though they differ in their opinions on other things, agree in this
confession alone, invoking the one and only God with inborn reasonings and
with self-taught and self-instructed thoughts. 1.4 What then? Does not the
whole element of earth also recognize him as master, displaying by the shoots
and living things it yields up its submission to the nod of the Better? Him
also the streams of rivers in flood with their waters, and the flowings of
springs from everlasting depths of unbounded abysses, gushing without cease,
ascribe as the cause of the unspeakable wonder-working; him also the abysses
of the sea, shut in boundless deeps, and the swelling waves, raised aloft on
high and threatening the earth with fear, advancing as far as the very shores,
cower, bound by the nod of a divine law; him also the measured streams of
winter rains, the crashes of thunders and the flashes of lightnings, the
back-turning blasts of winds, and the journeys of clouds through the air make
manifest to those unable to see his vision with their own eyes. 1.5 But the
all-shining sun as well, running the long age as a racecourse, knows him alone
as Lord, and serving his nod never dares to step outside its bounds; and the
moon, yielding its brightness to the sun and waning and waxing again in cycles
of times, is subjected to divine ordinances; and the beauties of heaven,
glittering in the dances of the stars and moving with order and harmony and
measuring out their own circuits, proclaim the Giver of lights of every kind;
and all the heavenly luminaries together, having composed one melody by his
nod and word, accomplishing a long course in cycles of long ages, ride through
the contests of ethereal stadia; and the alternating motions of nights and
days, the changes of seasons and times, the rhythms and orders of the
universe, honor the many-colored wisdom of the immeasurably great power. To
him also invisible powers, flying about the open plains through the air, send
up the theology due and fitting; this great King the whole cosmos itself hymns
together; him the heavens from above and the choirs higher than the heavenly
vaults honor; armies of angels hymn him with unspeakable hymns, and spirits
that are offspring of intellectual light speak theology of their own Begetter;
him the timeless ages before this heaven and before the world, and other
unbounded ages of ages before all the subsistence of things seen, ascribe as
sole and great Master and Lord. 1.6 Him also, above all and before all and
after all, his pre-existing only-begotten Word, the great High Priest of the
great God, eldest of all time and of all ages, consecrated first and alone in
the honor of the Father, propitiates for the salvation of all, being glorified
with the first place of the rule of all things and with the second place of
the paternal kingship, because he himself was the light beyond all things,
dancing about the Father and mediating and dividing from the substance of
things generated the unoriginate and

unbegotten idea, which, welling up from above out of an unceasing and
unoriginate deity, goes forth outside, illuminating the supercelestial region
and all things inside heaven with rays of wisdom better than those of the sun.
And he would be the leader of this whole cosmos, the Word of God who goes
through all things and in all things, both seen and unseen, from whom and
through whom the emperor dear to God, bearing the image of the highest
kingship, steering the helm of all on earth in imitation of the Better,
directs them. 2.1 The only-begotten Word of God, reigning together with his
Father from unoriginate ages, lasts through unbounded and endless ages; but
the one dear to him, supplied by the royal outpourings from above and
strengthened by the title of the divine appellation, rules those on earth
through long cycles of years. 2.2 Then the Savior of all things prepares the
whole heaven and cosmos and the highest kingship as fair for his Father; but
the one dear to him, bringing those under his hand on earth to the
only-begotten and saving Word himself, makes them fit for his kingship. 2.3
And the common Savior of all, by an invisible and divine power, like a good
shepherd, sets far off the apostate powers, as many as ever flying about this
air above the earth used to fasten on the souls of men, like wild beasts from
his own flocks; but the one dear to him, equipped from above by him with
trophies against enemies, chastens the visible enemies of the truth, subduing
them by the law of war. 2.4 And the Word, being before the world and Savior of
all, handing over rational and saving seeds to his own devotees, makes them at
once rational and knowing of the Father's kingship; but the one dear to him,
like some interpreter of the Word of God, calls every human race up to the
knowledge of the Better, crying into the ears of all and with a great voice
proclaiming to all on earth the laws of true piety. 2.5 And the Savior of all
throws open the heavenly gates of the Father's kingship to those who are
transferred from here to there; but he, in zeal for the Better, having
cleansed all the filth of godless error from the kingship on earth, invites
choirs of holy and pious men inside the royal houses, taking thought to save
complete, crew and all, the whole fleet of those steered under him. And this
festival this man alone of all who have ever led the kingship of the Romans,
already honored by God the All-Sovereign with three cycles of decades, does
not celebrate for earthly spirits after the manner of the ancients, nor for
apparitions of people-deceiving demons, nor for the deceits and babblings of
godless men, but he renders the thank-offerings to the very One who has
honored him, having perceived the goods supplied to him, not defiling the
royal houses with bloods and gore after the manner of the ancients, nor
propitiating earthly demons with smoke and fire and sacrifices of whole burnt
animals, but dedicating to him the sacrifice dear and gracious to the King of
all himself, namely his own imperial soul and his most god-befitting mind. For
this is the only sacrifice acceptable to him, which our emperor has been
taught to offer well with fireless and bloodless reasonings purified in
thought, confirming the things of piety with undeceiving doctrines of soul,
honoring the theology with magnificent speech, imitating the philanthropy of
the Better in imperial deeds, wholly devoted to him and setting himself as a
great gift, the first-fruits of the world entrusted to him; this greatest
victim the emperor offers well before all things, and he sacrifices as a good
shepherd, not sacrificing famous hecatombs of first-born lambs, but bringing
to his knowledge and piety the souls of the rational flocks shepherded under
him. 3.1 And he who

rejoices in such a victim and gladly welcomes the gift, admiring the
hierophant of the solemn and fair sacrifice, adds for him additions of long
cycles of the kingship, increasing the benefactions in return for the holy
things toward him, and grants him to complete festivals of every kind with
much ease of the monarchy, at each cycle of a ten-year festival appointing one
of his sons to a share of the imperial throne and as it were bestowing growths
of years on a thriving and vigorous plant. 3.2 First, at the first decade of
the imperial cycle, he declared the one named for the father a partner of the
imperial lot; second, at the second decade, the one next in time; the third
likewise at the third decade of the present feast. And already a fourth cycle
is turning, and as the times stretch into length, increasing the kingship by a
generous sharing of the family and by declarations of Caesars, he fulfills
oracles of divine prophets, which long ago and very long ago cried out in this
way: "And the saints of the Most High shall take the kingdom." 3.3 Thus God
himself the All-Sovereign, bestowing on the emperor most dear to God growths
of times together with sons, makes the hegemony over the nations on earth
flourish for him and young, as if just beginning to grow, and himself
completes the festival for him, making him victor over all enemies and foes
and showing him to all on earth as a pattern of true piety. 3.4 And he, as
with glitterings of sunlight, by the shinings of the Caesars illuminates those
settled farthest off in place with the rays sent a long way from him; here for
us who have been allotted the east he distributed his worthy fruit, and the
other of the sons to the other kind of men, and again another elsewhere, like
lamps and luminaries of the lights poured from him; then having yoked four
under one yoke of an imperial four-horse chariot, himself yoking to himself
the most manly Caesars as if some colts, and fitting them with reins of divine
concord and unanimity, he drives from above, charioteering on high, riding
through the whole that the sun looks upon, himself present to all and
surveying all things. 3.5 And then, adorned with the image of the heavenly
kingship, looking up according to the archetypal idea he directs those below
as he steers, strengthened by the imitation of monarchic power; for this the
King of all has granted to the nature of man alone of those on earth. For this
is the law of imperial authority, which defines the one rule over all. 3.6 And
monarchy lies above the constitution and administration of all things; for
polyarchy, when it is set in opposition from equality of honor, is rather
anarchy and faction. Therefore there is one God, but not two nor three nor
still more; for the many-godded is precisely godless; one emperor, and one
royal Word and law of his, not uttered in words and syllables nor spent by
length of time on writings and stelae, but a living and subsisting God the
Word, ordering the Father's kingship for all who are under him and after him.
And heavenly armies go about him, myriads of ministering angels of God and
multitudes of a supercosmic host and of the invisible spirits inside heaven
who serve the order of the whole cosmos, of all of whom the royal Word is
leader, like some viceroy of a great King. Commander-in-chief and great High
Priest and prophet of the Father and angel of great counsel and radiance of
paternal light and only-begotten Son, and a thousand other such titles, the
voices of theologians proclaim in oracles, whom the Begetter having
established as living Word and law and wisdom and fullness of every good, gave
as a gift of greatest goods to all under the kingship. And he, going through
all things everywhere and unfolding the Father's graces ungrudgingly to all

even as far as the rational beings on earth he extended the imitation of the
imperial power, adorning with divine powers the soul of man made according to
his image; hence there is present to it also the sharing of the other virtues
from a divine outpouring. For God alone is wise, and he alone is God. And he
himself alone is good in substance, and he himself alone is mighty in
strength, and begetter of justice itself, father of Word and wisdom, fountain
of light and life, steward of truth and virtue, and leader of kingship itself
and of every rule and authority. 4.1 But from where can men know these things?
Who ministered these things to the hearing of mortals? And from where can a
tongue of flesh utter things alien to fleshes and bodies? Who, having looked
through to the invisible King, saw these powers in him? For by sense the
elements akin to bodies and the compounds from them are grasped, but no one
yet has been proud of receiving with the eyes of bodies the invisible kingship
over all by sight, nor has mortal nature perceived the beauty of wisdom. And
who with the sense of fleshes has seen the face of justice? From where did
thoughts of lawful rule and imperial authority steal into men? From where is
autocratic power for one compacted of flesh and blood? And who declared to
those on earth the invisible and shapeless ideas and the bodiless and
shapeless substance? 4.2 But there was, then, one interpreter of these things,
the Word of God who comes through all, the father of the rational and
intellectual substance in men, alone fastened to the deity of the Father, and
watering his own offspring with the paternal outpourings. Hence for all men,
Greeks and barbarians alike, the self-taught reasonings according to nature;
hence thoughts of word and wisdom; hence seeds of prudence and justice; hence
the graspings of the arts; hence the knowledge of virtue and the dear name of
wisdom and the solemn love of philosophic education; hence the knowledge of
every good and fair thing; hence the imagination of God himself and a life
worthy of piety; hence for man the strength of kingship and unconquerable
might over all on earth. And as the Word, the father of rational beings,
established in the soul of man the stamp according to the image of God and
according to likeness, and made this animal royal, having shown this one alone
of those on earth to know how to rule and to be ruled, and to rehearse and be
taught beforehand from here the promised hope of the heavenly kingship, for
which he also came, the Father of the children himself did not shrink from
coming into converse with mortals, and cultivating his own seeds and renewing
the supplies from above he proclaimed the good news to all that they would
share in a heavenly kingship, and he called and exhorted them to be ready for
the upward journey, having prepared the garment worthy of the calling, and he
filled with unspeakable power the whole that the sun looks upon with the
proclamation, modeling the heavenly by the imitation of the kingship on earth,
toward which he also urges the whole race of men to hasten, having set this
forward as a good hope. 5.1 Of which the one dear to God will already share
from here, adorned with the virtues inborn in God and having received the
outpourings from there in the soul, and having become rational from the
universal Word, wise by participation in wisdom, good by communion with the
good, and just by sharing in justice, temperate by the idea of temperance, and
brave by having shared in the highest power. 5.2 Moreover this man would also
be styled king in true speech, who has been formed in the soul by imperial
virtues into the imitation of the kingship beyond. But the one alienated from
these and having denied the King of all, not having claimed the heavenly
Father of souls, nor having put on the adornment fitting an emperor, but
having taken up shapelessness and shame in the soul, and having exchanged a
wild beast's rage for imperial gentleness, and a hard-to-heal venom of vice
for a free disposition, and folly instead of prudence, and instead of reason
and wisdom

the most misshapen unreason of all, from which, as from a bitter drink, the
destructive offshoots attend, a profligate life, greed, murders, fights
against God, impieties; given over to these, even if he is thought at times to
rule by tyrannical force, yet this man will never be styled king in true
speech. And how would he be able to bear the imitation of monarchic authority
who has had myriads of falsely drawn images of demons stamped on his soul? And
how is he ruler and lord of all who has drawn upon himself myriads of bitter
masters against himself, a slave of shameful pleasure, a slave of unbridled
woman-madness, a slave of money procured from injustice, a slave of rage and
anger, a slave of fear and terrors, a slave of murderous demons, a slave of
soul-destroying spirits? 5.4 Therefore let him alone be proclaimed king for us
with truth as witness who is dear to God the All-Sovereign, who alone is free,
or rather who is truly lord, and above money, stronger than desire for women,
victor over pleasures and those according to nature, ruling rage and anger but
not ruled, truly autocrat, bearing the title that matches the deed; truly
victor who has won the victory over the passions that wrestle down the mortal
race, who has been imaged toward the archetypal idea of the great King and
formed in the intellect as in a mirror by the rays of the virtues from it, and
from them completed temperate, good, just, brave, pious, god-loving. Truly and
alone this is a philosopher king, who knows himself and knows well the
supplies of every good watered upon him from without, or rather from heaven,
who makes plain the reverend title of monarchic power by the exceptional
wrapping of his cloak and who alone has worthily been enveloped in the
imperial purple that befits him. 5.5 This is emperor who night and day calls
upon the heavenly Father, who invokes him in prayers, who reaches after the
highest kingship. For knowing that present things are not worthy of God the
All-Sovereign, mortal and perishable things flowing and perishing like a
river, he longs for the incorruptible and bodiless kingship of God, and prays
to obtain that, having by a great-minded reasoning lifted his thought above
the heavenly vault and having cherished in his breast an unutterable longing
for the lights there, by comparison with which he thinks the honors of the
present life differ in nothing from darkness; for he sees the rule of men as a
small and short-lived oversight of a mortal and temporary life, not much
better than the rule of goat-herds or shepherds or cowherds, or rather even
more toilsome and of more difficult flocks, and he sets the outcries of the
peoples and the voices of flatterers as more a nuisance than a pleasure,
because of a firm character and genuine education of soul. 5.6 But also the
escort of subjects, myriads of armies, masses of armed infantry and cavalry
together, slave and subject, seeing them he is in no way struck, nor is he
puffed up at the rule of these, turning his thought upon himself and seeing in
himself also the common nature of all. And as for a gold-woven garment woven
with varied flowers and the imperial purple together with the diadem itself,
he laughs as he watches the many astonished and, quite like children,
hallowing the appearance as some bugbear; but he himself, in no way having
suffered the like in his soul, through knowledge of the divine puts on as
wrapping an adornment variegated with temperance and justice and piety and the
remaining virtues, the adornment that truly befits an emperor. 5.7 Still
besides these, the money thrice-longed-for by the many, I mean gold and silver
and as many kinds of stones as are admired, he sees to be truly unhelpful
stones and useless matter, being such as they happen to be by nature, able to
help nothing toward a ward against evils; for what can these do toward

release from diseases or escape from death? Yet nevertheless, though he knows
these things, he arranges the use of them scientifically, with passionless
reasoning, toward the fair adornment of those ruled, laughing at those
fluttering over these things through infancy of mind; and from surfeit and
drunkenness and superfluous cookery, as proper to gluttons, he abstains,
taking these to belong to others but not to himself; for he is persuaded that
such things damage the great things and dim the intellectual part of the soul.
5.8 For all these reasons the emperor trained in divine things and thinking
great thoughts aspires from the present life to the better things, calling the
heavenly Father and longing for that kingship, doing all things with piety and
holding out to those ruled under him, as being trained under a good teacher,
the knowledge of God of the great King. 6.1 And he, wooing the pledges of
returns, distributes to him tricennial crowns completed in good cycles of
times, and having already gone round triple circles of decades he permits all
men to complete public, or rather cosmic, festivals. 6.2 And while those on
earth are made bright with flowers of the knowledge of God, it would not be
off the mark that the dances in heaven too, drawn by laws of nature, rejoice
with those on earth, and that the King over all himself, like a good father
over good sons who are pious, is gladdened, and in this especially honors with
long-timed honors the leader and cause of the goods, so that three cycles in
decades do not suffice for the kingship, but he assigns it for a very long
stretch and sends it on into a long age. 6.3 And the whole age, unaging and
endless, is not naturally seen by mortal reasonings either as beginning or as
limit, nor even taken from a center, nor does it allow those who wish to grasp
what is called its now, much less the future or the past of time; for the one
is not, that which has gone by, and the future is not yet present, therefore
it is not, and what is called its now escapes faster than thought and the
voice of speech. And it is not possible at all to lay hold of it as standing.
For one must either expect the future or survey what has passed; for at the
same moment as the thought it slips away and flees. Thus the whole age does
not bear being subjected to mortal reasonings, but refuses slavery among them.
6.4 Yet it does not decline to confess its own King and Master, and it bears
him riding on its back, taking pride in the ornaments from him. And he,
charioteering it from above, not binding it with a golden cord after the
poetic manner, but bridling it with bonds of unspeakable wisdom as with some
reins, laid down in it months and times, seasons and years, and the
alternating intervals of nights and days with all harmony, binding it round
with varied limits and measures. For the one, being straight and stretching
into the unbounded, and having received the title of age as being always, and
being like itself in its parts, or rather being without parts and without
interval, grows lengthened into a single straight line; but the other, taking
it with middle sections and as it were dividing a straight line stretched into
length, laid down a great multitude of centers in it, and being one and
likened to a monad he bound it with numbers of every kind, establishing the
variety in it as many-formed out of the formless. 6.5 For first of all he
established in it the one formless matter as some all-receiving substance, and
second, in matter, producing quality by the power of duality, he made form out
of the formless, and by the number of a triad, three-dimensioned, into breadth
and length and depth, he worked a body composed of matter and form. And having
conceived from duality doubled the tetrad of the elements, earth, water, air,
fire, he put forward everlasting fountains for the supply of this universe.
And a tetrad generates a decade; for one two three four complete the number
ten. And a triad

joined with a decade discovered the nature of a month, and a month with twelve
turnings filled the circuit of the sun, whence cycles of years and changes of
seasons formed the formless and shapeless age, as in the variety of a
many-flowered painting, for rest and gladness of those riding the course of
life in it. 6.6 For as for those who finish the races in a contest in hope of
prizes the intervals are marked off by stadia, and for those traveling long
journeys the highway is bounded by certain stations and signs, so that one may
not, stretching expectation into the unbounded, wear out his eagerness, in the
same way the King of all, having thrown the whole age about with ordinances of
wisdom, leads and carries it, charioteering it variously as it appears to him
to be well. And he himself, clothing the formerly shapeless age with fair
colors and fresh flowers, made the day bright and white with the sun, and
spreading night underneath with a darker color he made the glitterings of the
stars shine in it like some gold-woven specks, and having kindled the bright
rays of the light-bearer and the varied beams of the moon and the many-shining
conjunctions of stars he crowned the whole heaven like a great robe with
beauties of painting of every kind. And having stretched air into depth from
above out of height, and having ensouled the breadths and lengths of the whole
cosmos with its power, he allowed all winged things to be formed, spreading
out a great sea for the unseen and the seen that are borne through air to swim
through. And having balanced earth in the midst like a center, he threw ocean
about it, and the sea roars upon it, beautified with the dark-blue color of
the wrapping. 6.7 And having shown this as hearth and mother and nurse of all
living things on it, and having watered it with rain and spring streams, he
granted it to grow green with plants of every kind and fair flowers for
cheerful enjoyment of life. And having molded on it the most honored of living
things and dear to himself according to his own image, possessed of mind and
knowledge, offspring of word and wisdom, rational man, he gave him to rule
with ruling power the rest of as many as creep and graze on the earth. For man
was to him the most dear to God of living things on earth, to whom as a father
he also handed over the kinds of the irrational to serve, man, for whom he
also opened the sea to be sailed and crowned the earth with plants of every
kind, for whom he has also made the things that creep in the depths and the
things of birds on high subject, to whom he also granted scientific powers for
the reception of lessons of every kind, to whom he also uncovered the heavenly
contemplations, revealing courses of sun and turnings of moon and circuits of
wandering and fixed stars, man, to whom alone of those on earth he ordained to
know the heavenly Father and to honor with hymns the great King of the whole
age. 6.8 But besides all these the world-maker girded the unchanging age with
four turnings of a year, bounding winter seasons with spring, and balancing
spring, beginning of the year's seasons, with equal scales, and then having
crowned the whole age with the many-fruitedness of spring he handed it over to
the height of summer, then as if resting it from toil he restored it with an
autumnal interval, and wiping it with wet courses of winter rains as some
imperial horse and making it bright with the streams from itself, and
fattening it sufficiently with the waterings from rains, he sets it again in
the forecourt gates of spring. 6.9 From here then, in the cycle of the whole
year, the great King, having bound his own age about with such reins of
wisdom, ordained it to be borne under a greater steersman, his own
only-begotten Word, the common Savior of all, handing over the reins of the
universe. And he, having received the lot as from a good father, binding
together with one harmony all that the hollow of heaven holds inside and
outside it, finishes in a straight course, with justice looking after what is
fitting for his earthly

rational flocks. And having ordained measures of life for those who finish the
mortal life, he has granted all men to rehearse in this the preludes of the
life beyond the present age; for there is, there is even beyond the present

this would be outside multitude, farthest off and better than every number,

demons, more cruel than all barbarians, flying about this air, enslaved the
whole mortal race with engines of many-godded vice, so that the true God was

who is and who alone truly is as if he were not a side-matter. 7.3 Hence the

us of our Savior and to see together living works of a living God; for are not
such successes living works of one living and of a God living a life as truly
living? What then are these, you ask? Learn. 17.2 Certain god-fighters lately,
with more contentiousness and with greater power and hand, digging up from
foundations the buildings of his houses of prayer, took them down and made his
churches invisible, and with all engines they warred upon the one not seen
with the eyes, striking and hurling with myriads of casts of words, but the
invisible punished invisibly. 17.3 Then they no longer were, by one nod of
God, those a little before luxurious and thrice-blessed, those hymned as equal
to gods among all, those who for long cycles of years had steered the rule
distinguishedly, when the things toward the one later warred upon were dear to
them and peaceful; but when changing they dared to fight God, arraying their
own gods as champions and shield-bearers against ours, at once by one turn and
a nod of God and a power of the one warred upon all paid the penalties of the
things dared, so as to yield to the one warred upon and having given backs to
confess the deity together with him and to concede and permit the opposites of
the things dared before. 17.4 And he at once raised victory trophies
everywhere on earth, having adorned the whole inhabited world again from the
beginning with holy temples and solemn houses of prayer

dedicated things, having consecrated throughout cities and villages and all
countries and the deserts of the barbarians shrines and precincts to one God
the King of all, the Master also of all, whence also the consecrated things
have been deemed worthy of the Master's appellation, not having obtained the
invocation from men, but from the Lord of all himself, wherefore they have
also been deemed worthy of the titles of Lord's. 17.5 Let him then who wishes
come forward into the midst and teach, who after so great a taking-down and
desolation raised establishments from floor into height, who deemed the things
taken from every hope worthy of a second renewal much better than the one
before, and renewed them, the very greatest wonder of the word, not after the
end of those god-fighters, but while those very ones who had taken them down
were still remaining in life, so that through their mouth and through their
writing the palinode of the things dared by them was sung, and that they did
this not being in gladnesses one might perhaps think by a reasoning of
philanthropy, but being driven by God-sent scourges. 17.6 Who after so many
winters of the persecution and in the very height of the terrible things
constituted throughout the whole inhabited world myriads of men zealots of a
philosophic life and servants of God and priestesses of women and choirs of
ever-virgins having dedicated the whole time of their life in complete
chastity to the lessons of his inspired teaching, and to endure most eagerly
in abstinences from foods and in many-day fastings and wine-lessness and to
use temperance and strength of a firm life with sophrosyne? Who has persuaded
women and myriads of multitudes of men to exchange the foods of the body for
rational foods corresponding to rational souls through inspired readings? Who
taught barbarians and rustic men and women and children and kinds of household
servants and myriads of multitudes of myriads of nations to despise death and
to be persuaded that their soul is immortal, and that an eye of justice exists
as overseer of human affairs of pious and of impious, and to expect tribunals
of God, and for the sake of these to take care of a just and temperate life?
For not having been so disposed it is otherwise impossible to go under the
yoke of piety; which still even now is succeeded by our Savior alone. But let
us pass these things, 17.7 and come otherwise let us lead the one whose
thought has been turned to stone also from such questions, inquiring of him in
this way. Come then, you, having sent forth a rational voice, not putting
forward fruit from a silly heart but from an intellectual and rational soul,
say then, having often considered beside yourself, who else ever of those
cried wise from the age similarly to our Savior was known and proclaimed
beforehand from above before myriads of times with prophetic voices among the
children of Hebrews dear to God very long ago? Who also having taken
beforehand the place of his generation and times of the arrival and manner of
the life and powers and words and successes of his have laid them down in holy
books. 17.8 Or who has been shown so swift an avenger of the things dared
against him, as together with the impiety against him to go after the whole
nation of Jews with an invisible power, and for their royal place to be taken
up altogether from foundations, and for the temple itself together with the
solemn things in it to be brought down to the floor? And who, having uttered
predictions corresponding to the facts both about these very impious men and
about the church that arose from him throughout the whole inhabited world,
showed these faithful by the deeds, as our Savior? Saying about the temple of
the impious: "Your house is left to you desolate;" and: "There shall not
remain a stone upon a stone in this place, which shall not be loosed;" and
about his own church: "Upon the rock I will build my church, and gates of
Hades shall not prevail against it." 17.9 And the having transferred at the

in word and done in deed that they are fishers of men, and having provided

books, and to have established these to such a point that throughout the whole
inhabited world, being translated in every tongue of barbarians and of Greeks,
they are practiced among all the nations, and the things laid down in them are
believed to be divine oracles. =17.10= And of what sort also the himself
having prophesied the future and having witnessed beforehand to the disciples,
that they would be led before emperors and governors to be punished and to
undergo the last chastisements, not through some unfitting deed but only
through the confession unto this one? And the having prepared them to undergo
these things eagerly and having fenced them with weapons of piety so that they
appeared in the contests toward the opponents stronger in souls than adamant,
what word would it not surpass? =17.11= And the having so nerved the powers of
the soul not only of those who had followed him, but already also of the
successors of those and again of those who came afterward and of those still
to this day among us ourselves, so as, having done nothing worthy of death, to
undergo with pleasure chastisements of every kind and every form of
torture-engines for the sake of piety toward the God of all, what wonder would
it not surpass? But who ever of emperors continued ruling unto so great an
age? And who so generals after death and sets trophies against enemies, and
subjects every place and country and city, Greek and barbarian, subduing the
opponents with an invisible and unseen right hand? =17.12= And the head of the
things said, the peace stewarded everywhere on earth through his power, about
which having taken beforehand we have said the likely things, what mouth of
slanderers would it not stop, the concord and agreement of all the nations
having thus run together in deed with the teaching of our Savior and with the
proclamation laid down by him for the whole cosmos, and the cosmic peace of
the nations and of the word laid down by him for the nations having been thus
fore-sounded very long ago by the prophets of God? The whole length of the day
will fail me, emperor, attempting to gather and constitute under one the clear
proofs of the inspired power of our Savior from the things still now seen,
because no one ever of those from the age either among Greeks or among
barbarians has displayed so great a power of inspired virtue as our Savior.
=17.13= And why do I speak of men, when not even of those spoken of as gods
among all the nations has some such nature appeared on earth; or let him who
wishes show, and let every philosopher come forward and tell us, who from the
whole age ever heard a god and a hero having handed over to men lessons
procuring eternal life and a heavenly kingship, such as our Savior, having
made myriads of multitudes throughout the whole inhabited world train together
in philosophic doctrines, and having persuaded them to pursue heaven and to
hope for the sojourns in heaven stored up for souls dear to God? Who ever god
or even hero-man, having ridden the course all but equal to the sun from the
rising sun as far as the setting, shone through and lighted with the brightest
rays of his teaching, so that everywhere on earth every nation completes one
and the same worship for one God? Who ever god or even hero, having elbowed
aside all gods and heroes Greek and barbarian, legislated to think none of
those to be a god and having legislated persuaded; then having been warred
upon by all, being himself one, took down the whole camp of the opponents,
prevailing over all gods and heroes from the age, from ends as throughout
the whole inhabited world of men as only a child of God by all the nations

to be proclaimed? =17.14= Who handed over to those dwelling in the great
element of earth and to those on earth and those on sea to keep as a feast the
day styled the Lord's on each seventh, coming together upon the same, and
prepared them not to fatten the bodies but to rekindle the souls with inspired
teachings? Who god or even hero, having been so warred upon as our Savior,
raised victory trophies against the enemies? For they did not cease from
beginning and unto end besieging his lessons and the people, but the invisible
invisibly led his own in their holy houses unto a great glory. =17.15= And why
must one be ambitious to gather with words the inspired powers of our Savior
better than every word, when even while we are silent the facts themselves cry
to those who possess ears of soul, that this truly strange and unexpected and
one only from the age the cosmos of men has borne, and that the truly child of
God alone from the age has appeared to those on earth. 18.1 And the things
heard from us would be superfluous and otherwise for you, who having often
taken a perception of the saving deity by the trial itself, have been set as a
herald of the truth to all not in words but in the deeds themselves; for you
yourself, emperor, if leisure were given, would tell if you wished myriads of
divine appearings of your Savior, myriads of presences in sleeps, 18.2 I do
not mean his secret suggestions to us, but those laid down in the reasoning in
you yourself and setting beside the things beneficial to the common and the
things useful everywhere concerning the providence of all; 18.3 and worthily
you will go through for us the clear helps in wars of your champion and
guardian God, the destructions of enemies and plotters, the welcomes in
dangers, the abundances in impossibles, the assistances in desolations, the
findings in unmanageables, the foreknowledges of things to come, the
forethoughts about the universal things, the considerations about the unclear,
the undertakings about the great, the civic economies, the administrations of
the armies, the corrections according to each, the ordinances about the common
things, the life-useful legislations. And you would number without omission
each of the things escaping us, but recognized to you yourself alone and
stored like treasures in secrets in your imperial memories. For the sake of
all of which reasonably, having used these very bright proofs of the saving
power, you displayed a house of prayer, a trophy of his victory against death,
to all men faithful together and unfaithful, and a holy temple of a holy God,
and bright and great most beautiful dedicated things of immortal life and of
an inspired kingship, the things fitting very well and proper to a victor
emperor, offerings of the All-Sovereign Savior which you have thrown about the
testimony and memorial of the immortal life, imaging the heavenly Word of God
as victor and trophy-bearer with imperial stamps, proclaiming beforehand to
all the nations with bright and unshadowed voices, in deed and in word, the
pious and god-loving confession.
