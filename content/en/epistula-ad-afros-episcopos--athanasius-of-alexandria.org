through Him it came to be; and, In Him all things were created and consist.
But these men, being Arians rather than Christians, have dared in their other
synods to call Him a creature, and one of the things made, of which He
Himself, the Word, is the Craftsman and Maker. For if all things came to be
through Him, and He too is a creature, He would be creating Himself. And how
can that which is being created create? or how is the one who creates created?
But they are not even thus ashamed, saying such things, for which they are
hated even by all, naming Ariminum simply, yet being shown to have been
deposed even there. And as to the very thing written at Nicaea, that the Son
is homoousios with the Father, on account of which they pretend to fight
against the synod, and =26.1037= buzz around everywhere, like mosquitoes,
about the phrase, either they stumble at it as if in ignorance, as those who
stumble at the stone of stumbling laid in Sion; or they know, and for this
very reason they fight and constantly murmur, because it is truly an accurate
judgment against their heresy. For it is not the words that grieve them, but
the condemnation of them that came about in it. And of these things again they
themselves have become the cause, and if they themselves wish to hide this,
though they know it, yet it is fitting for us to speak, so that from these
things also the true accuracy of the great synod may be shown. For the bishops
who had come together wished to do away with the phrases of impiety invented
by the Arians: From things that are not; and calling the Son a creature and a
thing made; and, There was once when He was not; and that He is of a
changeable nature; and to write the acknowledged phrases of the Scriptures:
that the Word is by nature only-begotten from God, Power, the only Wisdom of
the Father, true God, as John said; and as Paul wrote, radiance of the glory,
and impress of the Father's hypostasis. Those around Eusebius, drawn by their
own evil opinion, kept talking with one another: Let us agree; for we too are
from God; for there is one God, from whom are all things; and, The old things
have passed away, behold all things have become new. And all things are from
God. And they also reckoned what was written in the Shepherd: First of all
believe that God is one, who created and ordered all things, and made them
from non-being into being. But the bishops, seeing their knavery and the evil
craft of their impiety, expressed more plainly the phrase from God, and wrote
that the Son is from the essence of God; so that the creatures, because they
are not from themselves without a cause, but have a beginning of coming to be,
may be said to be from God; but the Son alone is proper to the Father's
essence; for this is proper to an only-begotten and true Word toward a Father.
And this is the reason why it was written from the essence. Again, when the
bishops asked the few who seemed so, whether they would say that the Son is
not a creature, but Power, the only Wisdom of the Father, and eternal Image,
unvarying in all things from the Father, and true God, those around Eusebius
were caught nodding to one another, that these things also apply even to us;
for we too are called image and glory of God, and of us it has been said:
=26.1040= For we who live are always. And there are many powers; And all the
power of the Lord went out of the land of Egypt. And the caterpillar and the
locust are called a great power. And, The Lord of powers is with us, our
helper is the God of Jacob. But as for our being proper to God, we have this
not simply, but because He has also called us brothers. And if they also call
the Son true God, it does not grieve us; for having come to be, He is true.
This is the corrupted mind of the Arians. But here too the bishops, seeing
their deceit, gathered from the Scriptures the radiance, and the fountain and
the river, and the impress toward the hypostasis, and, In your light we shall
see light; and, I and the Father are one. And thereafter they wrote more
plainly and concisely that the Son is homoousios with the Father; for all the
things said before have this meaning. And their murmuring, that the phrases
are unscriptural, is exposed

from them as vain, they having been impious from unscriptural terms; and
unscriptural are, From things that are not, and, There was once when He was
not; they complain that they were condemned from unscriptural phrases
understood with piety. They themselves, as being from a dunghill, truly spoke
from the earth; but the bishops, not having found the phrases for themselves,
but having the testimony from the Fathers, wrote thus. For ancient bishops,
some hundred and thirty years before, of the great Rome and of our city,
writing, accused those who say the Son is a thing made and not homoousios with
the Father; and Eusebius who became bishop of Caesarea knew this, who at first
ran with the Arian heresy, but later signed at that very synod in Nicaea, and
wrote to his own people affirming that we have known some of the ancient
learned and distinguished bishops and writers to have used the name homoousios
of the Godhead of the Father and the Son. Why then do they still name
Ariminum, at which they were deposed? Why do they refuse the synod at Nicaea,
at which even their Fathers signed, and confessed that the Son is from the
essence and homoousios with the Father? Why do they run about? For they fight
not only against the bishops who came together at Nicaea, but even against
their own great bishops, and against their own people besides. Of whom then
are they heirs and successors? How can they call these men Fathers, whose
confession, written well and apostolically, they do not accept? =26.1041=
Since, if they think they can contradict, let them speak, or rather let them
answer, so that contradicting themselves they may also be exposed, whether
they believe the Son when He says: I and the Father are one; and, He who has
seen Me has seen the Father? Yes, they would say, since it is written, we
believe. But also, How they are one, and how he who has seen the Son has seen
the Father also, if they are asked to say, they will surely say, Through
likeness; unless they have wholly fallen in with those who think kindred
thoughts with them, and are called Anomoeans. But if they are asked again, How
is He like? they will say, growing bold: By perfect virtue and agreement,
willing the same as the Father, and not willing what the Father does not will.
But let them learn that one who is made like God from virtue and from willing
has also the choice of changing; but the Word is not thus, unless He is like
in part and as we are, because He is not like also according to essence. And
these things are proper to us who are originate and have a created nature. For
we too, though we cannot become like God according to essence, yet, being
improved from virtue, imitate God, the Lord granting this also, and saying:
Become merciful, as your Father is merciful. Become perfect, as your heavenly
Father is perfect. And that originate things are changeable, no one would
deny, angels having transgressed, and Adam having disobeyed, and all needing
the grace of the Word. But that which is changeable would not be like God who
is unchangeable; just as neither is that which has been created like the one
who created. Therefore concerning us the holy man said, considering: O God,
who shall be likened to you? and, Who is like you among the gods, O Lord?
calling gods those who were created, yet became partakers of the Word, as He
Himself said: If He called them gods, to whom the Word of God came. But things
that partake would not be the same as, nor like, that of which they partake.
Of Himself at any rate He said: I and the Father are one, as originate things
are not thus. Since let those who put forward Ariminum answer, whether a
created essence can say: What I see the Father doing, these I also do. For
originate things are things being made, and not making; otherwise they even
made themselves. Indeed if, as they say, the Son is a creature, and the Father
makes Him, the Son would surely also have made Himself, being able to make
what the Father makes, as He Himself said; but such a thing is absurd and
utterly incoherent; for no one can make himself. Again let them say whether
originate things can say: All things whatever the Father has are mine.
=26.1044= And He has creating, making, eternity, being almighty, the
unchangeable. But originate things cannot have making; for they are created;
nor eternity; for they have a beginning of being; nor the almighty and
unchangeable; for they are under sway and of a changeable nature, as the
Scriptures say. Therefore if these things belong to the Son, they are not from
virtue, as we said, but are clearly of His essence, as the synod said; He is
not of another essence, but of the Father's, of which these things also are
proper; and what is proper to the Father's essence, and an offspring from it,
what would it be, or how would one name it, if not homoousios? For what one
sees in the Father, these one sees also in the Son; and in a Son not from
participation, but according to essence. And this is: I and the Father are
one, and, He who has seen Me has seen the Father. Besides, from this again it
is good to show their folly: if from virtue there follows willing and not
willing, and improvement of ways, the Son is like the Father according to you;
but these things are proper to quality; clearly you say God is composite from
quality and essence. And who will endure you saying these things; for God who
composed all things into being is not composite, nor such as are the things
that came to be from Him through the Word, far be it! For it is a simple
essence, in which there is no quality, nor, as James said, any variation or
shadow of turning. Therefore if it is shown not from virtue; for there is no
quality in God, nor in the Son; clearly He would be proper to the essence; and
this you will altogether confess, unless understanding has been wholly lost in
you. And what is proper and the same as the essence of God and an offspring
from it by nature, what would it be, if not again even on this account
homoousios with Him who begot? For this is the mark of a Son toward a Father.
And he who does not say this does not think that the Word is Son by nature and
truly. Having understood thus, the Fathers wrote that the Son is homoousios
with the Father, and they anathematized those who say that the Son is from
another hypostasis; not having fashioned phrases for themselves, but they too
having learned from the Fathers before them, as we said. And these things
being thus shown, their Ariminum is superfluous, and superfluous also is the
other synod named by them concerning the faith. For the one at Nicaea is
enough, being in agreement also with the ancient bishops, at which even their
Fathers signed, whom it was fitting for these men to revere, so that they may
not be thought anything rather than Christians. And if even after so many
things, after also the =26.1045= testimony of the ancient bishops, and after
the signature of their own Fathers, they pretend, as if in ignorance, to fear
the phrase homoousios, let them say and think more simply and truly that the
Son is Son by nature, and let them anathematize, as the synod commanded, those
who say creature, or thing made, or from things that are not, or, There was
once when the Son of God was not; and that He is changeable and alterable, and
from another hypostasis; and so let them flee from the Arian heresy; and we
are confident that, genuinely anathematizing these things, they confess at
once that the Son is from the essence and homoousios with the Father. For this
is why the Fathers also, having said that the Son is homoousios, added at
once: But those who say creature, or thing made, or from things that are not,
or, There was once when He was not, the Catholic Church anathematizes; so that
through these things they may know that these things signify the homoousion.
And the force of the homoousion is known from the Son not being a creature or
a thing made; and that he who says homoousios does not think the Word is a
creature; and he who anathematizes the things said before at the same time
thinks the Son is homoousios with the Father; and he who says homoousios says
the Son of God is genuine and true. And he who says Genuine understands: I and
the Father are one; and, He who has seen Me has seen the Father. It would have
been fitting then to write such things at greater length; but since we write
to you who know, for this reason we have dictated concisely; praying that
among all the bond of peace be preserved, and that all who belong to the
Catholic Church say the same thing, think the same thing. And we are not as
teaching, but as reminding. And it is not we who write alone, but also all the
bishops in Egypt and the Libyas, some ninety. For this is one mind of all, and
we always sign for one another, if it happens that someone is not present.
Being thus disposed, since it happened that we came together, we wrote also to
our beloved Damasus, the bishop of the great Rome, concerning Auxentius who
has come upon the church in Milan, relating the things about him; that he is
not only a partner of the Arian heresy, but is also liable for many evils,
having done these things with Gregory, the partner of his impiety; and
wondering how until now he has not been deposed and cast out of the Church,
and we gave thanks to the piety of him and of those who came together in the
great Rome, because, casting out also those around Ursacius and Valens, and
those who think the same things as they, they saved the like-mindedness of the
Catholic Church; which we also pray =26.1048= may be preserved among you, we
exhort you not to endure, as we said before, those who put forward a crowd of
synods on a pretext of faith, the one at Ariminum, the one at Sirmium, the one
in Isauria, the one in Thrace, those in Constantinople, the many and
disorderly ones in Antioch. But let only the faith confessed at Nicaea by the
Fathers prevail among you; at which all, even the Fathers of those now
fighting against it, were present, as we said before, and signed; so that
concerning us also the Apostle may say: And I praise you, that you remember me
in all things, and as I delivered to you the traditions, so you hold them. For
this synod at Nicaea is truly a written pillar against every heresy. It also
overturns those who blaspheme against the Holy Spirit, and say it is a
creature. For the Fathers, having spoken concerning the faith in the Son,
added at once: We believe also in the Holy Spirit, so that, having confessed
the faith in the holy Trinity as complete and full, they might make known in
this the impress of the faith in Christ, and the teaching of the Catholic
Church. For it is established as clear both among you and among all; and no
one of Christians would have a doubtful mind toward this, that our faith is
not in the creation, but in one God Father almighty, maker of all things
visible and invisible; and in one Lord Jesus Christ, His only-begotten Son;
and in one Holy Spirit; one God known in the holy and perfect Trinity; into
which we also are baptized, and in this being joined to the Godhead, we
believe also to inherit the kingdom of the heavens in Christ Jesus our Lord,
through whom to the Father be the glory and the power unto the ages. Amen.
