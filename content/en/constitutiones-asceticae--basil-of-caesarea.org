OF OUR FATHER AMONG THE SAINTS BASIL, ARCHBISHOP OF CAESAREA IN CAPPADOCIA,
ASCETIC CONSTITUTIONS, TO THOSE WHO PRACTICE ASCESIS IN COMMUNITY AND IN
SOLITUDE.

PROEM.

Having chosen the philosophy according to Christ, and having lifted the mind
above worldly desires and pleasures and cares, and having been eager in every
way to separate and set apart the reasoning from the passions of the flesh,
you have often made many speeches to us, asking in what manner one ought to
complete the standing of the contest, nowhere being captured by the fleshly
desires that come through the body upon the soul; and what first is especially
to be guarded against, and what after the first; and which of the good things
are to be emulated, so that by the guarding one may flee the working of the
unseemly, and by the zeal accomplish the practice of the good. Then indeed you
thought it right that the setting forth of our counsel concerning this should
also become written for you. For this reason we ourselves also have been led
not to overlook your good zeal, but even in the way that is possible to
support and confirm it by the =31.1324= counsels from ourselves; not as
furnishing a speech sufficient for the subject, but as not hiding what there
is, as if by some mound, by silence, and thereby fleeing the threatened
judgment upon the one who hid the talent in the earth. The pretext then, as
for the most part, for men of the worldly desires and pleasures and cares is
marriage. For one would not find either the desire of the female in men, or in
women that of the male, more vehement and more violent lying in the nature of
the body; and very reasonably, as being naturally constituted toward the
sprouting of offspring; which, having a most timely working, was going also to
bring upon itself a more vehement rush; nor cares heavier stealing in upon men
than those that fly upon them all at once through the pairing, as Paul says:
He who has married cares for the things of the world, being burdened with the
cares. For the one who is alone might bear with his own and the necessary
things of the body, or perhaps even despise these, being able easily to
persuade himself to this; but the one who takes care of a wife and children is
no longer master of his own judgment, but has necessity to do the things
according to pleasures for her, and being occupied about the care of the
children, he draws up a great abyss of cares, which it would be longer than
the present occasion to list. He then who is eager to be free of the bonds of
the world has fled marriage as certain fetters; and having fled this, he has
dedicated his own life to God, and has confessed chastity; so that there is
not even license for him of a turning back toward marriage, but he must in
every way hold to the contests for chastity, wrestling with nature, and with
the more violent impulses of this. For such a one, having become a lover of
God, and desiring to lay hold of his impassibility, at least in the smallest
measure, and of the spiritual holiness, and of calm and undisturbance, and
gentleness, and desiring to taste of the gladness and joy born from these, has
been eager to lead the reasonings far from every material and bodily passion
that muddies the soul; and with a pure and unshaded eye of the soul he looks
round upon the divine things, being filled insatiably with the light from
there. Into such a habit and state


having trained the soul, he is made akin, according to the likeness that is
possible, to God, and becomes beloved and most longed-for to him; as having
endured some great and hard-to-accomplish prize, and having been able from the
mixing according to matter to converse with God with a mind unmixed and
separated from the =31.1325= admixture of bodily passions. It is therefore
fitting and consistent that the one who has gone up into such a habit through
the aforementioned ascesis not be dragged down again through the provocations
of the flesh toward communion with its passions; nor, receiving the vapors
that rise from there, have the eye of the soul darkened as by some deepest
mist; and fall away from the divine and spiritual contemplation, the gaze of
the reasoning being made sharp by the smoke of the passions.

CHAPTER 1. Concerning the need to set prayer before all things.

Every deed, beloved, and every word of our Savior Jesus Christ is a rule of
piety and of virtue. For this very reason he also became man, as drawing piety
and virtue for us in an image, in order that each man and each woman, seeing,
may emulate the archetype according to power. For this reason he wears our
body, in order that we also may imitate his way of life, as far as possible.
You then, when you hear a word or a deed of his, do not hear it as a
side-matter and simply and as it happens; but enter into the depth of the
contemplations; become a sharer of the things mystically handed down. For
Martha receives the Lord; but Mary sits at his feet; in two sisters there is a
good eagerness; yet distinguish the things. For Martha was serving, preparing
the things toward the reception of his bodily need; but Mary, sitting at his
feet, was hearing his words. The one then was giving rest to what appears, but
the other was serving the invisible. For he who was present was truly both man
and God, the same Master accepted the eagerness of both women. But Martha,
being pressed by toil, was calling the Lord to become a mediator, in order
that she might take her own sister as a helper of the service. Tell her, she
says, that she may rise and serve together with me. But the Lord to her:
Martha, Martha, you care and are troubled about many things. But of one thing
there is need. For Mary has chosen the good portion, which will not be taken
away from her; For we are not present for this, that we may recline upon
stuffing, and nourish the belly; but we are present, that we may nourish you
with a word of truth and with contemplation of mysteries. He did not then turn
the one =31.1328= away from the things she was doing, but he accepted the
other for the things to which she was attending. See then for me the two
portions being introduced through the two women; the one lesser, having chosen
the more bodily service, except that it too is most useful; but the one that
has gone up by the contemplation of the mysteries better and more spiritual.
These things you the hearer take spiritually, and choose what you wish. If you
wish to serve, serve in the name of Christ. For he himself was saying:
Inasmuch as you did it to one of these my brothers, the least, you did it to
me. For even if you receive strangers, even if you give rest to the poor, even
if you bend toward those in pain, even if you stretch a hand of help to those
in necessity and calamity, even if you serve the sick, Christ receives all
things unto himself. But if you wish to emulate Mary who has left the service
of the body, and has gone up into contemplation of the spiritual spectacles,
pursue the matter genuinely. Leave the body, let go farming, and cookery, and
preparation; but sit beside the feet of the Lord, and hear his words, in order
that you may become a sharer of the mysteries of the Godhead. For the
contemplation of the lessons of Jesus has gone up above the service of the
body. You have received then, beloved, the


examples and the demonstration. Emulate what you wish, either be a servant of
the poor, or a lover of the doctrines of Christ. But if you are able to
emulate both, from each side you carry off the fruit of salvation. First
indeed is the spiritual word, but all the remaining things are second. For
Mary, he says, has chosen the good portion. If then you also wish to be an
initiate of Christ, you will sit beside his feet, and you will receive his
Gospel, you will leave his whole life, and you will pass through without care;
and you will even forget your own body, and thus you will be able to converse
with his contemplations, in order that you may emulate Mary, and harvest the
highest glory. But when you pray, see that you do not ask other things instead
of other things, and provoke the Lord, not money, not human glory, not power,
not some other of the things that pass away; but ask the kingdom of God, and
he himself will furnish you all the things toward the need of the body, as the
Lord himself says: Seek the kingdom of God, and his righteousness, and all
these things will be added to you. And of prayer, beloved, there are two
manners; the one that of doxology with humility; but second the one of asking,
which is lower. Praying then, do not come straightway upon asking; otherwise
you slander your choice, as if you pray to God being compelled by the need.
Beginning then of prayer, leave yourself, wife, children; let go the earth, go
beyond heaven, leave all =31.1329= creation visible and invisible, and begin
from doxology of the one who made all things, and when you glorify him, not
with the mind wandering here and there, nor mythologizing in a Greek way, but
choosing from the holy Scriptures, and saying: I bless you, Lord, the
long-suffering, and the one who bears evil, who each day is long-suffering
toward me as I sin, and has given authority of repentance to us all. For this
reason you are silent, and you bear with us, Lord, in order that we may
glorify you who manage the salvation of our race; at one time through fears,
at another through exhortations, at one time through prophets, and later
through the presence of your Christ having visited us. For you formed us, and
not we. You are our God. And when you have glorified from the Scriptures, as
you are able, and have sent up praise toward God, then begin with humility,
and say: I, Lord, am not worthy to speak before you, because I am exceedingly
a sinner. And even if you are not conscious of anything base in yourself, thus
you must speak. For no one is without sin except God alone. For sinning many
things, we do not even understand the most. For this reason the Apostle says:
I am conscious of nothing in myself, but I am not justified in this; that is,
I sin many things, and I do not understand. Whence also the Prophet says:
Transgressions, who will understand? So then you do not lie in calling
yourself a sinner. For if you understand, even in this very thing you sin, in
saying that I am not a sinner; but rather say that Above all sinners I am the
one who transgresses the divine command that orders: When you have
accomplished all things, say that We are unprofitable servants; for what we
ought to do, we have done. Thus you must reckon continually, that I am
unprofitable; and again: In humility considering one another as surpassing
yourselves. Pray then to God with fear and humility. When then you stretch a
word of humility, and say: I thank you, Lord, that you were long-suffering
toward me in my transgressions, and until now you have left me unpunished; for
I was worthy long ago to suffer countless terrible things, and to be thrown
away from your face, but your forbearing philanthropy was long-suffering upon
me; I thank you, even if I am not sufficient toward thanksgiving of your
forbearance; and when you have fulfilled the two parts of doxology and
humility, then at last ask what you ought to ask; not wealth, as I said
before, not earthly glory, not health of body. For he himself formed you, and
cares for your salvation, and knows how it profits each, whether to be
healthy,


or to be =31.1332= weak; but ask, as you were commanded, the kingdom of God.
For concerning the need of your body, as I said before, he himself will take
care. For our king is most worthy of dignity, and he is indignant, if someone
asks him some small thing, if one of us asks him concerning the things that in
no way belong. Do not then in your prayer bring indignation upon yourself, but
ask for yourself things worthy of the king God. And asking the things worthy
of God, do not stand off, until you receive. For hinting at this the Lord says
in the Gospel: Who of you will have a friend, and will go to him at midnight,
and will say to him: Friend, lend me three loaves, since a friend has come to
me from a journey, and I do not have what I will set before him; and that one
from within answers: Do not furnish me toils; for already the door has been
shut, and my children are with me in the bed, I am not able to rise and give
to you. I say to you, even if he will not give to him, having risen because of
his being a friend, yet because of his shamelessness having risen he will give
him as many things as he needs. The Lord gives us an example, in order that he
may teach us to be intense and contentious in faith. For he takes an example
from man toward man, in order that you may learn never to give up; in order
that, when you have asked, and have not received, you may not stand off until
you receive, except, as I said before, if you ask the things that God wishes;
and do not say that I am a sinner, and I am not heard. For in order that you
may not despair, for this reason he says: Even if he will not give to him
because of his being a friend, yet because of his shamelessness he will give
him as many things as he needs. For the rest even if a month should pass, even
if a year, even if a three-year, even if a four-year time, even if more years,
until you receive, do not withdraw, but ask with faith, continually working
the good. For often one of us in youth laid claim to temperance, then pleasure
stole in, the desires according to nature were moved, the prayer became weak,
wine was thrown upon the youth, the temperance was destroyed, and the man
became another instead of another. Thus the changes happen, because of not
standing against the passions with a noble reasoning. One must then bring in
all things from oneself, but cry toward God, in order that he may fight
together with him. For if someone by slackness will give himself over to the
=31.1333= desires, and will betray himself to the enemies, God does not fight
together with this one, nor does he hear; for having gone first through the
sin he has alienated himself from God. For the one who wishes to be helped by
God does not betray what is fitting; and the one who does not betray what is
fitting is never betrayed by the divine alliance. One must then in nothing be
condemned by his own conscience, and thus call upon the divine alliance; and
call upon it not idly, not with the mind being lifted here and there; for such
a one not only will not receive the request, but will even more greatly
provoke the Master. For if, standing before a ruler and conversing, one stands
with much fear, having both the outer eye and the inner one of the soul
unlifted, lest he be in danger; how much more before God must one stand with
fear and trembling, having the whole mind stretched toward him alone, and
nowhere else? Because he himself not only sees the outer man, as men do, but
also looks down upon the inner. If then thus, as one must, you stand before
God, and you bring in all things from yourself, do not stand off, until you
receive your request; but if you are condemned by your conscience as
despising, and if you stand lifted into prayer, being able to stand unlifted,
do not dare to stand before God, lest your prayer become into sin. But if,
having become weak by the sin, you are not able to pray without distraction,
force yourself as much as you are able, and persist standing before God,
having the mind toward him, and gathering it toward yourself, and God makes
forgiveness, because not from contempt, but from weakness you are not strong,
as one must, to stand before God. If you thus force yourself into every good
work, do not stand off, until you receive


your request, but long-sufferingly knock his door, asking your request. For
everyone who asks receives, he says, and the one who seeks finds, and to the
one who knocks it will be opened. For of what other thing do you wish to
obtain except only the salvation according to God? Do you wish to learn,
beloved, how the saints were long-suffering, and did not despair? God called
Abraham younger, and from the land of the Assyrians transferred him into
Palestine, having said to him: To you I will give this land, and to your seed
after you; and as the stars of heaven, so will your seed be, which will not be
numbered. And a great number of years passed, and his nature was put to death,
and the end was in the doorways, and he did not say that, Lord, you always
announce children to me, and you foretell that I will be father of all the
nations. Dead =31.1336= are the movements of my nature through old age, and
Sarah, my wife, no longer endures anything womanly through old age; so that
the promise is false. For two old people, what hope do we have? He did not say
these things, he did not think them, but he remained unshaken in the faith;
and in age he was growing old, but the hope was growing young. And the body
was coming into feebleness, and was producing despair, but the faith was
nerving both the soul and the body. God, he says, is the one who promised, the
Master of nature, and it cannot become otherwise. He himself is the one who
even makes the impossible things possible; because he makes all things, and
rearranges as he wishes. Imitate the faith of Abraham. When then the nature
became weak, and the movements were put to death, then the promise of God
lived. Receive the examples. But we pray a year, and we withdraw; we fast two
years, and we cease. Let us not then become feeble toward the promise of God.
For also the one who promised to that one to multiply his seed, promised also
to us who ask to furnish our request. For he says: Come all to me, you who
toil and are heavy-laden, and I will give you rest. For you being far from
him, toiling and heavy-laden with the heaviest burden of sin, having had mercy
he called you, so as to lighten you of the burden, and to grant rest for the
rest; and you do not believe him? But indeed, even if we wish to be silent, we
are convicted by our conscience. For we do not disbelieve him as not being
able to give us rest, but we shrink from taking his yoke upon ourselves, the
useful and light, and through the narrow gate entering into the kingdom of the
heavens; but we choose rather to bear the burden of the sins, and through the
passions in pleasure to walk the spacious way, and through the broad gate to
enter into destruction. But often, he says, I asked, and I did not receive.
Wholly because you asked badly, or without faith, or lifted, or the things
that do not profit you. And if you also often asked the things that profit,
yet you did not remain. For it has been written that In your endurance acquire
your souls; and, The one who has endured unto the end, this one will be saved.
God knows the heart of those who pray. What then, he says, does God have need
of our asking? Does he not know of what things we have need? What then is the
need of asking? God knows of what things we need, and all the bodily things he
furnishes richly to us unto enjoyment, and being good, he rains upon just and
unjust, and he makes his sun rise upon evil =31.1337= and good, and before we
ask; but the faith, and the accomplishments of virtue, and the kingdom of the
heavens, if you do not ask with toil and much remaining, you do not receive.
For one must first long, and having longed seek from truth in faith and
endurance bringing in all things from oneself; being judged in nothing by his
own conscience, as asking either carelessly or idly, and then receive, when
the Lord wishes. For he knows better than you the things that profit you. And
perhaps for this reason he delays to give, managing your sitting-by toward
him; and in order that you may know what a gift of God is, and may guard what
has been given with fear. For everything that someone acquires with much toil,
he is eager


to guard this, lest, having lost it, he lose also his much toil, and having
set aside the grace of God, become unworthy of the eternal life. For what did
it profit Solomon quickly receiving the grace of wisdom, and having lost it?
Do not then be small-souled, if you do not receive the request quickly. For if
the good Master knew that, quickly receiving the grace, you will not lose it,
he was ready even before you ask to furnish it. But now caring for you he does
this. For if the one who received the talent, and guarded it sound, because he
did not do business with it, was condemned; how much more will the one who has
lost it be condemned? Knowing these things then, whether we receive more
quickly or more slowly, let us remain thanking the Lord; because all things
whatever the Master does, he manages on behalf of our salvation; only let us
not, having become small-souled, cease from the asking. For this reason the
Lord spoke the parable concerning the widow, who through her remaining bent
the lawless judge, in order that we also through remaining may receive our
requests. For from here both our faith and the love toward God is shown, when,
even not receiving quickly, we remain thanking him. Let us then thank him
continually, in order that we may be counted worthy to obtain his eternal good
things; because to him belongs the glory unto the ages of the ages. Amen.

CHAPTER 2. Concerning the attending of the reasonings, and that the body is
not evil, as some have supposed.

First then one must hold the reasoning in every =31.1340= way, setting the
oversight of the thought sober, so as not to allow the soul easily to give in
to the unconsidered impulses toward the pullings of the body. For the seeing
of the body is the eye, but the seeing of the soul is the mind connatural to
it; yet not as one thing in another, but the same both soul and mind; being
some natural power and not imported of the reasoning part of the soul. For
when the soul, moving its own intellectual, that which has been naturally sown
in it by the holy Trinity that made it, deliberates the things that are due
and fitting, then it flees the insolences of the body; and foreseeing and
stopping its disorderly movements, it leads the calm that is fitting to it,
and in an unstruck leisure it comes to be in the contemplations according to
nature; this indeed into the holy and worshipful Trinity, according to what is
possible, looking intently, and reckoning the unapproachable of the divine
glory through the excess of brightness, the translucent of blessedness, the
boundless of wisdom, the fixed and unwaved of undisturbance, the impassible
and unmoved nature. For to whom nothing from the unexpected could fall
together, as having treasured the knowledge of all the things that are and
that will be, and having grasped the wholes, and having all things under hand,
and nothing being able even at all to fall against and look against; to this
it is consistent that calm and continual undisturbance be present. For the
sudden circumstances of unhoped things are naturally able to produce the
disturbances in the thoughts of men. So that he whom no vice annoys, but every
virtue and everything that is beautiful follows, this one would reasonably be
gladdened with a gladness unmoved and endless. For the attendant of virtue and
goodness is gladness, as the Prophet says: The Lord will be gladdened upon his
works. A soul then that guards its intellectual in sobriety and in the fitting
workings will both be established in the aforementioned contemplations, and
will train its own character toward the straight and just, and the orderly and
peaceful. But whenever it ceases from thinking and looking intently into the
due contemplations,


then, as disorderly and bold puppies that have thrown off the overseer, the
passions of the body having risen, bark much against the soul; and each of the
passions attempts variously to tear it, partitioning its vital power toward
itself. For I at least think the power of the soul to be twofold, one and the
same existing; the one vital of the body, but the other =31.1341=
contemplative of the things that are, which indeed we also name reasoning. But
the vital power, since the soul has been mixed with the body, it furnishes
naturally through the mixing, and not from choice; for as it is not possible
for a sun that has shone not to light this, against which it brought the rays;
so it is impossible for a soul not to make alive a body, in which it has come
to be; but the contemplative power has the movement in choice. If then through
everything it prepares its contemplative and reasoning to be awake, as the
Prophet says: Nor let the one who guards you nod; it puts the passions of the
body to sleep doubly, both by the contemplation concerning the better and
connatural things with which it is occupied, and overseeing the undisturbance
of the body, it makes these temperate and settles them; but if, having
embraced idleness, it has the contemplative unmoved, the vital having found
the passions of the body at leisure, and having been partitioned, no one
overseeing and cutting back, they have dragged the soul down together upon the
proper impulses and workings. So that the passions of the body are violent,
when the reason in us is idle; but easily persuaded, when this orders and
manages. The body then is not blameworthy to those who wish to know rightly
concerning it. For it is fitting to the setting forth of the counsel also to
take away the evil opinions of those who have supposed wickedly concerning the
body. For as, beloved, a horse is beautiful, and by how much more it is by
nature sharper and hotter, by so much better; but it needs the one who will
drive and will steer, as being without share of reasonings. And when the
charioteer has mounted, it itself will try to use the nature. If then the
charioteer manages the impulses of the beast of burden as is due, he has both
used it toward what profits himself, and has obtained the aim that lies
before, and he himself has been saved, and the beast of burden has been seen
most excellent in the use; but whenever the charioteer leads the colt badly,
both the colt has often turned aside from the highway, and has fallen out into
pathlessness; and having been brought down it has sometimes also brought down
the rider himself, and the idleness of the charioteer becomes a danger of
both. Thus also upon the soul and the body suppose. For the body received the
natural impulses not unseemly, but toward something wholly beautiful and
useful; but this is without share of reasoning =31.1344=, in order that the
soul may be honored with the advantages of the word. For if it itself manages
the impulses of the body as is due, it has both saved it, and itself outside
of dangers; but if it neglects the overseeing, and having been held by a sleep
of idleness, it lets go the driving of the body, both it, as empty of
reasonings, turns from the straight, and has thrown the soul around with the
equal stumblings, not from proper vice, but through that one's slightness. For
if the passions of the body were such as not to be tamed by a soul, the body
would reasonably have the causes; but if they have become subject to many who
have been eager to master, the body is without blame to those who attempt to
slander it as a beginner of vice, but blameworthy of the slightness is the one
that has let go the authority of the body, not itself having the evil in
itself by nature, but being established in this by the leaving of the
beautiful. For vice is nothing other than a leaving of virtue.

CHAPTER 3. That one must not make the meetings of women unguardedly.




Concerning the attending of the reasonings, and the driving of the passions of
the body, and the spaciousness and calm of the inner man, and the
contemplation and eagerness of the things due to him, we have spoken
sufficiently before, as was possible. But one must not only oversee the
reasonings, but also be separated from the company of these, as far as
possible, especially as many as coming near to us and leading us into a
reminder of the passions, disturb and confuse the reasoning, and produce wars
and contests in the soul. For to receive the war that falls upon us
unwillingly would be necessary; but to create a willing one for ourselves most
unreasonable. For in the former even the defeat perhaps will obtain
forgiveness, but may it be far from the athletes according to Christ; but in
this the being captured, besides being laughable, is also deprived of
forgiveness. One must then also flee the companies and meetings of women as
much as possible, unless some unavoidable necessity should force the meeting.
And even when necessity has overtaken, it is fitting to be guarded as from
fire, and to make the sharpest and quickest deliverances. And consider what
Wisdom says concerning this: Will someone bind fire in a bosom, and will the
garments not burn? or will someone walk upon coals of fire, and will the feet
not burn? =31.1345= And if someone says, from meeting with women, and living
together still more, not to be harmed, this one either has not shared in the
male nature, and is some most paradoxical possession, standing in the
mid-space of either nature (such as they say the eunuchs from birth, if indeed
we should grant even to those the impassible and unmoved toward the female;
For the desire of a eunuch is to unvirgin a young girl, says the Wise); or, if
he has shared, being baptized by the passions, he does not perceive, imitating
those who are drunk or in frenzy, who, having suffered the most terrible
things, think they are outside of passion. But let us grant to the word the
thing outside of word, that there is someone not pricked by the male passion.
But even if he himself does not suffer, he would not easily persuade also the
others that he would not suffer. And to scandalize the many, no accomplishment
lying before, I think is not without danger for the one who does it. Then
there is another thing to see, that even if the man does not receive harm in
the reasonings, yet he will not already also dispute with us concerning the
woman, that she herself also is outside of the passions of the body; but often
that one, being weak in the reasoning, and having the passion sharp-turning,
has suffered something upon the one who made the meetings unguardedly. And he
himself has not been wounded, but he has often wounded, as he does not know.
And in a pretense supposedly of spiritual love the little woman coming
continually toward the ascetic, begins through eyes to be filled with the sin,
and with unchaste eyes to graze upon the form of the neighbor, and to corrupt
the inner virgin, whom especially the bridegroom loves, with the unclean
reasonings. In order then that none of the things said may happen, it is
fitting to be guarded, if possible, even wholly, but if not, at least the
frequent and stretched companies of women and meetings, not as hating the
race. far be it! nor as denying their kinship, but even standing before and
helping the possible things, every one who shares the human nature, and
especially those who have taken up the contests for chastity, as competing
together with us as sisters; but being guarded in the meeting, lest it become
a reminder of a passion, which we have declined and which we have forbidden.

CHAPTER 4. That one must measure continence by the power of the body; and that

carried worthily from that one alone. But we, having preferred the working
through glory of men to the having been eager through God at the beautiful
things, from those =31.1373= demanding the empty wage of the good-speaking,
reasonably and justly fall away from the divine exchanges, not toiling for
God, but working ourselves for the men; from whom carrying the damage of the
wages instead of wages, what also shall we demand from God, for whom we chose
beforehand to work nothing? And that this is true, hear the holy Gospel
saying, concerning those who do the beautiful things through men: Amen I say
to you, they have their wage. Let us then flee empty glory, the sweet stripper
of the spiritual wealth, the pleasant enemy of our souls, the moth of the
virtues, the one that with pleasure plunders our beautiful things, the one
that has anointed the poison of its own deceit with honey, and that stretches
to the thoughts of men the destructive cup, in order that, I think, they may
be filled of the passion insatiably. For human glory is sweet to the
uneducated. Otherwise also it prepares those who have been captured easily to
miss of a straight judgment. For the one who desires this has dissolved the
proper judgments; and he has thought this honorable, whatever is admired from
those who happen by. As, if being wicked or unreasonable, they should admire
the evil things, the one being praised will wholly do this, which he has
thought the judges of his own practices to have supposed beautiful; so that
the empty glory is not only a destroyer of the beautiful deeds, but also a
leader of the wicked. For which reason it is fitting, looking toward the
straight word, and toward God the leader of the straight word, to walk this
way, wherever God may lead; and if some praise such a way, not to set their
praise as some great thing, looking toward the praiser from above, but only to
rejoice with them of the straight judgment concerning the beautiful things;
and if others blame, in no way to sink, but to count those wretched who are
without share of a straight judgment, and who endure the most terrible
darkness of thought.

CHAPTER 11. Concerning timeliness of words.

These then are the heading things of the accomplishments, which have been
spoken not toward the greatness of the proper virtue, but toward the measure
of our power; but the virtues in sequence, as many as adorn the ready
character, I think it easy for all to look down upon, such as timeliness of
words and a bringing-forth toward the useful. And useful would be either to
converse concerning virtue in =31.1376= time, or to make the words toward the
present and pressing need, or even wholly toward the building of those who
hear; but to decline the remaining as superfluous and unprofitable.

CHAPTER 12. That the ascetic must not look toward facetiousness.

It is fitting to hold off from all facetiousness. For it happens many things
that those occupied about such things miss of the straight word, the soul
being poured toward jesting, and dissolving the collected and compacted of
prudence. And often the evil going by a way has also dissolved into shameful
speech and the last unseemliness, so that sobriety of soul and a pouring of
facetiousness do not happen at the same time. And if somewhere it should also
be needed, so as to let up the gloomy a little, to be brightened through the
words, let your word be full of spiritual grace, and with the


evangelical salt seasoned, in order that it may carry the fragrance of the
inner wise management, and may gladden the hearer doubly, both by the
relaxation and by the grace of the understanding.

CHAPTER 13. Concerning meekness, and in what manner love is constituted.

The ascetic must be as full as possible of meekness; since of the spirit of
meekness he has either shared, or desires to share. And it is fitting that the
host of this resemble the one being hosted. And if somewhere also indignation
should be needed against the idle, clearly the one ordered under us, let the
indignation be mixed with reasoning. For also swords both murderers use, and
children of physicians use. But the ones, since they handle the sword with
wrath and cruelty, do the most unseemly of all things, destroying the
same-born; but the others, since they come to the working through a sword with
reasoning, benefit the greatest from here; for they save those who are in
danger. Thus also the one who has known to be indignant with word has
benefited greatly this one, against whom he made the indignation, setting
right idleness or wickedness; but the one held by a passion of wrath works
nothing sound. And that even to those who take care of meekness the timely
indignation is fitting, is clear from here. For also Moses, having been
witnessed meekest of all men, when the time was calling, was indignant, and
went forward into so great a movement, as to define the indignation by a
murder of the same-tribe; this indeed, when they were fashioning the calf,
this, when they were defiled with Beelphegor. So that =31.1377= it is
possible, even being meek, to be warmed with word, and not to corrupt the
beautiful of the meekness. But to remain unmoved, or not to be indignant as is
due, would be of an idleness of the nature, not of meekness. And to the
meekness the forbearing is somehow naturally following. For meekness is a
mother of forbearance. And no less to those who are truly meek, but not struck
in the character by heaviness, the kind also has been mixed. For kindness is a
matter of meekness. And these, being mixed with one another and being
composed, complete the strongest of the virtues, the love.

CHAPTER 14. Concerning prudence.

It is fitting indeed for prudence to lead all the things that are done. For
without prudence everything that, even if it should seem to be beautiful, has
fallen over into vice through the untimeliness and the unmeasure. And when
word and prudence define for the good things the time and the measure, some
wondrous gain of their use comes to be both for those who give and for those
who receive.

CHAPTER 15. Concerning faith and hope.

Let faith the one into God lead all the undertakings, and let the well-hoping
follow, in order that by the faith we may prop the power of the soul, and by
the well-hoping we may be eager toward the beautiful things. For neither will
the undertaking of men concerning the beautiful things be completed without
the help from above; nor would the grace from above come to be upon the one
who is not eager; but it is fitting that both be mixed together, both human
eagerness and the alliance coming down from above through the faith into a
completion of virtue.




CHAPTER 16. Concerning humility.

Upon all the things accomplished from us let the soul carry up the causes of
the accomplishment upon the Master, reckoning that it accomplishes nothing at
all from proper power. For such a disposition is naturally able to produce the
humility in us. And humility is a treasure-guard of virtues. These to you from
us, as one might say, seeds of the words concerning virtue; but you, having
taken from here, work for us the fruit much-heaped, and fulfill the word of
the Wisdom, the one that commands to give starting-points to the wise, in
order that they may become wiser.

CHAPTER 17. According to how many manners the wicked reasonings are
constituted in a soul.

And since having already gone first we examined the things concerning the
=31.1380= reasonings, but we did not divide according to how many manners the
wicked thoughts grow upon the straight reasonings, now we have thought it just
also to add this, so that the examination of the part may be most complete.
Two manners then, according to which the unseemly thoughts annoy the straight
reasonings; either of the soul according to proper slightness wandering about
the things not fitting, and meeting with unreasoning fantasies from fantasies,
or according to a plot of the devil attempting to show under unseemlinesses of
things to the thought, and to lead this away from the contemplation and
consideration of the praiseworthy things. Whenever then the soul, having
loosened the compact and intense of the thought, brings up the memories that
happen and of the things that happen, then the reasoning, being borne
uneducatedly and unknowingly toward the things remembered of the things, and
being occupied still more with these, goes over from wanderings into long
wanderings, and often turns the last into shamefulnesses and unseemlinesses of
thoughts. But such a slightness of the soul and pouring one must set right and
lead back by a more compact and more turning attending of the thought, and
occupy it besides with the always present consideration concerning the
beautiful things. But whenever the devil attempts to plot, and with much
vehemence as some darts that have been fired forces the reasonings from
himself into a soul that is quiet and at rest, and to set this suddenly on
fire, and to work the memories of the things once thrown in long-timed and
hard-to-loosen; then one must by sobriety and a more intense attention stand
out from under such plots, just as some athlete by most exact guarding and
speed of body turning aside the grips of the opponents; and give the whole to
the prayer and to the calling-upon of the alliance from above, the taking-down
of the war and the turning-away of the arrows. For this Paul taught us saying:
Upon all having taken up the shield of the faith, in which you will be able to
quench all the darts of the wicked that have been fired. Even if then in the
prayers themselves he should throw under the wicked fantasies, let the soul
not stand off from praying, nor let it think the wicked sowings of the enemy,
and the fantasies of the various wonder-worker, to be proper farmings; but
having reckoned that the fantasy of the unseemly thoughts comes to be by the
shamelessness of the finder of the wickedness, let it rather stretch the
falling-toward, and =31.1381= let it supplicate God that the wicked wall of
the memory of the unseemly reasonings be scattered for it; so as unhinderedly
by the impulse of the thought, timelessly, and sharp-turning, to pass over
toward God, nowhere being cut by the approaches of the wicked thoughts. And if
also such


insolence of the reasonings should be stretched through the shamelessness of
the one who wars toward, not even thus is it fitting to give up, nor to leave
the contests half-worked; but to endure until so much, until God, having seen
our standing, shines the grace of the Spirit, driving the plotter to flight,
and purifying our thought, and filling it with divine light, and furnishing
the reasoning to serve God with gladness in an unwaved calm.

CHAPTER 18. Toward the canonicals in community.

Concerning then the each-one ascetic, and the one who has embraced the
solitary life, in the things before, as was possible, we have made clear; how,
both training the soul toward the beautiful, and managing the body toward what
is due, he would be able to characterize for us the exact philosopher; but
since the most of the ascetics live according to systems, sharpening the
thoughts for one another toward virtue, and by the comparison of the
accomplishments urging themselves upon the progress of the beautiful, we
thought it just, also to these to bring in the exhortation through the words.
And one must, having first known them, of how great and how large a beautiful
they lay claim, thus receive the turning-toward upon this, in order that they
may show an eagerness and zeal worthy of the virtue of the accomplishment.
First then they come back upon the beautiful according to nature, embracing
the communion and the living-together. For a most complete communion of life I
call, in which a property of possession has been banished, and an opposition
of judgment has been driven away, and every disturbance, and contention, and
strifes have stood out of the way; and the all things are common, souls,
judgments, bodies, and as many things as the bodies are nourished and treated
with; common the God, common the merchandise of piety, common the salvation,
common the contests, common the toils, common the crowns, the many one, and
the one not alone, but in more. What is equal to this way of life? and what
more blessed? what more exact of the joining and of the union? what more
graceful of the mixing of the characters and of the souls? Men from various
=31.1384= races and countries having been moved have been fitted together into
so great an exactness =31.1384= of identity, so that one soul is contemplated
in many bodies, and the many bodies are shown instruments of one judgment. The
one weak in the body has many who toil together by the disposition; the one
sick and falling down in the soul has many who heal and raise him together.
Equal-slaves of one another, masters of one another, and in the unfought
freedom they show in return to one another the more exact slavery, which a
necessity of circumstance did not bring upon toward force, carrying much
despondency to those captured, but the self-chosen of judgment created with
gladness; love subjecting the free to one another, and guarding the free by
the self-chosen. Such God has wished us to be from the beginning, and upon
these he created. These call back the ancient beautiful, covering the sin of
the first-father Adam. For division, and standing-apart, and war would not be
in men, if the sin had not cut through the nature. These are exact imitators
of the Savior and of the way of life according to him in flesh. For as that
one, having constituted a chorus of disciples, furnished all things common,
and himself common to the apostles; so also these obeying the one who leads,
those at least who well guard the rule of the life, have imitated the way of
life of the apostles and of the Lord with exactness. These have emulated the
life of the angels, having guarded the communal, as those, through exactness.
There is not in angels strife, not contention, not dispute; each has the
things of all, and all store up complete the beautiful things with themselves.
For the wealth of the


angels is not a circumscribed matter, needing a cutting, whenever it needs to
be distributed upon more; but the possession is immaterial, and the wealth of
thought. And through this the beautiful things remaining complete with each
work all equally-honored rich, furnishing them their own possession undisputed
and unfought. For contemplation of the highest beautiful, and a most clear
grasping of virtues, is a treasuring of the angels, toward which it is
possible for all to look, each taking their knowledge and their possession
complete. Such are the truly ascetics, not laying claim of the earthly, but
disputing the heavenly, and by undivided distributions all the same things
with themselves each treasuring complete. For the possession is of virtue, and
the wealth of accomplishments, and a greed that is praised, and a tearless
seizing, and an insatiability that is crowned, and the one who is not forced
is liable. All seizing, and no one being wronged, and through this peace
arbitrating for the wealth. These seize beforehand the good things of the
promised kingdom, presenting their virtuous way of life and communion as an
exact imitation of the conduct =31.1385= and state there. These have
accomplished the possessionlessness completely, having nothing proper, but the
all things of one another. Of how great good things the becoming-man of the
Savior has become a provider, these have clearly shown by the life of men,
gathering the torn-apart nature of men, and cut into countless, as far as upon
them, again toward itself and toward God. For this is the heading of the
management of the Savior according to flesh, in order that he may gather the
human nature toward itself and toward himself, and having taken out the wicked
cutting, may call back the ancient union; just as some best physician binding
together again a body divided according to many parts with saving drugs. And
these I have gone through, not in order that I may be ambitious of something,
and by the words lift the accomplishments of the cenobites (for there is not
so great a power of my words, as to make the great things solemn, but the
contrary rather even to darken by the weakness concerning the narration), but
in order that, as far as possible, I may go through and show the height and
greatness of the accomplishment. For what would come into equal with this
beautiful through a comparison? Where there is one father imitating the Father
above, and many children having been eager to conquer one another in the
good-wills concerning the president, children both being of one mind toward
themselves, and receiving the father with the virtuous deeds, not writing
nature as a cause of the joining, but having made a word more firm than nature
a beginner and guard of the union, and being held by a bond of holy Spirit.
What image of the things upon the earth would they have into a presentation of
the virtue of the accomplishment? But of the things upon the earth none; but
only the one above remains. Impassible the Father above; without passion this
one also, leading all by a word. Incorrupt the children of the Father above;
incorruption has also adopted these. Love binds the things above; love has
also fitted these toward one another. Truly even the devil himself gives up
toward such a phalanx, not sufficing toward so many contestants, campaigning
thus well-equipped and compacted against that one, and so much having locked
shields with one another by the love and being fenced by the Spirit, so as not
to furnish even some shortest sneaking-in to his throws. Consider for me the
harmonious contest of the seven Maccabees, and you will find with these the
harmony hotter. Concerning these David the prophet cried out in songs saying:
See then, what is beautiful, or what pleasant, but the dwelling of brothers
upon the same? presenting by the beautiful the approved of the life, and by
the pleasant the gladness from the oneness of mind and from the union. This
=31.1388= those who pursue the life with exactness, it seems to me, have
emulated the highest virtue.




CHAPTER 19. That the ascetic must go toward the ascesis with a firm judgment,
and concerning obedience.

For those then who are seeing in the mind, in the pre-narration of the
properties of the way of life the word defined the rule of the way of life.
And since it is fitting through the simpler of the brothers also to set forth
more clearly the rule of the way of life upon each, upon this then let us also
turn. One must then before all things the one who has come toward such a way
of life, have the thought supported, and seated, and unmoved, and a judgment
unattemptable and untransferable to the spirits of the wickedness, and show a
standing of martyrs by the firmness of the soul until death, both holding to
the commandments of God, and showing the obedience to those who lead. For this
is the heading of the way of life. For as God, being Father of all, and
counting it worthy to be called, demands from his servants the most exact
obedience; so also the spiritual father in men, fitting his own ordinances
toward the laws of God, demands the undisputed obedience. For if the one
giving himself over to some art of the banausic, these indeed the useful to us
toward the present life, in every manner obeys the artisan, and in nothing
opposes his commands, nor even is separated from this toward the shortest, but
every time is in the eyes of the teacher, and endures both food and drink and
the other diet this, which that one would define; much more those who come to
the learning of the piety and of the equality, having once persuaded
themselves, that they will be able to take such a science from the one who
leads, will bring in to these all obedience and the most exact obedience
concerning all things; and they will not demand the word of the things
commanded; but they will complete the work that has been commanded, unless so
much, as being ignorant of something of the things stretching toward
salvation, to ask and to be educated orderly and with the fitting respect. And
let all eagerness of the man be, how the high =31.1389= of the soul may not be
humbled through the rising of the pleasures. For how would the soul that has
been nailed below to the pleasure of the flesh still be able to look up toward
the connatural and noetic light with a free eye? For which reason one must
before all things practice the continence, being a safe guard of temperance,
and not allowing the leading mind to roam here and there. For just as the
water roofed around through pipes is borne upright upon the above by the force
that presses up, not having where it would be poured; so also the human mind,
as of a watertight pipe of the continence of them squeezing around from
everywhere, will somehow be taken up by the nature of being moved toward the
desire of the higher things, not having where it may flow through. For it is
not possible ever to stand for the ever-moved that has received the nature
from the one who made; and being hindered from being moved into the vain
things, it is impossible not wholly to go a straight way toward the truth. And
we think continence to be defined not only the holding-off from foods (for
this many even of the philosophers among Greeks have accomplished), but before
all things the roaming of the eyes. For what is the profit, if, holding off
from foods, you eat an unchastity of adultery through the eyes, or with the
ears you willingly hear vain and devilish voices? There is no profit to hold
off from foods, but not to hold off from a conceit of high-mindedness and
empty glory and every passion. Or what at least is the profit to be continent
from foods, and not to hold off from wicked and vain reasonings? For which
reason also the Apostle was saying: I fear lest somehow our thoughts be
corrupted. Let us then become continent from all these, lest ever


reasonably also upon us the blame of the Lord should come, as straining out
the gnat, but swallowing the camel.

CHAPTER 20. That one must not pursue the companies toward the worldly kin, or
take care of their things.

From kin indeed and friends and parents it is fitting to have been separated
so much by the disposition, as we see the dead having stood off from the
living. For also the one who has truly stripped toward the contests according
to virtue, and has bid farewell to the whole world with all those in the
world, and has crucified himself, in order that I may say the full, to the
world, has died to the world, and to all those in the world, even if they are
parents, even if brothers, even if holding a third or fourth or many-th fold
degree of the kinship. If then the ones who have begotten should change from
the life, and should rearrange toward the way of life of the child, they are
truly kin; not holding the rank of parents, but that of brothers. For a most
true father is, first the Father of all; and the second after that one the one
who leads the spiritual way of life. But if the kin should remain having
embraced the former life, they are a part of the world, from which we have
been separated, =31.1392= and in no way belonging to those who have put away
the man according to flesh, and have stripped the intimacy toward those. And
the one who embraces the friendship of the worldly with exactness, and desires
to company with these continually, has housed their dispositions into the soul
through the continual company. And again having been filled the thought with
the worldly mind, he has fallen away from the good standing, and has been
separated from the spiritual mind, and has wiped the former vomit upon the
soul, and has been wounded by the enemy, who has muddied the spiritual way of
life through the fleshly kinship. For the kin then let us pray the best
things, righteousness, I say, and piety, and these things, which we have made
honorable. For these it is fitting for us to pray, and for those to harvest
from us is profitable. But we ourselves let us loosen the reasonings from the
care and anxiety concerning those. For the devil, having seen us having
stripped every worldly care, and running well-equipped upon the heaven, having
worked in us the thought concerning the kin, and having prepared us to take
care of their things, also prepares the reasoning to be anxious into the
worldly cares; what is the abundance of the household, and if self-sufficient,
or lacking, and what the gains that have come to them from the contracts, and
upon how much increasing the wealth; and what damages falling from the
circumstances of the life, and how much lessening the existing abundance. And
he makes us rejoice together with their well-resourcings, and be despondent
together with their missings, and hate together with the enemies (and this,
being commanded to have no enemy), and rejoice with the friends who are often
not worthy of the spiritual intimacy, and even to rejoice upon their unjust
and unreasonable gains, and all the wicked dispositions of the worldly
subjects, which having stripped, we housed the spiritual thoughts, he again
introduces to the reasonings, and having again worked the material and worldly
mind in the soul, he dissolves the inner ascetic, and prepares only a statue
of an ascetic to carry around the figure, nowhere having been ensouled with
the virtues. And often through the stretched good-wills concerning the kin the
ascetic has dared even to work a temple-robbery, so as to treat the lack of
the kin. For the things stored for the saints, those who have dedicated
themselves to God, are thought and taken as holy and truly dedicated things.
He then who appropriates something of these is one of those who dare
temple-robbery. Knowing then of the


disposition concerning the household the unbearable harm, let us flee the care
concerning those, as being a devilish weapon. For also the Lord himself
forbade such a relation and custom, to one of the disciples not even
permitting, not even this only to take leave of the household; and to another
=31.1393= not even upon the body of the father who had died to heap earth. For
to the one wishing to take leave of the household he says: No one having
thrown his hand upon a plow, and looking into the things behind, is
well-placed into the kingdom of the heavens; and to the one calling to bury
the father, Follow me, he says, and, Leave the dead to bury their own dead.
And yet they were seeming to make most reasonable and most just the requests
either; but the Savior did not nod, nor even toward the instant at least did
he allow his nurslings of the kingdom of the heavens to be separated from him,
lest they should either do, or reckon, something more humble of the high and
heavenly mind by the inclination concerning the earthly and fleshly
dispositions, as it is not possible, for those who think the heavenly things
to have some account of the things upon earth, as already having changed and
having become above-world in the thought. And if someone should say, that How
then does the law command to take care of the household, saying: And from the
household of your seed you will not overlook; and similarly also the Apostle:
And if someone does not provide for his own, and especially of the household,
he has denied the faith, and is worse than an unbeliever, we will furnish a
short answer; that the divine Apostle has said this toward the worldly, toward
those who have the material wealth, and are able to console the lack of the
kin. And similarly also the law says these things, and in order that I may say
more shortly, toward the living, not toward the dead; for these at least are
without blame of every such debt. But you have died, and have been crucified
to the whole world. For having bid farewell to the material wealth, you have
loved the possessionlessness; and having dedicated yourself to God, you have
become a treasure of God. As dead, you are free of every contribution
concerning the kin; as possessionless, you have nothing which you will
furnish. And having even fruit-borne the body itself, and no longer having the
authority even of that as of a dedicated thing, it is not lawful for you to be
into a use of men; but only with those of like manner as also themselves
dedicated the whole to God to live together. Whence then will the remembered
sayings of the holy Scripture fit you? or how will you not sin setting aside
your own confessions concerning the ascesis?

CHAPTER 21. That one must not be cut off from the spiritual brotherhood.

One must indeed also have been persuaded this clearly, that the one who has
once come into a bond and joining of a spiritual brotherhood would not be able
to be cut off and to be separated from these, to whom he has been fitted. For
if men often coming together into a communion of this material =31.1396= life
would not be able to be separated beside the things agreed, or the one who
does this will be liable to the defined penalties; much more the one who has
come into a compact of a spiritual living-together that has acquired the
joining insoluble and eternal would not be able to divide and to cut himself
off from these, with whom he has been mixed. Or doing this, he subjects
himself to the heaviest penalties from above. For if a woman, who has come
into a communion of a man, and has the joining of him fleshly, if she should
be caught plotting against this one, is condemned to death; how much more the
one fitted by a spiritual communion under the Spirit itself as witness and
mediator becomes liable upon the separation? As then the members of the body,
fitted together by a bond of nature, would not be able to be torn from the
body, or, if it should be torn, the thing being torn would become dead; so
also an ascetic being joined to a brotherhood, and by a bond stronger than
nature by the joining of the Spirit


being held, would not have authority to be cut from these, to whom he has been
fitted. Or doing this, he is dead in the soul, and deprived of the grace of
the Spirit, as having set aside the compact upon it. And if someone should
say, that some of the brothers are base; for he will not charge all; for they
have not made the communion upon vice, so that all are in harmony evil; if
then he should say, that some of the brothers are base, and unguardedly
loosening the good, and neglecting the orderliness, and overlooking the
exactness fitting to ascetics, and through this it is fitting to be separated
from such, he has not thought this a sufficient defense of the separation. For
neither Peter, or Andrew, or John through the wickedness of Judas were torn
from the remaining chorus of the apostles, nor did some other of the apostles
make this a starting-point of standing-off, nor was anything toward the
obeying Christ hindered by that one's wretchedness; but obeying the teachings
of the Lord, they were emulating the piety and the virtue, not indeed turning
toward that one's wickedness. Thus the one who says, that Through the base I
am compelled to be separated from a spiritual joining, has not found a
reasonable pretext of his own unfirmness; but he himself is rocky earth, not
being able to nourish out the word of the truth through unfirmness of
judgment, but through an approach of a small trial, or an incontinence of
passions, not enduring the temperate way of life, and straightway is dried by
the burning of the passions the newly-grown sprout =31.1397= of the teaching;
and thinking up random pretexts and not sufficient toward a defense upon the
tribunal of Christ according to his own fullness, and easily striking himself
aside. For nothing is easier than to deceive oneself, since each becomes a
judge granted to himself, judging the pleasant things to be profitable. Let
such a one then be condemned before a truth that judges, as being established
a subject of scandals to more, either by a wicked example always irritating
toward a zeal of the equal unseemly things, and becoming an heir of the woe.
And it profits him that a millstone of an ass be hung about his neck, and he
be sunk in the open sea of the sea. For whenever a soul has been accustomed to
a standing-off, it is filled with much incontinence, both greed and gluttony,
and falsehood and every ill-manner; and last it turns into the bottom of the
wickedness being pierced with the last evils. He then who is established a
leader of such, let him consider of how many souls' destruction he becomes
liable; who is not even able to suffice toward the accounts of his own soul.
For why does such a one not rather imitate the great Peter, and become an

CHAPTER 34. That the ascetic who lives in a system must not possess privately
something of the material things.

It is fitting indeed for the ascetic, the one who has chosen the communion set
forth before, to be free of every privatizing possession of the material
things. For if he does not do this, first he has corrupted the exact communion
by the privatizing of the possession; then also he has brought out great signs
of unbelief against himself, as disbelieving God, that he will not nourish
those who have been gathered upon his name; and not having heard from the
prophet David saying: I became younger, and =31.1425= for I grew old, and I
did not see a just one having been left, nor his seed seeking breads, either
the noetic of the prudence, or the sensible, with which the body is nourished.
For if where two or three have been gathered upon the name of Christ, he is in
their midst, much more where a much more and more many-manned gathering.
Either then nothing


will lack of the necessary things, Christ being present to us, if indeed not
even to the Israelites in the desert a lack of the useful things came to be;
or even if it should lack toward our testing, it is better to have lacking and
to be with Christ, than without the communion of that one to be rich in all
the things according to the life. And not until this does such a possession
stand the harm, but it leads further. For the one who has been eager to have
something proper studies nothing other than a separation and a standing-off.
For the one who does not look toward this what would he even possess? knowing,
that by the grace of Christ his servants continue always in an ungrudging of
all the necessary things. Clear then is such a one studying a cutting-off and
a putting-to-death of his own soul, and giving away his own salvation for the
short obols, and (grant me to dare a little) becoming some second Judas,
beginning from a theft (for the privatizing possession is a theft), and
turning into a betrayal; for he himself also betrays the word of the truth, as
that one the Lord. For whenever the rule of the life has neither to be
separated from the system to which we have been joined, nor to provide some
privatizing possession, nor at all to do something secret, or upon a harm of
the brothers becoming a wicked example to those who are being saved; then,
someone having overlooked the fear of God, and the ordinances of the holy
Spirit, first an appropriation (for an appropriation is the privatizing
possession however and from wherever), then also having thought a standing-off
and a separation; how has he not betrayed the word of the truth, and become a
second Judas, according to what is possible betraying the truth? One must then
in every manner flee to possess privately something beside the things provided
commonly to all together; and not only by this to keep the thought unspotted,
but also to cleanse out the inner man of all pollutions; this indeed of
unclean and wicked reasonings, which muddy the inner lodging of the Spirit;
this of deceit, and hypocrisy, and envy, and strife, which pull up the love
from the root, and drive God from the soul of such. For if God =31.1428= is
love, the one who does not have the love has been deprived of the divine
grace. And in all humility, and sanctification, and readiness concerning the
good deeds each guarding his own soul, to shut the reasoning toward the
present things, considering, how he may not, according to the present time,
miss of some of the willings of God, and tear the standing of the way of life.
For such a state of the soul will deliver us from every lifting, and in
reverence and a seated thought will prepare us to follow all the beautiful
things, establishing this also before all things in the soul, that God is
good, and a cause of all good things, but of evil not in any way whatever of
none, even if we taste of a sharper therapy toward the need of the
setting-right. For God being a physician of the human souls, toward the nature
of the diseases mixes a sufficient power of the drugs, having cleansed out a
deepest vice, whenever this should need. Knowing this at least clearly, let us
always give thanks, even if we perceive of a more vehement therapy, being
cleansed of the slightnesses. But to endure through trials and a testing is of
very few and Abraham-like men. These things then as many as the grace of God
furnished to the humility of our thought into an exhortation of you who have
embraced the communal life. Of which, as the word made clear, also our thought
saw, nothing either more brilliant, nor more graceful, nor higher would a way
of life among men be found. And since also to those who attend to the monastic
life we brought in the exhortation that is in us, and one and the same aim for
either, to please Christ, and the educations concerning these are akin, let us
also go upon the word of those. For perhaps something useful will also be
brought in together from there to this word, able to make brilliant still more
and to order the way of life.
