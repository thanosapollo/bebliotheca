OF GREGORY BISHOP OF NYSSA ON THE DAY OF THE LIGHTS
Now I recognize my own flock; today I see the accustomed form of the church,
when, having hated even the occupation of fleshly cares, you have run together
with the fullness intact to the service of God. And the people crowd the
house, coming within the sacred inner shrines, and they fill the outer space
in the precincts, as many as are not contained by those within, after the
image of bees: for some work within, and others from outside hum around the
hive. So then do, O children, and never abandon this zeal; for I confess that
I suffer a shepherd's passion, and I wish, sitting upon this high watchtower,
to see the flock gathered in a circle about the foot of the peak. And if it so
happens for me, I am filled with wonderful eagerness and I labor at the word
with pleasure, as the shepherds do at the pastoral songs; but when it is
otherwise and you are wandering the outer wandering, as you did just recently
on the past Lord's day, I lament much and I embrace silence, =9.222= and I
consider flight from here and I seek the Carmel of the prophet Elijah or some
uninhabited rock; for somehow the unsocial and desert-loving is dear to the
disheartened. But now, seeing you keeping festival all at once with the very
families, I am reminded of a prophetic saying, which Isaiah from afar hymned,
announcing beforehand the fair-childed and many-childed church of Christ: Who,
he says, are these? They fly as clouds and as doves with their young to me.
And not only this but also that: The place is narrow for me; make me a place,
that I may dwell. For these things the power of the Spirit prophesied toward
the populous church of God, which in later times was going to fill the whole
inhabited world from end to end. Time then has come, bringing and carrying
round the remembrance of holy mysteries that cleanse man, which both wash away
from soul and body even that sin which is hard to wash out, and bring back to
the original beauty which God the master craftsman formed upon us. And for
this reason you, the initiated people who have made trial of the goods of the
day, have been gathered, and you bring also the still inexperienced, as good
fathers, by practice and leading, guiding the uninitiated toward the perfect
grasp of piety. And I rejoice with both, with those who have been initiated,
because you have been enriched with a great gift, and with the uninitiated,
because you hope a fair expectation: remission of accounts, release of bonds,
kinship with God, free boldness of speech, instead of slavish =9.223=
lowliness equal honor with angels. For these things, and whatever follows from
them, the grace of the laver guarantees and gives. Therefore, setting aside
the other subjects of the Scriptures for other times, let us remain with the
aim set before us, bringing as gifts, as far as we are able, the things proper
and fitting to the feast; for what is its own is dear to each festival.

So we welcome a marriage with wedding songs, and to mourning we bring the
customary things with funeral laments; in affairs we speak seriously, at
feasts we loosen the tightness and strain of the soul, and we keep each time
unclouded by things alien to it. Christ then was born a few days ago, he who
was begotten before every substance, sensible and intelligible; today he is
baptized by John, that he might cleanse the one who was soiled, and might
bring the Spirit from above and raise man to the heavens, that the one who
fell might be raised and the one who cast him down might be put to shame (and
do not marvel if what concerns us became so much a matter of earnestness to
God), that he might himself work the salvation of man; with the care



of the one who did us wrong we were plotted against, and with the forethought
of the Maker we are saved. And the wicked and envious one, innovating sin
against our race, found the serpent a disguise worthy of his own mind, the
unclean entering into what was like himself, the earthly and chthonic in will
dwelling in the creeping thing; but Christ, correcting that one's wickedness,
assumes the complete man =9.224= and saves the man and becomes type and figure
of us all, that he might sanctify the firstfruits of every action and leave to
the servants the zeal of the tradition unambiguous. Baptism then is a
cleansing of sins, a remission of trespasses, a cause of renewal and
regeneration; and understand regeneration as one contemplated in thought, not
seen with eyes; for we shall not truly, according to the Hebrew Nicodemus and
that thicker mind of his, exchange the old man into a child, nor shall we
recast the wrinkled gray-haired one into the tender and young, or bring the
man back again into the womb of the mother, but the one stained with sins and
grown old in evil practices we bring back by royal grace to the unaccountable
state of the infant. For as the newborn child is free from accusations and
punishments, so also the child of regeneration has nothing about which to give
account, having been released from the accounts by royal gift. And this
benefaction water does not grant (for then it would be higher than all
creation), but the command of God and the visitation of the Spirit coming
mystically toward our freedom, and water serves toward the showing of the
cleansing. For since we are accustomed, washing with water the body insulted
by dirt and mire, to make it clean, for this reason we take it also in the
mystical action, showing the bodiless brightness by the sensible thing. Or
rather, if it seems good, let us also persevere more finely in the inquiry
concerning the laver, beginning as from some fountain of the scriptural
command: Unless one is born, it says, of water and Spirit, he cannot =9.225=
enter into the kingdom of God. Why the two, and why was the Spirit alone not
thought sufficient for the completion of baptism? Man is composite and not
simple, as we know accurately, and for this reason to the double and yoked
together the kindred and similar medicines were allotted for healing, to the
appearing body the sensible water, and to the invisible soul the unseen
Spirit, called by faith, present unspeakably. For the Spirit blows where it
wills, and you hear its voice, but you do not know whence it comes and where
it goes. It blesses the body that is baptized and the water that baptizes.

Therefore do not despise the divine laver nor cheapen it as common because of
the use of water; for the thing that operates is great, and from that the
things accomplished become wonderful. Since this holy altar also, at which we
stand, is stone common according to nature, in no way differing from the other
slabs which adorn our walls and beautify the floors, but when it has been
consecrated to the service of God and has received the blessing, it is a holy
table, an undefiled altar, no longer handled by all but by the priests alone,
and these with reverence. The bread again is still common bread, but when the
mystery has worked it as a priestly act, it is both called and becomes the
body of Christ. So the mystical oil, so the wine, being worth little before
the blessing, after the sanctification from the Spirit each of them operates
differently. And the same power of the word also makes the priest august and
=9.226= honorable by the newness of the blessing, separated from commonness
toward the many; for yesterday and the day before, being one of the many and
of the people, he is suddenly shown forth as leader, president, teacher of
piety, mystagogue of hidden mysteries; and he does these things with nothing
of the body or of the form changed, but being according to what appears the
one he was, yet by some invisible



power and grace having the invisible soul transformed toward the better. And
so, carrying the mind upon many of the things, you will see the appearances
contemptible, but the things accomplished from them great, especially when
from the ancient history you gather the kindred and similar of what is sought.
The rod of Moses was a hazel rod, and what else but common wood, which every
hand cuts and carries and shapes toward what seems good and gives to the fire
at will? But when God willed to accomplish through that the lofty wonders
better than speech, the wood was changed into a serpent; and again elsewhere,
striking the waters, at one time it made the water blood, at another it gushed
forth a brood of frogs; and again it cut the sea, divided down to the depths
and not flowing together. Likewise the mantle of one of the prophets, being a
goat's skin, made Elisha a word of the inhabited world. And the wood of the
cross is saving for all men, being a piece, as I hear, of a sycamore, a
contemptible plant and more dishonored than the many. And a bush of bramble
showed Moses the theophanies, and the =9.227= remains of Elisha raised a dead
man, and clay enlightened the one blind from the womb; and all these things,
being soulless and insensible materials, mediated the great wonders, having
received the power of God.

And according to the like sequence of reasonings water also, being nothing
else than water, renews the man into the intelligible regeneration, the grace
from above blessing it. And if again someone doubting and wavering gives me
trouble, continually asking and inquiring how water regenerates and the
mystagogy accomplished upon it, I will say to him most justly: set forth for
me the manner of the generation according to the flesh, and I will narrate to
you the power of the regeneration according to the soul. You will say perhaps,
as giving some account: the cause of seed makes the man. Hear then in return
also from us, that water being blessed cleanses and illuminates the man. And
if you again strike back at me with the how, I will cry against you more
vehemently: how does the moist and formless substance become a man? And thus
the word, advancing through all creation, will be exercised upon each thing:
how heaven? how earth? how sea? how the particulars? For everywhere the
reasoning of men, at a loss toward discovery, takes refuge in this syllable as
those unable to walk take refuge in a seat. And to speak concisely, everywhere
the power and operation of God is incomprehensible and not to be technically
told, easily bringing into being whatever it wills, but hiding from us the
minute knowledge of the operation. Therefore also the blessed David, once
setting his mind upon the magnificence of creation and filled in his soul with
perplexed wonder, uttered that =9.228= saying sung by all.

How were your works magnified, Lord; you made all things in wisdom. For he
conceived the wisdom, but the art of the wisdom he did not find. Leaving aside
then the busy inquiry into what is beyond human power, let us rather seek that
which also shows a partial grasp: for what reason is the cleansing through
water, and toward what need are the three immersions taken? What then the
fathers also taught, and our mind receiving it accepted, this it is. We
recognize four elements, from which the world has its constitution, known to
all even if the names are silenced; but if it is fitting, for the simpler, to
say the appellations also: fire and air, earth and water. Our God then and
Savior, fulfilling the economy for us, underwent the fourth of these, the
earth, that from there he might raise up life; and we, receiving baptism in
imitation of our Lord and teacher and guide, are not buried into earth (for
this becomes the covering of the body that has been wholly dead, wrapping the
weakness and corruption of our nature), but coming to the element akin to
earth, the water, in that



we hide ourselves as the Savior did in the earth, and having done this a third
time we image for ourselves the three-day grace of the resurrection; and these
things we do, not receiving the mystery in silence, but with the three holy
hypostases being named over us, in whom we have believed, upon whom we also
hope, from whom both our now being and our being again comes to us. Perhaps
you are vexed, you who =9.229= boldly fight against the glory of the Spirit,
and you are jealous of the Paraclete for the reverence with which he is
revered by the pious; but leaving off wrestling with me, resist the voices of
the Lord, if you can, which legislated the invocation of baptism for men. And
what does the Master's command say? Baptizing them into the name of the Father
and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit. How into the name of the Father?
Because he is beginning of all things. How into the Son? Because he is maker
of the creation. How into the Holy Spirit? Because he is perfective of all
things. We bow then to the Father, that we may be sanctified; we bow also to
the Son, that this very thing may come to be; we bow also to the Holy Spirit,
that we may become that which that one is and is called. There is no
difference of the sanctification, as if the Father sanctifies more, and the
Son less, and the Holy Spirit less than the two. Why then do you chop the
three hypostases into different natures and work three gods unlike one
another, receiving one and the same grace from all?

And since examples always make the word more lively toward the hearers, I want
to instruct the mind of those who blaspheme with an image, making clear from
earthly and lowly things the great things not seen according to sense. For if
it happened to you, having endured the misfortune of captivity, to be among
enemies, and to be a slave and to be distressed and to groan for the ancient
freedom which you had, and suddenly some acquaintances and citizens, having
come to be in the country of your masters and tyrants, freed you from the
constraint lying upon you, giving ransom equally and dividing the expense of
the money =9.230= in equal share among themselves, would you not, having
obtained that grace, look upon the three alike as benefactors and bring the
repayment of the ransom in equal shares, the zeal and the expense having come
to be in common for you, if indeed you are a just judge of the benefaction?
And these things so far as in examples; for it is not now our aim to set
straight the word of faith, but let us run back to the present time and the
subject set before us. For I find that it was not the gospels after the cross
that proclaimed the grace of baptism, but even before the incarnation of the
Lord the old Scripture everywhere prefigured the image of our regeneration,
not manifesting the form clearly, but showing beforehand in riddles the love
of God toward man. And as the lamb was proclaimed beforehand and the cross was
prophesied beforehand, so also baptism was indicated in act and in word; and
let us remind the lovers of beauty of the types; for the time of the feast
demands the remembrance as necessary. Hagar the maidservant of Abraham, whom
Paul also allegorizes speaking to the Galatians, having been sent out of the
house of the master because of the anger of Sarah (for it is a hard thing for
lawful wives when a slave is suspected toward the master) was being carried
desolate toward a desolate land, having at the breast the infant Ishmael. And
when she came to be in lack of the necessities =9.231= she herself also came
near death, and before her the child (for the water that was in the skin was
spent, since it was not possible that the synagogue, she who was still living
among the types of the ever-flowing fountain, should have self-sufficiency of
life), an angel appears from the unexpected and shows her a well of living
water, and drawing from there she saves Ishmael. See then a mystical type, how
from the very beginning through living water salvation came to the one
perishing, water that was not before, but given through an angel according to
grace.



And at a later time again Isaac had to be yoked to marriage, for whose sake
Ishmael also with the mother was driven from the paternal hearth. And the
household servant of Abraham, sent as matchmaker so as to betroth for the
master, finds Rebecca at the well, and the marriage that was going to make the
race of Christ took its beginning and first covenant upon water. And not only
this, but Isaac himself also, when he stood over the flocks, everywhere in the
desert dug wells, which the aliens blocked and filled up, as a type of the
later impious, as many as hindered the grace of baptism and stopped it up,
fighting against the truth; but nevertheless the martyrs and the priests,
digging wells, prevailed, and the gift of baptism flooded the whole inhabited
world.

And according to the same force of the word Jacob also, hastening toward
betrothal, met Rachel unexpectedly at the well. And a great stone lay upon the
well, which a multitude of shepherds coming together used to roll away and
then drew water for themselves and for the sheep, but Jacob alone rolls away
the stone =9.232= and waters the sheep of the betrothed. I think the thing is
a riddle and a shadow of what was to come. For what is the stone lying upon it
but Christ himself? Concerning whom Isaiah says: And I will cast into the
foundations of Zion a costly stone, honorable, elect; and Daniel likewise: A
stone was cut without a hand, that is, Christ was born without a man. For as
it is new and paradoxical that a stone should be cut from a rock without a
stonecutter and stoneworking tools, so it is beyond every wonder that a birth
from an unwedded virgin should be seen. The intelligible stone Christ then lay
upon the well, hiding in depth and mystery the laver of regeneration still
needing much time for revelation, as a long rope; and no one rolled away the
stone except Israel, who is mind seeing God. But he also draws up the water
and waters the sheep of Rachel, that is, having revealed a hidden mystery he
gives living water to the flock of the church; and add also that of the three
rods of Jacob; for from when the three rods were set beside the spring, from
then Laban the polytheist became poor, and Jacob rich and many-flocked. And
let Laban be allegorized as the devil and Jacob as Christ; for after baptism
Christ took all the flock of Satan and himself grew rich. And Moses the great,
being a fair infant still at the breast, having fallen under the common and
gloomy decree which the hard Pharaoh brought out against the male infants, was
exposed at the banks of the river, not naked, but having been cast =9.233=
into an ark (for it was necessary that the law be typically in a coffer), and
he was set down near the water; for the law neighbors grace, and the daily
sprinklings of the Hebrews, which a little later were going to be covered by
the perfect and wonderful baptism. And as it seems to the divine Paul, the
people itself also, having crossed the Red Sea, proclaimed the good tidings of
the salvation from water; the people passed through and the Egyptian king with
the army was sunk, and the mystery was prophesied through the deeds. For even
now, whenever the people comes to be in the water of regeneration, fleeing
Egypt, the wicked sin, it itself is freed and saved, but the devil with his
own servants (I mean the spirits of wickedness) is choked with grief and is
destroyed, considering the salvation of men his own misfortune.

These things then are sufficient for us toward confirmation of the present
treatise; yet the next things also must not be neglected by the lover of
beauty. For the people of the Hebrews, having suffered many things as we were
taught and having completed the wicked circuit of the desert, did not receive
the land of promise before, with Jesus guiding and governing the life for it
and directing it, it was brought across into the Jordan. And Jesus, having set
down the twelve stones in the stream, is clearly preparing beforehand the
twelve disciples, the servants of baptism. And the wonderful priestly work,
exceeding every



mind of men =9.234=, of the old Tishbite, what else does it do but indicate
in act beforehand the faith into Father and Son and Holy Spirit and
redemption? For when the whole people of the Hebrews, having trampled the
ancestral piety, fell into the polytheist error, and the king Ahab was played
with by idolatry, having as a wicked partner of life and all-defiled teacher
of impiety the ill-named Jezebel, the prophet, filled with the spiritual
grace, and having come to a meeting with Ahab, with the king and all the
people as witness, stood against the priests of Baal toward a wonderful and
extraordinary contest; and proposing to them to offer the ox without fire he
showed them ridiculous and wretched, praying in vain and crying to gods that
are not. At last, himself having called upon his own and true God, with more
excesses and additions he wonderfully accomplished the proposed contest; for
he did not simply having prayed bring the fire from heaven upon the woods
being dry, but having commanded the servants and ordered them to bring much
water, and having poured the jars a third time upon the split wood, by prayer
he kindled the fire from water, that from the natural opposition of the
elements, having run together unexpectedly into friendship and cooperation, he
might display with much excess the power of his own God.

These things Elijah clearly announced beforehand to us, through that wonderful
=9.235= priestly work, the mystagogy of baptism that was going to be
accomplished later; for the fire was kindled by water drawn upon it a third
time, so that it is shown that where the mystical water is, there also is the
Spirit that kindles, the warm, the fire-like, that burns the impious and
illuminates the faithful. And not only this, but his disciple Elisha also,
when Naaman the Syrian who had fallen sick with leprosy came to him as a
suppliant, having washed him in the Jordan cleanses the sick man, and by the
generic use of the water and by the particular baptism of the river clearly
showing beforehand what was to come; for Jordan alone of rivers, having
received in itself the firstfruits of the sanctification and of the blessing,
as from some spring of its own type conveyed to the whole world the grace of
baptism. These then are active and practical indications of the regeneration
through the laver. And let us now look at the prophecies through words and
voices. Isaiah then has cried saying: Wash, become clean, take away the
wickednesses from your souls. And David: Come to him and be enlightened, and
your faces shall not be ashamed. And Ezekiel, writing more clearly and more
plainly than the two, promises the fair announcement: And I will sprinkle upon
you clean water and you will be cleansed from all your uncleannesses and from
all your idols I will cleanse you and I will give you a new heart and =9.236=
a new spirit I will give you and I will take away the heart of stone from your
flesh and I will give you a heart of flesh and I will give my spirit in you.
And most clearly also Zechariah prophesies both Jesus clothed in the filthy
garment, the slavish and our flesh, and stripping him of the gloomy clothing
he adorns him with the clean and shining robe, teaching us through the iconic
example that in the baptism of Jesus we all, putting off the sins as a poor
and much-patched tunic, put on in exchange the holy and most fair one of
regeneration. And where shall we set also that saying of Isaiah crying to the
desert: Rejoice, thirsty desert, let the desert exult and let it bloom as a
lily, and the desolate places of the Jordan shall bloom and exult. For it is
clear that he does not announce the good tidings of gladness to soulless and
insensible places, but he tropes through the desert the parched and unadorned
soul, just as David also when he says: My soul as a waterless land to you, and
again: My soul thirsted toward God the strong the living. And again in



the gospels the Lord: If anyone thirsts, let him come to me and drink, and to
the Samaritan woman: Everyone who drinks from this water will thirst again,
but whoever drinks from the water which I will give him will not thirst unto
the age. And the honor of Carmel is given to the soul that has the likeness
toward the desert, that is, the grace through the Spirit. For since Elijah
dwelt on Carmel =9.237= (and the mountain became named and famed because of
the virtue of the one dwelling there) and John the Baptist, shining in the
spirit of Elijah, was sanctifying the Jordan, for this reason the prophet
declared that the honor of Carmel would be given to the river. And the glory
of Lebanon also, from the comparison of the high trees, he transferred upon
the river; for as that Lebanon has a sufficient occasion of wonder, the trees
themselves which it grows and nourishes, so the Jordan is glorified
regenerating men and planting them in the paradise of God, of whom according
to the psalmic voice, always blooming and luxuriant with the virtues, the leaf
will not fall away, and God, having received the fruit in season, will be glad
as a good gardener rejoicing in his own works. And the divine David,
prophesying also the voice which the Father sent forth from the heavens to the
Son being baptized, that he might lead the hearers toward the natural dignity
of the godhead, who until then were looking at the sensible lowliness
according to the man, wrote that in the book: The voice of the Lord upon the
waters, the voice of the Lord in magnificence. But the testimonies from the
divine Scriptures must be stopped at this; for the word would go to
infinity, if someone should wish, picking the particulars, to set them beside
one book.

And you all, as many as are adorned with the gift of regeneration and bear as
a boast the saving renewal, show me after the mystical grace the change of the
ways, and make known the difference of the transformation toward the better by
the purity of the way of life; for of the things that fall under the eyes
nothing is altered, the =9.238= characters of the body remain unchanged and
the forming of the seen nature is not exchanged, but there is need altogether
of some clear proof, through which we shall recognize the newly born man,
distinguishing by certain manifest signs the new from the old. And these I
think are the motions according to purpose of the soul, from which, separating
itself from the old custom, and cutting a newer way of life, it will teach
clearly those who know it, that another has been born from another, dragging
no mark of the old wickedness. And this is the manner of the remaking, if,
persuaded by me, you keep the word as a law: the man before baptism was
unrestrained, greedy, a snatcher of others' things, a reviler, a slanderer,
and whatever is like these and follows from them. Let him now become orderly,
temperate, content with his own and from these sharing with those in poverty,
a lover of truth, honorable, approachable, simply practicing every praised
action; for as darkness is loosed by the light and the black disappears when
whiteness has been spread over it, so also the old man, adorned with the works
of righteousness, disappears. You see how Zacchaeus also by the change of life
killed the tax-collector, bringing back to those unjustly damaged a fourfold
restitution, and he divided to the poor the remainders, which formerly he had
stored badly from the poor being oppressed. Matthew the evangelist, another
tax-collector, of the same craft as Zacchaeus, immediately after the call, as
some mask, exchanged the life. Paul was a persecutor, but after the grace an
apostle, bearing heavy chains for Christ toward apology and repentance of the
unjust bonds, which once having taken from the =9.239= law he used to carry
against the gospels. Such it is fitting that the regeneration be, thus to wipe
out the custom toward sin, thus that the sons of God live as citizens; for
after the grace we hear that we are children of that one. And for this reason
it is fitting accurately



to look upon the properties of our begetter, that forming and shaping
ourselves toward likeness of the father we may appear genuine children of the
one who called us to the adoption according to grace; for a base accusation is
the bastard and the substituted, who on the works belies the paternal
nobility. Therefore it seems to me that the Lord himself also in the gospels,
arranging for us the canons of life, uses those words toward those being
discipled: Do well to those who hate you, pray for those who insult you and
persecute you, that you may become sons of your Father who is in the heavens,
because he makes his sun rise upon wicked and good and rains upon just and
unjust. For he says they then become sons, whenever they stamp in their own
reasonings the likeness of the paternal goodness in the love of man toward
those of the same race. For this reason also after the dignity of the adoption
the devil also plots against us more vehemently, being whetted with an envious
eye, whenever he sees the beauty of the newly born man hastening toward the
heavenly way of life, from which that one fell, and he raises against us fiery
temptations, striving to plunder even the second adornment as the former
=9.240= adornment. But when we perceive his assaults it is fitting to say over
ourselves the apostolic saying: As many as were baptized into Christ, into his
death we were baptized. And if we have become conformed to the death, sin is
altogether henceforth dead in us, having been run through by the spear of
baptism as that fornicator by the zealous Phinehas. Flee then from us,
ill-named one; for you want to shake a corpse, the one who long ago enlisted
with you, the one who long ago lost the senses toward the pleasures. A corpse
does not desire bodies, a corpse is not taken by wealth, a corpse does not
slander, a corpse does not lie, does not snatch the things that do not belong,
does not revile those who happen upon it. My life has been regulated toward
another life; I was taught to despise the things in the world, to pass by the
earthly and to hasten toward the heavenly, just as Paul also expressly
testifies, that the world has been crucified to him and he to the world.

These are the words of a soul truly regenerated, these the voices of the newly
initiated man who remembers his own confession, which he made toward God in
the tradition of the mystery, having promised to despise every punishment and
pleasure for the love toward him. These things then are sufficient toward the
holy subject of the day, which the circled year has brought to us in ordered
periods, and it is good for the rest to rest the word upon the lover of man,
the supplier of so great =9.241= a gift, bringing in return a small voice as
the repayment for great things; for you truly are, Master, a pure and
ever-flowing fountain of goodness, who turned away from us justly and had
mercy lovingly, hated and were reconciled, cursed and blessed, banished from
paradise and called back again, stripped off the leaves of the fig, the
unseemly covering, and threw around a much-honored garment, opened the prison
and released the condemned, sprinkled with clean water and cleansed of the
filths. No longer will Adam, being called by you, be ashamed, nor, being
convicted by conscience, will he cover himself, hiding under the thicket of
paradise, nor indeed will the flaming sword encircle paradise making the
entrance unapproachable to those who draw near, but all things have been
recast toward gladness for us the heirs of sin. And paradise is passable for
man, and heaven itself, and the creation both in the world and above the
world, which of old was at variance toward itself, has been fitted into
friendship, and we men have become in accord with the angels, piously holding
