LIFE AND CONDUCT OF THE BLESSED SYMEON THE STYLITE

1 A strange and paradoxical mystery has come to pass in our days. It seemed
good to me the sinful and least Antonius to write according to what I grasp.
For the composition has been filled with benefit and compunction. Whence I
entreat: incline your ear and hear the things I grasp.

2 The one among the saints and most blessed Symeon, being an infant in age,
used to shepherd the sheep of his father in the same manner as also the
prophet David; and on the holy Lord's day he would go into the church toward
the oracles of God and would gladly listen to the holy scriptures, not knowing
what it was he was hearing. And advancing in age and being pricked by the word
of God he goes away on one of the days into the holy church, and hearing the
apostle being read he asks a certain old man: Tell me, father, what is it that
he is reading. The elder says to him: Concerning continence of soul. Saint
Symeon says to him: What is continence of soul? The elder says to him: Child,
why do you ask me? For I see you young in age, but possessing an aged mind.
Saint Symeon says to him: I am not tempting you, father; but I am astonished
at the name of the expression. The elder says to him: Continence is salvation
of soul, leading into light, bringing into the kingdom of the heavens. Saint
Symeon says to him: Teach me, honorable father, the things you say, because I
am unlearned.

3 The elder says to him: Child, if anyone fasts to God unceasingly and renders
all the prayers to him with gentleness, that is, at the third hour one prayer,
likewise also sixth, ninth and twelfth and the things following these; just as
they are also performed in the monasteries. If then you know, child, what you
have heard, reckon these things in your heart; for you must both hunger and
thirst and be insulted and be struck and be reviled, groan and weep and be
afflicted and be made irregular, be healthy and desire to renounce and be
humbled and suffer many things from men, and thus be comforted by angels; see,
you have heard all these things, may the Lord of glory give you a good mind
toward his will.

4 And having heard these things saint Symeon went out of the church and goes
away to a desert place and sets himself upon his face for seven days weeping
and praying to God, partaking neither of food nor of drink. And after the
seven days, having risen, he comes running to a monastery. And he falls at the
feet of the archimandrite crying out and saying: Have mercy on me, father, a
humble and wretched man, and save a soul that is perishing and longing to
serve God. The archimandrite says to him: Who are you and from where? And what
is your name? And from where have you come? Blessed Symeon says: By race I am
free, and the name is Symeon, but how I have come here or of what parents I
am, do not ask me, master, I beg you, but redeem for yourself one soul that is
perishing. Having heard these things the archimandrite raised him from the
ground and the archimandrite says to him: If you are from God, the Lord will
guard you from every wicked and deceitful deed and you shall be doing the
service for all, that you may be loved by all.

5 But his parents did not cease weeping and seeking him; and the saint was
spending his time in the monastery serving all and being loved by all and
fulfilling the rule of the monastery. So on one of the days, having gone out
of the monastery, he finds upon the well a bucket, from which they used to
draw the water, having

a rope; and having loosed the rope he goes away to a private place and binds
himself about his whole body; and he puts on over the rope a hair tunic and
went into the monastery and says to the brothers: I went out to draw water and
I did not find the rope on the bucket. The brothers say to him: Be silent,
lest anyone learn of it to the archimandrite. And no one knew that within he
was bound about with the rope. So he remained up to one year and more having
the rope wound in his flesh, and it ate up his flesh, so that the rope was
covered by the putrefied flesh of the righteous one, and from his stench no
one was able to stand nearest him, and no one knew the mystery. And his bed
was filled with worms, and no one knew what had happened.

6 And he used to take his food and give it to the poor, no one knowing. So on
one of the days one of the monks goes out and finds him giving his bread and
the pulse which he received to the poor. And all used to fast until evening,
but saint Symeon used to taste from Lord's day to Lord's day. And one of the
monks, having gone in, slandered him to the archimandrite saying: I beg your
holiness; this man wants to dissolve the monastery, that is the rule which you
handed down to us. The archimandrite says to him: How does he want to dissolve
the rule? The monk says to him: We received to fast until evening, but this
one eats from Lord's day to Lord's day, and his bread, which he receives, and
the pulse he secretly gives to the poor each day; and not only this, but also
from his body an unbearable stench rises, so that no one is able to stand
nearest him; and his bed has been filled with worms, and we are not able to
bear it; But if you wish, keep this one here and we withdraw, or dismiss him
to go away, from where he came.

7 Having heard these things the archimandrite became astonished, and he
examines his bed, and finds it filled with worms, and from the stench he was
not able to stand there. And the archimandrite says: Behold even the young
Job. And having seized him he says to him: Why have you done these things,
man? From where is this stench of yours, why do you deceive the brothers, why
do you dissolve the rule of the monastery? Or are you a phantom? Go away
elsewhere and die apart from us; through you perhaps I the wretched one have
come to be tempted? For if you were a true man from understanding parents, you
would have announced to us who your father was and your mother and your race,
and from where you came here. Having heard these things the saint, looking
down toward the earth, was silent saying nothing, and from his tears the place
in which he stood was filled; and having become furious for a long time the
archimandrite says to the monks: Strip him, that we may see from where this
stench is.

8 So they wished to strip him and they were not able; for his garment had been
glued from the rotten flesh. So for three days they did not cease soaking him
with lukewarm water with oil, and thus with much pain they were able to strip
him, so that with the garment his putrefied flesh was torn off, and they find
the rope wound about his body, so that nothing of it appeared, except only the
ends of the rope; and of the worms that were in him there was no conjecture;
then all the monks became astonished at him seeing that incurable wound, and
they were reasoning among themselves how and by what device they might lift
the rope from him; and saint Symeon was crying out saying: Forgive me, my
lords brothers, let me die thus, the stinking dog, for according to my deeds I
ought also to be judged; for all injustice and greed has come to be with me,
for I am the

sea of sins. And the monks and the archimandrite were weeping seeing that
incurable wound; and the archimandrite says to him: You are not yet of
eighteen years and what sins do you have? Saint Symeon says to him: The
prophet David says: Behold I was conceived in lawlessnesses and in sins my
mother conceived me; and I have been clothed with the like of all these. And
the archimandrite was astonished at his intelligent answer, that being a
rustic how he was pricked unto the fear of God. And the archimandrite, having
called two physicians, with many toils and pains so that he was thought to
have died, they tore the rope from him † from the fleshes that had been glued
to it, and having tended him for fifty days they benefited him in part. And
the archimandrite says to him: Child, behold you have become healthy; go away
where you wish.

9 Then saint Symeon goes out from the monastery; and behold there was a well
lying near the monastery, in which there was no water in it, but a multitude
of unclean wicked spirits dwelt in it; and not only of unclean spirits, but
also of asps and vipers and snakes and scorpions an incalculable multitude;
whence all were afraid to pass through that place. And saint Symeon went
there, no one knowing, and throws himself into that well, having made the seal
in Christ, and hides himself in the side of the well.

10 So after seven days of his withdrawing from the monastery the archimandrite
sees in a dream an incalculable multitude of men clothed in white and
encircling the monastery, holding lamps and saying: Just now we burn you here,
unless you hand over to us the servant of God Symeon. And for what cause did
you drive him out, and what did he do, that you cast him out from the
monastery, and what is his fault? Tell us, before we burn you; do you not know
what you had in your monastery? For this one will be found greater than you in
that fearful and dreadful day. And having been awakened the archimandrite,
having become trembling, says to the monks: As I see, that man happens to be a
genuine servant of God; for behold I suffered many evils in a dream this night
because of him; but I entreat you, brothers, run about for me and find him for
me, since let none of you come here.

11 And having gone out they sought him in every place and not having found him
they came saying to the archimandrite: Truly, master, we did not leave a place
where we did not seek, except the place in which no one dares to travel
because of the multitude of the wild beasts. The archimandrite says to them:
Children, having made a prayer and having taken lamps, having gone, go down
and search for him. And having made a prayer upon the well for three hours
they let down into the well five monks with ropes holding lamps; and the
reptiles seeing them fled into the corners. And saint Symeon seeing them cried
out saying: I beg you, brothers and servants of God, grant me a little, that I
may give over my spirit, for it is enough for me, that what I set myself I did
not fulfill. And the monks, having forced him with much force, brought him up
from the well, dragging him as one who had done evil; and they led him to the
archimandrite. And he, having seen him, fell at his feet saying: Forgive me,
servant of God, become yourself my teacher and teach me the things which
endurance also has and grants.

12 And saint Symeon did not cease weeping and entreating God, and having spent
in that monastery three years he goes out, no one knowing, and goes away to a
desert place, in which sufficient villages lay nearby, and nearest to it was a

village called Gelasois, and he builds there for himself a small place of dry
stones, and he stands in the midst four years being snowed on, rained on,
scorched, and many used to go away to him; and his food was soaked lentils and
his drink water. And after these things he made for himself a pillar of four
cubits and he stands on it seven years, and his fame was running everywhere;
and after these things the crowds build for him two enclosures of dry stone,
and they set a door on the inner enclosure and they make for him a pillar of
thirty cubits, and he stands on it fifteen years performing many healings; for
many who were demon-possessed used to go there and were healed.

13 And saint Symeon used to imitate his teacher Christ, whom also calling upon
he used to make the lame walk, cleansing lepers, making the mute speak,
paralytics run, granting healing to the long-sick, admonishing and exhorting
each: If anyone says to you that who healed you, say you that God healed me;
let it not seem good to you to say that Symeon healed me, since you would find
yourself again in the same pains. And this I say to you: never lie and swear
God; and if also necessity comes to you of swearing, swear by me the humble
one, whether with truth or with falsehood; for it is a great sin and fearful
to swear unto God.

14 Hear a fearful and paradoxical wonder: his mother through twenty years
learns where saint Symeon is and having come running she wished to see him
after so many years and weeping much at seeing him and she was not permitted
to see him; and when for a long time she wished to be sanctified by his holy
hands, she was compelled to climb upon the wall; and climbing upon the wall of
the enclosure she was broken to the ground, not having been able to see him.
And saint Symeon sends word to her: Forgive me, mother, for now, and if we are
worthy in that age we see one another. And as she heard these things, she was
burned more to see him. And saint Symeon sends word to her: Cease, my lady
mother, since you have come from afar and have toiled for me the humble one,
but set yourself a little and resting take strength and after a little I see
you. And she having heard sets herself at his doorway and immediately she gave
over her spirit to God. And the attendants came to wake her; and they found
her dead and announced to the saint concerning her. And he having heard
commands her to be brought in and he sets her before his pillar, and having
seen her with tears he began to say: Lord, the God of the powers, the guide of
the light and charioteer of the Cherubim, who guided Joseph, who empowered
your prophet David against Goliath, who raised Lazarus four days from the
dead, raise your right hand and receive in peace the soul of your servant. And
as he was praying her holy relic was moved and she smiled in her face, and all
who were watching were astonished and were glorifying God; and having buried
her they buried her before his pillar, that, as he prays, he remembers her.

15 Hear another strange and paradoxical mystery: certain ones were coming to
prayer from a long distance, and a grazing doe meets them, having in the womb,
and one of them says to the doe: I adjure you by the power of the holy Symeon,
stand, that I may seize you. And immediately the doe stood, and having seized
her he slaughtered her and they ate her flesh; and the skin remained; and
immediately their speech was hindered, and they began to bleat as the
irrational animals, and they come running and fall before his pillar begging
to obtain healing, and they filled the skin of the doe with chaff and toward
the recognition of many men the skin was set a

sufficient time, and the men having spent a sufficient time in repentance and
having been healed withdrew to their own.

16 Hear another strange and wondrous thing: a certain woman being thirsty
through the night wished to drink water; and having taken hold of the pitcher
of water she drinks together with the water a small snake, and having been
nourished in her belly it became large, and her face became as the appearance
of green grass, and many physicians came, that they might heal her and they
were not able; and those in her house, having learned the wonders and the
healings which the saint of God Symeon was doing, having taken her they led
her to the saint and they announce to him all the things concerning her and he
commands them saying: Put in her mouth of this water and of this spring. And
having done thus according to what was commanded, the beast was moved before
all, and she dashes herself to the ground, and it comes out and having set
down its head in the midst of the railings it made a noise, and all were
glorifying God.

17 And they changed his pillar having made it of forty cubits, and he was
noised abroad into the whole inhabited world. Whence the multitude of the
Saracens came to him burning with the faith, and he pricked them unto the fear
of God. So the man-hating devil, having a custom of tempting the saints and of
being trampled by them, brought a plague upon his thigh in the manner of the
blessed Job, a pain of the so-called plague, and his thigh was putrefied
together and he was standing on one foot thereafter for a two-year time. And
incalculable worms were falling from his thigh upon the earth, whence those
nearest him had no other work except only to collect them and carry them up to
the place from where they had fallen, the saint saying: Eat, from where the
Lord has given you.

18 And according to the will of God it comes to pass that the king of the
Saracens comes to prayer to him, and as soon as he went in nearest the pillar
to be blessed by him of saint Symeon, the saint of God having seen him began
to admonish him. And as they were speaking a worm falls from his thigh, and
the king sets his mind, not indeed knowing what it was that had fallen, and
having run he snatches it. And he sets it upon his eyes and unto his heart and
he goes out outside holding it in his hand. And the saint sends word to him
saying: Come in, put down what you took up, you bring a burden to me the
sinner. It is a stinking worm from stinking flesh. Why do you destroy your
hands, being an honorable man? And when the righteous one had said these
things the Saracen comes in and says to him: This will be for me unto a
blessing and unto forgiveness of sins. And having spread out his hand there
was an honorable pearl in his hand. And having seen it he began to glorify God
and he says to the righteous one: Behold what you said is a worm, it is a
pearl if anyone has no price, through which the Lord has enlightened me. And
after these things having heard the saint says to him: As you have believed
let it be to you all the days of your life, and not only to you, but also to
your children. And having been blessed the king of the Saracens went away to
his own rejoicing in peace.

19 Hear another mystery. On the mountain on which he stands, toward the east a
great dragon was remaining. Whence not even herb used to come up in that
place. And it happened to that dragon, as he was coming out to cool himself,
that a piece of wood entered into his eye, and for a sufficient time from the
pain no one was able to bear his hissing. So on one of the days the dragon
comes out from his den being dragged and he comes with all seeing and sets
himself at the doorway of the enclosure. And suddenly his eye was opened and
the wood comes out from his eye, and he spent three days there, until he was
restored healthy. And thus he went away to his place with all seeing

having wronged no one, but as a sheep thus he was lying at the doorway of the
righteous one, and all were going in and going out being wronged in nothing by
him.

20 Hear another paradoxical wonder. There came to be a chief robber in Syria,
his name Antiochus, and his surname was called Gonatas, who was spoken of in
the whole world the things concerning him. And from here and there soldiers
were sent to hunt him and bring him in Antioch, and no one was able to hunt
him because of the great power of his strength. So in Antioch bears and
remaining beasts were prepared, as he being about to fight with beasts, and
the whole city Antioch was moved because of him. And they go out to hunt him
and they find him drinking, in a certain village in an inn, and the soldiers
encircled the inn. And that one having learned began to play the tragic part,
and a river was lying by the village, and that chief robber had a mare and he
used to command her as a man and having risen he throws his garments upon the
mare and says to her: Go away to the river and wait for me there, and the mare
goes out from the inn biting and striking, and having gone away to the river
she was waiting for him. And the chief robber also goes out from the inn
having bared his sword, crying out and saying toward the multitude of the
soldiers: Flee lest anyone die, and none of the soldiers mastered him. And
having escaped the notice of all who were guarding him and having crossed the
river together with the mare, and having sat upon her, he reached the
enclosure of saint Symeon. And having gone in he threw himself before his
pillar, and when the soldiers had gathered in the enclosure of the saint the
saint says to them: With our master Jesus Christ two robbers were crucified
together, the one according to his works received, and the other inherited the
kingdom of heavens. And if anyone is able to withstand the one who sent him,
let him come and himself snatch him away, for I neither brought him nor am I
able to dismiss him. For the one who sent him here, he also takes his part, so
let no one revile me the humble one, who has toiled much because of my many
sins. And having said these things to them he dismissed them. And as these
were going away the chief robber says: My lord, I am going away. And the saint
says to him: Do you go away again in the same evils? And the chief robber
says: No master, the Lord is calling me. And having stretched out his hands
into heaven, he said nothing more, except only: Son of God receive my spirit
in peace. And he wept for two hours, so as to make even the righteous one weep
together with those standing by. So setting himself before the pillar of the
righteous one, immediately he gave over the spirit, and the crowds wrapped him
and they buried him near the enclosure of the righteous one. So on the next
day there come from Antioch more than a hundred men with swords to snatch him
and they began to shout against the righteous one: Dismiss to us the one you
have. And the holy one said to them: Brothers, the one who sent him here is
stronger than you, and having need of him as he being useful, having sent
toward him two fearful soldiers in arms, who were able to thunderbolt your
city with those dwelling in it, he took him, and I the sinner having seen
their fearful form, having feared I did not dare to speak against them, lest
they kill also me the humble one as opposing God. And those men having heard
these things from the saint and having learned with what glory the chief
robber gave over the spirit, glorifying God and trembling they went away again
to Antioch.

21 Hear another fearful and glorious wonder. In the place in which the saint
was, water did not appear. And the multitude of the animals and of the men was
perishing of those coming in the place of saint Symeon. And the saint having
made a prayer for seven days did not speak to anyone, but he was having bent
the knees and praying, so as to think

all that he had already died. And about the fifth hour of the seventh day
suddenly water gushed from the east of his enclosure, and having dug they
found a form of a cave filled with water. And they built in it seven mouths.
And all were glorifying the God of heaven and of earth.

22 [And a certain queen of the Saracens being barren went away begging him
concerning her giving birth. For she was being reproached by her husband each
day and falling before much and begging for sufficient days she was remaining
at the pillar of saint Symeon. So the saint sent word to her: Go away to your
house and the Lord has the things profitable to grant you. And having gone
away to her house she came together with her husband and immediately she
conceived. And she bore a daughter. And for five years she neither spoke nor
walked. And she comes with her husband and her child weeping before, and saint
Symeon answering said to them: Remain and God has to help. And as they spent
seven days they were benefited nothing and being grieved they were withdrawing
saying that Wholly the Lord did not wish to heal her. And as they were
traveling and having gone away not a little they turn to see the column of
saint Symeon. And behold their daughter suddenly cried out saying: Glory to
you saint Symeon. And the parents of the child having seen glorified God
through saint Symeon.

23 And hear another mystery. On one of the days of soldiers traveling, a
certain woman from much desire having come to see the face of saint Symeon by
what pretext to see him; she was devising then to take on a man's form and to
see him. So on one of the days she saw sufficient soldiers going away to the
saint to pray. And having taken on the soldierly form she also was traveling
together with them. And having arrived by the place where the pillar of saint
Symeon was, she says to the soldiers with her: Brothers, if you wish, having
left me your animals go in and be blessed by the righteous one, and when you
come out I also go in again, that I may be deemed worthy to be blessed by the
righteous one. And when they had gone in and they had worshiped, saint Symeon
says to them: One soldier of you was left outside. And they said to him: Yes
master; he guards our animals. And the righteous one answering said to them:
Having gone out say to him: Do not trouble yourself, for you have been heard
and you are blessed to the Lord. And they having gone out were inquiring,
entreating him, What good has been done by you before God? for behold the
righteous one said to us that Say to the soldier with you who remained
outside: do not trouble yourself, for your petition has been heard and you are
blessed to the Lord. And answering she said to them: I confess to you,
brothers, I am a woman and from much desire I had to see the righteous one,
since I am a sinner. And the soldiers having heard were astonished blessing
God through saint Symeon and they were going in peace.

24 And another mystery if you hear. When a sufficient multitude had come †
according to the custom with incense crying to him: Servant of God, the people
wait to receive your blessing, command dismiss them, for behold they have a
long hour waiting, and they were receiving no answer. For neither was his
spirit present. And as I was crying to him persistently and not receiving an
answer the deacon began to lament above by the column having thought that he
had died. And the multitude of the people having seen began to groan and to
beat a great beating mourning their sins. And as after a sufficient hour his
holy spirit returned again, and answering he said to the people: Brothers, a
great ship in the sea was being storm-tossed violently at this hour,
having[ta] about three hundred souls; and they cried out

all the souls entreating the master God. And crying to me with dreadful oaths
and I having seen what was happening to them I entreated the God who is always
long-suffering over our sins to calm the sea, and having given them a hand I
saved all. And having heard the mystery the multitudes of the people were
glorifying God through saint Symeon. And the righteous one having blessed them
dismissed them in peace.

25 From a female snake a great swelling came out and from her pains she was
going for one mile. And the male having shared the pain with her pains, having
taken his female they were coming toward lord Symeon and having arrived by the
pillar they were separated from one another. For the female not daring to be
seen by the righteous one went away to the women's part, and the male having
gone in among that multitude was worshiping the pillar making his head up and
down and begging the righteous one. And the crowds having seen the enormous
size of the snake fled from him. And saint Symeon having seen said to the
crowds: Do not flee, brothers, for truly having a prayer he has come here. For
his female is very sick and she went away to the women's part. And he says to
the snake: Take up mud from the earth and carry it up to your woman. And
having set it on, blow upon her and it has to heal her. And the snake having
taken of the mud was going toward his female. And the crowds having seen
followed him to see what he is about to do. And they see his female standing
upright outside the railings having a great swelling and the snake having
taken the mud sets it on her and having blown upon her in the presence of all
he healed her. And having taken her he withdrew. And the crowds having seen
the mystery glorified God. 26 And another wonder came to be in his days.

26 For when the great threat of the fears had come upon the whole inhabited
world, the whole East and Antioch were being brought down to him begging him
to importune God. For many fallings of dwellings were coming to be and deaths
of sufficient men, so that even the righteous one together with the pillar was
being shaken as a reed by wind. And weeping together with them he was saying
to the crowds: Brothers, all have turned aside, together they have become
useless, there is not even one. You do not hear me, but unto who will defraud
whom your fornications and your injustices have overflowed. Truly I say to you
brothers: it is easier for me to speak with the master than with you the
faithless. And having commanded the litanizing to cease, he made a prayer, and
again the threat was remaining in the same shaking all things. And he
permitted them to cry the Lord have mercy. And as they were remaining for
sufficient hours and crying: Give us peace, he made a great prayer. And after
he fulfilled the prayer answering he said to the people: Brothers truly I say
to you, that from the whole multitude except one soul another was not heard;
and that I may persuade you, behold in the midst I make him come. And having
commanded him to come in the presence of all he questions him and says to him:
Brother believe me saying: from all these you alone were heard; but inform all
and tell us, what good has been done by you. And that one did not wish saying:
I am a sinner, forgive me. And as he was being compelled for a long time a
voice came to him from heaven saying: Your petition has been heard. And as the
rustic was standing among the whole people he began to narrate to them that I
am a farmer man and it is a custom for me, the labor of my day, whichever I
work, I divide them into three parts. And first I set aside the part of the
poor, after the end of the public tax, and thus that of my expense. Doing this
until now I have not ceased. And all greeting were worshiping him. And they
were pressing the man, each one being mad to see him.

28 So the blessed one stood on various columns forty-seven years and after all
these things he sought him; and it was Friday; and he was shut for prayer and
as he had a custom, he spent the whole Friday and on the Sabbath and on the
Lord's day and he did not nod, as he had a custom, to bless those who had bent
the knees. And as I saw this, I went up to him and I saw his face and it was

his honorable relic and behold his body was shaken together with the pillar
and I heard a voice saying: Amen amen amen, and from the fear I said: Bless
me, Lord, and remember me in your good rest.

29 And I came down and I did not make the mystery manifest to anyone, lest a
tumult come to be, but through a faithful man I signaled to the bishop of
Antioch Martyrius and to the general Ardaburius. And on the next day the
bishop of Antioch comes with also other six bishops; and Ardaburius also comes
with six hundred names, lest when the villages had gathered they snatch his
honorable relic; for thus they were also planning. And curtains came to be in
a circle of his pillar. And three bishops go up and kiss his garments having
said three psalms and they brought up a leaden coffin and they set his holy
relic and they bring it down through machines and then all knew that saint
Symeon had died, so that also all the Saracens were gathered in arms with
camels wishing themselves also to snatch the relic. And a confusion of the
crowd comes to be so that the mountain was not appearing from the multitude
and from the smoke of the incenses and of the candles and lamps that were
burning, of which there was no number. And the voices of the weeping of the
men and women and children were going until far and the whole mountain was
being shaken from the cry of the birds that had gathered and encircled the
enclosure of the saint. So as they brought him down, they set him upon the
marble altar, which he had before his pillar, already having the fourth day
having died. And thus his holy relic was spread as having rested before one
hour. And all the bishops gave him the love; and his face

was very bright as the light; and the hairs of his head and of the beard were
as snow. And the bishop of Antioch wished to take a hair of his beard unto a
blessing and his hand was dried, and all the bishops made a prayer over him
weeping and saying to the holy relic that Nothing of the members or of the
garments is lacking to you nor again will anyone take from your holy and
honorable relic. And as they were saying these things with weeping the hand of
the bishop was restored healthy. And then with psalms and hymns they put him
in the coffin.

30 And I the sinner was watching before five and six days of his rest a man in
a fearful robe, which I am not able to narrate, and his length being as of two
men and speaking to him a third of the day and giving him the love. And I
wished to narrate to some the things concerning him and my mind was being
snatched and I was being hindered from speaking until he was rested. So as the
man whom I said was coming to him, I was watching, he says, them eating; and
what they were eating, I did not know and they were psalming; and what they
were psalming, I did not know, except only the amen. And fear was holding me
toward the spectacle of the man.

31 So they set the coffin of the saint upon the carriage and thus with candles
and incenses and psalmody they carry him away into their city Antioch. And as
they were coming as from five miles of the city in a place called Merope the
mules stood and were not moving; for a paradoxical mystery was there; for
there was from the right of the road a tomb. And a certain man was remaining
at it; and the matter of the man was such. For he was loving a married woman
for twenty years and he was not able to master her. And the woman died, and
they set her in that tomb. And that the evil-hater might somehow gain the soul
of the man, having gone away in the memorial he opens aside the slab of the
tomb and he comes together with the deadened body. And immediately he was made
mute and he was held in the tomb and he was no longer able to withdraw from
that place. And as the travelers were passing they were looking to him sitting
on the little steps of the tomb, and each for God's sake was bringing to him,
one water, and one food. And according to the will of God as the honorable
relic was passing on that day and (read ho) neither speaking nor hearing, as
the carriage was standing and of the crowd he came out from the tomb crying
out and saying: Have mercy on me, saint of God Symeon, and having taken hold
of the carriage, immediately his bridles were opened and his wits came into
understanding. And all having seen what had happened glorified God, and from
the cry of the men that place was being shaken. And the man was crying out: I
today through you have been saved, servant of God, for I had been lost in the
sin.

32 And the whole city went out to a meeting of the honorable relic clothed in
white with candles and lamps. And they bring him into the church called of
Cassian. And after thirty days the general Ardaburius commands and they set
him in the Great Church, and from there again according to a revelation of God
a prayer-house temple of the holy and devout Symeon was built and thus with
much glory and hymns they transferred his holy relic into that prayer-house.

33 And many entreated having given gold and silver, that they might take a
blessing from his holy relics. And the bishop granted to no one because of the
oaths which he swore. And many healings are performed in the place where his
honorable relics are laid up through the grace that was given to him from our
master Christ. And the saint of God Symeon was perfected in the month
September 1, as our Lord Jesus Christ is reigning,

to whom the glory and the power unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
