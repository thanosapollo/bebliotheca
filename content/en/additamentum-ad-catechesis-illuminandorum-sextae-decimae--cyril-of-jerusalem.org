* Addition to the Sixteenth Catechesis of the Illuminated
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

16.1 But as having heard Word of God we did not think the Word some
unhypostatic thing, neither coming to be from learning nor being dissolved after
being brought forth, nor suffering any other such thing of the sort of
sufferings that are contemplated concerning our word, but essentially
subsisting both choosing and all-powerful, so also having learned Spirit of God,
that which accompanies the Word and manifests his energy, we do not think a
breath of panting, (for the greatness of the divine power would be brought down
to humility, if according to a likeness of ours the spirit in it also were
supposed,) but an essential power, itself contemplated upon itself in a peculiar
hypostasis, neither able to be divided from the God in whom it is or from the
Word of God which it accompanies, nor poured out into the non-existent, but
according to a likeness of the God Word being according to hypostasis, choosing,
self-moved, energetic, always choosing the good and having the power concurrent
with the will toward every purpose. 16.2 For there is a good spirit of God.
David says: and your good spirit will lead me in a straight land. This then is
the spirit of God into which we believe, the blessed spirit, the eternal, the
unturned, the unchangeable, the unspeakable, that which lords and kings over all
the generative essence, of both seen and unseen natures, that which masters both
angels and archangels, authorities, beginnings, lordships, thrones, the creator
of all essence, the co-throned of the glory of the Father and the Son, that
which kings beginninglessly and endlessly together with Father and Son, the
generated hypostases being present, that which sanctifies the ministering
spirits sent unto a service through those about to inherit salvation, that which
came down upon the holy and blessed virgin Mary, from whom Christ was born
according to flesh, and upon the Lord himself in a bodily form of a dove in the
Jordan river, that which came to be upon the apostles on the day of Pentecost in
a form of fiery tongues, the giving and furnishing of all the spiritual gifts in
the church, that which proceeds from the Father, that which exists from the
Godhead of Father and Son, that which exists of the same substance of Father and
Son, both inseparable and undivided.
