HOMILIES OF MACARIUS

Second letter of the same monk, the divine father Macarius, to abba Symeon the
ascetic from Mesopotamia of Syria and to the rest of the brethren who are with
him.

HOMILY 51

51.1 Macarius to the beloved and like-minded brethren in the Lord. Peace be
multiplied to you superabundantly from the Lord, the heavenly peace that
dawned the light upon the world; which prophets preached and righteous men
spoke and angels announced as gospel. That peace which Mary received, and she
bore the savior of the world; who came down to the dead, and split the rocks,
and opened the tombs, and made the dead live, and led out those shut in, and
loosed those in fetters, and freed those enslaved, and tore up the handwriting
of the condemnation of Adam, and wrote a heavenly law in the souls, and mixed
the godhead with humanity, and freed the creation, and killed sin, and took
the curse from the earth, and tore down the wall of enmity, and raised from
the dust the condemned body of Adam, and brought him into the paradise of the
promise, and made him walk in a new life that does not die again, that we
might become his brothers, and that he might beget us from his father. Which
barren women heard, and their embryos leaped in gladness; ears of deaf men
were opened, and the tongue of stammerers was loosed; lame men walked;
paralytics were healed, and they took up their beds. Whom the sinful woman saw
and worshipped, and filled up her debts with the kisses of his feet; whom
Zacchaeus seeing received life in his house; which the waters saw and it was
changed into wine, and a few loaves superabounded; who gave healing from his
fringe to the woman with the issue of blood and she was delivered from the
fountain of the blood of her flow; who walked upon waves of the sea, and
rebuked the wind and it was still, and shut the fishes in the net, and again
from another fish brought a stater; whom sinners saw and were justified, and
harlots became chaste, and virgins left their suitors, and married women
withdrew from their spouses, and bridegrooms and brides abolished their
marriages, and kings taking the diadems came into worship of him, and judges
left their courts, and wise men trampled their wisdom, and physicians threw
away their drugs, and rich men left their wealth, and merchants left their
stores; whom fools saw and became prudent, and unlearned men became scribes,
and fishermen became heralds, and those not having speech spoke in every
tongue. That peace which received the gift of Abel, and transferred Enoch so
as not to taste death, and sheltered Noah in the ark. Who multiplied the
blessings of Abraham, and appointed Isaac heir of the promise, from whose seed
was born the savior of the ages; who chose Jacob from the womb, to receive the
blessings of the firstborn; who was with Joseph in a foreign land and took the
yoke of


slavery from his neck, and having loosed him cast him out of the prison, and
made him lord of Egypt; who was seen to Moses in a bush of fire, and gave him
authority to subject all the creatures, and made the sea run before him; who
strengthened Joshua the son of Nun, and made him victor in all the encounters
of the wars; who chose Samuel as priest, and anointed David after flocks; who
from a little flour in the jar and a little oil in the cruse of the widow made
a fountain of fullness for three years and six months. Who sent horses and a
chariot of fire to Elijah and brought him into heaven, and granted Elisha a
double spirit as he asked; who commanded wood to do wonders in Egypt, and a
dry rod of Aaron to make fruit. That peace which strengthened Job in the
temptations, and led Lot out of the flood of fire. Who was with all the
fathers, whom also having believed and whom having loved they went out after
him enduring reproaches and afflictions; and they became strangers and poor,
and sojourners and persecuted, making nothing of the world, despising death,
and denying themselves; and they were tried with every trial, and were thrown
to beasts, and became a spectacle to the world; and having passed through fire
and water they came out into refreshment. 51.2 And these things I wrote to
you, beloved brethren, that you may know that from the day Adam was created
upon the earth and until the consummation of the age the evil one does not
rest from making war with the saints. For in that same body with which we are
clothed, already after the transgression of the first-formed, he dwells and
turns about with us evilly and craftily in his wiles and practices. And as
many as are infants and untrained in his wars are more abundantly wounded by
him because of the simplicity of their infancy; but so long as they seek the
Lord in truth they do not fall; for it is written, The Lord preserving the
infants; for Christians, having put on a heavenly weapon, make war in it, as
one against one, secretly, in their own souls. And men fighting opposite one
another have seasons in which they fight, and after the war toward one another
each of them rests and sits in his fatherland with quiet and peace; but as
many as make war with Satan, such stand in contest night and day; for in one
house, which is the body, a human soul and Satan have taken lodging, against
whom it fights; there he dwells in it and turns about, and because of this he
is nearer to the soul every hour; because of this they do not rest nor are
they at ease, until they put on weapon and power from on high, since they
believed and knew through God that the murderer of humanity is with them; and
they delight in those very wars of theirs, in which they knew that they love
God, and they fight with Satan together with the power of God. But few and
rare are those who know that the destroyer of souls is with them; because of
this they are in affliction, and there is none upon earth who will comfort
them, since they attend and expect from heaven, that they may receive
something in themselves, and in that power and the weapon of the spirit they
may conquer. For the power they receive is from heaven, not appearing to eyes
of flesh; for as much as they seek God from the whole heart, a power of God
secretly helps them every hour; and if they are also tempted by Satan because
of their weakness, yet the Lord does not abandon them to be hindered for long
and to be tempted still more; and the wars themselves teach them to know what
Satan is, and how he corrupts men; and the very sufferings of the wars teach
them to draw near to God the savior of the world; and when they know the
weakness of themselves,


not being able to conquer, then they seek a weapon from God earnestly; and
thus having put on the full armor of the spirit, they become victors, and the
enemy of souls falls under their feet. Through which weapons the walls of sin
that encircled the soul are thrown down, and souls that were shut into
darkness come out into light, and they draw near to their heavenly father, and
they put on a beauty of divine comeliness, and they gird on glory and power,
and they learn all the practices of the evil spirit, as the apostle says, For
we are not ignorant of his thoughts, and they are mixed with the holy spirit,
and they do the will of the father who is in the heavens. 51.3 Know therefore,
beloved brethren, that as many as prepare their own souls to become earth of
the heavenly seed, there the enemy hastens to sow his tares; wherefore you
also were tempted by the enemy, and having heard we were grieved greatly. And
know this also: that unless some will seek the Lord from the whole heart, they
are not tempted by Satan openly, but secretly and craftily through his deceit
he makes away with the souls of men, corrupting and alienating them from God;
and now, brethren, take courage and do not be cowardly in anything, and do not
be scared in the fantasies of the alien, and in prayer do not become confused
in many and unseemly cries, but you shall be awaiting the grace of the Lord in
toil of soul and hunger of mind; for such things have not happened toward you
alone, but here also similar things have happened toward some; and these
things occurred in the first season; for when they began to persevere in
prayers, they were tossed in temptations because they were untrained, and
because of their infancy they were confused. For the rest, brethren, take
courage and be comforted and wait, taking thought for your souls, being eager
to persevere in prayer, and let not your mind grow faint, for it is written,
You have need of endurance, that having done the will of God you may receive
the promises. And the Lord said, If two of you agree, amen I say to you,
whatever they ask, they will receive; for as many as seek God in truth receive
a divine power in the souls, and such, drawing near to the heavenly anointing,
perceive in themselves the taste and sweetness of the age to come; such are
greater than all men who dwell upon the earth. The peace of the Lord which was
with all the holy fathers and guarded them from every temptation, this shall
be with you in the name of the father and of the son and of the holy spirit;
amen.

52.τ HOMILY 2

52.1 The word of God seeks a work of truth. Then there is for a man the
speaking, the hearing, and the doing; for this is why one hears the speaker,
that he may accomplish the work of the hearing. On this day the Lord was born,
the life and the salvation of men; today there has come about a reconciliation
of godhead toward humanity and of humanity toward godhead; today all the
creation leaped; those from above sent to those below and those below to those
above; today death of the darkness has come about and life of the man; today a
way has come about toward God for men and a way of God into the soul; for
formerly the prophet said, The ways of Zion mourn because there are none
coming to a feast, hinting at the desolation and the disappearance of the
soul, for neither was there a way of God into a soul and a mind of a man nor
did a soul walk into God; today the plains rejoice and all things in them, the
earth of the soul receiving rain from heaven. The


Lord has reigned, he has put on comeliness; because formerly Adam, having been
appointed lord and king, behold because of the transgression has become a
slave of sin; today he reigns over his enemies, For he must, he says, reign;
today He has established the inhabited world, the soul formerly desert and
shaken and trembling, held in fear and cowardice of sin, For in the curse, he
says, groaning and trembling you shall be upon the earth; for since her house
had been built on sand, she was in shaking and trembling; today the house of
the soul has been built upon the unshaken rock of the godhead. Angels rejoice,
heavens, stars, sun, moon, the earth, and all things in it, mountains and
hills leap; for if when Israel was going out of Egypt all the creatures
rejoiced together, heaven covering with a cloud of light by day, and by night
in a pillar of fire, the mountains as rams and the hills as lambs of sheep,
how much more upon the true Christ born today. For formerly also all the
creation cried out, having been transferred into corruption, when Adam their
king fell, whom, being his own image of God, the Lord came to renew as was due
and to create anew; the time of the condemnation of the man has been
fulfilled; the seasons announced beforehand by the prophets have been
fulfilled; now he is recalled from the captivity and the exile, For the
kingdom of the heavens has drawn near; the time of the bonds and of the prison
and of the condemnation of Adam in the darkness has been fulfilled. Today
there has come about for him redemption and freedom of the reconciliation, and
the communion toward the spirit, and the union toward God; today the shame of
his face has been taken away, and boldness has been given him with unveiled
face to look upon and to be blended with the spirit; today the bride receives
her own bridegroom; today unity and communion and reconciliation of the
heavenly things and of the earthly things has come about, God and man. 52.2
For it was fitting for him, having put on the body, to come and to turn and to
reconcile men to his own Father; coming unarmed, he took the weapon of the
man, which is the body, and through it he fought and killed death; through a
dead body the enemy was put to death; through his own weapon he condemned sin
in the body; for just as enemies coming on through the city's own weapons and
engines, I mean the ballistae or certain others, are eager to throw down the
walls, so also the enemy, having taken Adam under his hand, using his body as
a weapon, through it overran and took captive all men; likewise also the Lord,
taking the man as a weapon, through him dissolved and made to vanish the walls
and the strongholds of the evil one and all his device and the evil
construction which he used against the man, according to what was said, As its
darkness, so also its light; For as in Adam all die, so also in Christ all
will be made alive; 52.3 for the human nature was formerly dead from God,
fruitless; barren and unbearing was the soul. Now it has received seed from
heaven, that it may be able to bear fruits of the spirit. Adam was one, and
from him the woman, and unless Adam was joined to his wife, he did not make
fruits; so unless the soul is joined and communes with Christ, it is not able
to bear fruits of the spirit. For the divine seed, the word, which fell into
the theotokos Mary, falls into all the believing souls, and thus they are born
from the spiritual offspring of salvation, as he says, Because of your fear,
Lord, we conceived in the womb and were in travail and we brought forth a
spirit of salvation, and thus a soul bears fruit to God. For the woman, he
says, when she is in travail, has grief; but when she has given birth, she
rejoices, because a man was born into the world; and the soul having received
the heavenly seed, until it is in travail has affliction, being tortured by


various temptations, but when it has given birth, that is has been perfected,
it rejoices continually with unspeakable joy, because a heavenly and new man
was born into the world above. 52.4 And just as the Lord, having taken the
body, raised it and sat above at the right hand of the majesty on high, so
also now he transfers the believing souls that are worthy of God and removes
them into his kingdom, having begotten them from above from his own spirit, as
the Lord says, He who believes in me has passed over from death into life. For
those who are faithful in truth, even remaining on the earth, serve the Lord
above in the heavens, and he himself, sitting above upon the throne of his
majesty, rests below upon the souls of the saints as upon thrones; for they
are his members and body, all the church of the saints, and he is the head of
the church; for just as one soul is in the whole body, and each member of the
body is governed by the one soul, so all the saints live into the spirit of
the godhead and are governed by it, and each member lives in the substance and
the divine soul. 52.5 And as the body, if it is lacking a hand or a foot or an
eye, the man is blemished, so also the soul without the heavenly soul and the
divine spirit is incomplete and blemished, being rejected from the kingdom;
For unless one, he says, is born from above, he cannot enter into the kingdom
of God. For two hands and two feet and two ears is a perfect man; but also
with two wings the bird is able to fly, but with one wing it is not able to
fly; but also two covenants were given to men, and without the new the one was
powerless; and all the pairs, being two, are one, and thus they are shown
perfect. So also the true Christian must be; for the Lord was well pleased
that he have two souls, one the created and one heavenly from the divine
spirit, and thus they are able to be perfect and useful for the kingdom of the
heavens, flying and being lightened by the wings of the spirit. 52.6 For just
as the heaven is named in the masculine and the earth in the feminine, so the
heavenly Christ is bridegroom, and his bride is the beautiful and passionless
soul; and from above rain comes down from God, and she as a most fair earth,
receiving, yields the fruits of the spirit; for just as Eve without the union
of Adam was barren and fruitless, so also a soul without the union of the
spirit and the blending of the heavenly bridegroom Christ is widow and
fruitless and barren to the kingdom of the heavens. But also the heavenly
bridegroom, without the eagerness of the man and the will, neither dwells in
him nor is blended; for just as wine mixed with water is drinkable and sweet,
so also the godhead, having mixed itself with humanity, came that it might mix
all who will and believe with its own spirit, and thus they may be changed
into a most sweet and well-flavored choice. And just as wool dyed in purple
has become one, so also souls perfectly baptized with the spirit are completed
as a spiritual purple according to what is written, That which has been born
of the spirit is spirit; just as also the body of the Lord, blended with the
godhead, is God; and just as iron thrown into fire is fire, and nothing is
able to touch it or draw near, since it vanishes and is consumed, unless only
the fire with the fire or coals of fire with coals are able to be unharmed, so
also every soul purified through the fire of the spirit and having become fire
and spirit is able to be with the undefiled body of Christ. But a soul
uninitiated in this spirit it is impossible there to draw near, or to look
upon that divine lightning, and to live in it. For each thing is there and
lives there, from where it was born; the animals that are in the water,
because they were born there, live there;


the birds of heaven flying in the air rest into it. Likewise also the way of
life of the souls, and the air of those who have the wings of the spirit, is
above into the heights of the godhead, for from there they were born. 52.7 And
just as a charioteer holding the reins drives and leads the animals as he
knows and as he wishes, so also the Lord in the highest drives and governs the
creatures in heaven and upon earth as he himself understands; for he himself
is in the heavens sitting at the right of the majesty, and he himself is
governing the things upon the earth and being with and dwelling with all the
saints; he himself is above, and he himself is below; he himself is God, and
he himself is man; he himself is the living one, and he himself is the one who
died; he himself is the Lord of all, and he himself is the slave of all; he
himself is the lamb, and he himself is the sacrifice; he himself is the calf
that was slain, and he himself is the sacrificing high priest; he himself is
the one who suffered, and he himself is the passionless; he himself is the
bridegroom, and he himself is the bride. He himself is the bridechamber, and
he himself is the nuptial bed; he himself is the paradise, and he himself is
the tree of life; he himself is the city Jerusalem, and he himself is the
temple and the holy of holies; he himself is the sea, and he himself is the
inhabited world; he himself is the food of the souls, and he himself is the
one fed by the salvation of these; he himself is the living bread, and he
himself is the water of life; he himself is the true vine, and he himself is
the wine of gladness; he himself is the pearl, and he himself is the treasure;
he himself is the net, and he himself is the warrior; he himself is the
weapon, and he himself is the one who conquers; he himself is the
circumcision, and he himself is the sabbath and the law; he himself is the
head of the church of the saints, and he himself is the grain of mustard; he
himself is the vineyard, and he himself is the plough; he himself is the
grace, and he himself is the faith; he himself is the marriage, and he himself
is the garment of the marriage; he himself is the way, and he himself is the
door; he himself is the sun of righteousness, and he himself is the light of
the souls; he himself is the life, and he himself is the kingdom; he himself
is the beginning and he himself is the end; he himself is the one beyond all,
and he himself is the one who becomes all things in all. This holy and divine
offspring was born for us on this day, the salvation and the life of our
souls; glory to his majesty; glory to the love of man; glory to his
incomparable providence concerning our race. Let us therefore also beseech and
believe to receive him, that we too, having found him, may enjoy him, so many
good things coming about in us by will of father and son and holy spirit, unto
the ages; amen.

53.τ HOMILY 53

53.1 He who wishes to please God and to be counted worthy to become a son of
God ought before all things, having laid hold of longsuffering, to give thanks
in the various afflictions that meet him, and in distresses and necessities; I
mean of diseases and passions, whether spiritual afflictions brought on by the
spirits of wickedness, or bodily losses and dishonors and insults brought on
by men; all which things are hindrances, to alienate from the kingdom and not
to let the soul draw near to God. In all these things then that meet him, he
who wishes to be well approved before Christ ought to rejoice, to be glad and
to exult, and to boil with the impulse toward God, and by choice to hasten
toward him, and when he is all the more hindered in such things, to be at rest
and nobly to leap over every affliction by the love toward the Lord; but if he
is not so disposed nor thus


does he prepare himself, he is pained and distressed, not playing the man;
such a one does not become a child of life, since he has not become an
imitator and follower of all the saints, nor does he follow the tracks of the
Lord. 53.2 For consider as a prudent man, and see how from the beginning the
fathers, the patriarchs, the prophets, the apostles, the martyrs and all the
sons of life rejoiced in the afflictions, and in the distresses and
necessities they were glad; and their rest was in the ill-treatments and the
toils. For rather, he says, choosing to be ill-treated with the people of God,
than to have a temporary enjoyment of sin; and again, Child, if you come
forward to serve the Lord, prepare your soul for temptation; make straight
your heart and endure, and do not hasten in a time of bringing-on; cling to
him and do not withdraw, that you may increase at your last things; whatever
is brought upon you receive gladly, because gold is tested in fire, and
acceptable men in a furnace of humiliation; trust him and he will take you up,
and the rest; and elsewhere, Receive all things brought upon you as good;
knowing that without God nothing comes about. And the blessed apostle more
completely adds saying, As ministers of God, in much endurance, in
afflictions, in necessities, in distresses, in blows, in prisons, in
persecutions, and the Lord, Blessed, he says, are you when they revile you and
persecute you, and the rest; if then you wish to become a son and heir of
such, you ought to take up the imitation of their manner of life and
citizenship, and to be a follower and doer of the words of the Lord; For
narrow and afflicted is the way that leads away into life, and few are those
who go through it. Whether then you are examined in distresses, or in
passions, or in diseases, or in revilings, or in insults, rather in these you
ought to exult and rejoice; For woe, he says, to those who live in luxury and
to those who are filled and to those who are called blessed in earthly things;
for the blessing has been prepared for those who mourn and are afflicted
because of the Lord; he therefore who desires to live the eternal life ought
in all things to give thanks, and to accept the afflictions and the
distresses, and to love the humiliation. 53.3 For there is not need of knees
only before God, that one may persevere every day in the prayer. This is a
good practice, truly good to pray and to persevere unceasingly in the prayer.
For it is the head of all the practices, but without the remaining members of
virtue it is dead. For prayers become well-accepted with good deeds; that one
not hate the brother; not to speak against anyone; to be humble, and not to
think high thoughts; not to think himself to be something, even if he do all
the righteousnesses. For the Lord, being sought thus sincerely and holily, is
present at once; but not being sought thus, it is reckoned as being willingly
in sleep, and the prayer being superficial is reckoned as nothing before God.
53.4 For why does the Lord not reveal himself to us quickly when he is sought
by us? Because he does not wish? Let it not be. For he wishes all men to be
saved and to come into knowledge of truth; and all the eagerness and labor of
the fathers came about for this and of the Lord himself, that God be made
known to men, that is an energy of grace, which is eternal life, but
altogether there is some private fault of the soul in the midst, and because
of this the Lord does not yet make known and reveal himself to us; for God is
not a respecter of persons, but he attends to the thoughts and notions of
those who seek. Because of our slackness then and neglect or unbelief or
certain other faults which we do not know, not discerning ourselves finely,
the Lord is not made known to us, who said he would manifest himself to those
who seek him holily and keep his commandments; for it is written, If you love
me, keep my commandments, and I and my father will come, and we will make a
dwelling with you. Let us be eager


therefore always to examine ourselves and to search our hearts, that we may
know our own faults, believing the liberator of our souls, Christ, to be
redeemed from every fault apparent and hidden, and when we seek him in truth
justly and holily, he readily manifests himself to us according to his
promise, purifying our soiled hearts from every wickedness and defilement.
53.5 Before all things therefore we ought to choose the afflictions and
distresses and insults, we who wish to please God, that we may be counted
worthy to live with him all ages; for who is worthy to hunger and go naked
because of the Lord and to rejoice in these things? Blessed is such a one,
truly blessed because he has acquired great boldness before God; for the Lord
wishes you to be tested in these, whether in the afflictions you do not forget
him, For unto all, he says, endurance and longsuffering with joy, and
elsewhere, Afflictions and necessities found me, your commandments are my
meditation; this is the contest; in this God is well pleased. 53.6 But most of
the men who indeed wish to please God, through lack of true knowledge, toil
with flesh only; but with the mind and the reasonings and the inward hidden
choice the man of God must contend, which is a true contest before God of a
soul toward invisible reasonings of the wicked powers. For it is not, he says,
our wrestling against blood and flesh but against the principalities and the
authorities. For the spiritual things of wickedness are invisible and bodiless
as far as concerns the things that appear; likewise also the soul is invisible
and bodiless as far as concerns thickness of bodies. Invisible therefore and
bodiless the contest and the athletic struggle must be in the inmost man.
Which contest the fathers from above from the beginning contended, even if in
the things that appear they appeared as the rest of men; those who contended
thus were able to please God. 53.7 For manly and noble souls and ones that
despise all things go away into war, as also Moses, hinting at the hidden war
of the wicked spirits toward the soul, and of what sort of souls are fit,
said, If anyone has betrothed a wife, let him not go out into war, and if
anyone has planted a vineyard, let him not go out. These things he says
concerning material bonds, lest anyone contending toward the spiritual things
of wickedness be bound in certain affairs, for if because of the attachment
toward the pleasures and the possessions he does not contend, he becomes
rejected from the heavenly gladness, as in the gospel the Lord says concerning
those called to the marriage and declining, The first says, I have bought a
field, and I cannot come; and the other, I have married a wife and I cannot
come, I ask you have me excused; speaking in agreement with Moses. 53.8 For
the noble and those ready to obey the will of God go forward in the spiritual
war; and a good preparation and fitness toward the war against the invisible
enemies is when one, having left father and mother and all the things of life,
brings himself to God, and besides these still also denies his own soul,
handing over whatever he has acquired and whatever belongs to him from the
fleshly parents, money and all things, to him to whom also he entrusted the
soul after God, a spiritual father and ascetic, so as to govern them in
whatever way he himself wishes, and not to be busybodies where it is needed,
and where it is not needed, lest he be found judging and testing him whom he
chose as judge and governor of his soul, having entrusted it as to one
experienced to bring it to God, and because of the cheap things of matter
rejecting him; for through these things he is shown an ascetic himself rather
than subject to the ascetic because of God. 53.9 For tell me, you who do this:
you say you have entrusted your own soul to the ascetic, and falling at his
feet you beg, saying, Take my soul and bring it to God,


knowing that you do this not to a man but to God who said, He who hears you
hears me, and lying and withholding what you brought to God do you not think
you sin? Or do you think it is not a sin, to take away again the things given
to God? And learn how you take away: that of your soul you make the ascetic a
trustworthy priest, giving this to bring to God, but concerning the cold and
pitiable and wretched affairs of the matter of this world you judge him and
test him. How then do you not think you sin a sin not ordinary but sacrilege
itself, whose judgment the blessed Peter shows in the acts, when having
convicted Ananias and Sapphira as having set apart from the price of the
field, which having offered they were commanded to sell, so as to go forward
into the ministry of the brethren, he condemned these to a bitter death, and
he put great fear in all the church. But you will say, I do not take away the
renunciation, but I take thought that it be managed as due and well. First of
all the word does not even permit you to be a tester of things that no longer
belong, for you do not understand the due or not due, and you are convicted as
taking away even the renunciation; you are convicted besides these also as
lying in saying, I do not take away. =53.10= For tell me: did you not give the
soul then also the money through the hands of the ascetic to God? Therefore
these are no longer in your authority, and a firm and true renunciation is, of
a soul when it does not accomplish its own wills at all, but, just as matter
to a craftsman, thus you subject all your own senses to him to whom you

ruminate again and meditate and train the mind into the faithful oracles of
the commandments of God, that always the antagonist and pious reasoning,
dwelling in these, may be strengthened against the vice that is present, until
he puts on the power from on high, which having destroyed with authority the

pure. Those therefore who contend thus and discern themselves are reckoned
pure before God, standing outside of willing sins, and having presented their

always, as much as power, to accustom himself to the meditation of the good

every hour in faith the visitation of the spirit, that through such a contest

the wealth and the remaining delights of the world the true heavenly wealth
above, or luxury, or unfading glory, of which these are shadows. For this
world is an image of the true and eternal; whatever therefore the man sees
within in the soul he ought to take for benefit, that the mind may never be
idle from the meditation from the good things, since these can be accomplished

in truth. =53.16= For he who wishes to flee and be delivered of the evil one

world; whence having entered into tests and trials they played the man, and
the taking away of all the things that appear grieved them not at all, since
they had acquired the great and true possession, the confidence toward God, as
Job, having been made desert of all the appearing hope and having the body
wounded, conquered by the love toward God alone; for transferring the thought

and remain under all things that meet us, having been wounded with the love

and afflictions, but she lives in luxury and freedom from care, clearly she is
not chaste, not preserving the due goodwill toward the husband, for she must
suffer with him and ache with him; so also souls that desire to be joined to
the heavenly bridegroom and to reign with him ought to travel the narrow and
afflicted way, in which he also traveled, offering himself as a type to us;
but if they turn aside into another way, not bearing the marks and the

be rejected from the kingdom. =53.18= Let us therefore beseech God in faith

promises in writing, that To him who knocks it will be opened, and that I will
dwell in them and walk about, for if men, distracted by countless cares and
darkened by temptations, by which they are even forced to lie unsparingly,
whenever they make written promises, even if they wish to be ungrateful they
cannot, how much more will the unlying God fulfill his promise toward us, that
is sending down to us the grace and gift of the holy spirit? But we are truly
liars, not seeking him as he commanded in undoubting faith. But then we too
will be able to ask him with much boldness, whenever, as much as is possible
for us, we give our whole selves over to the lord, withdrawing from our own
wills, and not as living for ourselves, but as having been bought with
precious blood, doing according to the master's will, and thus having denied

56.1 What a monk is and according to what manner he is truly named, we ought
to know; as therefore the christ gives we speak. First he is so called,
because he is alone, for he abstains from a wife and has renounced the world
from within and from outside; that is from outside the matters and the worldly
affairs and from within the thoughts of these, that he may not receive the
reasonings of the worldly care. And second a monk is so called, so as to
beseech God in the unceasing prayer, that he may purify his mind from the many
and harsh reasonings, and the mind may become by itself a monk alone toward
the true God, not receiving the reasonings from the wickedness, but always to
be pure as one ought, and to remain sincere toward God. 56.2 For this is in
the choice of the man; if therefore he should wish to give the choice only
toward the lord, he himself roots out the passions and the reasonings of
wickedness from him, and thus having purified him, he plants in him the fruits
of the holy spirit, which is, Love, joy, peace, longsuffering, kindness,
goodness, faith, meekness, temperance; concerning which the apostle says,
Against such there is no law. For as much as God sees the choice of the man at
leisure and drawing near to him undistracted always, so much also he himself
multiplies his grace in him, and enriches him in it; and as much as he sees
our choice neglecting and not drawing near to him but distracted in the
material actions, so much also he himself withdraws, and he does not care


for us. For he has need only of our choice, since he is ready always to have
mercy on us and to light us, if only we should wish to give ourselves over.
56.3 For the soul, having been created by God, has by nature to love him and
to be bound to him; but the body, taken from the earth, minds the things in
the earth; and not only this, but it is also energized by Satan, and it drags
down the soul and the mind into the earthy cares; the monk therefore ought to
be discerning and to make himself safe in this part lest he ever be defeated
secretly without knowing. 56.4 And we ought again to learn what is that said
by the christ, Take up your cross and follow me; for he does not understand it
thus as one who ought to hang himself on wood and follow the lord, but the
monk will crucify himself from the worldly affairs, so as not to touch them;
and again that he may crucify his mind through all in the prayer, that he may
not neglect his salvation. And he ought not to receive the wicked reasonings,
but to discern, and to know, that they are of the wicked one; for he must
loose his mind from every distraction, that he may not be troubled by them;
for if he does not loose, he also prays in vain; for the mind wanders about
those things and supposedly prays, and his prayer does not go up toward his
God; for if he does not have a pure prayer filled in faith, God does not
accept. 56.5 The monk from himself neither has strength nor is able either to
stand against the devil, or to throw off from himself the reasonings of sin,
or to do a will of God, or to keep his commandment, or to stand against the

pray and to beseech him, that he himself may purify him from Satan and from
the energies of this one, and that he may be well pleased through his grace to
come and to reign into his soul, and he himself will do his own commandments
and his own will in him, and will entrust to him every virtue of
righteousness, first a true faith, an active prayer, a love from the whole
soul and power, hope, fasting, temperance, humility, meekness, longsuffering,
endurance, and the rest. And then such a one is not able to boast in himself

watchful, choosing the good, into which we are able to be saved, and to love
God and one another not simply but in truth; this love therefore the monk

because every command is completed there. For the written law recounts many
