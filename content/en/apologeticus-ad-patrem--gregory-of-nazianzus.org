ORATION IX.
Apology to his own father Gregory, Basil being present with him, when he was
ordained bishop of Sasima.
A. Again chrism and Spirit come upon me; and again I go mourning and downcast.
You wonder, perhaps. Isaiah too, before he saw the glory of the Lord, and the
throne high and lifted up, and the seraphim about it, utters nothing of this
kind, neither is he impatient, nor does he fear; but he cries out against
Israel, and spares himself and holds back, as one in no way to blame. But when
he saw these things, and heard the holy and mystical voice, as if he had come
to a greater sense of himself: Woe is me, he says, for I am pricked, and
whatever follows in the speech, that I may escape the blasphemy. I find also
Manoah of old among the judges, and Peter later, the stay of the Church: the
one saying, We have perished, O woman, we have seen God, when he perceived a
sight greater than his own nature and power; the other not bearing the
Savior's oversight and working, which he showed in the fishing to those
sailing with him, and therefore wondering, yet sending him from the boat, and
adding the cause, that he was not worthy of a divine appearing and converse.
B. And when I hear also of the centurion in the Gospels, asking for the power,
yet declining the presence, as if his roof could not contain a divine dignity
and greatness, I cannot blame myself for this cowardice and this =35.821=
gloom. For the sun convicts the weakness of the eye, and God, when he comes,
convicts the sickness of the soul; and to some he is light, to others fire, to
each according to the underlying matter and quality. What do we suppose
concerning Saul? For he was anointed, and partook of the Spirit, and was then
spiritual, nor would I say otherwise about him, but he also prophesied, and so
contrary to hope and likelihood that that wonder even became a proverb: Is
Saul also among the prophets? still said and heard even now. But since he did
not give himself wholly to the Spirit, nor was he turned purely into another
man, as had been declared, but something of the old spark of wickedness
remained, and of the evil seed, and there was in him a battle of spirit and
flesh: why must I make a tragedy of all that belongs to him? You know the
opposing spirit, and the psalmist by whom he was soothed. Yet this too is
known from there, that even if grace does not touch the unworthy, nor an
instrument wholly evil and out of tune (for it is well said that wisdom will
not enter a soul that works evil, and I am persuaded), still to keep the worth
and the harmony, as my speech holds, is a work no less than to have been well
tuned from the first and counted worthy, because of the changeable and
alterable character of human habit and nature; for whom even grace itself
often, that I may name of our evils the most miserable and most paradoxical,
having begotten pride and lifted them up, has brought down from God those who
approached not well, and we were thrown down in being lifted up, so that sin
might become exceedingly sinful, working death for me through the good.
C. These are the things which I, fearing, was filled with bitterness and
dejection, and I suffered something of the kind that children suffer toward
lightning, mixing pleasure with astonishment from the spectacle; I both loved
the Spirit at once and feared; and I needed some time, having turned in upon
myself, to sober up and become of the safer and better portion, so that, when
that which grieves had withdrawn, as tares in the seed, and the base thoughts
had yielded to the better, the Spirit might conquer purely, and taking me
might hold me for his own ministry and liturgy, for the perfecting of this
people, for the =35.824= steering of souls, for teaching in word, and in deed,
and by example, through the weapons of righteousness on the right and on the
left, for a pastoral art well fitted, drawing away from the world, bringing
near to God, wasting the body, adding to the spirit, fleeing darkness,
rejoicing in light, driving out wild beasts, gathering the fold, guarding
against cliffs and deserts, driving toward mountains and heights. Concerning
which even the most admirable Micah seems to me to speak, drawing us up from
the ground to our own heights: Draw near to everlasting mountains. Arise and
go, for this is not rest for you, even if the things that grovel and turn
below seem to some to be so.
D. Teach me this pastoral art, O you who are now my friends, shepherds and
fellow-shepherds. Give me its tokens, both you the common Father, who have
over time perfected and outlived many shepherds, and you the examiner and
judge of my philosophy. But (that you may receive the speech philosophically
from me), are we even able, while we are turned about in the midst of a storm
and surrounded with noise, to shepherd with skill, and to rear the flock? More
humane among sheep (if you are not displeased with me), when we shared in the
irrational pasture, than among shepherds, when we were counted worthy of the
spiritual. You have what you longed for, us under your hands, and you have
conquered the unconquered; and behold, with the rest, here is the speech for
you, which knowing you sought, and which, praising, you used to pelt as idle
with many and dense snowfalls of your words.
E. But I have something even to blame in the friendship: who of our common
friends will judge for me? or who will be an unbribed judge, that he may bring
the just vote, and not suffer what the many suffer, and show partiality in
judgment? Do you bid me speak the blame, and will you not again unleash the
speech upon me? There came to pass also for you toward us something unspoken,
O admirable one, a thing truly unspoken and unbelievable, and never before
heard concerning =35.825= us. We were not persuaded, but we were forced. O the
wonder! How all things have become new! And how far it has set us apart! What
do you wish me even to say, the throne, or the greatness of the grace? Only
lead, and prosper, and reign, and shepherd us who shepherd. For we are ready
to follow, and to be led by your pastoral soul, the high and inspired; for the
truth will be spoken, if even contrary to the law we have dared something out
of love. Teach your love concerning the flocks, your care and intelligence
together, the oversight, the watchings, the withdrawal of your flesh, which
they yielded to the spirit, the bloom of your color as you labor for the
flock, the intensity in gentleness, the calm and mild in action (a thing not
found in many, nor having many examples), the wars for the flock, the
victories which you have won in Christ.
F. Say to which of the pastures one must lead, to what springs one must go,
and which one must flee, whether pastures or streams; whom one must shepherd
with the staff, whom with the pipe; when one must lead to pasture, and when
one must call back from pasture; how one must war against the wolves, and how
one must not war against the shepherds; and most of all in the present time,
when shepherds have played the fool, and have scattered the sheep of the
pasture, that I may lament the same things with the most holy prophets. How
shall I strengthen the weak, and raise the fallen, and turn back the
wandering, and seek the lost, and keep the strong? How shall I both learn
these things and keep them according to the right account of the pastoral art,
and yours; but let me not become a bad shepherd, devouring the milk, and
wrapping myself in the wool, and slaughtering or selling the fatter, and
leaving the rest to the beasts and the cliffs, shepherding myself, not the
sheep, which was the reproach of the old rulers of Israel. Teach me these
things, and support me with these words, and with these commands shepherd and
fellow-shepherd, and save, as by teaching, so also by prayers, both me and
this holy flock, for safety to me, for a boast to you in the day of appearing
and revelation of the great God, and of our chief shepherd Jesus Christ,
through whom and with whom is the glory to the Father almighty with the holy
and life-giving Spirit both now and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
