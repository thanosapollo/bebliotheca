* On Psalm 145
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

HOMILY Spoken in Great Week, in which there is teaching why it is called Great
Week; and on the text, "Praise the Lord, O my soul"; and on the jailer in the
Acts.

=55.519=

1. We have completed the voyage of the fast, and by the grace of God we have
now arrived at the harbor; but let us not be slothful on this account, because
we have arrived at the harbor, but rather let us be more earnest on this
account, because we have reached the end. For this is what pilots also do:
whenever they are about to bring a merchant-ship of ten thousand measures,
filled with grain and with cargoes piled high, into the mouth of the harbor,
they are anxious and in great fear, lest after the long seas they dash the
ship against some rock and there sink the whole cargo. So also we must fear
and be anxious, lest at the end we fall away from the reward of our labors.
Therefore we must even intensify our earnestness.

This is what runners also do: when they see themselves coming near the prizes,
then they intensify the race still more. So also athletes, after countless
wrestlings and countless victories, when they draw near the crowns, then they
rouse themselves still more, and they strain their eagerness. Let us then do
this now as well. For what the harbor is to pilots, and prizes to runners, and
crowns to athletes, this the present week is to us, the sum of the goods, and
the wrestlings about the crowns are now. Therefore we also call it Great: not
because its days have greater length than all others; for there are others
that are longer; nor because they are more in number; for they are equal to
the others; but because great achievements have been accomplished for us in it
by the Master. For in this Great Week the long-standing tyranny of the devil
was destroyed, death was quenched, the strong one was bound, his goods were
plundered, sin was taken away, the curse was destroyed, paradise was opened,
heaven became passable, men were mixed with the angels, the middle wall of the
partition was taken away, the barrier was removed, the God of peace made peace
of the things above and the things upon the earth.

For this reason it is called Great Week; and just as this is the sum of the
remaining weeks, so the Great Saturday is the head of this one; and as the
head is in a body, so is the Saturday in the week. Therefore many intensify
their earnestness in it; and some increase the fast, others the sacred vigils,
others work a more abundant almsgiving, by their earnestness about good deeds
and by their strained piety of life bearing witness to the greatness of the
benefaction that has come to us from God.

For just as, when the Lord raised Lazarus, all those in Jerusalem went out to
meet him, =55.520= and by the multitude they bore witness that he had raised
the dead (for the earnestness of those who went out was a proof of the
miracle); so also the present earnestness about this Great Week is a token and
proof of the greatness of the achievements in it. For we do not go out from
one city meeting Christ today, nor from Jerusalem only, but from the whole
inhabited world countless churches from every side go out to meet Jesus, not
holding and waving palm branches, but offering almsgiving and loving-kindness
and virtue and fasting and tears and prayers and vigils and all piety to the
Master Christ. And it is not we alone who honor this week, but also the kings
of our inhabited world have not honored it at random, granting a truce to all
who transact the public affairs of the cities, so that, having enjoyed leisure
from those things, they may attend throughout all these days to the spiritual
service. For this reason they have also shut the doors of the courts. Let
every dispute, he says, and form of battle and of punishment be idle; let the
hands of the executioners rest a little. The Master's achievements have become
common; let there be then also some good from us the servants. And they have
honored it not only by this earnestness and honor, but also by another not
less than this. Imperial letters are sent down, saying that those who dwell in
the prison are to be released from their bonds. For just as our Master, having
come to be in Hades, released all who were held by death; so also the
servants, contributing what is in their power, and imitating the Master's
loving-kindness, release from sensible bonds, since they have no strength for
the intelligible ones.

2. We too honor this week, and in place of palms I also have gone out with
you, putting forth a word of teaching, laying down the two mites like the
widow. Those men then went out holding palm branches, and they cried out and
said: Hosanna in the highest; blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord.
Let us also go out, and instead of palm branches displaying a flourishing
choice, let us cry out, as we sang in response today: Praise the Lord, O my
soul; I will praise the Lord in my life; both that Davidic saying, and this;
or rather neither that is Davidic, nor this; but both are of the grace of the
Spirit. For the prophet uttered, but the Paraclete moved the prophet's tongue.
For this reason he says: My tongue is the pen of a swiftly writing scribe.
Just as the pen does not write of itself, but from the authority of the hand
that moves it; so also the tongue of the prophets did not utter of itself, but
from the grace of God.

But why did he not say, My tongue is the pen of a scribe, simply, but, The pen
of a =55.521= swiftly writing scribe? That you may learn that the wisdom is
spiritual; therefore also the ease is great, and the course is great. For men
speaking of themselves compose, deliberate, delay, drag out much time; but
there, since the words welled up as from a fountain, and there was no
hindrance, but the influx of thoughts overcame the course of the tongue, for
this reason he said: My tongue is the pen of a swiftly writing scribe. The
streams well up below with much abundance, he says. Therefore the swiftness is
great.

We need no consideration, nor study and toil. But let us see what he says.
Praise the Lord, O my soul. Let us also psalm these things today with David.
For even if he is not present in body, yet he is present in spirit. And that
the righteous are present with us, and psalm with us, hear what Abraham says
to the rich man. For when that man said, Send Lazarus, that my brothers,
learning the things in Hades, may set their own affairs right; he says to him:
They have Moses and the prophets. And yet Moses and all the prophets had long
since died in the body, but they had them through the writings. For if
someone, setting up a lifeless image of a child or a friend, thinks that the
one who has departed is present, and imagines him through the lifeless image;
much more do we, through the divine Scriptures, enjoy the company of the
saints, having not the images of their bodies, but of their souls. For the
things said by them are images of their souls. Do you wish to learn that the
righteous live and are present? No one calls the dead as witnesses; but Christ
called them witnesses of his own divinity, and before these David himself,
that he may teach you that he lives. For when the Jews were disputing about
him, he says to them: What do you think about the Christ? whose son is he?
They say to him, David's. He says to them: How then does David in the Spirit
call him Lord, saying: The Lord said to my Lord, Sit at my right hand? Did you
see how David lives? For if he were not living, he would not have called him a
witness of his divinity. And he did not say, How then did David in the Spirit
call him Lord? but, He calls him Lord; that he may show that he is still
present, and speaks through the writings. David once psalmed in psalms, and we
with David today. He had a kithara of lifeless strings; the Church has a
kithara stretched of living strings. For our tongues are the strings of the
kithara, sending forth a different sound, but a harmonious piety. For both
women and men, and old men and young, are divided according to age, but are
not divided according to the word of hymnody; for the Spirit, blending each
one's voice, works one melody in all; as David himself also made clear,
calling every age and both natures to this symphony and saying: Let every
breath praise the Lord. Praise, O my soul, =55.522= the Lord.

Why did he leave the flesh aside? Why does he converse with the body about
nothing? Did he split the living being into two? By no means; but he first
rouses the craftsman. And that he did not split the flesh from the soul, hear
how he says elsewhere: O God, my God, unto you I rise early; My soul has
thirsted for you, how often my flesh for you, in a land. But in hymns show me
the flesh also being called, he says. Bless the Lord, O my soul, and all that
is within me, his holy name. Did you see the flesh also sharing in the
symphony? What then is, And all that is within me, his holy name? The sinews,
he says, the bones, the veins, the arteries, and all the inward parts.

3. And how can the things within us bless God? It has no voice, it has no
mouth, it has no tongue. For the soul can; but how can the things within,
having neither voice, nor tongue, nor mouth, bless God? Just as The heavens
declare the glory of God. For just as the heavens have neither tongue, nor
mouth, nor lips, but by the beauty of their appearance striking the beholders
they send them by the wonder to the praise of him who made them; so also if
you go over all the things within us with your reasoning, if you see the
difference of quality, of activity, of power, of symphony, of formation, of
position, of number, of harmony, you will utter that prophetic saying: How
magnified are your works, O Lord! in wisdom you have made them all. Did you
see how the inward parts bless God without voice and mouth and tongue? Why
then does he converse with the soul? So that while the tongue is speaking that
one may not wander, which we often suffer both in prayers and in hymnodies; so
that the symphony may be from both sides. For when, praying, you do not hear
the words of God, how will God hear your petition? When then he says, Praise
the Lord, O my soul, he says this: let the supplications be brought up from
within, from the depth of the heart. For Paul also says: I will psalm with the
spirit, and I will psalm also with the mind.

The soul is some excellent musician, it is a craftsman; the body is an
instrument, occupying the place of kithara and flute and lyre. Now the other
musicians are not always with all the instruments, but sometimes they take
them up, and sometimes they lay them aside; for the time of melody is not
continual for them; therefore they do not continually handle the instruments;
but God, wishing to teach you that you ought always to hymn and bless him, has
united the instrument to the craftsman continually. For that one must praise
always, hear what the Apostle says: Pray without ceasing, in everything give
thanks. Since then one must pray without ceasing, the instrument is without
ceasing with the craftsman. Praise the Lord, O my soul. Formerly David said
these things with one tongue; but now, since he has died, he utters these
things with countless tongues, not only those among us, but also those
everywhere in the inhabited world. Did you see that he has not died, but
lives? For how has he died who has so many tongues, and speaks through so many
mouths? Truly great is praise; for it cleanses our soul, and plants much piety
in us.

Do you wish to learn how great is the power of hymns to God? =55.523= The
three children, hymning, thus quenched the Babylonian furnace; or rather they
did not quench it, but what was far more wonderful, they trampled with their
feet the flame as it burned, as if it were clay. This hymn, having entered the
prison in Paul's case, loosed the bonds, opened the doors of the prison, shook
the foundations, and with much astonishment frightened the jailer.

For at midnight, he says, Paul and Silas were hymning. Then what happened?
What? The new and paradoxical thing, of course: the bonds were loosed, those
who were bound bound those who were loosed; and yet it is the nature of bonds
to hold the one bound in much exactness and to make him subject; but now the
jailer, loosed, came to the feet of the bound Paul. For it is the nature of
sensible bonds to hold the one bound; but so great is the power of the bonds
that are for Christ's sake, as to make those who are loosed subject to those
who are bound. He threw them into the inner prison, and being within they
opened the outer doors. He made their feet fast in the wood, and their feet
that had been made fast loosed the others' hands. Then the jailer fell before
him, he says, fearing, trembling, groaning, in agony, weeping. What has
happened? Did you not bind? Did you not hold in security? And why do you
wonder, O man, if he opened the prison, when he even received authority to
open the heavens. Whatever you bind, he says, on earth, shall be bound in the
heavens; and whatever you loose on earth, shall be loosed in the heavens. He
loosed bonds of sins; why do you wonder if he looses bonds of iron? He loosed
bonds of demons, he delivered souls bound under them; and why do you wonder if
he released the chains of the prisoners? And see the double wonder: he loosed
again, and he bound. For he loosed the bonds, but he bound their heart. For
that they were loosed, they did not know. He opened and he shut; for he opened
the doors of the prison, but he shut the eyes of the prisoners' heart, so that
they might not perceive those doors opened, lest even making use of the
license they should leap away into flight. Did you see him both binding and
loosing, and opening and shutting?

4. For this reason these things happened at night, so that the matter might
take place without tumult and without all disturbance. For the apostles did
nothing toward display or ambition. The chief jailer fell before him. What
then did Paul? Did you see his wonder? Did you see his paradoxical works?
Behold also his care, behold his loving-kindness.

He cried out, he says, and said: Do yourself no harm; for we are all here. He
who had bound him bitterly he did not let die bitterly; he did not remember
the wrong. For we are all here, he says. See the lack of pride. He did not
say, I did these paradoxical things, but what? For we are all here. Paul
numbered himself with the prisoners. And that man, seeing, wondered at what
had happened, was astonished at the miracle, gave thanks to God. The jailer
was truly worthy of much care and loving-kindness; he did not think the things
that had happened to be sorcery. Why then did he not think the things
happening to be sorcery? He heard them hymning God; and a sorcerer never hymns
God.

For he had received many sorcerers, since he was a jailer; but none of those
ever displayed such a work, he did not loose bonds, he did not display such
care. =55.524= For Paul chose to remain bound, and not to flee, so as to
deliver that man from death. For he had rushed in, having a sword in his hands
and a torch; for the devil wished through the slaughter to snatch him away
beforehand from repentance, but with a more vigorous voice Paul more swiftly
gained the salvation of his soul. For he did not simply cry out, but with a
great voice, saying: We are all here. The jailer wondered at the care, the one
loosed fell before the one bound, and what does he say? Sirs, what must I do,
that I may be saved? You bound, and you are at a loss? you made fast in the
wood, and you seek a way of repentance and salvation? Did you see heat? did
you see earnestness? He did not put it off, but when he was released from the
fear, he was not released from the benefaction, but immediately he rushes upon
the salvation of his own soul. It was night, and midnight. He did not say: Let
us consider, let day come; but immediately he ran upon salvation. This man is
some great one, he says, he surpasses human nature. I saw his wonder, I
wondered at the loving-kindness. Having suffered countless evils from me,
being in the last extremities, having taken me who bound him into his hands,
being able to slay me, not only did he do nothing, but even when I was about
to slaughter myself, and to thrust the sword through my throat, he prevented
it. Rightly he says: Sirs, what must I do that I may be saved. For it was not
the miracles alone among the apostles that drew those who believed, but before
the miracles the life.

For this reason he says: Let your light shine before men, that they may see
your good works, and glorify your Father who is in the heavens. Did you see
the jailer's heat? See also Paul's heat. He did not put it off, he did not
overlook; but being in the bonds, being in the wood, in the stripes, he
immediately initiated him, and with him the whole household, and after the
spiritual bath, and after the spiritual table, he also set before him the
fleshly one. But why did he shake the prison? He wished to rouse the jailer
toward the sight of the things happening; he loosed the sensible bonds of the
fellow-prisoners, that he might loose the intelligible bonds of the jailer.
Christ did the opposite of these things. A paralyzed man came to him having a
certain double paralysis, the one from sins, the other from the body. First he
loosed the paralysis from sins, saying thus: Child, your sins are forgiven.
Then since those men were in doubt, and were blaspheming, and saying, No one
can forgive sins, except God alone, wishing to show them that he is truly God,
wishing to judge them out of their own mouth, that he might be about to say,
Out of your mouth I will judge you; you said that no one can forgive sins,
except God alone; behold I forgive, he says, sins, and confess my divinity;
from your own judgment I bring forth the vote. But there the intelligible was
first, and the sensible later; here the contrary, first the sensible bonds,
and then the intelligible ones were taken away. Did you see how great is the
power of hymns? how much praise avails? how much prayer avails? Great then
always is the power of =55.525= prayer; but for prayer to be with fasting
makes the soul more powerful. Now there is much sobriety of reasonings among
us, now the mind is roused, and the soul looks round at all the things above.
For this reason Scripture always yoked prayer to fasting. How and when? Do not
deprive one another, he says, except perhaps by agreement, that you may have
leisure for fasting and prayer. And again elsewhere, This kind does not come
out, except in prayer and fasting. And again elsewhere, Having prayed, he
says, and having fasted, they laid hands on them.

5. Do you see prayer everywhere with fasting? For then both a more delightful
and a more approved melody is sent up from the lyre. The strings are not
soaked, dissolved by the excess of drunkenness, but the reasoning is taut, the
mind is roused, the soul is sober. Thus one must approach God and converse
only with the Only. For if, having something necessary to say to friends,
taking them aside, we converse thus; much more must we do this in the case of
God, having entered into the chamber with much quiet; and we shall altogether
obtain all things, if we make the request profitably. For prayer is a great
good, when it comes to be with a thankful and sober mind; and how will it also
be thankful? If we train ourselves to give thanks to God not only when
receiving, but also when failing to obtain. For sometimes God gives, and
sometimes he does not give; and he does both usefully; so that, whether you
receive or do not receive, you have received by not receiving; and whether you
succeed or do not succeed, you have succeeded in not succeeding. For there are
times when not receiving what we ask becomes more profitable than receiving.
For if it were not often advantageous for us not to receive, he would
altogether have given always; but to fail usefully is to succeed. For this
reason he also often postpones our request, not wishing to drag us along, but
by the delay of the giving devising the continual attendance. For since, often
having received what we ask, after receiving we dissolve our earnestness about
prayers, wishing to intensify our vigil in the supplications, he drags out the
giving. This is what affectionate fathers also do; the slothful of the
children, who also run off toward childish playthings, they often hold beside
themselves by a promise of a very great gift; for this reason they sometimes
postpone, and sometimes they do not give.

For there are times when we ask a harmful request; but God, who knows better
the things profitable for us, does not assent to our prayers, managing what is
advantageous for us even when we are ignorant. And what wonder, if we are not
heard, when this has happened even in the case of Paul? For he also, having
asked often, failed; and not only was he not pained, but he even gave thanks
to God; For this, he says, I besought the Lord thrice; and the Thrice
signifies often. And if Paul, having asked often, did not succeed, much more
then must we persist. But let us see how, when having asked often he did not
receive, he was disposed toward not receiving. For not only was he not pained,
but he even boasts, =55.526= because he did not receive. For having said that
For this I besought the Lord thrice; and he said to me: My grace is sufficient
for you; for my power is perfected in weakness; he added, that Most gladly
therefore I will boast in my weaknesses.

6. Did you see a household servant's good sense? He asked to be delivered from
the weakness, and God did not assent; and not only was Paul not pained, but he
even boasts in the weakness. So also let us prepare our own souls, whether God
gives or does not give these things we ask, let us give thanks to God for
both. For he does both usefully for us. If he is lord of giving, then also of
when to give, and of what to give, and of not giving. You do not know the
things profitable, as he knows them clearly. Often you ask things harmful and
perilous; but he, caring more for your salvation, does not attend to the
request, but before this he looks everywhere to what is profitable for you.

For if fathers according to the flesh do not grant all things the children
ask, not because they despise them, but because they especially care for them;
much more does God, both loving more, and knowing more than all what is
profitable, do this. Let us then have leisure for prayers continually, not
only through the day, but also through the night. For hear what the prophet
himself says: At midnight I rose to confess to you upon the judgments of your
righteousness. A man who was a king, being in so many cares, having been
entrusted with the rule of peoples and cities and nations, taking thought for
peace, dissolving wars, seeing an ineffable crowd of affairs always standing
about him, not having even a little to breathe, not only the days, but also
the nights he added to the prayers. And if the king who lived in such luxury,
who had so many cares, who was surrounded by so many affairs, did not even
rest at night, but continued making the prayers more exactly than the monks
sitting in the mountains, what forgiveness, tell me, shall we obtain, living
in much license, and having taken up the private and unbusied life, not only
sleeping through all the nights, but not even in the day accomplishing the
appointed prayers with the fitting vigil? Prayer is a great weapon, prayer is
a great adornment, and security and a harbor, and a treasure of goods, and
inviolable wealth. Now when we need men, we also need expenditure of money,
and slavish flattery, and much going about and business. For often it is not
possible to converse directly with the masters themselves to grant the favor,
but it is necessary first to serve their ministers and stewards and overseers,
both with money, and with words, and in every way, and then through those to
be able to receive the request; but in the case of God it is not so; nor,
being entreated thus through others, as through ourselves asking, does he
assent to the favor. And here it is possible to gain both when receiving and
when not receiving; but in the case of men we have often been harmed in both.

Since then the gain is greater and the ease is greater for those who approach
God, let us not despise prayer. For then he will be more reconciled to you,
then he more readily gives you what you ask, when you entreat through
yourself, when
=55.527= you present a pure mind, when a sober reasoning, when you do not ask
as a side-work, which many do, and their tongue says the words of the prayer,
but the soul goes about many places of the house, of the marketplace, of the
roads; this whole thing is the devil's device. For since he knows that at that
time we can obtain remission of sins, wishing to silt up for us the harbor of
prayer, he rises up at that very time, knocking our mind away from the things
said, so that we may go away more having suffered loss than having gained.
Knowing these things then, O man, when you approach God, consider to whom you
approach; and the trustworthiness of him who is about to give you the favor is
enough for you toward sobriety. Look up to heaven, and consider with whom your
word is. For if someone conversing with a man a little elevated in human
honors, even if he be more slothful than all, altogether rouses himself, and
bids the soul be sober; much more if we understand that we are conversing with
the Master of the angels, we shall take from this a sufficient occasion to
attend to ourselves. And if we must also mention some other =55.528= method,
through which we shall be able to escape this sloth, I would say this: Often
having completed the prayer, having heard nothing of what we have said, we
have gone away. If then we consider this, let us immediately take it up again;
and if we suffer the same thing again, let us say it also a third and a fourth
time, and let us not first stand away while praying, until we have said the
whole of it with a sober mind. If the devil perceives that we do not first
stand away, until we have said it with earnestness and a sober mind, the one
lying in wait will then stand away, knowing that there will be nothing more
for him from the plot, than to compel us often to take up the same prayer.

We receive many wounds during the day, beloved, from our own, from strangers,
in the marketplace, in the house, from public affairs, from private, from
neighbors, from friends. Let us apply the medicines of all those wounds at the
time of prayer. For God is able, if we approach him with a sober mind and a
kindled soul and a warm conscience, and ask pardon, to give us remission of
all our offenses; which may we all obtain, by the grace and loving-kindness of
our Lord Jesus Christ, with whom to the Father be the glory together with the
Holy Spirit, unto the ages of ages. Amen.
