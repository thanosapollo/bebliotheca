* Another Encomium on Saint Stephen the Protomartyr
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Another encomium of the same on Saint Stephen the Protomartyr.
Christ sojourned in the world for salvation, and after him the fruits of the
Church sprang up. The martyr of the truth shone forth, and the martyrs of the
great economy shone with him. The disciples followed the Teacher, walking in
the Lord's footsteps: after Christ, the Christ-bearers; after the sun of
righteousness, the luminaries of the inhabited world; and first among us
Stephen blossomed, not plaited from the Jewish thorns, but from the Church's
abundance offered as first fruit to the Lord. For the Jews, having plaited a
crown from thorns, set it on the Savior's head, displaying to the Master of
the vineyard fruits worthy of their evil husbandry, as he had foretold through
the prophecy, saying: The vineyard of the Lord of Sabaoth is the house of
Israel, and the man of Judah a beloved young plant. I waited for it to make
grapes, but it made thorns. But the laborers of the evangelical truth offer to
the Master, as a first prelude of piety and first-fruits of the husbandry,
Stephen the holy man, as a crown truly fitted together from many and various
virtues.
For first this marvelous man was entrusted with the care of the widows, having
been attested =46.724= by the judgment and election of the apostles to be a
man faithful and full of the Holy Spirit, then by the power of spiritual
wisdom. For by the divine word he displayed such advocacy for the preaching
that even the great wonders of the divine operation ran together with his
teachings. For Stephen, it says, full of faith and power, did great signs. For
he did not think it right that anything should become an obstacle to him in
the zeal concerning the widows, but he both held to this eagerly and did not
abandon that; and upon him there was a great wonder, and an advantage of a
labor-loving mind. He was entrusted with widows, and he traded in souls,
feeding some with bread, teaching others with the word, and setting a bodily
table before some, and putting forward a spiritual feast for others.
For he was a good man, and full of the Holy Spirit: by goodness of purpose
undertaking the ministry of the poor; by boldness of speech and power of the
Spirit stopping the mouths of the enemies of the truth. He opposed all, then,
and overcame all by the word of truth, casting down reasonings, and every
height raised up against the knowledge of God. And such strength of word was
present to him that, according to the testimony of Holy Scripture, no one was
able to withstand the wisdom and the spirit by which he spoke. But the herald
of the truth was being led to the council of unbelief: for we must, having
thus run through, render to the Protomartyr the due which yesterday the
weakness of the body did not permit us to fulfill, and today fulfill their own
memorial to the holy apostles.
For first the encomia of the saints are not bounded by days or times: For the
righteous, it says, shall be in everlasting remembrance; and then, for those
in whom the things of purpose are not divided. Neither then are martyrs
without apostles, nor again apostles apart from those. For teachers of martyrs
are apostles; and images of the apostles are the martyrs. So then the blessed
Stephen, bearing their image and stamp, a cross, also through death first
bound on the crown of martyrdom. But nevertheless through the endurance of
martyrdom he made the teachers more august, and became truly a crown: for a
crown of the good teachers was not an honor of praise, but was an advance of
the Church, as indeed the divine Apostle writing to the Corinthians says: My
beloved and longed-for brethren, my joy and crown, so stand.
But let us return to the matter at hand. The Christ-bearer entered into the
council of the Christ-killers; the sheep leapt into the synagogue of the
wolves =46.725=; but he was not as a sheep simply taken by wild beasts, but a
sheep shepherded by Christ, and contending against the wolves. For they were
raging and were sawn through, biting the flock with accusations and threats;
but he rather tore them with refutations than they with threats and
accusations.
Let us not run past what is said baldly or superficially. For this man,
against so great a synagogue of those doing evil, and against so great an
onslaught of wolves, dared to speak boldly against the injustice, and to say
after that long teaching: Stiff-necked and uncircumcised in heart and ears,
you always resist the Holy Spirit, as your fathers did, and what follows.
Thus he who appeared on earth, but was mirroring the things in heaven; and
clothed with the nature of a man, but transformed into the look and form of an
angel (and none of these things is unlikely: for it was truly fitting that in
the Protomartyr the dignity of the martyrs be shown, and that all thereafter
know the works of the new grace): the longing of martyrdom not only grants
angelic dignity, but also opens the gates of heavens; no longer sending the
souls off to death, but committing the spirit into the hands of Christ. For
the Lord's man, the Savior, addressed his own Father on the cross, saying:
Father, into your hands I commit my spirit; but the servant of Christ,
Stephen, stretching up toward the Master, says, Lord Jesus, receive my spirit;
and with this voice he laid down his soul.
Angels were receiving back their own dancer, casting praises upon him from
above rather than the Jews with the stones from below. But Stephen, having
thus contended the good contest, received the lot in the heavens. And with
this Stephen all the precious stones were suddenly plaited together, the most
divine heralds of the Gospels, after whom the martyrs, and after them again
those who shone with saving virtue; and first those commemorated at present,
flashing forth much and bright the beauty of piety, I mean Peter, and James,
and John, who are both leaders of the apostolic harmony, and crowns of the
Church's good repute.
For I do not depart from the name of Stephen; but saying it often and ten
thousand times, still thirsting I take it up again: for there is no satiety of
Stephen for those awaiting the blessed end of the crowns. Therefore, if one
must speak truth-lovingly, from Stephen again we feast on crowns, and we share
in their memorials, since we hope to share, and to remain, and we shall be
glorified together: for as the promise confirms =46.728=, the communion of
faith is multiplied.
And again for us, brethren, there is the enjoyment of good things, in that the
Lord's day of the resurrection has shone together with the memorial of the
martyrs. For on this day first the illumination of the glory of the Gospel of
Christ shone upon our minds, in which, having made the saving rays of
righteousness bloom, it made the darkness of impiety vanish, and brightened
the souls by the knowledge of the truth. And see for me the wonderful and
greatest of the benefaction.
For this sensible sun, rising from the east, and prefacing the day with the
forerunning gleams of its rays, covers and wraps with its sparkles the world
under heaven; but it hides and dims all together the choirs of the stars, so
that he alone is seen circling the heavenly vaults. But our Lord Jesus Christ,
having risen upon us from on high, according to the prophecy that says
concerning him, Whereby the Dayspring from on high has visited us, not only
did not hide the saints who shone like stars before his sojourn and became his
forerunners, but he also made those brighter, and made other luminaries shine
together with himself.
For prophets shone after his coming; rather even more than before it. For
prophecy being still dim in the evangelical fulfillments of the Scribes, the
Savior, having come into the world, brightened all and made them clear, having
become the fullness of the law and the prophets. For he did not come to
destroy the law and the prophets, but to fulfill.
And upon the new grace the Savior, saying concerning himself, =46.729= I am
the light of this world, the fountain of goodness, having come forth from a
good Father, did not think it unworthy to impart his own title to those who
serve him; but he says to his own disciples: You are the light of the world;
and, Let your works shine before men.
And what greater thing we have to bring forward for a reminder of what has
been said, this, by the grace of God, we will again set forth: John the
Baptist, named a lamp, and proclaimed beforehand in the Psalms, and attested
by the Lord. For the Prophet, in the person of the Father, says concerning him
through the hymnody: I have prepared a lamp for my Christ, that is, I have
prepared a servant and forerunner of the light; and the Lord, confirming the
Father's voice, says, He was the lamp that burns.
But nevertheless he who was the lamp was so far from being dimmed by the
presence of the Lord, who was sun of righteousness, that he even shone more,
the Baptist having become at once herald and theologian of the Savior himself.
John then, because he lighted only one house, that of Israel, was named a
lamp.
But the apostles of the Savior were proclaimed not lamps, nor stars, but
luminaries, not shining in one climate, nor in one corner, but illuminating
all that is under heaven. And leaders and chiefs of these are Peter, and
James, and John, who today are honored by their martyrdoms for Christ, having
run in concert toward the end of life, but having contended in different
manners of martyrdom.
For he who was shown as first-stander and chief of the apostolic choir carried
off a glory proper to the dignity, having been honored with the like-suffering
of the Savior: for having been nailed to a cross, he stamped the Master's
image of the King (and I call the cross a royal image); not being ashamed at
the passion, but being proud of the great trophy. For neither to them, nor to
us, nor to those after us, nor to any other, of our Lord Jesus Christ, as also
Paul says ... for so also Peter appears holding in honor the venerable ... For
he thought it right to be hung head-downward by those who crucified him,
through much reverence, that he might not seem to be equal to the Savior, who
was crucified for all humanity, and with broad hands, as it were through the
cross, embraced the inhabited world.
And James was beheaded, hastening to receive his true head, Christ. For the
head of a man is Christ, according to the Apostle, and head of the whole
Church together.
And the blessed John, having contended in many and various contests through
life, and having excelled in all the achievements of piety, empty into water
this =46.732= end ... having been judged, is numbered with the choir of the
martyrs; (for among those who judge, martyrdom is judged not from the outcome
of the passion, but from the choice of the longing);
But such was the manner of death, for those who through death have granted the
immortal memory also to the Churches. And it was truly fitting that the
memorials of the aforementioned men be celebrated together, not only because
of the agreement of piety, but also because of the equal honor of the dignity.
For these alone always spending time with the Lord, and having as it were an
exceptional place besides the rest of the apostles, and boldness of speech,
not according to a taking-up of human friendship, but according to a divine
judgment of truth.
One can find them alone taken aside by the Lord in the greatest of the
wonders, as most faithful and truest witnesses of the things that happened:
this in the vision on the mountain (for there was there only Peter, and James,
and John, when the Lord, having transfigured his own body, changed it into a
more glorious and brighter divinity, setting Moses and Elijah beside himself,
and overshadowing with the cloud of light, showed that great image of the
kingdom); and this at the daughter of Jairus, whom he raised when she had
died; for there too he had these as overseers of the wonder-working that took
place. And still, that I may not linger saying each thing, even at the very
time of the saving passion, taking the same men, he dared to say, as to
faithful and most steadfast men: Now is my soul troubled.
And these things have been said by us, not for the pulling-down of the rest of
the apostles, but for a witness of the virtue of those commemorated; and if
one must speak truth-lovingly, also for a common encomium of the apostles: for
to define preeminences and advances among the saints would not be of human
examination, but of God's judgment and truth.
And we who have been counted worthy to share in memorials of such and so great
men must give thanks, not as much as we owe, for this is impossible, but as
much as we are able, for this is acceptable. And the saints require these
honors from us, not that they may gain something by being praised, but that we
may share by being benefited.
And that again I think none of the pious is ignorant of, that we celebrate the
memorials not only of Peter, and James, =46.733= and John, but also of the
whole apostolic harmony together. For if according to the truth of the
doctrines, holding the rank of members, they fill up the form of one body,
clearly when one part of me is glorified, as the Apostle says, all the members
are glorified together, and especially in the case of those blessed and most
perfect men, for whom all things agree toward truth, and the manner is of one
mind, and the faith of one glory, and as the advantages of piety are common to
these, common also are the dignities from the truth.
And who would not reasonably be glad, and be filled with the Holy Spirit
himself, having been counted worthy to share with the apostolic choir, which
led the whole inhabited world to the knowledge of the truth, which spread the
nets of piety over all the inhabited world, and fixed everywhere the
hunting-snares of the truth, that having caught all humanity together, made
wild by wickedness, they might bring it to him who tames and saves? Their
sound then went out into all the earth. The foundations of the Church, the
pillars and the stays of the truth, these are the ever-flowing springs of
salvation, which pour forth a great and rich and God-like stream of teaching.
To these the prophetic voice also sends us, saying: Draw water with gladness
from the springs of Salvation. Peter the head of the apostles is commemorated,
and with him the remaining members of the Church are glorified together; and
the Church of God is made firm. For this man, according to the gift given him
by the Lord, is the unbreakable and most fortified rock, upon which the Savior
built the Church.
James and John again are commemorated, and being addressed by the Savior as
sons of thunder, they bring with them all the rain-bearing clouds; for when
the thunder bursts, the clouds also must be gathered. Clouds then one must
take to be the apostolic and prophetic words, of which, even if the times of
the preaching are different, yet the laws of piety are in concert; for the
gifts have sprung for them from one and the same Spirit.
But why dare to go out against the impossible, and try to commemorate worthily
the apostolic virtue? Our ambition of praises is not toward Simon, the one
known from the fishery, but toward that man's solid faith, and the stay of the
whole Church together. Nor again do we have the word toward the sons of
Zebedee, but toward the Boanerges, who are interpreted sons of thunder. Where
now will the briefest voice of the word appear, when so great and so mighty a
thunder is sounding round the ears of all?
Therefore, having hastened to fulfill this alone, our gratitude toward the
saints, we take refuge in =46.736= the safe silence, knowing well this, that
by this one thing we are counted worthy to share in the memorials of the
saints, in imitating and emulating their virtues; not carrying about their
life in the word, but preserving their manner in the purpose.
For what will show us genuine disciples of those is not custom with unreason,
but piety with reverence, and a life of like glory, and a manner of like zeal.
Do you honor the memory of martyrs? Honor also the purpose: for communion of
the memory is the agreement of the purpose. For did the illumination of the
knowledge of the glory of the Gospel of Christ shine upon those alone? Was the
grace sent forth to those alone? The commandments are common, the manner is
common, one is the president of the labors, one is the prize of the truth, of
which may it be that we all be counted worthy, by the prayers and
intercessions of the saints commemorated, by the grace of our Lord Jesus
Christ, to whom be the glory and the power unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
