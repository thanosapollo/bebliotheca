Encyclical letter of the blessed Athanasius, bishop of Alexandria 1.1 To the
fellow-ministers in each place, beloved lords, Athanasius greeting in the
Lord. The terrible things we have suffered are also unbearable, and it is not
possible to make mention of them worthily; but in order that the terror of the
things that have happened may be able to be known more quickly, I thought it
good 1.2 to recall a history from the Scriptures. A certain Levite, having
been wronged in the matter of his wife and having seen the excess of the
abomination (for the woman was a Hebrew and happened to be of the tribe of
Judah), and having been struck with amazement at the lawless deeds dared
against him, as the divine Scripture of the Judges declares, divided the woman
and sent her throughout every tribe of Israel, in order that such a wrong
might be believed to be not against him alone, but common also to all, and in
order that, if they should have fellow-feeling, they might avenge it, but if
they should overlook it, they might 1.3 be ashamed as having themselves
thereafter done the wrong. And those who were sent announced what had
happened, and those who heard and saw said that such things had never
happened, from the day the sons of Israel came up out of Egypt. So then every
tribe of Israel was moved, and all, as themselves having suffered, came
together against those who had offended, and at last those who had done the
lawless things were overcome in war and became anathema from all. For 1.4
those who came together did not look to kinship, but they looked to what had
been committed. You know the history, brethren, and the things accurately
signified about it from the Scripture; for I do not wish to work it out at
greater length, writing to those who know, and being urgent to signify to your
piety things now greater than those. For this is why I also recalled this
history, in order that, comparing the things then with the things now
happening, and knowing that these have surpassed that cruelty, you may be more
indignant than those who were against the 1.5 then lawless. For even the
bitter things of the persecutions that have come about against us have
surpassed them, and the disaster of the Levite is small compared with the
things now dared against the Church; and rather these things were never heard
in the inhabited world, nor has anyone had experience of 1.6 such evils. For
then it was one woman who was wronged and one Levite who suffered the
violence; but now the whole Church has been wronged and a priesthood has been
outraged, and what is greater, piety is persecuted by impiety. And then each
tribe, seeing a part of one woman, was struck with amazement; but now you see
members of a whole Church divided, and some sent to you, and some to others,
announcing the outrage and the wrong which they have suffered. 1.7 Be moved
then, I exhort you, not as if we only had been wronged, but also as if you
yourselves had been wronged, and let each help as himself having suffered, in
order that the ecclesiastical canons and the faith of the Church may not in a
short time be corrupted. For both are in danger, if God does not quickly
through you set right the things that have been committed and the Church
obtain vengeance. For 1.8 it is not now that canons and patterns have been
given to the churches, but they were well and firmly handed down from our
fathers. Nor did the faith begin now, but it has passed from the Lord through
the disciples to us. In order then that the things kept in the churches from
ancient times until us may not perish in these present days, and that the
things entrusted to us may not be required of us, be moved, brethren, as being
"stewards of the mysteries of God," and seeing these things being snatched 1.9
by others. The greater part you will hear from the letter-bearers, but I too
have been urgent to signify briefly, in order that you may truly know that
never have such things happened against the Church from the day when "the
Savior, having been taken up, commanded the disciples, saying: Go, make
disciples of all the nations, baptizing them into the name of the Father and
of the Son and of the Holy Spirit." 2.1 The lawless things done against us and
against the Church have happened thus. While we were gathering as usual in
peace, and the peoples were rejoicing at the assemblies and advancing in the
manner of life according to God, and the fellow-ministers throughout Egypt and
the Thebaid and Libya had love and peace toward one another and toward us,
suddenly the prefect of Egypt publicly posted letters having 2.2 the form of
an edict, as if a certain Gregory from Cappadocia were coming in as my
successor from the court. And such a report troubled all; for the thing was
new and now heard for the first time; but the peoples assembled still more
into the churches, because they especially saw that neither they themselves
nor a bishop nor a presbyter nor anyone at all had ever blamed us. But they
saw only Arians with him, and they recognized him as himself an Arian 2.3 sent
to the Arians by those around Eusebius. And you know, brethren, that those
around Eusebius have always been patrons and partners of the impious heresy of
the Ariomaniacs, and have always plotted against us through them, and have
become the cause of our sojourn into the Gauls. For these reasons the peoples
reasonably were indignant and cried out, calling the other judges and the
whole city to witness, that while no blame had come about from the
ecclesiastics, but only the heretical Arians had played these things against
us, such an innovation and lawlessness has come about against the Church. For
even if there had been some blame prevailing against us at all, it ought not
to have been of Arians nor of those who 2.4 think the things of Arius, but
according to the ecclesiastical canons and according to the saying of Paul,
"the peoples having been gathered and the spirit" of those who appoint, "with
the power of our Lord Jesus Christ," all things should have been examined and
done canonically, with the peoples who asked and the clergy being present, and
he ought not from outside, by Arians, as if trafficking in the name of bishop,
to have thrown himself upon those who did not ask and did not wish and did not
even know at all what had happened, with the patronage and violence of the
outside judges. But this both dissolves the ecclesiastical canons, and forces
the Gentiles to blaspheme and to suspect that the appointments come about not
according to a divine ordinance, but from trafficking and patronage. 3.1 So
then the wonderful appointment of Gregory has come about by Arians, and such
was its beginning. But as to what lawless things his entrance into Alexandria
has worked, and of how great evils it has been the cause, it is possible for
you to inquire and learn from the things written and from those who 3.2 are
visiting. For when the peoples were taking it ill, and for this reason had
assembled in the churches at such an innovation, in order that the impiety of
the Arians might not be mixed into the faith of the Church, Philagrius, who
even formerly wronged the Church and her virgins and is now prefect of Egypt,
having become an apostate, and being a fellow-countryman of Gregory, and not
having a respectable manner, but also having those around Eusebius as patrons,
and for this reason being eager against the Church, persuades the Gentile
crowds, both the Jews and the disorderly, with promises which he also later
delivered, and he stirs them up and suddenly sets them upon the peoples into
the churches with swords and clubs 3.3. And what has happened from these
things it is no longer possible simply to say; for neither is it possible to
signify it worthily, nor would anyone mention even the few things without
tears and lamentations. For what such things have been made tragedy among the
ancients? Or what such thing has ever happened in persecution and war? The
church and the holy baptistery are set on fire, and immediately there were
wailings and howlings and lamentation throughout the city, the citizens also
being indignant at the things happening and crying out against the governor
and witnessing to the 3.4 violence. For holy and undefiled virgins were
stripped and suffered things not lawful, and not enduring they were in danger;
monks were trampled and died, and 3.5 some were stoned, others were slain with
swords and clubs, and others were wounded. And upon the holy table, how great
an impiety and lawlessness has come about. They sacrificed birds and
pine-cones, praising their own idols, and blaspheming in the churches
themselves our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, the Son of the living God. The
divine books of the Scriptures, which they found in the church, they burned.
And into the holy baptistery, alas for the daring, the lord-killing Jews and
the godless Gentiles, entering without guard, practiced and spoke such
shameful things, stripping themselves, that one is ashamed even to speak 3.6
them. And certain impious men, imitating the bitter things of the
persecutions, seizing virgins and continent persons, dragged them and, pulling
them about, forced them to blaspheme and to deny the Lord, and those who did
not deny they cut down and trampled. 4.1 And besides these things the
wonderful and splendid entrance of Gregory the Arian, taking pleasure in such
evils, as if furnishing a prize and a wage of such a lawless victory to the
Gentiles and the Jews and those who worked such things against us, has given
the church for plunder. And when such lawlessness and disorder had been
allowed, 4.2 things worse than wars and more savage than robbers thereafter
came about. For some plundered what they met, others divided the deposits laid
up by certain persons; the wine, which was much, they either drank or poured
out or carried off; they snatched the oil that was stored; each carried the
doors and the lattices as refuse; they set the lamps down at once in the
Tychaeum; they kindled the church's candlesticks for the idols; and altogether
there was plunder and death 4.3 in the church. And the impious Arians were not
ashamed while these things were happening, but they still added worse and more
savage things; for presbyters and laymen were driven out, virgins, having
their veils stripped, were led away to the tribunal of the governor and were
thrown into prison, others had their goods confiscated, were scourged; the
breads of the ministers and of the virgins were forbidden. And these things
were happening in the holy Forty Days themselves about the Pascha, when the
brethren were fasting, but the wonderful Gregory, taking on the manner of
Caiaphas, with Pilate 4.4 the governor, rioted against those who were pious
toward Christ. So then, entering on the Preparation into one of the churches
with the governor and the Gentile crowds, when he saw the peoples turning away
from his violent entrance, he made the most savage governor in one hour
scourge thirty-four virgins and married women and well-born men 4.5 publicly
and throw them into the prison, among whom he caused one virgin, who was
studying and still holding the Psalter in her hands, to be scourged publicly;
and the book 5.1 was snatched by the public executioners, but the virgin
herself was shut up in the prison. When these things had happened they were
not quiet even for what followed, but they were planning also in the other
church, where especially I was dwelling in those days, to do the same things
as the former. And they were eager to extend their madness even into that
church itself, 5.2 in order that, having hunted me, they might destroy me.
Which I would also have suffered, if the grace of God had not helped, so that
I barely escaped and was able to dictate even the few things. For I, seeing
them raging to the last, and having reckoned, in order that neither the church
might be harmed nor the virgins in it suffer and deaths come about again and
the peoples be outraged a second time, stole myself away from the peoples,
having remembered the Savior saying: "If they persecute you in this city, flee
into 5.3 the other." For I knew them, from the evils which they did to the
other church, as not declining any of the terrible things against this one
either; since they did not even reverence the Lord's day of the holy feast
itself, but even on it they shut the people of the church into prison; when
the Lord set all free from the bonds of death, but Gregory and those with him,
as if fighting against the Savior and taking courage from the patronage of the
governor, have made the day of freedom a mourning for the servants of Christ.
5.4 For the Hellenes were rejoicing at the day that used to put them to shame,
but Gregory, perhaps fulfilling commands of those around Eusebius, was then
forcing the Christians to mourn by the maltreatment of the bonds. So then
through this violence the governor snatched the churches and, having snatched
them, delivered them to Gregory and the Ariomaniacs. And those who have been
proclaimed out from among us because of the impiety boast in the snatching of
the churches, but the people of God and the clergy of the catholic Church are
forced either to communicate with the impiety of the heretical Arians or 5.5
not to enter into the churches. For even upon the shipmasters and the others
who sail the sea Gregory laid no small violence and compulsion through the
governor, torturing and scourging, and binding others and throwing them into
the prison, in order that they might not even contradict his lawlessness, but
might even receive letters from him. 5.6 And in order that he might not be
content even as far as these things, but might also be filled with our blood,
he has made his most savage governor and fellow-worker write a decree as from
the person of the people against me to the most pious emperor Constantius,
containing invidious words, from which it is possible not only to flee, but
also to expect ten thousand deaths. And the one who wrote the decree is an
apostate from Christians and shamelessly worships idols, and those who
subscribed are 5.7 Gentiles and temple-keepers of the idols and Arians with
them. And in order that I may not become lengthy in writing, there is a
persecution here, and a persecution such as has never happened against the
Church. For altogether in the persecution before this, even if someone was
fleeing he prayed, and being hidden he was baptized; but now the great
savagery has even surpassed the 5.8 godlessness of Babylon. For as those then
against Daniel, so also the wonderful Gregory now slanders to the governor
those who pray in the houses, and he watches the ministers with every outrage,
so that from such violence many are in danger unbaptized, and many are sick
without those who visit them and lament, considering such a disaster more
bitter than the disease. For with the ministers of the Church being
persecuted, the peoples, condemning the impiety of the heretical Arians,
choose rather thus to be sick and to be in danger than that a hand of the
Arians should come upon their head. 6.1 Gregory then is an Arian and has been
sent to the Arians; for no one has asked for him except them only; and for
this reason, as a hireling and a stranger, he works terrible and bitter things
through the governor against the peoples of the catholic Church as not being
his own. 6.2 For since Pistus, whom those around Eusebius formerly appointed
for the Arians, after we wrote about him, all you bishops of the catholic
Church reasonably anathematized and proclaimed him out because of the impiety,
for this reason they have now sent Gregory, <those around> the same man, to
the Arians. Then, in order that they might not a second time incur shame when
we write against them again, for this reason they used the outside violence
against us, in order that, having got hold of the churches, they might seem to
escape the suspicion of the Arians. 6.3 But they have failed in this also; for
none of the people of the Church is with them, except only the heretics and
those cast out for causes and as many as play the hypocrite because of the
compulsion of the governor. And this is the stage-piece of those around
Eusebius; these things those around Eusebius were studying and composing from
of old, but now they have also been able to do them through the slanders with
which they slandered us to the emperor. Indeed they are not even thus quiet,
but even thus they seek to kill us and show themselves fearful to our
acquaintances, so that all are 6.4 being put to flight and expect death from
them. But you must not on account of these things also be struck down by their
lawlessness, but rather both avenge and be indignant against the things thus
innovated against us. For if when one member suffers "all the members suffer
with it," and according to the blessed apostle one must "weep with those who
weep," when so great a church is suffering, let each avenge the matter as
himself suffering. For the Savior who is blasphemed by them is common, and the
canons which are 6.5 being dissolved by them belong to all. For if, while you
were sitting in the church and the peoples were assembling without any blame,
if someone had suddenly come as a successor against one of you by an edict,
and such things had happened against you, would you not have been indignant?
Would you not have thought it right to be avenged? For this reason it is just
that you be indignant, in order that, these things being kept silent, such an
evil may not little by little pass also into each church, and our school
thereafter become a marketplace and a thing of the agora. 7.1 So then you know
the things concerning the Ariomaniacs, beloved; for often you have all
proclaimed their impiety out, privately and in common; and you know also those
around Eusebius, as I said before, as being of the same heresy as those, on
account of which they also plotted against us of old. But the things now come
about through them and by them, more bitter than wars, I have made clear to
you, in order that, according to the history from the beginning, taking on a
zeal that hates evil, you may take away those who have 7.2 worked such evil
things against the Church. For if even last year the brethren at Rome, these
things not yet having happened, but for the sake of the first things, wrote
that a synod come about, in order that those also might be avenged, which
those around Eusebius, being wary of it, anticipated by throwing the Church
into confusion, and they wished to destroy us, in order that thereafter they
might do with license what they wish and might not have one who refutes: how
much more at such great lawless deeds ought you all to be indignant and 7.3 to
condemn them, inasmuch as they have also added these things to the former? I
exhort you, do not overlook such great things, nor permit the renowned Church
of the Alexandrians to be trampled by heretics. For these reasons reasonably
both the peoples and the ministers are by themselves, being silent because of
the violences of the prefect, but turning away from the 7.4 impiety of the
Ariomaniacs. If then Gregory writes to you, or anyone else about him, do not
receive his letters, brethren, but tear them and put to shame those who bring
them as being impious and deacons of wickedness. And if he should even dare to
write a peaceful form, do not receive these either; for those who receive them
bring them through fear of the governor because of his many 7.5 violences. And
since it is likely that those around Eusebius also write to you about him, for
this reason I have been eager to remind you beforehand, in order that, in this
also imitating the impartiality of God, you may drive out those from them,
because in such a time they have arranged that persecutions and corruptions of
virgins and deaths and snatchings of the things in the churches, and burning
and blasphemies in the churches, come about by Gentiles and Jews for the sake
of the Ariomaniacs. 7.6 But the impious and maddened Gregory would not deny
that he himself is an Arian, having as proof the one who subscribes his
letters. For this Ammon who subscribes was of old cast out of the Church by
the blessed Alexander, the bishop before me, for many evils and for 7.7
impiety. For all these reasons then be willing also to write back to us and to
condemn the impious, in order that now also the ministers here and the
peoples, seeing your orthodoxy and hatred of evil, may rejoice at your
like-minded faith toward God, and those who have done such lawless things
against the Church, being re-educated from your letters, 7.8 may be able, even
if late, to change their mind. Greet the brotherhood with you. All the
brethren with me salute you. May the Lord keep you well and remembering us,
lords truly most longed-for.
