THAT ONE MUST FLEE A COUNTERFEIT AND UNTRUE APPEARANCE, A HOMILY.
=48.1073=
Again we must take up the evangelical trumpet, and set forth to you the Lord's
exhortation. For take heed, he says, of false prophets, who come to you in
sheep's clothing, but inwardly they are ravening wolves; by their fruits you
shall know them. For a counterfeit appearance, he says, is everywhere
convicted by the things done each day; for deeds come from the purpose, and
the solemn appearance does not change the purpose. Therefore among the wise
the crafty man toils in vain, among those who can hunt him down by the fruits
and learn who he is, of what sort he appears. Why then do you hide yourself in
a borrowed appearance? Why do you hide the wolf under the sheep's skin? For a
little later the truth will bring you into the open, and fleeing the truth you
will be caught by the truth.
Why do you put forward words of kindness, covering a bitter character? You
will not escape notice as bitter, even if you pretend to be very sweet. Why do
you put on the appearance of friendship while being an enemy? We do not live
in darkness; hidden things come into the open, and the hatred you were hiding
is exposed. Why do you pretend friendship? Why do you flatter without loving?
Even your flattery will become hateful, bringing harm as from an enemy, not
benefit as from a friend. For you praise even the base things for the sake of
flattery; but the one flattered is not so without sense; for he ought to hate
the one who praises him in his sins. Why do you put on the stamp of a just and
temperate man while remaining in injustices and indecencies? Why do you cover
pride with an appearance, and by a word of humility and piety play-act envy?
There is nothing covered that shall not be revealed, and hidden that shall not
be known. A wicked blemish of a man is a lie; let us flee the slander that
comes upon the lie. Do not make yourself untrustworthy to your friend, lest
even when you speak the truth he no longer believe; for he who has been found
a liar in one thing is no longer worthy to be believed, even if he should ever
speak the truth. For to speak just things, he says, and to tell the truth, is
more pleasing to God than the blood of sacrifices. You will offer to God an
acceptable gift, faith and truth; and it is easy for you also to speak the
things that are, and not to contrive the things that are not. For you will not
grow weary forcing the lie into truth, nor will you be exhausted at all by
speaking things contrary to yourself, because the false word is not in
agreement with itself. You will not be put to shame as one who lied to others,
you who keep the truth. Friends will hold your word more trustworthy than an
oath, and stronger than every sworn testimony your word will be received as a
just and undisputed witness. Whenever you come forward and bear witness, you
procure for yourself the fairest glory for the truth, and salvation for those
in danger, and benefit for the wronged in the court. True lips, he says,
establish a true testimony; but the witness of unjust things is deceitful. The
judge will look on you gladly, releasing you from the inquiry concerning
=48.1074= the case. But indeed the achievement of humility is praiseworthy
before the Lord, if we are able to achieve it. Many then, having accomplished
some good, wish to make themselves small contrary to the truth, preferring
rather to lie against themselves. But I say to you, be humble according to the
truth, O man; speak humble things about yourself into the ears of God, as
Abraham said: I am earth and ashes; as Paul said: I am the least of the
apostles, who am not sufficient to be called an apostle, because I persecuted
the Church of God.
And again, speaking truly, he added: But by the grace of God I am what I am.
These things being thus set forth from every side for our teaching, let us
flee the counterfeit appearance. Why do we put masks on ourselves, not being
such as we show ourselves to be, dragging our tunics down to the ground in the
appearance of saints, but savage in our manner, holy in word and dress, but
inhuman in mind and works? Adornment does not bring one into the kingdom of
heaven, but works of righteousness. Man will not see as God will see; man will
look at the face, but God at the heart. For he who searches hearts and reins,
he says, is God; and because of him who is coming to gather the thoughts of
men. For all things are naked and laid open to his eyes. There is no hiding
from his heat. A time will come when, having thrown off this mask, you will be
found naked, and then you will be ashamed to be seen by the face of God, as
Adam was when he fell. For there appearance is no longer sought, but works.
For here we often deceive, and escape exposure, and through the solemn
appearance we seem as righteous, and we are often counted blessed; but there
it is no longer possible to lie; there works are sought, no longer the solemn
appearance; rather we shall have the greater punishment and condemnation,
having worn the solemn appearance, but not having done the works of the
appearance. Hear the Savior speaking about the Jews, but holding out teaching
toward us: For they make broad their fringes, he says, and they pray in the
streets, that they may appear righteous to men; and these shall receive the
greater judgment. You desire the solemn appearance, and you have longed for
the name of Christianity; do not remain at this only, but as you have
confessed to God, pay the promise by the showing of works. Why are you rash
toward the promise, not confirming it by works? Therefore one must both pray
with security, and pay the debt of the promise with exactness. Vow and pay to
the Lord your God. Appear to him in a pure way of life; that is, in body and
spirit and soul. Do not subtract anything from the promise, lest you suffer
the things of Ananias and Sapphira. For they, having kept back from the price
of the promised property, =48.1075= were condemned to death; and we must fear
these things. For we must know, and not be ignorant, that what we promise to
God is no longer ours thereafter, but belongs to God. Our promise is a
treasure of God; and if we drag anything from these things, we are no longer
taking our own, but as those who plunder the treasures of God, and we shall
truly be judged as temple-robbers. And the promise is not only of money, but
even as far as word and choice. For every idle word, he says, you will give an
account in the day of judgment. For the divine and great mystery does not have
its substance in money, but in a perfect purpose and in the faith according to
God. For we have all made a promise, and we are all debtors of a promise. So
it is necessary that the virgin pay the virginity such as she also promised;
the continent man the continence; the one in marriage the temperance, and the
love toward one another. For she who has promised to remain a virgin or to be
continent through widowhood, but has lied about the promise, and has been
transferred into marriage, has a judgment, because she has set aside the first
faith.
And reasonably the presbyter also ought to pay back the priesthood
well-pleasing, and the deacon the ministry without blame, not stealing the
things of the ministry, lest he be condemned as Judas selling. Therefore much
exactness is needed here, lest anyone lie to God, not paying back the promise
whole, or rather the deposit, which each of us also promised. It is no longer
ours; but we ought to reckon thus, that we received a deposit from God, and
that having kept the promise with prayer, without keeping anything back, we
may present it at the fearful judgment seat of Christ, lest the sentence
overtake us as ungrateful, after the example of Ananias.
Therefore here much security is needed, not to stumble in anything, nor to
subtract from the promise, but let us be eager to pay the promise with
exactness. Let us not think that Christianity is as far as the appearance, nor
remaining in this, taking pleasure in it, but remaining belonging to the
confirmation of truth and righteousness through works, may we obtain from
Christ the honors that follow righteousness, to whom be the glory unto the
ages. Amen.
