286 "As I see, the things I hasten to build toward the salvation of your
God-beloved soul, you do not hesitate rather to throw down. For I pray not
only that you enjoy this temporary kingdom in a rich old age, but also that
you be shown forth as heir of the heavenly and endless kingdom of the heavens
through your good works. For what boast higher than this do we have, that
your noble excellence become known to all, living in all piety and
prosperity? I was truly astonished and beside myself when I heard what was
said by you today to Theodoric the patrician, a man living according to God:
that God has established you as lords of all earth and sea, and the empire
has no need of his possessions, and especially since he has in no way
offended against it. I therefore entreat your God-belovedness to seek from
him nothing of what you said to him, but to grant him all things, and to
command that he also live his civic life without distortion; and may our
Master and God Jesus Christ fulfill all your petitions for good according to
his own will, Christ-loving one. Farewell, remaining many years in the
polity of the Romans." =287.10= "As I think, your God-belovedness is not
unaware of the nobility and wealth of my parents and the distinction of their
rank; and again, that I never loved wealth, nor was I enamored of money or
human glory, but I often advised even them to put off such vain care and to
attend to their own salvation through giving to the poor, that they might
acquire the heavenly kingdom. And how shall I desire money, always
admonishing and rebuking in my expositions those who possess these things
and do not sprinkle them into the hands of the poor? For a little bread with
salt and poor vegetables is enough for my nourishment, because of the
weakness of the body. But concerning Theodoric you indicated that I
appropriated his property for my own gain. Let this then be known to you:
that he gave me nothing at all. And even if he had given, I would not have
taken anything whatsoever from him. But he lent those things to Christ,
depositing them here into the hands of the poor and the needy, that he might
receive them back from him in the age to come many times over. Would that you
yourself had emulated him, emptying your money into the eternal treasures,
where neither moth nor corrosion destroys, nor do thieves and plotters dare
to dig through. For he gave them to the guest-house of the church, that
through them the care of the needy might be done. If then you wish to take
these things and grieve Christ, you will see. For believe me, if you do
this, he is afflicted against you." 289 "The Christ-loving faith and manner
of life of your parents is evident to all. Therefore God, prevailed upon by
their prayers and noble deeds, assigned to you the scepters of this earthly
kingdom, waiting through your works also to show you forth as heir of the
heavenly kingdom; for wealth and glory and worldly distinction will not help
us on the fearful day of judgment, but only the conduct through our works.
Do not then become forgetful of this, nor of the fear of God, knowing
clearly that the breath of us all is in his hands, and to one he adds, and
from whom he wills he also takes away. Being dust and ashes, after a little
we are carried down to earth in dark and lightless places, just as also all
the kings and rulers and ruled before us. Look toward the kings before you.
Understand what they were then, and what they have now become: how on each
occasion some are remembered in good things, while those who passed through
the base things are remembered by all most often always for evils and for no
ordinary curses; nor are even their names spoken by the many. Let then also
your Christ-loving piety imitate those who kept the faith unharmed and who
have run the 290 good course in this life through good deeds, and let it
command that their vineyard be restored to the humble wife of Theognostos
and to her children, which they had for consolation. For the affliction and
calamity that has overtaken them is enough for them, he having died in
exile, and all their substance having been taken away. Yes, I entreat you to
loose your resentment toward her, remembering the Savior in the Gospels
commanding us not to let the sun set upon our anger. And again when he
speaks plainly to that rich man and says: Fool, this night they require your
soul from you; and the things you have prepared, whose will they be? Will
the sweetness of the fruit of the vine, or the sweetness of figs, or the
richness of oil, or abundance of money and possessions and the glory of
office, go down with us into Hades? Do we not leave all these things behind
and go away from the things here naked and unprotected? Remembering all
these things, then, allow the vineyard to be restored, that it may be well
with you on the day of judgment." 295 "I do not think, spiritual child, that
you are unaware that the authorities appointed by God according to the
apostle were appointed not so as to be permitted to do injustice, but so as
to correct those who do injustice and always to lead the subject toward the
lawful and just life and to order it; which is accustomed to succeed
especially whenever those entrusted with the offices themselves, first of
all others, appear claiming justice and the rest of virtue; for for the most
part the ruled also tends somehow to be disposed along with the ruler.
Since therefore Theodoric the patrician also, having never in any way
offended against your power after the manner of those who have collided with
it, has himself now fled for refuge to the church of God, asking to suffer
nothing unjustly, I ask that this petition be fulfilled for him by you, and
not 296 to wish to bring the empire's freedom from want down into the want
of one of those under your hand, while it holds all earth and sea. For thus,
appearing as practical teachers of virtue through the good conduct here
concerning the imperial office, you will also be deemed worthy of the
kingdom from there. Farewell, I pray, most glorious empress, honoring the
purple with a royal mind."
"I think you are not unaware, O child," he has written, "that I was born the
son of distinguished and wealthy people, and having given all things into
the hands of the poor I pray to live for Christ alone and to be rich; which
I also unceasingly entreat you and all the pious to do. This Theodoric also
accomplished, having given me nothing (for neither, even if he were giving,
would I have taken 297), but having furnished it through the poor to Christ
the King of kings. Not only then what we have done and advised, but having
accomplished something more with God, we ourselves taught that which holds a
much inferior and last rank of virtue. For we, toward abstaining from
others' goods, even gave our own to the needy; but you, while holding to
your own, we have admonished as good counselors to stand away from others'
goods. As for the claim that we are all wise at admonishing, I at least
would say the opposite, that no one is properly wise except only the one who
has received the grace of wisdom from God; and how do we not know even when
we sin, since the tribunal of conscience is for all not to be cheated?
Nevertheless, 'Blessed,' according to the divine apostle, 'is he who does
not judge himself in what he approves' ..." 503 Of Chrysostom among the
saints, to the empress Eudoxia, concerning the vineyard of the widow. God,
being creator of all nature, in essence stands above all authority and
lordship; but all human beings are equal, even if one seems to excel
another, and another yet another. And so he assigned the scepters of the
kingdom to you, not that you should think yourself superior to the others,
but that you should award equality of law and justice to the common body;
for glory and wealth and worldly distinction will not help us at all on the
fearful day of judgment, but only the keeping of the commandments, which
brings with it the preservation of the right doctrines. Of which do not
yourself appear forgetful; do not thrust the fear of God from your soul,
knowing clearly that the breath of us all is in his hands, and to one he
adds years of life, and from whom he wills he takes them away. For I, he
says, will kill and make to live, and who is he that delivers from my hands?
And it is a fearful thing to fall into the hands of the living God. We are
all human beings dust and ashes, a flower of grass and dust, a shadow, smoke
and a dream, and we live not living; for from the time that the word "you
are earth and to earth you shall go" was uttered against all our nature. We
have all put on mortality and corruption utterly, and in common we have all
already died, and one dies today, another tomorrow. For having come forth
from earth, after a little we are carried down to earth again, both kings
and private persons, rulers and ruled. Look therefore toward those who
reigned before you and reckon what they were then, and what they have now
become 504. Understand how those who did the good things are blessed both
living and after death and on the day of recompense will go forth to a
resurrection of life, while those who did the base things are even here
always struck by all with curses and slanders and rise no longer to a
resurrection of life but of condemnation. Let then also your
God-belovedness imitate those who kept the faith unharmed and who completed
the good course of the commandments. And restore their vineyard to the
humble wife of Theognostos and to her children. The griefs that have already
come are enough for them; let the terrible things not be stretched further,
nor let the painful things advance still more. Yes, I entreat you, quickly
loose from them the pain and calamity that lies upon them, remembering the
Savior in the gospels commanding us not to let the sun set upon our anger.
And again when he speaks to that rich man and says: Fool, this night they
require your soul from you; and the things you have prepared, whose will
they be? What does a man profit if he gain the whole world and lose his own
soul? Or what shall a man give in exchange for his own soul? Will the
sweetness of the fruit of the vine, or the sweetness of figs, or the
richness of oil, or abundance of money and the glory of office, go down with
us into Hades? Does not all human striving end in corruption? Do we not
leave all things behind and go away naked from the things here and
unprotected? Remembering all these things, then, allow the vineyard to be
restored, that it may be well with you on the day of judgment. 507 Of
Chrysostom to Eudoxia. I think, pious child, that it is not unknown to you
that as for money, if indeed I myself also wished to have it, I was in no
way hindered by my fathers. And I had dealings with men both greatly
powerful and possessing much. But of my own will I stood aside from wealth
for the needy, reckoning the matter least worthy of care for myself. How
then (fol. 154) would I not now have been ashamed to appear as a desirer of
those things which I myself with much zeal laid aside, and which I advise
all to despise as common?
