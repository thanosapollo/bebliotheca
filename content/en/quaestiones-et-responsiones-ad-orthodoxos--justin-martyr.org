OF SAINT JUSTIN THE PHILOSOPHER AND MARTYR ANSWERS TO THE ORTHODOX CONCERNING
CERTAIN NECESSARY QUESTIONS.
Question 1.
If God abolished the ancient worship as not being pleased with it, and
introduced instead that of the Christians as pleasing to himself, and the
orthodox, who alone please God, are not equal in number to the Greeks and Jews
and all the heretics but fewer, what is the proof that it is not according to
poverty of power of him who chose this worship instead of that one that the
error remains uneradicated? And how is the dissolution of that worship not
useless, while much error still holds the world?
Answer, or solution of the question.
God having dissolved the ancient worship of God, and having purposed to
introduce the new through the old covenant, if he did introduce this instead
of that, first, being proclaimed by words witnessed by divine powers, he
abolished both Judaism and Hellenism; and later by works likewise coming about
by divine powers he makes the uprooting of all the error in men and in demons,
and with this he also abolished the worship and wickedness of faith and of
doing of all the evil heresies in the world. If not all who received the
worship keep and have the same opinion concerning the same matters, but some
rightly, others not rightly, this is not an accusation of God for poverty of
power, that those from their own negligence or other weakness do not
understand rightly the things believed by them, but of their negligence. And
it has been said in the worship being introduced concerning the fewness of the
orthodox, at one time Many are called, but few chosen, and at another time
That narrow and afflicted is the way that leads to life, and few are those who
find it. Likewise also concerning the heresies, which the Lord in his own
parables calls tares and rotten fishes. If then nothing is false in the
worship of the Christians, as the truth of the facts bears witness, making the
things to come believed from the things that have gone before, there is
therefore in no way left a pretext for slandering God for poverty of power or
for saying that the worship introduced in place of worship is useless, which
now is dissolutive of Judaism and Hellenism, and later also of the heresies.
Question 2.
If the things that are going to happen, such as trophies of wars, inroad of
enemies and destructions of cities through those who attack them, prophets and
apostles have foretold, and among the Greeks also the words of oracles have
foretold, from where are those of these known as better than those outside,
from either part of the things that will be

prediction having come about?
Answer.
All these things are of the same God, both the prediction of the words and the
outcome of the works, of him who also through the prophets and apostles
foretold what he was going to do; and likewise also through those outside
piety he himself foretold what he was going to do. For just as through the
seer Balaam he blessed Israel and cursed his enemies, having done each through
the foretelling of the things that would be, and likewise also through
divination he foretold to Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon the capture of
Jerusalem, as the prophet Ezekiel says: And the king of Babylon shall stand
upon the ancient way, at the beginning of the two, to divine a divination and
to shake a rod and to inquire in the carved things and to look from his right;
and it came upon Jerusalem, and the rest. Thus also through the seers among
the Greeks he himself foretold as many things as received the outcome through
the works. And the difference of the prophets and of the seers is great:
first, that God, in whose name the prophets and the apostles prophesied, of
this one they have had the knowledge and the faith and the worship, and all
things that the prophets foretold concerning both the destruction of the Greek
gods and oracles and the establishment of the Christian things received the
outcome; and then none of the things which the seers foretold, either against
the God of truth and those who worship him or on behalf of the establishment
of the Greek things, received the outcome. And a witness of these things is
the destruction of the Assyrians, who starting from their own divination said
that they would not go up upon this land to make it desolate without the Lord:
The Lord said to me Go up and make it desolate; and having gone up contrary to
the divination he was made desolate.
Question 3.
If all who have sinned are punished in the resurrection, but more those who
have known the will of God and have not done it, what is the gain of the
Christian and of the Greek, of the baptized and of the unbaptized, of the
orthodox and of the not orthodox?
Answer.
If according to the apostle Paul the believer who does not provide for his own
is a denier of God and worse than the unbelievers, he who does not bring along
with the knowledge and faith and the baptism also the manner characteristic of
the discipleship of Christ, but having a form of piety, and having denied its
power, is worse than the unbelievers. Thus so much worse is it to sin with
knowledge than without knowledge, as the inexcusable sin is than the sin that
has a defense.
Question 4.
If the heretics with all power hastened to know and keep the truth of the
dogmas and were not able, how is it not

unjust to subject these as having fallen away from the truth to punishments?
Answer.
So far have the heretics not sought the truth with all power, that even when
it was being demonstrated to them by those who had found it they neither heard
nor endured to receive it. And if this defense were true, they ought to
forgive one another when they err, as having established the heresies that
fight both themselves and the truth by weakness of power and not by wickedness
of mind. But now, according as they condemn one another for what is alien to
the thinking, they are clear that from love of glory or from the antipathy of
the heresiarchs all the heresies have had the occasions of their
establishment; for which reason the leaders of the heresies were named by the
apostle grievous wolves. And that it is impossible for the one who seeks the
truth with whole heart and power not to attain the finding, the Lord bears
witness saying: Everyone who asks receives, and he who seeks will find, and to
him who knocks it will be opened.
Question 5.
If in the churches of the heretics powers are worked, such as healings of
diseases and drivings out of unclean spirits, bearing of fruits of the earth
and gushings of oil, how is it not necessary that from these those men be
confirmed in the error?
Answer.
Just as the sun rising upon evil and good and raining upon just and unjust is
not confirmative of the evil and unjust in the wickedness and injustice but
preparatory toward just punishment, so the working of certain powers among
them is not confirmative of the heretics in the error. For if the working of
powers were a proof and sign of piety, the Lord would not have declared
unapproved and unworthy of kinship toward himself those who have said: Lord,
did we not prophesy in your name and in your name cast out demons and in your
name do many powers? saying to them: I never knew you, depart from me you who
work lawlessness! Not having said, having taught us, to feed and give drink to
the hungry and thirsty, much more does he himself do the like of the things
which he taught us, heaping fire upon the heads of those who have not worthily
known the giver of the faith?
Question .
If in those who suffer pain and those who are pleased the passible is shown,
and of this the sinners and of that the just in the judgment, how is he true
who says that impassibility is brought to men after the resurrection?
Answer.
We say that they will rise impassible, because from the things which we are
according to nature we do not receive change, neither into the opposites
according to being and not being, nor into the contraries according to more
and less;

as for example from joy we are not turned into grief, nor do we rejoice over
the same good at one time more, at another time less, even if over a greater
good we rejoice more, and over a lesser less; but even thus according to the
proportion of the goods the quantity of the joy remains unchanged. And this is
upon those who rise unto life; and upon those who rise unto judgment the same
account, when they are released as from the judgment of death; for of those
who rise some rise unto life, and others unto judgment.
Question 7.
If a mark of change is to take the good and the base into thought, how are the
punished unchanged when they reckon the relief of the just, or again those who
are in relief when they consider the punishment of the sinners? For each gives
in the other a greater sensation, when the juxtaposition of the contraries
comes about.
Answer.
In the age to come the quantity of the sensation of both honor and punishment
lies in the measure, and it is not possible for the sensation to receive
increase or decrease by the juxtaposition of the opposites. For all things
there are unchanged, as according to quality so also according to quantity.
Question 8.
If we have received from God both the knowledge of good and evil and being,
how is he not cause of each who has also implanted in the nature both the
knowledge of both and power toward the fulfillment of those things?
Answer.
Not only of our being and of knowing and doing both the good and the evil has
God given us the power, but he also granted us the self-chosen and established
us lords of choosing according to preference what seems good of the things
known, and he set our being good or evil not in the knowledge of the things
known but in the choice of those who choose. God then is not cause of our
being good or evil, but the choice. For just as he who sees a harlot woman and
knows her to be a harlot is not a fornicator from the knowledge, nor even if
the knowledge should awaken the passion of desire is he even thus a
fornicator, but if the choice consents to the passion then he is a fornicator
either according to the act or according to the disposition, so also of good
and evil men the knowledge is not cause of their being good or evil, but the
choice that has chosen what seems good according to preference.
Question 9.
If God has given the things said before, and crowns and punishes each
according to the act of men, how does he justly do both, when the man has each
from himself?
Answer.

The "we" and the "upon us" differ from one another. We are male and female,
but it is upon us to be temperate or fornicators. We are therefore crowned or
punished because of the upon us; for toward the things to which our choice
leads us, because of these we are either crowned or punished. For God has also
given us powers toward acting, but he subjected these to the authority of the
choice, and God has the judgment toward the choice, as governing of the
choosing powers in us, and not toward the nature. And we received from God the
power of acting and of not acting: to act the just things, and not to act the
unjust things. When therefore we thus act and do not act, we are justly
crowned; but transferring the acting and the not acting into the opposite, we
are justly punished.
Question 10.
If the bodily things said by the poets concerning the gods among the Greeks
and by the prophets concerning God have according to allegory another meaning,
how are both not shown to be myth?
Answer.
The things said allegorically by the prophets concerning God were said by
reference from the things by nature to that which is not by nature, as for
example The Lord smelled a smell of fragrance. Upon men he smelled according
to nature, but upon God by catachresis and not by nature. But the things said
mythically by the poets concerning the gods are not said by reference from the
things by nature to that which is not by nature; for a man does not swallow a
man according to nature, so that we may understand also by reference Cronus
having swallowed Zeus; and the myth concerning Zeus being swallowed has
nothing else besides the falsehood.
Question 11.
If that which contains is greater than that which is contained according to
substance, but less according to worth, as heaven and earth of angels and men,
a house of those who dwell, and a body of a soul, and God contains the all,
how is he not less than the all by the account of unworthiness?
Answer.
This account is not universal; wherefore it was not even asked soundly. For of
those things of which the contrary can be found through the same, these as not
having the necessary do not even have the credible. For there are some
contained things that are less than the containing, both in substance and in
worth, as the pips of the fruits. And further the containing things that have
been said contain the contained things by being; but God alone contains the
all by will. And the other containing things themselves also need that which
contains; but God does not need that which contains him. And for the other
contained things the being and the remaining is not from those that contain
them; but for the all both exist from God. And the containing things that have
been said exist because of the contained things;

but God does not exist because of the all. Wherefore God is greater than the
all, as in substance so also in worth.
Question 12.
*) If that which contains is for the guard and preservation of that which is
contained, how does God containing the all not contain the all for the guard
of the evil and its continuance? For also in the part of the all the evil is
contemplated.
Answer.
The all is a work of God, and God keeping the all keeps his own work; and just
as God did not make anything evil by nature, so neither does he keep anything
evil by nature.
Question 13.
If the resurrection comes about because of the requital of the things lived to
each, how do the infants or even those who have died in the womb not rise
superfluously, neither receiving exchanges of works nor being able to receive
sensation of relief or affliction because of the untimely of the age?
Answer.
To the one who believes it to be true that It is sown in weakness, it is
raised in power, to this one it is possible and fitting also to believe the
resurrection of the infants. For he who provides the incorruption to them is
able also to grant them the sensing power of the goods that belong. Of whom if
resurrection does not come about, God will be found to have formed them in
vain; and if God does nothing in vain, it is necessary then that the infants
also come to being through the resurrection. And how is it not untimely to
condemn Herod toward the vengeance of the destruction of the infants, if the
infants are not those being avenged? And the leapings of gladness of the
baptist John in the belly of the mother and the hymn of the infants and those
who suckle bear witness to these things.
Question 14.
If the baptism given by the heretics happens to be false and vain, why do the
orthodox not baptize the heretic who flees to orthodoxy, but leave him in the
bastard baptism as in a true one? And if he should also happen to have
received ordination from those, they themselves also accept this as firm. How
then do the one received and those who received have the blameless?
Answer.
When the heretic comes over to orthodoxy the error is corrected, of the evil
opinion by the transfer of the thinking, and of the baptism by the anointing
of the holy myron, and of the ordination by the laying on of hands, and
nothing of the former things remains unloosed.
Question 15.
If Job did not sin having cursed the day, it is clear that as a just man he
was heard. And if he was heard, God overturned his own bound, and it is
necessary to seek in the divine scripture the

destruction of that day. And if he was not heard, how must one not say that
being a sinner he was unheard? For God will do the will of those who fear him.
And concerning the sinners it has been said that If you multiply the petition,
I will not listen to you.
Answer.
The aim of Job in the curse set forth was this, as if he said: Would that that
day, in which I was born, were cursed according to this curse! Why? In order
that I, he says, might not have been born. For it is not possible for someone
to be born in such a day thus cursed. This curse then is not thus cursed as
awaiting the outcome; for if the curse had come about thus as awaiting the
outcome, he would have taken back his own voice, when he had been released
from the afflictions and had come to be in the better things. Because of the
excess of his affliction then he uttered the words of the heart; of which from
him, it is clear that he no longer had the same mind concerning the same day.
Question 10 .
If according to our nature, if something is an offspring, this is
consubstantial with the one who generated, how not according to this, if
something has been generated, does it exist after the one who generated? And
if not the second, neither then will the first likewise be understood by us
upon the divine generation.
Answer.
We, as having the existence preceding the generation, generate after the
existence; but God generated at the same time as existing. Wherefore there is
not with God after-existence of an offspring, since neither is there
pre-existence of generation.
Question 17.
If the union of the divine hypostases is undivided, how can three hypostases
and three persons be named and not rather one hypostasis three-named or
three-personed?
Answer.
Of the divine hypostases the one of the substance is undivided, and the three
of them is divided. Wherefore the three hypostases by particular names are
three; and the hypostasis which we call father, we no longer call the same
hypostasis also son but another. For we say that the triad of the names is of
the triad of the hypostases, not of their monad. Wherefore God is one by the
one and undivided of the substance, and the persons are three by the division
of the hypostases.
Question 18.
If God is indicative of the substance, and father of the person, how is saying
son of God the same as saying son of the father?
Answer.
If we say the son disjointedly, at one time son of the father and at another
time son of God, yet disjointedly we never

think him. The son of the father then is the son of the God according to
substance. And saying son of the father is the same as saying son of God,
since, if something is of the person, this is also of the substance of the
person.
Question 19.
If among the things forbidden by the pious is the observation of palpitations,
why do the pious wishing with all power to push this away not prevail, when
this announces either a bright or a gloomy thing? And if this is base, how
does it come about contrary to our purpose? And if good, which I do not think,
for the sake of what has it been forbidden?
Answer.
The foreknowledge of the things to come is of the soul being illumined by the
divine spirit toward the knowledge of the things until then unclear, and not
by the unwilling motion of the body; but the palpitation is a bodily passion
arising from the running-through of the natural spirit in the body of all the
animals. Wherefore the saints judged such a bodily movement to be unworthy to
be a criterion of the things to come. And just as the sneezes of noses and the
sounds of ears come about contrary to our purpose, so also the palpitation.
And further for those who have received the hope of the future condition, and
have one aim to look toward that through all things in a prepared way, it is
superfluous to foreknow here the bright or the gloomy through palpitations.
And if the same palpitation that foretells wealth comes upon the worldly man
and upon the ascetic, how has the foretelling of wealth through palpitation
not been falsified of the choice of the ascetic, which not only does not
choose the additional acquisition of the things not present but not even the
possession of the things present?
Question 20.
If the fishermen take the fishes from the waters toward death and corruption,
how did the master Christ, being about to lead men into eternal life through
the apostles, promise to make them fishers of men?
Answer.
Since it was necessary that the men hunted by the apostles by the net of the
kingdom die from their former life in sins, for this reason the Lord likened
the hunt of men to the hunt of the fishes; and he added the into life, having
added to the thing shown what was lacking of the example.
Question 21.
If the monks held by temperate reasoning have turned away from the marital
pleasures, why in the fantasies according to sleep do they suffer the
unwilling things, and not only toward chance women, but there are times when
they think in the fantasy to have intercourse even with mothers and sisters?
What then must one use toward the deliverance from such a war? And if the one
warred upon must of the mysteries

abstain, and, if it is necessary to approach them, either having bathed or
having used some other such thing, so as not to be purified judaically, teach;
since there is much inquiry about this even among them.
Answer.
Since those dedicated to God have a sleepless adversary, who by all the ways
possible to him contrives to put a blame upon the pious, for this reason they
suffer unwilling things in sleeps. And yet the adversary knowing that in the
things not upon us we are neither praised upon the solemn nor blamed upon the
shameful (for the praises and the blames are of the things done by us with
consent of the reasoning) and that the dreams are of the things not upon us,
yet nevertheless even through the dreams he moves in us the acts of both, both
of the solemn and of the shameful, having these hopes in himself, lest somehow
either by the working of the solemn he should raise us into vain boasting of
things not done by us, or by the working of the shameful he should be able to
find us taking pleasure in a lawless act. But to see such dreams is not upon
us, and to take pleasure in the thus unwilling act or to be grieved, this is
upon us. If then he should find us taking pleasure in the unwilling act, the
adversary boasts as victor, but if grieving, he is made vain in the counsel
being defeated. Sufficient then toward the deliverance from the evil
contrivance is to be grieved at what has happened unwillingly and with tears
to wash away the abominable of the fantasies thus unwillingly arising in us.
And for those who thus suffer the unwilling things to abstain from the
communion of the divine mysteries is not at all just; or else we take away
according to this the difference of the willing and of the unwilling evils.
Question 22.
If the master Christ declared that the tares grow together with the wheat, how
have the tares been multiplied, and the wheat has almost failed? And what is
the proof that the one that has failed is wheat? And how has the prediction of
the co-growth of these not been falsified?
Answer.
Proof that the one that has failed is wheat is this prediction from him
saying: Because of the multiplying of the lawlessness the love of the many
will grow cold. And again: In later times some withdraw from the faith and
give heed to deceiving spirits and teachings of men. And again: They turn away
from the truth, and will be turned aside toward the myths. And again: Grievous
wolves will enter into you, not sparing the flock, to drag away the disciples
after themselves. And that until the consummation of the age the wheat does
not fail, the Lord makes clear saying: And because of the elect, whom the
father chose, those days will be shortened. But if something happened to the
wheat that was not said concerning

it growing and failing, it is clear that the account concerning it has been
falsified; but if from the beginning of the preaching until the consummation
of the age all the things that will happen to it have been given through the
predictions, it is evident that the account concerning it has in no way been
falsified. But if the tares exist, it is necessary that the wheat also exist;
for from the juxtaposition of one another the wheat is known as what it is,
and likewise also the tares; and if the one is not, neither is the other known
as what it is.
Question 23.
If before the passion the Lord prayed that the disciples be sanctified by the
father, and after these things Judas perished, how does the prayer offered on
their behalf appear received? And how is this also shown true, I know that you
always hear me?
Answer.
The destruction of Judas came about from then, from when Christ having taken
the morsel and having dipped it gave it to him; and Judas having taken the
morsel, Satan entered into him, and having gone out he tore himself away from
the remaining disciples. And the prayer on behalf of the disciples came about
after the separation of Judas; and this is shown from the prayer itself, when
the Lord having prayed said: Those whom you have given me I kept, and none of
them perished, except the son of destruction, that the scripture might be
fulfilled. First then came about the destruction of Judas, and later the
prayer on behalf of the disciples, and then the passion of the savior.
Question 24.
If God is craftsman and master of the creation, how do the talismans of
Apollonius have power in the parts of the creation? For they hinder, as we
see, onsets of sea and courses of winds and inroads of mice and of beasts. And
if the wonders done by the Lord are carried in the narrative alone, but those
from that man very many and shown upon the facts themselves, how does he not
deceive those who see? And if this came about according to divine concession,
how did such a concession not become a guide into Hellenism? And if not this,
how were those things not generated by power of the demons? And again if God
as taking pleasure in the thing coming about as good cooperated with that man,
why did such things not come about through prophets or apostles? And if he was
not pleased as with a base thing, for the sake of what did he either not
hinder the base thing at once or not dissolve it after a little, but conceded
that it prevail until an age of the days of the creation?
Answer.
Apollonius, as a man skilled in the natural powers and in the sympathies and
antipathies in them, used to make the talismans according to this skill, not
according to the divine authority; wherefore in all the accomplished things he
needed the taking along of the suitable materials, cooperating with him toward
the fulfillment of the thing being performed. But our savior Christ, doing the
wonders according to his own divine authority, in no

way needed matter, but the facts followed and follow his commands and
predictions. And the talismans that have come about by Apollonius, since they
have been generated according to the skill of the natural powers toward the
bodily working of men, the Lord did not overturn; but the demon himself
established in that man's statue, who in the divinations deceived the men to
worship and honor Apollonius as a god, he silenced, having abolished his
divinations; and with him he also took down the domination of the remaining
demons honored by the Greeks in the name of gods, as the facts are seen. And
having in these things the marks of the divine power of Christ, one must not
say that the wonders of Christ lie in a bare narrative.
Question 25.
If Moses was educated in all wisdom of Egyptians and was powerful in works and
words, as the divine scripture says, how did the same blessed prophet go
through astronomy and geometry and astrology and the things following these?
For the wisdom of the Egyptians then was teaching the teachings of the error.
Why then is he admired by the scripture upon such words or works?
Answer.
The divine scripture admires the prophet upon the power of the words and of
the works, not for the sake of the words and of the works in themselves; for
those were unworthy in themselves toward the encomia of the prophet. But since
through each the prophet was notable to the Egyptians, of the education of the
word and of the worldly splendor of the life, and having honored the piety
toward God of both, for this reason the divine scripture admired him. And
astronomy and astrology and geometry among the Egyptians then were reckoned
vulgar and pedestrian and marketplace lessons. And honorable lessons then
among them were the so-called hieroglyphic, those handed over in the inner
shrines not to chance persons but to the approved; of which the prophet, even
if he had the knowledge, yet no longer also the use, as opposed to the way of
life according to God of the Hebrews, for the sake of which having despised
all the royal power in Egypt he chose to be maltreated together with the
people of God.
Question 20 .
If all the waters have been made blood by Moses, how does the scripture add
And the enchanters of the Egyptians also did likewise? For either it is false
that all the waters became blood by Moses, or again that the enchanters have
done likewise. And concerning the remaining wonders according to them the same
account.
Answer.
All the waters upon the earth having become blood, the Egyptians were
compelled to dig wells round about the river and to draw water and to give
drink from it both to themselves and to the animals

of them and the cattle. From this water drawn from the wells the enchanters
made the blood, and in no way has the account been falsified. And the wonders
that came about by Moses, as having come about according to the divine energy,
have been generated according to a change of nature of the thing set forth
into the nature of the thing being accomplished, but those that came about by
the enchanters came about according to the energy of the demons, who made the
sights of those seeing imagine, to see the non-serpent as a serpent, and the
non-blood as blood, and the non-frogs as frogs.
Question 27.
Did Moses not teach things contrary to himself, bringing along the bones of
Joseph, and abhorring the one who touches a corpse as unclean? And if there
was some reasonable cause with this man, let us now learn this. And for the
sake of what did the scripture not say, for which cause Moses doing those
things taught these?
Answer.
Since Joseph dying made the sons of Israel swear concerning the carrying over
of his own bones, and both things were set before Moses for keeping, not to
transgress the oath and not to touch a corpse, for this reason by the loosing
of the law saying not to touch a corpse he kept the oath untransgressed; for
it was impossible for him to keep both untransgressed, both the law and the
oath. By the greater keeping of the oath then he loosed the lesser keeping of
the law; for everywhere the lesser evil is more choiceworthy than the greater.
And many such things are found in the divine scripture, which God does not
reckon unto sin of those who transgress them because of the necessity that has
happened in them, as the eight-day circumcision and the seven-day circling of
Jericho and the bringing of the sacrifices on sabbath, which contain the
loosing of the sabbath. Further if the prophet ordered those bearing the bones
of Joseph in succession and being unclean for seven days to be purified
through the keeping of this number in the purification according to the law of
Moses, the prophet did not even do anything contrary to the things which he
said. And to seek, why the prophet did not say the cause of these, is like
saying, why were not all the scriptures handed over interpreted by those who
set them forth; since in the narrative of the fact the sense of the fact is

Question 73.

Answer.
The evil is nothing other besides the turning-out of the good; wherefore the

clear that the good does not appear good from the comparison of the evil, but
from its own nature. And the not-being before the world, altogether not being,
was neither good nor evil; wherefore nothing is compared with the thus
not-being. And further the altogether not-being is neither from God nor from
another nor automatic. Before the world then there was nothing other except
God; therefore the comparison of the beings being toward the beings, the world
then is not compared with the not-world, nor is the not-world contained in the
world. For the not-world is nothing, and that which is contained is something.
And when we say that God has made the beings from the not-being, we do not
give a position of being to the not-being, but the complete taking-away of the
not-being.

Question 77.

If by the cooperation of the senses of the body the soul receives the

be sensible, how does the soul not lack sensation having been separated from
the body? And if it lacks sensation, it is clear that it has been deadened;

Answer.
All the created and rational substances have double grasping powers, both

the knowledge of that above them. Not then by cooperation of the body do the
souls receive the sensation of the sensible things, but the soul itself
providing sensation by its own presence makes the animal sensing; and as
toward the animal it is sensing, and as toward itself it is sensing, and the
soul never becomes dead; for deadness exists of the ensouled, and not of the
soul. The ensouled then and the ensoulment need cooperation of the body toward
the grasp of the sensible things, not the soul; for another is the ensouled
and another the soul, and another that according to participation and another
that according to substance.

exist alien to faith, how is it possible that the things mythologized by him
are true? And further if, as Greeks say, the things spun by the Fates it is
impossible to unspin, how are the divinations not useless to those who use
them, the Fates necessarily leading the things spun by them unto the
inescapable end? But if these things have themselves thus, how are both the
Fates and the divinations not false, being taken away by one another? And how
is Menoeceus not having slaughtered himself in vain according to what seemed
good to the divination, the cause of the enemies being destroyed, and the city
being saved, not existing from the demon who oracled through a seer, but from
the concession of the master the true God?
