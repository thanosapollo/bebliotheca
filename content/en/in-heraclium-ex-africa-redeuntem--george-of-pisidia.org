* On Heraclius Returning from Africa
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

To the emperor Heraclius, who has returned from Africa and become
emperor, and against emperor Phocas
and become emperor, and against emperor Phocas}1 Speech has not the
strength to speak of you, since God the Word himself has set you to be
ranked above these flowing words. But I see many hymning you, master,
armed horsemen, <well->aimed beast-slayers, in whom all-wise mind is not
portrayed and a divine character is not made beautiful. But for us it is
fitting to hymn the God-inspired prudence of your soul's movements; which,
having taken as it were from on high as a panoply, you guard your mind with
the God-inspired Scriptures. For hunting them by memory and by speed you
delight, being winged through them toward God. You always pursue all anger as
untamed beasts from an all-day lookout; you wish malice to be slain as a
beast, through which the common race of the sojourn is hunted by the plotting
scandals. By your nature, most mighty one, even the beasts are often taught to
be tamed. And the Medes and barbarians are images of these, who, having often
boiled in a savage manner, were quenched by your gentle word. And a horse is
present to you, running swiftly: your reasoning, moving everywhere, filling
the earth and carrying toward heaven. And you stretch bows from a
philanthropic impulse, always sending forth the honey-dripping words, by which
each one is wounded with pleasure. And you do these things and constrain time,
whenever affairs with so many entanglements flood the earth after the manner
of streams. But there is hope that the cares of the present difficulties will
have ceased everywhere from you. For if the commonwealth often perished,
having fallen by the inattentions of those who ruled, now too the commonwealth
will be saved by God through the pious good deeds of the one who rules. For
even before, seeing us made wretched, when the bitter wounds of the tyrant,
having taken pasture, were fastening upon the members, you had opportunity to
remain without toils, as not having been by nature a cause of the evils. But
nonetheless you did not hold back, master, from those entangled in boundless
disasters, through which each one was savagely bitten by the ruling beast of
affairs. And having run gladly toward so many toils, you despised your own
blood, alone wishing to snatch all from bloods. For you did not tremble at the
long passage of the sea, nor were you then drawn back toward a mother's pity.
But a certain love, kindled toward God, held you so as to quench the tempest
through which the commonwealth was coming to destruction. And you were not
disappointed of so great a hope, but as the faithful Phinehas in the spirit
you had faith as the slayer of the murders. For from the time you freed us
from the tyrannical harm and have us as slaves from God, the terrible pouring
of the bloods has ceased, from which the stream of the evils was born. Having
always the memory of these things, most mighty one, and having cast your hopes
upward toward God, you will soon see the same God again as savior of your hope
the second time. For he himself will open for us the doors toward peace
everywhere by your exertions, showing her as sharing the throne with your
power. For recently, I think, she was modestly hiding, blushing to look upon
the bastard murders. But, O dispenser of the good gifts of grace, of those
that do not bear toward flowing affairs but that lead into remaining
substance, receive the small things and teach greater ones. For he who grants
you breadth of heart, so that you suffice for all and are in no way
straitened, will show how to steer even the present affairs from the storm
toward the calm which you have, so that, being deprived of care henceforth,
imprinting your soul's beauty as in an image in our own hearts, we may depict
an indelible grace, showing what blessed flower was hidden in the
entanglements of the thorns.



Having hymned you with these small words, and having appeared wholly lacking
in the dignity, I am reasonably taught "know thyself," I too along with all,
longing for the fair defeat.
