CANONICAL LETTER OF SAINT GREGORY, BISHOP OF NEOCAESAREA, THE
WONDERWORKER, CONCERNING THOSE WHO, IN THE RAID OF THE BARBARIANS,
ATE FOOD OFFERED TO IDOLS, OR COMMITTED CERTAIN OTHER OFFENCES
Concerning those who ate polluted food in the raid, and concerning
the women corrupted by the barbarians.
It is not the foods that trouble us, holy father, if the captives
ate what their captors set before them; especially since there is
one report from all, that the barbarians who overran our parts have
not sacrificed to idols. And the apostle says: Foods are for the
belly, and the belly for foods; but God will bring to nothing both
this and these. And the Saviour too, who makes all foods clean: It
is not what goes in, he says, that defiles the man, but what comes
out. And as for the captive women being violated, the barbarians
outraging their bodies: if even before this a woman's life was
already under accusation, as one who went after the eyes of those
who play the harlot, according to what is written, it is clear that
the harlot's habit is suspect even in the time of captivity, and one
must not readily admit such women to share in the prayers. But if
one has lived in the height of chastity, and has shown her former
life to be pure and beyond all suspicion, and now has fallen by
force and necessity into outrage, we have the example in
Deuteronomy, that of the young woman whom a man found in the field
and, forcing her, lay with her: You shall do nothing, it says, to
the young woman; there is in the young woman no sin worthy of death,
because as when a man rises up against his neighbour and slays his
soul, so is this matter; the young woman cried out, and there was
none to help her.
2 Against greed. Such then are these things.
But greed is a dreadful thing, and it is not possible in a single
letter to set out the divine Scriptures, in which not robbery alone
is proclaimed as a thing to be fled and as terrible, but greed in
general, and laying hold of what belongs to another for shameful
gain; and every such person is to be proclaimed out of the church of
God. And that in the time of the raid, amid so great a wailing and
so many dirges, some should dare to count the season that brought
ruin upon all as a season of gain for themselves, is the work of
impious men, hated of God, who have no further excess of enormity
left. For this reason it has been judged right to proclaim all such
men out, lest the wrath come upon the whole people, and first upon
those set over them who do not inquire. For I fear, as the Scripture
says, lest the ungodly destroy the righteous with himself. For
fornication, it says, and greed, on account of which the wrath of
God comes upon the sons of disobedience; therefore do not become
partakers with them. For you were once darkness, but now light in
the Lord; walk as children of light; for the fruit of the light is
in all goodness and righteousness and truth, proving what is
well-pleasing to the Lord, and do not share in the unfruitful works
of darkness, but rather even expose them; for the things done by
them in secret it is shameful even to speak of; but all things
exposed are made manifest by the light. Such are the apostle's
words. But if, paying the penalty for their former greed, that which
took place in time of peace, some at the very season of wrath turn
again to greed, making gain from the blood and ruin of men who have
been overthrown, or of captives, or of those who have been murdered,
what else is to be expected than that, as men who contend further in
greed, they heap up wrath both upon themselves and upon the whole
people?
3 [Concerning Achan] Was it not the case that Achan the son of Zerah
committed a trespass in the matter of the devoted thing, and wrath
came upon all the congregation of Israel? And this man sinned
alone; did he die alone in his sin? For us, everything that is not
ours, but another's, at this time ought to be counted as devoted
gain. And that Achan took from the spoil, and they now take from
spoil; and he took the goods of enemies, but they now take the goods
of brethren, having made a deadly gain.
4 Concerning those who in the raid seized the goods of their own
people.
Let no one deceive himself, not even as one who has found; for
neither is it permitted to the finder to make gain. Deuteronomy
says: You shall not see your brother's ox or his sheep going astray
on the road and overlook them; you shall surely turn them back to
your brother. And if your brother is not near you, and you do not
know him, you shall gather them, and they shall be with you until
your brother seeks them, and you shall restore them. And so shall
you do with his donkey, and so shall you do with his garment, and so
shall you do with every loss of your brother, whatever is lost from
him and you find. These things Deuteronomy says. And in Exodus, not
only if one finds what belongs to a brother, but even to an enemy:
You shall surely, it says, turn them back to the house of their
owner. And if in time of peace it is not permitted to make gain from
a brother or an enemy who is careless and living in luxury and
neglecting his own, how much more from one who is in misery and
fleeing from enemies and abandoning his own by necessity?
5 [Concerning those who seize what belongs to others in place of
their own] And others deceive themselves, holding what they found
that belongs to others in place of their own that was lost; so that,
since the Boradi and the Goths have done the works of war upon them,
they themselves may become Boradi and Goths to others. We have
therefore sent to you our brother and fellow-elder Euphrosynus on
account of these things, so that according to the pattern here he
may likewise give judgment, both whose accusations ought to be
admitted and whom it is necessary to exclude from the prayers.
6 Concerning those who by force hold the captives who have escaped
from the barbarians. Something unbelievable has also been reported
to us as having taken place in your country, surely somewhere by
unbelievers and impious men who do not even know the name of the
Lord: that some have gone so far in inhumanity and cruelty as to
hold by force certain of the captives who have escaped. Send some
into the country, lest thunderbolts also fall upon those who do such
things.
7 Concerning those who were enrolled among the barbarians and dared
certain outrages against their own people. Those, then, who were
enrolled among the barbarians and were with them in the captivity,
forgetting that they were Pontics and Christians, and becoming
barbarized so as even to kill their own people by club or by
hanging, and to point out roads or houses to barbarians who did not
know them, must be shut out even from the hearing, until some common
decision is reached concerning them by the saints assembled, and
before them by the Holy Spirit.
8 Concerning those who dared to enter the houses of others in the
incursion of the barbarians. Those who dared to enter the houses of
others, if they are accused and convicted, are not even to be deemed
worthy of the hearing; but if they declare themselves and make
restoration, they are to prostrate themselves in the rank of those
who are returning.
9 Concerning those who in the field or in their own houses found
what was left by the barbarians. Those who found something in the
field or in their own houses, left behind by the barbarians, if they
are accused and convicted, likewise among those who prostrate
themselves; but if they declare themselves and make restoration, let
them also be deemed worthy of the prayer.
10 Concerning not demanding a finder's reward. Those who fulfill the
commandment must fulfill it apart from all shameful gain, demanding
neither informer's fees nor salvage nor a finder's reward, or
whatever name they give these things.
11 Concerning the stations of repentance. The weeping is outside the
gate of the oratory, where the sinner must stand and beg the
faithful who are going in to pray for him. The hearing is inside the
gate in the narthex, where the one who has sinned must stand until
the catechumens and go out from there; for hearing, it says, the
Scriptures and the teaching, let him be cast out and not be deemed
worthy of prayer. The prostration is that, standing inside the gate
of the nave, he go out with the catechumens. The standing-with is
that he stand with the faithful and not go out with the catechumens.
And last is the participation in the holy things.
