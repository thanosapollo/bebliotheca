4. Of the same, that it is a grave thing to despise the loving-kindness of
God.
And it was said when the parable of the ten thousand talents had been read.
And against the Jews, when the earthquake happened.
=64.17=
The munificence of the Master's loving-kindness just now being heard has
worked a great bankruptcy of sins of even ten thousand talents, being given as
a gift to sinners; but many often, leaping against the Master's goodness, and
using badly the gifts from it, construct for themselves the wealth of
loving-kindness as a store of punishment. For God does not, simply scattering
compassions, nor being sick with a prodigality of munificence, display the
wealth of loving-kindness, but by shaming with the abundance of the gifts; and
when he sees the things of loving-kindness being thrown away in vain for him,
he contracts the springs of =64.18= goods, he holds back the rains of
loving-kindness, he hesitates as he grants. With how many rains of goods
flooding the peoples of the Jews at times, when he saw them fruitless toward
fruits of thanksgiving, as if filled with their ingratitude, he cried: You
have become to me a satiety. You have been shown, he says, more contentious
toward me than my loving-kindness toward you; I grew weary granting to you;
You have become to me a satiety. Reasonable is the satiety of long-suffering
upon them; for a way of their correction is not found
