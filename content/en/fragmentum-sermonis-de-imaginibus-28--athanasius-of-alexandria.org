* Fragment of a Discourse on Images 28
:PROPERTIES:

=28.709= Of Saint Athanasius of Alexandria from a section a discourse concerning
the holy images.
Not as gods, they say, do we the faithful worship the images; may it not be! but
only showing the relation and the love. Whence often, the character having been
smoothed, it remains as idle wood. And this also happens upon the type of the
cross. As long as the two woods are yoked together, and it saves the type of the
cross, I worship the cross rightly and exactly; but when they are divided from
one another, and they loose the type by the division, I no longer worship the
woods divided from one another. Thus henceforth I also worship the image because
of Christ, and through the image of Christ I worship Christ himself. For even if
Christ is not present to my lips bodily, yet in heart and in mind I am present
spiritually toward Christ. And that these things are thus is clear from the
example concerning the cross. And after a little: For he who has thus known that
the Son and the Father are one knows that he is in the Father, and the Father in
him. For the Godhead of the Son is of the Father, and this is in the Son. And he
who has grasped this is persuaded that he who has seen the Son has seen the
Father. For in the Son the Godhead of the Father is contemplated. And this also
from the example of the image of the king someone will be able to understand
more closely; for in the image of the king is the form and the shape; and in the
king indeed is the form that is in the image. Unalterable is the likeness in the
image of the king; so that the one looking at the image sees in it the king, and
recognizes that this is the one in the image. And from the likeness not being
changed, to the one wishing after the image to contemplate the king the image
would say: I and the king are one; I then am in him, and he in me; and what you
see in me, this you see in him, and what you have seen in him, this you see in
me. He then who worships the image worships in it the king; for his shape and
form is the image.
