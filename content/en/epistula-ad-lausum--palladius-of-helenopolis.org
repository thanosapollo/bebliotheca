Copy of a letter written to Lausus the praepositus by Palladius the bishop
I bless your choice; for it is worthy to begin the letter from a blessing;
because of all gaping toward vain things and building stones from which they
have not rejoiced, you yourself wish to be taught words of building. For
unteachable 7 is only the God of all, since he is also self-grown and having no
other before him; and all the rest are teachable, since they are made and
created. And the first ranks have as teacher the highest Trinity; and the second
learn from the first; the third from the second; and so in sequence according to
order, even to the last. For those better in judgment and virtue teach those
lesser in knowledge. Those then who think they do not need teachers, or are not
persuaded by those who teach in love, are sick with ignorance the mother of
pride; of whom those who by the same passion fell from the heavenly way of life
lead into destruction, the demons flying in air having run away from the
teachers in the heavens. For neither the words nor the syllables are teaching,
which even those most worthless sometimes have; but the accomplishments of
character, both freedom from grief and fearlessness and un-cowardice and freedom
from anger, and the boldness upon all, which also begets the words as a flame of
fire. For if this were not, the great teacher would not have said to his own
disciples: Learn from me that I am meek and humble in heart; not rhythmizing the
apostles by fair diction, but by the piety of character, grieving no one except
those who hate words and hate teachers. For the soul practicing according to God
must either learn faithfully what it does not know, or teach clearly what it has
known. And if it does not wish either, if it can, it is sick with madness. For a
beginning of apostasy is satiety of teaching and a lack of appetite for the
word, which the soul of the God-lover always hungers. Be strong then and be
healthy and play the man, and God will grant you to pursue the knowledge of
Christ.
