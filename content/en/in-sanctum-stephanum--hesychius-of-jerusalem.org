Of Hesychius, presbyter of Jerusalem, an encomium on Saint Stephen
1 Let all the earth complete the festival for Stephen; for he cultivated all
of it in succession with words, and he filled the inhabited world universally
with sacred laws. He sowed everywhere the doctrines of piety; he granted to
all the churches to glory in the light of his teaching. And just as the sun,
rising from some one corner of the heaven, has obtained to light the whole
visible creation, so Stephen of the graces, having flashed from Jerusalem,
filled whole cities and lands, nations and peoples, tribes and tongues with
unquenchable theology.
2 But we reasonably owe him more festivals, since he is a citizen of the
Cross, a household-member of Bethlehem, an offspring of the Resurrection, a
table-setter of Zion, a herald of the Ascension. Among us he pitched the
courts and the tents; among us he obtained the lot of the ministry and the
portion of the martyrdom. Here he has the altar of the sacrifice and the
platform of the blessing, the field of the teaching, the theater of the
rhetoric. From here he was taken up into the heavens, and from us he departed
to Christ.
3 Hence frequent among us are the theaters of the festivals for him, and often
we crown his bridal chamber, continually leaping we go up the winepress, and
we sing a wedding song, saying a vintage-song: "Lord, as with a shield of good
pleasure you have crowned us." For the zeal of Stephen is a shield for the
faithful, and the tongue a sword for the pious, and the boldness a preparation
of confession, the courage an exhortation of manliness, the rhetoric material
of theology, the teaching a catechesis of the law, knowledge of the prophets,
acquaintance of the gospels, the contemplation of the things according to
heaven, or rather of the things above heaven, an interpretation of mysteries.
4 But who is sufficient to speak the encomia of Stephen in part? For what
shall I call him? A martyr of the light, a deacon of life, a soldier of
Christ, a lampstand of the Word, a pearl of the Spirit, a flower of endurance,
a foundation of philosophy, an example of impassibility, firstfruits of
teachers, a pillar of the church, a pedagogue of grace, a ladder of virtues, a
monument of progress, a forerunner of the apostles, firstborn of the martyrs,
a leader of knowledge?
5 He was a minister of widows, but having ordained himself he becomes of the
churches; assigned to bring in cooked dishes, he brings in instead the
doctrines. He changed the tables into altars; he used the breads from heaven,
not those from earth. He transferred the mixing-bowl of drunkenness into bowls
of grace; instead of wine he mixed the spirit, instead of perceptible water he
set forth the mystical and intelligible.
6 He was great both speaking and acting, teaching and working wonders,
refuting Jews, driving out the demons, healing at once the infirmities of the
body and of the soul. He opened the mouth, and he hunted every hearing; he
moved the tongue, and death fled, the falsehood was bound, love advanced.
Being a lamb, he fought against the wolves on behalf of the shepherd; being a
dove, he subjected the serpents to the eagle. Silent he presented the
character of an angel to those judging. He laughed, seeing the slanderers
circling; and seeing the false witnesses standing by, he said these things to
himself from the harp-song of David: "Their blows have become the arrows of
infants." For however many things the slanderer may shoot, the worker of truth
is not wounded; however many arrows the wicked one sends forth, they are moved
and sent in vain; for he does not sting the upright, he does not strike the
innocent, he does not bite the uncorrupted.
7 And what sort of thing was the form of the slander? "This man does not cease
speaking words against this holy place and the law." "This man." What sort?
"The one working wonders and signs beyond man; the one who has drawn the whole
fountain of wisdom; the one who has emptied into himself the treasuries of
grace; the one doing powers which we are not able to endure; at whose zeal and
at whose envy we have wasted away; whom the deaf hears when called; and the
blind sees toward him, having received eyes from him; and the lame asks for
feet, having borrowed even hands from this one's tongue; on whose account the
little beds of the sick have been emptied, on whose account there is no
difference of those in health and those who are ill, on whose account the
lepers have forgotten the passion, on whose account there is no need of
physicians, on whose account the rubbed drugs are superfluous."
8 "This one does not cease speaking words against this holy place and the
law." What then are these? "For we have heard him saying that this Jesus the
Nazarene will destroy this place, and will change the customs which Moses
delivered to us." Reasonably you are called false witnesses and slanderers,
because you utter the truth as not truth, because you name the prophecies from
him and about him blasphemies of God, and you think to be a setting-aside of
the law the things which he himself threatened would come to pass.
9 For whose voice, whose prophecy, whose preaching is it? Who commanded Isaiah
so to speak, who Micah: "Therefore because of you Zion shall be ploughed as a
field, and Jerusalem shall be as a storehouse of fruits"; "The daughter of
Zion shall be left as a tent in a vineyard and as a storehouse of fruits in a
cucumber-field"? Who also inspired David to say such things: "Remember, Lord,
the sons of Edom the day of Jerusalem, of those saying: Empty, empty, until
the foundation in her"?
10 And what of Moses? Does not he himself also vote with the things said by
Stephen? Did he not write down these curses of yours, which now by the very
experience have come out into work, and provide to all to see the causes from
which they have been brought forth? Let the wounds that have befallen the
people of the Jews be set beside the words of Moses. Seeking them according to
the letter, you will find that nothing anywhere small or great has come out
having lied. "And if after these things you walk sideways"; for they never
acquired a heart straight toward God; but much more in the later times, in
which they were going sideways against the calling of Christ. Wherefore he
also added: "And if you do not wish to hear me" (they did not then obey when
Christ was calling), "I will add to you seven plagues according to your
lawlessnesses." For since they denied the seven gifts of the Spirit, the seven
spirits of wickedness become for them seven plagues, which having entered
dwelt in them; and their last things have become worse than the first.
11 "And I send upon you the beasts of the earth." He means the nations; for
they were formerly beasts because of ignorance, because they worshipped the
divine in the image of beasts and four-footed things and reptiles. Against
whom David speaking in curse said: "May those who make them become like them,
and all who trust in them." These nations God has now sent upon them, doing
the things said next by the lawgiver: "And it shall eat you, and it shall
consume your cattle, and it shall make you few; and your ways shall be
desolate." For each of these things has come to be for them, according to the
perceptible and according to the intelligible, when they were mastered by the
nations.
12 Nor indeed did God stop the threats through Moses to the people of the
ungrateful at this point, but things harsher and greater than those said. For
he says: "You shall take a wife, and another man shall have her. You shall
build a house, and you shall not dwell in it; You shall plant a vineyard, and
you shall not harvest it. Your calf slaughtered before you, and you shall not
eat of it." And having threatened many other things besides these, he did not
hide the cause from which these come, but he brings it on after a multitude of
threats, which happen to be yoked to those just now said to us: "And your life
shall be hanging opposite your eyes; and you shall fear day and night, and you
shall not believe in your life. In the morning you shall say: How might
evening come to be? And in the evening you shall say: How might morning come
to be? and from the fear of your heart, which you shall fear, and from the
visions of your eyes, which you shall see."
13 Hear then the end of these things; hear also the outcome, and do not blame
Stephen when he discourses in agreement with the law and the prophets. "You
shall take a wife": the ancient covenant, "and another man shall have her":
for the people from the nations acquired it. "You shall build a house, and you
shall not dwell in it." The temple, clearly; for you see it fallen. "You shall
plant a vineyard, and you shall not harvest it": for it did not produce a
grape but thorns. Or let us also understand the vine of Christ; for from them
was Christ, the vine of our life; but we harvest his grape, we mix the wine,
we drink the must. "Your calf slaughtered before you, and you shall not eat of
it": for the sacrifice of Christ was theirs, they themselves brought the calf
from heaven onto the altar, but the supper is also ours, the table is ours.
14 And for what reason? Because they fell sick with unbelief, they offered
ignorance instead of knowledge, in the season of theology they slipped into
blasphemy. They entered the bridal chamber and did not receive the bridegroom,
they crowned the wedding bridal-room and denied the marriage; and while the
cross was being accomplished, the elements were shaken, but they themselves
were not changed. For this is what he added next: "And your life shall be
hanging opposite your eyes." What is the life? Christ saying: "I am the way
and the life and the truth." And where does it hang? Upon the wood of the
cross. For the one who hung the earth by a word hangs upon the wood, in which
he is borne while bearing, in which he hangs while hanging, in which he is
lifted up while lifting up; "For when I am lifted up," he says, "I will draw
all to myself." "And you shall fear day and night, and you shall not believe
in your life." Therefore unbelief has become the cause of the fear.
15 "You shall fear day and night": for the same was day and night, because of
"There was darkness over all the earth from the sixth hour." Hence Zechariah
is at a loss what one must rather name it, what it is fitting to call, whether
from the darkness that occurred or from the law of the day the season of the
cross is to be named. Thus then he says: "And it shall be in that day, there
shall not be light; and there shall be cold and frost. It is a great day, it
shall be known to the Lord. Not day nor night; and it shall be toward evening
there shall be light. And living water shall go out in that day from
Jerusalem; half of it into the first sea, and half of it into the last sea."
And these things were coming to pass perceptibly and intelligibly.
16 "It shall be in that day, there shall not be light": for the sun set in the
middle of the day; and darkness and ignorance had been poured out upon the
hearts of the Jews. "There shall be cold and frost." And Peter bears witness,
being warmed at the charcoal-fire because of the peak of the winter season.
But toward this there was cold and frost, since then especially the love of
the Jews toward God had grown cold; for they would not have crucified the
savior and redeemer who had come from him. "It is a great day." For how is it
not great, in which Adam was refashioned, sin was abolished, death was
destroyed, the potter received back the vessel, the shepherd the sheep, the
merchant the pearl? "It shall be known to God." For how shall the one who
knows all things not especially recognize that day, in which he gave over the
only-begotten son to sacrifice? "Not day nor night", since it was a course of
day, not of night. "Toward evening there shall be light": when after the ninth
hour the darkness ceased. Therefore the same became both night then and day.
17 "And living water shall go out from Jerusalem." From where? From the side
of the life; for from where Eve was refashioned, from there reasonably the
church is formed. "Half of it into the first sea." Clearly of baptism. "And
half of it into the last sea." Of repentance, clearly. For the tears of
repentance are a remnant of the gift of baptism, and a clear proof: the one
not baptized repents in vain, if he does not run and be baptized; in vain he
sacrifices the prayer, in vain he asks pardon, in vain he wells forth tears.
For without baptism forgiveness of sins is not accomplished. But the one who
has been baptized, this one possesses repentance as operative; he weeps and is
loosed; he groans and mourns and prays, and immediately he is cleansed.
18 But hear how the remaining things of the proclamation of the law also come
out: "In the morning you shall say: How might evening come to be? And in the
evening you shall say: How might morning come to be?" From where? "From the
fear of your heart, which you shall fear, and from the visions of your eyes,
which you shall see." Since because of the signs and the wonders which were
being done at the time of the cross, the earth being shaken, the sun fleeing,
the heaven being hidden, the rocks being split, the veil being torn, the tombs
being opened, fearing and being troubled, they were reasonably compelled to
say to one another: "How might evening come to be?" in order that we may take
down the life from the cross, hide in earth the one from heaven, wrap in a
tomb the immortal, put the wonder out of the midst, from which such great
terrors come to us.
19 But even having done these things, they gained nothing of the aim; but the
attempt becomes the contrary for them. And for this reason they were saying in
the evening: "How might morning come to be?" Since again fearful are the
wonders accomplished in the night of the resurrection, since they were seeing
the tomb a bridal chamber, the monument sealed and the dead running out with
life, the soldiers as dead, the women bearing myrrh, angels guarding the
linens, another sitting upon the stone a herald of the resurrection, then they
were saying: "How might morning come to be?" in order that we may provide
silver to the soldiers, that we may corrupt Pilate, that we may steal the
truth by deceit, that we may report "that his disciples came by night and
stole him, while we were sleeping".
20 These things the law and Moses through it, and in each of these rather God
testifies against the ungrateful people; whom Stephen the theologian,
refuting, is named a blasphemer by them. And the high priest says: "If these
things are so?" For do you not know how they are? Did you not yourself
construct the deceit? Did you not yourself hire the slanderers? Do not the
witnesses of the deceit work for wages for you? "But there is need," he says,
"of falsehood and hypocrisy; and we need deceit for the snare that has been
set forth. Thus we sold Joseph, we murdered Naboth, we crucified Christ."
21 But Stephen, having found a season of teaching, poured out as many saving
things as there were into the hearings of the people, and he added a
conclusion of the rhetoric, fitting both for himself to speak, as a witness of
truth, and for those to hear, as having set out to fight against God and being
sick with the contradiction of the Spirit: "Hard-hearted and uncircumcised in
the heart and in the ears, you always resist the Holy Spirit; as your fathers,
so also you."
22 And how at the beginning do you say: "Men brothers and fathers, hear me"?
"Those things," he says, "are of the nobility of Abraham, but these of the
wickedness of these men. Those of the good root, these of the wicked branches.
Those show the notable of the flock, these the disorder of the many, the hard,
the unbridled." Toward that prologue Isaiah was urging: "Say, our brothers, to
those who hate us and abominate, that the name of the Lord may be glorified."
But toward this ending they themselves even against judgment compel the word:
"I called brothers, I addressed fathers, lest anyone suppose I accuse as of
enemies, I refute as of adversaries. But I addressed the same as hard-hearted
and uncircumcised, since one must prefer the truth to kinship and spare
neither friends nor brothers nor fathers nor other household-members, when
they transgress the law."
23 The children of the Jews did not endure these words, but they gnashed the
teeth like beasts, hastening to swallow Stephen while he was still speaking.
"They gnashed the teeth." For it had been said concerning them: "Sons of men,
their teeth are weapons and arrows, and their tongue a sharp sword." But
Stephen had the eyes not toward them, but toward God, and leaving the earth he
was hastening into the courts of the heavens, and with a bright voice he was
proclaiming "Behold I see the heavens opened". That is: the gain is common,
the way is ready for all, the gate has been opened for those wishing to enter.
Let the courageous contend intensely; let the genuine hoplite now use the
weapons, now display the greatness of the strength. For the president of the
contest looks on; he stands distributing the crowns and the prizes.
24 "Behold I see the heavens opened." And who the one who opened, he did not
leave the one wishing to know in ignorance. And for this reason he has cried
out: "And the son of man standing at the right of God", meaning the God Word
who became flesh. For from when the son of God has become son of man, from
when the one from the Father himself has stood from a virgin as a boundary to
the earth and the heavens, the middle-wall of the enmity has been taken away,
the fence is out of the way, the things of the citizenship are common to the
angels and to men, if men wish. It is permitted for those being on earth to
campaign in heaven, to be below in the flesh, to be present above in the
spirit, to be with the clay by the clay, but with God by the mind.
25 These things then Stephen was theologizing; but disordered shouts
interrupted the theology in order, and shouts succeeded shouts; and slings of
stones followed upon the doctrines of the sacred laws. Lacking weapons, they
were armed from the earth; swords not being present, they improvised stones
with the right hands, and taking him outside the city they were sacrificing.
For the old Jerusalem was not fitting for the victim; but it was necessary
also that the lamb be slain beside the lamb of the shepherd, that the soldier
receive the pledges of the victory there, where the king planted the trophy
over all.
26 But what provision for the end was he receiving? Prayer and the incense
from this; and stripping off the flesh he was putting on the prayer. And what
was the prayer? "Lord Jesus, receive my spirit", that is: You take what is
yours; take up the deposit which you gave; receive the pearl which you
entrusted; what I have according to your image, this I send to you. "Receive
my spirit": for no longer do the souls of the righteous enter into Hades of
weeping; no longer do the gloomy regions of death receive the spirits of the
living. After you became firstfruits of the living and of the dead at once,
that you might be lord of both dead and living, the lump of all the dead takes
courage, not outside reason. "Receive then my spirit." Do not permit your
sparrow to be ambushed by those who hunt, nor those enemies who meet in gates
to harm my departure from the earth, nor to throw into confusion my ascent
into the heavens.
27 But this prayer on behalf of himself, and again another he was sacrificing
on behalf of the people of the Jews: "Lord, do not stand this sin to them."
And this is: for he did not say: "Do not reckon it to them", but "Do not
stand", perhaps altogether in order that you may learn that our judge is an
accurate weigher, weighing the deeds of each at the season of the judgment.
For if "he stood the mountains in a balance and the valleys in a scale", how
does he not much more balance our things, whether failings then or successes?
"Do not stand this sin to them." Do not bring it up onto the scale of the
judgment; do not weigh down from me those already weighed down from
themselves; nor let my blessing become for others a starting-point of a curse,
nor my gain a procurer of loss for the Jews.
28 I do not wish my sacrifice to become harm to those sacrificing. For even if
they sacrifice with a wicked reasoning, yet I am ashamed before these, who are
a starting-point even against their judgment for me of the presence toward
you, of the kingdom of the heavens, of the release from the clay, of the
translation above, of the victory of martyrdom, of the confession toward you
and from you. For yours is the voice toward us, by which having been urged I
readily gave myself over to sacrifice, and I emptied the blood as into a
mixing-bowl through the confession into your hands: "Everyone who shall
confess in me before men, I also will confess in him before my Father who is
in the heavens." To him the glory unto the ages of the ages; amen.
