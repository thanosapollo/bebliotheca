9 Mt 2:16 through which they were traveling the cause for which they were
departing into Judaea. He commanded not only the children in Bethlehem, but
also those in its borders, to be destroyed, thinking that in the multitude of
those being taken the one sought would also be destroyed together. And he
commanded those from two years old and under to be slaughtered; for he
suspected so much time to have passed from the generation of Christ according
to the flesh, in part because the magi delayed on the journey, in part also
because he himself was occupied with royal care. Yet he also added to the time
so as to hit the mark. But the <cause> of the destruction of the children is
not the birth of Christ, but an uncovering of Herod's child-murder, which then
took occasion to do evil, since those who crucified Christ did not then become
wicked when they seized Christ, but being wicked they then took occasion to
commit the murder. And for this the children are going to obtain some good
recompense, carrying off equal honor with the martyrs. For as much time as
Herod suspected to have passed from the birth of Christ through the coming of
the magi, of so many years he ordered children to be killed. Do not then
suppose that the cause of the unjust slaughter of the children is the birth of
Christ, but the atheism and unholiness of Herod; for neither did those who
crucified Christ then become wicked, but already existing so and seizing the
moment they manifested who they were. 11 Mt 3:2 The form of the promise was
exceedingly attractive and able to persuade <those> who approached him with
much eagerness, from whom they were going to receive complete remission of
sins. 12 Mt 3:2–11 And this too not incidentally, but as in mystery and type;
for the camel is numbered neither among the completely unclean beasts nor
among the clean, but has a certain middle rank and is ambiguous in its
properties; for bringing up the cud, that is, bringing up the food once
swallowed and sending it again to the teeth and chewing it, is proper to clean
animals, which also belongs to the camel; but not having a cloven hoof is
native to the unclean. And the camel's tread is not divided according to the
impact of the nails. For this reason then John put on the hairs of this
animal, showing that the evangelical calling and the kingdom of God which he
said was drawing near will receive both those from Israel, the clean people,
and those from the unclean nations, and that he was preaching repentance
equally to both these and those. 13 Mt 3:15 The baptism of John was both
perfect and imperfect at once: perfect according to the commandment of the
law, but imperfect, because it did not give remission of sins, but made them
fit for the perfect. Wherefore also Christ, being perfect according to the
law, was baptized with this baptism, that is, that of John. And this he made
clear saying: for thus it is fitting for us to fulfill all righteousness. 14
Mt 3:15–17 It is asked by many, what then was the baptism with which the Lord
was baptized? What then is it? Our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, having become
man for the salvation of all and indeed of a certain paradoxical life

being about to show himself as a beginning, (according to which he is also
called Adam, so that through him and of the rest of risen humans he might
become beginning of that everlasting life, just as it happened that Adam
became originator of this present and mortal life), prefigured all the things
that concern us upon himself; for just as he died and rose because we too were
going to, so, because we were necessarily going to, being typically
transferred through baptism from the present life, be established in the life
that is to come, he took care that this be fulfilled first upon himself. And
this baptism of adoption, economically receiving first that through water and
Spirit, he showed baptism great and honorable, having truly received it first
upon himself. And he himself also lived the way of life outside the law, of
grace, which we too had to live. It was fitting then that the Lord, humbling
himself as one man from the people, be subject to the prophet and baptizer for
baptism, in order that he might sanctify the waters and prefigure in himself
the regeneration through washing and adoption and remission of sins and as
many other goods as he granted us from our baptism, himself needing none of
these as God and taking away the sin of the world. For this reason Christ was
baptized by John not as liable for sin, but in order that as man he might
fulfill all righteousness; second, in order that he might bear witness to John
that he had been sent by God to baptize; third, in order that through the
descent of the Holy Spirit he might sanctify the waters of the Jordan and all
who are baptized in it might be sanctified. And the heavens are opened and the
Spirit comes down when he is baptized and a voice comes, so that no one might
suppose John greater than him; and the one baptizing is not in every way
better than the one baptized; for neither was Ananias of Paul. 15 Mt 3:16 For
this reason the Holy Spirit was likened to a dove, because of the affection
which it has toward us, that God, being rejected by men, no less again through
his own goodness draws near to men; for so also the dove does not depart from
its familiars, even if those wrong it, tearing its young from it. The Holy
Spirit appeared in the form of a dove as affectionate and loving toward men,
and though often rejected by you it again holds to us and does good through
its own goodness; for the dove being an affectionate and man-loving animal,
though wronged by men who tear away and eat its young, does not depart from
its familiars, but holds to them in every way. 16 Mt 3:17 Through the witness
of the Father and the descent of the Spirit the hypostases of the Holy Trinity
are shown; for the Father witnessed from above that the one being baptized was
God, and the one baptizing a man. And this, so that no one might suspect that
John was greater than Christ as baptizing him. And the one witnessing was the
Father, the Son the one witnessed, and the Holy Spirit the one pointing out
the one witnessed. 17 Mt 4:1–2 For since Adam, being in paradise and in
luxury, declined to the worse through deceit, he necessarily leads this one
into the wilderness so as by greater endurance to slacken the tension of the
devil, and he fasts nights and days forty. 18 Mt 4:2 When Christ hungered, as
it is written, then the devil attacks to tempt, since he did not altogether
marvel at the fast in forty days, knowing that Elijah fasted so many days.
Wherefore also taking courage he attacked, thinking him like that one and not
God. 19 Mt 4:4 We are taught from the divine scriptures that not only bread
knows how to nourish the flesh, but also a mere word of God; for if he should
declare and command me to live even without food, it is possible for him, if
he wills, to do this too. Mt 4:7 If as God Jesus

overcame the devil, he did nothing great in conquering the apostate angel
formed by him, nor was the victory inscribed to the humanity; but as man he
prevailed over him by forbearance, teaching us that one must prevail over the
devil not through signs, but through forbearance and longsuffering, and do
nothing for display and mere ambition. 21 Mt 4:8 And the Lord endured the
devil leading him here and there, as also those who dragged him to slaughter
as a sheep, in order that through extreme humility he might pull down that
one's arrogance, not even from this being harmed in purpose by running toward
that to which he was challenging him, yet not being defeated by him and
obeying the things commanded by him. 22 Mt 4:8 <Economically> one must
understand that the devil led him away, since this was being economized while
Christ was eager to do it and seeking it, unto a clear defeat of the one
attempting in vain to tempt. For also in the case of Job he says that the
devil said to the Lord; and yet who is so simple as to think that the devil
converses with God? But because he wished it, God permitted it for a
demonstration of the steadfastness of Job. So also from here he ascribes to
the devil what, while that one wished it to happen, was being economized for
the sake of all. And "he showed" is clear, that he was not showing according
to hypostasis, since it is not even possible to find a mountain so high
through which one who wishes can look down on the whole world, but according
to imagination, as it is the custom of demons for the most part to show, of
which a clear mark is that those who know how to practice sorcery exactly show
many things that are neither present nor happening as present and happening.
23 Mt 4:10 The devil is mad, promising to give to the God Word the things
which he himself made. And the Lord speaks to the devil rebukingly, saying
that we have been taught to worship God alone, not another; for all are his
ministering spirits sent for service on account of those who are going to
inherit salvation. 24 Mt 5:13 There is need then of virtue of one's own
nature; salt is useful for many things, but when it is wholly turned it is
useless as not being able to receive correction from anything else. Wherefore
it is also thrown out. So also you, if you lay hold of virtue, will also
benefit others; but if you turn aside toward things from which no one
<prudent turns aside>, there being no other who can correct you, you will also
be punished. 25 Mt 5:14 25 Just as through light one sees all things, so also
those persuaded by you see through you the works of virtue. 26 Mt 5:15 He
called the bushel wickedness, and the lamp virtue. What then is it that the
Savior says: a bushel under which some put the lamp? Here he calls the bushel
wickedness, and the lamp virtue; and just as those about to do something
unlawful go in darkness, avoiding the light, so also those who work wickedness
hide virtue under it so as to seem not to work evil, but rather good. 28 Mt
5:22 And by saying to the judgment and to the council and to the gehenna of
fire he brings on the same punishment; for he does not signify one thing and
another through the different words, nor a different penalty. If it is not
permitted to say fool, how did the Lord say fools? It is not fitting to call a
brother a fool; for the word is of anger and not of righteousness, but also
impious when it is said against a holy one. But the Lord names fools those in
no way his own and of righteousness, justly, not toward anger, but toward
truth. And gehenna is the eternal and tartarean place of punishment. 31 Mt
5:28 He who has looked in order to desire in a certain way goads the passions
that are at rest through seeing. 32 Mt 5:31 Because of wanting to be with
other women he cast out the first, and through this device the men were taking
the wives of others, and the women the husbands of others. 33 Mt 5:31–32 And
the word about divorces he mixed with the fornicators; for those who dismiss
their own wives at random because of wanting to mix with others were doing

this. And the women the same. He does not then permit the woman who is cast
out to be married, but that the one who is with her be made to pay the
penalties of an adulterer; for even if according to appearance she is outside
the husband, yet according to what is understood she is his body, having once
in the first place been joined by God in the yoking and fitted to the husband
as into one flesh. Therefore neither can the husband marry another. 34 Mt 5:40
Is it fitting not to turn aside from contention toward opponents? When it
happens to be for piety, rather even to endure terrible things and rejoice in
them; but if it is some other of the things of life, gladly to yield to the
one who wishes and not to speak against in any way whatever. 35 Mt 6:24 Mammon
is not only gold, but every finest kind of the things on earth. 36 Mt 6:34 Is
the present day then of maltreatment and toil and misery and labor. 37 Mt 6:34
The day is the things toward need, the taking thought about things to come. 38
Mt 7:15–19 But others say these things have been said about the heterodox
teachers and those who mix falsehood with the truth. And as it is not possible
to take worst fruits from a tree that yields good ones, nor to receive a grape
or a fig from those that bear thorns, so it is not possible to hear good words
from a wicked mind, nor yet to receive bad teaching from a pious mind. And
having secured the rest against being persuaded readily, he then also turns
away those who attempt the teaching of the worse; for every tree, he says,
that does not make good fruit is cut down and thrown into fire. As in the case
of woods, he says, the fruitless is thrown into fire, so also in the case of
men the one who does not bear through the mouth the fruits of piety will
receive the corresponding punishment. 39 Mt 7:26–27 A fool building on sand is
all who do some evil, which these do without toil; and sand is simplicity; and
he calls the fall great even of those who have been turned, as of those who
have fallen from the highest tower; for the unbelievers do not fall, but are
always on the ground. 41α Mt 8:5–9 He now calls a child a household servant,
and Luke makes this clear naming him a slave. And it was of understanding, he
says, that from the probable reasonings concerning himself he believed such
things about Christ. For I too, he says, am a man, but nevertheless of those
over whom I have received authority, of these I am lord. So that it is not
even unlikely that you too, having received this authority from God, by a word
alone commanding, drive out the passions. For the centurion did not approach
as to a son of God and one who is lord of all creation (for this not even the
disciples then knew before the cross), but as to a man who through virtue had
received from God authority greater than according to man. Whence he says
thus: for I too am a man. For since he said to him speak by a word, and this
seemed to belong to God alone, he well added for I too am a man, instead of:
it is nothing marvelous if you can do this being a man, having received from
God, since I too being this receive subjects, excelling in commanding as I
wish. These things the one who is customarily opposing the holy fathers, and I
leave to those who encounter them to see the difference of the
interpretations, and who more attains the evangelical and true sense. 42 Mt
8:29 As then having time in the interval to do what they wish concerning men
they were crying out against the Lord as forcing them before the fitting time
and subjecting them to punishments. 43 Mt 8:30–31 It is asked for what reason
the Lord permitted the demons who wished to enter into the swine. The demons
were inflicting very great evils on men. And from this many, as is likely,
were examining with one another: for what reason does God endure them doing
these things? wishing therefore to show to all the

Lord both what sort of advantage the demons have against men and that they
work far fewer evils on men than they wish and can, being hindered by the
power of God, provided this demonstration not upon men. And he permitted them
to enter into the swine, so that from those both the wrath of the demons and
the power might appear; and those who so quickly destroyed so many swine were
not unable to inflict the same on men, dragging them down and doing to them
all they wish. 44 Mt 8:34 They therefore begged him to pass over to other
places as not being worthy to have so great a good in their own country,
fearing lest they suffer something even greater from the power of the one
present on account of their own sins. 45 Mt 9:5 According to utterance both
are equally easy to say; but what he means is such: you think me a blasphemer
as promising what I cannot. Therefore if you take the voice to be of
boastfulness, what has been thought by you to be easier to promise, the
remission of sins or the healing of the paralysis? But indeed this is harder
to say for one who cannot, the hearers being able to take the proof of his
weakness from nearby. For by as much as it happens to believe the healing
under the eyes, by so much it is easy to take the demonstration of the
speaker, whether he promises justly being able or also lies promising these
things which he is unable to do. But what he means is such: you think me a
blasphemer as promising what I cannot, that is the forgiveness of sins;
therefore if you take the voice to be of a boastful man, what has been thought
by you easier to promise, the remission of sins or the healing of the
paralysis? But indeed this is harder to say for one who cannot, the hearers
being able to take from nearby the proof of his weakness, inasmuch as the
healing is under the eyes. So that you may not think that I promise the unseen
to men, which it is also possible for one not providing it to escape notice,
what is hard to promise for one who cannot, this I both say and do, so that
from here you may take the demonstration of my power. 46 Mt 9:8 They know the
deed to be divine, but they see the one who did it a man. For this reason they
marvel as at a gift of God and not as at his power. 47 Mt 9:14–17 Just as, he
says, if an unfulled rag should be put upon an old garment, by its silent
firmness it tears that too, and new wine put into old wineskins tears the
wineskins by its own heat, it is not possible for them to be enslaved to the
old laws; for you, reasonably following the <old> customs, keep the fasts of
Moses, but these, since they are going to hand over to men certain new and
paradoxical ordinances, are in no way compelled to keep these. But in due time
along with the rest of virtue they will also display the fast, not keeping it
by necessity to certain ordinances and old customs as you do; for the fasting
of those in the law has differed from those who fast in grace in this, that
the former had appointed fasts which they had to fast in any case even if
unwilling, but the latter fast rather by judgment according to what seems good
to them, pursuing it by choice of virtue. And if also the season of the forty
seems to have been appointed for a reminder of the Lord's things, yet even
this fast we pursue rather of judgment. 48 Mt 9:15 And this he said
reasonably, so as both to pull down their boastfulness and to show that he
does not lead his own disciples toward luxury and ease, but there is a time
after which they will display their courage and endurance in terrible things;
and showing further also the difference of the

disciples toward them, that for the time being there is no necessity similarly
for them, these too, he says, will fast. 49 Mt 10:5 And since the cities of
the Samaritans were in the midst of Judaea,

For this dove, even if someone takes away the nestlings, again nests in the
place. So, he says, you too, being plotted against by the opponents, do not

truth by nature; for that which is by placement, being likened toward this,
will never surpass that which is by nature, but its highest end is the
likeness. For this reason he also says there is one instructor, saying that he
himself is the instructor; for the teachers among men are type rather than
truth, because each imparts what he has previously received. Wherefore one
might rather even surpass, having been made more familiar with the good that
has been given. 54 Mt 10:25 There is no greater than this reviling, if indeed
they called me ruler of the demons, than which nothing is worse. And Beelzebul
those around Aquila rendered as swallowing flies or ruler of the dead as we
found in the Books of Kings. 55 Mt 10:26 Perhaps he also speaks about that
time when God will judge the hidden things of men and will light the hidden
things of darkness and will manifest the counsels of the hearts. 56 Mt 10:28
And through gehenna he made clear the perpetual character of the punishment,
having said gehenna as a justice awaiting those who receive the requital
through generation. And by the names he also showed much difference of manner,
here saying of those who kill, but there of those who are destroyed, nor is
there still <salvation>. 57 Mt 11:2–3 But others say: that John sent the
disciples not being ignorant nor wishing to learn, I think is clear even to
one who has been occupied even moderately with the divine scriptures; for this
was his work, to show to all that the Christ was present. And that too is
simple, that John, being about to go through death to those who have died,
sent these to ask if he himself is that one who is going to free also those
held by death so as to preach the good news to them too; for the one who said
behold the lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world clearly <knew> the
passion to be offered to God on behalf of all rather. And altogether if he
knew him to be the Christ, he was ignorant of nothing of the things concerning
<Christ>; for he knew exactly as many good things as would come to men through
him, which it is possible to see him saying to men in various ways from the
things said about him to the evangelists. How then John as a mirror, who had
so great knowledge about Christ as to say many things about him, and to show
him even present now after the witness and the multitude of those words, is
ignorant of the things concerning Christ, <but> to suspect and wish to learn
from him? which itself again is not

consistent; for neither would anyone wishing to learn so great a matter use
one of those around <him> by himself as witness and teacher. And besides these
things the present life is a time of conduct, but after death judgment and
punishment. Yet the death of Christ did not loose the sins of those who had
died before; for what was said, that gates of bronze and bars of iron were
crushed, was said for this reason, because when the body of Christ then first
appeared immortal, death was shown destroyed. What then? Were those before the
coming of Christ wronged? By no means. For it sufficed for salvation not to
worship idols but to worship the one only and true God. But now this alone
does not suffice, but there is need also of the knowledge of Christ. Therefore
one must not say that in Hades who will confess to you, since all there repent
and no one is punished. 59 Mt 11:11 If John is judged according to the birth
from woman toward the rest of men, he will be found greater than all, alone
having been filled with the Holy Spirit in the womb of the mother so that he
leaped, and the mother sharing in this also prophesied; but if he is compared
according to this very thing with those who are going to share in the Spirit
in the kingdom of the heavens, he will be found, <he says>, less even than the
least of all, that he may say of those being regenerated into incorruption
after the resurrection from the dead, that he has not yet shared in so great
grace as not to taste death. And the abundance of the Spirit then will be so
great around men that not even the one who has shared in a lesser measure will
be able thereafter to fall under death. 61 Mt 11:14 Just as Elijah toward the
consummation of the present life will come, preaching my future appearing that
will be from heaven, so also this one, having given an end to the old things,
announced my coming as good news, upon whom a certain new constitution, also
in type of the things to come, is going to come about. 62 Mt 11:19 Those who
look toward the true, he says, accepted the conduct of John and of Christ as
the change of lives having been economized with wisdom toward the benefit of
those who see; for this very thing he calls wisdom, the things that have come
about wisely; for even if the Jews did not believe in Christ, neither through
the fasting of John and the desert life, nor through the condescending diet
and economic way of life of the master Christ himself, yet he himself who
accomplishes all things wisely, by omitting nothing of the things that
contribute to their benefit and salvation, was justified from them. And they
no longer have anything to accuse him of; for he fulfilled all his things,
leaving them not even a shadow of either unreasonableness or ingratitude. 63
Mt 11:25 He calls the Jews wise, because the oracles of God were entrusted to
them, or because they were wise to do evil; and the apostles infants. 64 Mt
11:27 If he is lord of all, those who have not obeyed and recognized the Lord
of all are to be blamed. 65 Mt 11:27 Showing altogether the equality of both
his nature and his dignity he well added to whom the Son may wish to reveal;
for since he said above to the Father that you have revealed them to infants,
introducing his own knowledge here equally with the Father he declared that he
too is unknown to all, if indeed the Father reveals him to whomsoever he also
wishes. He added to whom the Son may wish to reveal, saying the Father, so
that through all things both the equality of the incomprehensible might appear
and of the authority to make known, the Father revealing the Son, and the Son
the Father, to whom they wish. 67 Mt 11:28–30 How then that he himself also
demands much exactness? You have not yet received experience of my things and
for this reason you think these things so. But if you come under my yoke and

Mt 12:29 Here he calls the earth a house, and the men vessels. Therefore since
men have become vessels of the demons and of the devil through wickedness,
having come under that one's authority, it was impossible for the demons to be
deprived of their own possession, except their first being defeated and bound
with bonds so as not to be able again to fight for their own properties. And
this would never have come about by the demons. 69 Mt 12:33 But if these
things, he says, seem to be an unreasoning rebuke and the Holy Spirit is not
truly that which is blasphemed, having once assigned the work to the demon say
also that the thing happening is good, or say the thing happening is evil. But
if you do not say this (for it is worst for men to be held by demons, and good
that the demons be driven from men), how is it possible to ascribe a work so
good to the demons, whom you would never say not to be exceedingly evil? For
fruit is a mark of the nature of a tree. 70 Mt 12:34 O, he says, most wicked
of all men and in nothing standing off from the wickedness of vipers, it is
not marvelous if you utter nothing good; for the words follow the disposition
of the soul; but how do you utter fair things, having once for all chosen the
worse and declining toward the worse? 71 Mt 12:46–50 These things he said not
rejecting the mother and the brothers, but showing that he prefers the kinship
of the soul to all bodily kinship; for toward the one who thought he was
calling him as to something more serious, the converse of his own, it was
necessary to say this, at once also for the teaching of those present; for as
he himself said to the disciples: he who loves father or mother above me is
not worthy of me, so understand for me Jesus preferring the disciples above
mother and brothers. 72 Mt 13:11 The disciples asked him for what reason he
makes the teaching through parables. And he, indicating that it is because of
the unworthiness of the hearers, says to you it is given to know the mysteries
of the kingdom, but to those it has not been given, that he may say that you,
being worthy, will learn all things that tend toward teaching, and also the
clarity of the parables. But toward these he speaks with parables, since they
are not even worthy to learn because of the wickedness of their manner. Then
through the prophetic voice he convicts their wickedness as proclaimed from of
old and sung among almost all by the prophets. 73 Mt 13:13 For these two
reasons it is often his custom to use parables, either because of speaking
about things that are unseen, so as by the parable to make the unclear things
manifest as far as possible, or because of the unworthiness of the hearers,
when no benefit comes to them from the things said. And there is also another
third cause of the parable, that when saying something for conviction he often
dissolves by the parable the harshness of the rebukes, as when he says that
about the vineyard and that he will badly destroy the bad husbandmen, and will
give out the vineyard to others; for saying these things toward Pharisees he
clearly fled the roughness of the words.

13:44–46 Since many of those altogether alien to piety suddenly by divine
