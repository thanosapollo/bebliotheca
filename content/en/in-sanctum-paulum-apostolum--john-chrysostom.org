* On Saint Paul the Apostle

:END:

Proof: that Saint Paul is greater than all the saints. See with what large
letters I have written to you with my own hand, he says. For as among the many
he is without blame, so here too he is of the first rank, using a newer ink;
for there is nothing black for him, nor anything dark with him, nor anything
human. He surpassed all nature, he left crea428tion behind him, thinking to
converse with God alone; or rather I am persuaded that this is the one of whom
the Lord said he is little here, but in the kingdom greater than John, the
greatest of the prophets. For he did not say, there has been born among those
born of women, but there has not been raised. For to be born is one thing, to
have been raised another. To be born is plainly of a woman; but to be raised
is to be advanced, whether to a kingdom or to the episcopate, or to some other
such office, as when one says, one was raised as king, another as governor. So
the Lord, foreseeing that the Apostle was about to be raised up with him and
to bear his name and to take the cross upon himself and to preach him among
all the nations, was saying: Why do you marvel at John as great? Of this great
one another is greater, who has not yet believed, who has not yet been raised
to preach. He has not yet been ordained to the apostleship; I have not yet
appeared to him while he persecutes, but indeed he is in the act of
persecuting. For compared with those already raised, John is truly greater,
but compared with the one about to be raised he is least. For the lesser here
is greater than he in the kingdom, and the one who is going to be greater
among you, let him be your servant and last. Let us see then who is the least
and last of all, that we may know the greater as well, since the last is as it
were a lesson concerning the greater. What then does the Apostle say? And to
me, the least of all saints, this grace was given, and last of all, as to one
untimely born, he appeared also to me. If then this man has been shown last
and least of all, it is clear that he is greater than all, because the Lord
declared it so.
He is greater not only than John, but than all the saints. Are they Hebrews?
So am I. Are they seed of Abraham? So am I. Are they ministers of Christ? I am
more. What is clearer than this? And in the case of the Hebrews and
Israelites, as of lesser ones, he kept silent about the more, lest someone say
he is greater than those but equal to these; but the more honorable claim that
covers all, he added the greater, that he might make the question beyond
dispute. And again, lest he seem to give occasion for true boasting, he at
once sets down the reason why he is beyond them: in labors more abundantly, in
stripes above measure, in death and dangers often, and I labored more than all
of them; and that if each of all will receive according to his own labor, it
is clear that this man will be honored more than all, as having labored more.
What is more splendid than this? If you wish, hear also another proof of the
same kind: Christ came into the world to save sinners, of whom I am first, and
for this reason I obtained mercy, that in me first he might show forth his
great longsuffering.
How did he show it forth in this man, appearing to him last, if according to
times he is not first? 429 For before him he appeared to the disciples, before
the disciples to the prophets, and after these again to those in the world
before them. How then first in this man, unless because first in honor? As
therefore John is last in time among the prophets, but greater in honor, so
also the Apostle is last of the apostles in time, but first in honor of all
the saints. But if you are not persuaded by the truth, be persuaded at least
by the type prefigured concerning him at the recognition of Joseph's brothers,
how Joseph reclined each of the brothers according to seniority, and last of
all the last-born Benjamin. And he made his portion five times the portion of
the brothers, which is a type of what was to come: Joseph of the Lord, the
rest of the brothers of the disciples, and Benjamin of the Apostle, as he
testifies of himself saying, Of the tribe of Benjamin, and there, Benjamin a
young man in ecstasy. The younger is because he is last, and in ecstasy
because of the vision on the road. He stood outside the things of the law,
outside the heavenly things as well, and outside the bodily things; he counted
all things dung, that he might gain Christ.
Be persuaded then that this younger one, the last-born, the last of the
disciples, the untimely birth beyond all, the offscouring of the whole world,
will be honored fivefold by the Lord, as Benjamin was by Joseph, who was not
present at the selling of the brother, who did not consent with the brothers,
who always remained with his own father and grieved him in nothing. Likewise
this man too will not be honored according to grace, but according to what is
owed, as having labored more than all, as always remaining with God the
Father, as not being present at Judas's betrayal. For he became blameless in
the law and incomparable among apostles: For I fill up in my flesh the
deficiencies of the afflictions of Christ for his body, which is the church,
of which I became a minister, according to the saying, He who wishes among
you. Do you see what he utters, what he testifies of himself? How whatever
afflictions Christ fell short of, not arriving at them, he himself filled up,
saying, I fill up the deficiencies of the afflictions of Christ? He means to
say: Christ is the fullness of the law, I am the fullness of afflictions;
truly in stripes above measure, in afflictions exceedingly, in necessities, in
distresses, in labor and toil night and day, and the things he himself
enumerated, saying, besides those that are without.
And who dares to say it? The same man has said, I am conscious of nothing
against myself, and having become blameless, that he might show us he is great
by not being conscious of anything against himself, and, as a second point of
what we are seeking, saying, But I am not justified in this; and again: I have
fought the good fight, I have finished the course, I have kept the faith;
henceforth there is laid up for me the crown of righteousness, which the Lord
will render to me. He did not say, he will give me; 430 for to give is
according to grace, but which he will render; for to render is of a debt. For
what is given according to grace he speaks of thus: Do not neglect the gift
that is in you, which was given to you, not which was rendered to you. But the
crown is given not according to grace, but according to the debt, to those who
have contended lawfully. For if anyone contends, he is not crowned unless he
contends lawfully. Who of those who have gone said, who of those to come will
say these things: 20I have finished the course,20 and 20he will render to me20
the crown as a kind of debt, and I fill up the deficiencies, and I labored
more than all, and are they ministers? I more. Not unjustly then a chosen
vessel, as a servant, as Moses. Not faithful as Abraham, not guileless as
David, not blameless or godly as Job, but he named him a chosen vessel. And
the choosing shows what is better; for no one who chooses chooses the worse.
For he chose us, he says, before the foundation of the world, that we should
be blameless and holy before him. From among the elect, then, the Apostle is
one elect. For these things I endure, he says, for the sake of the elect, that
they also may obtain salvation; and imitate the Lord, who endured the cross
for us, that we might obtain salvation. Rightly then he set it forward,
saying, Be imitators of me, as I also am of Christ.
Did you see the good confidence of a good soul? Did you see one more elect
than the elect, last of all in the preaching, but first of all; last in the
world, first in the kingdom; last to believe, first to be saved and crowned?
It is right to say, The last shall be first. For he fell short in nothing of
the very chief apostles, I dare to say. I now press on with the speech in
boldness, not for lack of proofs, but because the time and the sequence of the
discourse do not allow saying more: not only that he is above every man, but
even above every spiritual creature. Of creation, then, first in honor, and of
the elect more elect.
