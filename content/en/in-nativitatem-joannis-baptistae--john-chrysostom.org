* On the Nativity of John the Baptist
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of our father among the saints John, archbishop of Constantinople, the
Chrysostom, a discourse on the birthday of the holy prophet, forerunner,
and baptist John.
1 Blessed is God: for behold, the leapings of John in the womb have been
shown forth in the works; the good tidings of the righteous one, given
through his feet, have gone forth; the grace which he announced while
still in the womb has come to meet us; the world has seen the gladness
which the Forerunner painted with his feet in the belly. Truly, "others
have labored, and you have entered into their labor"; prophets, driven
forth, cried out beforehand; the righteous, when struck, did not keep
silent; the roll of the patriarchs was in danger; tyrants opposed, kings
made war, rulers drew up in array; the peoples were angry together, the
whole world contended against the contests of the righteous; from this
side they were led before tribunals, from that they were torn with
scourges; everywhere punishments and tortures, everywhere afflictions
and lamentations, everywhere dejection and darkness; but being in such
dejection and so great a darkening of life, we are given the good news
of the winter's release.
2 For John, the great Forerunner of the Lord, while still bound in the
swaddling-bands of nature, pointed out to us the saving spring, and
hastening to proclaim with his voice him whom he announced with his
feet, he forces even the bounds of nature themselves to give way; he
startled Elizabeth as one slow to give birth; he leaped, dancing and at
the same time chafing at being held by the belly; he cried out against
the circuit of the months; he shook the womb as an unjust prison; he
kicked against the bolts of nature; he blamed the manner of childbirth,
and with lips still shut he cried out by his gestures, all but uttering
such things as these to Elizabeth by his movement: "You do wrong, O
mother, by delaying; you carry a prophet in the womb; you hold back the
joy of the inhabited world; you bear the Forerunner of the King, Christ;
you restrain the soldier of the royal proclamation; you compress the
witness and the greater than all who are born of women, and you fetter
the swift runner of piety. You do wrong, O mother; you do not know the
things that are coming, you are ignorant of what shall be. The Light is
arriving: send forth the dawn. The sun is rising: let the morning star
shine first. The Word is at the doors, and there is need of a voice. The
Bridegroom is near, and it is right that the bridal chamber be spread in
the Jordan. There is need of blood that purchases the world, and I must
point out the Lamb from afar. The human race has been swaddled in sins;
he has come who looses those who are bound. Henceforth there is need of
a herald, that all, having learned, may all come to meet him. And do you
hold the Forerunner in the womb?
Do you not consider, O mother, Adam held for so many ages in Hades? Do
you not regard the groaning of Eve? Do you not see the dejection of the
world, or rather its destruction? Still the blood of Abel cries out,
making petition against Cain. With these even the elements themselves
groan together; for this, surely, you also were delivered from the
passion of barrenness, that he who is from a barren woman might announce
him who is from a virgin; for this I leaped in the womb with gladness,
that the devil might dance no longer in the world." Such things, then,
John spoke to Elizabeth while not yet born. And having come forth from
her and having received a greater shining, as was fitting, he pointed
out the Maker to the things made, and with his finger he indicated the
Fashioner to the fashioned things, crying with a loud voice: "Behold the
Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world."

3 But O, how shall I celebrate that ineffable and fearful generation?
How shall I speak the things beyond mind and word? What tongue shall I
take up sufficient for the wonders? A strange conception, birth-pangs
without pain, an untilled ear of grain, fruit without seed, a delivery
of a strange nature, an unknown begetting, a child upon earth having no
father, truly a narrative incomparable in this life; a virgin gave
birth, and having given birth remained a virgin still; the birth did not
hinder the virginity, it did not harm the womb, it did not corrupt her
who gave birth. Fittingly also he who was born granted such privileges
to his mother; for of such things as he alone possessed, such he also
gave to her alone; he himself had no brother in generation, nor indeed
did he give to his mother a sister of the wonder. O the generosity of
God toward men: of what gifts was nature counted worthy through the
female? From where the war began, from there also the King awarded the
peace; for from the time that a virgin is mother and a mother is virgin,
the groaning of Eve has ceased; the daughter has dissolved the maternal
charge; for through her the Master, having been incarnate, sojourned
among us; and he came to change the sorrowful things into joy; he came
to exchange the painful custom; he came to make peace between the things
below and the things above; and how, hear.
There paradise was insulted, here the cave was glorified; there the two,
Adam and Eve, driven by sin, here the ends of the inhabited world led by
joy; no longer do we suspect the reproach of exile, no longer do we
dread the flaming sword, no longer do we fear the bolts of the doors of
paradise, no longer do we fear the bites of the serpent, no longer do we
borrow leaves from a fig tree, no longer do we tremble at the sound of
the footstep; for we have worshipped the coming-forth from the Virgin,
we have beheld the physician of nature. For who has seen, who has heard,
who has ever known a physician blossoming from a weak root? Who appeared
as mother of her own Father? Who generated him who generated by divine
power? Who gave birth to one who is seen as a creature, yet understood
as Fashioner? Who both gained the having of a son and was not deprived
of being a virgin?
4 What then do you say, O Jew? Why do you seem to make your mind
childish, while covered with the gray hair of wickedness? Why do you
read in vain your prophets, or rather mine? For you hold the letters,
but I the realities; you the testament, I the inheritance; you boast in
the Law, but I am magnified in the grace. Why then do you fight against
the prophets? Why do you flee their God-inspired and unlying
predictions? How many generations ago were you reading the things that
have now come to pass? Did you not know that God the Word comes among
men? How then did the prophet Jeremiah say: "This is our God; no other
shall be reckoned in comparison with him; he found out every way of
knowledge and gave it to Jacob his servant and to Israel his beloved.
Afterward he appeared upon earth and consorted with men." But concerning
the Virgin you disbelieve, O Jew, that she gave birth? How then were you
reading the prophet Isaiah crying: "Behold, the virgin shall conceive in
the womb and shall bear a son; and they shall call his name Emmanuel,
which is, being interpreted, God with us." But that after the birth she
remained a virgin, this too you were taught beforehand by Ezekiel the
prophet: "The angel," he says, "led me and set me at the gate of the
east and said to me: Son of man, what do you see? And I said: I see a
gate that has been shut. And he said to me: This is the gate; no one
shall enter through it nor go out, except the Lord Sabaoth alone; he
shall enter and go out; and the gate shall be shut." But that the Master
of all creation was born in a humble place of Bethlehem, this too you
were learning beforehand from the prophet Micah saying: "And you,
Bethlehem, land of Judah, are by no means least; for from you shall come
forth for me a ruler who shall shepherd my people Israel."

But that Herod slays the infants because of Christ the incomprehensible,
not even this was passed over in silence, but it is written thus: "A
voice was heard in Rama, much lamentation and wailing; Rachel weeping
for her children, and she would not be comforted, because they are not."
These things, then, you were learning beforehand, O Jew, through the
prophets, that you might be prepared for his coming to meet him, not
that you might prepare a cross. But leaving those for the present, let
us bring the word back to its former course.
5 And for Elizabeth the time was fulfilled for her to give birth; and
she bore a son; and her neighbors and kinsfolk heard that the Lord had
magnified his mercy with her; and they rejoiced with her. And it came to
pass on the eighth day, when they came to circumcise the child and they
were calling him by the name of his father Zechariah, his mother
answered and said: "He shall be called John." And they made signs also
to his father as to what he would wish him to be called; and having
asked for a writing-tablet he wrote on it saying: "John is his name."
And all marveled; for his mouth was opened immediately and the bond of
his tongue was loosed, and he spoke clearly, blessing God. Indeed: the
infant is born and the voice of the father returns; for thus the voice
of John is shown crying out, because even the name itself has appeared
as an operation of a voice; "For I," he says, "am the voice of one
crying in the wilderness: Prepare the way of the Lord, make his paths
straight." For John, having received the illumination of the Holy
Spirit, pointed out the Maker to the things made and with his finger
indicated the Fashioner to all, crying with a loud voice: "Behold the
Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world." For no longer,
according to the ancient type and law, is there one temple that receives
the sacrifices and is honored in them; but throughout the whole
inhabited world churches and altars send up glory purely to the God who
loves mankind.
For we have received as high priest of our souls Jesus Christ our Lord,
who did no sin, nor was deceit found in his mouth. For we have, says
Paul, a great high priest who has passed through the heavens, Jesus
Christ our Lord; to whom belongs all glory, honor, and worship, with the
unoriginate Father and the all-holy and life-giving Spirit, now and
always and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
