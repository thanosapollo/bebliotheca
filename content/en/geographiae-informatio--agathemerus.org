*** [On the Geography of the Ancients]
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1-h1-h1
:END:

Anaximander the Milesian, hearer of Thales, first dared to draw the inhabited
world on a tablet; after him Hecataeus the Milesian, a man much-traveled,
worked it out with such exactness that the thing was marveled at; for
Hellanicus the Lesbian, a man of much learning, handed down the history
without a map. Then Damastes of Sigeum, having transcribed most things from
the works of Hecataeus, wrote a periplus; next Democritus and Eudoxus and
certain others treated circuits of the earth and peripli.

** Section 2
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1-h2
:END:

The ancients, then, drew the inhabited world round, and Greece lying in the
middle, and of this Delphi; for it has the navel of the earth. But Democritus
first, a man of much experience, perceived that the earth is oblong, having
the length half as much again as the breadth; Dicaearchus the Peripatetic also
agreed with him; but Eudoxus made the length double the breadth, and
Eratosthenes more than double; Crates like a semicircle, Hipparchus
trapezium-shaped, others tail-shaped, and Posidonius the Stoic sling-shaped
and broad in the middle from south to north, narrow toward east and west, yet
the parts toward the Eurus broader, [those] toward India.

** Section 3
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1-h3
:END:

Bounds of the continents: of Europe and Libya the Pillars of Heracles; of
Libya and Asia the Nile, but some the isthmus from the Serbonian lake and the
Arabian gulf; of Asia and Europe the ancients the river Phasis and the isthmus
as far as the Caspian, but the later (the more recent) the Maeotis lake and
the river Tanais.

** Section 4
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1-h4
:END:

And they were called continents (epeiroi) as being boundless (apeiroi) through
ignorance; and Asia from being nearer (asson) to those going from Europe both
on foot and by islands lying in a row, of which Euboea, Andros, Tenos,
Myconos, Icaria, Samos, Mycale; and Europe was named from the breadth (euros);
but Libya was quite unknown to the Greeks, and they were named in Phoenician
from a notable people [Libyans, that is] lions; and Ocean from running swiftly
(okeos) round the earth.

** Section 5
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1-h5
:END:

But Dicaearchus divides the earth not by waters, but by a pure straight cut
from the Pillars through Sardinia, Sicily, the Peloponnese, (Ionia,) Caria,
Lycia, Pamphylia, Cilicia, and Taurus in succession as far as Mount Imaus. Of
the regions, then, he names the one northern, the other southern.

*** [On the Winds]
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h2-h1-h1
:END:

Winds blow: from the equinoctial sunrise Apeliotes, from the equinoctial
sunset Zephyrus, from midday Notos, from the Bear Aparctias, from the summer
tropic Caecias, and next from the equinoctial sunrise Apeliotes, and from the
winter [sunrise] Eurus; and of the western, from the winter sunset Lips, and
next again from the equinoctial sunset Zephyrus, from the summer sunset
Argestes or Olympias; then Notos and Aparctias blowing opposite. So they come
to eight.

** Section 7
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h2-h2
:END:

But Timosthenes, who wrote the peripli, says there are twelve, adding between
Aparctias and Caecias Boreas, between Eurus and Notos Phoenix, also called
Euronotos, between Notos and Lips Leuconotos or Libonotos, and between
Aparctias and Argestes Thrascias or Circius, [so named] by the neighbors. And
peoples dwell at the extremities: toward Apeliotes the Bactrians, toward Eurus
the Indians, toward Phoenix the Erythraean Sea and Ethiopia, toward Leuconotos
the Garamantes beyond the Syrtes, toward Lips the western Ethiopians [the
ones] beyond the Mauri, toward Zephyrus the Pillars and the beginnings of
Libya and Europe, toward Argestes Iberia now Spain, toward Thrascias [the
Celts and the neighboring lands, toward Aparctias] the Scythians beyond
Thrace, toward Boreas Pontus, Maeotis, the Sarmatians; toward Caecias the
Caspian Sea and the Sacae.

*** [Periplus of the Seas]
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h3-h1-h1
:END:

The Great Syrtis is of 5,000 stadia, the Little of 1,600 stadia, and the mouth
of the Adriatic, which some call the Ionian sea, has a crossing of 700 stadia
from the cape of Iapygia to the Ceraunian mountains of Epirus.

** Section 9
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h3-h2
:END:

The Hellespont succeeds the Aegean, ending at Abydos and Sestos; then the
Propontis ends at Chalcedon and Byzantium, where the straits are, from which
the Pontus begins; then the Maeotis lake. Again from the beginning of Europe
and Libya the Iberian is that from the Pillars to the Pyrenees mountain, the
Ligystican that as far as the bounds of Tyrrhenia, the Sardonian that beyond
Sardinia, turning down toward Libya, the Tyrrhenian that as far as Sicily,
beginning from the capes of Ligystice; then the Libyan, then the Cretan and
Sicilian and Ionian [the] also Adriatic poured out from the Sicilian sea,
which * they call the Corinthian gulf or Alcyonis sea. The sea enclosed by
Sunium and Scyllaeum is Saronic, then Myrtoan and Icarian, in which are also
the Cyclades, then Carpathian and Pamphylian and Egyptian; and beyond the
Icarian next the Aegean is poured out.

** Section 10
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h3-h3
:END:

The coasting of Europe from the mouths of the river Tanais as far as the
Pillars of Heracles is of 69,709 stadia; of Libya from Tingis as far as the
Canobic mouth, of 29,252 stadia; of Asia, from Canobus as far as the river
Tanais, the coasting with the gulfs is of 40,111 stadia. Together [the
country] along the sea with [the] gulfs of the inhabited world about us is of
139,072 stadia, the Maeotis lake being measured in, whose perimeter is of
9,000 stadia. The mouth, the Cimmerian Bosporus, is of 78 stadia.

** Section 11
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h3-h4
:END:

The Pontus is of 23,083 stadia, and the Thracian Bosporus, the straits, where
Darius yoked the raft against the Scythians, of 6 stadia.

** Section 12
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h3-h5
:END:

The Persian sea, being circular, drawing the mouth together at the cape of
Carmania and Arabia, has a perimeter of two myriads of stadia. The mouth
between Arabia and Carmania is of 400 stadia.

** Section 13
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h3-h6
:END:

The Hyrcanian or Caspian sea, being crescent-shaped, and according to some
also oblong, is of 22,000 stadia; of the mouth it has a crossing of 4 stadia.

** Section 14
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h3-h7
:END:

The Arabian gulf, being narrow and oblong, begins from Heroopolis, along the
Troglodytic as far as Ptolemais of the Hunt, 9,000 stadia in length; [and from
there as far as the straits at Deire] a voyage of 4,700 stadia; the straits at
Deire of 60 stadia; from there next a voyage along the Erythraean Sea as far
as Ocean of 5,000 stadia; the rest is not sailed. The side along Arabia from
the Aelanite recess is of 14,000 stadia. Arabia itself comes down to the
Erythraean and is elongated for 12,000 stadia.

*** [On the Length and Breadth of the Inhabited World]
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h4-h1-h1
:END:

The length of the inhabited world from the Ganges to Gades is of 68,545
stadia, thus: from the river Ganges as far as Myriandrus on the Issic gulf of
41,725 stadia, thus: from the Ganges to the mouths of the river Indus 16,000
stadia; from the Indus as far as the Caspian Gates 15,300; to the Euphrates
10,050 stadia; to Myriandrus 375 stadia.

** Section 16
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h4-h2
:END:

And from Myriandrus to Gades 26,820 stadia, thus: from Myriandrus to the
Cleides of Cyprus 1,400 stadia; to Acamas the promontory 1,300 stadia; to the
Chelidoniae through the Pamphylian sea 1,300 stadia; to Patara 800 stadia;
(as) to Rhodes 700 stadia; to Astypalaea through the Carpathian 940 stadia; to
Taenarum 1,450 stadia; to Pachynum of Sicily 4,600 stadia; to Lilybaeum
through the Libyan sea 1,520 stadia; to Caralis of Sardinia through the
Tyrrhenian sea 2,800 stadia; to Gades, sailing past the Gymnesiae islands,
10,000 stadia; and from Gades through the Sacred promontory to the harbor of
the Artabri 7,932 stadia. Together they come to 76,477.

** Section 17
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h4-h3
:END:

And otherwise: from the Ganges as far as the river Euphrates 41,350; from the
Euphrates to Mazaca of the Cappadocians 1,950 stadia; from Mazaca through
Phrygia Paroreia and Great Phrygia [and] Caria as far as Ephesus 3,170 stadia.
Together from the Ganges to Ephesus 47,290 stadia; and from Ephesus to Delos
through the Aegean 1,600 stadia; to the Isthmus 1,700 stadia; from the Isthmus
through the Corinthian gulf to Patrae 720 stadia; to Leucas 700 stadia; to
Corcyra 700 stadia; to the Acroceraunian mountains 660 stadia; to Brundisium
700 stadia; from Brundisium for one traveling on foot as far as Rome 2,880
stadia; from Rome to the Alps as far as Scingomagus, which is under the Alps,
4,152 stadia; then through Celtic land as far as the city of Illiberis [3,747
stadia; from Illiberis] through the inns as far as Gades 6,651 stadia, and the
crossing to Gades 60 stadia. Together from the Ganges to Gades 71,560 stadia.

** Section 18
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h4-h4
:END:

And the breadth of the inhabited world from the Ethiopian sea to Meroe is of
5,000 stadia; from Meroe to Alexandria of 10,000 stadia; and from Alexandria
to the Tanais of 18,056 stadia (for the parts above the mouths of the Tanais
toward the north are unknown), thus: from Alexandria to Lindos of Rhodes 4,500
stadia; to the Thoanteion, for one sailing past Rhodes on the right, 400
stadia; to Telos 260 stadia; to Lacter of Cos 320 stadia; to Drepanon of Cos
100 stadia; to the island Arcitin 330 stadia; to Corsiae 100 stadia; to
Ampelos of the Samians 30 stadia; to the (Aegean) Argennon through the Aegean
500 stadia; to the Erythraean Corynaeum 25 stadia; to Phlion, a cape of Chios,
50 stadia; to Melanea, a cape of Lesbos, 450 stadia; to Sigrium of Lesbos 500
stadia; to the island Tenedos 450 stadia; to Sigeum 100 stadia; to the mouth
of the Pontus 2,500 stadia; to Carambis 2,800 stadia; to the mouth of Maeotis
2,500 stadia; to the Tanais 2,200 stadia.

** Section 19
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h4-h5
:END:

Otherwise, from city to city: from Alexandria to Rhodes 4,670 stadia; to
Cnidus 700 stadia; to Cos 200 stadia; to Samos 800 stadia; to Chios 750
stadia; to Mytilene 520 stadia; to Tenedos 950 stadia; to Sigeum 100; to the
mouth of the Pontus 2,500; to Carambis 2,800; to the mouth of Maeotis 2,500;
to the river Tanais 2,200. Together from Alexandria as far as the Tanais
18,690 stadia.

*** [Measurement of the Islands]
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h5-h1-h1
:END:

It remains for us to tell the perimeters of the islands about us, taking them
from Artemidorus and Menippus and other trustworthy men. Gades has length of
108 stadia, breadth of 16 stadia; and the strait at the Pillars of Heracles is
narrowest at 80 stadia. In the Iberian sea the islands: Pityusa the greater
and inhabited of 300 stadia in length, the lesser of 100 stadia. Of the
Gymnesiae, which the Carthaginians call Baliares (for the slingers are so
called, (the) Baliares), the greater has length of 1,200 stadia, breadth of
400 stadia; the lesser of 300 stadia. And the Stoechades, true to their name,
lie next in a straight line before the Massaliot cities; the greater three,
and two small near Massalia itself. Sardinia has a shape like a footprint,
hollow in the middle; the length 2,200. Cyrnus is near Sardinia, but of much
meaner quality, and has less than half the length of Sardinia. The
starting-place to Sardinia and Cyrnus is Populonium of Tyrrhenia; the crossing
is of 2,200 stadia. Of Sicily according to Timosthenes the perimeter is of
4,740 stadia, the shape a scalene triangle. It has a crossing from cape
Pelorus to Italy of 11 stadia as a strait; a side of the island from Pelorus
to Pachynum 1,360 stadia; from Pachynum to Lilybaeum 1,600 stadia; according
to Timosthenes from Lilybaeum to Pelorias 1,700 stadia; from Lilybaeum the
crossing to Aspis of Libya is nearly 1,600 stadia.

** Section 21
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h5-h2
:END:

The island Cercina has length [200], breadth 70, and where it is narrowest 40
stadia. It lies off the mainland city Thenae, which lies at the beginning of
the Little Syrtis. Beside Cercina lies the island Cercinitis, joined by a
bridge, length 40 stadia, and breadth 25 stadia.

** Section 22
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h5-h3
:END:

From Cercina to the island Meninx, the Lotus-eaters' island, the crossing is
600 stadia, as much as the mouth of the Little Syrtis is said to be. Meninx
has length of 200 stadia, breadth of 180 stadia. And there are great tides
about it.

** Section 23
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h5-h4
:END:

The island Cephallenia of four cities has length of 400 stadia. There are also
islands in the Adriatic along Illyria, of which the more notable are Issa and
Black Corcyra and Pharos and Melite, whose perimeters I do not know.

** Section 24
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h5-h5
:END:

The periplus of the Peloponnese, the gulfs also being measured in, is 5,627
stadia, and without putting into the gulfs the periplus is 4,000 stadia. The
length from Malea as far as Aegium is 1,400 stadia. It has a shape like a
plane-tree leaf, being cut by great gulfs. It is drawn together to the Isthmus
of the Corinthians, of 40 stadia in breadth, by the Corinthian gulf and [the]
Saronic; [and the Saronic gulf extends] to cape Sunium on the left [and to
cape Scyllaeum on the right], where the island Calauria, sacred to Poseidon,
lies in front. Next is the Argolic gulf as far as cape Malea, which juts far
out into the sea. (Then the Hermionic gulf.) Next after Malea the Laconic gulf
as far as Taenarum on the right, which bounds on the left the Messenian gulf,
of which Acritas is a cape on the right. Then cape Ichthys juts out, off which
Zacynthus lies, and another, Chelonatas; and last cape Araxus facing
Acarnania; after which the Corinthian gulf is poured out, closed by a mouth of
7 stadia at cape Rhion, which is of the Peloponnese, and Antirrhion of Locris.
From Taenarum to Phycus of Libya the crossing is 3,000 stadia.

** Section 25
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h5-h6
:END:

The island Aegina is oblong, 160 stadia; it lies opposite Attica. Salamis has
length 70 stadia; Ceos 120. Of the Cyclades the greatest is Naxos; therefore
it is called little Sicily. On the right are the Sporades. Euboea is joined by
a bridge to Boeotia; it stretches along Boeotia, Phocis, the Locrians, and on
the other side for a certain way also Attica; for it is long, and therefore
was named Macris, length of 1,700 stadia.

** Section 26
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h5-h7
:END:

Crete at Malea draws near the Peloponnese; its perimeter is of 4,100 stadia,
length 2,300; for it is much elongated. Cythera is between Crete and Laconia.
Cyprus is like a hide in shape; periplus 3,420 stadia, and length 1,300. From
Paphos to Alexandria a crossing with the north wind of 3,800 stadia. The
perimeter of Rhodes is 1,300 stadia; of Cos 550 stadia; Samos 630 stadia.
Icaria is long, rugged, length of 300 stadia. The perimeter of Chios is 660
stadia, of Lesbos 1,100 stadia.
