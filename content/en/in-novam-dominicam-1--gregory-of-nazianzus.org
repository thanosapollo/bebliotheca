ORATION 44.
On the New Sunday.

1. That Dedications be honored is an ancient law, and one that is good; or
rather, that new things be honored through Dedications; and this not once, but
often also, each turning of the year bringing on the same day, so that the
good things may not become faded with time, nor slip away, dimmed, into the
depths of forgetfulness. Islands are dedicated toward God, as we have read in
Isaiah, and what islands these are to be taken to be: I think the Churches
from the nations, just now being established, and rising out of the salt
unbelief, and taking firmness, a footing for God. And a bronze wall is
dedicated, in another prophet; I think a soul that is firm, and gold-like, and
unbroken toward piety. And we are commanded to sing to the Lord a new song,
whether we who were dragged down to Babylon and the wicked confusion by sin,
then saved back to Jerusalem (and there not able to sing the divine song, as
upon a foreign land, but here setting up a new song and a new way of life); or
those who have remained in the good and are advancing; having already
accomplished the one, and accomplishing the other, from the Holy and renewing
Spirit.

2. And the tabernacle of the testimony is dedicated, and very sumptuously,
which God showed forth, and Bezalel completed, and Moses pitched. And the
kingdom of David is dedicated, and not once, but when he was anointed the
first time, and proclaimed the second. And there were the Dedications in
Jerusalem, and it was winter, the winter of unbelief, and Jesus was present,
God and temple, eternal God, recent temple, the one destroyed that same
=36.609= day, and raised in three days, and remaining unto the ages, that I
might be saved, and recalled from the ancient fall, and become a new creation,
being remolded through such loving-kindness. And the divine David seeks a
clean heart being created in himself, and a right spirit being renewed in the
inward parts; not as not having it (for who, if not David of such greatness?),
but as knowing as new what is now always being added. And what need have I of
more Dedications, when it is possible to recognize the present ones, and those
which we celebrate today, having drawn near to life after death? Dedication,
Dedication is the feast, brothers. For let it be said often from delight. And
what is this? You who know, teach; you who do not know, let your hearing be
dedicated.

3. There was a light unapproachable and without succession, God, neither
having begun nor about to cease, nor measured, ever-shining, triple-shining,
seen as to what it is by few, and I think not even by few. And second lights,
radiances of the first Light, the powers about him, and the ministering
spirits. And this light among us not only began later, but is also cut by
night, and cuts night in equal share, entrusted to sight, and poured into air,
and receiving what it gives. For it both grants seeing to sight, and is first
seen by sight, and flowing round the things seen, grants them freedom to
appear. For God, having willed to compose this world, the one composed of
things visible and invisible, the great and wonderful herald of his majesty,
to the everlasting ones he himself is light, and no other (for what need of a
second light for those who have the greatest?); but to those below and about
us he first flashes forth the power of this light. For it was fitting for the
great light of creation to begin from light, by which he dispels the darkness,
and the disorder and lack of order that held until then.

4. And this not having shown it as belonging to an organ from the beginning,
nor as solar, as my discourse has it, but incorporeal and sunless; then also
given to the sun, to illuminate the whole inhabited world. For since for the
other things, having first established the matter, he later gave form, putting
around each order and shape and size, in order that he might work some greater
wonder, here he first established the form of the matter =36.612= (for light
is the form of the sun); and after this he brings on the matter, having
created this sun, the eye of the day. Thus something first among days is
numbered, and second, and third, and the rest, until the seventh and rest-day
of the works, among which the things coming to be are distributed, ordered by
unspeakable reasons, and not given forth all at once by the Word who is able
to do all things, and for whom to think only, or to speak, is a work standing
present. And if man was shown forth last, and this though honored with the
hand of God and with the image, it is no wonder. For it was necessary, as for
a king, that the palaces first be established, and thus the king be led in,
already escorted by all. If then we had remained what we were, and had kept
the commandment, we would have become what we were not, having come to the
tree of life, after the tree of knowledge. And having become what?
Immortalized, and having drawn near to God. But since by the envy of the evil
one death entered into the world, and stole man away through deceit, for this
reason God suffers with our suffering, having become man, and is poor by being
fixed as flesh, that we might be rich by his poverty. From this, death, and
burial, and resurrection. From this, the new creation, and a feast after the
feast, and again I am a keeper of the feast, dedicating my own salvation.

5. What then, someone says? Was not the first Sunday the Dedication, the one
after the holy night and torch-procession? But you give this to today, you
lover of feasts, inventing many brightnesses? That one was the saving day, and
this is the birthday of salvation; and that is the boundary of the burial and
the resurrection, and this purely of the second birth; so that, just as the
first creation takes its beginning from Sunday (and this is clear: for from
this the seventh becomes the Sabbath, being a rest of the works), so also the
second again begins from the same, being first of those after it, and an
eighth from those before it, higher than the high, and more wonderful than the
wonderful. For it bears toward the upper constitution, which also the divine
Solomon seems to me to hint at, legislating to give a portion to the seven, to
this life; =36.613= and also to the eight, to the one to come; from well-doing
here and restoration there. But also the great David seems to sing to the same
the psalms concerning the eighth, just as also to this day of Dedications
another psalm, naming a certain dedication of a house; which we are, who have
been counted worthy both to be and to be called, and to become, a temple of
God.

6. You have the account of the Dedications. But be dedicated yourselves, and
having thrown off the old man, live in newness of life, having put a bridle on
all things from which death comes, having trained all the members, having
hated, or vomited out, all the wicked eating of the tree, and remembering the
old things only for this, that we may flee them. Fair to the sight, and good
for eating, was the fruit that put me to death; let us flee the fair
complexions, let us look to ourselves. Let not desire of beauty conquer you,
nor be snatched away by your eyelids, if possible, even as far as a glance,
having called Eve to mind, the sweet bait, the honored poison. For how will
the alien easily save the one whom his own destroyed? Let not your throat be
sweetened, down which everything given is dragged; and before it is taken, it
is honored, after the taking it is dishonored. Has smell made you soft? Flee
the fragrances. Have you been made tender by touch? Renounce the smooth things
and the softnesses. Has hearing led you aside? Set a door to the deceitful and
over-curious words. Open your mouth to the word of God, that you may draw
Spirit, not gulp death. Whenever something of the forbidden things baits you,
remember who you were, and from where you have been lost. If you turn aside a
little from the word, bring yourself back, before you fall wholly outside, and
are carried down toward death, and become new instead of old, and celebrate
the Dedications of the soul.

7. Let your anger be against the serpent alone, through whom you fell. Let
your whole desiring part be stretched toward God, not toward anything else of
the plotting and slippery things. Let reason superintend all things, and let
not the better be dragged down by the worse. Do not hate your brother, and
that without cause, for whom Christ died, and became your brother, being God
and Master. Do not envy the one who succeeds, you who were envied, and were
persuaded to be envied, and for this were brought down. Do not dishonor a
tear, you who have suffered things worthy of many tears, then been shown
=36.616= mercy. Do not thrust away a poor man, you who have been enriched with
godhead; or if you will, at least do not grow rich against the poor man. For
this too is much among the insatiable. Do not dishonor a stranger, for whom
Christ became a sojourner, of whom we all are strangers and sojourners, lest
you be alienated from paradise as before. Give a share to the one in need of
shelter, covering, food, you who revel in these even beyond need. Do not love
wealth, except it help the poor. Forgive, you who have been forgiven; have
mercy, you who have been shown mercy; acquire for the loving-kind One the
loving-kindness, while there is time; let your whole life be dedicated, every
path of living.

8. You who are under the yoke, give something also to God; for you have been
taken besides. Virgins, the whole to God; for you have been set free. Do not
become thieves of slave pleasure, having fled freedom, from dwelling together,
not with husbands, yet with husbands all the same. I do not accept always
toiling at the reminders of pleasure; I hate even the custom through air. You
who are rulers, fear the more powerful; you of the high thrones, the higher.
Marvel at nothing that does not remain; do not overlook what remains; nor
squeeze tight anything that flows away even when held; do not envy any of the
things that are not envied, but hated; do not be lifted up greatly, lest you
be brought down more greatly; do not think it enough to appear better than the
bad; but grieve at being inferior to the good; do not laugh at the fall of
your neighbor; but pass by safely, as much as you can, yet also give a hand to
the one lying on the ground. Neither when downcast, despair of prosperity; nor
when doing well, of dejection. One year brings four seasons, and one turn of
time, many changes of affairs. Let care cut off pleasure for you, and the
better hope cut off grief. Thus a man is dedicated, thus the day of
Dedications is honored, with such luxury, with such foods. You shall not
appear empty before me, it says; but if there is anything good, bringing it
with yourself. But now appear new, in manner another, wholly changed. The old
things have passed away; behold, all things have become new. This bear as
fruit to the feast, be changed with the good change; and not even so think
highly, but speak the word of David: This is the change of the right hand of
the Most High, from whom is every success for men. The word does not want you
ever to remain in the same, but to be ever-moving, easily moved, altogether
newly-built; if you sin, turning back; if you succeed, =36.617= stretching
further.

9. Yesterday you had the faith of the times, today recognize that of God;
until when will you limp on both hams? Until when will you keep managing? And
sometime be eager also to build; yesterday you honored seeming to be someone,
today prefer being. How far the dreams? And let the truth sometime be your
zeal. Yesterday you were theatrical, today appear contemplative; yesterday
abusive, bold, today well-speaking, gentle; yesterday a reveler, today a
trainer of temperance. Today a wine-drinker, tomorrow a water-drinker. Today
living in luxury on ivory beds, and anointed with the first myrrhs, tomorrow a
ground-sleeper and sleepless; instead of a jester, thoughtful; instead of a
beautifier, poorly clad; instead of proud and boastful, cheap in appearance;
instead of gold-roofed, cramped; nodding downward, instead of high-necked. If
you so think, and so do, there will be for you a new heaven, and a new earth,
and the rest also and that of these

10. But let us now go away, celebrating together also what is fitting to the
season. For all things run together and rejoice together with the feast for
good; for see what sort the things seen are. The queen of seasons processes to
the queen of days, and brings as gifts from herself all that is most beautiful
and most delightful. Now heaven is more translucent; now the sun higher and
more gold-like; now the circle of the moon more manifest, and the choir of
stars cleaner. Now waves pour a libation to the shores, and cloud to the sun,
and winds to the air, and earth to plants, and plants to the eyes. Now springs
flow more clearly; now rivers more abundantly, the winter bonds having been
released. And the meadow is fragrant, and the plant bursts forth, and the
grass is cut, and lambs skip upon the green fields. Just now a ship is led up
from harbors with shoutings, and these for the most part god-loving, and is
winged with the sail; and a dolphin skips around, blowing up as most sweetly
and being sent up, and escorts the sailors with good cheer. And just now a
farmer is fixing a plow, looking up, and calling upon the giver of fruit, and
leads under the yoke a plowing ox, and cuts a sweet furrow, and is gladdened
by hopes. And just now shepherd and cowherd =36.620= fit pipes, and breathe a
pastoral song, and make spring among plants and rocks. And just now a planter
tends a plant, and a bird-catcher builds reeds, and looks under at shoots, and
busies himself with the wing of a bird. And a fisherman looks through the
depths, and cleans a net, and sits upon the rocks.

11. Just now the industrious bee, having unfolded the wing, and risen from the
hives, displays her own wisdom, and flies upon meadows, and plunders the
flowers; and one kills the combs, weaving the hexagonal and opposed tubes, and
interchanging the straight lines with the angles, a work at once of beauty and
of safety; and another deposits the honey in the storehouses, and farms for
the host a sweet and unplowed fruit. Would that we too, the apiary of Christ,
had taken such an example of wisdom and love of labor! And just now a bird is
fixing a nest, and one returns, and another settles in, and another flies
around, and fills the grove with voice, and chatters around the man. All
things hymn God and glorify him with unspoken voices; for in all things God is
thanked through me; and thus their hymn becomes ours, from whom I take the
hymning. Now every kind of living thing laughs, and we feast every sense. And
now a high-necked and haughty horse, impatient with the houses, and having
tyrannized the bonds, stamps upon the plain, and is made beautiful by rivers.

12. What of the rest? Now martyrs keep open-air festival, and process, and
with bright steps call together a Christ-loving people, and make public their
contests. Of these one is also my crowned one (for he is mine, even if not
with me; let envy fall, I speak to those who know), Mamas the much-talked-of,
both shepherd and martyr; who formerly milked the deer that pressed upon one
another, that a righteous man might be fed with strange milk; but now
shepherding a people of a metropolis, and dedicating the spring today with the
many thousands of those pressing from everywhere, both various in beauties of
virtue, and worthy of shepherds, and of victory-speeches. And still more
briefly to say, now a cosmic =36.621= spring, a spiritual spring, spring for
souls, spring for bodies, a seen spring, an unseen spring; of which may we
also partake from there, having been well exchanged here, and as new ones be
sent on toward the new life, in Christ Jesus our Lord, to whom be all glory,
honor, and power with the Holy Spirit, unto the glory of God the Father. Amen.
