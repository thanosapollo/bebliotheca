Must one then speak also concerning these things? And shall we write
concerning the unspeakable, and bring out what must not be brought out, and
utter the unutterable? Who is Attis, or Gallus; and who is the Mother of the
gods; and of what sort is this manner of holiness; and moreover for what
reason this rite was from the beginning shown forth to us as it is, handed
down by the most ancient Phrygians, and received first by Greeks, and these
not chance men, but Athenians, taught by deeds that they did not well to mock
the one celebrating the orgies of the Mother? For they are said to have
insulted and driven out the Gallus as innovating in things divine, not
understanding of what sort the matter of the goddess is, and that she is Deo
and Rhea and Demeter who is honored among them. Then from this, the wrath of
the goddess and the healing of the wrath. For she who became leader to the
Greeks in all that is noble, the prophetess of the Pythian god, commanded them
to propitiate the wrath of the Mother of the gods; and the Metroon, they say,
was set up for this, where all the public records of the Athenians were kept.
And after the Greeks the Romans received these things, the Pythian advising
them also, for the war against the Carthaginians, to bring the goddess from
Phrygia as an ally. And perhaps nothing hinders us from adding a short history
here.

* Section 2
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h2
:END:

For when they learned the oracle, the inhabitants of god-beloved Rome send an
embassy to ask from the kings of Pergamon, who then ruled Phrygia, and from
the Phrygians themselves, the most holy image of the goddess. And taking it
they brought the sacred freight, placing it in a broad merchant-ship able to
sail easily over so many seas. And having crossed the Aegean and the Ionian,
then having sailed around Sicily and the Tyrrhenian sea, she put in at the
mouths of the Tiber; and the people poured out of the city together with the
senate, but before the others there met her all the priests and priestesses
and all in the fitting order according to the ancestral custom, looking up
expectantly toward the ship running with a fair wind, and they watched the
surge about the keel as the waves were split; then they greeted the ship as
she sailed in, each worshipping as he happened to stand from far off. But she,
as if wishing to show the Roman people that they were not bringing a lifeless
wooden image from Phrygia, but that what they had taken from the Phrygians and
were carrying has some greater and more divine power, when she touched the
Tiber, stops the ship as if suddenly rooted in the Tiber. So they pulled
against the stream, but she did not follow. And thinking they had met with
shallows they tried to push the ship, but she did not yield to those pushing.
Then every device was brought to bear, but she was no less immovable; so that
a terrible and unjust suspicion falls upon the maiden consecrated to the most
holy priesthood, and they accused Claudia (for this was the name of the
reverend maiden) of not keeping herself altogether unstained and pure for the
goddess; that the goddess was therefore angry and openly wrathful; for already
the matter seemed to all to be more than human. But she at first was filled
with shame at the name and the suspicion; so very far was she from the
shameful and unlawful deed. But when she saw the charge already prevailing
against her, taking off her girdle and putting it about the prow of the ship,
as if from some inspiration she ordered all to withdraw, then she prayed the
goddess not to overlook her held by unjust slanders. And crying out as if some
naval command, they say, "Lady Mother, if I am chaste, follow me," she said.
And indeed she not only moved the ship, but even drew it a long way against
the stream; and the goddess, I think, showed the Romans two things on that
day: that they were bringing from Phrygia not some small freight of little
worth, but one worth everything, and not as a human thing, but truly divine,
not lifeless earth, but some living and daemonic thing. This then is one thing
the goddess showed them; and another, that not even one of the citizens would
escape her notice, whether he were good or base. And the war of the Romans
against the Carthaginians was immediately set right, so that the third war
took place only about the walls of Carthage itself.

Now as for the things of the history, even if they will seem to some
incredible and to belong in no way to a philosopher or a theologian, let them
be told no less, being recorded in common by most historians, and preserved
also on bronze images in strongest and god-beloved Rome. And yet it has not
escaped me that some of those who are too wise will say these things are old
wives' tales not to be endured. But it seems to me better to trust the cities
in such things than these clever men, whose little soul is keen, but sees not
one thing sound.

:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h3

And concerning the things it has just now come upon me to speak of, at the
very season of the holy rite, I hear that some things have been philosophized
also by Porphyry concerning them, yet I do not know, for I have not met with
them, even if they should somewhere happen to agree with the discourse. But
this Gallus and Attis I myself conceive from my own store to be the substance
of the generative and demiurgic intellect that begets all things as far as the
last matter, having in itself all the reasons and the causes of the enmattered
forms; for the forms of all things are not in all things, nor in the highest
and first causes are those of the last and final things, after which there is
nothing but the name of privation with a dim notion. There being then many
substances and very many demiurges, of the third demiurge, who has the reasons
of the enmattered forms as transcendent and the causes as continuous, the last
nature, which by abundance of the generative power descends even as far as
earth through that which comes down from above from the stars, is the Attis we
seek. And perhaps I must distinguish more clearly concerning what I say. We
say that there is a certain matter, but also an enmattered form. But if some
cause has not been set before these, we would unawares introduce the Epicurean
doctrine. For if nothing is older than two principles, some automatic motion
and chance allotted them together. But we see, says some keen Peripatetic like
Xenarchus, that the fifth and circular body is cause of these. But Aristotle
also is ridiculous in seeking and being busy about these things, and likewise
Theophrastus; at any rate he did not know his own voice. For as when he came
to the incorporeal and intelligible substance he stopped, not being busy about
the cause, but saying that these things are so by nature; he ought surely also
in the case of the fifth body, taking that they are by nature in this way, no
longer to seek the causes, but to stop at them and not fall out toward the
intelligible, which is nothing by nature in itself, but has otherwise an empty
notion. For such things I remember having heard Xenarchus saying. Whether then
he said these things rightly or not, let it be left to the extreme
Peripatetics to pick at; but that they are not agreeable to me is surely clear
to everyone, since I even suppose the Aristotelian hypotheses to be rather
deficient, unless one should bring them to the same as Plato's, or rather
these also to the prophecies given from the gods.

* Section 4
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h4

And this perhaps is worth inquiring: how the circular body can have the
incorporeal causes of the enmattered forms. For that without these generation
cannot subsist is surely evident and clear. For why are the things that come
to be so many? And from where male and female? And from where the difference
of the things that are according to kind in defined forms, unless there should
be some pre-existing and pre-subsisting reasons and causes pre-subsisting in
the manner of a paradigm? Toward which if we are dim-sighted, let us still
purify the eyes of the soul. And right purification is to turn toward oneself
and to understand how the soul and the enmattered intellect is as it were a
mould and image of the enmattered forms. For there is not one of the bodies,
or of the incorporeals that come to be and are contemplated about the bodies,
whose image the intellect cannot take incorporeally, which it would never have
done if it did not have something akin to them by nature. For this reason
Aristotle also said the soul is a place of forms, except not in actuality but
in potentiality. Such a soul then, and that which is turned toward the body,
must have these things in potentiality; but if some soul should be unrelated
and unmixed with this, one must think the reasons no longer exist in
potentiality, but all in actuality. And let us take these things more clearly
through the example which Plato also used in the Sophist, toward another
argument, yet he used it nonetheless. But I do not bring the example as a
demonstration of the argument; for one must not take it by demonstration, but
by direct apprehension alone, for it is concerning the first causes, or at
least those of the same rank as the first, if indeed for us, as it is right to
think, Attis also is a god. And what and of what sort is the example? Plato
says somewhere that of those who spend their time about imitation, if one
should wish to imitate so that the things imitated should subsist, it is
laborious and hard and, by Zeus, nearer to the impossible; but easy and ready
and very possible is that which imitates the things that are through seeming.
Whenever then we take the mirror and carry it about, easily taking impressions
from all the things that are, we show the types of each. From this example let
us transfer the likeness to what has been said, so that the mirror may be what
Aristotle calls the place of forms in potentiality.

But the forms themselves must first subsist altogether in actuality before
that which is in potentiality. Since then the soul in us, as Aristotle also
thinks, has the forms of the things that are in potentiality, where shall we
first set these in actuality? In the enmattered things? But these are clearly
the last. It remains then to seek immaterial causes set in actuality before
the enmattered, with which our soul, subsisting alongside and coming forth
together, must receive from there, as mirrors from certain things that are,
the reasons of the forms, and give them through nature both to matter and to
these enmattered bodies. For that nature is a demiurge of bodies we know, as
being some whole of the universe, and the particular nature of each of the
things in part, is surely evident and clear, but nature in us is in actuality
without imagination, while the soul above this has taken on imagination as
well. If then nature is agreed to have the cause even of things of which it
does not have the imagination, why in the gods' name shall we not give this
very thing still more and as something older to the soul, where we already
know it in imagination and grasp it by reason? Then who is so contentious as
to agree that the enmattered reasons belong to nature, even if not all and at
the same time in actuality, yet all in potentiality, but not to give this very
thing to the soul? Therefore if the forms are in nature in potentiality and
not in actuality, and still in the soul in potentiality, more purely and more
distinctly, so that they are even grasped and known, but nowhere in actuality:
from where shall we hang the cables of everlasting generation? And where shall
we seat the arguments for the eternity of the cosmos? For the circular body is
from substrate and form. It is necessary then, even if these are never in
actuality apart from one another, that those things at least in conception
exist first and be thought older.

Therefore since some altogether immaterial cause of the enmattered forms also
has been granted, under the third demiurge, who for us is father and master
not of these only but also of the visible and fifth body; separating Attis
from him, the cause that descends as far as matter, we have believed Attis and
Gallus to be a generative god, whom the myth says blossomed when exposed
beside the eddies of the river Gallus, then appearing beautiful and great was
loved by the Mother of the gods. And she entrusted all other things to him and
put the starry cap about him. But if this visible heaven covers the head of
Attis, must one not perhaps take the river Gallus to hint at the Milky Way?
For there they say the passible body is mixed with the impassible circular
motion of the fifth. Thus far the Mother of the gods permitted this beautiful
one, and this intellectual god resembling the sun's rays, Attis, to leap and
dance. But when going forward he came as far as the last things, the myth said
he went down into the cave and consorted with the nymph, hinting at the
wetness of matter; and it did not now say matter itself, but the last
incorporeal cause, which pre-subsists before matter. It is said also by
Heraclitus /it is death for souls to become wet;/ this Gallus then, the
intellectual god, the unifier of the enmattered forms under the moon, coming
together with the cause set before matter, coming together not as one thing
with another, but as we say a thing itself sinking down into itself.

Who then is the Mother of the gods? The source of the intellectual and
demiurgic gods who govern the visible ones, she who both bore and dwells with
great Zeus, a goddess who came to subsist as great after the great one and
with the great demiurge, mistress of all life, cause of all generation, who
most easily accomplishes the things that are made, and generates without
passion and creates the things that are together with the father; she is both
a virgin without mother and shares the throne of Zeus and is truly mother of
all the gods. For having received in herself the causes of all the
intelligible and hypercosmic gods she became a source for the intellectual
ones. And this goddess, being also providence, a passionless love of Attis
came upon; for not only the enmattered forms are voluntary for her and
according to her will, but much more their causes. The myth then said that the
providence which preserves the things that come to be and perish loves the
demiurgic and generative cause of these, and commands her to beget rather in
the intelligible and wishes him to be turned toward her and dwell with her,
and makes it a command, toward none of the others, at once pursuing the
uniform that saves, and fleeing that which inclines toward matter; and she
commanded him to look toward herself, being a source of the demiurgic gods,
not dragged down into generation nor enchanted; for thus the great Attis was
going to be also a better demiurge, since in all things the turning toward the
better is more effective than the inclination toward the worse. Since the
fifth body also is more demiurgic than the things here and more divine, by
being more turned toward the gods, for surely the body, even if it be of the
purest aether, no one would dare to say is better than an unstained and pure
soul, such as the demiurge sent forth the soul of Heracles. Then however it
both was and seemed more effective than when that soul gave itself to a body.
Since also for Heracles himself now, having gone over wholly to the whole
father, the care of these things has become easier than it was before, when he
went about among men wearing bits of flesh. Thus in all things the
standing-apart toward the better is more effective than the turning toward the
worse. Which the myth wishing to teach says the Mother of the gods advised
Attis to serve her and neither to go away nor to love another.

But he went forward as far as the last things, having gone down to matter. And
since the unlimited had to cease sometime and stand, Corybas, the great
Helios, who shares the throne with the Mother and creates all things with her
and provides with her and does nothing apart from her, persuades the lion to
become an informer. And who is the lion? We hear of him as the burning one, so
then the pre-subsisting cause of the hot and fiery, which was going to war
against the nymph and be jealous of her communion with Attis; and we have said
who the nymph is; and the myth says he served the demiurgic providence of the
things that are, that is, the Mother of the gods; then having detected and
become an informer he became the cause of the castration for the youth. And
what is the castration? A checking of the unlimited; for the things of
generation stood in defined forms, being held back by the demiurgic
providence, not without the so-called madness of Attis, which departing from
the measure and going beyond it and for this reason as if growing weak and no
longer able to be itself; which it is not unreasonable should subsist about
the last cause of the gods. Consider then, viewing the fifth body unaltered
according to every alteration, about the illuminations of the moon, where then
the cosmos that is continually coming to be and perishing neighbors the fifth
body. About her illuminations we observe some alteration and affections
befalling.

It is not strange then that this Attis should also be some demigod; for the
myth also wishes this; or rather a god in the whole; for he proceeds from the
third demiurge and is led back again to the Mother of the gods after the
castration; but since he seems wholly to slope and incline toward matter, one
would not err in taking him as last of the gods, but leader of all the divine
races. And the myth calls him a demigod for this reason, indicating his
difference from the unchanging gods. For the Corybantes given by the Mother
escort him, the three ruling hypostases of the races stronger after the gods.
And he rules also the lions, who having divided among themselves the hot and
fiery substance together with their leader the lion are causes first for fire,
and through the heat from there they are causes of motive activity and of
safety also for the others; and he wears the heaven about him instead of a
tiara, as if setting out from there toward earth.

This is the great god Attis for us; these are the until now lamented flights
and hidings and disappearances of king Attis and the settings into the cave.
And let the time in which it happens be my evidence of this. For they say the
sacred tree is cut on the day when the sun comes to the extremity of the
equinoctial arc; then next the sounding of trumpets all around is taken up;
and on the third the sacred and secret harvest of the god Gallus is cut; after
these, they say, the Hilaria and festivals. That then the castration talked of
among the many is a standing-still of the unlimited is clear from this: when
the great Helios has touched the equinoctial circle, where there is what is
most defined; for the equal is defined, but the unequal is unlimited and
cannot be traversed; according to the reason the tree is cut at once; then
next the rest happens, some things because of the mystic and secret
ordinances, and some also that can be spoken to all. And the cutting of the
tree, this belongs to the history concerning Gallus, but nothing to the
mysteries in which it is taken up, the gods teaching us I think symbolically
that we must pluck the fairest thing from earth, virtue with piety, and carry
it to the goddess, to be a symbol of the good way of life here. For the tree
grows from earth, but hastens as it were into the aether and is fair to see
and can give shade in the heat, and already also put forth fruit from itself
and give it as a gift; so great a share of the generative has it in abundance.
The ordinance then commands us, who are heavenly by nature but have been
brought to earth, having reaped virtue with piety from the way of life on
earth, to hasten to the ancestral and life-giving goddess.

Straightway then after the castration the trumpet gives the recall to Attis
and to all of us who once flew from heaven to earth and fell. After this
symbol then, when king Attis stops the unlimited through the castration, the
gods command us also to cut out the unlimited in ourselves and to imitate the
leaders, and to run back to the defined and the uniform and, if it is
possible, to the One itself; which having happened the Hilaria must altogether
follow. For what could be more cheerful, what more glad, than a soul that has
escaped unlimitedness and generation and the surge in it, and has been led up
to the gods themselves? One of whom, Attis also being, the Mother of the gods
in no way overlooked going further forward than he ought, but turned him
toward herself, commanding him to stop the unlimited.

And let no one suppose me to say that these things were once done and came to
be, as if the gods themselves did not know what they would do, or were
correcting their own errors. But the ancients, the causes of the things that
always are, either the gods guiding them or searching by themselves, or better
perhaps to say seeking under the gods as leaders, then having found them
covered them with paradoxical myths, so that through the paradoxical and
incongruous the fiction being detected might turn us toward the search for the
truth, the irrational benefit through the symbols alone sufficing I think for
the unlearned, but for those excessive in prudence the truth concerning the
gods being useful only in this way, if one examining it under the gods as
leaders should find and take it, being reminded through the riddles that one
must seek something concerning them, and at last and as it were at the crown
of the matter, having found it through inquiry, should go forward, not by
reverence and faith in another's opinion so much as by his own activity
according to intellect.

What then do we say it is, as in a summary? Having discerned as far as the
fifth body that not only the intelligible, but also these visible bodies,
belong to the impassible and divine portion, they held that as far as this
there are gods unmixed; but by the generative substance of the gods, of the
things that have come to subsist alongside here, matter having come forth from
eternity together with the gods, and from them and through them because of the
overfullness of their generative and demiurgic cause, the providence of the
things that are, being coessential with the gods from eternity, and sharing
the throne with king Zeus, and being a source of the intellectual gods,
ordered and set right and transferred to the better even what seems lifeless
and barren and refuse and, as one might say, the offscouring and lees and
sediment of the things that are, through the last cause of the gods, into
which the substances of all the gods terminate.

For this Attis, having the tiara spotted with stars, it is quite clear that he
made the visible lots of all the gods into the apparent cosmos the beginnings
of his own kingship; upon him the unmixed and pure was as far as the Milky
Way; but about this place already, the passible being mixed with the
impassible and matter subsisting alongside from there, the communion with this
is a descent into the cave, not happening involuntarily for the gods and their
Mother, but said to have happened involuntarily. For by nature the gods being
in a better state, the better things do not wish to drag them from there to
these things here, but through the condescension of the stronger to lead these
also up to the better and more god-beloved lot. Thus also the Mother is said
not to hate Attis after the castration, but she is no longer angry, yet is
said to be angry because of the condescension, that being better and a god he
gave himself to the inferior; but when he has stopped the procession of the
unlimited and has ordered this unordered thing through sympathy with the
equinoctial circle, so that the great Helios may govern the most perfect
measure of defined motion, the goddess leads him back to herself gladly, or
rather has him with herself. And there never was a time when these things did
not have this manner which they now have, but Attis is always a servant and
charioteer to the Mother, and always is eager toward generation, and always
cuts off the unlimited through the defined cause of the forms. And being led
up as if from earth he is said to rule again the ancient sceptres, having in
no way fallen from them nor falling, but said to have fallen from them because
of the mixing with the passible.

But this perhaps is worth raising as a further difficulty: for there being a
double equinox, they prefer not that in the Claws, but that in the Ram. What
then is the cause of this is surely clear. For since the sun then begins to
approach us from the equinox, the day I think increasing, this season seemed
more fitting. For besides the cause which says that light is concurrent with
the gods, one must believe that the leading-up rays of the sun are akin to
those who hasten to be released from generation. And consider it clearly: he
draws all things from the earth and calls them forth and makes them sprout by
his life-kindling and wonderful heat, separating I think the bodies toward
extreme fineness, and lightens the things that by nature are carried downward.
Such things must be made evidence of his unseen powers. For he who in bodies
through the body-like heat accomplishes this thus, how will he not through the
unseen and altogether incorporeal and divine and pure substance seated in the
rays draw and lead up the fortunate souls? Therefore since this light has
appeared akin to the gods and to those who hasten to be led up, and such a
thing increases in the cosmos among us, so that the day is greater than the
night, when Helios the king has begun to travel through the Ram; it has been
shown also that the nature of the god's rays is leading-up, both through the
visible activity and through the unseen, by which countless souls have been
led up from the senses, having followed the brightest and most sun-like. For
this sort of sense of the eyes the divine Plato hymned as not only desirable
nor useful for life, but also a guide toward wisdom. And if I should also
touch the unspeakable mystagogy which the Chaldaean raved concerning the
seven-rayed god, leading up the souls through him, I shall speak things
unknown, and very unknown to the crowd, but known to the blessed theurgists;
wherefore I will be silent about them for now.

And as I was saying, that one must not take even the season as unreasonable,
but as having been added to the ordinance by the ancients as far as possible
with likely and true reason, a sign of this is that the goddess herself
divided the equinoctial circle. For about the Balance the solemn and secret
mysteries are celebrated for Deo and Kore. And this happens reasonably. For
one must be initiated again also when the god is departing, so that we may
suffer nothing difficult under the godless and dark power when it prevails.
Twice then the Athenians celebrate the mysteries for Deo, the Lesser
Mysteries, they say, in the Ram itself, and the Greater when the sun is about
the Claws, for the reasons I just now spoke. And I think they have been named
Greater and Lesser also for other reasons, but most of all, as is likely, for
this, the god departing more than approaching; wherefore in these there is
only so much as for a reminder. Inasmuch as the savior and leading-up god is
also present, the preliminary rites of the initiation were laid down; then a
little later continuous purifications and the holy rites of the priests. But
when the god is finally departing toward the opposite zone, both for guard and
for safety the very chief of the mysteries is accomplished. And see: as here
the cause of generation is cut off, so also among the Athenians those who
touch the unspeakable things are all-holy, and the hierophant who leads them
has turned away from all generation, as having no part in the procession to
the unlimited, but in the defined and always remaining substance held together
in the One, unstained and pure. Concerning these things then so much is
enough.

It remains then, as is likely, to go through concerning the holy rite itself
and the holiness, so that from here also we may take whatever contributes to
the subject. And that at once seems ridiculous to all: the sacred law allows
one to touch meats, but forbids the seeds. Are not those lifeless, and these
living? Are not those clean, and these filled with blood and many other things
not easy for sight and hearing? Is not, the greatest thing, this attached to
those, that no one is wronged from the eating, but to these, that the animals
are sacrificed and slaughtered, feeling pain, as is likely, and being worn
down? These things even many of the excessive would say; but those other
things already even the most impious of men mock. They say the shoots of the
vegetables are eaten, but the roots are declined, as for instance turnips. And
figs they say are eaten, but pomegranates no longer, and apples besides.
Having heard these things often from many whispering, but also having said
them myself before, I seem to be the only one of all who will know much
gratitude to the master gods, most of all to all, but before the others to the
Mother of the gods, as in all other things, so also in this, that she did not
overlook me wandering as if in darkness, but first commanded me to cut off,
not according to the body, but according to the irrational impulses and
motions of the soul, the superfluous and vain things, for the intellectual
cause that pre-subsists of our souls. And she put into my mind certain reasons
perhaps not altogether out of tune with the true and at the same time holy
science concerning the gods. But I seem, as if not having what to say, to run
around in a circle. Yet it is possible for me also, going through each, to
give clear and far-shining causes why it is not lawful for us to bring forward
these things which the divine ordinance bars; and I will do this a little
later; but it is better now to set forth as it were certain types and rules,
following which, even if something often by haste should pass the argument by,
we shall have means to judge concerning these things.

And it is fitting first to recall in brief what we said Attis is and what the
castration, and of what the things that happen after the castration as far as
the Hilaria are symbols, and what the holiness wishes. Attis then was said to
be a certain cause and a god, the one who proximately creates the enmattered

circumference of the all, whose name is the equinox according to the work. And

otherwise than through the recall and rising-up toward the older and more
original causes. And of the holiness itself we say the aim is the ascent of
souls.

Therefore he does not first allow one to eat the seeds that sink under the
earth; for earth is the last of the things that are. And here Plato also says
the evils have been driven and turn about, and through the oracles the gods in
many places call it refuse, and command one to flee from here. First then the
life-giving and provident goddess does not permit one to use even as far as
the nourishment of bodies the things that sink under the earth, advising us to
look toward heaven, or rather even above heaven. Some have used one seed, the
pods, thinking it not a seed so much as a vegetable, because it is somehow by
nature upward-borne and upright and not even rooted down in the earth; but it
is rooted as a fruit hangs from some ivy tree or even a vine, and from a
stalk. It is forbidden us then to use the seed of plants for this reason, but
it is permitted to use fruits and vegetables, not those that hug the ground,
but those that are lifted from earth up aloft. Thus also of the turnip she
commands one to decline the earth-loving part as chthonic, but the part that
comes up and is lifted into height she allows one to bring to oneself as being
clean by this very thing. Of the vegetables then she grants one to use the
shoots, but forbids the roots, and especially those that are nourished in and
sympathize with the earth. And moreover of the trees she did not permit
apples, as sacred and golden and images of unspeakable prizes and of
initiatory things, to be destroyed and consumed, being worthy of the
archetypes for the sake of being revered and served; but pomegranates she
declined as a chthonic plant, and the fruit of the palm perhaps one might say
because it does not grow about Phrygia, where the ordinance was first
established; but it seems to me rather that as a plant sacred to the sun and
being unaging she did not allow it to be consumed in the holy rites for
nourishment of the body. Besides these it is forbidden to use all fish. And
this problem is common also with the Egyptians.

And it seems to me that for two reasons one would abstain from fish, most of
all always, but at any rate in the holy rites: one, that of those things which
we do not sacrifice to the gods it is not fitting even to eat. And perhaps
there is no fear that some greedy glutton here should catch me, as I remember
already having suffered it somewhere before, hearing him say /But why? Do we
not often sacrifice them to the gods too?/ But we had something to say to this
also. And we do sacrifice them, I said, O blessed one, in certain initiatory
sacrifices, as the Romans a horse, as many other beasts and animals, dogs
perhaps the Greeks to Hecate and the Romans too; and there are many of the
initiatory things among others, and publicly for the cities once or twice a
year such victims, but not in the honorific ones, of which alone it is right
to have a share and to set a table for the gods. But we do not sacrifice fish
in the honorific sacrifices, because we neither tend them, nor care for their
generation, nor have we herds of them as of sheep and cattle, and so also of
fish. For these animals being helped by us and multiplying would justly for
this reason assist us both for the other needs and before the others for
honorific sacrifices. This then is one argument why I do not think one should
bring fish as food in a time of holiness. And another, which I think even more
fits what has been said before, that in a way they themselves, having sunk
down in the deep, would be more chthonic than the seeds, and he who desires to
fly up and, aloft above the air, to fly toward the very peaks of heaven, would
justly turn away from all such things, and would chase after and run after the
things that stretch toward the air and hasten toward the upward way and, to
speak more poetically, that look toward heaven. He permits one then to use
birds except a few who happen to be altogether sacred, and of the four-footed
the customary ones except the pig. And this as being altogether chthonic in
form and in way of life and in the very reason of its substance. For it is
full of residue and thick in flesh; he banishes it from the sacred food. For
it is believed to be a dear victim to the chthonic gods not unreasonably. For
this animal is without sight of heaven, not only not wishing, but not even
being by nature able ever to look up. Such then are the causes the divine
ordinance has spoken concerning the abstinence from the things from which one
must abstain; and we who understand share them with those who know the gods.

And concerning the things he permits one to use we say this much, that not all
things for all, but the divine law, looking to what is possible for human
nature, permitted the use of these many things, not so that all of us should
of necessity use all; for this perhaps is not easy; but so that for that man
for whom first the power of the body grants it, then some abundance runs with
it, and third the choice, which in the sacred things it is right so to
stretch, that one even beyond the power of the body should start and be eager
to follow the divine ordinances. For this is most of all more effective for
the soul itself toward salvation, if it should make a greater account of
itself, but not of the safety of the body, and besides the body itself appears
to have a share, without noticing, of a greater and more wonderful benefit.
For when the soul gives its whole self to the gods, having entrusted all that
is its own to the stronger, the holy rite I think following and before this
the divine ordinances leading, there being no longer anything that bars and
hinders; for all things are in the gods and all things subsist about them and
all things are full of gods; at once the divine light shines upon them, and
they, having been made divine, put a certain tension and strength upon the
connate spirit, and this being tempered by them as it were and strengthened is
a cause of safety for the whole body. And that most of all all diseases, or if
not, that the most and greatest have come to be from the turning and
displacement of the spirit, there is no one I think of the Asclepiads who will
not say. For some say all, and some the most and greatest and hardest to heal;
and the oracles of the gods also bear witness to these things, and I say that
through the holy rite not the soul only, but also the bodies are thought
worthy of much help and safety; for the gods promise that even the /mortal
covering of bitter matter/ is saved for them, commanding this to the
exceedingly holy of the theurgists.

What argument then remains for us, especially as we have been granted to
string these things together without drawing breath in a short part of the
night, having neither read anything beforehand nor considered concerning them,
nor even chosen to speak concerning these things before asking for these
tablets? And the goddess is my witness of the word. But, as I said, what
remains is for us to hymn the goddess together with Athena and Dionysus, whose
festivals also the law has set in these holy rites? I see the kinship of
Athena with the Mother of the gods through the providential likeness in both
their substances, and I observe also the divided demiurgy of Dionysus, which
the great Dionysus, having received from the uniform and abiding life of great
Zeus, inasmuch as he also came forth from him, distributed among all the
visible things, governing and ruling the whole divided demiurgy. And it is
fitting with these to hymn also the Charming Hermes; for this god is so called
by the initiates, as many as say they light torches for Attis the wise. Who
then is so thick in soul that he does not understand that through Hermes and
Aphrodite all things of generation everywhere are called back that have the
for-the-sake-of-something altogether and in every way, which is especially
proper to reason? And is not Attis this one who a little before was unwise,
but now is called wise because of the castration? Unwise because he chose
matter and governs generation, but wise because he ordered this refuse into so
great a beauty and transferred it, as much as no art and understanding of men
could imitate. But what will be the end of my words? Or is it clear that it is
the hymn of the great goddess?
