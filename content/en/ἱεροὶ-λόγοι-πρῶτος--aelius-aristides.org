would not tell all /as many as are the trials of stout-hearted Odysseus./

But taking some one deed of his, I think, she recounts it to Telemachus and
Menelaus; and I would not tell all the contests of the Savior, of which I have
had the benefit up to this day. And here I will no longer add Homer's, /not
even if I had ten tongues, and ten mouths;/ for that at least is a small
thing. But not even if I should surpass all power and voice and mind among men
would I ever come even near them. But also as many of my friends as ever asked
or urged me to speak and write about them, I never obeyed any of them, fleeing
the impossible. For it seemed to me much the same as if, having gone through
the whole sea under water, I were then forced to give an account of how many
waves in all I had met and of what sort of sea I had experience at each of
them and what the saving thing was. For each of our days, and likewise of the
nights, has a narrative, if someone being present either wished to write down
the things that befell, or to recount the providence of the god, of which some
he showed being present in the open, and some by the procession of dreams, at
least as much as it was possible even to get sleep; and that was rare because
of the triple waves about the body. Thinking of these things, then, I had
resolved to offer myself in truth as to a physician, to the god, in silence,
to do whatever he wishes. But now I wish to make clear to you how the matter
of the abdomen stood. And I will reckon each thing by day. For it was the
month Poseideon, you know of what sort of winter; and the stomach was ailing
by nights and there were extraordinary sleeplessnesses, so that it was not
possible even to digest the slightest things. And the cause not least was the
continuity of the storms, which not even any tile was said to have borne;
since even the sweat stood still this whole time, except as much as when
bathing. And on the twelfth of the month the god prescribes abstinence from
bathing, and on the next day the same thing, and on the day after that. These
three days in a row I passed wholly without sweat night and day, so that there
was not even need to change a tunic, and not yet

So much then the things from the abdomen. And like to that about the abdomen
there happened also that of the tumor many years before. For the god foretold
from long ago that I must guard against dropsy, and among other things he gave
antidotes and Egyptian sandals, which the priests are accustomed to use. And
indeed it seemed good to him to draw the flux down. And a tumor comes to be
from no visible beginning, at first such as might come to be even for some
other man, then it went forward to an extraordinary bulk, and the groin was
full and all things were swollen, and terrible pains followed and fever on
some days. Here the physicians let out all voices, some to cut, some to burn
with drugs, or that it must altogether become suppurated and be destroyed. But
the god set the opposite, to hold out and to nourish the bulk; and clearly

was still raised more and there was much helplessness. And of the friends some

These things then happened thus. And Zosimus was counted blessed by all and he
himself did not know what to become, having gratitude to the god for the

more time, if not through some virtue of his own. For having learned that one

season, forty stades, as to visit and to help whatever he was able; for he was
indeed also good at the medical art. And having fallen from the vehicle in
much snow and frost and having suffered many and terrible things, both going
and returning he falls a second time into a terrible and hard disease; so that
not even he himself dared at first to show any of the things that had happened
to him toward me. And I when I perceived it would not go to him, being angry
that he had not persuaded. For on the night that had passed such a
dream-vision had happened to me; the temple-warden Asclepiacus seemed to me to
say, and Zosimus must take himself up, while it is possible. I then upon this

he lived on was a grace of the god, as truly having guarded him for me, and he
died having been moved contrary to the things shown; and the things shown at
the beginning by the god, when I took hold of his head and supplicated, ended
in this. Then the ancient nurse, than whom nothing is dearer to me, Philoumene

raised her, having sent me out from Pergamum, having said beforehand that I
would also make the nurse lighter. And at the same time I take some letter
lying before the feet of Zeus Asclepius, making it a token; I found then each
