On Holy and Great Friday.
=50.811=
The contest of the fast has now been fulfilled for us, and it has ended at the
cross. For where ought the end of the victory to come, if not to end upon a
trophy? For the cross is a trophy: once planted, it always puts demons to
flight. Where now are the idols, and the slaughters of vain beasts? Where the
temples, and the fire of impiety? It has been quenched; all things have fallen
through one holy blood. And the cross is a manifold power, an invisible dart,
an immaterial medicine, a spring that relieves pain, a glory that is a
reproach. So that even if I speak ten thousand other things concerning Christ,
even if by recounting ten thousand wonders I strike the hearer with awe, I do
not glory in those as I do in the cross. I mean something of this sort: Jesus
came forth from a virgin. Great is the wonder, to overstep marriage and to
make a new beginning in nature. But if there had been no cross, the first
Virgin of paradise would not have been saved by the works. But now, in the
time of the cross, a woman is first saved, healing the ancient evil with new
gifts. For a dead man was raised in Galilee, but he died again. But I, having
been raised through the cross, can no longer fall toward death.
Jesus sailed the sea, God in a skiff. The wood gave a passing benefit. But I
have received an eternal wood, a good one, using which as a rudder =50.812= I
thrust away the spiritual waves of wickedness. Five thousand were fed under
one sign of the cross. Where then are the remnants of the food? How shall I,
who was not present, receive the remnants of those who were present?
For there were twelve baskets of leftover fragments: a measured grace. Christ
was crucified, and we are always fed. And being filled, we seek again, and
what remains is more. For the grace is without loss. Let the day that begets
light be praised, through which the remaining days were called to gladness.
How? Listen.
Today Adam was formed, on the sixth day; today he put on a divine form; today
a little world was constituted in the world; today man the helmsman took hold
of the fair rudders of the human race, ruling all living things; today he
received commandments of free will; today he fell from paradise; today he was
brought in again. O many-turning day! O much-mourning and unmourning! O you
who grieved in the morning, but gladdened at evening! Or rather, O how many
things you have healed! I confess to you with a downcast face, when I meet
with the ancient sufferings, hearing that Adam fell from his ancestral hearth,
a man who was a citizen of paradise, fed without tillage, luxuriating without
rain, needing nothing of sweats or mattock or toil =50.813= or labor for life,
rejoicing in ever-blooming trees, passing from flowers to flowers, going from
fruits to fruits, having need follow his desires, and not knowing, because of
the beauty of the things seen, to which he should first put his hands: I have
often wept at such great blessedness, seeing him fallen. For the suffering was
common, and it touches our race.
But when I met with the Gospels, and laid hold of this day (for that too was
the sixth, and this is), I lost the grief, and I put on white in my speech,
and I say to myself and to you: Pain, grief, groaning have fled away. The
ancient things have passed; behold, all things have become new. For just as
the sons of physicians heal the serpent-wounds, pouring out from them the
antidote drugs, and having prepared them, warring against the suffering with
the very occasions of the suffering: so the Savior used all the occasions of
evils toward good, making the bitter sweet, changing the bile into healing,
turning the sting of death against itself, transferring the harm of the wood
into salvation, taking a day that made the world cower, and giving in return a
day that gladdened the world. Do not be persuaded by me, but by your own eyes.
Look at the present church, and cease wrangling with us. It is a day of the
cross, and we all rejoice, and we fast from evils, and we are purified in all
things, the inward, the outward. This is the word of the feast, this is the
manner of the gladness. I speak small wonders of the power of the cross. Look
toward the circuit of the inhabited world: how many villages, how many cities,
how many places, how many nations, how many islands, how many rivers, how many
shores, how many races and tribes, and tongues of barbarians. All these fast
because of the cross, having crucified the passions by its power.
And night is ended for many, and the toil of the fast is not ended.
And now why have we all come together to hear something concerning the cross,
and we fill the church, and we shove one another, sweating, toiling, we who
before judges often claim the front seats, but before Jesus stand gladly?
Because Jesus also stood for us, that he might stop the streams of wickedness.
What then was done today? Let not the wonders of this day simply run past us.
There was light already, even the very dawn, and he is led in, his hands
bound, into the praetorium of Pilate. What hands? Hands that heal the blind,
that cure the lame. And fingers that formed eyelids were driven into bonds,
and the healer of nature was held so as not to fulfill the proper work of his
craft. These things have been paid back to the Lord. He was bound who binds
waters in clouds, who unbinds those fettered in might, who grants release to
captives. He was bound who unbound Lazarus from the bonds of death. He who is
escorted by ten thousand angels was led into the praetorium. He who has heaven
as his throne stood before Pilate. And the Creator endured being dragged by
the creatures, the Fashioner by the thing fashioned, the Maker by what was
made. What then after these things? What happens? And they themselves, it
says, did not enter the praetorium, that they might not be defiled, but that
they might eat the Passover. O the great lawlessness! They work a murder of
injustice, and they decline to enter the praetorium, guarding themselves lest
they be defiled, they who are already defiled, destroying the Sheep by a
sheep. The Judge of the whole inhabited world awaited a tribunal, then. He who
is witness of souls awaited witnesses: having fashioned, and being judged. And
men sat judging, but God stood, and standing he was silent, standing at the
doorways of men, the Master of the heavenly gates. Pilate asked, perhaps more
humane than the Jews. Why do I say perhaps? The things done will show the
truth: "What accusation do you bring against this man?" Who has it in him to
accuse God? For I am forced to utter the words of Pilate. For Jesus is silent,
not because the Word is at a loss for words, but lest by answering he undo
=50.814= the crown of the cross.
Did he who has no possessions desire possessions? Houses of others, he who
never had where to lay his head? Money, he who stripped even his own disciples
down to the belt? Not having a donkey, he acquired another's, that he might
bless your children. Speak a pretext, invent one, slay him justly. The Jews
answered him: "If this man were not an evildoer, we would not have delivered
him to you." A great demonstration of the facts, an undefined sentence. Name
the wickedness, and do not lead the hearer around. Pilate says to them: "Take
him yourselves, and judge him according to your law." A well-disposed judge
laid the burden upon the head of the Jews: You take him; you share the crime.
The Jews say to him: "It is not lawful for us to put anyone to death." How
then did you put Isaiah to death, how Zechariah, how each of the prophets? But
it is not yours to put to death, not because you do not wish it, but because
you cannot. For they had already been stripped of such authority by the
Romans.
The law then has already been dissolved, idle in deeds, lying in ink. And the
bound Jesus dissolved it. But Pilate: "I find no cause in him." Not you alone,
O Pilate, but not the Jews either, nor the blind, nor the dead, nor the sun,
nor the moon, nor the world, nor all the righteous prophets and witnesses, nor
the prophet who says: "Who did no sin, nor was deceit found in his mouth." All
run together with Pilate, bringing a just vote. The Jews alone fight, judging
by a shout, and striving to overshadow the truth with uproar, and confirming
the word of Isaiah: "I waited for it to make a grape-cluster, but it made
thorns, and not righteousness, but a cry."
While these things were being done, and Pilate could neither speak nor hear
because of the mixed confusion, and a sedition was being rehearsed, his wife
sent to him, a good helper, holding back a husband who was rushing on: "Have
nothing to do with that just man," saying; if you can, save him; if you
cannot, save yourself; all but saying the saying of David, "Do not destroy
your soul together with the ungodly, and your life with men of blood." "Have
nothing to do with that just man; for I have suffered many things in a dream
because of him." Another Joseph, seeing the truth through dreams, bearing
contrary witness to the shouts of the Jews. For it was fitting that they be
defeated by women. Rahab the harlot defeated them, the woman with the issue of
blood defeated them, the Canaanite woman defeated them. And now again a woman
against them. They are hard pressed. The Jews answered him: "We have a law,
and according to our law he ought to die."
What law? Confirmed by what words? Perhaps by those read today? "He was led as
a sheep to slaughter; and as a lamb before its shearer is voiceless, so he
does not open his mouth; from the lawlessness of my people he was led to
death." These syllables I find useful for them. Elsewhere I find Jesus nowhere
justly slaughtered. Pilate went into the praetorium, yielding the wrath of the
Jews, striving to quench an unquenchable flame. He went in, and he came out.
He kindled the fire all the more. For he came out having Jesus crowned, an
unwished wonder for the Jews, showing their enemy already crowned, already
victorious, already clothed in royal form. For what was done was for insult,
but it was a riddle of the kingdom according to nature. But when they saw him,
whom they had often seen, whom they never saw,
they always catch fire looking at him, being consumed by their own fire
(for they will wish they had been burned with fire =50.815=), they lifted
their Sodomitic cry, they lifted it
and were lifted: "Away, away, crucify him." "Shall I crucify your king?" "We
have no king but Caesar," they say. A denial without persecution, godlessness
after the evils of Egypt: "These are your gods, Israel, who brought you up
from the land of Egypt." You have no king but Caesar? Who then led you in the
wilderness? Or who fed you? Whom does Moses cry upon, saying: "The Lord is
king forever, and unto the age, and still"? Therefore, since you have denied
your own king, remain henceforth without a king, dragging an eternal yoke of
slavery.
These things were done until an hour. But let us see also the remaining part
of the day. For it is all holy. They took him crowned. With what? With thorns,
the gifts of the Jews. "I waited for it to make a grape-cluster, but it made
thorns." He was slapped, he was struck, he received scourges. But be not at
all ashamed. Stand with Isaiah, and look at God with that man's eyelids. What
then does that man say? "Lord, who has believed our report? We saw him, and he
had no form, nor beauty. But his form was without honor, failing beside the
sons of men." He had no beauty, nor form, the craftsman of all beauty. For he
was mourning the evils of the Jews, a man, being in a blow, and knowing how to
bear infirmity. A man, not God. For it was a man, and not God, who was being
struck.
Who then is this much-toiling and much-pained one, struck by all? Might it be
that he also suffers justly what he suffers? This one bears our sins, and is
in pain for us. "I find no cause in him." But do not be ashamed of the reviled
goods. For having suffered so much, he remained impassible. He was wounded, he
was spat upon, he endured the most shameful things, and he remains the same,
honored, glorious, as one of those who have been struck, says Isaiah himself:
"And we reckoned him to be in toil, and in a blow, and in ill-treatment. But
he was wounded because of our sins, and was made infirm because of our
lawlessness." Therefore both he and we lie as wounded men. "By his bruise we
were healed."
A dead man, physician of the dead. A wounded man, the antidote of much-toiling
men. But how long do you lead our leisure around, O man? Speak more clearly
what is sought. "He was led as a sheep to slaughter, and as a lamb voiceless
before the shearer." A good sheep delivered to evil butchers. If you
slaughter, O Jews, do not shear. If you shear, spare a sheep that bears good
fruit. And two other evildoers were being led with him. For the word has come
around for us to the eleventh hour of the day, that no one may despair of his
own life. And I wished to pass over the history, because it has often =50.816=
been spoken, but I see a robber always forcing me. And it is no great thing.
For he forced the doors of paradise, turning his craft toward salvation. A
lamb stood upon the cross, and two wolves. But the one remained upon the earth
in his mind. The other was already being changed, saying, "Remember me when
you come in your kingdom." O the power of Jesus! The robber already a prophet,
preaching from a cross: "Remember me, Lord, when you come in your kingdom."
For what of a king do you see, O robber? A slap, spitting, nails and wood, and
the mockeries of the Jews, and already the spear of the soldiers being bared.
"But I do not see the things that appear," he says. "I see angels standing
around, the sun fleeing, the veil being torn, the earth trembling, the dead
already planning to flee. Jesus receiving all, who also gave the same denarius
to the workers who came about the eleventh hour." "Amen I say to you" (receive
the amen you too, O robber, who today are a robber), "today you shall be with
me in paradise. I who cast you out, I bring you in, I who shut the doors of
paradise, who walled it with a fiery sword. For if I do not bring you in, the
doors remain shut. Come, O robber, who robbed the devil, and were crowned
against him. Seeing a man, you worshipped God, throwing away the old weapons,
and taking up those of faith."
And these things being so, and all things being made holy, from the aether the
sun, from plants the wood, from animals the gall, from woven things the
untearable tunic, from the sea the purple robe, from metals and iron the spear
and nails, and twin springs of blood and water poured out, the Savior was
doing his own work: "Father, forgive them the sin." Forgiveness for whom? For
Greeks, for Jews, for strangers, for barbarians, for all simply. For he spoke
once, and the work is always accomplished. For he did not say "Forgive" only
over the enemies. He says it each year, and he always says it, and he who
wishes receives.
They all came together to the praetorium of Pilate. Saying what? "We know that
that deceiver said while still living: After three days I rise." Do you know
it at all? Do you remember at all that he has been raised? Secure the tomb.
For you are securing it for me. Guard a dead man, lest he flee. "You have a
guard," he says, "go. Secure it as you know how. Shut it as you know how,
prepare as you know how, guard as you know how. If you did not keep watch, you
leave me a word of pretext."
But now I hand him over to you yourselves, him who while he lived was held by
you, but when he was put to death escaped you all. Let us remain then sober,
that we may see the deep sleep of the Jews, and keep the feast together with
the heavenly armies, in Christ Jesus our Lord, to whom be the glory and the
power, now and always and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
