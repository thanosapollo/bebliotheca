* On the Three Children

A discourse on the three children, and on the Babylonian furnace.

αʹ. Truly new and very great is the theatre of piety which the chorus of the
three children established, having contested the noble contest in Babylon, yet
assembling the wonder of the martyrdom throughout the whole inhabited world.
For the glory of the saints is not circumscribed by place, nor is the memory
of the righteous bounded by times; For the righteous shall be in everlasting
remembrance. Hence even if the martyrdom was perfected in the most ancient
times, yet unto every age the generalship of the endurance is sung. We know
the deeds through the readings, we know the history through the memory, we
know as in an image by the word both the lawlessness of the tyrant, and the
confession of the saints =56.594=, and the furnace burning with fire, yet
shining back against the wrath of the tyrant, and displaying the confession of
the martyrs unquenchable even at the threat of the fire; or rather, what is
there to hinder us, taking it up from the beginning, from telling the contests
of those who are truly God-loving and thrice-blessed? Nebuchadnezzar the king,
or rather the tyrant (for this is a name proper to the lawless man), held sway
over Babylonia; and he was barbarian in soul, and savage in manner. This man,
drunk with much injustice and wealth and impiety, was ignorant of his own
nature, and not even supposing himself a man, expected to be worshipped as
God. And there nourished for him the overweening boastfulness both his
companion madness, =56.595= and the long-suffering of God, bearing the ungodly
with forbearance, and permitting all things to come to pass for the training
of those who are pious. And the lawless man made a golden image, that is a
golden statue, and he compelled those who had come into being according to the
image of God to worship the image which he made. And using much magnificence,
he gives it a height of sixty cubits, and a breadth of six cubits; and he
constructs the statue well-fitted and well-proportioned, so that either by the
size, or by the beauty of the composition, the falsehood might prevail, having
done violence to the truth. So then the art gleamed, the gold shone, the
herald cried, the tyrant threatened, the furnace was kindled, and in addition
to all these the so-called instruments of music terribly drove the mindless
into atheism as in a bacchic frenzy, and altogether the appearance of the
things seen stole the thought of all who saw. But the command of impiety was
not about to be stronger even than the saints; and while all, so to speak,
were being swept down as by some torrent of the violent stream of deceit into
the precipice of idolatry, these three and noble youths, having faithfully
braced themselves upon piety as upon a rock, were not swept away [ἴσ. they
were swept] by the stream of injustice. For it was fitting for them to say: If
it had not been that the Lord was among us, then they would have swallowed us
alive. When their wrath was kindled against us, then the water would have
drowned us. Our soul passed through a torrent; then our soul passed through
the water that cannot be withstood. But they were neither drowned by the
stream, nor caught in the snares, but having run well toward piety, and using
the high wing of faith they were being saved, according to the proverb-writer,
As a gazelle from snares, and as a bird from a snare. And all but seeing the
nets of the devil spread out against all humanity, they repeated the pious
voice according to the hymn-writer, saying: Sinners shall fall in their own
net. And the three captives did not look to their own smallness, even though
driven by so many, but they knew exactly that even the smallest spark of piety
is sufficient to burn up and destroy all impiety. For this reason, although
they were three, to one another


they were being strengthened and bound together. For they knew that Narrow and
afflicted is the way that leads to life, and few are they who find it. They
remembered the patriarch Abraham, that being alone in all the earth a
worshipper of God, he did not follow the multitude of the ungodly, but a law
of piety was to him, and a prize of righteousness; hence reasonably from him
as from some good root these fruits of piety sprang. For from him also
patriarchs, and the lawgiver Moses, and the prophets, and all the theologians;
and from him first of all also that saving and immortal flower of
righteousness, the incarnation of the Savior; from that root also these three
children did not deny the nobility; they remembered also Lot sojourning in
Sodom, yet far removed in manners; they took into mind also Joseph, alone in
Egypt laying claim to chastity, and being a school of piety. Hence these also,
being caught among so many myriads, seeing the many, reasoned that Broad and
spacious is the way that leads to destruction, and many are they who find it;
but looking again toward themselves and the furnace, they remembered also
Wisdom saying somewhere thus, that As gold is tested in a furnace, so God
chooses hearts. For this reason neither did a trumpet sounding the warlike
strain frighten them, nor did a lyre charming the ears dissolve the tones of
piety, nor indeed did all the remaining symphony of the music dissolve the
noble and harmonious symphony of piety, but they set against the noble
symphony the noble confession. But that evil-minded and impious tyrant, when
those about Ananias were reported as disobeying the =56.596= command of
lawlessness, having his soul pressed with diabolic pride, and having taken on,
so to speak, even the very face of the ruler of wickedness, having summoned
them says: Is it true, Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, that you do not serve
my gods, and you do not worship the golden image which I have set up? He
supposed piety to be a pretense, and he asks whether those who profess piety
truly dare to oppose royal commands; but he was about to be taught by the
experience itself, that the men of God not only spit upon the threats of the
tyrant, but will even trample the peak of the fire itself by the power of
piety. Now then, whenever you hear the sound of the trumpet, and of pipe and
of kithara, of sambuca and of psaltery, and of symphony, and of every kind of
music, falling down worship the golden image which I have set up.

βʹ. Well did he interpret the worship of the demons, Falling down, worship. It
is impossible that those who worship the demons should not fall into
destruction, and fall away from the truth. But if you do not worship, he says,
in that very hour you shall be cast into the furnace of the burning fire.
Certainly if it is a furnace, it is clear that there is fire; and if there is
fire, it is clear also that it is burning; but he magnifies and widens the
threat, so that he may bend the resolve of piety; In that very hour you shall
be cast into the furnace of the burning fire. And the things of the
boastfulness are bearable up to this point; but see what he adds after these
things: And who is the god who shall deliver you out of my hands? This is
another Pharaoh; for that one also says to Moses: Who is the god whose voice I
shall hear? I do not know, says the impious, the Lord, and I will not send the
people away. O the much boastfulness of men! O the great long-suffering of
God! A man utters, and God endures; the clay speaks, and the Fashioner is
long-suffering; a fleshly tongue utters, and the Master of the bodiless angels
is moved with compassion. Who makes his angels spirits, and his ministers a
flame of fire. It is timely on this to remember Isaiah saying, Why is earth
and ashes boastful? Do you wish to marvel at the long-suffering of God?
Consider the outrage that has come upon you; how it has been thought to be
unbearable to you. Someone is often outraged by a household slave, and
immediately


putting forward the outrage of his freedom, he demands penalty for the daring,
punishing the one who outrages with inexorable death; again someone is
outraged by a cheap and private person, himself being examined in the polities
of the world; and immediately bitten by the outrage he hastens to punishment,
not looking to the common nature and the equal honor of all, but looking to
the dignity that is laid about him, which if it is stripped away one may see
the single equal honor of the race, that we were all formed from earth, and we
are dissolved into earth; and one is the way into life, and the going-out
equal. From clay, he says, you have been fashioned, as I also. But the clay
demands such a superiority from those of equal honor; but God, surpassing all,
both by nature and by law, and being as much higher as one must think the
maker higher than the things made, being blasphemed and set at nought by the
ungrateful, is not provoked; for he is impassible; but a little later he
punishes those who persist in the folly, because he is a judge of truth and an
inexorable judge. So that not all who sin may be destroyed at once, he
postpones the punishment, and offers forbearance, so that he may draw them
toward repentance. But let us return to the matter at hand. A man wearing
flesh dared to say: Who is the god who shall deliver you out of my hands? But
the thrice-blessed children, hearing these things, did not rise up at the
blasphemy; for they too were companions of the long-suffering of God; but
taking up a voice of faith against the words of unbelief they answered back to
the tyrant, dissolving the lawlessness by law, and overcoming the threat of
injustice by the freedom of righteousness, saying, Be it known to you, O king,
that we do not serve your gods, and we do not =56.597= worship the golden
image which you have set up. Cease from the mindlessness, O man; be ashamed of
the insulting worship of the image. For if you set up the image, how do you
worship the things which you made? Who ought to be craftsmen of whom? Men of
God, or God of men? For if they are truly gods, the dignity of craftsmanship
fits them; but as we have already said many times, if art were not present to
men, the Greeks would not have had gods; rather let us also attend to this,
that if there were any perception in the idols, it would have been fitting for
them to worship the men who work them. For the laws of nature are that the
thing made should worship the one who made it, not that the craftsman should
bow to his own craftsmanship. For this reason we who are companions of piety,
following the divine law, We do not serve your gods, and we do not worship the
golden image which you have set up; but there is the God in heaven, who shall
deliver us out of your hands. Then, so that they may not seem either to be
tempting God, or to be making bold against the fire in hope of being
delivered, they immediately add: And if not, that is, even if he does not
deliver, but permits our bodies to be burned up by the fire, not even so do we
withdraw from piety; for we are not godly for a wage, but we confess the truth
with a good mind. And when he heard this profession of the faith, the tyrant
is the more inflamed, and he commands the furnace to be kindled sevenfold; for
the most proven silver had to be purified sevenfold; For the oracles of the
Lord are pure oracles, silver tried in the fire, proven to the earth, and
purified sevenfold; wherefore the furnace was kindled sevenfold, so that the
saints might be purified sevenfold. And that the men of God have been called
silver, remember Wisdom saying: The tongue of a righteous man is silver tried
in the fire; and hear the prophet Jeremiah, saying concerning those who are
rejected from piety: Call them rejected silver, because God has rejected them.
And if the imperfect are unproven silver, then those who have been perfected
are clearly most proven silver; for as much as the furnace was kindled, so
much was the desire of martyrdom made bright. For these three holy children,
having entered with faith, were trampling the flame, breathing a thin and dewy
air in the very midst of the peaks of the fire. For he who makes and
transforms all things


blunted the heat of the fire, and cut off its burning energy, that hymnody
being made true by the wonder: The voice of the Lord cutting through a flame
of fire. The fire had been put to sleep, remaining quiet within, but the holy
children were dancing, all but enjoying that promise which God through the
prophet Isaiah promises to every soul full of piety and faith, that Even if
you pass, he says, through fire, I am with you, and a flame shall not burn
you. And one could see the promise going forward through works. For the fire
did not touch the members of the saints; it did not burn eyes, those that look
rightly toward piety, and through the good order of the things seen have
recognized the world-maker; it did not wrong ears filled with the divine law;
it did not come near the mouth, nor did it touch the lips, being put to shame
by their hymn-singing and hymn-writing tongues. Each member of the saints had
its own safeguard; the hands the stretching-out of the prayers, and the
distribution of almsgiving; the breasts the power of piety dwelling in them;
belly and the lower members the exercise of chastity; the feet the course of
virtue. And why must one linger saying the things one by one? For the fire did
not even dare against the hairs, because they had rather been covered with a
tiara of piety; and so it spared even the clothing because of the seemliness
of the saints. What then? Upon these things God commands the fire to lay hold
of the Chaldeans, and so that they may not, thinking it magic that the energy
of the fire was being dimmed, blunt the glory of the martyrs, and slander the
wonder of the truth, for this reason those within remained quiet, but the fire
outside was feeding on the Chaldeans, so that it might securely persuade those
who saw, that upon the saints it did not fail to know its own nature, but
honored the piety; just as the lions upon the pit did Daniel. Thus from this
they truly established an angelic chorus, and the thrice-blessed children were
hymning God, and they gathered all creation, both the supercosmic, =56.598=
and that contemplated in the eyes, into one choir of hymnody.

γʹ. And it is necessary not to leave even that unexamined, for what reason
they did not make known all creation alike, but set forth the craftsmanship
part by part. It would have sufficed then to say, as far as truth is
concerned: Bless God, all the works of the Lord; but since this great theatre
of piety was being assembled in the country of the ungodly, the Babylonians
had to be taught what the things made are, and who is the maker of all things.
And they begin from angels, and they end at men. Angels were thought to be
gods; and among the Greeks it is fabled that These, they say, whom we worship
as gods, are angels of the great God. For so that the mindless may be taught
that the angels are not of those who are worshipped, but of those who worship,
for this reason they cry: Bless the Lord, you angels of the Lord. Sun and moon
and all the chorus of the stars were worshipped. For this reason they take
these also into the worship of the hymns. Bless, he says, sun and moon; bless
the Lord, you stars of heaven. Then after these things, Every shower and dew
the Lord. What then the, Every shower and dew, and all the winds, wishes, it
would be good to consider. There come to be often droughts and untimely
violences of winds; and the inventors of the lie, and those who rejoice in
vanity, always love to ascribe all things to some matter and to a vice of
disorder, not knowing that nothing is without a master, nor has come into
being in vain, but that God dispenses all these things, both toward the
training of men and toward the fleeing of impiety. For the good order of the
things made is accustomed to proclaim the Craftsman, but the disorder demands
the worship of the creatures. For if the shower, or the winds, were something
divine and venerable, they would not be disorderly; for the divine does not
admit disorder. For this reason they say: Every shower, and dew, and all the
winds the Lord. Showers also were worshipped, and winds, the ones as
nourishers, the others as husbandmen of the fruits of the earth; and the


earth was worshipped, and its fruits were allotted to various demons; saying
the vine to be a gift of Dionysus, and the olive of Athena; and other fruits
to another. And this the same word of the truth, making it secure, says, Bless
the Lord, all things that grow in the earth. For he himself is Craftsman and
Master of all things that have grown and that grow. Then after this, mountains
and hills. Are then the mountains and the hills outside? By no means; but
since on the hills the unclean things of the demons had been constructed, and
there the statues were worshipped, for this reason he says: Bless the Lord,
mountains and hills. Yet having remembered the hills, he remembers also the
springs and rivers and sea; for they too were made into gods; and they called
the springs Nymphs, the sea Poseidon, certain Sirens and Nereids; and that
rivers also were worshipped, the things in Egypt unto this day bear witness;
they sacrificed to the rising of the Nile, not marveling at God himself for
the nature, but serving the water itself as a god. For this reason they call
rivers and springs and sea also into the hymnody. Then the birds of heaven and
the cattle; for neither are these outside of deification; for an eagle also was
worshipped, and a hawk; and the Egyptians have honored even the wild beasts
and the cattle with the title of the gods; and so far did the error prevail,
that they named the cities also from the animals that were worshipped; and it
is said among them, Of Dogs and of Sheep and of Wolves and of Lions. But after
all these things the human race is brought forward at last. Bless the Lord, he
says, you sons of men. The human race is ranked last, not according to worth,
but because of the craftsmanship. Bless the Lord, O Israel. What was the
chosen race of the men upon the earth, this then he brings forward, and at
that time he sets forth many differences in the nation, and he brings forward
the priests of the Lord unto the conviction of the priests of the falsely
named gods. Then he says: Ser=56.599=vants of the Lord. Then, so that those
who have gone before may not seem to be alien to this chorus, they number them
with the living before God, saying: Bless the Lord, spirits and souls of the
righteous; then, Holy and humble of heart, the Lord. Why does he remember the
holy and the humble? So that he may show that God resists the proud, but gives
grace to the humble; he was burning up the boastful outside the furnace, he
preserved the holy and humble in the midst of the fire. Wherefore the fire
also danced together with the saints; for after all things it too was
commanded to hymn the Craftsman; Bless the Lord, fire and heat; so that the
Babylonian magi, among whom the fire is honored, may be taught that this is
not of those who are worshipped, but of those who worship. Yet having come to
the last let us bring the discourse to a close. Bless the Lord, he says,
Ananias, Azarias, Misael. What need was there, when so many ranks had been
numbered, for them to be added by the last name? For were they not among the
sons of Israel when they blessed? Were they not numbered with the servants of
the Lord, when they were saying, Bless the Lord, you servants of the Lord? or
when, Holy and humble of heart, not numbered among the holy? What then does
the ad=56.600=dition wish, Bless the Lord, Ananias, Azarias, Misael? But since
the wonder was beyond nature, since it was contrary to human nature (for
having entered the furnace in the body they trampled it), so that none of
those who have been led astray might dare, because of the strangeness of the
wonder, to address them as gods, and to worship those who had prevailed over
the fire more justly than the fire, so that those who look may
suffer no such thing, for this reason they make known their own servitude, and
they confess the worship, saying, Bless the Lord, Ananias, Azarias, Misael.
And it is said that God for this reason did not permit Daniel to be taken into
this martyrdom. For since he interpreted the dream of the king, and was
worshipped as a god by the king himself, and was called Belteshazzar by the
name of the god among the Persians, so that it might not be thought that
because of the name of


Belteshazzar that had been brought upon him the energy of the fire had ceased,
God arranges that Daniel not be brought forward into the midst, so that the
wonder of piety might be unmixed. And these things thus far; and may it come
to pass that we, by the prayers and intercessions of those who bore witness,
having taken up the same zeal, may be counted worthy of the same praises, and
may obtain the same kingdom, by the grace and loving-kindness of our Lord
Jesus Christ, to whom be the glory and the power unto the ages of ages. Amen.
