HISTORICAL ENCOMIUM OF ALEXANDER THE MONK, HISTORIOGRAPHER, CONCERNING THE
FINDING OF THE PRECIOUS AND LIFE-GIVING CROSS.

=4077= At that time Macarius, the bishop of Aelia, true to his name, had
succeeded Hermon. And Constantine the Great, having become sole ruler,
transferred all his care to the divine things. Rebuilding the churches, since
he was munificently wealthy from the public treasury, both in moneys and
expenditures, and in treasures of every kind. And first he wrote a law that
the temples of the idols be given over to those dedicated to Christ, and that
those still practicing idolatry be punished with capital penalties. Second he
wrote a law, that only Christians who are orthodox serve in the army, and that
these govern nations and camps. Third he wrote a law, that the two Paschal
weeks be idle, one before and one after. And thereafter there was deep peace,
and joy in all the inhabited world, all the nations daily running to the
faith, and being baptized, and with their own hands smashing the ancestral
gods.

But these things were not bearable to the accursed demon, but again he plotted
in himself how he might cut off so great a joy of men. And to persuade men to
deify the creation he was no longer able; for his deceit had been detected.
But he tried to persuade the unstable to class the Creator with the creatures.
And again there was no small disturbance in the Churches of God, a certain
Arius of Alexandria going up and down, and troubling the inhabited world. And
a few bishops in number were carried away with him, of whom the first was
Eusebius of Nicomedia, and Eusebius the son of Pamphilus, and others with
them. Constantine the Great, seeing the great contention, was grieved in soul;
and first he tried through hortatory letters to quench the strife of both
parties. But when he saw the evil becoming incurable, he commanded an
ecumenical synod to take place in the city of the Nicaeans, and bishops came
together, three hundred and eighteen in number. Of whom the greater part were
confessors, bearing the marks of Jesus in the body. And with them also was the
all-praised Constantine, and when the matter was moved, they brought forth a
written exposition of the faith, the one now proclaimed throughout every
Church of the orthodox. And those around Arius and Eusebius of Nicomedia who
persisted in the evil doctrine they denounced, and they were exiled, and
others were ordained in their place. And the emperor, having honored the
bishops munificently, and having embraced them, dismissed them in peace to
their own dioceses, rejoicing at the concord of the Churches.

And he commanded Macarius, the bishop of Aelia, who was present at the synod
and fighting for the apostolic doctrines, to search out the life-giving Cross,
and the tomb that received God, and all the holy places, and he likewise
exhorted the other bishops =4080= to ask, if each saw anything for the
completion of his own Church. And it was the nineteenth year of his reign when
the synod at Nicaea took place.

After these things the emperor sent his own mother Helen, the praiseworthy and
God-loving, to Jerusalem, with letters and abundance of money, to Macarius,
true to his name, bishop of Aelia, for the search of the life-giving wood of
the glorious Cross and the building of the holy places; the empress herself
having asked this, saying she had seen a certain divine vision commanding her
to reach Jerusalem, and to bring to light the holy places buried by the winds,
and become unseen for so many years.

And the bishop, learning that the empress had arrived, gathering the bishops
of the province, met her with due honor. And immediately she commanded the
bishops to make the search for the longed-for wood. And when all were at a
loss concerning the place, and one telling one suspicion and another another,
the bishop of the city exhorted all to keep quiet, and to offer more earnest
prayer to God for this.

And when this was done, immediately the place was shown from God to the
bishop, in which the temple and the statue of the unclean demon had been set
up.

Then the empress, using imperial authority, gathering a great multitude of
craftsmen and laborers, commanded that the abominable building be overthrown
from the foundations, and the earth be thrown far away. And when this was
done, the divine tomb appeared, and the place of the Skull, and not far off
three crosses, separated; and searching carefully they found also the nails.

From there then perplexity and grief seized the empress, seeking which then
might be the Master's Cross. But the bishop resolved the discernment through
faith. For to a woman of the notable people who was ill, and given up by all,
and breathing her last, bringing each of the crosses, he found the one sought;
for as soon as the shadow of the saving Cross drew near to the sick woman, at
once she who was breathless and motionless by divine power immediately sprang
up, crying with a great voice and glorifying God.

And the empress Helen, crying aloud and glorifying God, with great joy and
fear taking up the life-giving Cross, brought back a certain part together
with the nails to her son. And for the remainder, having made a silver casket,
she delivered it to the bishop of the city, for a memorial to all generations,
and having decreed that a church be made at the life-giving tomb and at holy
Golgotha and in Bethlehem in the cave, where our Lord Jesus Christ underwent
the birth according to the flesh, and on the Mount of Olives, where the Lord,
having blessed the disciples, was taken up. And having done many other things
in Jerusalem, she returned to her son.

And he, having received her with joy, having laid away the =4081= portion of
the precious Cross in a golden chest, delivered it to the bishop for keeping,
commanding that they celebrate with yearly commemorations the manifestation of
the precious Cross. And of the nails, some he forged into his own helmet, and
some he mixed into the bridle of his horse, that what was spoken of the Lord
through the prophet might be fulfilled, saying: "In that day, that which is
upon the bridle of the horse shall be holy to the Lord Almighty."

And the emperor wrote to bishop Macarius to hasten the building, and he sent a
director of the work with abundance of money, charging him that the holy
places be built munificently, so that there should not be such beauty in all
the earth. And he wrote also to the governors of the province to assist the
work in every way from the public treasury, both in moneys and expenditures
and materials. And the emperor was keeping brightly the feast of the twentieth
year, giving thanks to God for all the good things which he had done in the
times of his reign. And the years from the coming of Christ until the finding
of the Cross are gathered together as three hundred and fifty-two years. And
from Adam until the coming of Christ, five thousand five hundred years. And
according to others five thousand four hundred seventy-five.

And in Aelia Maximonas succeeded Macarius, true to his name. And this man was
gentle and very distinguished. For in the persecution he endured many tortures
for God, and having lost the right eye he was released upon the confession of
the faith.

And at that time the blessed Helen the empress made an end of life, being
eighty years old, having charged her son much concerning piety toward Christ.

Let the history concerning the finding of the life-giving Cross be as far as
here. And let no one deceive himself, talking vainly of things unprofitable
and introducing myth-making through strange names into the subject. For no
other bishop of Jerusalem ever came to be besides those written before, nor
another emperor of the Romans, nor indeed was the precious Cross found in
another manner. For from Augustus the emperor, in whose time our Lord Jesus
Christ was born according to the flesh, until the pious emperor Constantine,
thirty-five emperors came to be. And likewise from James the apostle and first
of the bishops until the same reign, thirty-five bishops of Jerusalem came to
be.

And the venerable day of the dedication of the holy places, and of the
exaltation of the worshipful Cross, the Fathers with imperial command
appointed to take place each year on the fourteenth of the month of September,
which is the eighteenth day before the Kalends of October, to the glory of the
Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit.

And since by the grace of God we have arrived in the discourse at the notable
day of our feast, which is the manifestation of the life-giving Cross, come,
as far as =4084= it is possible, having greeted it a little, let us bring the
discourse to a close.

Rejoice then, precious Cross, rejoice, because on you our Lord Jesus Christ
was lifted up according to the economy, in whom are all the treasures of joy
and of grace. Rejoice, precious Cross; for through you unending joy is awarded
in heaven and on earth. Rejoice, precious Cross, because from above and from
the beginning the Creator painted your image in all creation. Rejoice,
glorious Cross, because all the powers in heaven equally send up most fearful
hymns to the Master for the salvation through you. Rejoice, glorious Cross;
for through you both in cities, and in islands, and in every nation Churches
of the orthodox have been founded. Rejoice, glorious Cross; for through you
every mouth of the faithful has been opened unto theology. Rejoice, blessed
Cross; for through you heavenly angels with men on earth hymn Christ. Rejoice,
blessed Cross; for through you on earth men likewise stand before God in the
heavens, glorifying the Maker with unspeakable joy. Rejoice, blessed Cross;
for through you Adam, freed from the curse, leaping and rejoicing has entered
into paradise, having received back the ancient dignity. Rejoice, holy Cross;
for through you every diabolic power has been cast down, and every demonic
energy has been dissolved. Rejoice, holy Cross; for through you the
thrice-holy and consubstantial Trinity is known in every place and believed
and glorified. Rejoice, holy Cross; for through you the inalienable goods have
been given to men.

And what shall we say and what shall we leave out? How shall we hymn the power
of the Master's Cross? O name of the Cross, in which the great things of the
mysteries of God are hidden, venerable to angels, and worshipful to men! For
what is more lovely than the name of the Cross, or sweeter to Christians? And
what more effective than this for wonder-working? Cross, most effective sign
of divine power. Cross, joy of angels and mourning of demons. Cross,
foundation of the Church. Cross, ever-appearing and all-appearing luminary of
the whole world. Cross, unshaken and unbreakable and unconquerable wall of all
the inhabited world. Cross, glory of priests, priestly and unassailed. Cross,
might and power of emperors, and victorious weapon against enemies. Cross,
most all-powerful panoply of armies, and undaunted before the adversaries.
Cross, guardian of cities, secure from plots and from plunder. Cross,
unceasing and unending gladness of peoples. Cross, unceasing and unstopping
total destruction of the devil's. Cross, eternal and extraordinary fall of
demons. Cross, sure and unashamed and undiminished hope of Christians. Cross,
most seemly healing of the sick, of souls and of bodies. Cross, unfailing
health of those in good condition. Cross, most calm harbor of those
storm-tossed. Cross, unshaken peace of those at war. Cross, boast of boasts of
the orthodox, and unceasing condemnation of heretics. =4085= Cross,
dissolution of idolatry and restoration of piety. Cross, teacher of virginity
and guardian of chastity. Cross, safety of the righteous and repentance of
sinners. Cross, ornament of monks and seemliness of those living honorably.
Cross, guardian of infants, and chastener of the young, and staff of support
of the old. Cross, inexhaustible treasure of the poor, and teacher of
sufficiency of the rich. Cross, way of those who have gone astray, and
unerring guide of those who are perishing. Cross, rest in daily toils, and
help in nightly fears. Cross, good order of nations, and deepest calm of all
the world.

And what shall we say, or what shall we leave out, or how shall we hymn you,
all-glorious Cross? Because you alone from all creation the King of glory
showed forth as an unconquered weapon against the devil, and against sin, and
against death. Wherefore blessed are we all who have been deemed worthy to see
the holy day of your manifestation. You are the preaching of the prophets; for
concerning you they daily cry out rejoicing, saying to God: "You have given a
sign to those who fear you, to flee from the face of the bow." You are the
boast of the apostles. For they cry without ceasing in the holy Church through
one of them, a sacred herald: "But far be it from me to boast, except in the
cross of our Lord Jesus Christ." You are also the crown of the martyrs; since
they too, having taken up the cross, followed the Lord. You are the boast and
the exultation of those dwelling in mountains and caves and the holes of the
earth for the name of the Lord; because they too have crucified the flesh with
the passions and the desires. You are refreshment and rest of those near and
those far. O the unspeakable grace of the Master's Cross! O the unspeakable
and incomprehensible mysteries in it!

And that I too may say something ecstatic with the sacred trumpet: "O the
depth of the riches and wisdom and knowledge of God, how unsearchable are his
judgments!" For by the tree of life having died, by the tree of condemnation
we were made alive. Because of the measureless grace of the Master who saved
us it is right for us to appoint this all-festive day "in the thick ones even
unto the horns of the altar." And those that are thick, as those skilled in
these things say, are the things soiled from neglect or old age, then from
care and zeal again arranged unto what is more precious, for the gladness and
delight of those who will feast at weddings in some other bright cheer. Let us
then be disposed as we have been commanded, and having put off the
unseemliness that has come upon our conscience from sloth, let us celebrate
this bright and all-venerable day of the memory of the life-giving Cross, with
almsgiving and faith, as the divine Scripture says, having taken up =4088=
boldness adorned with reverence, and having lifted upon our shoulders the
victorious weapon of the life-giving Cross, given to us from heaven for the
cessation of all wrath, let us cry as is customary an angelic hymn to God:
"Holy God, holy mighty, holy immortal, have mercy on us;" holy Trinity,
incomprehensible, unbounded, inseparable, consubstantial, and sharing the
throne, and co-eternal, and equally glorified, have mercy on us, and be
propitious to our sins for the sake of your holy name, and grant us to find
mercy and compassions in the day of your just recompense. For you are our God,
and to you, the Father, and to the Son, and to the Spirit belongs glory,
majesty, might and authority before every age, and now and unto all the ages
of ages. Amen.
