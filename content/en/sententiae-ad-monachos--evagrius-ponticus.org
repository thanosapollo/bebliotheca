* Sayings to Monks
:PROPERTIES:

To the monks in coenobia or brotherhoods. 1 Heirs of God, hear the words of
God, and fellow-heirs of Christ, receive the sayings of Christ, that you may
give them to the hearts of your children, and teach them the words of the
wise. 2 A good father disciplines his sons, but a wicked father will destroy
them. 3 Faith is the beginning of love, and the end of love is knowledge of
God. 4 The fear of the Lord will keep a soul, and good self-control will
strengthen it. 5 A man's endurance begets hope, and good hope will glorify
him. 6 He who brings his flesh into subjection will be passionless, but he who
nourishes it will suffer pain from it. 7 The spirit of fornication is in the
bodies of the incontinent, but the spirit of chastity is in the souls of the
self-controlled. 8 Withdrawal in love purifies the heart, but withdrawal with
hatred troubles it. 9 Better a thousandth in love than one alone with hatred
in innermost caves. 10 He who binds up resentment in his soul is like one who
hides fire in straw. 11 Do not give many foods to your body, and you will not
see evil fantasies in sleep. For as a flame consumes a thicket, so hunger
quenches shameful fantasies. 12 An irascible man will be terrified, but the
meek will be without fear. 13 A violent wind drives away clouds, and
resentment drives the mind from knowledge. 14 He who prays for enemies will be
without resentment, and he who spares his tongue will not grieve his neighbor.
15 If your brother provokes you, bring him into your house, and do not
hesitate to go in to him, but eat your morsel with him. For doing this you
will deliver your soul, and there will be no stumbling block for you at the
time of prayer. 16 As love rejoices in poverty, so hatred delights in wealth.
17 A rich man will not attain knowledge, and a camel will not enter through
the eye of a needle, but none of these things will be impossible with the
Lord. 18 He who loves silver will not see knowledge, and he who gathers it
will be darkened. 19 The Lord will lodge in the tents of the humble, but in
the houses of the proud curses will be multiplied. 20 He who transgresses
God's law dishonors God, but he who keeps it glorifies the one who made him.
21 If you emulate Christ, you will become blessed; your soul will die his
death, and it will not draw wickedness from its flesh, but your departure will
be as the departure of a star, and your resurrection will shine forth like the
sun. 22 Woe to the lawless man on the day of death, and the unjust will perish
in an evil time. For as a raven flies from its nest, so an unclean soul from
its own body. 23 Angels guide the souls of the righteous, but demons will take
the souls of the wicked.

24 Where wickedness enters, there also is ignorance, but the hearts of the
holy will be filled with knowledge. 25 A merciless monk will be in want, but
he who feeds the poor will inherit treasures. 26 Better poverty with knowledge
than wealth with ignorance. 27 The ornament of the head is a crown, but the
ornament of the heart is knowledge of God. 28 Acquire knowledge and not
silver, and wisdom above much wealth. 29 The righteous will inherit the Lord,
and the holy will be nourished by him. 30 He who has mercy on the poor
destroys anger, and he who feeds them will be filled with good things. 31
Wisdom will rest in a meek heart, and the throne of passionlessness is a
practical soul. 32 Craftsmen of wicked things will receive an evil wage, but
to craftsmen of good things a good wage will be given. 33 He who sets a snare
will be caught in it, and he who hides it will be taken by it. 34 Better a
meek worldly man than an irascible and wrathful monk. 35 Anger scatters
knowledge, but long-suffering gathers it. 36 As a strong south wind on the
sea, so is anger in a man's heart. 37 He who prays continually escapes
temptations, but thoughts trouble the heart of one who is negligent. 38 Let
wine not gladden you and let meat not delight you, lest you nourish the flesh
of your body, and shameful thoughts will not depart from you. 39 Do not say:
today is a feast and I will drink wine, and tomorrow is Pentecost and I will
eat meat, because there is no feast among monks, nor for a man to fill his
belly. 40 The Lord's Passover is a passing over from wickedness, and his
Pentecost is a resurrection of the soul. 41 The feast of God is amnesty of
evils, but mournings will seize the one who keeps resentment. 42 The Lord's
Pentecost is a resurrection of love, but he who hates his brother will fall a
terrible fall. 43 The feast of God is true knowledge, but he who attends to
false knowledge will die shamefully. 44 Better a fast with a pure heart than a
feast in uncleanness of soul. 45 He who destroys evil thoughts from his heart
is like one who dashes his infants against the rock. 46 A sleepy monk will
fall into evils, but he who keeps vigil will be as a sparrow. 47 Do not give
yourself in vigil to empty tales, and do not reject spiritual words, because
the Lord watches your soul and will not hold you guiltless from every evil. 48
Much sleep thickens the mind, but good vigil thins it. 49 Much sleep brings on
temptations, but he who keeps vigil will escape them. 50 As fire melts wax, so
good vigil melts wicked thoughts. 51 Better a man sleeping than a monk keeping
vigil over vain thoughts. 52 An angelic dream gladdens the heart, but a
demonic dream troubles it. 53 Repentance and humility have set a soul upright,
and almsgiving and meekness have established it. 54 Remember always your
departure and do not forget the eternal judgment, and there will be no
transgression in your soul. 55 If a spirit of acedia rises upon you, do not
leave your house and do not turn aside at the time. Again it is profitable.
For as if one should whiten silver, so your heart will be made bright.

56 A spirit of acedia drives away tears, but a spirit of grief crushes prayer.
57 Desiring money you will be anxious about many things, and clinging to them
you will mourn bitterly. 58 Let a scorpion not linger in your bosom, nor a
wicked thought in your heart. 59 Do not spare to kill the offspring of
serpents, and do not travail with the thoughts of their heart. 60 As fire
tests silver and gold, so temptations test a monk's heart. 61 Put pride away
from yourself and make vainglory far from you. For he who fails to obtain
glory will be grieved, but he who attains it will be proud. 62 Do not give
your heart to pride and do not say before the face of God: I am strong, lest
the Lord abandon your soul, and wicked demons humble it. For then the enemies
will terrify you through the air, and fearful nights will come upon you in
turn. 63 Knowledge guards a monk's way of life, but he who descends from
knowledge will fall among robbers. 64 From a spiritual rock a river flows, and
a practical soul will drink from it. 65 A pure soul is a vessel of election,
but the unclean will be filled with bitterness. 66 Without milk a child will
not be nourished, and without passionlessness a heart will not be exalted. 67
Passionlessness goes before love, and love before knowledge. 68 Wisdom is
added to knowledge, and prudence begets passionlessness. 69 The fear of the
Lord begets prudence, and faith in Christ grants the fear of God. 70 A flaming
arrow kindles the soul, but a practical man will quench it. 71 Knowledge turns
away from shouting and blasphemy, and wisdom flees deceitful words. 72 Honey
is pleasant and the honeycomb is sweet, but knowledge of God is sweeter than
both. 73 Hear, monk, the words of your father and do not make his admonitions
void. When he sends you, take him with you and travel with him in thought. For
in this way you will escape evil thoughts, and wicked demons will not prevail
against you. If he entrusts silver to you, you will not scatter it, and even
if you work it, you will restore it. 74 A wicked steward will oppress the
souls of the brothers, and one who keeps resentment will not have mercy on
them. 75 He who scatters the possessions of the monastery wrongs God, and he
who neglects them will not go unpunished. 76 An unjust steward will divide
badly, but the just will give according to worth. 77 He who speaks ill of his
brother will be destroyed, and he who neglects one who is sick will not see
light. 78 Better a worldly man serving a brother in sickness than an anchorite
who does not pity his neighbor. 79 A foolish monk will neglect the tools of
his craft, but the prudent will take care of them.

80 Do not say: today I will stay and tomorrow I will go out, because you have
not considered this with prudence. 81 A wandering monk will rehearse false
sayings, and he will deceive his own father. 82 He who adorns his garments and
fills his belly shepherds shameful thoughts and does not sit with the chaste.
83 If you enter a village, do not approach women and do not linger in words
with them. For as if one should swallow a hook, so your soul will be drawn. 84
A long-suffering monk will be loved, but he who provokes his brothers will be
hated. 85 The Lord loves a meek monk, but he will thrust the bold one away
from him. 86 A sluggish monk will murmur much, and a sleepy one will make his
headache a pretext. 87 If your brother is grieved, console him, and if he is
in pain, share the pain with him. For doing this you gladden his heart, and
you will store up a great treasure in heaven. 88 A monk who falls short of
keeping a father's words will blaspheme the gray hair of him who begot him and
will speak ill of the lives of his children, and the Lord will set him at
nought. 89 He who seeks a pretext separates from the brothers, and he will
accuse his own father. 90 Do not give ear to words against your father and do
not stir up a soul that dishonors him, lest the Lord be angry at your works
and blot out your name from the book of the living. 91 He who obeys his own
father loves himself, but he who contradicts him will fall into evils. 92
Blessed is the monk who keeps the commandments of the Lord, and holy is he who
keeps the words of his fathers. 93 A sluggish monk will suffer much loss, but
if he takes courage he will add even his habit. 94 He who guards his tongue
makes his ways straight, and he who keeps his heart will be filled with
knowledge. 95 A double-tongued monk troubles the brothers, but the faithful
one keeps stillness. 96 He who trusts in his own self-control will fall, but
he who humbles himself will be exalted. 97 Do not give yourself to the filling
of the belly, and do not be filled with sleep of the night. For in this way
you will become pure and the Spirit of the Lord will come upon you. 98 When
one sings psalms, anger is still, and when one is long-suffering he will be
unafraid. 99 From meekness knowledge is begotten, but from boldness ignorance.
100 As water increases a plant, so humbling of anger exalts the heart. 101
When one tracks after drinking-parties, the lamp will be quenched, and his
soul will see darkness. 102 Set your bread by the scale and drink your water
by measure, and the spirit of fornication will flee from you. 103 Give wine to
the old and offer foods to the sick, because they have worn out the flesh of
their youth. 104 Do not trip your brother and do not rejoice at his fall. For
the Lord knows your heart and will deliver you up on the day of death. 105 A
prudent monk will be passionless, but the foolish will drain out evils.

106 The Lord blinds an evil eye, but he will deliver the simple eye from
darkness. 107 As the morning star in heaven and as a palm tree in paradise, so
is a pure mind in a meek soul. 108 A wise man will search out the words of
God, but the unwise will mock them. 109 He who hates knowledge of God and
rejects his contemplation is like one who pierces his own heart with a spear.
110 Better knowledge of the Trinity than knowledge of the incorporeal beings,
and contemplation of it than the reasons of all the ages. 111 The gray hair of
elders is meekness, and their life is knowledge of truth. 112 A meek young man
bears many things, but who will bear a faint-hearted elder? I have seen a
wrathful elder lifted up on his throne, yet his young man rather had hope. 113
He who causes worldly people to stumble will not go unpunished, and he who
provokes them dishonors his name. 114 Fire will consume one who troubles the
church of the Lord, and the earth will swallow one who resists a priest. 115
He who loves the bee will eat its honeycomb, and he who gathers it will be
filled with spirit. 116 Honor the Lord and you will know the reasons of the
incorporeal beings, and serve him and he will show you the reasons of the
ages. 117 Without knowledge a heart will not be exalted, and a tree will not
blossom without drink. 118 The flesh of Christ is the practical virtues, and
he who eats them will become passionless. 119 The blood of Christ is
contemplation of the things that have come to be, and he who drinks it will be
made wise by it. 120 The breast of the Lord is knowledge of God, and he who
reclines upon it will be a theologian. 121 The gnostic and the practical man
met one another, and the Lord stood in the midst of both. 122 He who has
acquired love has acquired a treasure, and he has received grace from the
Lord. 123 Wisdom recognizes the doctrines of demons, and prudence traces out
their craft. 124 Do not reject the holy doctrines which your fathers set down,
and do not abandon the faith of your baptism, and do not cast away the
spiritual seal, that the Lord may be in your soul and shelter you on an evil
day. 125 The words of heretics are angels of death, and he who receives them
will destroy his own soul. 126 Now then, son, hear me, and do not approach the
doors of lawless men nor walk upon their snares, lest you be caught. And set
your soul apart from false knowledge. For I too have spoken with them many
times, and I have traced out their dark words, and I have found the poison of
asps in them. There is no prudence and there is no wisdom in their words. All
who receive them will perish, and those who love them will be filled with
evils. I have seen the fathers of their doctrines and I have encountered them
in the desert. For enemies of the Lord met me and demons contended with me in
words, and I did not see true light in their utterances. 127 A lying man will
fall away from God, and he who deceives his neighbor will fall into evils. 128
Better the paradise of God than a garden of vegetables, and the river of the
Lord than a great river that darkens the earth.

129 Heavenly water is more trustworthy than the water of Egyptian wise men
drawing from the earth. 130 As those who go up on the wheels are below, so
those who exalt their words have been humbled in them. 131 The wisdom of the
Lord exalts the heart, and his prudence purifies it. 132 The reasons of
providence are dark and the contemplations of judgment hard to understand, but
a practical man will come to know them. 133 He who purifies himself will see
intellectual natures, and a meek monk will come to know the reasons of the
incorporeal beings. 134 He who says the Holy Trinity is a creature blasphemes
God, and he who rejects his Christ will not know him. 135 Contemplations of
worlds widen the heart, and the reasons of providence and judgment exalt it.
136 Knowledge of the incorporeal beings lifts up the mind and presents it to
the Holy Trinity. 137 Remember the one who has given you in the Lord clear
proverbs, and do not forget my humble soul at the time of prayer.
