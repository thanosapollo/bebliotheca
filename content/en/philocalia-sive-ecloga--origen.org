26.1ν Concerning what the good things are and what the bad, and that these are
in things of choice and in an unchosen, and according to the teaching of
Christ, but not as Aristotle thinks. From the volume on the 4th psalm, on the:
"Many say: Who will show us the good things?" There being much seeking among
men concerning what the good things are and what the bad; and some saying that
the good things and the bad are unchosen, as it were declaring pleasure a
good, and pain a bad; and others shutting the good things and the bad in
things of choice only, and saying that the virtues and the actions according
to them alone are good things, and the vices and the workings according to
vice bad; and there being a third of those who mix, and saying that the good
things and the bad are at once in things of choice and in unchosen things;
reasonably the many of those who have believed, being dragged around by the
persuasions, being fond of learning unto the place concerning good things,
would say the: "Who will show us the good things?" That then the nature of the
good things is in things of choice, everyone whoever who accepts the place
concerning judgment would unhesitatingly confess; for he says that that is
good upon which someone will hear the: "Well done, good and faithful servant;
over few things you were faithful, over many I will set you; enter into the
joy of your lord"; and good also that which is brought forth from the good man
from his heart, as the savior says: "The good man from the good treasure of

tree, being of choice, is a good; as "love and peace and joy and
long-suffering, both kindness and goodness and faith and meekness and

other-doctrined both about goodness of God and about there not being a nature
being destroyed. 27.13n And again from the second volume of the things on the
Song. And attend also to these unto the place, that the sun being white and
bright seems to have the cause of blackening, not beside itself, but beside
the one being blackened as we have given back. And thus perhaps also the Lord
hardens the heart of Pharaoh, the cause of this being about him <the one> who
was making bitter the life of the Hebrews in the hard works, in the clay and
the brick-making, and in all the works, not those in mountains and hills, but
those in the plains. For having become some material one from his own vice,
and living according to flesh according to all things, happening to be a
friend of clay he wishes also the Hebrews to make clay, having the
leading-part not clean of clay; which, as clay is hardened by a sun, so was
hardened by the rays of God visiting Israel. And that such things are in the
things according to the place, and that it does not lie before the servant to
write a bare history, will be clear to the one who sees together that, when
the sons of Israel groaned, they groaned neither from the brick-making nor
from the clay nor from the straws, but from the works; "and their cry went up
toward God" not from clay, but again "from the works". Wherefore also God
heard their groanings; not hearing a groaning of those who cry toward him not
from works, but from clay and the earthly actions.
