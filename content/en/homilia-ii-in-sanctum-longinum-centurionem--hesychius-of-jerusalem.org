* Homily II on Saint Longinus the Centurion
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of Hesychius, presbyter of Jerusalem, an encomium on Saint Longinus the
centurion.

1 The victors of the contest, the citizens of heaven, the soldiers of Christ,
the heirs of the kingdom, the ambassadors of the world to the Lord, the
companions of the cherubim and physicians of the sick, the cleansers of lepers
and putters-to-flight of demons, the witnesses of the faith, the tramplers of
the idols, who through the body trampled the devil and counted tortures as
nothing, who muzzled wild beasts and blunted swords, who were sealed by Christ
and known by him, the fair flowers of the churches, the mighty ambassadors,
the stars of the inhabited world, the holy proto-martyrs John the Baptist,
Stephen, Longinus the centurion.

2 For when our Master "Jesus Christ and Savior was born in Bethlehem of
Judea," it was announced to king Herod that a king of Israel had been born.
And Herod, receiving the good news, for a little while storing it up in the
hope of the Magi, but afterward fighting in wrath, sent Longinus the
centurion, saying: "Go, learn accurately whether a king has been born, that I
also may come and worship him, because I was mocked by those Magi." And
Longinus, going away to Bethlehem of Judea, saw on the road a great light
shining around him, and he heard a voice saying to him: "Come, Longinus, my
beloved, and I will make you centurion of holy martyrs." And falling upon the
ground he worshipped the one who had given him the voice and said: "Let it be
to me according to your word, Lord, God," and he went away to Bethlehem of
Judea to worship the child, the Lord Jesus Christ. And he said to him: "Hail,
king of the world."

3 And returning to Herod he said to him: "Truly the one who has been born is
Son of God, king and redeemer of all the world." And Herod, angered, said to
him: "Do you bear witness concerning him under oath?" And the centurion
Longinus says, repeating: "Truly he is Son of God and redeemer of the world."
And Herod said to Longinus: "What power have you seen being done by him that
you say these things and think him to be Son of God?" And the holy one of God,
Longinus, said to Herod: "As I was going in Bethlehem of Judea and reasoning
in myself who then the one born might be, behold on the road suddenly a great
light flashed around me and I became as in a trance, and I was found in
Bethlehem of Judea, and I beheld 'the heavens opened and the angels of God
ascending and descending upon' the infant and worshipping him, and the Holy
Spirit descending upon him and power and glory overshadowing him, and I knew
and was persuaded and believed that truly Christ is Son of God and redeemer of
the world."

4 And king Herod said to Longinus: "I give you the half of my kingdom, if you
give him into my hands." And the holy Longinus said to Herod: "I have
transferred my service into the army of the eternal King and I cannot do this;
but if you wish, believe also yourself, and you will become heir of eternal
life and of the kingdom; for this reason, having been sent away, I said: going
to him I will announce the good news to him, that he may become heir of
eternal life and of the kingdom, and that I may be counted worthy of much
honor, because I brought you a good gospel." And Herod, fighting in wrath
against him, said to his magnates: "Since I set upon him the greatest honor
and made him centurion, for this reason he thinks himself to be something,
being nothing. Strip him therefore of the cloak, and take the torque from him,
and lift the sword from his loins, because he has shown himself impious, and
cast him into the inner prison, putting upon him heavy collars about the neck
and the waist, until I invent many tortures for him. And after this I will
command his head to be cut off."

5 And immediately at that hour he issued such a decree, saying to his own
soldiers: "The infants in Bethlehem, 'from two years old and under,' even to
those of this very day, slay them." And the soldiers went out according to the
king's decree and slew all the infants. And Herod, fighting in wrath because
of the Savior, that he had been mocked by the Magi, calling the holy Longinus,
having taken his seat upon the tribunal, commanded him to be brought. And as
he was being brought, the king saw a golden torque about his neck and a royal
cloak lying upon the saint and a sword about his loins; and he gnashed like a
lion and raged like a wild beast against his magnates, saying: "Did I not say
to you, O most wicked soldiers, take from him both the torque and the sword
and the garment?"

6 And the magnates said to the king: "By your health, O king, we did according
to your command, and having stripped him we so cast him into the prison; but
whence this robe is his, or who clothed him, we do not know." And Herod said
to the holy Longinus: "Wretched man, who have cast yourself down from glory,
confess to me before tortures who it is that clothed you with this robe." And
the holy Longinus said: "As I speak in truth and do not lie, I received it
from no other except from the eternal King Jesus Christ." And Herod said: "Is
this the one who clothed you with this robe?" And the holy Longinus said: "He
sent his angel and put this garment upon me and sealed me with the seal of the
eternal King Jesus Christ."

7 And Herod said to his magnates: "Since he lies greatly, thinking that the
things belonging to him have been granted by his king, lifting therefore from
him the garment that lies upon him you shall burn it with fire." And the
magnates came forward to do what had been commanded them and found nothing to
touch or to take from him. "What," said the magnates therefore to the king,
"behold we see the garment wrapped about him as a shadow, and we cannot take
anything from him." And Herod said: "By Adrasteia among men, now I understand
that he is a magician in making the covering invisible." And the holy Longinus
said: "By the heavenly King who has now come down from the heavens, I do not
know what magic is; for he himself also hates the magicians and the sorcerers.
And how can I, being his servant, possess a heart filthy with magic and
deceit? But I told you that a power of God flashed around me and this keeps
me."

8 And Herod, troubled at the answers of Longinus, commanded his own soldiers
that he be beaten with leaded scourges. And the holy Longinus said: "Now I am
at rest, suffering for the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, having become his
friend and having been loved by him; for I was held by much fear because of
your evil devices." And Herod, gnashing his teeth against Longinus, says to
his magnates: "Casting Longinus the impious soldier into the three holes of
the wood, put heavy irons upon him, that wasting and being torn apart he may
fail in strength and become ugly in beauty, that all the men who knew him at
first may groan when they see him."

9 And they did the things commanded them. And when he had spent thirty days in
the prison, the collars lying upon him without weight, Herod again sought the
holy Longinus, saying to his magnates: "Present to me the prisoner Longinus
upon the tribunal, that seeing him gloomy and withered I may be at rest." And
the magnates, going away into the prison and seeing his face flashing, were
afraid to bring him forward upon the tribunal; nevertheless, taking him
because of what had been commanded them by Herod, they led upon the tribunal
the fair combatant of Christ. And Herod, having seen his face as the face of
an angel, gnashed his teeth against his magnates and tore his garments and
said: "Instead of punishments you have presented this prisoner to me."

10 And Herod, gone mad against his magnates, banished them, and the
prison-keepers he commanded to be beheaded, and the soldiers who stood by him
he decimated; and he gnashed against his army and raged, as thinking that some
plot was being prepared against him. And to the holy Longinus he said: "Swear
to me by the salvation of the gods that you will no longer speak or swear
concerning the one who has been born, nor say that he is king, that if you do
this you may escape the tortures and the punishments that await you." And the
holy Longinus said to the king: "By the glory of Jesus Christ who saves those
who believe in him, you shall not persuade me to deny my God; for hoping in
him, I shall not fear your tortures." And Herod commanded him to be examined
with scourges. And the saint was praising and saying: "Lord, God, the eternal
King of those who reign and Lord of those who rule, who seats kings upon the
ground and raises up the poor from the earth, the only merciful one, yourself
send your angel strengthening me."

11 And Herod, having seen the holy Longinus not perceiving the tortures, said
to his magnates: "Leading him away into the prison, bind him with chains and,
having punished him, bring him to me, that I may count you worthy of much
honor." And they, taking the holy Longinus, led him with much grief into the
prison and did to him the things ordered them. And when he had spent three
days in the guard, and much perplexity was upon Herod because of the king who
had been born, the holy Longinus was praying in the night, saying: "Lord Jesus
Christ, send forth your angel, and deliver your servants who are being
punished here." And when he had prayed, there was a great earthquake, so that
the prison was shaken, and light shone around the holy Longinus and a voice
was heard saying to him: "Rise quickly; for it is not now the time for you to
complete the testimony."

12 And immediately the bonds of all fell off and the prison was opened and men
about a thousand came out and fled from the city; and there was a great uproar
in the city. And the king heard the things that had happened in the prison;
and coming to the place and not finding the holy Longinus there, he was at a
loss what then had happened. And the holy Longinus was moving about in the
city and no one was able to see him, because their eyes were darkened. And
Herod was seeking him; and there was a great uproar and much noise in the city
and they spoke against those who were pious. And Herod sent down warrants to
the nations, that they should bring the holy Longinus bound, thinking that the
servant of God had fled from the city; for being struck with blindness he lay,
and so "Herod, becoming worm-eaten, expired."

13 And Archelaus the son of Herod reigned in his stead, having the surname of
the father. And the holy Longinus was in the holy city awaiting the salvation
from God. And when Archelaus had died, Herod, the brother of Philip, reigned
over Judea and Galilee. And Longinus was received back into his own rank; and
he was beholding the mighty works of God and the wonders done every day by our
Master Jesus Christ, until when, in the fifteenth year of the reign of
Tiberius Caesar, king of the Romans, and of Herod the brother of Philip, who
because of Herodias cut off the head of John the Baptist, who also himself,
having given up the spirit most miserably, expired. And Herod son of Herod,
brother of Archelaus, reigns over Galilee in place of Herod the brother of
Philip; and in the twenty-fifth year of his rule, on the eighth day before the
Kalends of April, which is the twenty-fifth of March, in the consulship of
Rufus and Rubellius, in the fourth year of the second olympiad, under the high
priest of the Jews Joseph and Caiaphas, who also delivered our Lord Jesus
Christ to Pilate to be crucified.

14 And Pilate, knowing that he is a Galilean and under the authority of Herod,
sent him up to Herod, who was in Jerusalem. Who also, "having mocked him" and
having set him at nought, "put a bright garment about him and sent him up to
Pilate." And he, "having called together the chief priests and the rulers,"
disputed with them that "you brought this man to me, being guiltless." And
they all delivered him to be crucified, who also crucified "the one who takes
away the sin of the world," nailing him to the wood; for Pilate had delivered
him to the soldiers, and he set over them the centurion Longinus, who had been
renamed Primianus in the army. And the holy Longinus was with the soldiers
guarding Christ who had gone up upon the cross.

15 "And from the sixth hour until the ninth hour darkness came over all the
earth." And the centurion, standing by about the ninth hour guarding the
cross, beheld a great light about him, but darkness was over all the earth.
And he saw also a multitude of angels standing by the cross and all the earth
being shaken, and "the veil of the temple was torn," and he saw the face of
the Lord as lightning. And before he gave up the spirit, the holy Longinus was
considering in his heart, saying that "truly this is the Son of God and
redeemer of all the world." And considering these things he fell upon his face
toward the feet of the Lord; and a trance came upon the soldiers and they were
turned to sleep. And the holy Longinus cried out with a great voice, saying:
"Lord Jesus Christ, Son of the living God, have mercy on me; for I have
believed that you are the Son of God, the Savior of the world." And Jesus said
to him: "Man, today you have been born, and your memorial shall be spoken
until the last days."

16 And the holy Longinus, having heard these things, said to Jesus: "Lord, I
am going from this city because of the threat of the magnates." And Jesus said
to him: "On your forehead make the type of the cross, because with your blood
I will baptize you, and go." And after he had made the cross, the centurion
was guarding with the soldiers the precious cross; and standing by "about the
ninth hour," when Jesus had cried "with a great voice: Eloi, eloi, that is: my
God, my God, why have you forsaken me?", "he gave up the spirit." And behold a
great thunder came, and the earth was shaken, and the sun was darkened from
the sixth hour until the ninth hour, and the stars of heaven showed their
shining, and "the rocks were split and the tombs were opened," and "the veil
of the temple was torn from top to bottom into two," and "many bodies of the
saints who had fallen asleep were raised."

17 And the centurion Primianus, who is also Longinus, who stood by the cross,
glorified God at all the wonders that had happened and said: "Truly this is
Son of God and redeemer of the world." "And all the crowds who came together
to this spectacle, seeing the things that had happened, beating their own
faces, returned." And the centurion Longinus, who had also been entrusted with
the cross, reported to the governor concerning the wonders that had taken
place. And leaving all things there, he came to his own fatherland, in Second
Cappadocia, near Tyana, to a place called Andrales, surnamed in Hebrew
Gabrales, and he changed his garments, and he was pasturing the sheep. And
Herod, having read through the archives, found that the holy Longinus was
shaking the kingdom and stirring revolt concerning his father and telling all
to become Christians.

18 And having learned from the prefect Lucius the exact account and the things
of the confession of the centurion and the things of the endurance, he raised
a persecution against the Christians; and the holy Longinus was sought by the
idolaters. And when the holy Longinus had come into his own country, there was
in those days a plundering against all who were pious, and the king sent three
principes with a great force to search out the holy Longinus. And Herod,
calling the three principes and the soldiers, gave them commands, saying thus,
that "If you do not bring me the head of Longinus the centurion, your heads
also are cut off with swords." Taking these commands therefore they were going
to the search, grieving exceedingly; for having gone about from Antioch even
as far as Rome and Illyricum, they were not finding him.

19 And lamenting and turning back upon Cappadocia they were walking about, and
coming near Tyana they were seeking the holy Longinus; and coming into the
village of the Andralesians, they were inquiring from those there and no one
was giving an answer concerning him; for they loved him exceedingly. And not
obtaining an answer the soldiers went out from the village in great grief. And
the holy Longinus was very near the road pasturing sheep, through which the
ones sent were passing; and the holy Longinus was sitting in the road. And
seeing them coming from afar he recognized them and stood up; and when they
had come to him he said to them: "Welcome, brothers." And when they had not
recognized who the one speaking to them might be, Longinus said to them: "Why,
brothers, are you gloomy and as if wearied from a great journey?"

20 And they said: "Hear, brother, our bitterness, that a command has gone out
against Longinus the centurion from Herod the king, containing thus, that if
you do not bring his head, your heads shall be delivered to the sword. We
therefore, O man, have nine months seeking him and we have not found him.
Behold, you have heard the evils that have overtaken us. If therefore you know
anyone called Longinus the centurion, tell us." And the holy Longinus said: "I
do not know the man. But going into my house remain, and tomorrow you shall go
your way." And they went into his house to remain with him. And morning came
and the holy Longinus said to them: "What is the cause of Longinus, that you
seek him with such haste?" And the men said to him that he is sought by the
king because he makes his kingdom unsettled, persuading men to worship the
crucified Christ and King of the ages.

21 And they were grieving and reasoning with one another and saying: "What
shall we do?" And the holy Longinus said to them: "Do not be troubled,
brothers, nor be downcast; for I am the one you seek, the servant of God and
of Jesus Christ, Longinus, to whom you were sent." And the men, hearing, were
troubled and became speechless, unable to speak. And the holy Longinus said to
them: "Do not be downcast, brothers, but let us eat and drink and go away to
the one who sent you. For I have sold my body to Christ, that I may buy my
soul from him." And they, hearing these things, said to him with tears: "No,
but we will go away and make defense for you to the one who sent us; for if
you are truly Longinus, we also die with you. And this we will not do against
you, to lead you away to the one who sent us."

22 And seeing that they had been pricked by the Spirit of God, he said to
them: "If you do mercy with me, do thus: take my head and carry it to the
king, and you will be free of care all the days of your life." And they,
fearing, were not willing to consent. And he adjured them twice, entreating
them to do this. And they, having been long entreated to do this, said to him:
"Intercede therefore for us in your army, that it may not be reckoned to us as
sin." And when he had declared to them that he would do this, they cut off his
head. Having beheaded therefore the holy Longinus, they brought the head to
the king; and the uproar of the lawless ceased. And the head of the holy
Longinus was being kept by the prefect Lucius. And the men of the village,
where the holy one of God was, taking the body, laid it in that village.

23 And a certain widow woman named Chreste was troubled by a savage spirit,
terribly cutting herself, being of the nobles. And she was considering on the
morrow to go to the East; and having heard concerning those healed by Jesus,
on that night she wished to go. And the holy Longinus appeared to her without
his head. And when she said, "Who has come here?" the holy Longinus said to
her: "Do you wish to be healed?" And she said: "Yes, Lord, this I pray." And
the saint said to her: "Go to Jerusalem, and learn the house of Lucius the
prefect, and seek the head of Longinus the centurion, and convey it to my
body, and you shall be saved; for I am Longinus, who was pasturing the sheep
beside the road, and I have not found rest in the burial in which I was buried
until now; but do this and you shall be healed in the name of Jesus Christ.
And not only shall you be healed, but I will also enlist your son, because I
who speak to you am without the head."

24 And the woman, hearing these things, rising and taking leave of the friends
she had, was going upon the road, and coming to the tomb she cried out with a
great voice, saying: "Lord, who are in this place, as you have said to me in
the vision, I am going, and help me." And immediately the place was shaken and
a voice came to her saying: "Go, it is Christ who helps you." And she, hearing
this voice, was going in joy the whole of her way; and coming to Jerusalem,
she learned from those of the city the house of the prefect, in order to seek
out the head of the holy Longinus. And the prefect gave it to her for a price
of silver, the number of two hundred denarii. And taking it, the woman was
praying and glorifying God that she might be fully counted worthy of the grace
of God.

25 And taking the head of the precious Longinus with her son Christion, she
was preparing to go out in the evening. And behold three angels appeared to
her, whose glory she was not able to look upon, and in their hands white
tapers, and going before they hymned God through the whole night. And when
dawn was rising, they rested at the place they had reached, to take food. And
within three days she brought the head of the precious Longinus into his
village, having journeyed with her son Christion, and she was going to the
place where he lay, it being the sixth hour of the night. They arrived
therefore at the house which the friends of Christ had built, worthy of the
martyr, and immediately the door was opened to them of itself, and they saw
the tomb opened, and light shone in the dwelling round about. And the young
man, going into the tomb with myrrh and spices, set the head beside the body,
and being joined together it became as before it was cut off. And when he had
gone out from the tomb, immediately the place was made fast of itself. And
they returned to the house, rejoicing and glorifying God.

26 And on the second night, the holy Longinus appeared to Chreste, saying:
"Now I have found rest, because you have furnished me the head, and you shall
be healed from this day. And I ask this: shall I enlist your son in the
heavenly army, or in the earthly?" And she, answering with fear, said: "I
beseech you, enlist him in the heavenly army." And hearing these things, he
departed from her. And on the following day, the young man went into the
vineyard for the care of the vine. And as he was working until the fifth hour,
the holy Longinus appeared to him and said: "Young man Christion, do you wish
to be enlisted according to your name unto Christ?" And he, looking up, saw
him as lightning and said to him: "Who are you, Lord, who wish to enlist me?"
And he said: "I am Longinus, who was granted the head through your mother, but
as she brought my head, so I also wish to offer you to God." And Christion
said to him: "If these things are so, let it be to me according to your word."
And immediately the young man gave up the spirit.

27 And his mother, at the accustomed hour of the days that had passed, taking
the food of the midday meal, was going to her son in the vineyard; and coming
she found him dead. And seeing this, she fell upon her face upon the earth;
and fainting and tearing the tunic, she lay as one dead. And the angel coming
strengthened her; and he said to her: "Why do you weep?" And she said: "My son
has perished, who is my only-begotten, the fruit of my womb has been harvested
untimely." And the angel said to her: "Do not be led astray, woman, nor weep
at this; for he has not been harvested, nor has he perished, but he is in the
bosom of Jesus Christ. Go therefore into the village, and call devout men, and
coming they shall carry the body of your son; and let them bury him in the
tomb in which the most faithful Longinus lies, and after three days you see
your son in the heavenly army."

28 And having said these things the angel became invisible; and she, rising,
was going into the village, and immediately the angel came comforting her; and
they were learning what had happened. And she announced to them everything,
and they, hearing, went to the bishop of Tyana and announced to him the things
that had taken place. And immediately bishop Paphnutius, taking devout men,
came to the place where the young man had died, and he commanded that his
mother be called. And when she had come, the bishop said to her: "Do not weep
at this; for he, possessing the heavenly kingdom, greatly intercedes for us.
Let us rejoice therefore rather and not be gloomy." And having said these
things, he commanded the body of the young man to be taken up and laid with
the holy Longinus, and they offered to the heavenly God an acceptable
sacrifice. And the pious woman became a deaconess and minister of the glorious
faith; and having offered and having exhorted much, the bishop went away into
the city.

29 And when evening had come and night had come on, it being the fourth hour
of the night, the holy Longinus appeared to her, saying: "Why do you blaspheme
me?" And she, becoming afraid and trembling, said: "Who are you, Lord?" And he
said: "I am Longinus who enlisted your son." And Chreste, turning over in her
mind concerning her son, crying out was saying: "Lord, if you had enlisted
him, you would not have killed him." And the most faithful Longinus said to
her: "See, even now you speak blasphemies. But rise and go out from your
house, and you shall see your son, that he has been enlisted." And taking her
by the hand, he led her out, and she sees the heaven opened and a great light
in the night; and looking up, she saw as in a trance the heavenly King and her
son going before. And the holy Longinus said to her: "Even now that you have
seen your son, that he has been enlisted, have you believed now?" And she
said: "Yes, Lord; enough for me are the things I spoke against you, Master."
And immediately he became invisible.

30 And she was attending night and day in the holy house, continuing in
prayers and hymns and alms, and likewise also performing healings through
invocation of Christ and of the holy martyr, glorifying God in her days at all
the good things that had befallen her. And she died in the days of bishop
Paphnutius; and they laid her near the holy Longinus in the same house <.....>
to the Father and to the Son and to the Holy Spirit, to whom be the glory and
the might unto the ages of ages. Amen.
