SECOND PRAYER OF JOHN CHRYSOSTOM.
=63.923=
Lord my God, the great and fearful and glorious, the Creator of all visible
and intelligible creation, who keep your covenant and your mercy toward those
who love you and keep your commandments: both now and always I give thanks to
you for all your benefits done toward me, both seen and unseen. And until now
I praise and glorify and magnify you, because you have made marvelous upon me
your rich mercies and your compassions, taking me up from my mother's womb,
and providing for all things, having preserved and governed what concerns me
in holiness through your goodness and loving-kindness alone. For you did not
overlook my lowliness because of my unworthiness and my passion-bound state,
but through your loving-kindness and compassion you have not ceased doing good
and providing. And unto old age and gray hairs, my God, do not forsake me,
Jesus Christ, the fair name, my sweetness, and my desire, and my hope; who
became man for us, and endured death through the cross, and ordered and
disposed all things in wisdom for the sake of our salvation. I confess to you,
Lord my God, with my whole heart; I bend the knee of body and soul, declaring
to you my God all my sins. Incline your ear also to my supplication, and
forgive the impiety of my heart. I have sinned, I have broken the law, I have
transgressed, I have provoked, I have embittered my good Master and nourisher
and guardian; there is no form of evil, spoken or unspoken, which I have not
done both in deed and in word, both in knowledge and in ignorance, both in
thoughts and in notions, having sinned exceedingly unto excess; and having
often promised to repent, so many times I have fallen into the same things.
More easily shall the drops of rain be numbered than the multitude of my sins;
for they have gone up over my head, and like a heavy burden they have weighed
me down. For from my youth even until now, having opened a door to unseemly
desires, I have used unbridled and disorderly impulses, defiling the tunic
woven from above of my holy baptism, staining the temple of my body, polluting
my wretched soul with the passions of dishonor =63.924= and committing every
other lawlessness and injustice; of which, if I should wish to recall them in
part, time would fail me as I recount them. But since you yourself know all
things (for there is no creature hidden before you, but all things are naked
and laid open to your eyes), what need is there to say to one who knows the
things that are not unknown to you? But my heart is crushed, and the bones of
my soul, and I sink wholly into a depth of perplexity, considering that having
sinned so greatly and so much, I have shown not even some small work of
repentance; and the time of the cutting is near, and the appointed term of
death has come, but the time of repentance is nowhere. Therefore my soul is
troubled, and is in pain and full of dejection; for being unready and
unprepared, reasoning and examining the things concerning myself, I find
nothing sufficient for a defense, nor any way or device through which I shall
be delivered from the eternal fire. For if the righteous is scarcely saved,
the sinner that I am, where shall I appear? And if through many tribulations
the kingdom of the heavens is allotted to those who are worthy, and the way of
life is narrow and hard-pressed, how shall I, living always in luxury and
wantonness, be counted worthy of salvation? And if all the righteousness of
man is as a rag of one sitting apart, this so great mire
and injustice, what shall it be counted? If even for an idle word one must
give account, for so many sins what acceptable defense shall I have? Woe is
me, O soul! that our affairs have come to this. Short is life and easily
entangled, passing swiftly and sending us on to death; eternal is the
punishment of sinners, as also the kingdom of the righteous, and the life of
both is not cut off by death. What then shall I do? What shall I accomplish?
Into what chaos shall I hurl myself? For death too is fearful, and especially
of sinners, since it is also evil: for the death of sinners is evil; and
fearful also are the terrors after death, but far more fearful still is to
fall into the hands of the living God, from which there is none who is able to
deliver. When therefore he comes to be glorified in his saints, and to render
to each according to his works; =63.925= when thrones are set, and the
impartial Judge sits fearful, and the river of fire drawing before him, and
the brightness and the joy of the righteous prepared, and all the myriads of
the angels, and all men from of old, and all creation together, both the
visible and the intelligible, stands trembling; what shall I then do, filled
with shame, condemned by conscience, deprived of all boldness and defense?
Groanings from every side. Woe is me for the evils! What shall I lament first?
What shall I groan second? What shall I bewail? The loss of the good things,
or the pain of the grievous? The endlessness of the punishment, or the
separation from God? Weep, wretched soul, considering these things, and what
will meet you after the departure, how very heavy and painful they are, and
cry out: O God of the powers, the eternal God, the God of mercy and
compassions, do not forsake me, do not overlook me, do not take your mercy
away from me; Give heed to my help, O Lord of my salvation.
And even being thus and thinking thus I do not give up the good hope, nor do I
despair of my salvation. For I know the ready compassion of my Master, I know
the unresentfulness of the Lover of mankind, and that he is one who wills
mercy, not wishing the death of the sinner, but that he should turn and live,
wishing all to be saved and to come to knowledge of the truth, and especially
those who turn from sin. For you did not come to call the righteous, but
sinners to repentance. For those who are well have no need of a physician, but
those who are ill. Now I have begun to speak to my Lord; but I am earth and
ashes, a worm and not a man, a reproach of men and despised of the people.
Give me a word in the opening of my mouth, you who give prayer to the one who
prays, for from you Every good giving and every perfect gift is from above,
coming down from you the Father of lights; that I may both entreat worthily
and supplicate profitably.
Let me not be turned away humbled, put to shame, but having obtained what I
hoped for; and so I shall go away rejoicing in the full assurance of my heart.
Have mercy on me, O God, according to your great mercy, for my soul has
trusted in you; have mercy on me, Lord, for I am weak, O physician of souls
and bodies. As standing at your fearful judgment seat, as touching your
undefiled feet, so I entreat and beg and beseech with a crushed and humbled
heart: Be propitious to me the sinner, forgive me the useless and lowly, look
from your holy dwelling upon my wretchedness, look from your holy heights upon
the prayer of your humble servant, and do not overlook my supplication; spare
me, that I may take breath, before I go away, from where I shall no longer
return. As man I have sinned; as God forgive; for you know, Master, the
slipperiness of human nature, and that the thought of man is carefully set
upon the evil things from youth. Remember that we are dust; remember that you
alone are pure and undefiled and unstained, but we all are under penalties;
remember your compassions from of old and your mercy, and do not condemn me
together with my lawless deeds, nor repay me according to my sins. You know
the multitude of my lawless deeds, that it is great and not subject to number;
but I also know the sea of your loving-kindness, that it is incomparable and
unconquerable. For you are the one who takes away the sin of the =63.926=
world, who came down from heaven upon the earth, to seek the sheep that was
led astray and lost; the good shepherd, who lays down his life for the sheep,
and came into the world to save sinners, of whom I am first. Have mercy, have
mercy on the work of your hands; do not abhor me the unworthy, but pity your
work, you who for me endured a cross, and accepted the marks of the stripes,
yet having healed, wipe away all things with the medicine of compassion and
the sponge of your loving-kindness; For you can all things, and nothing is
impossible for you.
Pierce my hardened heart with compunction, lighten the weight of conscience,
receive my tears and my groaning, as of the harlot, as of Peter the chief of
the apostles; receive this small confession and repentance of mine, you who
received the acknowledgment of the thief on the cross; receive the fruit from
my lips, as a living sacrifice, well-pleasing, for a savor of fragrance. I
entreat, be entreated; I implore, be implored. Manasseh also, that king,
sinned, but he was not lost when he repented; David also sinned before him,
but having wept out the sin he was shown mercy. I have many such examples
comforting and consoling my despondency, driving despair away from me, and
nourishing my hope of salvation. Do you also comfort my heart, the Father of
compassions and God of all consolation; cheer my soul and make it glad, O
fountain of mercy and of good things. You have done many great and marvelous
things from of old, both glorious and extraordinary, of which there is no
number; but if you save me the prodigal, if you present me the unworthy, you
will be more greatly and more highly marveled at; because such is the power of
your mercy and loving-kindness, as even to make a pearl from mire, and from
being a son of Gehenna, to make a son of the kingdom. And still I will cry
to my Lord, and I will entreat my God: govern the remainder of my life
according to your will; establish me in your fear; strengthen me in your love,
and in the multitude of your goodness; grant me a good end, worthy of your
loving-kindness, and look upon my bones and my joints and all the composition
of my being in your mercy and loving-kindness, and having led my wretched soul
into a place of relief and rest, restore it, for there are many mansions with
you, distributed to each according to worth. Still I beg and entreat, give
then, Lord, also a grace of understanding to my unworthiness, to think the
things well-pleasing to you, and profitable to me, and not only to think but
also to do: not to be snatched and carried away with vain things, not to do
the things that are not fitting, to be humbled without vainglory with the
humble, and to have compassion on those who suffer, and to forgive those who
sin. For I know that if I do not forgive, I shall not be forgiven.
Therefore I entreat, forgive all things to all who sin against me; for they
are not the cause, but I the wretched one, who do not do your will, and do not
keep your commandments. And those who love us, repay with your rich gifts; and
my spiritual father, and the brothers whom you have given, O compassionate
one, having pitied them with me in judgments which you know, in
loving-kindness, have mercy. Let these words of my prayer make intercession
for me both living and dead; let this confession and the tears be directed as
incense before you =63.927=; and I each day await the inescapable hour of
death. And my wretched body, given over to burial, will be corrupted, and
will be dissolved into the things from which it was composed, which you the
Giver of life will raise incorruptible in the time of regeneration; but my
spirit I commit into your hands. Give rest, holy Master, in the light of the
living, and in the dwelling of those who rejoice, also to my parents,
ancestors, and brothers, your well-disposed servants; for though we have
sinned, yet we have not departed from you, nor have we spread out our hands
toward a strange God, but we have known you, and we have loved you, and we
have believed in you, and we worship =63.928= you the one God in Trinity, and
in you we pray, and on you we hang the hopes of salvation.
Have mercy on us according to your great mercy, and save us into your heavenly
and eternal kingdom. Yes indeed, my Lord, Lord, so let these things come to be
in us who hope in you, through your great and unsurpassable goodness, and
through your unspeakable compassion and loving-kindness, by the intercessions
of our all-glorious, all-hymned, highly blessed, and highly favored Lady, the
most holy Theotokos and ever-virgin Mary, of the heavenly and noetic powers,
and of all who from of old have been well-pleasing to you. Amen.
