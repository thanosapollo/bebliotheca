HOMILY 15.
On Faith.
To remember God continually is pious and admits no satiety for the
God-loving soul; but to set forth in speech the things concerning God
is daring: thought falling far short of the worth of the realities, and
speech in turn presenting dimly the things that have been thought. If
then our thought is left far behind the greatness of the realities, and
speech is lesser even than the thought itself, how is silence not
necessary, lest in the cheapness of words the wonder of theology should
seem to be put in danger? The desire to glorify God has been sown by
nature in all rational beings; but all alike fall short of speaking
worthily. In zeal for piety we differ one from another; yet no one is
so blinded, and so cheats himself, as to suppose that he has climbed to
the summit of comprehension; but the more he seems to advance in
knowledge, the more he will perceive his own weakness. Such was
Abraham, such was Moses; when they were able to see God, as it is
possible for a human being to see, then especially each belittled
himself: Abraham naming himself earth and ashes, and Moses saying he
was weak-voiced and slow of tongue. For he saw the weakness of his
tongue, unable to serve the greatness of the things thought.
Nevertheless, since every ear is now opened toward the hearing of
theology, and there is no satiety in the Church of such hearings,
confirming the word of Ecclesiastes, that The ear will not be filled
from hearing, it is necessary to speak =31.465= according to our power.
And we shall speak, not how great God is, but how much is attainable
for us.
For neither, because we are unable to traverse with our eyes the whole
space between heaven and earth, do we already refuse even as much as we
can behold; so also now through small words let us fulfill piety, and
let us concede to the majesty of the nature to have the victory over
every speech. For neither the tongues of angels, whatever they may be,
nor those of archangels, gathered together with the whole of rational
nature at once, will be able to reach even a small fraction, still less
to equal themselves to the whole. But you, if you wish to say or hear
something concerning God, leave your body, leave the bodily senses,
forsake the earth, forsake the sea, put the air beneath yourself, pass
by hours, the good order of seasons, the arrangements about the earth;
come to be above the ether; pass through the stars, the wonders about
them, their good order, their magnitudes, the services they furnish to
the whole, their order, their brightness, their position, their motion;
how they stand in relation or distance toward one another.
Having passed through all by thought, having looked over the heaven,
and having come to be above it, with mind alone look round upon the
beauties there: heavenly armies, choruses of angels, the presidencies
of the archangels, the glories of the dominions, the seats of the
thrones, the powers, the principalities, the authorities. Having run
through all things, and having raised your reasonings above all
creation, and having lifted the mind beyond these, conceive the divine
nature: standing, unchanging, unalterable, impassible, simple,
uncompounded, indivisible, light unapproachable, power unspeakable,
magnitude unbounded, glory flashing beyond, goodness to be desired,
beauty inexpressible, laying hold violently of the wounded soul, yet
impossible to be declared by speech according to its worth.
There is Father and Son and Holy Spirit, the uncreated nature, the
lordly dignity, the natural goodness. Father: the beginning of all, the
cause of being for the things that are, the root of the living. From
whom came forth the fountain of life, the wisdom, the power, the
unvarying image of the invisible God, the Son begotten from the Father,
the living Word, who is God, and who is with God; who is, not who was
added; existing before the ages, not acquired later; Son, not a
possession; maker, not a thing made; creator, =31.468= not a creature;
being all things that the Father is; Son, I said, and Father. Keep
these properties for me. Remaining then in being Son, he is all that
the Father is, according to the voice of the Lord himself saying: All
things that the Father has are mine. For in truth all things that
belong to the prototype form belong to the image. For we beheld, says
the evangelist, his glory, glory as of the Only-begotten from the
Father; that is, not of wonders given to him from gift and grace, but
of the Son having from the communion according to nature the dignity of
the paternal Godhead.
For to receive is common even toward the creation; but to have from
nature is proper to the one begotten. As Son, then, he possesses the
things of the Father naturally; as Only-begotten, he has them all
gathered in himself, nothing being partitioned toward another. From the
very appellation of Son, therefore, we are taught that he is a sharer
of the nature; not created by a command, but flashing forth from the
essence, joined to the Father without interval, without time, equal in
goodness, equal in power, sharer of the glory. And what else but a seal
and image, showing in himself the whole Father? And whatever after this
he discourses with you from the bodily constitution, managing the
salvation of human beings, which he displayed to us having appeared
through flesh, saying that he is sent, and that he can do nothing from
himself, and that he has received a commandment, and such things, let
them not furnish you occasions for diminishing the Godhead of the
Only-begotten. For the condescension toward your weakness ought not to
become a lessening of the dignity of the mighty one; but think the
nature in a God-befitting way, and receive the humbler words as of the
economy.
Concerning which if we should wish in the present to narrate with
precision, we would unawares introduce a great and boundless multitude
of words into the subject. But let us return to the matter at hand.
That a mind which has been able to be purified of material passions,
and to leave behind all intelligible creation, and like some fish to
swim up from the deep toward the surface above, having come to be in
the purity of creation, there it will see the Holy Spirit, where the
Son is, and where the Father is, itself also having all things
coessentially according to nature: the goodness, the uprightness, the
sanctification, the life. For Your Spirit, =31.469= he says, is good;
and again, an upright Spirit; and again, the Holy Spirit. And the
Apostle: The law of the Spirit of life. None of these is acquired by
it, nor is present as something later generated; but as heating is
inseparable from fire, and shining from light, so also to the Spirit
belong sanctifying, making alive, goodness, uprightness.
There then the Spirit stands, there, in the blessed nature, not
numbered with a multitude, but contemplated in the Trinity; announced
as unique, not included in the companies. For as there is one Father,
and one Son, so also one Holy Spirit. But the ministering spirits in
each rank suggest to us a multitude hard to number. Do not then seek in
the creation what is above the creation; do not bring down the one who
sanctifies with those who are sanctified. This fills angels, fills
archangels, sanctifies powers, makes all things alive. This, being
distributed into all creation, and being participated otherwise by
another, is in no way diminished by those who participate. To all it
gives the grace from itself; yet it is not spent upon the participants;
but those who receive are filled, and it itself is without need. And as
the sun shining upon bodies, and being participated by them in various
ways, is in no way diminished by those who participate, so also the
Spirit, furnishing to all the grace from itself, remains undiminished
and undivided.
It enlightens all toward the comprehension of God, breathes upon the
prophets, makes lawgivers wise, perfects priests, strengthens kings,
equips the righteous, dignifies the temperate, works gifts of healings,
makes the dead alive, looses those who have been bound, adopts those
who have been alienated. These things it works through the birth from
above. If it takes a believing tax-collector, it shows him an
evangelist; if it comes to be in a fisherman, it makes him a
theologian; if it finds a persecutor repenting, it works him an apostle
of the nations, a herald of faith, a vessel of election.
Through it the weak are strong; the poor become rich; the unlearned in
speech are wiser than the wise. Paul is weak, but through the presence
of the Spirit the handkerchiefs from his skin furnished healing to
those who received them. Peter also himself wearing a body of
weakness, but through the indwelling grace of the Spirit the shadow
falling from the body drove away the diseases of those who were
oppressed. Poor were Peter and John; for they had neither silver nor
gold; but they granted health more precious than much gold. For having
received gold from many, =31.472= the lame man was still a beggar; but
having received the grace from Peter, he ceased begging, leaping like a
deer, and praising God. John did not know the wisdom of the world, but
he uttered words by the power of the Spirit, at which no wisdom can
look.
This both stands in heaven, and has filled the earth, and is present
everywhere, and is contained nowhere. It dwells wholly in each, and is
wholly with God. It does not minister the gifts in a servile way, but
divides the charisms authoritatively. For it divides, he says, to each
one individually, as it wills. It is sent economically, but it works
autonomously. Let us pray that this be present to our souls, and that
it leave us at no time in the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ; to whom
be the glory and the power unto the ages of ages. Amen.
