* Other Fragments

And the most admirable Justin rightly declared that those aforementioned
resemble robbers.
And Justin the Neapolitan, a man neither far from the apostles in time nor in
virtue, says that what is dying is inherited, and that what is living
inherits, and that flesh dies, but the kingdom of the heavens lives.
God, having formed man at the beginning, suspended the things of nature from
his will, making the trial by one commandment. For He made him, if he kept
this, to be of the immortal allotment, but if he transgressed, of the
contrary. Man having thus come into being and having immediately come to the
transgression, received corruption naturally. And corruption having been added
by nature, it was necessary that He, wishing to save, destroy the
corruption-making substance. And this could not otherwise take place, unless
the life according to nature were joined to that which had received the
corruption, destroying the corruption, and preserving immortal thereafter that
which had received it. For this reason it was necessary that the Word come to
be in a body, that He might free us from the death of our corruption according
to nature. For if, as you say, He prevented our death by a mere nod, death
would not have approached because of the will, but we would nonetheless still
have been corruptible, carrying about in ourselves the natural corruption.
We shall not harm God by being ignorant of Him, but we shall deprive ourselves
of His friendship.
Neither will light ever be darkness, so long as it is light, nor will the
truth of the things among us be refuted. For it is truth, and nothing is
stronger than this.
A sound word does not enter a hard and disobedient heart, but as if rebounding
it returns upon itself.
And as health is a good of the body, so knowledge of God is a good of the
soul, being a kind of health of the soul, according to which likeness to God
comes about.
Just as for all bodies that have come to be by God there coexists also the
having of a shadow, so also for God, being just, it is consequent that He
assign to each according to desert, both to those who have chosen the good
things and to those who have preferred the evil.
The soul is hard to lead back up to those good things from which it slipped,
and hard to lead out from those evils to which it has become accustomed. If
you had ever been willing to blame yourself, I might perhaps have nourished
some good hopes concerning you by the medicine of repentance; but since you
have utterly despised fear and have spat upon the faith in Christ itself, it
were better for you that you had not been born from the womb.
It escapes them, as is likely, that he has stood out from error to the truth
through the real faith; but he himself truly knows himself, not, as they say,
as being mad, but as having been freed from the unstable and changeable
corruption around the manifold variety of error, through the simple and ever
likewise remaining truth.
I consider, O men, that to do well is nothing other than to live according to
truth; and to live well or according to truth is not without perceiving the
nature of things.
The inexperience of the teacher destroys those being taught, and the
negligence of those being taught brings danger to the teacher, and especially
when those men would be careless because of his own lack of knowledge.
To fall under and yield to the passions is the last slavery, just as to master
these is the only freedom.
The greatest good being not to sin, the second good is to be justified; but
whoever remaining a long time in injustice remains unchastened, this man one
must consider the most unfortunate.
It is necessary that by inexperience and vice of a charioteer the yoked beasts
be carried down precipices, just as by experience and virtue they are saved.
The end for the one philosophizing is likeness to God according to what is
possible.
There is neither cramped space with God nor anything unnumbered.
He does not speak concerning foreign nations, but concerning the one who
agrees with the nations, according to what was said by Jeremiah: It is bitter
for you to leave me, says the Lord your God, because from of old you have
broken your yoke and have burst your bonds and have said: I will not serve
you, but I will go upon every high mountain and under every tree, and there I
will be dissolved in my fornication.
