ORATION XXXIII.
Against the Arians, and concerning himself.
A. Where then are they who reproach us with poverty, and boast of wealth? Who
define the Church by multitude, =36.216= and spit upon the little flock? Who
both measure Godhead and weigh the people? Who honor the sand and insult the
luminaries? Who treasure pebbles and overlook pearls? For they do not know
that sand is not so much more abundant than stars, and pebbles than
translucent stones, as these are purer and more precious than those. Again you
are indignant? Again you arm yourself? Again you insult? The new faith? Hold
back the threat a little, that I may speak. We will not insult, but convict;
we will not threaten, but reproach; we will not strike, but heal. Does this
too seem an insult to you? What pride! Do you here too make a slave of the one
equal in honor? If not, receive my frankness. Even a brother convicts a
brother when he is defrauded.
B. Do you want me to utter to you the words of God toward Israel, stiff-necked
and hardened? My people, what have I done to you, or wherein have I wronged
you, or wherein have I wearied you? Rather, my speech is toward you the
insulter. Badly indeed we watch one another's seasons, and having loosed the
fellow-soul by difference of opinion, we have become almost even more inhuman
than the barbarians now warring against us, whom the Trinity, being dissolved,
has banded together. We have become more inhuman and bolder toward one
another, except that we do not, as foreigners, strike foreigners, nor as men
of another tongue those of another tongue (which is also a small consolation
of the disaster), but one another, and as it were those of the same house, we
carry and lead; or if you will, we the members of the same body consume and
are consumed; and this is not the terrible thing, though it is so great, but
that we even think the subtraction a gain. And since we are thus, and believe
with the times, let us set the times against one another: you your emperor,
and I mine; you Ahab, I Josiah. Tell me your mildness, and I my boldness.
Rather, yours many books and =36.217= tongues carry, which even the time to
come will take up, as I think, the immortal pillar of the deeds; but I will
tell my own.
C. What people have I led against you, borne along by boldness? What hoplites
have I arrayed? What general boiling with rage, and bolder than those who
command him, and this one not even a Christian, but bringing to his own demons
a private worship, the impiety against us? Whom have I besieged while praying
and lifting their hands to God? Whose psalmody have I stopped with trumpets?
Whose mystic blood have I mixed with murderous bloods? Whose groans have I
stopped with dirges, spiritual with deadly, and tears of compunction with
tears of tragedy? What house of prayer have I made a burial-place of many?
What liturgical vessels, untouchable even to the many, have I given over to
the hands of the lawless, either to Nebuzaradan the chief cook, or to
Belshazzar who feasted wickedly among the holy things, and indeed was charged
with penalties worthy of the madness? Altars beloved, as the divine Scripture
says, but now outraged; and which of yours, for our sake, has a wanton youth
danced down, twisting and bending shamefully? Or rather, through what such
person have I danced out the great and divine mystery? Precious throne, and
seat and rest of precious men, which has exchanged many pious priests, who
from above initiated the divine things: who has mounted upon you, a Greek
demagogue, and an evil tongue, pillorying the things of the Christians?
Modesty and dignity of virgins, that cannot bear the looks even of temperate
men: which of us has shamed you and outraged you even as far as the unseen
things, and set you as a pitiable spectacle before impious eyes, worthy of the
Sodomitic fire? For I leave aside speaking of deaths more endurable than the
shame.
D. What beasts have we let loose upon the bodies of saints, as some who have
made human nature a public thing, having charged one thing only, not
consenting to the impiety, =36.220= nor being stained by the communion, which
we flee as the poison of a serpent, not harming the body, but blackening the
depths of the soul? For whom has even burying the dead become a charge, whom
even beasts reverenced? And how great the charge? Worthy of another theatre
and other beasts. Whose bishops' aged flesh has been carded with claws, while
those initiated were present and had no help except to weep; hung with Christ,
conquering by suffering, and sprinkling the people with the precious blood,
and at last carried away to death, to be crucified with Christ, and buried
with him, and glorified with him, with Christ who conquered the world through
such slaughters and sacrifices? What presbyters have contrary natures, water
and fire, divided, raising a strange torch upon the sea, and burned together
with the ship on which they were put to sea? Who (that I may cover the greater
part of our evils) have been accused of inhumanity even by the rulers
themselves, who granted such things? For even if they served the desires, yet
they hated the cruelty of the purpose. For the one was of the time, the other
of reasoning; and the one of the emperor's lawlessness, the other of the
consciousness of the laws, by which they had to judge.
E. Or that I may speak of older things (for these too are of the same
brotherhood), whose hands have I cut off, either of dead or of living saints,
and brought a false charge, that I might war against the faith through the
outrage? Whose exiles have I numbered as benefactions, and not even reverenced
the sacred companies of sacred philosophers, from where I sought the
suppliants? On the contrary, I have made those too martyrs, running risk for
the good. Upon whom have I brought in harlots, upon men almost fleshless and
bloodless, being myself accused of wantonness about words? Which of the pious
have I banished from the fatherland, =36.221= and given over to the hands of
lawless men, that, shut up like beasts in unlighted houses, and separated from
one another (for this indeed is the heaviest of the tragedy), they might
suffer with hunger and thirst, having food measured out, and this through
narrow passages, and not even allowed to see those who share the pain? And
these, who? Of whom the world was not worthy. Thus do you honor faith? Thus do
you show hospitality? You do not know the greater part of these; and very
reasonably, because of the multitude of the dramas, and of the luxury in doing
them; but the one who suffers is more remembering. Why should I speak of
things far off? But some have even become more violent than the time, just as
swine driven against fences. I seek my yesterday's victim of yours, the old
man, and Abraham-like father, whom, brought from exile, you greeted with
stones, in the middle of the day, in the middle of the city. But we even
sought out the murderers when they were in danger, if it is not burdensome to
say. For which of these shall I be gracious to you? says God somewhere in
Scripture. Which of these shall I praise? Or rather, from which shall I crown
you?
F. And since yours are such, and thus they stand, tell me also my injustices,
that I may either cease being bad, or be ashamed. For I for my part pray most
of all to sin nothing; but if not, even while doing wrong to be brought back;
this is the second portion of those who think well. For even if I do not
become my own accuser in the first speech according to the just man, I rejoice
at least to be healed by another. Your city is small, he says, and not even a
city, but a dry village, and graceless, and inhabited by few. This I have
suffered rather, my good man, than done, if indeed it is a terrible thing; and
if unwilling, I endure being unfortunate (for let it be said thus); but if
willing, I philosophize. Which of these is a charge? Unless one should also
blame the dolphin because it is not of the land, and the ox because it is not
of the sea, and the moray because it is amphibious. But we, he says, have
walls and theatres, and hippodromes, and palaces, and beauties of stoas and
magnitudes, and this unbelievable work, the underground and aerial river, and
this splendid and notable pillar, and a marketplace =36.224= full, and a
people surging, and a praised council of well-born men.
G. And why do you not also speak of the convenience of the site, and land and
sea, as if contending which of them the city would rather belong to, and
greeting the queen with the goods from themselves? This then is our wrong,
that you are great and exceedingly splendid, but we are small and have come
from small things? Many others also do you this wrong, or rather all whom you
surpass; and we must die, because we have not raised a city, nor thrown walls
around ourselves, nor boast in hippodromes, nor in stadia and hunts, and the
madnesses about these things, nor in the charms and splendors of baths, and
costly marbles, and paintings and embroideries shining with gold and of many
kinds, almost imitating nature? But we have not yet broken the sea around
ourselves, nor mixed the seasons (which you clearly have done, the new
creator), that we might live both most pleasantly and most safely together.
Add, if you will, other accusations also, you who say: Mine is the silver, and
mine is the gold, the voices of God. We neither think great thoughts of
wealth, on which, when it flows, our legislation is not to set the heart, nor
do we count yearly and daily revenues, nor are we ambitious with bulk of
table, and the drugs of the senseless belly. For we do not praise any of the
things after the throat as equal in honor, or rather, equally without honor
and to be thrown away; but we live thus simply and from hand to mouth,
differing a little from the beasts, whose life is without apparatus and
without artifice.
H. Or will you also accuse the raggedness of my clothing, and the disposition
of the face as not well-formed? Since even in these I see some of the very
lowly taking pride. And will you leave the head, and not mock the things with
which the children also mocked Elisha? For what follows I will keep silent.
And will you not charge lack of education, or that I seem to you to speak
roughly and rustically? And where will you put not being glib, nor some
jester, and pleasing =36.225= to those who are with me, nor frequenting the
marketplace for the most part, nor talking and chatting with whoever happens
and as it happens, so as to make even the words burdensome, nor visiting the
new Jerusalem, the Zeuxippus, nor exchanging house for house, flattering and
stuffing the belly; but remaining at home for the most part, downcast and
sullen, and in quiet associating with myself, the genuine examiner of the
things done, and perhaps worthy of being bound because of the uselessness? How
do you concede these things to us, and do not charge them? How sweet you are
and humane!
I. But I am so ancient and philosophical as to suppose one heaven, and the
same common to all, and a common circuit of sun and moon, and a common order
and position of stars, and a common equal share and usefulness of day and
night; and still changes of seasons, and rains, and fruits, and the
life-giving power of air; and rivers drawn, the common and abundant wealth
likewise for all; and that there is one and the same earth, mother and tomb,
from which we were taken, and to which we shall be turned back, having nothing
more than one another; and still before these, common reason, law, prophets,
the very sufferings of Christ, through which we were remade, not one yes and
another no, but all who have shared the same Adam, and were deceived by the
serpent, and put to death by sin, and saved again through the heavenly Adam,
and led back to the tree of life, through the tree of dishonor, from which we
had fallen.
J. And I was deceived also by the Armathaim of Samuel, the small fatherland of
the great man, which did not dishonor the prophet, nor became more honorable
from itself than from him; from which he was hindered in nothing, both from
being given to God before birth, and from being called one who sees the things
in front; and not only this, but also from anointing kings and priests, and
judging those from the splendid cities. And concerning Saul I used to hear,
that seeking the asses of his father, he found a kingdom. And David himself is
taken up from the flocks of the sheep, and shepherds Israel. What then of
Amos? Being a goatherd, and scraping sycamore fruit, is he not entrusted with
prophecy? And how did I pass over Joseph, =36.228= who both became a slave,
and a giver of grain to Egypt, and father of many myriads, which were
announced beforehand to Abraham? And Abraham, that I may say the greater
thing, was he not a wanderer? And Moses, was he not first exposed, then a
lawgiver, and general of those pressing toward the land of promise? Are these
not the great and wondrous narratives? I was deceived also by the Carmel of
Elijah, which received the chariot of fire; and the sheepskin of Elisha, which
was able to do more than the threads of silk, and the gold forced into
clothing. I was deceived also by the desert of John, which has the greatest
among those born of women, with that food, and the girdle, and the garment. I
dared something even beyond these: I found God an advocate of my rusticity. I
will be ranked with Bethlehem, I will be dishonored with the manger, because
of which you, dishonoring God, what wonder if from the same cause you also
despise the herald? I will bring before you also the fishermen, and the poor
who proclaim the good news, preferred before many rich. Will you ever cease
being lifted up by cities? Will you ever reverence the wilderness spat upon by
you and without honor? I do not yet say gold born in sands, and translucent
stones, products and gifts of rocks; to which if I should also oppose as many
things as are without honor in cities, perhaps I would not enjoy frankness for
good.
K. But the herald is also a stranger to us and from beyond the borders,
perhaps someone of those very much circumscribed and flesh-loving would say.
And the apostles, were they not strangers of the many nations and cities, into
which they were divided, that the Gospel might run everywhere, that nothing
might be unlit of the threefold light, and unenlightened of the truth, so that
even for those sitting in darkness and the shadow of death the night of
ignorance might be loosed? That we, he says, to the nations, and they to the
circumcision, you have heard Paul saying. Let Judaea be Peter's; what has Paul
in common with the nations, Luke with Achaia, Andrew with Epirus, John with
Ephesus, Thomas with India, Mark with Italy? And what then for all the others,
that I may not speak the things of each, toward those to whom they sojourned?
So that either rebuke those too, or concede to me also, or show that, being an
ambassador of the true word, you are being outraged by the nonsense. And since
I have discussed these things with you in a small-minded way, come, I will
also philosophize more magnificently. =36.229=
L. For all who are high, O you, there is one fatherland, the Jerusalem above,
into which we store up the citizenship. For all, one race, if you wish to look
at the things below, the dust; but if the higher things, the inbreathing, of
which we have partaken, and which we were commanded to keep, and with which I
must stand to give an account of the nobility from above and of the image.
Everyone then is well-born who has guarded this from virtue, and from the
inclination toward the archetype; but everyone is ill-born who has confused it
with vice, and put upon himself another form, that of the serpent. And these
fatherlands below, and these races, have become playthings of our temporary
life and scene. For a fatherland is what each occupied first, either by
playing the tyrant, or by misfortune, of which all alike are strangers and
sojourners, even if for long we play with the names. And a race well-born is
either the one long rich, or the one now puffed up; but ill-born, that of poor
fathers, either through disaster, or through mildness. Since how is it
well-born from above, of which one part now begins, and another is dissolved;
and to some it is not given, and to others it is written? Thus I stand
concerning these things. And because of this, I leave you to think great
thoughts of tombs, or of myths. But I try, as I can, to cleanse myself of the
deceit, that I may either guard the nobility, or call it back.
M. Thus then and for these reasons I, the small one and of a bad fatherland,
have come upon you, and this not willingly, nor self-announced, according to
the many of those now leaping upon the presidencies, but called, and forced,
and having followed fear and Spirit. Or may I be warred against here longer in
vain, and free no one of the error, but may those who pray for the barrenness
of my soul succeed, if the word is false. And since I came, and perhaps not
with paltry authority (that I may boast a little of the things of folly),
which of the insatiable have I imitated? What of the time have I emulated,
although I have such examples, apart from which it is among the hard and rare
things not to be bad? Which Churches have we disputed with you? What money,
although those who are rich in both beyond need, and the others lacking in
both? What imperial decree, spat upon, have we been jealous of? Which of the
rulers have we courted against you? Whose boldness have we denounced? And the
things toward me, which? =36.232= Lord, do not set this sin to them, I said
even then (for I remembered in season the words of Stephen), and now I pray.
Being reviled, we bless; being persecuted, we endure; being blasphemed, we
entreat.
N. And if, because I bear being tyrannized, I do wrong in this, grant me this
wrong; I have also borne being tyrannized by others; and thanks, that I was
charged with mildness as folly. For I reckon thus, using reasonings far higher
than according to you: How small a part are these of the spittings of Christ
and the blows, for whom and through whom the dangers? I do not value all as
worth one of the thorny crown, which uncrowned our victor, through which I
also learn to be crowned by the roughness of life; of one of the reed, through
which the rotten power ceased; of one of the gall, of one vinegar, through
which we were healed of the bitter taste; of one of the long-suffering in the
passion. If he is betrayed with a kiss, he convicts, but does not strike. If
he is suddenly seized, he reproaches, but follows; and if with a sword you cut
the ear of Malchus through zeal, he will be indignant, and will restore it;
and if someone flees in a linen cloth, he will clothe him; and if you ask
Sodomitic fire upon those who lead him, he will not pour it; and if he takes a
robber hanging through vice, he leads him into paradise through kindness. Let
all the things of the lover of men be humane, as also of the sufferings of
Christ, to which what of the greater things could we give, if, God even having
been put to death for us, we ourselves should not concede even the small
things to those like us?
O. And besides I both used to reckon and still reckon these things, and see if
not also very rightly, which I have often also philosophized to you. These
have the houses, we the inhabitant; these the temples, we the God; and to have
become temples of the living God and living, living sacrifices, reasonable
whole-burnt-offerings, perfect victims, gods through the Trinity that is
worshipped. These have peoples, =36.233= we angels; these boldness, we faith;
these threatening, we praying; these striking, we bearing; these gold and
silver, we a word that has been purified. You have made for yourself
two-storied and three-storied (know the words of Scripture), a house fanned,
divided with windows? But these are not yet higher than my faith, and than the
heavens toward which I am borne. Is the flock small for me? But it is not
borne upon cliffs. The fold is narrow for me, except it is unapproachable to
wolves, except it does not receive a robber, nor is it climbed over by thieves
and strangers. I shall see this, I know well, even wider. Many of the present
wolves I must number among sheep, perhaps even among shepherds. This the good
shepherd proclaims as good news to me, for whom I lay down my soul for the
sheep. I do not fear the little flock, for it is easy to see together; because
I know my own, and I am known by my own. Such are they that know God, and are
known by God. My sheep hear my voice, which I heard from the divine oracles,
which I was taught by the holy Fathers, which I taught at every time alike,
not being conformed to the times, and I will not cease teaching, with which I
was born, and with which I depart.
P. These I call by name (for they are not nameless, just as neither are the
stars that are numbered and named), and they follow me, for I nourish them
upon water of rest; and they follow also every such shepherd, whose voice you
see how gladly they heard; but a stranger they will not follow, but will flee
from him, because they already have a distinguishing habit of their own voice
and a stranger's. They will flee Valentinus's cutting of the one into two, not
believing the Creator other than the Good, and the Depth, and the Silence, and
the mythical Aeons, things worthy of depth and of silence that is. They will
flee Marcion's God from elements and numbers; Montanus's wicked and feminine
spirit; Manes's matter with the darkness; Novatus's boastfulness, and the
purity in words; Sabellius's dissolving and confusion, and the, that I may so
speak, swallowing, contracting the three into one, but not defining the one in
three that subsist; Arius and those under Arius, the alienation of natures,
=36.236= and the new Judaism, circumscribing the Godhead by the unbegotten
alone; Photinus's Christ below, and beginning from Mary. But they themselves
worship the Father, and the Son, and the Holy Spirit, one Godhead; God the
Father, God the Son, God, if you are not harsh, the Holy Spirit, one nature in
three properties, intellectual, perfect, subsisting by themselves, divisible
in number, and not divisible in Godhead.
Q. Let everyone who threatens today yield these voices to me; but of the
others let those who wish take possession. The Father does not endure being
deprived of the Son, nor the Son of the Holy Spirit; and he is deprived if
they are at some time, and if they are creatures. For what is created is not
God. Nor do I bear, I too, being deprived of the perfection. One Lord, one
faith, one baptism. If this is canceled for me, from whom shall I have the
second? What do you say, you who drown baptism, or baptize again? Is it
possible to be spiritual without the Spirit? And does he partake of the Spirit
who does not honor the Spirit? And does he honor who is baptized into a
creature and fellow-slave? It is not, not so, you will say many things. I will
not lie to you, unoriginate Father; I will not lie to you, only-begotten Word;
I will not lie to you, the Holy Spirit. I know to whom I confessed, and whom I
renounced, and to whom I joined myself. I do not receive to be taught the
voices of a faithful man, and to learn unfaithful ones; to confess truth, and
to become with the lie; to come down as being perfected, and to go up more
incomplete; to be baptized as one who will live, and to be deadened in the
water, just as the embryos that die in the birth-pangs, and receive death
concurrent with the birth. Why do you make me blessed in the same and
wretched, newly-illumined and unillumined, divine and godless, that I may
shipwreck even the hope of the remaking? The word is short: remember the
confession. Into what were you baptized? Into Father? Well; except, still
Jewish. Into Son? Well; no longer Jewish, but not yet perfect. Into the Holy
Spirit? Excellent; this is perfect. Was it then simply into these, or also
some common name of these? Yes, common. What is this? Clearly that of God.
Into this common =36.237= name believe, and prosper, and reign, and you will
pass from here into the blessedness there. And this is, as it seems to me at
least, the more distinct apprehension of these very ones; to which may we also
arrive, in Christ himself our God, to whom be the glory and the might, with
the unoriginate Father, and life-giving Spirit, now and always, and unto the
ages of ages. Amen.
