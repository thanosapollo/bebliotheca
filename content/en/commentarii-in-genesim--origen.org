OF TOME 1 OF THOSE ON GENESIS. =12.45= For God did not begin to be Father,
being hindered, as men who become fathers, by not yet being able to be
fathers. For if God is always perfect, and the power of his being Father is
present to him, and it is good that he be Father of such a Son, why does he
delay, and deprive himself of the good, and, so to speak, from when he is able
to be Father of a Son? The same, however, must also be said concerning the
Holy Spirit. =12.48= FROM ORIGEN'S ON GENESIS And if it offends anyone,
because of the human craftsmen, not to be able to accept that God constructs
the things that are without ungenerated matter underlying, since neither can a
statue-maker do his own work without bronze, nor a carpenter without wood, nor
a builder without stones: it must be inquired of him concerning the power of
God, whether God, having willed to bring into subsistence whatever he wishes,
his will neither being at a loss nor growing feeble, is able to bring into
subsistence what he wishes. For by the account by which, according to all who
introduce Providence, he by his own account brings into subsistence the
qualities, which were not, as he wishes, for the ordering of the all, by his
ineffable power and wisdom, by this account his will is also sufficient to
make the substance, as much as it needs, come to be. For we will be at a loss
toward those who do not wish these things to be so, whether it does not follow
for them that God has been fortunate, having found the substance ungenerated,
which if the ungenerated had not been laid under for him, he was able to do no
work, but remained not a craftsman, not a father, not a benefactor, not good,
nor any other of the things reasonably said concerning God. And whence also
the measuring of so much of the underlying substance, so as to suffice for the
subsistence of so great a cosmos? For it will be as if =12.49= some providence
older than God that has necessarily laid the matter under God, providing that
the art existing in him not suffer emptiness, there being no substance with
which, being able to associate, he ordered so great a beauty of cosmos. And
whence also has it become receptive of every quality which God wishes, if God
himself has not made for himself so much and such as he wished it to have? At
any rate, hypothetically accepting that the matter is ungenerated, we will say
these things to those who wish this, that if, Providence not laying the
substance under God, it has become such, if Providence were existing, what
more would it have done than the automatic? And if he himself, matter not
existing, wished to construct it, what more would his wisdom and his divinity
have done than that which has come to subsist from the ungenerated? For if it
is found that the same would have come to be by Providence which also existed
without Providence, why will we not also upon the cosmos set aside the
craftsman and the artisan? For just as it is absurd upon the cosmos so
artfully constructed to say that such a thing has come to be without a wise
artisan, so also that the matter, so much and such and so yielding to the
artisan Word of God, has existed ungeneratedly, is equally irrational. To
those, however, who compare, that no craftsman makes without matter, it must
be said that they compare dissimilarly. For Providence lays the matter under
every artisan, coming from a prior art, either human or divine. These things
then will suffice for the present toward those who, because it is said: "And
the earth was invisible and unformed," think that the bodily nature is
ungenerated. OF TOME 3 OF THOSE ON GENESIS. Concerning the luminaries having
come to be for signs, being no others than sun and moon and the stars, it is
among the most necessary things to treat, not only of many nations alien to
the faith of Christ, erring concerning the place of =12.52=


fate by the interweaving of the wandering stars with those in the zodiac, all
things on earth and those concerning each of men, and perhaps even of
irrational animals, being thought by them to occur; but also of many of those
supposed to have believed being distracted, lest perhaps the affairs of men
have been necessitated, and it is impossible to come to be otherwise than as
the stars according to the various configurations bring to pass. And it
follows for those who dogmatize these things to destroy what is in our power
entirely; and therefore also praise and blame and acceptable actions, and
again those that are blameworthy; which if they are so, the things of the
proclaimed judgment of God are gone, and threats toward those who have sinned,
as about to be punished, and honors again toward those who have given
themselves to the better things, and blessednesses; for none of these will any
longer reasonably come to be. And if one should look at the consequences for
himself of the things he dogmatizes, even the faith will be vain, and the
sojourn of Christ accomplishing nothing, and all the economy through law and
prophets, and the labors of apostles for the establishing of the Churches of
God through Christ; unless perhaps even Christ, according to those who dare
so, coming under the necessity of the motion of the stars by having taken on
gene =12.53= sis, has done and suffered all things, not of God and Father of
all having granted him the paradoxical powers, but of the stars. To which
godless and impious accounts it also follows that those who believe are said
to believe in God being led by the stars. And we would ask them what God
wishing made such a cosmos, so that some in it, being men, suffer the things
of women, in no way having become causes to themselves of the licentiousness,
and others, having received the constitution of wild animals, because the
motion of the all has made them such, because God has so ordered the all, give
themselves to most savage and very inhuman deeds, murders and piracies. And
why must we speak of the things that occur among men, and the sins by them
being as many as countless, whom those who stand for these noble accounts,
releasing from every charge, ascribe to God the cause of all things done badly
and blameworthily? And if some of them, as apologizing concerning God, say
that the good one is another, having the origin of none of these, and attach
such things to the craftsman of all, first they will not even be able, as they
wish, to demonstrate that he is just; for how would the Father of so many
things according to them reasonably be styled just? And second, what they will
say concerning themselves must be examined: whether they are subject to the
motion of the stars, or have been freed, and being in life have nothing
operated into themselves from there? For if they say they are subject to the
stars, it is clear that the stars granted them to think this, and the
craftsman will have laid under, through the motion of the all, the account
concerning the God fabricated as higher, which they do not wish; but if they
will answer that they are outside the laws of the craftsman of the things
according to the stars, in order that what is said by them not be an
undemonstrated assertion, let them try to bring us more compellingly, having
set around a difference =12.56= of some mind subject to genesis and fate, and
of another free from these; for it is clear to those who know such people
that, having been required to give an account, they will in no way be able to
give it to them. And in addition to the things said, prayers also are
superfluous, being taken up in vain; for if it has been necessitated that such
and such things come to be, and the stars do them, and nothing can come to be
apart from their interweaving with one another, we unreasonably ask God to
grant us such and such things. And why must one lengthen the account further,
presenting the impiety of the place concerning fate that has been well-trodden
unexamined among the many? For the things said also suffice for a sketch. And
from where, examining the: "Let the luminaries be for signs," we have come to
these things, let us remind ourselves. Those who learn true things about some
matters, either


having become eyewitnesses of the things, declare such and such things
soundly, having seen the affection and the activity of those who have been
affected or have acted, or having heard from those in no way causes of the
things that have come to be announcing, they know such and such things. But
let there now be excepted from the account the being able of those who have
done or suffered, narrating what they have done or suffered, to lead into
knowledge of the things done the one who was not present. If then the one
taught by the one in no way cause of the things coming to be, that such and
such things have come to be or will happen to these, does not distinguish that
the one teaching about something as having come to be or about to be is not in
every way the cause of the thing being such, he will think that the one who
presented that such and such things have come to be, or that such and such
things will be, has done or will do the things about which he teaches; and he
will think this clearly erroneously; as if someone, having come upon a
prophetic book, making clear beforehand the things concerning Judas the
betrayer, should think, having learned the thing about to be, seeing it being
completed, that the book is cause of this something having come to be later,
since from the book he has learned the things about to be done later by Judas;
or again should not suppose the book to be the cause, but the first one who
wrote it, or the one who operated, say God. And just as in the things
prophesied concerning Judas the words themselves being examined show that God
has not become maker of the betrayal of Judas, but has only made clear, having
foreknown the things about to be done from this one's wickedness by his own
cause; so if someone should go deep into the account of God's foreknowing all
things, and into those in which he as it were imprinted the accounts of his
own =12.57= foreknowledge, he would understand that neither the one who has
foreknown is in every way cause of the things foreknown, nor the things that
have received the types of the accounts of the foreknowledge of the one who
has foreknown. That then each of the things about to be, God knows long before
it will come to be, even without Scripture, from the very notion concerning
God, is clear to the one who understands the dignity of the power of the mind
of God. And if it is necessary also to present this from the Scriptures, the
prophecies are full of such examples; and the things according to Susanna
also, of God knowing all things before their genesis, speaking thus: "O God
the eternal, knower of hidden things, who knows all things before their
genesis, you know that these have borne false witness against me." And most
clearly in the third of Kingdoms both the name of one who will be king and
deeds were written, being prophesied many years before they came to be, thus:
"And Jeroboam made a feast in the eighth month, on the fifteenth day of the
month, according to the feast in the land of Judah. And he went up upon the
altar in Bethel, which he made to sacrifice to the calves which he made." Then
after a little: "And behold a man of God came from Judah in a word of the Lord
in Bethel, and Jeroboam stood upon the altar to sacrifice. And he called upon
the altar in a word of the Lord, and said: Altar, thus says the Lord: Behold a
son is born to the house of David, Josiah his name, and he will sacrifice upon
you the priests of the high places who sacrifice upon you, and he will burn
bones of men upon you. And he gave in that day a wonder, which the Lord spoke
saying: Behold the altar is split, and the fat upon it will be poured out."
And after a little it is made clear that the altar also was split, and the fat
was poured out from the altar according to the wonder which the man gave in a
word of the Lord. And in Isaiah, who came to be long before the captivity into
Babylon, after which captivity later at some time Cyrus the king of the
Persians comes to be, having cooperated with the building of the temple, which
came to be in the times of Ezra, these things are prophesied concerning Cyrus
by name: "Thus says the Lord God to my christ Cyrus, whose right hand I have
held to hear nations before him, and I will break the strength of kings; I
will open doors before him, and gates will not be shut; I will go before you,
and I will level mountains; I will crush bronze doors, and I will break iron
bars. And I will give you dark treasures,


hidden, unseen =12.60= things I will open to you, that you may know that I am
the Lord God, who calls your name, God of Israel. For the sake of my servant
Jacob and Israel my chosen I will call you by your name, and I will receive
you"; for clearly also from these it has been made clear that because of the
people whom Cyrus benefited, God, he not knowing the piety according to the
Hebrews, granted him to rule more nations; and it is possible to learn these
things also from the Greeks who have written the things concerning the
prophesied Cyrus. And further also in Daniel, the Babylonians then ruling, to
Nebuchadnezzar are shown the kingdoms that will be after him; and they are
shown through the image: gold of the rule of the Babylonians being named, and
silver of the Persians, and bronze of the Macedonians, and iron of the Romans.
And again in the same prophet the things concerning Darius and Alexander and
the four successors of Alexander the king of the Macedonians, and Ptolemy who
ruled Egypt, the one surnamed Lagi, are prophesied thus: "And behold a he-goat
of goats came from the southwest upon the face of all the earth and the goat
had a horn between the eyes. And he came to the ram that had the horns, which
I saw standing before the Ubal, and he ran toward him before his strength. And
I saw him reaching to the ram, and he was enraged against him, and he struck
the ram, and he crushed both his horns, and there was no strength in the ram
to stand before him, and he threw him upon the earth, and he trampled him; and
there was none delivering the ram from his hand. And the he-goat of the goats
was magnified exceedingly; and in his becoming strong his great horn was
crushed; and there rose up four other horns under him to the four winds of
heaven. And from the one there came out one strong horn, and it was magnified
exceedingly toward the south and the west." And why must one speak of the
prophecies concerning Christ, such as the place of his genesis Bethlehem, and
the place of his rearing Nazareth, and withdrawal into Egypt, and wonders
which he did, and in what manner he was betrayed by Judas the one called to
apostleship? For all these are signs of the foreknowledge of God. But also the
Savior himself, "When," he says, "you see Jerusalem surrounded by armies, then
you will know that her desolation has drawn near." For he foretold the end of
the razing of Jerusalem that later occurred. It having then been demonstrated
to us concerning God's being a foreknower =12.61=, not unseasonably, in order
that we may narrate how the stars come to be for signs, it must be understood
that the stars have been so ordered to be moved, the so-called wandering ones
moving contrary to the unwandering, in order that, taking signs from the
configuration of the stars of all the things coming to be concerning each and
of the general things, they may know, not men (for it is far greater than
according to man to be able in truth to receive from the motion of the stars
the things concerning each of those who at any time act or suffer), but the
powers, which it is necessary for many reasons to know these things, as we
will show according to ability through what follows. And men, having
understood from certain observations, or also from teaching of angels who have
transgressed their own order, having taught some things about these for the
wearing down of our race, have thought that those from whom they think they
take the signs are causes of these things, which the account says they
signify, concerning which also themselves as in an epitome according to
ability we will immediately treat more carefully. These problems then will be
set forth: how, God being a foreknower from eternity concerning the things
supposed to be done by each, what is in our power is preserved; and in what
manner the stars are not productive of the things among men, but significative
only; and that men cannot have the knowledge concerning these things
accurately, but the signs lie with powers better than men. For what the cause
is of God's having made the signs for the knowledge of the powers will be
examined fourth. And so let us see the first, which many of the


Greeks, having been cautious about, thinking that the affairs have been
necessitated, and that what is in our power is in no way preserved, if God
foreknows the things about to be, have dared to take up an impious dogma
rather than to admit that which, as those people say, is glorious concerning
God, but destroys what is in our power, and through this praise and blame, and
the acceptable of the virtues, and the blameworthy of the vices; and they say:
If from eternity God knew that this someone will do injustice, and will do
these injustices, and the knowledge of God is unlying, and he will in every
way be unjust, who will do these injustices, having been foreseen to be such,
and it is impossible that he not do injustice. And if it is impossible that he
not do injustice, his doing injustice has been necessitated, =12.64= and it is
impossible for him to do anything else than what God knew. And if it is
impossible for him to do anything else, and no one not having done impossible
things is blameworthy, we blame the unjust in vain. And from the unjust and
the injustices they come also upon the other sins; then from the contrary also
the things supposed to be right actions; and they say that it follows from
God's having foreknown the things about to be that the things in our power
cannot be preserved. To whom it must be said that God, applying himself at the
beginning of the cosmopoiesis, nothing coming to be without cause, goes
through in his mind each of the things about to be, seeing that, since this
has come to be, this follows; and if this following thing comes to be, this
accompanies, which having come to subsist, this will be; and thus having gone
through until the end of the affairs, he knows the things that will be, not in
every way being to each of the things known the cause of its happening. For
just as if someone seeing a person through ignorance rash, and through the
rashness irrationally setting foot on a slippery road, should also grasp that
he will fall having slipped, does not become cause of that slip to him; so it
must be understood that God, having foreseen of what sort each will be, also
sees the causes of his being such, and that he will sin these things, or will
do right these things; and if it is necessary to say, not the foreknowledge
becoming cause of things coming to be (for God does not touch the one
foreknown as about to sin when he sins =12.65=), but we will say something
more paradoxical, yet true, that the thing about to be is cause of the
foreknowledge concerning it being of such a kind. For it does not come to be
because it has been known, but it has been known because it was going to come
to be. And a distinction is needed. For if someone interprets the "it will in
every way be" thus, as that there is a necessity for the thing foreknown to
come to be, we do not grant it to him; for we will not say, because it has
been foreknown that Judas would become a betrayer, that there was every
necessity for Judas to become a betrayer. At any rate in the prophecies
concerning Judas there are written censures and accusations of Judas,
presenting to everyone his blameworthiness. And blame would not have been
attached to him if he were of necessity a betrayer, and it was not possible
for him to become like the remaining apostles. And see whether these things
are not made clear through the words we will set beside, being thus: "And let
there not be one who is merciful to his orphans, because he did not remember
to do mercy, and he pursued a man poor and needy, and one pierced in heart, to
put to death. And he loved cursing, and it will come to him, and he did not
wish blessing, and it will be far from him." And if someone will narrate the
"it will in every way be" according to its signifying it, saying that these
things will be, but it was also possible to come to be otherwise, this we
concede as true. For it is not possible for God to lie; but it is possible,
concerning the things that can come to be and not come to be, to think the
coming to be of them and the not coming to be. And we will say this more
clearly thus: If it is possible for Judas to be an apostle similarly to Peter,
it is possible for God to think concerning Judas that he will remain an
apostle similarly to Peter. If it is possible for Judas to become a betrayer,
it is possible for God to think concerning him that he will be a betrayer. And
if Judas will be a betrayer, God by his foreknowledge of the two
aforementioned, it being possible for one of them to be, knowing beforehand
the true, will foreknow that Judas will become a betrayer; but that concerning
which the knowledge is, it is possible also to come to be otherwise. And the
knowledge of God would say that It is possible for this one to do this, but
also the


contrary; and both being possible, I know that he will do this; for not as God
would say, It is not possible for this some man to fly, so =12.68= giving an
oracle, say, concerning someone, will he say that It is not possible for this
one to be temperate. For there is in no way a power of flying in the man at
all, but there is a power of being temperate and of being intemperate. Both of
which powers existing, the one not attending to turning and educating words
gives himself to the worst; and to the better the one who has sought the true
and has wished to live according to it. And this one does not seek the true
things, since he inclines toward pleasure; and this one examines concerning
them, having been chosen by the common notions and a protreptic word. And
again this one chooses pleasure, not as not being able to look against it, but
as not struggling; and this one despises it, seeing the unseemly that often is
in it. That however the foreknowledge of God does not put necessity upon those
concerning whom it has grasped, in addition to the things already said this
also will be said, that in many places of the Scriptures God commands the
prophets to preach repentance, not pretending to have known whether those who
have heard will turn, or will remain in their own sins; as in Jeremiah it is
said: "Perhaps they will hear and will repent." For God, not being ignorant
whether they will hear or not, says, "perhaps they will hear and will repent";
but as it were showing the equality of the powers to come to be from the
things said; in order that his foreknowledge, having been announced
beforehand, may not make those who hear fall, presenting an opinion of
necessity, as if it were not upon them to turn, and as if it itself also
become a cause of the sins; or again for those who, from being ignorant of the
good that has been foreknown, are able by struggling and straining against
wickedness to live in virtue, the foreknowledge may become a cause of
slackening, no longer standing vigorously against sin, as if the thing said
beforehand will in every way be. For thus also the foreknowledge of the good
about to be would become as it were an obstacle. At any rate God, economically
arranging all the things according to the cosmos usefully, reasonably also
blinded us toward the things about to be; for the knowledge of them would have
released us from contending against wickedness, and would have turned us,
seeming to have been grasped, toward our becoming more quickly subject to sin,
not having wrestled against it. And at the same time it would also have become
fighting against someone's becoming fair and good that the foreknowledge had
come to this someone, that he will in every way be good. For in addition to
the things we have, there is need also of more vehemence and tension toward
becoming fair and good; and the knowledge having been grasped beforehand, of
his being in every way fair and good, would slacken the exercise. Wherefore
beneficially we do not know whether we will be good or whether we will be
wicked. =12.69= And since we have said that God has blinded us toward the
things about to be, see if we are able thus to make clear a certain saying
being sought from Exodus: "Who made the hard of hearing, and the deaf, and the
seeing, and the blind? Have not I the Lord God?" in order that he may have
made the same one both blind and seeing: seeing toward the things present, but
blind toward the things about to be. For the thing concerning the hard of
hearing and the deaf is not of the present time to narrate. That however very
many of the things not in our power are causes of many of the things in our
power, we too will confess; which not having come to be, I mean those not in
our power, certain of the things in our power would not have been done; but
certain of the things in our power are done following these things that have
come to be before, not in our power, it being possible upon the same things
having come to be before also to do other things besides those we do. And if
someone seeks that what is in our power be released from the all, so that we
not through certain things that have happened to us choose these things, he
has forgotten that he is a part of a cosmos, and is encompassed by a communion
of men and of the surrounding. Moderately then as in an epitome I think it has
been demonstrated that the foreknowledge of God is not necessitating of the
things foreknown in every way. Come then let us contend also concerning the
stars being in no way productive of the things among men, but significative
only. And it is clear that if this some configuration


of the stars should be thought productive of certain of the things coming to
be concerning the man (for let the account now be sought concerning this), the
configuration that has come to be today, say, concerning this one cannot be
understood to have made the things that have passed concerning another or even
concerning others. For everything that makes is older than that which has been
made. But as far as the learnings of those who profess such things =12.72= the
things concerning men are thought to be foretold as older than the
configuration. For they profess, having taken in this some manner the hour of
this man, to grasp how each of the wandering ones is according to a
perpendicular, either of this degree of the sign, or of the minutes in it, and
which star of the zodiac happened to be according to the eastern horizon, and
which according to the western, and which according to midheaven, and which
according to anti-midheaven. And when they have set the stars which they think
have configured themselves, having been configured thus according to the time
of the genesis of so-and-so, at the time of the birth of the one concerning
whom they inquire, they examine not only the things about to be, but also the
things that have passed, and the things before the genesis and the sowing of
the one concerning whom the account is, concerning the father of what sort he
happens to be, rich or poor, whole in body or damaged, better or worse in
character, without possessions or with many possessions; having this action or
this; and the same things also concerning the mother, and concerning older
brothers, if they happen to be. And let us for the present admit that they
grasp the true things in the place, concerning which itself we will later show
that it is not so; we will then ask those who suppose that the affairs of men
have been necessitated by the stars, in what manner today's configuration of
such a kind is able to have made the older things. For if this is impossible,
inasmuch as it is found that the thing concerning the things older than the
time is not true, it is clear that the stars moving thus in heaven have not
made the things that have passed and come to be, before they were thus. And if
this, perhaps the one who admits that they speak truly, having attended to the
things said concerning the things about to be, will say that they speak truly
not by the stars making, but by signifying only. And if someone should allege
that the stars do not make the things that have passed, but that other
configurations have become causes of the knowledge of those things; and that
the present configuration has signified only; but that the things about to be
are made clear from the present configuration =12.73= of the genesis of
so-and-so, let him present the difference, of being able to show from the
stars that these things have been thought true as from things making, and
these as from things signifying only; and not having to give the difference,
they will reasonably agree that none of the things according to men come to be
from the stars, but, as we have said before, if at all they are signified, as
if one did not take from the stars the things that have passed and the things
about to be, but from the mind of God, through some prophetic word. For just
as we have shown before, that it in no way harms the account concerning what
is in our power that God knows the things about to be done by each, so neither
do the signs, which God has ordered for signifying, impede what is in our
power; but similarly to a book containing the things about to be prophetically
the whole heaven is able, being as it were a book of God, to contain the
things about to be. Wherefore in the Prayer of Joseph what is said by Jacob
can be understood thus: "For I read in the tablets of heaven whatever will
happen to you and to your sons." And perhaps also the, "The heaven will be
rolled up as a book," makes clear the accounts contained, significative of the
things about to be, about to be completed, and so to speak about to be
fulfilled, just as the prophecies are said to have been fulfilled by having
come out. And thus the stars will have come to be for signs, according to the
voice that says: "Let them be for signs." And Jeremiah, turning us toward
ourselves, and taking away fear at the things thought to be signified, and
perhaps also supposed to come from there, says:


"Do not fear from the signs of heaven." Let us see also a second argument, how
the stars cannot be productive, but if at all significative. For from as many
geneses as possible it is possible to take the things concerning one man; and
this we say hypothetically, conceding that their science can be taken up by
men. For say concerning this one's being about to suffer this, and being about
to die having fallen among robbers and having been killed, they say it is
possible to take both from his own genesis, and if he happens to have more
brothers, from that of each of them. For they think that the genesis of each
contains a brother about to die by robbers, and similarly also that =12.76= of
the father and that of the mother, and that of the wife, and of his sons, and
of the household slaves, and of the dearest, and perhaps even of the killers
themselves. How then is it possible that the one encompassed in so many
geneses, that this be conceded to them, come to be by the configuration of the
