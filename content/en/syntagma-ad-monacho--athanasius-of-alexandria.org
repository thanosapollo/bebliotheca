* Treatise for Monks
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

From the treatise of Saint Athanasius, archbishop of Alexandria,
which was composed for monks and for all Christians, clergy and
laity alike.
One must guard against being double-tongued, of two minds, a liar, a
slanderer, shame 122 less, arrogant, a wanderer, given to untimely
walks, bringing a rotten word from the lips, or an oath at all, but
be content with yes and with no; and if somewhere necessity arises,
adding "know this," "I speak the truth," "I do not lie"; but the
venerable Name is not to be taken upon an oath, nor any other of the
oaths, according to the divine Gospel, we say; for none of these
things befits Christians, but it even casts out of the Church the
one who is not on guard toward them. Do not strip yourself before
anyone, except when necessity or illness has brought you into a
bath. Do not call a brother raca, nor a fool. Do not take part in
the festivals of the nations. Sorceries [γοητείας μς.] or the
so-called amulets and phylacteries: neither practice them, nor
endure others who wish to work them upon you. Keep the body from
all shamefulness 123 and licentiousness, and do not have a woman
brought in to dwell with you, as some have called beloveds. Do not
hold anything against anyone in your soul, for the prayer of such a
one is unacceptable to God. Do not pray together with heretics or
pagans, nor transgress the fast of Wednesday or Friday, except in
Pentecost and at Epiphany, unless some grave illness constrains;
but break a fast at the arrival of some brother, not this appointed
one, that is Wednesday and Friday and the forty days of the saving
Passion, but the one chosen of one's own will, namely that on
Monday and Tuesday and Thursday; Saturday and Sunday are in no way
to be fasted, except the Great Saturday alone, as it is called; and
the hour of Wednesday and Friday has been appointed to extend until
the ninth [ἐννάτης Cor. 2a manu]; but whatever more you yourself
shall do, and if you are able to use extensions of days, let this
rest with your own purpose. On Sunday, however, or in the season of
Pentecost, one must neither fast nor bend the knee, for the
ordinance of the Church does not permit this; but neither endure
nor be persuaded at all by Marcionites or any other of the heretics
to fast on Saturday or Sunday. Do not neglect the synaxes, and make
yourself worthy of the holy mysteries, lest what is received become
judgment for you instead of sanctification. Do not disdain to wash
the feet of the brethren who have come to you 124, for this
commandment will be required of those who have held themselves
proudly toward it, even if they be bishops, since our Lord himself
first practiced it and, giving the pattern, commanded that it be
imitated. Do not be a lover of money, nor greedy of shameful gain,
nor a lover of possessions, nor a lover of wealth, for it is
impossible for such as these to please God. But neither be given
over to trade at all, although I know certain regions which,
because they neither sow nor pursue any of the crafts, are
compelled to use trade, and even if they be monks they do not
abstain from it; they do this badly, yet from necessity. Besides
these, interest is not to be taken, nor is one to buy in advance so
as to sell at a greater price what was gotten as at a lesser. When
giving for use, take the principal only, without interest. Do not
deal deceitfully before the unerring eyes of God, for if, refusing
to lend while ostensibly being scrupulous about interest,



you bring the one in need to buy your goods at a high price, you
are found disposed toward your brother more greedy and more
inhuman than in the matter of interest. Be humble and quiet, and
let the oracles of God put trembling in you always. Mock no one
among men, nor condemn anyone from his appearance; be care 125
fully watchful about clothing, so as neither to use soft garments
nor to go about in filthy ones; hair is not to be tended at all,
for such a fashion has been cut off by the Apostle; but neither
ought one to shave the beard, nor to use barbers while being a
monk. Abstain from meats [πεχου ms. super rasuram] not as one who
abhors them but as one cutting away the occasions of harm, and as
counting yourself weak and unworthy. Wine is not to be partaken
of, or only to taste and to bless the Maker; but if, as with
Timothy, because of the great strictness of your life the body has
been worn down by illnesses, use a little, for the one is a
medicine of healing, but much is harm to both body and soul. If
possible, go barefoot; but if you wish also to use sandals, let
them be plain, neither ornate nor superfluous. If while ill you
partake of an egg, this will not be sin for you, and if necessity
comes upon you to partake of the flesh of a bird, let this not be
counted as sin, but only grieve insofar as the things of
strictness have been cut away from you, and the tension of the
ascesis has given way. 126 And even when ill, if a necessary need
arises, bathe; for a priest and a monk in health have no need of a
bathhouse. But one must also choose to sleep on the ground, when
the body is in health. Work with your hands, so as not to eat idle
bread, but rather to have from the fruit of your hands, and to
share with brethren and strangers and, above all, with widows and
orphans and the poor. If you dwell among men, and there is a
paternal plot of land for you, work it justly, and of its fruits
first offer firstfruits to the priests, then also to widows and
orphans and the others; share from just labors, not from interest
or excess, nor from trades or extortion and such things. But if
you reside in a monastery, do not insult the ascesis of the
monastery; for if you possess a plot of land while dwelling in a
monastery, this is not withdrawal but mockery. If you have some of
the young about you, first, being humble-minded, do not wish to be
a teacher; but if you see souls being saved through your manner of
life, and you wish to double the five talents given you, have
their souls in care, and while they are young, be austere with
them; and prepare each of them to live apart except 127 at table
and at the synaxis in psalmody; but also keep vigil in due measure
both in psalms and in prayers; and by day pray more frequently
rather than at greater length, neither crying out nor babbling.
Let there be measure also in the psalmody, so that the souls
neither grow weary nor give up. And be eager that the youths be
formed to be silent. These are the commandments for monks and for
devout Christians, all laid down in the divine Scriptures and in
the tradition of the Church, even if for the sake of not speaking
at length we have not set out the testimonies. The priest,
however, ought also to have exactness concerning the things
offered, or rather concerning those who offer; for if he receive
from one of those in military service who has shed blood, or who
has extorted, or who has stolen, or from one of the traders, or
perjurers, or rich men who defraud, or 128 tax-collectors still
holding to the trade, or usurers, or those who inflate the price
of grain, or simply from these sinners, such a one is offering to
God what is lame and blind; but you who are a layman are not
permitted to judge the priest; yet if you see a transgressor, do
not associate with him [explicit].
