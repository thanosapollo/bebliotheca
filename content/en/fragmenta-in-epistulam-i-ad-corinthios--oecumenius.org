1 Cor 2:8
If you receive the rulers of this age as referring to Herod and Pilate, you
will understand what follows more simply; for, he says, if they had known,
they would not have crucified the Lord of glory; for they would not have been
angered by so much envy over something not yet in being. But if you take it as
referring to the chief priests and scribes, who knew that he was the Christ,
and this is clear from what the husbandmen of the vineyard said: this is the
heir, come let us kill him and the vineyard will be ours; but they were
blinded by envy, so you will understand what is below: that if they had known,
he says, that such salvation would come to the nations from the cross, they
would not even have come to crucify him, choosing rather to melt away with
envy than that the nations be saved and he cast them out and put others in
their place. 1 Cor 2:12 He calls the spirit of the world, I think, human
wisdom and education; which, he says, we did not receive. It renders the cross
of no effect, as he says above: not in persuasive words of wisdom, that the
cross not be emptied; but we received the spirit of God, that we might know
the things granted us by God, that is, the things according to the economy of
Christ. 1 Cor 2:16 Or does he call the Father the mind of Christ? We have, he
says, within ourselves the Father of Christ, according to "I will dwell in
them and walk among them"; for it is the custom of the saints to call the
Father mind, as Gregory: 433 and in mind and word and spirit, in the one
consubstantiality and godhead; and again: first he conceives the angelic and
heavenly powers, and the intelligible conception was a work being completed by
the word and perfected by the spirit. 1 Cor 3:1415 Can we not also understand
it thus? Those who built the readily-combustible material have been cast out
altogether; for all their labors were penalized. But he himself will be saved.
He himself, who? The one who built on the gold and silver and precious stones.
For having said about him that he will receive a reward, he now says what kind
of reward: salvation, that is, he will be saved. Yet painfully and he himself,
as is fitting for one passing through fire and being cleansed of the slight
rust that is in him. For God alone is completely without sin, but men, even if
they are most righteous, are not completely pure. For it has been said that we
all stumble in many ways, and no one is clean from defilement, not even if his
life were one day; for it was also said to Malachi, that as fire in a crucible
and as fuller's herb God will wash and cleanse some. 1 Cor 3:16 But if we are
a temple of God, since the Spirit dwells in us, then the Spirit is God. 1 Cor
4:7 Thus someone used to read: but if also you received, why do you boast yet
as one not having received, he said as a question, that it might be understood
thus: why do you boast, then, as if you had not received it but have it of
yourself, even though you have received it? 1 Cor 6:8 As if he said: it is
good neither to wrong nor to be wronged, but in heresy it is better to be
wronged. 1 Cor 6:9 Therefore also he first placed "nor the sexually immoral." 434 1
Cor 6:12 Or still, since they were infants, it was necessary to condescend also
to their infancy and to speak to their infantile ways. 1 Cor 6:14 The
improbability of the word is certified through the power of God, which he did
not place in the resurrection of Christ, for it had already come about, but in
ours, that we not be unbelieving and that he silence those who contradict. 1
Cor 6:16 Or that through the Holy Spirit as intermediary one is joined to
Christ. 1 Cor 6:15-18 Or that the other sins arise also from a passion of the
soul, such as anger in the case of murder, vainglory in those who wish to be
rich; but fornication has the springs of desire from the body itself. He
wished to remove the charge, since the exhortation before him concerns it; for
not always is fornication worse than all. Severianus of the saints composed
many interpretations on this, in homily 57, chapter 19, saying both this same
one with that of John of the saints, and another: that, he says, the fornicator
sins against his own constitution and against that very structure of the human
body, insulting the workshop of our body; and another: that man is body of
Christ and members of Christ; therefore the one who sins against his own body
sins against Christ, our head, of which the body is also a temple. Receive with
all these also this, if you wish: wishing to remove the sin of fornication, he
says something of this kind: that the fornicator is worthy of ten thousand
punishments; for he who does not spare the defilement of his own body, how
would he spare another's? For he who is evil to himself, to whom is he good?
And otherwise: it is written about the man and the woman: the two shall be one
flesh. Therefore the one who is not committed to law and divine marriage but
fornicates, though he ought to hold marriage honorable and the bed undefiled,
insults by his fornication and the uncleanness 435 from it both marriage and the
woman; and the one doing this sins against his own body, sinning against the
woman who is one flesh with the man, and is body of the man since she is his
rib and was taken from him. I think the last is the most beautiful, for it
preserves the meaning of the text uninterrupted. For it is found thus: every
sin which a man commits is outside the body, except when one joins with the
wife in the mixture of drunkenness and impurity, and the fornicator sins
against the wife, filling her with impurity and shamefulness. And of the holy
Athanasius there is also, besides all these, an interpretation in the work On
Solemn Marriage. Besides all these, someone also interpreted thus: writing to
those living in luxury and flesh-lovers, Paul used their own flesh-lust as an
ally for the refusal of fornication, saying this: that the other sins wrong the
soul and send the harm to it alone; but the fornicator wrongs the body along
with the soul, corrupting it and weakening it and stripping away the psychic
and vital tone, as if to say: spare even your own body, which you clothe and
warm and for which you take the greatest care. Interrogatively: for what? Does
not envy melt the body? Why then does he say fornication alone does this? Very
safely spoke the apostle. For he says: every sin which a man commits is outside
the body; but envy does not come from us, but is set in motion toward us. We
therefore suffer it, but we do not make envy; and it is a passion, but not an
activity. But now the apostle's discourse is about activity. For he says: every
sin which a man commits. But not about passion. 1 Cor 6:18b This means: he
makes it itself unclean. 1 Cor 7:56 For not as "you shall not commit adultery,
you shall not murder" is already also "do not defraud one another" a
commandment, and praiseworthy the one doing this as fulfilling a law. He said
it, he says, by way of concession, condescending to your weakness; but from
saying "concession" he showed that the one who abstains only in fasting and
prayer is doing a thing even though condemned. 436 1 Cor 7:18 For it is likely that
the circumcision can return to its former state through some treatment. You
were called, he says, in uncircumcision; remain what you are. For neither does
this contribute to salvation, nor does that injure; for one thing is needed,
to fulfill the commandments. 1 Cor 7:21-22 In the things of Christ, he says,
you are equal to your master; for he is a slave of Christ, while you, he says,
perhaps even have more; for you are the freedman of Christ, for he himself
freed you from every intelligible slavery. Nevertheless from the abundance of
the words he wishes to show the equality of slave and master, since we are all
freedmen of Christ, inasmuch as he freed us from the tyranny of Satan, and
slaves of Christ, inasmuch as he, freeing us from there, led us under his own
kingdom. 1 Cor 7:25-28 Since he comes to a greater philosophy, he fears to
prescribe it purely, fearing the failure of those who pursue it; therefore he
gives it not as a commandment but as advice. He interpreted the things about
virgins more carefully, as taking up the things about this in a separate book,
and there give heed: but I wrote the things that came upon me by the grace of
God. Shown mercy, he says, to be faithful, like a herald and counselor. See
again how he punishes the commandment of necessity: I think, he says. But what
do I think? that it is good to be without marriage on account of the
difficulties in it and the evils of marriage. The weakness of the hearers is
corrected, fearing they cannot bear virginity. He says virgin here not the one
consecrated to God, but the still-unmarried girl; for she who has been
consecrated to God, if she marries, brings in an adulterer to Christ to whom
she is betrothed. Those who marry, he says, will have tribulation because of
the sorrows in marriage. But I, he says, spare you as children and I want you
to be free 437 and free of sorrow; for until then they are in bonds. For you are
bound, he says, to a wife; then they do not have authority even over
themselves, as he says above. 1 Cor 7:29-31 Since he said that they will have
tribulation in the flesh, lest someone say: but also pleasure; he attempts to
cut off the things of pleasure from the contracted season. Therefore, he says,
I say that they will have tribulation, since they are able to have neither
pleasure, in the season being thus contracted and hastening toward
dissolution; for already the kingdom of God has drawn near, and we are commanded
no longer to sojourn toward it. Or that he wishes them to be temperate also in
the mixture. Because of the shortness of the season, all the things now existing
in earnest we ought, he says, to reckon as not being, and having a wife, and
rejoicing, and grieving; for when death is near, what need is there to dwell
among these things and to think that there is marriage and purchases, or to
use the world at all? He calls the very enjoyment of the world, as it were in
luxury and wealth and dominion, misuse, that is, signifying not using it as one
ought. For not for this were we led into the world, that we do these things;
for we were created for good works. Therefore the one who abandons the things
in which he was formed and uses the world otherwise misuses it. 1 Cor 7:32-35
See how he exhorts to virginity by the difference of anxiety: for to be
anxious about the things of the Lord is not anxiety but salvation, whence also
he says above: I want you to be free from anxiety. Divided, that is, not having
the same care, but divided in their devotions. For the virgin, he says, is
anxious about the things of the Lord, that she be holy in body and in spirit;
in body through her purity, in spirit through her intimacy with God and the
indwelling of the Paraclete Spirit. But she is anxious about the things of the
world; for she wishes to please a husband. What are the things of the world?
Perhaps she also wishes to be well-appearing and a keeper at home, and not even
to the one in need stretching out her hand kindly. 438 The discourses about
virginity I have raised, he says, knowing this to be your advantage, not that I
may compel you, both willing and unwilling, to remain virgin; for he calls
necessity a noose. For I do not say this, he says, that I may cast upon you a
noose and necessity, but wishing to bring you to what is comely and
undistractedly attentive to the Lord. 1 Cor 7:36-38 Thus how? Let him do what
he wills, he says, whether he marries her, or keeps her a virgin; for neither
is sin. But the one keeping her a virgin excels more, as he says going forward.
You see how from the outset he admires the one keeping her a virgin, who
stands, he says, steadfast in his heart; clearly that one, the one who,
caving in to the seeming indecency, married, does not stand steadfast in the
work of God. He has, he says, no necessity not to marry her; for if it were
otherwise, neither would it be so admirable not to marry under necessity, for
the work was reckoned to necessity and not to himself; but now his whole
ambition, and a God-beloved choice. He posits a steadfast man, whom the seeming
indecency among men could not shake, in the case of those keeping their
daughters virgin. And that indeed, he says, is good; for it is not sin to
marry, but everything which is not sin is good; but the one not marrying does
something even of achievement, which is better. See how by the comparison he
advises not to marry. 439 1 Cor 7:3940 By law he says, the law about adulterers;
for by this she is held, not to be entangled with another. But having said she
is bound, by the deed he shows that the one not marrying does better; for from
a bond he frees her. For she has been freed both from the first bond and law
and is loosed. But having said she is loosed, he advised her not to marry again
and be bound. Lest you think the thing is necessity, he says: according to my
own judgment; as far as I know, he says. Great humility is the I think, that
is, I suppose, I suspect. 1 Cor 8:2 Or that the one known by God is deemed
worthy of revelation, as he himself says: and heard unutterable words, which it
is not lawful for a man to speak. 1 Cor 9:13 The Levites, he says, who
received the tithes, those working the sacred things; but those attending the
altar he says are the priests, who had portions from the sacrifices. Good, the
division: for some things were also whole burnt offerings and belonged to the
altar alone, and from the sacrificed animals the blood was poured to the altar
and the fat burned as incense, and the priest received certain portions of the
flesh, such as the right arm and the breast and the cheek. 1 Cor 9:15-18
Boasting, he says, is the same shown from one's own ambition, not reckoning the
masterly commandment, as a faithful slave, as his own achievement; for he heard
the master saying: when you have done all things, say we are unprofitable
slaves, for we have done what we ought to do. What then is the boasting that
comes from duty? Or thus: if I do this willingly, placing the gospel free of
charge, I have a reward; but if unwillingly, I do not fail even so. Why
therefore? I have been entrusted with a stewardship. But to those entrusted
with such a stewardship it is permitted to receive; for the Lord commanded
those preaching the gospel to eat from it. But this is the difference of the
willing and the unwilling: in the willing both the judgment consents, bearing
it lightly and rejoicing at what is done, but in the unwilling where he does it
with force and necessity of judgment. 1 Cor 9:24 Do you not know? They were
not, then, apprehending. 440 1 Cor 10:2 But into Moses, he says, since they
imitated him, seeing him entering upon the division of the sea; therefore Moses
became for them the leader of it. 1 Cor 10:5 Or on account of Jesus and Caleb
and their young sons. 1 Cor 10:16 Or what we bless, we prepare; you know what I
say. 1 Cor 10:24 Not only this, he says, seek, whether you eat with a clean
conscience, but whether also what happens benefits your brother. 1 Cor 11:3
Except that also of Christ God is head thus, just as of us Christ, inasmuch as
he is also understood as man. 1 Cor 11:17 The cause, then, of the present
chapter is this: there were among them dinners of the Lord. And it came about
thus: on the day after the participation of the holy mysteries, common meals
were held, the rich preparing them and the poor being invited. This then was a
pretext for love and humility. Since therefore the cause of these goods was
corrupted, he needed to write. 1 Cor 11:23 Or that he was taught these things
from the Lord, that "I received" be in place of "I was taught." 441 1 Cor 11:28 Of
the Lord's supper the discourse was to him, but since he fell into the
discourse about the mysteries in some sequence of discourse, the things before
him are corrected; for this is Paul's habit, to correct all the things that
intervene. 1 Cor 12:9 Or also that of doctrines. 1 Cor 13:12b Or understand
this to be said by him about the Son. 1 Cor 13:13 There are also tongues and
prophecies and knowledges now, even if they are dim; but the "but now remains"
understand, that these three virtues, that is the gifts, are as it were more
enduring than the rest, faith and hope and love; and of these very things again
love is greater, since it is extended also into the time to come. Therefore
these three are more enduring than the rest, as it were of prophecy and of
tongues and of knowledge, since with faith standing down the prophecies and
tongues according to breadth were about to cease, and the knowledge among the
Corinthians was to have an end, being dimmer than the knowledge now present by
the grace of Christ in the world. 1 Cor 14:21 Or also the "neither thus will
they hear me" as said with reference to the common lot, and the "not all will
hear," since some not even one. 1 Cor 14:32 Outrightly or of the soothsayers;
for those after the enthusiasm from them, even unwilling, like madmen, speak
what they do not wish. If therefore the gift is subject to the prophets, how is
it not absurd that you not be subject to the common good, that when there is a
time of silence, you be silent. 442 1 Cor 15:5 We say or it is a scribal error, or
that the Lord, knowing with the foreknowing eye that he would be numbered with
the eleven, appeared also to him, that in this also he not have less than the
other apostles. Something of this kind also John indicates, nowhere saying that
he appeared to the eleven, but discussing Thomas said: Thomas, one of the
twelve; for we would rather say that he appointed Matthias by foreknowledge
with the other apostles, than Judas after the betrayal and the noose. 1 Cor
15:24 For the cause he said it thus. 1 Cor 15:25 Similar David says: and in the
shadow of your wings I will hope, until iniquity pass away. For then it passes
away, when the principalities and powers are rendered ineffective and remain
inoperative. 1 Cor 15:27 And the fear is just; for he was writing to men who
had believed from the Greeks, who glorify Zeus for having rebelled against his
own father and cast him out of the kingdom. There was dread then, lest they
suspect something of the kind also among us. 1 Cor 15:28 For he appropriates
the things of the Son as Father, and whatever the Son is able, this is referred
to the Father; for he himself is the cause to him of being able, the one who
begat him timelessly. 1 Cor 15:32 Or thus: if not for the hope and recompense
to come did I fight with beasts, but for human glory, what do I profit? But he
calls fighting with beasts the contending with the Jews, the contending with
Demetrius the silversmith; for how did these differ from beasts? 443 1 Cor 15:44
But Christ had a spiritual body, having received the whole presence of the Holy
Spirit; and the dove remaining upon him shows it. For inasmuch as the Lord is
also understood as man, he had the activity of the Paraclete, even though the
Spirit is his own, not inasmuch as he is understood as God. Note also that the
Spirit is life-giving. Even though the sequence required to say "living
spirit," he himself said "life-giving." Correspondingly then also the psychic
body, so called, is the one governed by the soul and not by the Holy Spirit. 1
Cor 15:52 Can we not also understand it thus? A little must be said beforehand
of the meaning: the evangelist John says in the Apocalypse that the end comes
upon men not all under one, but part by part upon them by the mercy of God, so
that those left behind of the first perishing, through the fear of those
consumed before them, come to repentance; and he says this doctrine in the
opening of the seventh seal of the book. These things being so, understand the
"in a moment, in the blink of an eye" to have been said in common and to fit
both, so that it be thus: all indeed we shall not sleep, that is, die, in a
moment, in the blink of an eye, because the end comes part by part, according
to the word of the Apocalypse; yet all we shall be changed in a moment, in the
blink of an eye, at the last trumpet; for the first trumpets, being seven
according to the Apocalypse, do not raise the dead, but complete men; but the
last makes both the raising and the change of those already raised, in a moment
and in the blink of an eye, together with the creation; for also the creation
will be freed from the bondage of corruption into the freedom of the glory of
the children of God, as it is said in the Epistle to the Romans.
