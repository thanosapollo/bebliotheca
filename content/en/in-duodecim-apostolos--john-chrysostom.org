On the holy twelve apostles.
=59.495=
It is fitting that today also, at the coming of the Holy Spirit, this net of
the fishermen has been filled, our rational fish leaping into it of their own
accord. For the word of the Word, having made the unlettered wise, and having
given an edge to the slow of tongue with tongues of fire, transferred their
craft to what is more divine, saying, Come after me, and I will make you
fishers of men. He said, Come after me, and at once all things were changed
for the better. Twelve chosen athletes they were, and of these not one
remained uncrowned. For the one grace came with them all and ran with them,
and made each man's victory more proven. Only wretched Judas loosed himself
from such an anchor, pointing out the sheep to the wolves by a kiss, and
selling the Lord for thirty pieces of silver: whose blood bought the world.
But O the blessed and splendid pair of you, apostles! that by grace you have
become all things to all, catching the herds of men skillfully not with a
reed, but with the word. For you are unmoved pillars of orthodoxy, the rock of
the Church, the scepters of the kingdom, and above all watchful protectors of
this flock, unpaid physicians of sick humanity, and compassionate sureties of
our repentance toward God.
For being as it were living anvils, in being struck you forged those who
struck you toward knowledge. Peter from here makes Rome a disciple. Paul from
there evangelizes the world. Andrew sets right the wise of Greece. Simon
teaches God to the barbarians. Thomas by baptism whitens the Ethiopians. Judea
honors the seat of James. Alexandria by the Nile greets the throne of Mark.
Luke and Matthew write the Gospels. John, still theologizing, even after the
end as one living heals Ephesus. Bartholomew trains the Lycaonians to be
sober. Philip, working wonders, saves Hierapolis. All of them, everywhere, do
not cease doing good to all. They have left immortal dust in tombs, now
healers, and shortly sitting as judges of the world.
A lame man drawing near to this one went away sound of foot. A paralytic
meeting that one was commanded to carry his bed. Another called the dead, and
had them obeying at once. Another's mere shadow drove out diseases. Demons
shuddered at another's invocation, and their handkerchiefs dripped healing.
For they were disciples of him whose fringe, stolen by the touch of the woman
with the flow of blood, dried up the red sea of her bloods. But I return in my
word to the chiefs of the apostles, and I remember that prophetic voice:
Behold, what is good, or what is pleasant, but that brothers dwell together?
Let Peter, then, lead the word, since he also was hired first into the
vineyard of the Lord, Peter the firstborn sheep of the good shepherd's
=59.496= flock, Peter who went up from a lake to heaven, who left the sailors
and was counted worthy to speak with angels, who abandoned the ship and
received the Church to govern; who was summoned as keeper of the keys of the
heavens, and received in authority the binding and loosing on earth; who was
swift toward the call, and without hesitation toward the faith, and in all
things a fervent advocate of the Teacher, having uttered, You are the Christ,
the Son of the living God; who had set himself to die together with the
Master, and who went in with the bound Redeemer as far as the court of
Caiaphas; who even in denying is pardonable, and in repenting is wonderful.
For he is not so worthy of blame for the denial as of blessing for the
repentance. For even in falling he is useful to us, because he quickly drowned
the fault with tears, and though shaken for a little, he was found for an age
the foundation of the faithful. For if you consider, beloved, the time of that
timely cowardice, you would not find Peter much liable to blame.
For when the sun fled, heaven was hidden, earth was shaken, rocks were
shattered, unable to bear the outrage against the Creator: then Peter gave
way, then the chief denied; and he found at once the irrational messenger of
the day as the proof of the denial that had been foretold: Before a cock
crows, you will deny me three times. But having denied first, he ran first to
the tomb, he first saw the Giver of life to the dead risen, having learned
from Mary what the angel sitting upon the stone did not dare to tell him. But
let Paul come after him into the midst, the all-wise orator of the inhabited
world, the lamb from a wolf, the cluster from thorns, the wheat from tares,
the friend from an enemy, the Hebrew remaining a Hebrew from among the
Hebrews, who among them was Saul breathing threat and murder, but among us
Paul, called apostle of Jesus Christ, the theologian from a blasphemer, the
evangelist from a persecutor, the helmsman from a pirate, the soldier from a
traitor; who with Peter obtained Rome for burial, and with him did not
overlook this Shechem either; who according to Benjamin is brother of Joseph,
and according to Thecla is both father and teacher of the children; who was
blind before Damascus, and in every city an eye of the preaching; who was
called from heaven, and caught up into heaven; who saw paradise before his
departure from here, and received the crown of victory while still wrestling,
and again, as he says, still owed to him.
For what does he say? Which the Lord will give me on that day. Paul the first
from last, the complete man from an untimely birth, who even while persecuting
was pleasant, and while being persecuted was sweet; the Roman, and Pharisee,
and becoming all things to all through faith. For Christ who said to Peter,
You must suffer, said also concerning Paul how much he must suffer for his
name, so that the one being crucified, the other =59.497= having his head cut
off, having preached one faith, having finished one course, might be true
witnesses of the resurrection of him who suffered and was buried and rose, as
he saw, and reached the bosom of the Father, which he had never left. To them
the Master himself speaks prophetically: Go round this new Zion, and encompass
it; that is, Guard it, wall it, fortify it with prayers, so that when I am
timely angry, and shake the whole, seeing your unending tomb, and the marks
you willingly bear for me, I may conquer wrath with compassion, and anticipate
your embassy through this. For whenever I see priesthood and =59.498= kingship
weeping, I am at once bent toward pity as one who is compassionate, and I
remember that voice of mine: I will defend this city for the sake of David my
servant, and Aaron my holy one; who, having been of one mind in the faith as
you are, and having covered their dignities with voluntary humility, and
having shown the lowliness of the soul by the robe, brought me many myriads of
disciples, bearing me, and asking of me propitiation for the world. For I
said, Ask, and it will be given you; seek, and you will find. To him be the
glory and the power, now and always, unto the ages of ages. Amen.
