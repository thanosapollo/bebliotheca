* Christological Homily on Lazarus
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of our father among the saints and confessor Eustathius, bishop of Antioch, on
the supper of Lazarus and on Mary and Martha his sisters.

1 Let us hear, beloved: even to this point the holy trumpet of the Gospels
evangelizes the Church of God. And what does it add for the joy of the
faithful? For this let us make the crown of the discourse and a guide toward
what follows. John says, the evangelist, the son of thunder, who drew the
unspeakable mysteries from the all-bearing treasury of wisdom, the heart of
the Lord: "Six days before the Passover, Jesus came to Bethany, where Lazarus
was, the one who had died, whom he raised from the dead. They made him a
supper therefore; and Martha served, but Lazarus was one of those reclining
with him."

2 Well did the nursling of the truth bring again into the midst the one who a
little before had been bound in the prison of Hades, who had leapt out and
mocked the bonds of death with death's own trophies, released from the
unwelcome grave-clothes, and, passing by all those eating with him, numbered
this one by name. For the evangelist's aim was not to beautify the one
proclaimed through him by sojourns and feastings among glorious persons, nor
to declare things meant to please, but from his own works to make the
wonder-working God known to all.

3 Now a work of Christ it was, and of works the greatest, the raising of
Lazarus, who had been dissolved and melted into corruption. For this reason he
shows him dining with the Lord, and publishes to the inhabited world the
miracle as no phantom, and makes a feast of the paradox, that of that splendid
feast, in which Lazarus was set forth as an early guest-gift, spiritual food
for the diners; that of that paradoxical supper in which the pleasure of the
belly was checked and the souls of those reclining were feasted; that of that
paradoxical gladness in which the one who had been made a table for corruption
became again, for living men, a table full of wonders.

4 For it was fitting for him who is resourceful in all things and who gushes
forth the good things from his will as from an ever-flowing spring, having
entered under a roof of the poor, to add rich things to the needy, just as
also to those in Cana of Galilee the wine-pouring without tillage, and to
bring that released one opportunely into the midst and, as a paradise of every
banquet, both feasting and being feasted, to open him to his children; in whom
no smell of corruption appeared, for he had partaken, through the divine
calling, of the incorruptible fragrance; nor again was mutilation of members
shown, for the Creator was the one who had refashioned him by his presence;
nor anything small or great which marks alteration in those who are brought
down, but the image was unimpaired, entire from every side and well-formed
even to a hair.

5 Fittingly, therefore, he nourishes that gladness, I mean both from the pure
love of all virtues of those who received him, and from the wonders of the God
who was shown as lacking nothing. For he knew, he knew clearly that this
especially is accustomed to sweeten the divine eye, if the one being
benefited, the Savior .., as he had heard from him that "my food is that I may
do the will of him who sent me," and the will of Father and Son is the
salvation of men. The salvation of men is faith in him who is always
co-glorified with the Father and the Holy Spirit in the highest, as he says in
the Gospels: "this is the will of my Father, that everyone who sees the Son
and believes in him may have eternal life."

6 And longing for our salvation, he has cried elsewhere saying: "I came to
cast fire upon the earth," the one who wills mercy, the one who thirsts to be
thirsted for, the one who has sworn by himself "not to will the death of the
sinner as that he should turn and live"; fire the Savior came to cast upon the
earth which he fashioned with his own hands and sanctified with the divine
inbreathing; fire the Savior came to cast upon it and to rekindle its free
will, with which it was honored by him from the beginning, which the inventor
of evil and deceit took away from it and enslaved in passions of dishonor;
fire the Savior came to cast upon it, that fire which is illuminating,
life-giving, the fire of the knowledge of God, consuming of the thorns of
impiety, increasing of the pious seeds, cutting and uprooting of the things
contrary to nature, and planting and farming of the goods according to nature,
so that just as man was willingly carried off with the deceit and of his own
choice drew upon himself the death previously testified from disobedience, so
again, having washed off the stupor, he may recognize what is due, and may be
made familiar to God through confession and through the inclination toward
him.

7 This divine and illuminating fire the Lord kindled and set ablaze also in
Lazarus toward the salvation of the Jews, furnishing them a perception worthy
of faith unto being saved. For to the unbelieving always the signs are given,
and not to those who believe, as an occasion of salvation. For when they were
contradicting and fighting against the truth, through the God-powered works he
manifested himself to be God incontestably, as he himself says somewhere: "if
you do not believe me, believe my works, that you may know that the Father is
in me and I in the Father"; that is, as a ray of the sun in the sun and as the
sun in a ray, undividedly and unalterably, because of the consubstantial and
the self-powered; and elsewhere: "he who has seen me has seen the Father";
just as in the fruit the tree is recognized; and elsewhere: "I and the Father
are one"; showing forth the equal honor and equal power; but let us return to
the matter at hand.

8 "They made him a supper, he says; and Lazarus was one of those reclining
with him"; very magnificently toward a true confirmation of the unfeigned
resurrection of the one who had been saved. For so also the Lord himself after
his saving resurrection manifested himself to his genuine disciples, taking
and eating before their eyes by the law of the nature of men. For this reason
also now the one who by the Master's command had conquered the law of nature
and of death was dining with the Master; the one raised was rejoicing with the
one who raised him; the clay was reclining with the potter; the one who had
fled the darkness was celebrating with the light; the one who had been leaping
in bonds was rejoicing in courage with the one who unbinds those bound; the
one who in the very reins of death, I mean the grave-clothes, had vigorously
cheated Hades with a rush, was feasting with the Lord who raised him.

9 But I will say to you: open for us, O divine head, John, your holy tablets;
for we are unable to grasp the yawning depth of the miracle. Yes, he says,
Lazarus was reclining with Jesus, the old and the new man, whom he called from
corruption through a living and all-powerful word into life; the newly
fashioned one was reclining as with a nurse, with the one who by command had
shown forth a new birth of men. For it is a custom for the offspring of the
Jews to recline with women in childbed filled with joy. "For the woman when
she gives birth has sorrow because her hour has come; but when she has borne
the child she no longer remembers the affliction because of the joy that a man
has been born into the world."

10 So also our Lord, being about to raise Lazarus from Hades, was weeping, the
pity-loving one, and was moved with compassion, seeing the nature of man being
corrupted and at the same time looking gloomy with those who had come
together, but he rebuked invisibly the death that was holding him, and having
called visibly to the one being held, he performed the great sign of his own
power, having manifested the earnest of the general resurrection. For this
reason he reclined with the one who had been made, rejoicing, as having
confirmed through the works that he himself is "our life and resurrection";
for this too had been proclaimed beforehand by him through the prophet: "I
will rejoice in doing them good."

11 At this miracle and at their own feast the honored female disciples of the
Savior and sisters of the one raised were serving, the one bringing the things
of hospitality, the other the things of the love of God. For Martha was
somehow hospitable as Abraham-like, a lover of the poor as Job, and a lover of
Christ in her confession as Peter, having the loving door of hospitality open
to everyone who comes; whence not secretly, nor in ignorance, as Lot of old,
did she entertain angels, but in good knowledge and disposition she received
under her roof the creator and lord of the angels. And Martha was such and so
great and in such great things.

12 And her sister Mary also, "taking a pound of ointment [of nard] of pure and
costly kind, anointed the feet of Jesus and wiped his feet with her hair, and
the house was filled from the fragrance of the ointment." The holy women,
breathing the ointment of piety, filled the house also with the fragrance of
ointment, Martha through the confession, Mary [through] the work. Martha a
second Peter: for grace breathed also upon her, and for this reason she
herself rained down the faith of the chief.

13 She heard: "he who believes in me, even if he dies, shall live"; she
recognized the giver of life and, using a well-aimed voice, cried out: "I have
believed that you are the Christ, the Son of God, who is coming into the
world." For it was necessary that the female nature recognize and confess the
Creator and God. First Peter confesses, taught by God; second Martha cries
out, initiated into God; third John in tablets theologizes in a God-minded way
and concerning the Only-Begotten. Peter and Martha: "you are the Christ, the
Son of the living God"; John: "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was
with God, and the Word was God. And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us."

14 Attend, beloved, to the illumination of the truth; flee the darkness of
error. "You are, he says, the Christ": not a mere Christ, a man, but Son of
the living God, the Word who became flesh, unchangeable, for us, the only Son
of the living God, the Word who became flesh; not one of the Jewish christs,
not one of the Jewish and interpolated christs, but you, according to Paul,
"the one Lord, the one blessed over all unto the ages of ages, amen"; you
alone are Christ and only-begotten Son of God, of the living; "we beheld, says
the evangelist, his glory, glory as of the only-begotten from the Father, full
of grace and truth";

15 you are the firstborn and the only-begotten and you alone, with the Holy
Spirit, consubstantial and equal in honor and equal in power to the one who
begot you, without beginning and co-eternal with God the Father; you are the
Christ, not one of the invisible and ministering powers created by you, but by
nature Son of the living God. "For not an envoy nor an angel, but the Lord
himself, he says, as Isaiah said, saved us, saved them"; so that the angels
are not christs; you are the Christ, that is, not a man Christ, but God
Christ, God who became man, not a man deified, but one who anointed his own
and voluntary incarnation with his own divinity, for which reason it has also
been said: "your name is ointment poured out," and "we will run to the
fragrance of your ointments"; you are the Christ who is worshipped by all
creation, as one Lord and God, as one Son, as one Master with the Father [and]
the Holy Spirit.

16 Let us bring the discourse back again upon the fairest brotherhood of the
pious and faithful siblings. O the fellow-breathing of the friends and genuine
servants of Christ Jesus, one of the uncreated and consubstantial Trinity. Ah,
the unbreakable cord of the threefold love; nothing tore these from the love
of the teacher and master, not the unbelief of Jews, not the disobedience of
Pharisees, not the barking of Sadducees, not the scandals of priests, but
having been grafted once upon the cultivated olive by faith, they remained
fruitful, unshaken and unmoved by every assault, knowing Christ as God,
confessing the Son of God as God and man undividedly and unconfusedly,
proclaiming Emmanuel;

17 and then Martha said to him: "I have believed that you are the Christ, the
Son of God." This Lazarus confirmed in himself, having been buried together
with this faith and hope; for while he was still living and joining in
supplication to the one present everywhere, his sisters begged on his behalf
saying: "Lord, behold, he whom you love is sick." But Mary, through the things
she was doing, was manifesting her own piety also as in agreement with the
sister's confession and the brother's faith.

18 For taking the ointment, she did not anoint a lifeless stone into a house
of God, as Jacob did to the one who appeared to him when he was fleeing Esau,
but she anointed the living temple, united from the virgin Theotokos to the
uncreated God, creator of all; not only preserving an imitation of the kinship
toward Jacob in such things, as a true daughter, but also surpassing the
patriarch by far more in faith and work. Inasmuch as he was typing the shadow,
but she was handling and worshipping the truth itself.

19 Jacob then appears to have done this twice, both in fleeing the envy
equipped for murder, and in returning after the divine vision at Bethel. But
not even so is he equaled to the piety of the daughter in this part; for he
learned to anoint nothing more than a stone as a type of the cornerstone, but
she not only handled and anointed the holy and living and God-built temple of
the uncreated and eternal God, but also, worshipping the God who is in it
undividedly, with it and through it, she was confessing one undivided Lord,
perceiving in the visible seemliness the invisible glory, and in flesh that
can be handled learning the intangible and unhandleable God the Word.

20 For his conformation toward us did not diminish in their eyes the glory of
the invisible God; the lowliness of his reclining with them did not insult in
their eyes the magnificent authority of God; the weak wrapping of the flesh
did not lessen the criterion of their mind [toward] the transcendence of the
unapproachable; their love, made bold toward his condescension, did not blunt
the fear and trembling of the unbearable power; but being guided from the
appearing signs to the invisible might, they were wondering exceedingly at the
paradox of the voluntary poverty

21, and being illumined in mind from the wonders being accomplished, wonders
beyond word, and being taught the authoritative power of the lordship, they
were shuddering more intensely at the philanthropic incarnation, and seeing
the stream of healings given through the touch of the hem, they were
worshipping as God the one who works unhindered and gushes forth the helps
from his own treasury, and perceiving the resurrection of the dead they were
exceedingly astonished at his sojourning with the mortal, the sojourning of
death.

22 Whence, having mixed fear with affection, they clung to the insatiable
longing, and the like-souled and like-believing women, having divided between
themselves the ministry of the one and the same, by union God [and] perfect
man, the one was serving the human things because of the condescension toward
us, the other was offering the God-pleasing and most fragrant zeal as a
fruit-offering, pouring ointment as upon an altar, upon his life-bearing
temple. And this not incidentally but carefully, kissing it, and with the
wrapping from nature wiping the holy thing, she set a crown of glory upon her
own head, not composed of corruptible and earthly matter, but flashing the
gleamings of the divine brightness.

23 If then according to Moses "everyone who touches the altar shall be
sanctified," what would one say concerning her who touched the Lord himself,
the altar; with what words shall we adorn the one-breath of these siblings?
Yes, their works conquer every praise. It is enough for you, Martha, the
confession of Christ for a crown of glory; it is enough for you, Lazarus, to
be called friend of Christ for an abundance of glory; it is sufficient for
you, Mary, the vote from God. "For Mary, he says, has chosen the good portion,
which shall not be taken from her";

24 Mary the lover of quiet, the bee of her own manner, the one flying along
the meadows of the divine contemplations, who became a sojourner and migrant
from the mixed life and arrived at the untroubled and divine life that befits
the peaceful. "There a thin breeze, and there the Lord" according to the
oracle of Elijah; from which knowledge of divine and human things springs, as
it is written that "be still and know that I am God"; the innocent dove, who
took up her wings gently and came to rest toward the still and blessed harbor
of calm, through which she was initiated into the things desired by prophets
and righteous men. Jacob, having fled a turbulent spear, attained in a timely
moment of God, and anointed a pillar of memorial, as has often been said; and
Mary, having been separated farthest from mixed reasonings and from the flight
of the rabble that somehow roughen the most transparent cell of thought and
mind, heard, unmuddied, the beatitudes of God, and was deemed worthy to grasp
through flesh the ungraspable.

25 But the Jew in blindness leaps upon the discourse and says: do not
blaspheme, Christian, for it is written: "no one shall see my face and live,
says the Lord." Nay rather, O foolish one, in your law it is written that
Jacob said: "I have seen God face to face, and my soul was saved"; for the
seeing of God is life for man. And these things being so, I will neither annul
your word, nor leave my word unattested; for to perceive the essence of God is
impossible and impracticable, but for God to be made known to men is easy for
him who is able to do all things and who manifests himself to the worthy as he
wills, for it is written: "I multiplied visions and was likened in the hands
of the prophets."

26 Jacob therefore saw God, and did not lie, but he saw as the one who
appeared was pleased to be seen; for God knows how to manifest himself [as] he
wills, and there is nothing that hinders him and walls off what is according
to his mind, even if not even the highest have room to bear his glory to the
utmost; so also Mary saw God who had become man without change, and upon his
holy and co-worshipped temple she poured a libation of fragrance, that is, it
upon itself, for the union is undivided and the mystery of piety is unconfused
and the confession toward the one of the Trinity, the Lord Jesus Christ, is
undivided.

27 But you, O beloved, do not be startled hearing something strange and
discordant from those who are strangers to the truth; for they are brothers of
the blind and traitor Judas, who foolishly found fault with such a
God-pleasing purpose at the supper. But what toward this man is the unbribed
vote? He convicts Judas as a thief, but accepts her as a lover of God, and
proclaims what is being done a benefaction and arrays himself against those
who bring charges, and says these things also through Matthew the evangelist:
"why do you cause troubles for the woman? for she has worked a good work
toward me." And I counterbalance to the loving of such work the eternal
memory: "for wherever this gospel of the kingdom is preached in the whole
world, what she has done will also be spoken for a memorial of her."

28 And worthy of the sowing is also the harvest of Mary, worthy of the good
purpose also the after-fruit; she hastened to fulfill the Scripture, she was
taken beforehand by the crowning divine grace; she learned the things sung in
the prophets, she recognized also the timely outcome of the prophecy; she
heard Solomon singing as "where the king is in his reclining, nard gave
fragrance"; and she understood the king of those who reign and lord of those
who lord, who had been foretold by a servant-king;

29 whence, delaying no work, she fulfilled the learning, and having brought to
the one who lacks nothing the things previously oracled, she carried off the
famous and incontrovertible vote; with which may it be that all of us, knowing
and worshipping one and the same Lord, begotten before the ages co-eternally
and without beginning together with the Father, unspeakably and without flux,
from the Father, that is from the uncreated essence, and in the last times
from the immaculate virgin, without change and without confusion, beyond
thought and word, having become flesh, may attain the beatitude from him in
mercy and in the unending life, because to him is the glory with the Father
and the Holy Spirit unto the unaging ages of ages. Amen.
