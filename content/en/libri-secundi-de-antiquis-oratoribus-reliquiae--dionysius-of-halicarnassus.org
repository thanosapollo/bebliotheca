/And he seems to have used the metaphor more tragically right at the beginning, but the same thought to have been said more politically by Demosthenes in that of the False Embassy right at the beginning, having thus: How great, O men of Athens, eagerness and instruction has come to be, and the rest./

/And someone might blame the proem as tragic and excessive and more like an epilogue. scholion ibid. p. 353 r. And see how he began in an epilogue-like way from a throwing-out of advocates, as also Demosthenes./

cf.  /Some think it right to blame the orator for having made a throwing-out of advocates in a contest, when it ought to have been in an epilogue./
