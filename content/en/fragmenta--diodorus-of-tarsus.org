For from the beginning this is a custom for you, to benefit all brothers
in various ways, and to send provisions to many churches throughout every
city; here refreshing the poverty of those in need; and supplying brothers
who are in the mines; through the provisions which you send from the
beginning, you Romans preserving a custom of Romans handed down from the
fathers; which your blessed bishop Soter has not only kept, but also
increased; supplying the abundance that is sent to the saints; and with
blessed words exhorting the brothers who come up, as a loving father his
children. Eusebius brought this in book 4 Hist. c. 23. 180 from the same
letter. And in this same (letter) he also mentions the letter of Clement
to the Corinthians, (the words of Eusebius are marked in an oblique
manner) making clear that from of old from an ancient custom its reading
is done in the church. He says then (Dionysius): Today then we spent the
holy Lord's day, in which we read your letter; which we will always have,
reading it, to be admonished, as also the former one written to us through
Clement. From the same letter.
These things (Valesius emends ταύτῃ) you also through such great
admonition mixed together the planting that came from Peter and Paul of
both Romans and Corinthians. For both, having planted us also into our
Corinth, taught likewise. And likewise also having taught together into
Italy, they bore witness at the same time. Eusebius brought forth this in
book 2 Hist. c. 25. 181 from the same letter. And still the same man also
concerning his own letters (the words of Eusebius are in book 4 Hist. c.
23.) as having been adulterated. He says these things.
For letters, when brothers thought it right that I write, I wrote. And
these the apostles of the devil have filled with tares; taking some things
out, and adding others; for whom the woe lies. It is not wonderful then if
some have also set themselves to adulterate the writings of the Lord
(perhaps one should read τινας), when they have also set themselves upon
those that are not of such a kind (others ἐπιβουλεύκασι). Concerning the
remaining seven letters Eusebius set this in the same place. And first at
least one must speak concerning Dionysius; that he had been entrusted with
the throne of the episcopate of the parish in Corinth; and that of his
godly industry he shared ungrudgingly not only with those under him, but
already also with those in foreign parts; making himself most useful to
all, in the catholic letters which he drafted to the churches. Of which
there is,
THE ONE TO THE LACEDAEMONIANS, catechetical of orthodoxy, and advisory of
peace and unity.
182 AND THE ONE TO THE ATHENIANS, stirring of faith and of the way of life
according to the gospel; those who had neglected which he rebukes, as
having all but stood away from the word, from the time when their
president Publius happened to bear witness in the persecutions then. And
he mentions Quadratus who was appointed their bishop after Publius who
bore witness; testifying besides, that they were gathered together through
his zeal, and received a rekindling of the faith; and he makes clear
besides these things, that also Dionysius the Areopagite, having been
turned to the faith by the apostle Paul according to the things made clear
in the Acts, was first entrusted with the episcopate of the parish in
Athens.

And another letter of his TO THE NICOMEDEANS is current; in which, warring
against the heresy of Marcion, he stands by the canon of the truth.
And having written also to the church sojourning in GORTYNA TOGETHER WITH
THE OTHER PARISHES IN CRETE, he accepts Philip their bishop, inasmuch as
the church under him is witnessed for very many acts of manly virtue; and
he reminds them to guard against the distortion of the heretics.
And having written also to the church sojourning in AMASTRIS TOGETHER WITH
THOSE IN PONTUS, he mentions Bacchylides and Elpistus, as having urged him
to write; and he has set forth 183 interpretations of divine writings;
indicating their bishop by name Palmas; and he exhorts the same people
many things concerning marriage and chastity. And he commands that those
who turn back from any fall whatever, whether of transgression, or even of
heretical error, be received.
With these another letter TO THE CNOSIANS has been counted in, in which he
exhorts Pinytus bishop of the parish not to lay upon the brothers of
necessity a heavy burden concerning chastity, but to take aim at the
weakness of the many.
And still another letter of Dionysius besides these is current, he having
written to CHRYSOPHORA a most faithful sister. Writing the things suitable
to her, he also imparted to her the fitting rational food.
