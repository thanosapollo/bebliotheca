OF OUR FATHER AMONG THE SAINTS ATHANASIUS, HOMILY ON THE MAN BLIND FROM BIRTH.
The sweetness of parents is much sought after by all men, genuine, longed-for,
more precious than all gold. There is in the present life no possession that
is weighed against parents. Gold often perishes; but the affection of parents
does not wither. Silver often rusts; but the birth-pangs of those who begot do
not diminish. Pearls often are destroyed; but the tears of parents stand
before the Lord. Therefore the divine Scripture also, making known the honor
of parents, exhorts the children, crying: "He who speaks evil of father or
mother, let him die the death." But someone may certainly say: And if the
sweetness of parents is much sought after, for what reason did David,
slandering the parents, say: "My father and my mother have forsaken me; but
the Lord has taken me up"? What do you say, O friend, to the present things?
The sweetness of parents is more precious than every creature; but not as
compared with God the Creator of all; there is no creature that can be
compared with the Creator. If David had said: "My father and my mother have
forsaken me," and so-and-so took hold of me, the neighbor, or the kinsman, or
the friend, he would have been confirming something contrary; but if he
declared the Lord more compassionate even than the parents, he both did not
insult the parents, and he glorified God. And for what grace did David accept
the syllables, saying: "My father and my mother have forsaken me; but the Lord
has taken hold of me"? You all know clearly, as many as are lovers of letters,
how the God of all, wishing to succeed Saul, as a worthless man, in the
kingdom, and to accept David, as a friend, into the =28.1004= kingdom, sends
his own servant Samuel to the house of the parents of David, carrying the horn
of the anointing, the Lord saying to Samuel: Go to the house of Jesse, anoint
for me one of his sons as king. And Samuel, as one being sent on a service,
answered back to the Lord, saying: And whom shall I appoint? Tell me the name,
Master, that I may pass over more eagerly to what seems good to you. And the
Lord to Samuel: I do not want you to know the one being appointed, lest you be
slandered as a respecter of persons. You have the horn of the anointing;
wherever the horn shall nod, and the oil shall pool, appoint that one for me
as king. Holding the horn, an impartial umpire, by no means be distressed.
Samuel, having heard these things, and having received the knowledge as from
God, immediately with unhesitating foot ran down to the house of Jesse, saying
to him: The Lord has sent me to appoint one of your sons as king. And
immediately Jesse and his wife brought forward the first son, as bearing the
blessing of the firstborn, and keeping good order among the children. But
Samuel, immediately raising the hand, and being shaped toward the anointing,
and nodding the horn, dismissed the first unanointed, the oil not being
willing by a divine nod to drip. And when he had brought each one of his sons
to him except David, they all returned plain and unanointed. And when these
had returned plain, Jesse answered back to Samuel, saying: What have you done,
O man? You have erred, and you do not know. Either you have erred, or you have
passed by to play with me. You came in by the doors; you were sent elsewhere,
and here you bared the horn; in vain you made us glad, having rumored, and not
having fulfilled the promise. And Samuel to Jesse: Why do you strike with
amazement, old man? You are voting against the one who sent me. Is a prophet
ever to be led astray? If I had come by my own judgment, you do well to doubt;
but if the Knower of hearts sent me, why do you refuse? And Jesse to Samuel:
And on whom then shall I nod? I have brought before you all the beauty of my
children, and nowhere the token of the promise. Did you not rather fail to
know the one who sent you? Were you perhaps sent elsewhere? Did you perhaps
run down to anoint me, the old man? And Samuel to Jesse: Why do you strike me
with amazement, old man? Have you not another son? If you do not have one, I
have erred; but if you have one, I have not been led astray. And Jesse to
Samuel: I do have another son, but nevertheless a small one, cheap, not making
a king. I am even ashamed to bring him forward. And Samuel to him: Do you
array yourself against the Creator? Are you ashamed to bring this one forward?
What then? Does God anoint abundance of flesh? =28.1005= Appoint this one
then; the oil will certainly pool, the horn will nod, I know. Let your house
be glad. What then? Because he is small, does he not make a king? Wait a
little, and you will see how this small one will overthrow the giant tower, I
mean Goliath. Therefore David, having considered this reproach of the parents,
and handing over the paradoxical benefaction of the Lord to divine Scripture,
cried out: "My father and my mother have forsaken me; but the Lord has taken
me up." This thankful voice of the prophet David, then, the man blind from
birth also, having received his sight at a fitting time, will himself say with
a loud voice: "My father and my mother have forsaken me; but the Lord has
taken me up." You all know clearly how this one too was spat upon by the
parents, but was not abandoned by God. And that we may pay the debt, and take
a little rest, that we may again be entrusted with debts: come then, let us at
a fitting time bring forward the man blind from birth. You heard, beloved, on
the former day the evangelist John crying out: "And as he passed by," he says,
"Jesus saw a man sitting who was blind from birth; and his disciples asked
him, saying: Rabbi, who sinned, this man or his parents, that he should be
born blind?" And the Lord to them: "Neither this man sinned, nor his parents;
but that the works of God might be manifested in him. Having said these things
the Lord spat on the ground, and made clay from the spittle, and anointed the
eyes of the blind man." And he sent him to Siloam; and he went and washed; and
having washed he received his sight; and having received his sight he believed
the one who sent him. Hear intelligently. The disciples asked the Lord,
saying: "Teacher, who sinned, this man or his parents, that he should be born
blind?" Just is the questioning of the apostles; from this it is shown that
they approached the Lord Jesus as God. For who knows the hidden things of men,
except the tracker of hearts? Therefore the Lord also did not refuse the
questioning, but immediately made the answer, at once both giving full
assurance to the questioning of the apostles, and confirming the faith that he
is God. For the Lord answering said: "Neither this man sinned, nor his
parents; but that the works of God might be manifested in him"; that is, the
present blindness of this man is not a stumbling of sins, but a fault of
nature. For just as a plant, before it sprouts from the earth, often receives
the harm in the root from an aerial circumstance, so also this man blind from
birth received the maiming in the womb; either the maternal bosom being unfit,
or the paternal seed being cast down of bad humor. But the one able to set
right the nature that has failed will be found to be nothing other than the
Fashioner of natures. Therefore the Lord also declared =28.1008= saying:
"Neither this man sinned, nor his parents, but that the works of God might be
manifested in him." And having said these things he immediately makes known
through the work that he himself is the Fashioner of natures, and that he was
speaking about himself: "that the works of God might be manifested in him";
that is, that I may be made known through him, that I am not a plain man, but
a mighty God. For the Lord was able by the word alone to heal the blind man,
he who by a word made firm the heavens, according to the divine saying that
speaks: "By the word of the Lord the heavens were made firm"; he was able then
by a word to heal this man also who was blind from birth. But he does not do
this, but anoints clay on his eyes, that he may make it manifest to all that
he himself is also the potter of Adam, and through the like filling up the
like. He does not make the procedure strange, lest he scatter the
wonder-working; he anoints clay upon clay, through the like filling up the
like. But someone may certainly say also that for this reason the Lord
anointed the eyes of the blind man with clay, that he might make it manifest
to all that he himself is also the one who molded Adam of earth. And for what
reason did he send him to Siloam? Did he also let Adam down into Siloam? Hear
intelligently. When the Lord molded Adam of clay, then none of the
contradicting Jews was present; since therefore there was no one
contradicting, then the Lord constructs the rational statue more compactly;
but now, since there are many who contradict, he widens the procedure, that he
may strengthen the authority, so that he may prepare even the contradictors
unwillingly to nod to the wonder. For this reason then he sent the clay-bearer
to Siloam to wash, not because the Master of Siloam had need of Siloam; but
that, as the blind man was running, those who see might follow and the one who
does not see might become the guide of those who see, and they who ran down
might themselves become witnesses of the wonder. For when the Lord did this
also, and sent him to Siloam, countless multitudes ran down witnessing to the
wonder. And still the children of the Pharisees were sounding, being
busybodies, and crying out that the wonder-working was a fantasy. What were
they going to do, if he had not widened the wonder-working? And otherwise
again, if you should wish to take Siloam as a type of the baptismal font, you
would not err; since in that all wash off the darkness of ignorance, and
receive the light of piety. And that after so great a widening of the wonder
the more part of the Jews were contradicting the wonder, you heard the
evangelist saying: "But the neighbors, and those who had seen him before, that
he was a beggar, said: Is not this the one who begs, the one blind from birth?
Others said: This is he. But others said: It is not this man, but he is like
him. But the one who had received his sight said that, I am he, whom you say."
=28.1009= Did you see blindness becoming a cause of receiving sight? The blind
man received his sight and those who see were blinded by wickedness. For
wickedness is always blind. Therefore also the children of the Jews from
wickedness were sounding against the man blind from birth, saying to him: Tell
us, O man, for what reason do you champion among us the son of the carpenter,
and want him to be rumored as God? You are not the one blind from birth; we
know that one; we have often supplied him the things for his needs. How then
do you rumor, and say, that He opened my eyes? If you need money, and for this
reason you speak falsely, take as much as you want; only do not hold back from
the truth. They were doing all things, then, that they might somehow scatter
the wonder. But the one who had received his sight said to the contradicting
Jews: Why do you slander me? Why do you choose to blind the sight of my soul?
I have received my sight, I am not being blinded; in vain you throw dust of
blasphemy at me. Another has shown philanthropy, and you answer back. You do
not worship, but you revile. And you do well to address him as son of the
carpenter; for he has refashioned the windows of my eyes. Grant me a little,
if I converse with you after a little. Grant me first to consider the beauty
of heaven, the center of the sun, the light of the moon, the bound of the day,
the measure of the night, the dance of the stars, the course of the clouds,
the straightness of the earth, the hollow of the sea, the pathless passage of
the ships, the circuit of the uneven things, the swellings of the mountains,
the streams of the rivers, the flowing of the springs, the drawings of the
wells, the skippings of the cattle, the variegations of the beasts, the
split-wings of the birds, the peculiarities of the fishes, the heights of the
trees, the sweetmeats of the fruits. Grant me first to behold these and things
like them, and then thus I converse with you, how I received my sight. Long
ago I was born, but now for the first time I have entered into the world by
the part of sight. I heard the prophet crying out: "From the greatness and
beauty of created things the Fashioner of them is beheld analogously." I from
the created things, you from my eyes, analogously behold the Fashioner. Why do
you promise me abundance of money? I do not sell the gift, I do not traffic
the receiving of sight of my eyes. It is better to beg with a good mind, than
to be rich with an evil turn. For the rest I no longer beg; for I have
received the lamp of the body that kindles me toward work. I no longer then
beg, but I farm; I no longer ask a favor, but I demand a wage; I no longer
feel the earth with a staff, but I prune the vine with a sickle; I no longer
gather silver, but I reap handfuls; I no longer tighten the wallet, but I draw
a wagon; I no longer receive fragments of bread, but I bring wheat; I no
longer cry groaning: Give to him who does not have the light, but =28.1012=
with a loud voice I cry: "The Lord is my illumination and my savior, whom
shall I fear?" The contradicting Jews, having heard these things, when they
saw him not only healed, but also advocating for the wonder, still approached
asking him: How did you receive your sight? What did that man do to you, that
you persist in the matter? But the one who had received his sight said to
those who had been blinded: How did I receive my sight? Do you say before God,
how the sequence of nature comes to be? If you ask the how, I too will ask you
in turn: How did he change the water into wine, indicating sequence or
wonder-working? How did he call Lazarus from the tomb, naturally or in a
manner befitting God? How did he release the leper from the passion, having
gone about, or having willed? How did he save the woman with the issue of
blood, shipwrecked from the flow of the bloods, from the surge? Having
searched out the passion, or having considered the faith? But the
contradicting Jews, when they saw him persisting, having seized him more
sharply, dragged him with force to the Pharisaic tribunal, saying to him: Now
we will prepare your flesh to be torn, that you may pursue slavery, and not
teaching. You do not keep silence, but you make a public speech, and you want
him to be rumored as God, that son of Joseph? From some accident you received
your sight, and you address us, as if you were born blind from birth, and you
bring upon us that son of the carpenter? You do not keep silence, but you cry
great things? But the one who had received his sight said to the
contradictors: If you do not want me to cry out, why do you ask me frequently?
You rumor yourselves, and you attach the charge to me. As you say, if from
some accident I received my sight, why have you set my parents beside me? You
are troubled in vain; you cannot hide the wonder; for even if I keep silence,
my eyes cry out. A certain man called Jesus, whose name also is healing,
anointed clay on my eyes, and said to me: "Go, wash in the pool of Siloam."
And having gone I washed; having washed, I put off the clay, and I received
the light. The Pharisaic judges, having heard these things, and an infinite
multitude being gathered; for it was a sabbath, when Jesus had done this
wonder; were asking him the same things as the Jews, saying: You know, O man,
in what place you have stood, and that the present tribunal happens to be
unforgiving, and that it is not possible here for error ever to escape notice.
Tell us with all exactness: How did you receive your sight? What did that man
do to you, whom you speak of? But he, having received his sight, answered to
the Pharisaic tribunal: How I received my sight, do you again busy yourselves?
again do you examine me as a condemned man, saying to me: How did you receive
your sight? If I tell you, you are again troubled. How I received my sight, I
will tell you. The cause of the procedure I tell, but the authority of the
wonder-working I do not have to tell; "For who has known the mind =28.1013= of
the Lord? or who has become his counselor? or who has first given to him, and
it shall not be repaid to him?" If you wish to learn the procedure, this is
it, which I already said before: "He made clay, and anointed my eyes; and he
said to me: Go, wash in the pool of Siloam. And I went, and I washed, and
immediately I received my sight"; not the water having worked, but the spittle
having done the paradox. The Pharisaic judges, having heard these things, when
they saw him still persisting in the same things, having thrust this one away,
called the parents of the one who had received his sight, and were inquiring
of them saying: "Tell us: is this your Son, who is said to have been born
blind? How then does he now see?" But the parents of the one who had received
his sight, answering back to those who asked, said the things of the blessed
Susanna: Strait things for us from every side. If we say that he is our Son,
we shall not escape your hands; for this you have promised; but if we say that
he is not our son, we shall undergo eternal punishment; for this is laid up
for those who hide the truth. Therefore, having given precedence rather to the
eternal punishment, we will say so much as we may not also undergo loss in the
present things: "This child, O rulers, that he is our son, we know, and that
he was born blind we are not ignorant; but how he received his sight, we do
not know; or who opened his eyes, we do not know. He has age, ask him, he
himself will speak concerning himself." The Pharisees, having heard these
things, and being greatly at a loss; for it brought upon them no ordinary
shame that it should be confessed at all, that he is their son, and that he
was born blind; when therefore the Pharisaic judges saw the parents of the
blind man persisting, and saying, that This is our son, and that he was born
blind, being stung at this, since the truth was being confessed, they sent
some of the deceivers from behind to the parents of the blind man, sounding
about their ears, and saying in secret: Deny your child, and do not confess
that he was born blind, that you may have the rulers as friends, and be
counted worthy of many gifts. But the parents of the one who had received his
sight, as parents, answered weeping to those advising vain things: What do you
say, O men? Why do you drag us under? Shall we deny our own child? You want us
to be more savage than beasts. A dragon embraces its own children, and shall
we deny our own child? For he never disobeyed the parents; but even being
blind he used to nourish us, and having received his sight he will much more
tend us in old age. Let it suffice you then; be it so, that we do not wish for
your sake to proclaim the one who treated our child, nor to say with boldness
who this one is. But someone may certainly say: And from where do we have the
knowledge, that through the Pharisaic fear the parents of the one who had
received his sight hid the proclaiming of the Master Christ =28.1016= before
all? From where then is the proof for us, the evangelist John himself
recorded, saying: "These things the parents of the one who had received his
sight said, because they feared the Jews. For the Jews had already agreed,
that if anyone should confess him Christ, he should become put out of the
synagogue." Did you see Pharisaic wickedness? It was necessary for them to
confess the Lord, both to fall down, and to worship the Creator, and still
also to seek the loosing of the passions; but those not only did not confess,
but even scourged those who confessed. Therefore the Lord also, condemning
them, said: "Woe to you, Scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites, because you have
taken away the key of the kingdom of the heavens, and you yourselves do not
enter, nor do you permit those who wish to enter." What then did the Pharisaic
judges? When they saw the parents of the one who had received his sight
answering nothing toward what seemed good to them, having thrust these away
through another door, and having called a second time the child who had
received his sight; for the child was not present, when the Pharisees were
bringing the inquiry to his parents; speaking plausibly they were asking again
the one who had received his sight, saying to him: At least now confess the
truth, and make it manifest to all, that you are not the one blind from birth;
for already being present you said you were of those parents, and behold we
have heard from them, that you are not their son, nor did they have a child
blind from birth. How then do you persist saying, that A certain man called
Jesus anointed clay upon my eyes, and I washed, and I see? The one who had
received his sight, having heard these things, and having recognized their
wickedness, answered back to them saying: I marvel, if you speak truly saying
these things. Would the parents have denied their own child, and especially a
mother a male, or a father not having been provoked? What is said appears to
me very unbelievable, and unacceptable to my hearings. But if this has
happened, it is nothing marvelous; this belongs as proper to human nature,
therefore they will also be counted worthy of pardon, as having done this by
fear. You the scribes deny God, and you are disposed as doing nothing
worthless. And the word is about my parents; what you do toward God, this you
slander the parents as doing toward me. Did not the Lord cry out concerning
you from many times through the prophet Isaiah saying: "I have begotten sons,
and raised them up; but they have set me at nought"? even if to deny is dear
to you, nevertheless I do not endure to do this. I confess also the one who
healed me, and I recognize also those who begot me; for even if my parents
denied me, even so I do not doubt. I know, I know clearly David crying: "My
father and my mother have forsaken me, but the Lord has taken me up." But the
Pharisees, having been stung, said to him: What do you say, vain-minded one,
do you compare yourself to David? and do you confess the son of the carpenter
as Lord, who has not =28.1017= where to lay his head? Let it lie, that you
received your sight; hymn God. Why do you give thanks to this one? This one is
not from God; if he were from God, he would not have loosed the sabbath. "Give
glory to God; for we know that this man is a sinner." But the one who had
received his sight, having heard that they call the Lord a sinner, his soul
having grown dizzy, and having stopped his hearings, answers back to the
Pharisees saying: What do you say? Is he a sinner, the light-giver of my eyes?
Why do you mix honey with bitterness? In the same you command me both to
glorify and to blaspheme. To glorify God is good, but to say sinner is an
attempt of a darkened mind. If he is a sinner, show me an appearance of sins
in him, and I am persuaded by your teaching; either you show an appearance of
sin in him, or I have pledges of divine wonder-working existing in him. I
leave aside the things concerning myself, and for the rest I make the
questioning from the others: Is he a sinner, as you say? Did not this one by a
word raise the son of the widow being carried out dead? Was it the work of a
sinful man, or of God the Word? Did he not of the ten lepers, cleansing by a
word at once the leopard-like hide spotted by the passion, transfer it to the
single human-formed appearance? Was it the work of a sinful man, or of God the
Word? Did he not kingly release from the tyranny the man being troubled by a
legion of demons, who could neither be held, nor received a house, but was
lodging in the tombs? Was it the work of a sinful man, or of God the Word? Do
you want me to be more ungrateful than the demons? For the demons, even being
scourged by him, call him Son of God; and shall I who have been healed by him
address him as a sinner? He works as God, and he is rumored as a sinner? "If
he is a sinner, I do not know; but one thing I know, that being blind, I now
see." And this is the work of God, and not of a sinner. The Pharisees, having
heard these things, when they saw themselves for a long time full of shame,
answered to the one who had received his sight, saying: You ought indeed as a
condemned man to have been scourged, and to have been punished according to
the laws; but that we may not be slandered by the vain-minded as envious, we
accommodate ourselves to your habit; therefore answer to the questioning what
that one did to you; how did you receive your sight? Tell, what have you
suffered? But the one who had received his sight, having taken courage, for he
was advancing in the faith, answered back saying: Again you ask me, how I
received my sight? You act against yourselves, and you do not know. As much as
you wish to quench the wonder, so much more it is made bright. Again you ask
me, how I received my sight, already a third time I have told you, and you
have not believed me. "Why do you wish to hear again? Do you also wish to
become his disciples?" Is it not for this that you search out the procedure
with exactness? If you wish this, for the rest come to him without hesitation
=28.1020=, and be enlightened; know him without doubt; he does not turn away
the one who approaches him with faith. The Pharisees, having heard these
things, reviled the one who had received his sight, saying to him: "You are a
disciple of that one; for we are disciples of Moses. We know that God has
spoken to Moses; but this one we do not know, from where he is. You are a
disciple of that one." But the one who had received his sight, smiling a
little, answered saying: Am I a disciple of that one? You have spoken well,
having reviled me. O glorious insult! You have rather glorified me, and you do
not know. Are you disciples of Moses? And what work of power have you shown?
You are disciples of Moses; but the apostles are disciples of my physician.
You too, the disciples of Moses, show something sometime against the disciples
of my physician. If you have any power at all, I do not say the dead, wake
those who sleep, as the apostles the dead. If you can be equaled to the
apostles, I do not say upon water, walk upon earth, as Peter upon the sea. If
you have any power, contend with the apostles, I do not say spirits, scare the
birds, as the apostles the demons. Tell me: Are you disciples of Moses? If you
are disciples of Moses, you would have obeyed Moses saying concerning him:
"That the Lord God will raise up for you a prophet from your brethren, as me.
You shall hear him in all things, whatever he shall speak to you. And it shall
be, every soul that shall not hear that prophet, shall be utterly destroyed."
See that you be not utterly destroyed, and scattered into all the nations, as
not knowing the power of the one prophesied. Do you not know who is the one
who treated my eyes? You do not know, since you seek him toward murder. But
you both know who he is, and from where he is. For when you were asked by
Herod, where the Christ is born, you announced both the place and the manner
with a straight shot, saying that he is born in Bethlehem. When he is sought
toward slaughter, you know; but when he is displayed toward wonder-working,
you do not know. But someone may certainly say, And from where do we have the
knowledge, that the blind man who received his sight had so great a defense to
make toward the Pharisaic judges? What do you say, O friend? Iron having
conversed with fire is wholly made fiery, and having been tempered cuts most
sharply; and this one, having been counted worthy of the Master's hands, and
of the divine spittle, did he not have unanswerable things to sophistize
against the lawless rhetoricians? Was it not concerning this one and such as
these that the divine Scripture said: "The Lord makes the blind wise"? It is
timely also to address this one who paradoxically received his sight, and
genuinely believed the Lord, and was strongly empowered against the Jews, as a
rational, obedient, much-milked sheep. For he recognized the chief shepherd
Christ, he leapt away from the Pharisaic wolves; he entered through the
cruciform door into the ecclesiastical fold. You heard just now the Lord
saying: "Amen, amen I say to you, he who does not enter through the door
=28.1021= into the court of the sheep, but climbs up from elsewhere, is a
thief and a robber." The Lord addressed himself as a door; and indeed so that
some, having heard door, may not think that he is speaking simply about a
plain door, he adds the interpretation, that he may strengthen the teaching;
for immediately adding he said: "Amen, amen I say to you: I am the door of the
sheep; through me if anyone enters and goes out, he will find pasture." Well
did the Lord address himself as a door, as guarding those within of the faith,
as scaring away those peering about outside, and leading in to the converse of
the master of the house, the Father. For no one knows the Father except
through the Son, according to him who says: "No one knows the Father except
the Son, and he to whom the Son may wish to reveal." I am the door of the
sheep. Well did the Lord address himself as a door, and not as doors; for one
is the Father, and one is the Son, and one the Holy Spirit, and not three
gods; but one door of knowledge. And who is witness of this? Paul crying: "One
Lord, one faith, one baptism." Nowhere, not two, not three, not four, not
five, but everywhere the one, since also one door, one Lord, undivided
essence; one faith, the unstepped doxology, one baptism, and unadulterated
illumination; he who has this knowledge enters through the door, and embraces
the door of Christ, and is not separated from the door. The Lord just now
addressed us as his own sheep without flattery; his own sheep, as multiplying
wool by love of the poor; as making abundant the milk of the nourishing word;
as being made green with the body of the Lord; as being persuaded by the
cruciform staff; as being gathered by the triple-plaited chain of the faith;
as not deserting, but leaning upon the cornerstone Christ. The Lord addressed
himself as a door; and well a door; for he has two doorposts, the Old and New
Testament; a way and a lintel, circumcision and uncircumcision; a house the
church; a table the altar; bread the lamb; a mixing-bowl the mystical wine;
gold the unadulterated faith; silver the bright way of life; pearls the divine
dogmas; a cloak the robe of baptism. You heard just now him saying: "I am the
Good Shepherd; the good shepherd lays down his soul for the sheep." The Lord
was hinting at himself; for what he was about to do, he was saying beforehand;
for no one accepted death for the sheep except the Lord alone. He alone
accepted death for the rational sheep, not involuntary, but voluntary; a death
not damaging, but profitable; a death not receptive of corruption, but a
supplier of incorruption =28.1024=; a death contemplated by faith, not busied
about; a death not having come to be according to the likeness of death, but
found to be the death of death itself. For if the Lord had not accepted the
voluntary death, the devil would not have undergone death. Therefore Paul also
crying said: "Death has been swallowed up into victory. Where, O death, is
your sting? Where, O Hades, is your victory?" but having sealed the word here,
let us give thanks to God, to whom be the glory and the power unto the ages of
the ages. Amen.
