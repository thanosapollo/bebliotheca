make known to you the Gospel," he says, "which was preached by me, that it is

"Even if we have known Christ according to the flesh, yet now we know him no

become flesh, who did not disdain the birth from a woman for our sake? {B}
Most certainly. For I will remember the blessed Gabriel saying to the holy

Fear not, Mary; for behold you shall conceive in the womb and bear a son, and
you shall call his name Jesus. And I think this name was given as new to the
Word from the Father through the voice of the angel. For a prophetic oracle
had also cried out beforehand thus: "And they shall call his name the new
which the Lord shall name him." {A} When therefore the Son and Only-Begotten,
coeternal with the Father and before every age, became man in the last times
of the age, and was born through a woman, and was appointed Son, and was also
named firstborn, and came to be among many brethren, then also the Father by
nature defines the name, 700 following, so to speak, the laws of fatherhood.
For you would gladden us most excellently if you understood the manner of the
economy. {B} He is then the same both Only-Begotten and Firstborn. {A} Do not
suppose for yourself that it is otherwise than thus, my friend. For the
Only-Begotten as God is firstborn among us according to an economic union, and
among many brethren as man, in order that we too as in him and through him may
be sons of God both naturally and according to grace. Naturally indeed as in
him and in him alone, but by participation and according to grace we through
him in the Spirit. As therefore the only-begotten became proper to the
humanity in Christ because of being united to the Word according to an
economic concurrence, so being among many brethren and the firstborn became
proper to the Word because of being united to flesh. Having his being God
firmly established, and always being better than change, he does not depart
from his own glory. Therefore even the holy and all-blessed multitude of the
spirits above has been commanded to worship him with us. For it was surely
even very likely that they would refuse the worship, and hesitate to honor
what they saw as the meanness of the humanity, and would not deem worthy of
doxology him who <became> like us for our sake, withdrawing as far as possible
from seeming to have gone astray. For the mystery concerning Christ was still
unseen even to them, but the Spirit was unveiling it to them, and was not
permitting the sanctified to be impious.

Therefore the divine Paul says: "And when he brings the Firstborn into the
inhabited world, he says: And let all the angels of God worship him." For he
who by natural property excelled the whole inhabited world, and exists outside
it as God, has entered into it, appearing as a part of the world as man, yet
not for this reason did he slip from the divine glory. For he is worshipped as
Only-Begotten even if he should be called firstborn, which is clearly most
fitting to the measures of the humanity. {B} Shall we then worship Emmanuel as
a man? {A} God forbid. This is already nonsense and deceit and wandering. And
we shall differ in nothing from those who have served the creation rather than
the Creator and Maker, who exchanged the truth of God for the lie, according
to what is written, with whom if we should think kindred things, we shall
certainly also hear together: "Claiming to be wise they became fools and
exchanged the glory of the incorruptible God for the likeness of an image of
corruptible man and of birds and of four-footed things and of reptiles." 701
For shall not we ourselves also be both co-workers and of like mind with those
mentioned, exchanging the glory of God in the lie for the likeness of an image
of corruptible man, if we offer the worship to Emmanuel as to a mere man and
one of those like us? {B} Most certainly. {A} What then? Will not even the
multitude of the angels above be ranked with those who have come to this
madness? {B} Necessarily. {A} And I think we shall put upon even the herd of
the nations the inerasable writing of having gone astray, and the harm of the
ancient charges will be hard to wash away. For it wanders, I think, even now
and no less than of old, and has not known the path that goes straight. And
superfluous, as it seems, is the blessed Paul addressing them and saying: "But
then indeed not knowing God, you served


those who are not by nature gods. But now having known God, or rather having
been known by God, how do you turn again to the weak and poor elements, to
which you wish to be enslaved again from the beginning?" For what God have
they still known if Christ, in whom they have believed, is not God by nature?
And if they have served a man, they are held in the snares of the ancient
wandering. Or is what I say not true? {B} Most certainly. {A} Consider then
how from necessary syllogisms already we are all but unwillingly pressed
toward the necessity of worshipping the Son as God by nature, even in the form
that has appeared like us, the concurrence into unity being not insufficient
to abolish perhaps that which sometimes grieves toward a suspicion of
humanity. {B} How did you mean? {A} For the nature of the Word, having taken
on the human, will not be mere humanity, but rather overcoming by its own
glory that which was assumed, is preserved in unshaken continuance of the
God-befitting splendour. Thinking these things the disciples worshipped
saying: "Truly you are the Son of God," even though they saw him going on foot
and in flesh like us; for he was running strangely as God on the crest of a
wave. {B} And who was it who said to the woman in Samaria: "You worship what
you do not know, we worship what we know"? Then how will he who is ranked with
those who worship be worshipful? {A} And indeed, my friend, this little word
"Who" you keep crying out most often, I know not how invented for the
unintelligent. 702 For Christ is in no way divided. And he who was speaking to
the woman was the one and only Lord Jesus Christ, composed ineffably of the
worshipping humanity and of the worshipped divinity, just as one might say
also otherwise concerning him. For inasmuch as he is God, he would be thought
to be himself the Lord of glory, but inasmuch as he became the man who is
glorified by participation from God, he also needed glory, saying: "Father,
glorify your Son." But "There is one Lord, one faith, one baptism," according
to what is written. As therefore there is one faith, that in Christ, and one
baptism truly, although we are baptized and have believed in Father and Son
and Holy Spirit, in the same manner and account, I think, there is one worship
of Father and of the incarnate Son and of the Holy Spirit. For the
Only-Begotten will in no way be driven out from needing to be worshipped both
by us ourselves and by the holy angels, even if he became flesh and
tabernacled among us, and was named firstborn among many brethren. Since what
would then be the account of the faith in him, how is it not worth seeing? For
I do not think those who would choose to think rightly would say that we have
believed only in the Word born from God, stripping him of the flesh. Nor again
shall we omit to say that; for it shall be said. {B} What do you mean? {A} For
the faith is not practiced as toward one of those like us, nor indeed as
toward a man, but <as> toward God by nature and truly in the person of Christ.
And the wise Paul writing will also help the argument: "For we do not preach
ourselves, but Christ Jesus as Lord. And ourselves your servants through
Christ, because the God who said light shall shine out of darkness, who shone
in our hearts toward the illumination of the knowledge of his glory in the
person of <Jesus> Christ." See clearly and evidently the illumination of the
knowledge of God and Father shone in the person of Christ. Therefore he also
said: "He who has seen me has seen the Father, I and the Father are one." And
the divine character is not bodily, but in power and in the most God-befitting
glory.

And this was unmixed in Christ; and he thought it right to be known also
through these things, and through the superiority of the things done he wished
those who heard to leap up to thoughts concerning him, the seen flesh quietly
diminishing him. For "if I do not do the works of my Father 703," he says, "do
not believe me. But if I do, even if you do not believe me, believe


my works." And I think Christ said these things at that time knowing the
matter not unprofitable for benefit. For since they thought that he who had
become man for us was not God by nature, but simply a mere man like us, some
also made the faith in him unacceptable for this reason. Necessarily cutting
away the fear and the hesitation about these things, assigning the faith to
the nature of the divinity, as in the person of the Father, and not allotting
it to the meanness like us, he said: "He who believes in me does not believe
in me, but in him who sent me, and he who sees me sees him who sent me." Is it
not equal to saying: O you who hear my words, do not glorify the things
concerning me as small and thrown to the ground; but know that, putting forth
the faith toward me myself who am seen in flesh, you will not have believed
simply in a man, but in the Father himself, through me the Son who is equal
and indistinguishably the same in every respect whatever, having been made
flesh for you, and appropriating a mean wrapping, the humanity, yet having the
equal-natured and equal-working, and in identity of glory altogether
unimpaired toward him? {B} It seems so. {A} And you might learn also otherwise
that he does not thrust away the faith, but without any cutting and difference
has received it as in his own person even if he became flesh. For when he
healed the man born blind, and implanted in him the sweet, though
unaccustomed, light, he was reasonably marvelled at by all. But he who had
been freed from the suffering was being judged among the Jews, and was
confessing the physician. And Christ meeting him said, "Do you believe in the
Son of God?" And when he cried out "Who is he, Lord, that I may believe in
him?" he answered saying: "You have both seen him, and he who is speaking with
you is that one." And he said: "I believe, Lord." And he worshipped him.

And yet how is it not apparent to all that the divine and highest nature is
altogether unseen? For "no one has ever seen God," according to what is
written. If then the Word from God the Father, distinguishing the human from
himself, as having appeared as this very thing, naked and alone, thought it
right to be believed in, why did he not rather command the one who had been
well treated to consider the nature of God, whatever it is, but displayed it
bodily, so as even 704 to behold it present with the very eyes? For he said
that "You have both seen him, and he who is speaking with you is that one." Or
shall we not say that he displayed the flesh? {B} Most certainly. {A} Then how
would he himself thereafter be the flesh, unless he were thought according to
union, as himself being his own, just as indeed it would also be in our case?
For one might point out not in a divided way nor incompletely the man like us,
I mean the one from soul and body, even from his flesh alone. {B} I agree; for
you speak rightly. {A} And the wise John has written somewhere for us that
"Jesus then also did many other signs before his disciples which are not
written in this book. But these have been written that you may believe that
Jesus is the Christ, the Son of the living God, and that believing you may
have life in his name." And one might be no less astonished at the divine
Peter addressing the Jews clearly and openly: "Rulers of the people and
elders, if we are examined today concerning a benefit to a weak man, by what
this man has been saved, let it be known to all of you and to all the people
of Israel that in the name of Jesus Christ the Nazarene, whom you crucified,
whom God raised from the dead, in this this man stands before you healthy."

And after other things again: "And there is salvation in no other. For there
is no other name under heaven given among men in which we must be saved." Who
then is he who both underwent death and has been raised in glory and is from
Nazareth, if not Jesus Christ, that is, he who before every age was ineffably


begotten from the Father, but in the last time that has also come to the end
of the age also bodily from a woman? He therefore who admits the faith in him
will carry off the exceptional prize; for he shall be called a son of God:
"For as many as received him, he gave them authority," he says, "to become
children of God, who were born not of bloods, nor of the will of flesh, nor of
the will of a man, but of God." For that he himself might become first in all
things, as it is written, he has been born through a woman. And since he is
firstfruits of the creation being reshaped through sanctification toward God,
and before the others he himself was shown begotten of the Spirit, leaping
over the union of man and woman, and not condemning the nature with dishonor
and blame (for marriage is honorable, and he who fashioned from the beginning
705 made them male and female), but already somehow allotting the human things
to the greater and incomparably superior. For he wished us still to be named
begotten of the Spirit, not of men. And indeed, he says, "Call no one your
father on the earth; for one is your Father, the heavenly, and you are all
brothers." Therefore, to believe in him is altogether blameless, or rather it
has even been honored with the amnesty of transgressions. For the excellent
Paul writes thus again: "And knowing that a man is not justified from works of
law, except through faith of Jesus Christ, we also believed in Christ Jesus."
And I shall in no way hesitate to say again, circling back, what I already
said before, that Christ Jesus is not thought as the Word from God naked and
by himself, but when he took on the human and was entangled with flesh. Being
thus and being seen, and being in the form like us, the Father displayed him
to the holy apostles, a voice of this sort having been made from above: "This
is my beloved Son in whom I am well pleased, hear him." Understand then how he
does not say, in this is my Son, lest he be thought in part as another in
another perhaps, but one and the same according to economic union. And to
resist is a fault, and of the most slippery things, for John writing
will persuade thus: "That this is the witness of God, that he has witnessed
concerning his Son; he who believes in the Son of God has the witness of God
in himself."

He who does not believe God has made him a liar, because he has not believed
in the witness which God has witnessed concerning his Son. And he has
witnessed that this one, he who is with flesh and in the form of a servant, is
uniquely and properly my true Son. What then? Shall we not also grant that the
glorious grace through holy baptism, and the life-giving in it, and the
participation of God through sanctification in the Spirit has been
accomplished through Jesus Christ? {B} Necessarily. For I will remember John
saying: "He who comes after me is stronger than I, whose sandals I am not
sufficient to carry. He will baptize you in the Holy Spirit and fire." {A}
Shall we then, companion, say that to be able to baptize in the Holy Spirit
and fire is a work of the humanity like us? {B} And how could this be? 706 {A}
And indeed, speaking of a man who was about to be present and to be seen, he
said that he himself baptizes in fire and Holy Spirit, not putting into those
being baptized an alien Spirit in a slavish and ministerial way, but as God by

you put on Christ, he says. {B} Have we then been baptized into a man, and

You are laying our hope down to earth. For we have been baptized not into a
mere man, but into God become man, who remits from penalty and from the
ancient charges those who have received the faith in him. And indeed the
divine Peter said addressing the Jews: "Repent therefore, and let each of you

you shall receive the gift of the Holy Spirit." For releasing from sins the
one who is attached to him, he then anoints him with his own Spirit, which he
himself as Word from God the Father puts in, and which wells up for us from
his own nature, making the matter as it were common to the economy with flesh
because of the union, and as man he breathed it bodily. For he breathed on the
apostles saying: "Receive Holy Spirit," and he does not give the Spirit by
measure, according to the voice of John, but he himself puts it in from
himself just as indeed also the Father. And indeed the divine Paul, having set
aside the whole difference in this, is seen sometimes allotting it to God and
Father, and sometimes again to the Son.

For he writes thus: "But you are not in flesh, but in Spirit, if indeed the
Spirit of God dwells in you. But if anyone does not have the Spirit of Christ,
this one is not his. But if Christ is in you, the body is dead because of sin,
but the spirit is life because of righteousness." 707 {B} It is then fixed
that the Spirit is proper to the Son, and not only inasmuch as he is Word who
has appeared from the Father, but even if he should be thought as having
become man like us. {A} Proper as from the Father in a consubstantial
offspring, the Word born from him, even if he who as in his own nature is
lacking the properties of the divinity should be said to receive when he
became man. And indeed, himself being the life of all, through the ineffable
generation from the living Father, he is said to be made alive with us. Yet it
is possible to see him both granting to his own flesh the glory of the
God-befitting energy, and again appropriating the things of the flesh and as
it were according to an economic union, and putting them around his own
nature. {B} In what manner do you mean? {A} Or will you not say that it most
of all befits the Word who is by nature from God the Father to come from above
and from heaven to be able to give life to those into whom he should wish to
put life? {B} I say so. {A} What then? Would you grant me that to create, tell
me, in a godlike way, belongs to human things? {B} Not I. {A} Why then does he
give us life as God, yet not only by partaking of the Holy Spirit, but having
also set forth as food the assumed flesh? For he said that "Amen amen I say to
you, unless you eat the flesh of the Son of man and drink his blood, you do
not have life in yourselves." And when the Jews were at some time railing at
him, and having attempted, I know not how, to kindle the vote concerning the
better things upon the blessed Moses, and having said openly: "Our fathers ate
the manna in the wilderness, as it is written, He gave them bread from heaven
to eat. What sign do you do, that we may believe you? What do you work," as
having brought down the body to us from above and from heaven? He says: "Amen
amen I say to you, Moses has not given you the true bread from heaven. For the
bread of God is that which comes down from heaven and gives life to the
world." And besides these things again, all but pointing to himself with a
finger as embodied, "I am," he says, "the living bread that came down from
heaven. If anyone eats of this bread, he shall live unto the age. And the
bread which I shall give is my flesh for the life of the world. He who eats my
flesh and drinks my blood remains in me, and I in him. As the living Father
sent me, and I live because of the Father, 708 and he who eats me, he also
shall live because of me." And yet how is it not exact to say that the flesh
has not come down from the heavens,

For to Nicodemus not understanding the mystery, and crying out ignorantly:
"How can these things come to be?" "If I told you the earthly things," he
says, "and you do not believe, how if I tell you the heavenly things will you
believe? And no one has gone up into heaven except he who came down from
heaven, the Son of <man>." And again to the Jews suffering the same lack of
education as he, and having chosen, I know not how, to mock him since he said
that his own body is life-giving and from heaven, "Does this scandalize you?"
he says. "If then you behold the Son of man going up where he was before?" Or
shall we not say that Emmanuel has been born from a woman? {B} How not? {A}
Where then was he before? Or how has he gone up where he himself says he is,
although the body united to him was born through the holy Virgin? And would we
not also confess that the flesh from earth is inactive toward being able to
give life, as far as concerns its own nature? {B} True. {A} How then, tell me,
is the flesh life-giving? Or how would that from the earth also be thought
from heaven? {B} According to union, I think, with the Word who both lives and
is from heaven. {A} Know that you have thought things exceedingly right and
agreeing with the sacred words. For he would also be Creator in a godlike way
in no other manner, even when he is not thought without flesh. And I shall
again make the divine Paul who has written thus a helper of the argument:
"Giving thanks to God and Father, who made us sufficient for the portion of
the lot of the saints in the light, who delivered us from the authority of
darkness, and transferred us into the kingdom of the Son of his love, in whom
we have the redemption, the remission of sins, who is image of the invisible
God, firstborn of all creation, because in him all things were created, both
those in the heavens and those upon the earth, the visible and the invisible,
whether Thrones, or Lordships, or Principalities, or Authorities, all things
through him and unto him have been created, and he is before all, and all
things consist in him, and he is the head of the 709 body of the Church, who
is beginning, firstborn from the dead, that he himself might become first in
all things, because in him all the fullness was well pleased to dwell, and
through him to reconcile all things unto himself, having made peace through
the blood of his cross, whether the things upon the earth, or the things in
the heavens." See again openly he says that all things have been created
through him and unto him, and indeed that he is before all, and that all
things consist in him, and he says that he is firstborn from the dead, having
made peace through his blood both the things in the heavens and those upon the
earth. Then who is the firstborn from the dead, if not Christ Jesus, that is,
the Word in flesh and with flesh? For the Word being God would in no way have
died, nor would he be thought Creator of the whole as being a man like us,
unless he created as God, even if after the union he is not thought without
flesh, firstborn also from the dead, inasmuch as he appeared as man, not
casting away being God through the incarnation. Do you think anything should
be blamed in these things? {B} Not in any way. {A} And you might see also
elsewhere the spirit-bearing ones going through the same words. For John: "In
the
beginning," he says, "was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word
was God. This one was in the beginning with God. All things came to be through
him, and without him not even one thing came to be." And Paul again: "One God
the Father from whom are all things, and one Lord Jesus Christ through whom
are all things." But if there was that which intervened after the Word had
been united to flesh, and dividing into otherness, I mean that as in a dyad of
sons, according to what seems good to you, how would all things have been
created through Jesus Christ? But indeed


all things were created through him. It is therefore evident that the things
that exist by nature and properly in the Word who is from the Father have been
preserved again for him even when he became flesh. It is therefore unsafe to
dare to cut apart. For there is one Lord Jesus Christ, and through him the
Father has created all things. {B} True. {A} He is therefore both Creator in a
godlike way, and life-giving as life, composed into some one intermediate of
both human properties and those above man. For he is mediator of God and men,
according to the Scriptures, being by nature God, and not without flesh, and
truly man, and not mere like us, but being what he was even if he became
flesh. For it has been written that "Jesus Christ yesterday and today the 710
same also unto the ages." Or do we not believe that Emmanuel has been born
through the holy Virgin in the last times of the age? {B} Yes. For we shall
not contradict the divine Scriptures. {A} And yesterday, tell me, and today,
my friend, would they not signify for us the present time and that already
past? {B} I say so. {A} How then would he be the same unto the past, although
he had not yet received the generation according to the flesh? {B} Because the
Word was also in the beginning, and as having appeared from the eternal and
unchangeable God and Father, he also has in his own nature the eternal and the
unchangeable. {A} I praise the opinion; for it is thus and exceedingly right.
Yet you would tell one who is inquiring, good sir. {B} What? {A} Is not Jesus
Christ a name altogether newest for the Word, and concurrent with the times of
the incarnation? {B} And indeed this has been shown to us through very many
words. {A} Understand then that he says Christ Jesus, and not the Word in a
single form, is the same yesterday and today and unto the ages. And how would
the nature of a man have the unchangeable and the continuance in identity,
although it undergoes motion, and before the others that from not being into
being and living? {B} Has then the sacred Letter missed the truth for us, and
does it say that he who was not yesterday pre-existed? {A} I would not say
this. Far from it. Yesterday and today the same and unto the ages is Christ
Jesus. And the most ancient and unswerving of the Word I shall least of all
deny, even if he became flesh. But I shall think that according to union with
his own flesh he is again shown as being yesterday and pre-existing. And
indeed the insane Jew was disgusted, and attempted to stone, not admitting the
faith. For he was astonished that, although seen as a man like us, he was
witnessing to himself the seniority of an antiquity above man, saying as God:
"Amen amen I say to you, before Abraham came to be, I am." When they also said
to this: "You do not yet have fifty years, and have you seen Abraham?" And
John also said: "He who comes after me has come to be before me, because he
was first of me." And yet how, knowing and naming Emmanuel a man, does he say
that the late-born and after him came to be before him and first? {B} He says
that he came to be before and first according 711 to the glory, as it occurs
to me to think. {A} And indeed that it is counterfeit and ignorant to think
thus you will know not from long words. For if we accept "before" as the
better in glory, by the same account we shall also say that "after" very well
shows the second as in splendour. It has then come about, as it seems, to
suppose unintelligently that Christ is inferior to the glory of John, and runs
behind. For he said that "After me comes a man." {B} Alas for the absurdity.
For we shall not think it is thus, having rightly understood what is written
in the Psalms: "For who in the clouds shall be equalled to the Lord? And who
shall be likened to God among the sons of God?" {A} One must therefore assign
to him even with flesh the most ancient, as to God by nature united to flesh
and accustomed to make common to his own body the goods of his own nature. {B}
You have said most excellently. {A} And you might learn that this is so and
has been said rightly also from other testimonies, my friend, if you should
choose. For God said


somewhere concerning Christ from the seed of David according to the flesh
through one of the holy prophets: "And you, Bethlehem, house of Ephratha, you
are too little to be among the thousands of Judah; from you shall come forth
for me to be for a ruler in Israel, and his goings forth are from the
beginning from days of an age." And concerning the sons of Israel the most
holy Paul: "That all were baptized into Moses in the cloud and in the sea, and
all ate the spiritual food, and all drank the same spiritual drink. For they
drank from a spiritual rock that followed. And the rock was Christ." Consider
then in Christ Jesus according to economic union the properly most ancient of
the Word. Or is the argument not evident? For he says very well that the
Bethlehemite as man and from a woman has the goings forth from the beginning
of an age. For in the beginning was and before every age the Word who became
man, and he himself was the rock intoxicating thirsty Israel with the streams
beyond hope and unexpected, although he was born according to the flesh and

And the divine Luke also says that the holy apostles became both eyewitnesses
and ministers of the Word. For the bodiless became apparent, and the
intangible tangible, no longer having the flesh from earth as a foreign
wrapping, but having made it his own temple, and being known with it as God
and Lord. And you know somewhere the most holy Paul having written: "For none
of us lives to himself, and none dies to himself. For if we live, we live to
the Lord, if we die, we die to the Lord. Whether then we live or we die, we
are the Lord's. For unto this Christ died and lived, that he might be lord of
both dead and living." It is then fixed that he has truly been lord of both
dead and living who died and was raised for no other reason than this and this

Most rightly and unerringly. Therefore, not without flesh, but rather with it

Lord and the Teacher, and you say well; for I am." And confirming it by deed,

was not the man maimed in the feet and sitting at the Beautiful gate healed in
the name of Jesus Christ the Nazarene? And Aeneas shook off so long an
infirmity, and escaped a disease altogether hard to flee, to whom the divine
said [Paul]: "Aeneas, Jesus the Christ heals you." {B} True. {A} Being
therefore pressed from all sides toward the truth, and being very eager to
track what seems good to the sacred Letters, and following the opinions of the
fathers, we believe that he from the root of Jesse, he from the seed of David,
he from a woman according to the flesh, he with us under law as man and above
us above law as God, he for us and with us among the dead, he above us through
himself life-giving and life, is Son of God according to truth, neither
stripping the human of divinity, nor indeed unclothing the Word of humanity
after the ineffable intertwining, but confessing one and the same Son, from
two things, having appeared ineffably into some one from both, according to
the highest union clearly, and not a turning-aside of nature. For as much as
the gain will remain for those who glorify thus, the disciple of Christ will
make clear saying: "Whoever confesses that Jesus is the Son of God, God
remains in him, and he in God." And that Jesus Christ, he from David according
to the flesh, is by nature and truly Son of God, inasmuch as the Word became
flesh and tabernacled among us, the wise John will seal saying: "And we know
that the Son of God has come, and has given us understanding that we may know
the true God, and we are in the true, in his Son 714 Jesus Christ. This is the
true God and eternal life," and through him and with him to God and Father the
glory with the Holy Spirit unto the ages of ages. Amen.
