And Athanasius the great luminary of the Church of the Alexandrians, in the
aforementioned letter to bishop Epiphanius, teaches saying thus: Therefore as
you have contended together, and have rejoiced together, so also cease from
blaming; and rather pray that henceforth the Church may have firm peace, and
that the heresies may cease from the murderous choice; and that these too may
cease from being contentious, who have invented questions for themselves, on
the pretext of the saving Pascha, but in deed especially for the sake of their
own strife; because, seeming to be from us and boasting to be called
Christians, they emulate those Jews who have betrayed. For what even plausible
defense could there then be for them, since it is written: And on the first of
the Unleavened, =26.1260= and on which it was necessary to sacrifice the
Pascha. But it happened then well; now, according to what is written: They
always wander in the heart. =26.1240= The benefit of the Lord's likeness
toward us is great, because we also are made like toward him. For if he
himself had not been made like toward men, men would not have been able to be
made like toward God. But
