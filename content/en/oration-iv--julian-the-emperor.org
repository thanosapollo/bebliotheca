* Section 1
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

I suppose this discourse belongs especially to all, /ὅσσα τε γαῖαν ἔπι πνείει
τε καὶ ἕρπει,/ and to whoever has a share in being and in a rational soul and
in intellect, and not least of all others to myself; for I am an attendant of
King Helios. And of this I have at home with myself the more exact proofs; but
what it is lawful for me to say and not liable to envy, a terrible longing for
the rays of the god has been melted into me from childhood, and toward the
ethereal light I was so beside myself in mind from a mere little boy that I
did not desire to look intently at him alone, but also, if ever at night when
it was cloudless and the sky was pure I went out, leaving everything at once I
attended to the heavenly beauties, no longer understanding anything if someone
said something to me, nor myself attending to what I was doing. And I seemed
to be over-curious toward them and to be a busybody, and already someone took
me for an astrologer when I was just growing a beard. And yet, by the gods,
never had such a book come into my hands, nor did I yet know at all what the
thing even is. But why do I say these things, when I have greater things to
tell, if I should relate how I thought at that time concerning the gods? But
let there be forgetfulness of that darkness. But that the heavenly light
flashing all around me both woke me and spurred me to the spectacle, so that
already even the motion of the moon contrary to the universe I myself
understood from myself, having not yet met with any of those who philosophize
such things, let what has been said be signs for me.

* Section 2
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h2
:END:

I myself then envy the good fortune even if to someone the god granted a body
compacted from a holy and prophetic seed, so that taking it up he might open
the treasures of wisdom; but I do not dishonor this portion of which I myself
was thought worthy by this god, having been born in the race that rules and is
king of the earth in my own times, but I hold, if one must obey the wise, that
this one is the common father of all men. For it is said rightly that a man
begets a man, and the sun, sowing souls into the earth not from himself alone
but also from the other gods, and for what work these show by the lives which
they choose. Finest then, if it happened to someone even before the third
generation from very many ancestors in succession to serve the god, but not to
be blamed either is whoever, having recognized himself by nature a servant of
this god, alone of all or with a few gives himself over to the service of the
master.

* Section 3
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h3
:END:

Come then, as we are able, let us hymn his festival, which the ruling city
honors with yearly sacrifices. It is, I know well, hard even only to
understand concerning him, how great the unseen one is for one reasoning from
the seen, and to speak it is perhaps impossible, even if someone should be
willing to fall short of the worth. For to attain what is according to worth I
know well that none of all could, but not to miss the measured in the praises
is the chief point of the human power in being able to speak. But for me may
there stand as helper both the eloquent Hermes with the Muses and Apollo the
Leader of the Muses, since the discourses belong to him also, and may they
grant me to say as many things as it is dear to the gods to be said and
believed concerning them.

* Section 4
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h4
:END:

What then will be the manner of the praises? Or is it clear that going through
concerning his substance and whence he came forth and the powers and the
activities, as many as are seen and as many as are unseen, and concerning the
giving of the goods, which he makes throughout all the worlds, we shall make
the encomia not altogether out of tune with the god? And one must begin from
here.

* Section 5
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h5
:END:

This divine and all-beautiful cosmos from the highest vault of heaven as far
as the last of earth, held together by the unbreakable providence of the god,
has come to be from eternity ungenerated and for the remaining time eternal,
guarded by no other than immediately by the fifth body, whose chief point is a
ray of the sun, and as if at a second step by the intelligible cosmos, and
more anciently still through the King of all, about whom all things are. This
one then, whether it is lawful to call him that which is beyond intellect, or
the idea of the things that are, which I say is the intelligible entire, or
One, since the One of all things seems somehow most ancient, or what Plato is
accustomed to name the Good, this then the single-formed cause of the wholes,
leading for all the things that are beauty and perfection and unification and
a power beyond resource, according to the first-working substance remaining in
itself, from the middle of the intellectual and demiurgic causes, showed forth
Helios, a greatest god, from himself all things like himself; just as also the
daemonic Plato thinks, /Τοῦτον τοίνυν,/ saying, /ἦν δ’ ἐγώ, φάναι με λέγειν
τὸν τοῦ  ἀγαθοῦ ἔκγονον, ὃν τἀγαθὸν ἐγέννησεν ἀνάλογον ἑαυτῷ, ὅτιπερ αὐτὸ ἐν
τῷ νοητῷ τόπῳ πρός τε νοῦν καὶ τὰ νοούμενα, τοῦτο τοῦτον ἐν τῷ ὁρατῷ πρός τε
ὄψιν καὶ τὰ ὁρώμενα./

* Section 6
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h6
:END:

His light then has this analogy, I think, toward the visible, which truth has
toward the intelligible. And he himself entire, as having come to be offspring
of the first and greatest, of the idea of the Good, having come to subsist
about its abiding substance from eternity, received also the sovereignty among
the intellectual gods, giving to the intellectual those things of which the
Good is cause for the intelligible. And the Good is, I think, cause for the
intelligible gods of beauty, substance, perfection, unification, holding them
together and shining around them with a goodness-like power; these things
Helios also gives to the intellectual, appointed by the Good to rule and be
king of them, even if they came forth with him and came into being with him,
in order that, I think, a goodness-like cause leading the way of the goods may
also for the intellectual gods steer all things according to intellect for
all.

But also third, this appearing disk is clearly cause of safety for the
sensibles, and of as many things as we said the great Helios is for the
intellectual gods, of so many this appearing one also is cause for the
visible.

* Section 7
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h7
:END:

And the proofs of these are clear for one examining the unseen from the
appearances. Come then, first, is not the light itself some incorporeal divine
form of the actually transparent? And whatever the transparent itself is, it
is, so to speak, underlying together with all the elements and being their
immediate form, not body-like nor mixed nor admitting the qualities proper to
body. You will not then say a heat of its own, nor the cold opposite to that,
nor will you assign the hard, nor the soft, nor any other of the differences
according to touch, nor then taste nor smell, but such a nature falls under
sight alone, being led toward activity by the light. And the light is a form
of this, as of a matter laid under and extended along with the bodies. And the
light itself being incorporeal, the rays would be some summit of it and as it
were a flower. The opinion of the Phoenicians then, wise in things divine and
knowing, said that the ray going forth everywhere is an unstained activity of
the pure intellect itself; and the account also is not out of tune, if the
light itself is incorporeal, if someone should not even suppose its source to
be a body, but an unstained activity of intellect shining into its own seat,
which has obtained the middle of the whole heaven, from where shining it fills
the heavenly circles with all vigor, and shines around all things with divine
and unstained light.

* Section 8
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h8
:END:

The works among the gods going forth from him have been said by us moderately
a little earlier and will be said after a little. But as many things as we see
first with sight itself are a name only, lacking the work, if they do not take
in addition the leading help of the light. And what would be visible at all
that has not first been brought to light as matter to a craftsman, in order, I
think, that it may receive the form? For gold simply poured out thus is gold,
yet not a statue nor an image, before the craftsman puts the shape around it.
Therefore also as many things as are by nature to be seen, if they are not
brought to those seeing together with light, are altogether deprived of being
visible. Giving then to those seeing the seeing and to the things seen the
being seen, he perfects two natures by one activity, sight and the visible;
and the perfections are both forms and substance.

* Section 9
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h9
:END:

But this perhaps is too subtle; yet what we all follow, unlearned and laymen,
philosophers and men of letters, what power in the universe has the god rising
and setting? He works night and day and visibly changes and turns the whole.
And yet to which of the other stars does this belong? How then do we not
already from these things believe also concerning the more divine, that the
unseen and divine kinds of intellectual gods above the heaven are also filled
from him with the goodness-like power, to whom every chorus of stars yields,
and generation follows, steered by this one's forethought? For that the
planets, dancing about him as about a king, are carried in a circle most
harmoniously at certain defined distances toward him, making certain stations
and a course forward and backward, as those who know the spherical theory name
the appearances about them, and how the light of the moon waxes and wanes,
suffering this according to the distance of the sun, is clear to all, I
suppose. How then do we not reasonably suppose that the more ancient order of
bodies among the intellectual gods also has an analogy to such an arrangement?

Let us then take from all things the perfecting power from his making the
visual things see throughout; for he perfects them through the light; and the
demiurgic and generative from the change about the universe, and the holding
of all things together in one from the concord of the motions toward one and
the same, and the middle from his own middle, and that he is seated as king
among the intellectual from the middle rank among the planets.

* Section 10
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h10
:END:

If then we see these things about some one of the other visible gods, or as
many other things, let us not assign to this one the leadership concerning the
gods; but if there is nothing in common for him with the others besides the
doing of good, of which itself also he gives a share to all, calling to
witness both the priests of the Cyprians, who show common altars to Helios and
Zeus, and still before these calling Apollo who sits with this god as witness;
for this god says /Εἷς Ζεύς, εἷς Ἀίδης, εἷς Ἥλιός ἐστι Σάραπις·/ let us
suppose a common, or rather one, sovereignty of Helios and Zeus among the
intellectual gods; whence also Plato seems to me not unreasonably to name
Hades a prudent god. And we call this same one also Sarapis, the unseen
clearly and intellectual, toward whom he says the souls of those who have
lived best and most justly travel upward. For let no one suppose this one whom
the myths persuade us to shudder at, but the gentle and mild, who altogether
releases the souls from generation, and does not, when they have been
released, nail them to other bodies, punishing and exacting penalties, but
leads them upward and stretches the souls toward the intelligible cosmos.

* Section 11
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h11
:END:

And that the opinion is not altogether new, but the most ancient of the poets
anticipated it, Homer and Hesiod, whether thinking thus or also by divine
inspiration, as the seers, being in ecstasy toward the truth, would become
known from here. The one, giving his genealogy, said he was of Hyperion and
Theia, all but riddling through these that he was born a genuine offspring of
that which exceeds all things; for who else would Hyperion be besides this
one? And Theia herself, in another manner, is she not called the most divine
of the things that are? And let us not suppose pairing nor marriages,
unbelievable and paradoxical playthings of the poetic muse. But let us think
his father and begetter the most divine and highest; and who else would such a
one be than that which is beyond all things and about which all things are and
for the sake of which all things are? And Homer calls him Hyperion from the
father, and shows his self-authority and that he is stronger than all
necessity. For Zeus, as that one says, being lord of all, constrains the
others; but in the myth, when this god says that because of the impiety of the
companions of Odysseus he will leave Olympus, he no longer says /Αὐτῇ κεν γαίῃ
ἐρύσαιμ’ αὐτῇ τε θαλάσσῃ,/ nor does he threaten bond nor force, but says he
will impose the penalty on those who have erred, and thinks it right that he
himself shine among the gods. Does he not through these things say that
besides being self-authoritative Helios is also perfecting? For for what do
the gods need him, unless by flashing unseen toward substance and being he
happens to be the filler of the goods we spoke of? For the /Ἠέλιόν τ’ ἀκάμαντα
βοῶπις πότνια Ἥρη Πέμψεν ἐπ’ Ὠκεανοῖο ῥοὰς ἀέκοντα νέεσθαι/ says that night
was thought to be before the time because of some hard mist. For this goddess
somewhere, also elsewhere in the poem, says /ἠέρα δ’ Ἥρη Πίτνα πρόσθε
βαθεῖαν./ But let us leave the things of the poets to farewell; for they have
with the divine also much of the human; and what the god himself seems to
teach us concerning himself and the others, those things let us now go
through.

* Section 12
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h12
:END:

The place about earth has its being in becoming. Who then is the one granting
it eternity? Is it not he who holds these things together by defined measures?
For it was not possible for a nature of body to be unlimited, since it is
neither ungenerated nor self-subsistent; and if from the substance something
were altogether coming to be continuously, and nothing were being resolved
into it, the substance of the things coming to be would fail. Such a nature
then this god, moving by measure, when approaching sets upright and wakes, and
when going far away lessens and destroys, or rather he himself always makes
alive, moving and channeling life into it; and his leaving and the change
toward the other side becomes a cause of destruction for those that waste.
Always then the gift of the goods from him comes down equal upon the earth;
for at another time another region receives such things, so that generation
may not fail and the god may never do less or more than the customary good to
the passible cosmos. For the sameness, as of the substance, so also of the
activity, is among the gods and before the others with the King of the wholes,
Helios, who also makes the simplest motion above all those who are carried
contrary to the universe; which very thing also the famous Aristotle makes a
sign of his superiority toward the others;

* Section 13
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h13
:END:

but also from the other intellectual gods powers not dim come down into this
cosmos. Then what of this? Do we shut out the others when we agree that the
leadership has been given to this one? And much more we think it right to
believe from the visible concerning the unseen. For as he appears to perfect
the powers given from there to all into the earth and to fit them together
both toward himself and the whole, so one must think also among the unseen
that their associations toward one another have that one as leader, and the
others together in concord toward her. Since also, if we said the god is
seated in the middle among the intellectual gods, of what sort the middle-ness
is, of which again one must suppose him middle, may King Helios himself grant
us to say.

Middle-ness then we say is not that observed in opposites, standing equally
distant from the extremes, as in colors the yellow or grey, and in hot and
cold the lukewarm, and as many such things, but the unifying and bringing
together the things standing apart, of which sort Empedocles says harmony is,
banishing strife from it altogether. What then are the things he brings
together, and of what is he middle? I say then that of both the visible and
around-the-cosmos gods and of the immaterial and intelligible, who are about
the Good, as if the intelligible and divine substance were being multiplied
without passion and without addition.

* Section 14
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h14
:END:

That there is then some middle, not blended from the extremes, but perfect and
unmixed from all the gods visible and unseen and sensible and intelligible,
the intellectual and all-beautiful substance of King Helios, and of what sort
one must think the middle-ness, has been said. And if one must also go through
the particulars, so that we may look down with the intellect also according to
forms at the middle of his substance, how it stands toward the first things
and the last, even if it is not easy to go through all, still let us try to
speak the things that are possible.

* Section 15
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h15
:END:

The intelligible, altogether one, always pre-existing, and having taken all
things together in the one. What then? Is not also the whole cosmos one living
thing, entire through entire, full of soul and intellect, perfect from perfect
parts? Of this double one-formed perfection then (I mean that in the
intelligible holding all things in one, and that about the cosmos gathered
into one and the same perfect nature of unification) the middle perfection of
King Helios is one-formed, seated among the intellectual gods. But next after
this there is some cohesion in the intelligible cosmos of the gods arranging
all things toward the one. What then? Does there not also appear about heaven
the substance of the fifth body traveling in a circle, which holds together
all the parts and binds them toward themselves, holding together what is by
nature scattered of them and flowing away from one another? These two
substances then, causes of cohesion, the one in the intelligibles, the other
appearing in the sensibles, King Helios joins into the same, imitating the
holding-together power of the one among the intellectual, as having come forth
from it, and pre-ruling the last, which is observed about the visible cosmos.
Is it not then also that the self-subsistent, existing first in the
intelligibles, and last in the things appearing according to heaven, has as
middle the self-subsistent substance of King Helios, from which first-working
substance the ray shining around all things comes down into the visible
cosmos?

* Section 16
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h16
:END:

And again for one looking according to another point, one is the demiurge of
the wholes, but many are the demiurgic gods going about according to heaven.
One must then set as middle of these also the demiurgy coming down from Helios
into the cosmos. But also the generative of life is much and overfull in the
intelligible, and the cosmos also appears full of generative life. It is clear
beforehand then that the generative of life of King Helios is also middle of
both, since the appearances also witness to this; for some of the forms he
perfects, some he works, some he orders, some he wakes, and there is not one
thing which without the demiurgic power from Helios comes forth into light and
generation. Still besides these, if we should think the unstained and pure
immaterial substance in the intelligibles, nothing coming to it from outside
nor any alien thing existing in it, but full of its own unstained purity, and
the nature in the cosmos about the body carried in a circle, unmixed toward
all the elements, exceedingly genuine and pure, of an unstained and daemonic
body, we shall find also the bright and unblemished substance of King Helios
middle of both, of the immaterial purity in the intelligibles and of the
unstained and unmixed toward generation and destruction, the pure genuineness
in the sensibles. And the greatest evidence of this is that not even the
light, which most of all is carried from there to earth, mixes with anything
nor receives dirt and stain, but remains in all the things that are altogether
unstained and undefiled and without passion.

And one must still attend to the immaterial and intelligible forms, but also
to the sensible, as many as are about matter or about the underlying. There
will appear again here as middle the intellectual of the forms about the great
Helios, by which also the forms about matter are helped, never being able
either to be or to be saved if they are not worked together toward substance
from that one. What then? Is he not cause of the separation of the forms and
of the combining of the matter, furnishing us not only to think him but also
to see him with eyes? For the distribution of the rays into all the cosmos and
the unification of the light show the demiurgic separation of the making.

* Section 17
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h17
:END:

And there being still many goods appearing about the substance of the god,
which establish that he is middle of both the intelligible and the encosmic
gods, let us pass to his last visible allotment. First then of the things
about the last cosmos is that of the solar angels, having as in a paradigm the
idea and the hypostasis; and after this the generative of the sensibles, of
which the more honorable has the cause of heaven and stars, and the lesser
oversees generation, holding from eternity in itself the ungenerated cause of
it. To go through all the things about the substance of this god is not
possible even if this god should grant someone to think them, since even to
take all things around with the intellect seems to me impossible.

* Section 18
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h18
:END:

And since we have gone through many things, one must put as it were a seal on
this account, being about to pass to other things needing no less
contemplation. What then the seal is, and as in a chief point the thought
about the substance of the god taking all things around, may he himself put
upon our mind, who wish to gather in brief both the cause from which he came
forth, and himself who he is, and of what things he fills the visible cosmos.
One must say then that from one god King Helios came forth, one from one
intelligible cosmos, middle of the intellectual gods, stationed in the middle
according to every kind of middle-ness, the like-minded and friendly and
bringing together the things standing apart, leading the last things into
unification with the first, having in himself the middles of perfection and
cohesion and generative life and the one-formed substance, and leading the way
of goods of every kind for the sensible cosmos, not only through the ray with
which he himself shines around, ordering and brightening, but also having
brought into being together with himself the substance of the solar angels and
holding around the ungenerated cause of the things that come to be, and still
before this the unaging and abiding cause of the life of the eternal bodies.

* Section 19
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h19

What then it was necessary to say concerning the substance of this god,
although most things have been left aside, has nevertheless been said, not a
few things; but since the multitude of his powers and the beauty of the
activities is so great as to be an excess of the things observed about his
substance, since also the divine things going forth into the visible are by
nature multiplied because of the surplus and generative of life, see what we
shall do, who are stripping for a yawning sea, barely and as a welcome thing
resting from the long account before. Yet one must dare, taking courage in the
god, and try to lay hold of the account.

* Section 20
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h20
:END:

In common then one must suppose that the things said before concerning his
substance belong to the powers. For it is not that the substance of a god is
one thing, and power another, and by Zeus a third besides these, activity. For
all things whatsoever he wishes, these he is and can and does; for he neither
wishes what he is not, nor what he wishes to do does he lack strength, nor
what he cannot does he want to do. These things then about the human being are
not thus; for there is a double nature fighting, blended into one of soul and
body, the one divine, the other dark and gloomy; and there seems to be some
battle and faction. Since also Aristotle says that because of such a thing
neither the pleasures nor the pains agree with one another in us; for what is
pleasant, he says, to one of the natures in us is by nature painful to that
set against this; but among the gods there is nothing such; for the goods
belong to them by substance and continuously, not at one time yes and at
another time no. First then as many things as we said, wishing to set forth
his substance, one must think have been said by us also concerning the powers
and activities.

* Section 21
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h21
:END:

And since in such things the account seems to convert, as many things as we
next examine also concerning his powers and activities, these one must think
not works only but also substance. For there are gods akin to Helios and grown
together with him, summing up the unstained substance of the god, being
multiplied in the cosmos, but being about him in a one-formed way. Hear then
first as many things as they say who look at heaven not as horses and oxen or
some one of the irrational and unlearned animals, but from it being busy about
the unseen nature; and still before these, if it is dear to you, concerning
his hypercosmic powers and activities, and from myriads in multitude look at a
few.

* Section 22
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h22
:END:

First of his powers is that through which, gathering the summits of the
intellectual substance entire through entire into one and the same, he shows
it one. For just as about the sensible cosmos it is possible to understand
clearly, air and water taken in the middle of fire and earth, a bond of the
extremes, would not someone reasonably think that this has been so arranged
also upon the cause separated before the bodies, which having the beginning of
generation is not generation, so that also in those the extreme causes,
altogether separated from bodies, being gathered into the same by certain
middle-nesses from King Helios, are unified about him? And the demiurgic power
of Zeus also runs together with him, through which we said also earlier that
the precincts have been founded for them in Cyprus and shown in common; and we
also called Apollo himself to witness of the accounts, who it is likely, I
suppose, knows better concerning his own nature; for this one also is with
Helios and shares because of the simplicity of the intellections and the
abiding of the substance and the being according to the same of the activity.

But also the divided demiurgy of Dionysus the god nowhere appears to separate
from Helios; and always subordinating it to this one and showing it sharing
the throne he is an interpreter for us of the finest thoughts upon the god.
And containing in himself all the beginnings of the finest intellectual
blending, this god Helios Apollo is Leader of the Muses. And since he also
fills out for us the whole life of good order, he begets Asclepius in the
cosmos, and has him also before the cosmos with himself.

* Section 23
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h23
:END:

But many other powers also one looking at about this god would never reach
all; and it is enough to have contemplated, of the separated and before the
bodies, upon the causes themselves I think, which separated pre-exist the
visible demiurgy, that the sovereignty belonging to Helios and Zeus is equal
and one; and having seen the simplicity of the intellections together with the
everlasting and according to the same abiding with Apollo; and the divided of
the demiurgy together with Dionysus who oversees the divided substance; and
having contemplated that of the finest symmetry and intellectual blending
about the power of the Leader of the Muses; and thinking the filling out of
the good order of the whole life with Asclepius.

* Section 24
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h24
:END:

So much concerning his procosmic powers, and works of equal rank with these
above the visible cosmos are the filling up of the goods. For since he is a
genuine offspring of the Good, having received from it the perfect good
portion, he himself distributes to all the intellectual gods, giving them a
good-working and perfect substance. This is one thing. And a second work of
the god is the most perfect distribution of the intelligible beauty in the
intellectual and incorporeal forms. For of the generative substance appearing
in nature, desiring to beget in the beautiful and to set the offspring under,
there is still necessity that that in the intelligible beauty which does this
very thing everlastingly and always lead the way, and not now yes and later
no, and at one time begetting and again barren. For as many things as here are
at some time beautiful, these in the intelligibles are always. One must say
then that the ungenerated offspring in the intellectual and everlasting beauty
leads the way of his generative cause in the appearances, which this god has
set up about himself, to which he also distributes the perfect intellect, just
as giving to eyes the sight through the light, so also among the intelligibles
through the intellectual paradigm, which he holds forth much more apparent
than the ethereal ray, he furnishes I think to all the intellectual the
thinking and the being thought. Another activity besides these appears
wonderful about the King of the wholes, Helios: the better portion given to
