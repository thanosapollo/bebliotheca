Of Origen on the first of Kingdoms.

1 The things that have been read are more, and since it is necessary to speak
cutting them short, they are 4 sections. There were read the things following
those concerning Nabal the Carmelite. Then after this the history concerning
David's having been hidden among the Ziphites and having been slandered by
them, and Saul's having come wishing to take David and having come to watch
for a time, and David's having come upon Saul and having taken, while he and
those guarding him were sleeping, "the spear and the flask of water," and
after this having brought a reproof to those who had been entrusted to guard
him, but who had fallen asleep. Then [the things] following was the third
history, that David fled "to <Achish son of> Ammach king of Geth," and how
much favor David found with him after the many deeds of valor, to whom he says
"I will set you as chief bodyguard." Following these was the notorious history
concerning the ventriloquist and concerning Samuel, that the ventriloquist
seemed to have brought up Samuel and Samuel prophesies to Saul. There being
four sections, of which each has not a few matters, but even such as can
occupy those able to examine them for hours not of one assembly but even of
more, whatever of the four the bishop wishes, let him propose, that we may
occupy ourselves about this. Let the things concerning the ventriloquist, he
says, be examined.

2 Some histories do not touch us, but some are necessary toward our hope; and
I said histories thus, since we do not yet arrive at the things of the
anagogy, useful to everyone who knows how to raise them or to hear things
being raised. <Some things of the history then are useful> to all, but some
not to all. For example, as in an illustration, the history concerning Lot and
his daughters, if it has something useful according to the anagogy, God knows
and he to whom he may grant to examine those words; but if according to the
history, you would inquire: for what benefit is there to me from the history
concerning Lot and his daughters? Likewise what benefit is there to me from
the history of Judah and Tamar and the things according to her, simply told?
Since however the history concerning Saul and the ventriloquist touches all, a
necessary truth according to the word. For who, having been released from this
life, wants to be under the authority of a demon, so that a ventriloquist may
bring up not some chance one of those who have believed, but Samuel the
prophet? Concerning whom God says through Jeremiah: "even if Moses and Samuel
stood before my face, I will not even hear these," concerning whom the prophet
says in hymns: "Moses and Aaron among his priests, and Samuel among those who
call upon his name; they called upon the Lord, and he himself heard them, he
spoke to them in a pillar of cloud," and elsewhere "if Moses and Samuel should
stand and pray" and the things following. Is it then, if one so great <was>
under the earth and the ventriloquist brought him up, that a demon has
authority over a prophetic soul? What shall we say? These things have been
written: are they true or are they not true? To say that they are not true
urges toward unbelief (it will go upon the heads of those who say it), but
that they are true furnishes us with inquiry and perplexity.

3 And indeed we know some of our brothers who have set themselves against the
scripture and say: I do not believe the ventriloquist. The ventriloquist says
she has seen Samuel, she lies. Samuel was not brought up, Samuel does not
speak. But just as there are some false prophets saying: "thus says the Lord"
and "the Lord has not spoken," so also this demon lies, promising to bring up
the one commanded by Saul. "For whom shall I bring up?" she says; "Bring up
Samuel for me." <And> these things are said by those who assert that this
history is not true: Samuel in Hades? Samuel is brought up by a ventriloquist,
the exceptional one of the prophets, the one dedicated to God from birth, the
one said before birth that he would be in the temple, the one who at the same
time as being weaned put on an ephod and was wrapped in a double cloak and
became priest of the Lord, to whom while still a child the Lord gave an oracle
speaking? Samuel in Hades? Samuel among those under the earth, the one who
succeeded Eli because of the sins and lawless deeds of the children who had
been condemned by providence? Samuel in Hades, whose God heard in the time of
wheat-harvest, and gave rain to come from heaven? Samuel in Hades, the one who
was so bold, if he took a desired thing of anyone? He did not take the calf,
he did not take the ox, he judged and condemned the people, remaining poor; he
never desired to take anything from so great a people and so large. Samuel,
why in Hades? You see what follows for Samuel in Hades? Samuel in Hades? Why
not also Abraham and Isaac and Jacob in Hades? Samuel in Hades? Why not also
Moses who is yoked with Samuel according to the saying: "not even if Moses and
Samuel should stand, I will not even hear those"? Samuel in Hades? Why not
also Jeremiah in Hades, to whom it has been said: "before I formed you in the
belly I know you, and before you came out of the womb I have sanctified you"?
In Hades also Isaiah, in Hades also Jeremiah, in Hades all the prophets, in
Hades.

4 These things the one not wishing to accept the contest will say, that it is
Samuel who was truly brought up; but since it is necessary to be fair-minded
in hearing the scriptures, the argument having plausibly dinned about us and
being truly able to disturb and move us, let us see whether at some time the
scripture has been understood by the one who has not accepted this, or he
attempts from reputable things, but says things contrary to what has been
written. For what are the things written? "And the woman said: whom shall I
bring up for you?" Whose person is it that says: "the woman said"? Is it the
person of the Holy Spirit, from whom it is believed the scripture has been
written down, or the person of some other? For the narrative person everywhere
(as those also know who have become about all kinds of words) is the person of
the writer; and the writer upon these words is believed to be not a man, but
the writer is the Holy Spirit who moved the men. Therefore the Holy Spirit
says: "And the woman said: who<m> shall I bring up for you? And he said: Bring
up Samuel for me." Who says: "And the woman saw Samuel, and the woman cried
out with a great voice saying"? We will say to that one who has dinned so many
things about us and has said countless things as if then Samuel was not in
Hades: "the woman saw Samuel," the narrative voice said this, "and the woman
cried out with a great voice and said to Saul: why have you deceived me? and
you are Saul. And the king said to her: what is it? do not fear; what have you
seen? And the woman said to Saul: I saw gods coming up out of the earth. And
he said to her: what is the form of them? And she said to him: an old man
coming up, and he himself wrapped in a double cloak ephod." It says that she
has seen also the priestly garment. And I know that <the one from the>
contrary [from the] argument says: "no wonder; for Satan himself is
transformed into an angel of light. It is no great thing then, if his servants
also are transformed as servants of righteousness." But what is it that "the
woman saw"? "Samuel." And why has it not been said: the woman saw a demon,
which pretended to be Samuel? But it has been written that "Saul knew that it
is Samuel." <If it were not Samuel, it ought to have been written: and Saul
thought him to be Samuel. But now it has been written: "Saul knew," and no one
knew what is not; therefore "Saul knew that it is Samuel"> "and he fell upon
his face upon the earth and worshipped." Then again the person of the
scripture: "And Samuel said to Saul: why have you provoked me to bring me up?"
"He said" (says the scripture which one must believe) "Samuel said: why have
you provoked me to bring me up?" Then Saul answers to this: "the foreigners
war against me exceedingly, and God has departed from me and has not answered
me yet, even in the hand of the prophets and in the dreams I have called for
him to make clear to me what I shall do." Again the scripture did not say
otherwise, but that Samuel himself said: "And why have you inquired of me? and
the Lord has departed from you." Does he speak truth or lie saying these
things: "the Lord has departed from you and has become against you and has
made another for himself, as he spoke by my hand, and he will tear the kingdom
from your hand," and does a demon prophesy concerning the kingdom of Israel?
What does the contrary argument say? You see how great a contest there is in
the word of God, having need also of hearers able to hear holy words, great
and secret things concerning the departure, the former things still being in
perplexity and the second things not being clear,

5 but the word is still being examined. I say that both the history and the
examination concerning it are necessary, that we may see what holds us after
the departure. "He has spoken by my hand, and the Lord will tear the kingdom
from your hand and will give it to your neighbor David." But a demon is not
able to know the kingdom of David which has been appointed by the Lord.
"Because you did not hear the voice of the Lord, you did not execute the wrath
of his anger in Amalek." Are these not words of God? Are they not true? For
truly Saul did not do the will of the Lord, but "he <preserved> the king of
Amalek alive," for which both before his falling asleep and at the departure
Samuel reproached Saul: "and because of this the Lord has done this word to
you on this day; and the Lord will give even Israel into the hand of
foreigners." Can a demon prophesy concerning the whole people of God that the
Lord is about to deliver Israel? "And even the camp of Israel the Lord will
deliver it into the hand of foreigners. But make haste, Saul; tomorrow both
you and your sons with me." And can a demon know this, <concerning> a king
appointed with a prophetic anointing, that tomorrow Saul was about to depart
from life and his sons with him? "Tomorrow you and your sons with me."

6 These things then <make clear> that the things written down are not false
and that it is Samuel who has come up; what then does a ventriloquist do here?
What does a ventriloquist do about the bringing up of the soul of the
righteous one? That one fled who spoke the first argument (so that [for] he
may not seem to have a contest against so many other things being sought
according to the place) and he says: it is not Samuel, the demon lies, since
the scripture cannot lie. But the words of the scripture [are] are not from
the person of the demon [himself], but from the person of it: "And the woman
saw Samuel," "Saul said" ............. .......... the things spoken from
Samuel. How then will the things of the ventriloquist appear as being solved
according to the place? I inquire of the one who has said the former things
(Samuel in Hades? and the things following) and let him answer to what has
been asked: who is greater, Samuel or Jesus the Christ? Who is greater, the
prophets or Jesus the Christ? Who is greater, Abraham or Jesus the Christ?
Here at least no one of those who have once arrived at knowing the Lord Jesus
Christ who has been proclaimed beforehand by the prophets will dare [to be] to
say that the Christ is not greater than the prophets. When therefore you
confess that Jesus Christ is greater, is Christ in Hades, or has he not come
to be there? Is the thing said in the Psalms not true, interpreted by the
apostles in their Acts concerning the Savior's having descended into Hades? It
has been written that the thing in the fifteenth Psalm is applied to him: "for
you will not abandon my soul into Hades, nor will you give your holy one to
see corruption." Then Jesus Christ is in Hades, but you fear to say that yes,
he also descends there to prophesy and to come to the other souls? Then after
this if he should answer that Christ has descended into Hades, I will say:
Christ has descended into Hades to do what? To conquer or to be conquered by
death? And he has come down into those regions not as a slave of those there,
but as a master to wrestle, as we were saying formerly interpreting the 21st
Psalm. "Many calves have surrounded me, fat bulls have enclosed me; they
opened their mouth against me, as a lion seizing and roaring. My bones were
scattered." We remember, if indeed we remember the sacred writings; for I
remember them having been spoken toward the 21st Psalm. Therefore the Savior
has come down to save; has he come down there having been proclaimed
beforehand by the prophets or not? But here he was proclaimed beforehand by
the prophets, and elsewhere he comes down not through prophets? And Moses
proclaims him as about to sojourn to the race of men, so that it is well said
by our Lord and Savior: "if you believed Moses, you would believe me; for
concerning me that one wrote. But if you do not believe that one's writings,
how will you believe my words?" And Christ has sojourned to this life and
Christ is proclaimed beforehand as sojourning to this life. But if Moses
prophesies him here, do you not want him to have descended there also, that he
might prophesy that Christ will come? What then? Moses, but not the prophets
following? And not Samuel? What is out of place for the physicians to go down
to those who are in a bad way? And what is out of place that the chief
physician also should go down to those who are in a bad way? Those were many
physicians, but my Lord and Savior is chief physician; for even the inward
desire, which cannot be healed by others, he himself heals; which "could not
be healed by anyone" of the physicians, Christ Jesus heals it. Do not fear, do
not be astonished; Jesus has come to be in Hades, and the prophets before him,
and they proclaim beforehand the sojourning of Christ.

7 Then I want to say also something else from the scripture itself. Samuel
comes up, and what does the woman say to Saul that she has seen? She does not
say she has seen a man; she cowered at this one whom she saw. Whom does she
see? "Gods I," she says, "saw, gods coming up from the earth." And perhaps
Samuel has not come up alone even then being about to prophesy to Saul; but it
is likely, just as here "with the holy you will be hallowed, and with a
blameless man you will be blameless, and with the elect you will be elect,"
and there are here associations of holy ones with holy ones, but not of holy
ones with sinners, and if ever at all, the association of the holy ones with
the sinners is for the sake of saving the sinners also, so perhaps also as
Samuel was coming up there have come up together either holy souls of other
prophets or they were angels who had come up together with the spirits.
Perhaps you will inquire whether they were angels upon their spirits? The
prophet says: "the angel who speaks in me"; and all things are filled of those
needing salvation and "all are ministering spirits sent for service on account
of those who are about to inherit salvation." Why do you fear to say that
every place has need of Jesus Christ? The one who has need of Christ has need
of the prophets; for it is not that he has need of Christ, but has no need of
those preparing the presence and sojourning of Christ. And John, than whom no
one was greater among those born of women according to the witness of our
Savior saying: "there is no one greater among those born of women than John
the Baptist," do not fear to say that he has descended into Hades proclaiming
beforehand my Lord, that he might say beforehand that he would come down. For
this reason, when he was in the prison and knew the departure lying upon him,
having sent two of his disciples he was inquiring, not: "are you the one who
is coming?" (for he knew), but: "are you the one who is coming, or do we look
for another?" He saw his glory, he spoke many things concerning his
wondrousness, he first bore witness to him: "the one coming after me has come
to be before me," he saw his glory, "glory as of an only-begotten from a
father full of grace and truth." Having seen such great things concerning
Christ he hesitates to believe, he doubts and does not say: tell him: are you
the Christ? For now some, not having understood the things said, say: John,
being so great, did not know Christ, but the Holy Spirit departed from him.
And he knew this one, to whom he bore witness before birth and at whom he
leaped, when Mary also came to him, as his mother bore witness to him saying:
"for behold, as the voice of your greeting came into my ears, the infant
leaped in gladness in my belly." This John then who leaped before birth, who
said: "this is he concerning whom I said: the one coming after me has come to
be before me" and: "the one who sent me said to me: upon whom you see the
Spirit descending and remaining, this is the Son of God," this one, they say,
no longer knew Jesus Christ? For in the belly he knew him. But through excess
of glory he has done something like Peter. What like? This one knew something
great concerning Christ. Who am I? "Whom do men say that I am?" And he: this
and that. But you, what? "You are the Christ the Son of the living God"; for
which he is also blessed, that "flesh and blood did not reveal" to him, "but
the Father who is in the heavens." Since therefore he heard great things
concerning Christ and supposed great things and did not accept the divine cry
toward him: "behold we are going up to Jerusalem, and he will be perfected"
and: "the Son of man must suffer many things and be rejected by the chief
priests and elders, and be killed, and on the third day rise," he says: "mercy
to you, Lord." He knew great things concerning Christ, he did not wish to
accept the more humble thing concerning him. Understand for me some such one
also John. He was in prison knowing great things concerning Christ, he saw
heavens opened, he saw Holy Spirit coming down from heaven upon the Savior and
remaining upon him; having seen such great glory he was in doubt and perhaps
disbelieved, whether one so glorious will come down even as far as Hades and
as far as the abyss; for this reason he said: "are you the one who is coming,
or do we look for another?"

8 I have not digressed nor forgotten the matter at hand, but we want to
establish this, that if all have descended into Hades before Christ,
forerunners of Christ, the prophets of Christ, so also Samuel has descended
there; for not simply, but as a holy one. Wherever the holy one may be, he is
holy. Is Christ no longer Christ, since he was once in Hades? Was he no longer
Son of God, since he has come to be in the under-earth place, "that every knee
may bow in the name of Jesus Christ of those in heaven and on earth and under
the earth"? Thus Christ was Christ even being below, <and,> that I may speak
thus, being in the place below, he was above <in> choice. Thus also the
prophets and Samuel, even if they go down where the souls below are, they can
be below <on the one hand> in place, but they are not below in choice. And I
inquire: † they prophesied the things above the heavens. But I am not able to
give a demon such great power, that he prophesies concerning Saul [Samuel] and
the people of God, and prophesies concerning the kingdom of David that he is
about to reign. Those who say these things will know the things of the truth
according to the place; they will not find how to present how a holy one
<would not> also come to be for the salvation of those in a bad way into the
place of those in a bad way. Let physicians come to be into the places of the
soldiers who are sick, and let them enter where the stenches of their wounds
are; this is what medical philanthropy suggests. So this the Word has
suggested to the Savior and to the prophets, both to come here and to go down
into Hades.

9 And this too must be added to the argument, that <if> Samuel was a prophet,
and when he had gone out the Holy Spirit departed from him, and the prophecy
departed from him, then the apostle who says does not speak truth: "now I
prophesy in part, and I know in part; but when the perfect comes, then that
which is in part will be abolished." Therefore the perfect is after life. And
if Isaiah prophesied anything, he prophesied in part with all boldness; and
David has been witnessed the things here toward the perfection of prophecy.
Therefore Samuel did not throw away the prophetic grace. And that he did not
throw it away, did he use it thus, as those who speak in tongues, so that one
might say: "my spirit prays, but my mind is unfruitful"? And yet the one who
speaks in a tongue does not build the church; for Paul says that the one who
prophesies builds the church, saying in the very words: "but the one who
prophesies builds a church." And if "the one who prophesies builds a church,"
he had <and> prophetic grace (for he had not lost it not having sinned; for
only he loses prophetic grace, who after prophesying has done things unworthy
of the Holy Spirit, so as to abandon him and flee from his ruling part; which
then after the sin David also feared, and said: "and take not your Holy Spirit
from me"), if then the Holy Spirit prophesies, and Samuel was a prophet, "and
the one who prophesies builds a church," whom does he build? Does he prophesy
into heaven? To whom? To angels, who have no need? <"They have no need> the
strong of physicians, but those who are in a bad way." Some have need of his
prophecy; for prophetic grace is not idle, no gift among those in the holy one
is idle. Therefore the souls of those who have fallen asleep had need of the
prophetic grace (<that> I may dare even to say). But here Israel had need of
the prophet; and the one who has fallen asleep too, the one released from
life, had need of the prophets, that the prophets again may proclaim to him
the sojourning of Christ. And otherwise also before the sojourning of my Lord
Jesus Christ it was impossible for anyone to pass where the tree of life is,
it was impossible to pass the things appointed to guard the way of the tree of
life; "he appointed the Cherubim and the flaming sword that turns to guard the
way of the tree of life." Who was able to make a way? Who was able to make
anyone pass through the flaming sword? Just as it was not for <anyone> to make
a way through the sea except God and the pillar of fire, the pillar of light
from God, just as it was not for anyone to make a way through the Jordan
except Jesus (that Jesus was a type of the true God); so through the flaming
sword Samuel was not able to pass, not Abraham. For this reason Abraham also
is seen by the one being punished, and "being in torments the rich man lifting
up his eyes sees Abraham," even if "he sees from afar," but he sees, "and
Lazarus in his bosoms." Therefore both patriarchs and prophets and all were
waiting for the sojourning of my Lord Jesus Christ, that this one might open
the way. "I am the way," "I am the door." There is a way upon the tree of
life, that it may come to be "if you pass through fire, a flame will not burn
you." Of what fire? "He appointed the Cherubim and the flaming sword that
turns to guard the way of the tree of life"; so that for this reason those
blessed ones were waiting there, making an economy and not being able to come
to be where the tree of life is, where the paradise of God is, where God is a
farmer, where the blessed and elect and holy ones of God are.

10 There is then no stumbling-block according to the place, but all things
have been written wondrously and have been understood by those to whom God may
reveal. But we who have come at the completion of the ages have something
extra. What extra? If we are released from here, having become good and noble,
not bringing upon ourselves the burdens of sin, we too will pass through "the
flaming sword," and we will not go down into the country where those who fell
asleep before his presence were waiting for Christ; but we will pass through,
being harmed in nothing by the flaming sword. "And each one's work, of what
sort it is, the fire itself will test. If anyone's work is burned, he will
suffer loss, but he himself will be saved thus as through fire." We will pass
through then; and we have something more, and we cannot, having lived well,
depart badly. The ancients did not say, nor the patriarchs nor the prophets,
what we can say, if we live well: "for it is better to depart and to be with
Christ." Wherefore having thus something more and much gain in having come at
the completion of the ages, we first receive the denarius; for hear the
parable, that "beginning he gave the denarius from the last; but the first
thought that they would receive something more." You then first, the one who
came last, receive the wages from the householder in Christ Jesus our Lord, to
whom be the glory and the power unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
