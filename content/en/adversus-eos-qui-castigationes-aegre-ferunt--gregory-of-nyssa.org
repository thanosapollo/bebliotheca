* Against Those Who Take Rebukes Badly

OF THE SAME, TO THOSE WHO ARE VEXED AT REBUKES.
The word of God is truly a divine and holy thing, an exceptional possession,
not added from elsewhere, but blended with nature, a most honorable gift to
man that came into him from him who made him. Therefore he is also said to
have been made according to the likeness of God. By this, then, he has his
difference from the other animals, and he is specially characterized by this
godlike advantage, though in the other things he has much fellowship with the
cattle. For the form of eyes, and the shaping of flesh, both of what is on the
surface and of what is hidden in the belly, does not dignify man; since
participation in those things is seen also in land animals, and in water
creatures, and in birds, and in simply all animals. But this makes him leader
of all, and shows him blessed; which God, having richly, granted us a little,
so that first we might look to him, and know the Word as giver of the word,
and worship the one who has so well adorned us with his own grace. Through
words we have power more than the strong, though being weaker in body, and we
enslave all things, and we command them to serve our needs. Thus we tame bulls
and bring them under the yoke, and we prepare the earth to be cut by ploughs,
and choking a swift horse with bits we have him obedient, and driving the slow
donkey with clubs we make him quicker, and we compel the hard mules to draw
carriages and to bear burdens; and the overflowing bulk of elephants, and the
size of camels, we handle skillfully toward what we think fit. From this we
also cross the deep, and with a small piece of wood through the art of
steering we traverse the unspeakable expanses of the seas, and not having
signs of that journey, when we hide the land, we are safely guided by the
signs in heaven, and a star guides the sailor as it did the Magi.
And the breadths and figures of heaven have been found out, and the multitude
of stars, and how much size each has, and interval, and the marking of the
moon, and what the sun suffers when at certain times it covers its ray; and
already we even philosophize about the earth when it is moved, how, being
loosed from its natural supports, it itself is shaken, and it shakes the
inhabitants together with what is laid upon it. And by the signs that have
been observed we also observe droughts =46.309= and we foretell rains, and
applying reason to the things that grow from the earth, we examine the natures
of plants, and we declare one saving for the wounded, another suited for sleep
for the one who does not sleep, another healing of the liver, and another that
settles the swellings of the spleen. I pass over the sciences, I leave aside
the necessary arts, those toward all food, the varied circuit of occupations.
But such an animal, the wise, the practical, the energetic, the remembering,
the one being here and seeing the things elsewhere, because it is dragged
about by varied pleasures and different passions, is careless of one thing, of
true life and of its own salvation. And you who foresee turns of the
weather, do you not see the resurrection; you who know the changes of the
years, do you not see the change of life; you who demand of your own slave
accounts of what he does, do you despise the authority of your Judge and Lord.
These things are not of the rational one, but of one turning the rational into
folly. You do not practice justice, you do not learn virtue, you neglect
prayer. And yesterday's day made this clear. For with what eyes do you look
upon the Lord's day, you who have dishonored the Sabbath? Or do you not know
that these days are sisters? And if you outrage the one, you strike against
the other? Having mind and word, you do not foresee what is fitting and
advantageous, nor do you make the care of your own immortality in due measure,
nor do you examine your own nature, who you are, and what you can become; but
to belly and lusts, and idleness, and sleep you betray the gift of God and
Benefactor, so that it may become useless and unprofitable and vain for you.
This is most shameful, and of an infant mind, and worthy of the burdensome
accusation. And it would have been a lesser evil if, not understanding from
home and from ourselves what is advantageous, we at least were persuaded when
another proposed what is useful. But it is not so, but we are even greatly
vexed at the teachers, and we bear their admonition with difficulty, and we
are weighed down by the counsels. And we are nauseated at the training in the
good, as the sick who have no appetite are toward the foods offered them by
the physicians. And if a rebuke happens, we grow angry; and if we hear a
harsher word, we cannot bear it; and if the doors of the church are shut to us
by exclusion, we blaspheme. But this is not the manner of those who learn, nor
the obedience of disciples, but the contentiousness of rebels and fighters.
For the disciple ought to hold the rank of a child, whoever will desire one of
the arts or sciences from outside; but the one practicing piety ought to be an
infant with a greater addition; since even the Lord honored that age as
obedient with praises from himself. The child, then, does not go beyond the
characters and the lines which the teacher may stamp in the wax; nor does he
invent new letters, innovating about the letters under a rash authority; but
first he trains his hand with the stylus to the teacher's models; and he does
not assign other names to the letters =46.312=, but those which he heard; and
in every manner and word and deed he imitates the tradition of the instructor.
And if somewhere, having been idle, it is beaten with the leather, it is not
bold at the blow, nor, having torn the tablets at the teacher, does it depart
from school; but having dripped a bitter tear for a little at the pain, it
holds to the lessons, and becomes more intense about the study, not more
careless. At another time again, having been negligent as a youth, he
is ordered to remain without food, and his idleness is condemned with hunger.
And he remains at the school even alone, the other children having withdrawn
to breakfast, keeping the command with much reverence. But the Christian is
not so, who has heard, "Unless you turn and become as the children, you shall
not enter into the kingdom of the heavens"; but when he sees the priest more
austere in form and voice correcting the fault, he contradicts openly, and he
murmurs under his teeth, and going about the marketplace and the streets he
reviles. And if he is shut out of the church, he despises prayer, being cut
off openly from the people and the mysteries; or perhaps not even
having been subjected to this punishment, he withdraws himself from the
church, through anger toward the bishop, and turning away from God and Master.
And one must say to such a one what was said to Paul, when he was still Saul:
"It is hard for you, man, to kick against the goads." Whether you yourself
have left God, consider that the one separated from the sun spends his
life in darkness and gloom; or whether you have been cast out of the prayers
as unworthy, call back by repentance the first state. Nothing false has been
written in the Gospels, nor does a prediction of Christ have a mistaken
outcome. Through Peter he gave to the bishops the key of the heavenly honors;
know that having been loosed you have been loosed, and having been bound you
are tightened with the invisible bonds. If you had eyes toward the essence of
the soul, I would have shown you, the excluded one, the form of one condemned,
his neck pressed by heaviest bonds, having nothing of the members free or
relaxed. And would that the punishment were bounded by this life! But now if
something human happens, and the end stands by suddenly, as a thief in the
night, know that the things there have been shut to you. The doorkeepers of
the kingdom are careful and not to be played with; they see the soul bearing
the signs of the exclusion. As one of the prison, accused by the hair and the
filth, they drive it from the road that leads to the good things; they will
not allow it to see the ranks of the righteous, and angelic gladness. And the
wretched one then, blaming herself much for her folly, groaning and lamenting,
and sighing, and in some gloomy place, as if thrown into a corner, will
continue, paying the unceasing and inconsolable lament unto ages. "Do not
become," he says, "as horse and mule, in which there is no understanding; with
muzzle and bridle =46.313= you will squeeze their jaws." The psalm, softening
the hard one, says these things, and as if softening with oil by the
exhortations. Bend then the neck, yield as to a yoke to the commands; the hard
is broken off, what yields and is bent is set straight; and the experience
upon plants teaches you this. Do not, having bitten the bit, rush upon the
cliff, or the pit, but having your neck turned by the rider's hand, be
directed upon the saving road; when you receive a rebuke, say from a reverent
soul the Davidic word, "It is good for me that you humbled me, that I might
learn your statutes." Do not think the exclusion to be of episcopal arrogance;
the law is ancestral, an ancient canon of the Church beginning from the Law
and strengthened in the grace. Consider holy Paul sending through letters the
sentences of exclusion against those liable, and healing the Corinthian young
man with such a medicine, the one who was satanically mad for his stepmother;
"For whom the Lord loves he trains, and he scourges the son whom he
receives." For the roots of training are not sweet, but bitter; but later they
sprout a fruit sweeter than honeycombs.
And for this reason there is need for the one being trained of toil, and for
the one training even of blow and roughness. And by the Mosaic law the four
tens, the legal blows, remain even until now, but not for the evangelical
life; since of all things is the word, both mercy, and rod, and consolation,
and blow. For we do not beat you as a household slave, but we train you as a
free man; for your race is from Sarah the free woman, not from Hagar the
slave. We respect you as a son of the free woman, we do not dishonor you as
offspring of the maidservant. Therefore with free rebukes we grieve you when
you err, not lacerating the body, but paining the soul. And if we spare even
this, how shall we train you? The teaching word is hard to handle, and the
leading of virtue, and it seeks a varied economy of oversight, adapting itself
to the underlying characters. Is there someone obedient and easy to lead in
mind? For this one the gentle and soft word fits. Another hard and untrained?
He needs scourges. What then shall we do, since we do not handle rods? Shall
we leave him without provision? Not so. But we will reshape the same one by
word into another form suitable to the underlying need; and as the foods by
the small addition of seasonings are exchanged toward opposite qualities,
being changed from the astringent into the sweet, and from the sweet into the
astringent; so also our word by the addition of forms is recast toward
different needs, that the nourishing of each may be devised. Do not then speak
against me in the corners, naming the teaching harsh, nor, making the partners
of the sins partners of the complaints against me, sit judging a bishop with
the council of vanity. =46.316= And I will not be disturbed in soul as
suffering something new, if the harder of my disciples are vexed at me. For
this is a human passion following life from beginning to end; and the ruler is
heavy, as a rule, to those ruled; and to the polluted and lawless even worthy
of hate. For he grieves them by hindering the sin; and this, besides the
things outside, the ecclesiastical history also sufficiently teaches me.
Who a better shepherd than Moses? And who a ruler so well-tuned and gentle,
becoming all things to the people, nourisher, general, priest, a father
himself, saving in wars, in the desert supplying goods unsown and
unploughed, judging in due measure, leading without wandering? And yet
over these things he was rebelled against as doing injustice, he was insulted
as harming, he was murmured against as those who steal and embezzle, he was
blasphemed as an unskilled general and a protector not good, he was in danger
even of being driven from the priesthood itself, when Dathan and Abiram and
the sons of Korah, having torn away with themselves a rebellious crowd of
the people, contended to be profane priests of the holy things, and they
handled the censers, and they all but touched the holy things, and they
kindled the mystic fire, which before the incense burned those who were
offering incense. And so
liable to offense is the presidency of a people and the teaching occupation,
that not even the brothers of Moses spared him; but Miriam also spoke against
him, and Aaron reviled, and the burden of the rule provoked the natural
goodwill, and set the closest against the much-toiling man.
But nothing about this; for he was Moses, and he became nothing worse than
himself; but God required of them the penalties of the offenses against the
leader, and they paid them. What of those after him? Was not
Isaiah sawn while teaching piety? Was not Jeremiah cried down while denouncing
idolatry, and pits and the punishments of plunges received him? Was not
Zechariah warred against and murdered between the temple and the altar? Was
not our Lord himself, the shepherd, slain for the sheep? was he not crucified,
hated for those being trained? What cut off Paul's head? what lifted Peter
upon the cross? what delivered each of the disciples to the varied
punishments? Was it not the hindering of sin, and the training in
righteousness? For always the friends and teachers of the truth are enemies of
the disciples being rebuked. But we have not yet been struck for the truth, we
have not yet received the danger in the flesh. And what great thing if we are
murmured against, we the ministers of the crucified? As a father or mother I
receive your hardnesses and provocations.
