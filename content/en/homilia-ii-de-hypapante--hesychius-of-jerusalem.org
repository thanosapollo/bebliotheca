* Homily II on the Meeting
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of Hesychius, presbyter of Jerusalem, a discourse on the Meeting of our Lord
Jesus Christ.
1 Do not be ignorant that the Spirit appointed Simeon a prophet and addressed
an oracle to him, that he should "not see death before he had seen the
Christ"? "Now you dismiss your servant, Master, according to your word, in
peace." Did he not, supplied by him, bless Joseph and Mary? Was he not
commanded by him when he addressed Mary: "Behold, this child is set for
the fall and rising of many in Israel"? Was not Anna through him possessed as
a prophetess? Was she not filled from him when she proclaimed the redemption
that was to come through the infant?
2 It was necessary to weave the interpretation of these things for you part by
part, but perhaps the time has been spent, and it is fitting to cut the
discourse short out of regard for your attention. But if the word is not
endurable for you, since you are insatiable fellow-banqueters at the table of
the sacred oracles, from the point where the writer of the Gospels began the
narrative for you, from there it is fitting that we too, as far as possible,
cut short the interpretation, so as to provide what is suitable both to the
time and to your longing.
3 "And behold, there was a man in Jerusalem whose name was Simeon." No small
encomium did the Spirit weave for this Simeon in the opening: "For behold," it
says, "there was a man in Jerusalem," whom the prophets of old were seeking
and the law was longing for, but grace found him, and having found him crowned
him with what follows. From where is the crown woven? Attend: "And this man
was righteous and devout, waiting for the consolation of Israel." It is
fitting to emulate Simeon and to call him blessed, because he was not only
righteous but also devout and kept himself from all wickedness; and as one
"waiting for the consolation of Israel," he neither grew numb while laboring
at the virtues, nor grew weary while abstaining from wickedness.
4 "And the Holy Spirit was upon him." This too is marvelous and in harmony
with what was said before; for he was a temple, he kept the bridal chamber of
the flesh clean, and the inner chamber of the soul was purified of every
worldly cobweb; from there he drew to himself the dwelling of the Spirit. "And
it had been revealed to him by the Holy Spirit that he should not see death
before he had seen the Christ of the Lord." You see how great a thing it is to
be a temple of God: those who have trained their life in this way pass from
life to life. Hence also it had been revealed to Simeon "that he should not
see death before he had seen the Christ of the Lord," that is, before he
should see the one who abolishes death, before he should see the one who
raises the dead, before he should see the one who turns the tombs into a
bridal chamber, before he should behold the one who makes his departure from
here fearless for him.
5 "And he came in the Spirit into the temple." This too it is not right to
pass by or run past; for it is a proof that he acted according to the command
of the indwelling Spirit, and that he did not at all enter into marketplaces
or any affairs without him; seeing that even into the temple he comes not
otherwise, but with him leading him by the hand. "And when the parents brought
in the child Jesus, to do for him according to the custom of the law, he also
received him into his arms." Who made known to him the infant, which he did
not know? But it is clear: the Spirit dwelling in him and giving oracles, and
surely saying to Simeon somewhere: "Run, old man, run, hasten already, quickly
seize the blessing, before the star appears, before the Magi arrive, lest we
be put to shame. For the light that is from me must anticipate the star's
arrival at Bethlehem, so that it may be known that from me it both is guided
in its course and borrows its ray. But see that you are not deprived if the
uncircumcised should reap the firstfruits of the blessing."
6 "And he blessed God and said: Now you dismiss your servant, Master,
according to your word, in peace." He blessed God the Father together with the
Son, the Son together with the Spirit, since the Spirit was the one giving the
oracle, and he was carrying the Son in his hands, who had been well pleased to
appear in flesh and to become an infant for our sake; and the Father himself
was also at work in these so many and so great mysteries. But for what did he
offer the thanksgiving? "Now," he says, "you dismiss your servant, Master,"
when there is no fear of death for me, but I depart with joy to the dead,
proclaiming life to them. "And you dismiss according to your word in peace,"
that word which David the prophet, having borrowed from you, used to say: "In
peace I will both lie down and sleep."
7 "For my eyes have seen your salvation," which many of the prophets desired
to see; but now by the very experience you have presented to the race of men
the Most High so greatly humbled for our sake as to become an infant; for the
potter could not otherwise save the spoiled clay, but he himself was going to
be mixed together with it. "My eyes have seen your salvation, which you have
prepared before the face of all the peoples," all, because being creator of
all you necessarily set forth the mystery of salvation for all. "A light for
revelation to the Gentiles and glory of your people Israel": first the
Gentiles are enlightened and after this Israel is glorified; for this one was
born, upon whom the last have become first and the first last, the former
having been deprived of the privileges through sloth.
8 "And his father and mother were marveling at the things spoken about him."
For what reason do you call Joseph father, O evangelist and writer of the
divine genealogy? For you yourself showed Gabriel speaking to the virgin, when
how the conception would be and in what manner the virgin would give birth was
being arranged: "The Holy Spirit will come upon you, and the power of the Most
High will overshadow you." "I have not forgotten my own words," he says; "for
even if I were falling into forgetfulness, the Spirit would have reminded me.
But the one who was made flesh wished Joseph to be called so then, in order
that the one lying in wait against the mystery of our salvation might be
deceived." "And they marveled at the things said about him," because the old
man was carrying an infant, yet was uttering hymns of God. "And Simeon blessed
them": for it was necessary that those who had ministered the blessing to
those under the curse should be blessed.
9 "And he said to Mary his mother: Behold, this child is set for the fall and
rising of many in Israel": for this is the one concerning whom Peter, taking
up the prophecy of Isaiah, wrote to the churches: "Behold, I lay in Zion a
cornerstone, elect, precious, and he who believes on him" shall not be put to
shame. Then he adds, interpreting the prophecy and speaking in agreement with
Simeon: "To you therefore who believe is the honor; but to those who do not
believe, a stone which the builders rejected, this has become the head of the
corner and a stone of stumbling and a rock of offense; those who stumble,
being disobedient to the word." Therefore he was set for a fall to the
unbelievers and for a rising to the faithful; for those, seeming to stand
according to the law, threw themselves down by unbelief; but these, being
among the fallen through the multitude of their sins, running to the staff of
faith, raised themselves up.
10 "Behold, this child is set for the fall and rising of many in Israel and
for a sign spoken against." What is this? The cross; for that sign, since it
was full of both sufferings and wonders, was spoken against; for he was naked
and he hid the sun, he was pierced with nails and he shook the rocks, his side
was opened with a spear and he opened the tombs of the dead. Hence the sign
was spoken against; for the unbelievers, leaving these things aside, looked at
those; but the faithful, together with the sufferings, beheld the wonders.
Thus the crowd of the Pharisees then mocked him as a deceiver, but the
centurion said he was Son of God, and having said it he worshipped.
11 What follows is in agreement with these: "And a sword will pass through
your own soul also, that the thoughts of many hearts may be revealed." "A
sword through your soul": discernment will pass through at the time of the
cross; for you will marvel seeing hanging on a cross the one born without
corruption, the one conceived without a man, the one who did not open the womb
and who accomplished his own birth without passion, without corruption.
12 And what will come of this? "That the thoughts of many hearts may be
revealed": perhaps of others as well, but much more of those who had been
disciples of the Lord, and had seen the sufferings concerning him and the
wonders from him, and had endured a storm from the thoughts on both sides,
such as Cleopas suffered <from> the things which Christ, meeting him on the
way to Emmaus, revealed to him; for when Cleopas was asked why they are
downcast and their heart is confused, having spoken a little about Christ and
as much as the time allowed, not knowing to whom he speaks, he adds: "But we
were hoping that he was the one who was going to redeem Israel," that is,
things foreign to our hope have come about concerning him. The thought of Mary
Magdalene also was revealed as being weak; when Christ had been crucified it
grew weak, when she too was saying to him: "Lord, if you have carried him
away, tell me where you have laid him, and I will take him away."
13 Let the tongue of the Gospel narrative run through what remains: "And there
was Anna, a prophetess, daughter of Phanuel, of the tribe of Asher; she was
advanced in many days, having lived with a husband seven years from her
virginity, and she was a widow of about eighty-four years." Splendid is Anna's
wealth; from every side the treasure of good things is gathered to her, first
because she was a prophetess; for though she had a weak vessel as being a
woman's, she kept it clean, and she drew the Spirit of God to herself for a
dwelling; then because she was an old woman and, having grown old in body, she
did not grow old in mind; third because she was a widow, and not simply a
widow, but having lived with her husband seven years in number, and being
advanced in many days and drawing the yoke of widowhood for so many years, she
did not betray the bed to a second bridegroom, for which reason she was
driving her age toward the eightieth year of life.
14 But since she had surely seen the one clearly proclaimed by the Spirit in
the eighty-fourth psalm, she sought to say concerning him such things as David
the psalmist, putting them forth, had sung: "You have been well pleased, Lord,
with your land, you have turned back the captivity of Jacob; you have forgiven
the iniquities of your people, you have covered all their sins. You have made
all your wrath to cease, you have turned from the wrath of your anger. Truth
has sprung from the earth, and righteousness has looked down from heaven"; for
in these things is "the redemption of Jerusalem," which the woman is next
recorded thus both to proclaim and to prophesy; for it says: "who did not
depart from the temple." Many dwell in the temple, but while dwelling they
insult it, as many as do not dwell as Anna happened to dwell, "serving with
fasts and prayers night and day"; for by the fasts she purified the body, and
by the prayers she winged the mind of the soul, and she was dwelling as a
temple in the temple.
15 "And coming up at that very hour": for surely the Spirit led her too, just
as also Simeon, to the doxology of the infant. "She gave thanks to God": she
was not simply glorifying but giving thanks in return, crying out things
similar to what had been said by Simeon. "And she spoke about him to all who
were waiting for redemption in Jerusalem." Saying what things, and of what
sort? "Do not be led astray by the flesh, do not stumble at the form of a
servant, let not the infant disturb you." He who forms infants appearing also
as a child, the Father of the ages seen as an infant; for otherwise we could
not contain seeing the Redeemer, otherwise we were not able to receive him.
For our sake he humbled himself, for us he came down; sparing our eyes, he
hides the glorious things, he puts forward the lowly. To him be the glory unto
the ages of ages. Amen.
