Of the same
To those everywhere in each place who practice the monastic life and are
established in the faith of God and sanctified in Christ and who say
"behold, we have left all things and have followed you," to the beloved
and most longed-for brothers, very much greeting in the Lord.
1.1 Persuaded by your disposition and often urged by you, I wrote in few
words concerning the things which we have suffered, both we and the
Church, and as far as power was mine refuting the abominable heresy of the
Ariomaniacs and showing how far this is alien to the truth. And what I
suffered while writing I thought it necessary to make known to your piety,
so that from this too you might learn how truly the blessed apostle has
said "O depth of riches and wisdom and 1.2 knowledge of God," and that you
might pardon me also, being a man weak by nature. For as much as I wished
to write and forced myself to think concerning the Godhead of the Word, so
much did the knowledge withdraw far from me, and I knew myself to fall
short as much as I seemed to grasp. For not even what I seemed to
understand was I able to write, but even what I wrote became less than the
shadow of the truth that had come to be in the mind, even if it was a
slight one.
2.1 Seeing then, as it is written in Ecclesiastes, that "I said, I will
become wise, and it receded from me far beyond what it was, and a deep
depth; who will find it?" and that in the Psalms: "Your knowledge was made
wonderful from me; it was strengthened, I shall not be able toward it,"
and Solomon says: "The glory of God hides a word," I often resolved to
hold back and to cease from writing; yes, believe it. But lest I should
seem both to grieve you and those 2.2 among you who have inquired and
contended, to wear them down into impiety from remaining silent, I forced
myself to write a few things, and these I sent to your godliness. For even
if, as the grasping of the truth is, it is now far from us because of the
weakness of the flesh, yet it is possible, as Ecclesiastes himself said,
"to know the fol 2.3 ly of the ungodly and, having found it, to say that
it is more bitter than death." For this reason, then, as knowing this and
being able to find it, I wrote, knowing that for the faithful the
condemnation of impiety is knowledge sufficient for piety. For even if it
is not possible to grasp what God is, yet it is possible to say what he is
not. And we know that he is not as a man 2.4 is, and that it is not lawful
to conceive anything of generated things in him. Thus also concerning the
Son of God, even if by nature we are far from grasping, yet it is possible
and easy to condemn the things put forward by the heretics and to say that
these things the Son of God is not, nor is it lawful concerning his
Godhead even to think such things as those men say, much less also to
utter them through the lips.
3.1 Thus then, as far as I was able, I wrote; but you, beloved, receive
these things not as having a perfect interpretation concerning the Godhead
of the Word, but as only refuting the impiety of the Christ-fighters, and
as having and giving to those who wish a starting-point toward piety of
the sound faith in Christ. And if anything has been left out (and I think
everything has been left out), pardon with a pure conscience and only
receive 3.2 the boldness of the purpose toward piety. For for a perfect
condemnation of the heresy of the Arians the judgment that took place from
the Lord concerning the death of Arius is sufficient, which you already,
having come beforehand, also learned from others. "For the things which
the holy God has planned, who will scatter?" And whom the Lord has
condemned, who is the one who justifies? For who from such a sign that has
taken place does not know henceforth that the heresy is hated by God, even
if it has men as cham3.3 pions? Having read, then, pray and urge one
another to do this on our behalf, and immediately send these things back
to us. And give a copy to no one at all, nor transcribe it for yourselves,
but be content, as approved money-changers, with the reading, even if you
wish to read it many times. For it is not safe for those after these
things, the writings from us who stam3.4 mer and are unlearned. Greet one
another in love and all who come to you in piety and faith. "For if
anyone," as the apostle said, "does not love the Lord, let him be
anathema. The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you. Amen."
