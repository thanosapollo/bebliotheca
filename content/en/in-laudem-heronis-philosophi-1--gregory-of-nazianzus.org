ORATION XXV.
On Hero the Philosopher.
A. I will praise the philosopher, even if I am weary in the body; for he is a
philosopher; and I will praise him even very reasonably. For he is the
philosopher, and I a servant of wisdom; so that the praise is also according
to reason for me, that, if nothing else, I may at least philosophize this, to
admire a philosopher. For either one must philosophize, as my word has it, or
one must honor philosophy, if we are not going to fall altogether outside the
good, nor be condemned for irrationality, having been made rational, and
hastening through the Word toward the Word. And let the man philosophize for
us, as one of the other things, being praised, and let him endure the eulogy.
For we will not praise in order to gratify (we know the philosopher's lack of
ambition, and besides the word would add nothing to the deeds, except that it
might even fall short of the worth through its own weakness), but that we may
benefit ourselves. For this philosophy will no =35.1200= longer despise, for
which to do some good to our life is work and pursuit. And first of the
benefactions is that the good things be praised. For praise is a patron of
zeal; and zeal, of virtue; and virtue, of blessedness; and that is the summit
of things desired, and toward which every motion of a serious man tends.
B. Come then, O best and most perfect of philosophers; and I will add, that
also of martyrs of the truth. Come to me, O refutation of the bastard wisdom,
that which lies in word, and bewitches through eloquence, but beyond this is
neither able nor willing to be lifted up at all; the one ambidextrous in
virtue, both as much as is of contemplation, and as much as is of action; the
one philosophizing our things in a foreign habit; or perhaps not even foreign,
if the wearing of bright clothing, and the brightness, is angelic, when they
are formed bodily; a symbol, I think, this of the purity according to their
nature. Come to me, philosopher and wise man (for until when is the loving of
wisdom, if wisdom is nowhere?), and dog, not the shamelessness, but the
boldness of speech; nor the gluttony, but the living by the day; nor the
barking, but the guard of the good, and the sleeplessness on behalf of souls,
and the fawning on as much as is akin according to virtue, but barking at as
much as is foreign. Come to me, stand near the sacred things, and this
mystical table, and me who through these initiate into deification, to which
word and life lead you, and the purification through suffering. Come, I will
crown you with our crowns, and I will proclaim you with a bright voice, not in
the midst of Olympia, nor in a small theater of Greece, nor as one who has
contested the pancration, or boxing, or the double course, or some of the
small contests, and for small prizes, nor unto the honor of some heroes or
daemons, honored by misfortune and myth (for such are their august and honored
things, having taken on time as an ally of the folly, and lawful custom); but
before God, and angels, and all the fullness of the Church; and as one who has
conquered the lie of heresies, unto the honor of the living God, who also
teaches the suf =35.1201= fering by his own sufferings, and whose prize is the
kingdom of heavens, and to become God higher than suffering.
C. But what is the proclamation? If you long for the shorter and more
well-known: This is a most unlying contestant of the truth, and a champion of
the Trinity unto blood, and a pursuer of those who persecute by doing evil, by
suffering eagerly; for nothing so conquers the persecutor as the eagerness of
the one who suffers. And if the more perfect and broader: This is best from
the best, and

from nobles the most noble. And I speak of nobility, not that which the many
think; away with it! It is not for us so to admire, nor for a philosopher,
that which comes from myths and tombs and a long-since-rotted eyebrow; nor
that which is added from bloods and from letters, which nights grant, and the
hands of kings perhaps not even noble, commanding, as some other thing, the
nobility; but that which piety characterizes, and manner, and the ascent
toward the first good, from which we have come to be. Of this nobility there
is one proof. For the noble man is not only an athlete for us, but also from
martyrs; so that the pattern of virtue is from his own house. And a citizen,
in wisdom, of the whole inhabited world (for the Cynic way does not endure to
be circumscribed by small bounds); but in body, of the city of the
Alexandrians, which is numbered with you, or immediately after you; of which,
though it prevails over all in all things, heat is the most peculiar, and of
this the fairest, Christianity, and the disposition concerning this, beginning
from choice, but being confirmed by nature. For the heat having approached
piety became zeal; and zeal, security of faith.
D. And having been reared and educated thus, as was likely for one born from
such and about to be such, when the time of a choice of lives seemed to be
(which I set as the foundation of acting well or ill), he saw something great,
and youthful, and higher than the many. Luxury he dishonors, and abundance,
and power, more than those who excel in these things dishonor the others; and
the one as first misery, and the other as last poverty, and the other as the
highest weakness, he spits upon and sends away. For nothing is a good, which
neither makes those who possess it better, but even worse as for the most
part; nor remains through to the end with those who have it. And he sets
philosophy in front, the mistress of the passions, and he advances toward the
good youthfully, and from the matter he is cut =35.1204= off, before being
separated from the matter; and he rises up against the things seen, by
greatness of nature and nobility of choice, having attached himself to the
things that stand. When he had thus thought, this he did not even think worthy
of counsel, which of the philosophies one must rather choose, the outside one
and playing with the shadows of the truth in the habit and pretence of
philosophy, or ours, humble in the appearing, but high in the hidden, and
leading toward God; but by all votes he chooses ours, having turned aside the
mind toward the worse not at all, nor having been swept away by the elegance
of words, in which those who philosophize the things of the Greeks think great
things. And that he philosophizes first, to know of our ways the more
choiceworthy and more profitable equally for himself and for all Christians.
For everywhere to take together the thing according to himself and the common,
he thought to be of a most perfect and most philosophic soul. For each of us
has not been born for himself only, but also for all who share the same
nature, and have come to be from the same, and upon the same things.
E. And seeing the desert life and the private, and one foreign and alien from
the many, great and high, and above the human things, but standing as far as
those who succeed only, and denying the social and philanthropic of love,
which he knew to be among the first of things praised; and besides also
untested, as being neither exercised in the affairs, nor measured against
others; but the social and mixed, besides the trial of virtue, still also
stretching unto the many, and near to divine economy, which both made the all,
and bound it with friendship, and our race that had fallen from the good
through the evil that came in, called back again from the mixing and converse
toward us; these things having thought through and divided, and at the same
time having thought it among the good things to chasten the Greek pride, who
put on the august under the cloak and the beard; what does he do, and how does
he handle philosophy? He goes a certain middle way of both their

arrogance, and our wisdom, and of them he philosophizes the habit and the
stage, but of us the truth and the height. =35.1205=
F. For this reason Peripatoi, and Academies, and the august Stoa, and the
automaton of Epicurus with the atoms and the pleasure, having crowned with
wool, as one of those did the poet, he sends as far away as possible and beats
off. And of the Cynic, having spat out the godless, but having praised the
unadorned, this is what you now see, a dog against the dogs in truth, and a
philosopher against the unwise, and a Christian above all, and conquering
their stubbornness by the likeness of the habit, and of some among us the want
of taste by the newness of the garment, showing that the pious is not in small
things, nor the philosophic in the downcast look, but in firmness of soul and
purity of mind, and a genuine inclination toward the good, however we may have
the habit, and with whomsoever we may converse, whether gathering the mind
from the senses unto ourselves alone, or being private in the common with the
many and those of the same race, and philosophizing among those who do not
philosophize (as that ark of Noah, in a flood lighter than a flood; and the
bush of the mountain, which the fire burning did not consume, the great vision
of Moses), neither ourselves suffering anything from the many, no more than
adamant from those who strike, and through ourselves making the others better
unto our power. Fruit of this philosophy is not cities molded in word (certain
skindapsoi, as they themselves say, and goat-stags, which tongue alone
composes); nor certain categories, and analyses, and mixtures; nor accidents
and para-accidents, and the technologized wisdom; nor certain lines lying
nowhere, nor weavings and figures of stars invented against Providence. For
these things are for him of second account and by-works, and played so much,
as not to be played by those who pretend to know. =35.1208=
G. And what is the first and the thing pursued? The pleading toward rulers,
the boldness of speech toward kings, according to the divine David speaking
before kings and not being ashamed, to restrain the irrationality of peoples
surging, the authority of dynasts raging, the infirmity of houses in faction,
the rusticity of the uneducated, the arrogance of the educated, wealth being
lifted up, satiety committing insolence, poverty doing evil, anger being
carried out and carrying out the reasoning, the measurelessness of pleasure,
the incontinence of laughter, to stop the distress of grief, the unevenness of
youth, the smallness of soul of old age, the desolation of widowhood, the
despair of orphanhood. These things than the syllogisms and the lines, and the
gaping toward the stars, will not one who thinks well honor them far before?
having considered this, that if all were gathering, or geometrizing, or
astronomizing, our life would not be benefited by this, except that even all
things would be dissolved; but if these things I spoke of were taken away, the
all is disorder and confusion. These things than the arrogance of Antisthenes,
and the dainty-eating of Diogenes, and the common-marriage of Crates, what
need to say how much better and higher they are? Only we spare those also by
reverence of the name, that they may enjoy something of the man. And we, that
we may gather the things in the middle, the temperate, the continent, the
unpuffed, the gracious, the social, the philanthropic, the other things in
which the man is above all, will turn to the last in order, but first in
power.
H. There was a time when we had a calm from the heresies, when Simons, and
Marcions, and certain Valentini, and Basilidae, and Cerdons, Cerinthi and
Carpocrates, and all the nonsense and marvel-mongering about them, having cut
the God of the all to the utmost, and having warred for the Good against the
Demiurge, were then swallowed by their own depth and given over to the
silence, as was worthy. And the wicked spirit of Montanus, and the darkness of
Manes, and the rashness of Novatus, or

purity, and Sabellius's evil advocacy of the monarchy yielded, and withdrew,
the one having been separated into an opposing portion, and the other having
been altogether despised =35.1209= and thrown aside through the weakness; and
nothing else was troubling the Church. For the persecutions even made it more
bright by the sufferings. Not much in the middle, and a second storm is raised
against the Church, the typhoon of injustice, the fullness of impiety, the
legion of the spirits, the antichrist tongue, the mind that spoke injustice
unto the height, the cutting of the Godhead; of which the undertaking is
terrible, but the end more terrible, and worthy of the betrayal of Judas,
which that man dared against our Savior, Arius, who is well named together
with the madness. This man, having begun from the city of the Alexandrians,
and having practiced the terrible thing there, then, as some wild flame, from
a small spark having run over the much of the inhabited world, is dissolved by
our Fathers, and by the pious number that then occupied Nicaea, and set the
theology within circumscribed bounds and words.
I. Again a wicked empire, and again the evil lives, and as the festering of
wounds is opened and broken out. And grievous wolves, another from another
place having taken us, tear the Church. And priests having been armed against
priests, and peoples having gone mad against peoples; and a king giving
boldness of speech to impiety, and legislating against the right opinion; and
those who are neither men nor women ruling with him. Who would tragedy the
then evils worthily? the exiles, the confiscations, the dishonors, the
assemblies in the desert, whole myriads and cities being distressed in the
open air, and being pressed by rains and winters? the uprisings from there
(for they did not even have the desert without danger); the things still
harsher than these, the outrages, the deaths, the triumphs of bishops,
philosophers, men, women, young, aged; those inventing the terrible things,
those inventing further, the dynasts serving the impiety, for whom this alone
often sufficed unto good repute, to appear more bitter than the will of the
one in power?
J. Just now the bright persecution had an end, and =35.1212= Persia well
judged for us, having dissolved the accursed one, and having exacted justice
of many bloods through one blood; and just now the unseemly one begins, and
having the defending of Christians as a pretence of the impiety, he sets upon
the truly Christians; and he becomes so much harsher than that one, by as much
as then the contest was more conspicuous and more bright, but now even the
suffering is without honor, at least to the unjust reckoners of the
sufferings. Do you wish that I move tears for the theater, and perhaps even
for the most enduring himself, and one greater than the sufferings, having
recalled one of the things that then happened? And witnesses of the word are
many; for the tragedy of the suffering came even unto many; and I think even
the time about to be will take up the narrative of the season. A ship having a
cargo of one of the presbyters, and this one not even in danger for some evil,
but for faith, is let go upon the sea, not that it might save the passenger,
but that it might destroy him. And the cargo is eager; for he is pious. And
fire is a fellow-traveler to the cargo; and the persecutor luxuriates in the
novelty of the punishment. Alas of the spectacle! Alas of the drama! The ship
is on the sea; the theater upon the shores, some taking pleasure, and some
lamenting. How might I present the much in a little? the fire is kindled, the
ship is consumed, the cargo is consumed with it, fire is mixed with water, and
the contraries run together unto punishment of a pious man, and two elements
divide one body, and a strange beacon is raised over the sea. To which perhaps
someone approached, as to a gentle and philanthropic thing; but having
approached, he found a pitiable and unbelievable spectacle, a voyage without a
pilot, a shipwreck without a storm; and the presbyter is dust, and not even
dust, having been sown in the waters. And not even so much did the priesthood,
as

to obtain at least a more seemly end; and if not an end, yet a burial at any
rate, which is owed even to the impious. Such was the expedition of the
impious; such the end of the pious; and nowhere a fire from above more holy,
nor punitive of the one kindling such things.
K. But what is it to me of the things from outside? One must already go upon
your own contest, and your contests on behalf of piety, which upon all the
things that went before you set as some fair seal. The evil of this heresy was
at its height in your city, from which =35.1213= it also began. And when the
most holy eye of the inhabited world, and high priest of the priests, the
leader and teacher of your confession, by his own contests concerning piety,
the great voice, the support of the faith, the second lamp and forerunner of
Christ, if it is lawful to say this, having fallen asleep in a good old age,
and full of the days according to God, after the slanders, after the contests,
after the famous hand, after the living dead, the Trinity transfers toward
itself, with which he lived, and on behalf of which he was in danger
(Athanasius I know that you all have read in the words); and a second plague
and scourge of Egypt introduces himself to the Church, the betrayer of the
truth, the shepherd of wolves, the thief climbing over through the court, the
second Arius, the turbid and undrinkable overthrow, the more abundant river of
the godless spring; I shrink from speaking the then lawlessnesses and
blood-guiltinesses, with which the beast takes the holy throne, and the
preliminaries of the wicked entrance; yet I lament a few from many (which you
also lament and have lamented beforehand), having used together the divine
David: O God, nations came into your inheritance, they defiled your holy
temple, and the things upon these; They set the corpses of your servants as
food of birds, and torn things of beasts. And I will add from him also those
from another lament and another song: How many things the enemy did wickedly
in your holy place, and they who hate you boasted in the midst of your feast.
For how did they not boast? and how did they not defile your holy temple with
manifold misfortunes and all kinds of evils?
L. An atheist and lawless man was general, not even wearing the name of a
Christian (for this was the most terrible of the outrage), but hastening from
the idols upon the temple of God, from the unclean bloods upon the more
abominable and more detestable, and perhaps celebrating this as a sacrifice to
the daemons, the outrage against us; and a force boiling with anger was
arrayed, an alien and untamed army against the unarmed and unwarlike. The
priest successor of the holy one was thrust out, the one anointed by law and
order of the Spirit, and honored by grey hair and prudence; and Tabeel was
king, the heir =35.1216= of the things of others. Weapons against the holy
ones, polluted hands against the inviolable, trumpets against the songs.
Consider for me the things following these, men falling in the holy places,
women being trampled, some even with the cargo of nature, the untimely births
and un-births, to speak more properly; virgins being dragged pitiably, being
outraged more shamefully (I am ashamed to speak the manner both to women and
to men, and to strip by the word the hidden things of the shame, at which even
then having been stripped I am ashamed), some being carried down wells of
those within the temple, and some being thrown from the upper rooms at the
absurdity of the things seen, and some being heaped upon those lying; the
murders upon the murders, the corpses upon the corpses, the holy things being
trampled by profane feet, altars being outraged by wanton figures and songs,
and as I hear (what will this daring tongue utter?) even by the dances and
twistings over them; blasphemous tongues speaking to the people upon the
sacred thrones, mysteries being made comedy, psalmodies being silenced, wails
being raised against them, channels of bloods, springs of tears, priests being
led, solitaries

being torn, every image of the Assyrians' raid, which once they raided upon
holy Jerusalem; which neither word is able to present worthily, nor hearing to
contain, but of Jeremiah's soul and voice alone to lament worthily; who both
seeks springs of tears, and calls forth a lament from walls upon such
sufferings, and casts mourning upon the ways of Sion, not leading those who
keep the feast.
M. This drama the eastern lot saw, and the western also lamented, to which the
fugitive priest triumphed the symbols of the madness. In what manner? He set
forth to the Church of the Romans, instead of the dead, the bloodied garment,
and he moves nationwide tears through the silent accusation, that he might
both present the suffering, and take an ally of the terrible things, as indeed
we have also known him to have taken; since that which excels most loves to be
bent toward the weak, and by willing goodwill to be added to the one being
diminished. This not even one of the other pious men bore; but it moves you
even more, by as much as you are also more perfect in the word, and hotter in
the zeal. For this reason you war on behalf of the Word, and you are warred
upon by the impiety; and besides many other things, in which acting and
speaking on behalf of the good you have contested, teaching, admonishing,
delivering, refuting, rebuking, con =35.1217= founding, private men, rulers,
privately, publicly, at every time and place; at last, having been seized by
the madness of an impious authority, (O the noble misfortune! O your sacred
wounds!) you are carded as to the fair body by the whips, as if a spectator of
another suffering; and you are bent as to the thing seen, but you are not
thrown down as to the thing thought; you set up the manliness as a pillar in
the sights of all; and you become even being silent a teacher of endurance,
since at least the tongue has given up uttering.
N. Then what? You are banished from the fatherland, you who know no own and
foreign, as it seems to me, that others also may be educated through you in
the piety; Oasis is your place of exile, the inhuman desert, the place
henceforth holy through you. Share with us also of the good things of your
exile, since at least you have shared of those of the return. Make the return
a theater for us. Whom did you teach there to philosophize? whom did you
cleanse of the impious opinions? whom did you lead to the piety? I seem to
myself to see the school there, the rite and festival about you. Say this
also: Did you have some consolation for the remnants of the body? or did you
philosophize even the poverty? Did you have some partners of the contest? or
did you bear this also being poor? Did you long for the sisters, the partners
with you both of the purity and of the endurance? or were you even higher than
the familiarity of these? Did the desolation of an aged mother bend you? or
did you even exceedingly take courage, as having left them piety as a greatest
thing unto security? But since at least you have returned to us doing well,
and he who glorifies those who glorify him has given you back again longing to
those who long, and who is jealous of those who are jealous, who does the will
of those who fear him, and breathes resurrection into the dead; who life-makes
Lazarus of four days, but you of four years beyond hopes, and gathers bone
toward bone, and joint toward joint, the vision of Ezekiel, of the most
wondrous and highest of the prophets; hold for me again to the same work, and
boldness of speech, that you may not seem to have been cut short by the
sufferings, nor to betray philosophy by cowardice. =35.1220=
O. Confound also the superstition of the Greeks, as before, and their
polytheist atheism, and the old gods and the new, and the shameful myths, and
the more shameful sacrifices of those cleansing clay with clay, as I heard one
of them saying, I mean bodies with bodies, their own with those of the
irrational animals, and the august moldings, and the unseemly marvels; by
which if they define the Divine, of the ill-fortune; and if they exhibit it,
of the simplicity. For let them teach, what is the word, and what the mystery
of the unseemliness about these things? for not even the appearances of the
good things must have the shameful. And if they say there is some other thing
beside these, what

this is, let them persuade, and from what words, or theologians. Confound also
the uprisings of the heresies, and so much the hotter, by as much as you have
suffered beforehand. For the philosophic is more noble from the suffering, as
iron fiery by cold, thus being tempered by the dangers. And define also our
piety, teaching to know one unbegotten God, the Father; and one begotten Lord,
the Son; being addressed as God, when he is spoken of according to himself;
but as Lord, when he is named with Father; the one through the nature, and the
other through the monarchy. And one Holy Spirit, having proceeded from the
Father, or also proceeding; God, to those who noetically understand the things
that lie beside; being both warred upon by the impious, and being understood
by those above these, and being also spoken by the more spiritual. Neither to
make the Father under a beginning, that we may not introduce something first
of the first, from which even the being first will be overturned; nor the Son
or the Holy Spirit without beginning, that we may not take away the proper of
the Father. For they are not without beginning, and they are without beginning
in a way; which is also paradoxical. For they are not without beginning as to
the cause; for they are from God, even if not after him, as light from sun;
but without beginning as to the time. For they are not under time, that the
flowing may not be older than the things that stand, and the unsubstantial
than the substances.
P. Neither three beginnings, that it may not be Greek, or the =35.1221=
polytheist; nor one, but a certain Jewish narrow, and envious, and impossible;
either by consuming Godhead into itself, which pleased those who lead the Son
forth from the Father, but dissolve him into him again; or by throwing down
the natures, and alienating from Godhead, which pleases the wise men now, as
if fearing they might be set up against, or being able nothing beyond the
creatures. Neither unbegotten the Son, for the Father is one; nor the Spirit a
Son, for the Only-begotten is one; that they also may have this as divine, the
unique, the one of the sonship, and the other of the procession, and not of
sonship. Truly Father the Father, and much more truly than those among us,
because uniquely, for in a proper manner, and not as the bodies; and alone,
for not with a pairing; and of one only, for of the Only-begotten; and only,
for he was not a Son before; and whole Father, and of the whole, for ours is
unclear; and from the beginning, for not later. Truly Son the Son, because
only, and of one only, and uniquely, and only; for he is not also Father, and
whole Son, and of the whole, and from the beginning, never having begun to be
Son; for the Godhead is not from a change of mind, nor the deification from
advance, that there should ever be lacking, to the one the being Father, and
to the other the being Son. Truly holy the Holy Spirit; for there is not also
another such, nor thus, nor is the sanctification from addition, but holiness
itself; nor more and less, nor having begun temporally or about to cease. For
common to Father and Son and Holy Spirit is the not having come to be, and the
Godhead; and to Son and Holy Spirit, the from the Father. And proper to Father
is the unbegottenness; and to Son, the generation; and to Spirit, the
sending-forth. And if you seek the manner, what will you leave to those
testified to know one another only, and to be known by one another, or even to
those of us who will be illumined from there later?
Q. Become some of the things said before, or such, and then you will know so
much, as to be known by one another. But now teach to know this much only, a
monad in Trinity, and a Trinity in monad being worshipped, having both the
division and the union paradoxical. Do not fear the passions, confessing
generation. For the Divine is impassible, even if it has generated. I am a
surety of this to you, that it is divinely, but not humanly. For not even the
being is human to it. But fear time and creation. For he is not =35.1224= God,
if he has come to be, lest advocating for God in vain, you destroy God, making
the co-divine a fellow-slave, which also frees you from the slavery, if you
genuinely confess the lordship. Do not fear the procession; for God does not
have a necessity, either not to send forth, or to send forth likewise, he who
is rich in all things; but fear the alienation,

and the threat that lies, not for those who theologize, but for those who
blaspheme the Holy Spirit.
R. Neither honor the monarchy badly, gathering together or cutting around
Godhead; nor be ashamed of the charge of tritheism, so long as another also is
in danger of ditheism. For either you have dissolved together, or you have
been in difficulty together, or the one has been shipwrecked with the
reasonings and Godhead, but Godhead has remained for you. And if the word has
been weak, it is better to toil in the reasonings with the guidance of the
Spirit, than to be impious readily, pursuing the easiness. Spit for me upon
the objections, and the oppositions, and the new piety, and the petty wisdom;
and spit more than upon the threads of spiders, which hold flies, but are
broken by wasps, I do not yet say by fingers, nor by some other of the heavier
bodies. Teach to fear one thing only, the dissolving of the faith in the
sophisms. It is not terrible to be defeated in word, for the word is not of
all; but it is terrible to be fined of Godhead, for the hope is of all. These
things then you will philosophize also by yourself, I know that, both more
eagerly and more perfectly; your wounds guarantee it to me, and your body that
has toiled on behalf of piety; and we also will philosophize together, as much
as power.
S. And you, when you go abroad the fair going-abroad, remember for me the
Trinity dwelling in tents, if at all God dwells in things made with hands, and
this small harvest, not from small seeds of the piety, yet still small and
poor, and being gathered little by little. For we have become as those
gathering stubble in harvest, if one must speak the thing of the Prophet in
season, and as a grape- =35.1225= gleaning in vintage, there not being a
cluster. You see how great the gathering is; and for this reason hasten to
make the threshing-floor richer for us, and the wine-vat fuller. Narrate also
our calling, and the unbelievable sojourn, which we have made not so as to
luxuriate together, but so as to suffer evil together; that having shared the
terrible things, we may also share the glory. This people you have as a helper
with the prayers, this as a fellow-traveler, the flock narrow in number, and
not narrow in piety; whose narrowness I revere more than the breadth of
others. These things says the Holy Spirit; with this you will also pass
through fire, and you will put beasts to sleep, and you will tame dynasts.
Thus to go abroad, thus to be sent, and to return to us again, richly rich, a
second crown-wearer, and with us about to sing the victory-song, both now and
later in Christ Jesus our Lord, to whom the glory unto the ages. Amen.
