8. There was a certain rich man, making merry splendidly each day; and a
certain poor man Lazarus had been thrown at this man's gate, ulcered,
according to the evangelical history. It therefore came to pass for both to
die and for this poor man to go away into the rest, and the other into the
punishment. Have these things already happened and has a worthy recompense
been allotted to each, or does it form in these the image of the coming
judgment? But, he says, when he names the appellation of Lazarus, it truly
happened and was done. For why did he not say A certain poor man, but Lazarus?
that by the appellation he may show that these things have been done in
experience and in truth. Solution The judgment etc.
9. If Hosea the prophet took a harlot wife and begot from her in deed and in
energy, or he speaks things understood prophetically. If Melchizedek is not
simply a man nor a spirit, but a man having a beginning of generation not from
men but recently created by God.
CYR. Concerning these chapters a long word has been made by us, when we wrote
on Hosea the prophet, and in the book concerning Genesis much testing is found
concerning Melchizedek, and it is permitted for your piety to meet with the
books and from there to take the mind of the things said on each.
