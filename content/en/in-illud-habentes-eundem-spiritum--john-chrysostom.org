Again on the same saying
"But having the same Spirit of faith, according to what is written;" and why
they all receive the good things in common, and concerning almsgiving.
1. Having taken one saying of the Apostle at the former assembly and at the
one before that, we spent the whole discourse on the interpretation of it; and
today too we are eager to linger again on this same one; and we do this on
purpose for the benefit of your love, not for our own display. For it is not
so that I may show myself some fertile and many-minded man, but so that I may
both un=51.290=cover the wisdom of Paul to you, and awaken your eagerness,
that I handle the discourse in this way. For both the depth of that man's
understanding appears greater, when from one saying he begets for us so many
rivers of thoughts; and you, having learned that even from one apostolic word
it is possible to harvest an unspeakable wealth of philosophy, will not run
past the Epistles simply, but will be led on, being fed by these hopes, to
examine each of the sayings that lie in them with much =51.291= diligence. For
if one word has begotten for us a discourse of three days, how great a
treasure will a whole passage well up for us when it is contemplated with
exactness? Let us not then grow weary, until we have gathered the whole. For
if those who dig mines of gold, however much wealth they empty from there, do
not stand off before they have taken up all the gold; much more must we use
greater eagerness and diligence about the search of the divine oracles. For we
too are digging gold, not sensible, but spiritual; for we are working not
mines of earth, but mines of the Spirit.
For the epistles of Paul are mines and springs of the Spirit; mines, because
they furnish us wealth more precious than all gold; springs, because they
never fail; but however much you empty from there, so much and much more flows
in again. And a clear proof of this would be all the time that has passed. For
since Paul came to be, five hundred years have now gone by; and through all
this time many writers, many teachers and interpreters have often drawn much
from there, and have not emptied the wealth that lies stored. For the treasure
is not sensible; for this reason it is not spent by the many hands of those
who dig, but it increases and abounds. And why do I speak of those before? How
many after us will speak, and after them others again, and the wealth will not
cease to spring forth, nor will the mines fail in this? for they are
spiritual, and are not of a nature to be spent at any time. What then is the
apostolic saying, concerning which we discoursed even formerly toward your
love? But having the same Spirit of faith, according to what is written: I
believed, therefore I spoke.
2. At that time we were inquiring for what reason he said, the same Spirit of
faith; and we have said one cause so far; and this was, to show that the Old
is in agreement with the New. For when the same Spirit of faith appears both
to have moved the tongue of David that says, I believed, therefore I spoke,
and to be at work in the soul of Paul, it is quite clear that there is much
kinship of prophets and apostles, and that there must be much agreement of Old
and New. But so that we may not, stirring the same things again, trouble you,
come, let us also say the other cause for which he said, the same; for then
too we promised you that we would say another cause of this saying. But rouse
yourselves; for this thought which I am about to speak toward your love is
deep, and

needs a discerning mind, and a most sharp soul; therefore I entreat you to
follow with exactness the things that are about to be said. For if the labor
is ours, yet the gain is yours, or rather not even the labor is ours but the
gift of the grace of the Spirit; and when it reveals, neither the speaker nor
the hearers grow weary; for great is the ease of the revelation. Let us
attend then with exactness; for even if you follow the greater part, but nod
off about a small portion, you will be ignorant of the whole of the beauty,
the sequence having been cut off thereafter. And just as those who do not
know the roads and need others to guide them, even if they go far forward
following them, but having been a little careless lose the leader, there will
be no benefit to them of the former following, but they stand thereafter, not
knowing where they should go forward; so also those who follow=51.292= the
speaker, if, having attended through all the teaching, they are a little
careless, having thrown away the whole sequence, they will no longer be able
thereafter to stand upon the end of the thoughts. So that you may not suffer
this, through all the things that are about to be said give me equal
diligence, until we come to the very end.
3. For what reason then he said, But having the same Spirit of faith, and is
eager to show that both in the Old and in the New faith is the mother of the
good things, it is necessary to speak a little from above; for thus the cause
will be more clear to us. What then is the cause? A great war surrounded the
faithful when these things were being said, a hard and undeclared war. For
both whole cities and peoples were rising against them from every side, and
all tyrants were plotting, and kings were making ready, and arms were being
moved, and swords were being sharpened, and armies were being prepared, and
every kind of punishment and vengeance was being devised; hence plunderings
and confiscations of goods, and daily arrests and deaths, and rackings, and
prisons, and fire, and iron, and wild beasts, and the wood, and the wheel, and
pits, and precipices, and everything that came into thought toward the
destruction of the faithful was being moved; and not even as far as these did
the war stand. For it was not being kindled only by the enemies, but even
nature itself was being split against itself. For fathers were making war on
children, and daughters were hating those who bore them, and friends were
turning away from friends, and this war, creeping, was entering into kindreds
and into houses, and there was then much tumult throughout the inhabited
world.
And just as a ship, when waves are rising, clouds bursting, thunders breaking,
gloom circling the hull from every side, the sea raging, wild beasts rising
up, pirates attacking, those within themselves in faction, would not escape
the danger, unless the hand from above, the strong and great, should beat off
the war, and having loosed the storm should set those who sail in a calm; so
indeed it also happened then at the beginning of the preaching. For the storm
was not attacking from without only, but those within also were often in
faction against one another. Who says this? Paul himself writing, Without,
fightings, within, fears. And that these things are true, and that countless
evils had fenced around both teachers and disciples, and that the war was
feeding on all, I bring forward again the testimony of Paul himself. And you
remember all the things that are being said, so that when you learn the
dangers, the temptations, the countless evils, which those who believed then
endured, you may give thanks more greatly even for this to God, who loosed all
those terrible things, and brought in a deep peace, and drove away the war,
and prepared a great calm; so that no one, being slothful, may think to escape
punishment, nor, succeeding, may be lifted up now.


4. For it is not the same thing to be able to stand nobly when warred upon
from every side and flooded around with countless evils, and now, sitting as
in a harbor and enjoying all freedom from fear, to show the same eagerness.
For those men then were in no better state than those who are tossed on the
open sea and surged upon; but we now live more without fear =51.293= than
those who sit in a harbor. Let us not then either think great things of our
achievements, nor fall down at the temptations that befall, nor use the
freedom of peace toward sloth; but let us always be sober and watch. For
there is a struggle for us too against the desires of nature. Men do not now
rise against us, but the pleasures of the flesh rise; tyrants and kings do
not make war, but anger makes war, love of vainglory, envy, jealousies, and
the countless passions of the soul. Since then we have been freed from those
temptations, let us prevail over these. For this reason I reminded you of the
vexations of those times, so that both he who is now in affliction may take
sufficient consolation from there, and he who enjoys much freedom from fear,
in place of the helplessness of those dangers, may bring in much eagerness
into the battle against the absurd reasonings.
For all those things were written for our admonition and consolation and
endurance; which it is necessary now to speak toward you, and to teach the
greatness of the terrible things that then surrounded the faithful, not the
teachers only, but also the disciples. Hear then what Paul says writing to the
Hebrews: Remember the former days, in which, having been enlightened, you
endured a great contest of sufferings. For not even a short time passed, but
in the very prefaces of the catechesis and of the teaching temptations rose
against them, and having been baptized they were at once in danger; what they
suffered, hear: This, being made a spectacle by reproaches and afflictions.
For all spat, insulted, laughed, mocked, called them fools, senseless, because
having stood off from the ancestral way of life they received a new doctrine.
And this is not a small thing toward shaking a soul, if the faith is not
rooted in the depth. For nothing so bites a soul as reproach; nothing so melts
soul and mind as jeers and revilings; for many men have often been tripped up
when reproached. And I say these things now, so that we may have the faith
with boldness. For if then, when the whole inhabited world was reproaching
them, they were not tripped up, much more now must the preaching of the truth
be held with boldness, when the whole inhabited world has changed sides toward
us. And that those men did not stop at accusation and reproaches and reviling,
but that those who suffered these things even rejoiced, hear what follows. For
you received with joy, he says, the plunder of your goods. Do you see that
even their substances were confiscated of old, and were laid out for plunder
to all who wished to abuse them. And these things he was saying writing to the
Hebrews.
5. And to the Thessalonians he testifies other such things again, saying: For
you became imitators, he says, of the Lord and of us, having received the word
in much affliction. See these too being afflicted, and not simply, but in much
affliction. For the temptation was with intensity, the danger continual, not
granting even a little breathing to those who were contending then. But
=51.294= nevertheless, even suffering these things they were not distressed,
nor did they despair, but rather they even rejoiced. From where is this clear?
From the very words of Paul; for having said, In much affliction, he added,
With joy of the Holy Spirit; showing that the temptations made the affliction,
but the cause of the temptations begot the joy for them. For it was enough for
consolation to be conscious to themselves that they were suffering these
things for Christ.


Therefore I do not so much marvel at those then, that they were afflicted, as
I marvel that being afflicted they rejoiced because of God. For this is of a
noble and God-loving soul, to be afflicted and to suffer ill; but to bear the
temptation nobly, and to give thanks to him who permits the afflictions, this
is of the greatest courage, this is of a soul that has been awakened and has
been freed from all human things. And not here only, but also elsewhere,
showing how many evils those who believed then suffered from their own people
and kinsmen (for this was the hardest), he says somehow thus: For you became
imitators, brethren, of the churches of God that are in Judea. In what
respect imitators? Because you also suffered the same things from your own
countrymen, just as they also from the Jews. Behold also a war, but also a
civil war, which makes the pain greater. For if an enemy had reproached me, I
would have borne it, he says; but you, a man of equal soul, my guide and my
acquaintance; which then was happening in a figure. Therefore they also
needed much consolation. Which then Paul also seeing, and seeing those ranked
under him growing weary and sweating, being weighed down by the greatness of
the misfortunes and pained by the successive blows, in many ways he raises up
their thoughts, now saying, If indeed it is a righteous thing with God to
repay affliction to those who afflict you, and to you who are afflicted rest
with us; now saying, The Lord is near, be anxious for nothing; and again, Do
not cast away your boldness; for you have need of endurance, so that having
done the will of God, you may receive the promise. Then, anointing them
toward endurance, he adds: For yet a little, how little, how little, he who
is coming will come, and will not delay. And just as someone sitting by a
child that is whimpering, distressed, and seeking its mother, consoles it
saying, Wait yet a little, and the mother will certainly meet you; so also
Paul, seeing those who believed then taking it ill, lamenting, seeking the
presence of Christ because of the unbearable onset of evils, consoling them
said: Yet a little, how little, how little, he who is coming will come, and
will not delay.
6. That the disciples then were afflicted, and suffered countless terrible
things, and like lambs seized in the midst of wolves were thus driven from
every side, is clear from these things; but so that you may learn that the
teachers also endured then not less than these, but even much harder things
(for by as much as they more greatly grieved the enemies =51.295= of the
truth, by so much also they were struck by more), let us hear these things
also from him who said the former things. For writing to the Corinthians he
spoke thus: Giving no offense in anything, so that our ministry may not be
blamed, but in everything commending ourselves as ministers of God, in much
endurance, in afflictions, in necessities, in distresses, in stripes, in
prisons, in tumults, in labors, in watchings, in fasts. Did you see how many
contests he numbered, how many snowstorms of temptations? Again writing to the
same, Are they ministers of Christ? he says, I speak as a madman, I more. Then
wishing to persuade us that to be afflicted for Christ is much more splendid
than to do signs, and making a proof of apostleship, and showing that he is
better than they, I do not mean than the apostles, but than the false
apostles, he makes the proof of the superiority not from wonders and signs,
but from successive dangers, saying thus: In labors more abundantly, in
stripes above measure, in prisons more abundantly, in deaths often; five times
I received from the Jews forty save one, three times I was beaten with rods,
once I was stoned, three times I was shipwrecked, a night and a day I have
spent in the deep; in journeyings often, in perils of rivers, in perils of
robbers, in perils from my own race, in perils from the nations, in perils in
the city, in perils in the wilderness, in perils in the sea, in perils among
false brethren; in labor


and toil, in watchings often, in hunger and thirst, in cold and nakedness,
besides the things that are without.
These are the marks of exact apostleship. For signs even many others have
often done, and they had no profit from the wonders, but after all those
things they heard: Depart, I do not know you, you who work lawlessness; but of
those able to say the things which Paul has now numbered, no one will hear
that voice; but with much boldness he will set foot upon the heavens and will
enjoy all the good things in the heavens.
7. Perhaps the discourse has become rather long for us; but do not fear; we
have not forgotten the promise, but we will come back to it again at once. And
we have not lengthened these things in vain, but wishing by a fuller
preparation to make the demonstration undisputed and clearer, and at the same
time to console the afflicted souls, so that each of those who are in
temptations and dangers, having taken sufficient consolation, may go away,
having learned that through the sufferings he becomes a partner of Paul, or
rather of Christ the Master of the angels; and sharing his sufferings here he
will share the glory there; For if, he says, we suffer with him, so that we
may also be glorified with him; and again: If we endure, so that we may also
reign with him. For it is necessary that the faithful be afflicted in any
case. For all who wish to live piously in Christ will be persecuted; and
again: Child, if you come forward to serve the Lord, prepare your soul for
temp=51.296=tation, make straight and endure. Fair are the promises from the
prefaces, to fall into temptations; a great encouragement and a clear
consolation, to taste at once the dangers of the service. Greatest then and
wonderful, and having the greatest gain.
And what is this? Hear what follows: For as gold is tested in fire, so
acceptable men in a furnace of humiliation. And what he says is something like
this: Just as gold, being tried by fire, becomes purer, so also the soul,
conversing with afflictions and dangers, comes up more radiant and more
splendid, and will throw off every stain of sins. Hence also Abraham said to
the rich man, that Lazarus received the evil things, and here he is consoled.
And Paul writing to the Corinthians wrote: For this reason many among you are
weak and sickly. For if we judged ourselves, we would not be judged; but being
judged by the Lord we are disciplined, so that we may not be condemned with
the world. And the one who had committed fornication he delivered for this
very reason unto the destruction of the flesh, so that the spirit may be
saved; showing that the present temptation works salvation, and the dangers,
for those who bear them with thanksgiving, are a greatest cleansing of the
soul. That then the faithful were afflicted, and suffered countless terrible
things, both disciples and teachers, and did not even breathe a little, being
fenced around from every side by various and all kinds of wars, the discourse
has sufficiently shown, and it is possible for the industrious to gather more
than what has been said from the divine Scriptures.
8. But for the rest let us bring the discourse to the matter at hand. And what
was the matter at hand to say? For what reason Paul said, Having the same
Spirit of faith. For what reason then did he say it? This was troubling the
disciples, that the terrible things were in experience, but the good things in
hopes; and the one was present, the other stood off; and the one was
happening, the other was being hoped for. And what wonder, if then at the
beginning of the preaching some suffered this, when even now after so much
time, after the preaching has been stretched throughout the inhabited world,
after receiving so many proofs of the promises, there are many who suffer
this? And not this only was disturbing them, but also another thing not less
than this. What then


is this? They were thinking to themselves that in the Old Testament the
affairs were not so ordered, but the contests and the rewards of virtue they
received at once, those who

the terrible things hanging over them, and that to these God promised the
reward of the labors after the departure from here, but repaid their ancestors
from here, and seeing much listlessness coming upon them from these
reasonings, wishing to raise them up and to teach that even in the case of
their fathers the affairs had been so ordered, and that many received the
reward in faith, not in experience, he reminded them of the prophetic saying,
saying: Having the same Spirit of faith, according to what is written: I
believed, therefore I spoke; all but saying, that the great David also, that
wonderful and noble prophet, obtained the recompense by faith, not by
experience; for if this were not so, he would not have said, I believed,
therefore I also spoke. For faith is the substance of things hoped for, not of
things seen; and what one sees, one does not altogether also hope. If then he
believed, he believed in the things hoped for. And if he believed in the
things hoped for, and the things hoped for are not yet seen, he had not yet
received those things in which he believed; for this reason he says, Having
the same Spirit of faith, that is, the same faith, the one in the Old, we also
have. For this reason also elsewhere he says, speaking of the saints of that
time: They went about in sheepskins, in goatskins, being in want, afflicted,
ill-treated, of whom the world was not worthy; then teaching that they endured
the terrible things, but have not even now received the rewards, he added
saying: According to faith these all died, not having received the promises,
but having seen them from afar and greeted them. And how, tell me, did they
see the things not yet present? With the eyes of faith, which overpass heaven,
and survey all the things there.
9. But do you consider with me the wisdom of God, how he both showed them the
prizes from afar, and did not give them at once, so that he might work in them
greater endurance; but he showed them from afar, so that being fed by these
hopes, they might not even have a perception of the present labors. =51.298=
But perhaps someone of those who attend more sharply will think that the
discourse has fallen into a contradiction with itself. For if not even the
former ones, he says, received the good things at their feet and


the recompenses, how have you stretched out a long discourse for us,
numbering a good blending of seasons, health of body, good children, many
children, a good year, abundance of fruits, herds, flocks, all worldly
prosperity? What then would we say to this? That God then led the multitude
and the weaker people in one way, and in another way the noble and those
already practicing the philosophy in the New. For to the many who creep on
the ground and are able to see nothing great, nor to stretch the hope of the
soul toward the enjoyment of the good things to come, he furnished these
present good things, consoling the weakness of their soul, and through these
leading them toward the working of virtue, and casting them into the desire
of the good things; but Elijah and Elisha, Jeremiah, Isaiah, and simply all
the prophets, and as many as have become of the choir of the saints and great
men, he called up to the heavens and to the good things prepared there for
those who have been well-pleasing. Therefore Paul also did not simply number
all, but those in sheepskins, in goatskins, those in the furnace, those in
prison, those who were beaten to death, those who were stoned, those in
famine, those in poverty, those in deserts, those in caves, those in the
holes of the earth, those who suffered the countless terrible things. And
then he said that all had died according to faith, and had not yet received
the promises; not hinting at the Jewish multitude, but at those according to
Elijah.
And if someone should say: And these themselves, for what reason have they not
even now received the crowns owed to them? let him learn this cause also from
Paul. For having said, According to faith these all died, not having received
the promises, he added, God having foreseen something better concerning us, so
that they should not be perfected apart from us. For the festival is common,
he says, since the pleasure also is greater, when we are all crowned in

has called those from the whole inhabited world who have been well-pleasing at
different times to a common and spiritual feast, he commands those who have
already arrived and have gone before to wait for those who are about to go
away after these things, so that thus, all having arrived in common, both the
honor and the pleasure may become one for all.
10. For consider how great a thing it is in respect of honor, that Paul and
all those according to him, Abraham, and those according to him, and those
before him again, who contended and conquered so many years before, sit now
waiting for our being well-pleasing. For that Paul has not yet received the
crown, nor anyone else of those who from the beginning have been
well-pleasing, but they will not even receive it, until all who until the end
are about to be crow=51.299=ned have arrived, hear Paul himself saying: I have
fought the good fight, I have finished the course, I have kept the faith;
henceforth there is laid up for me the crown of righteousness, which the
righteous judge will give back to me. When? In that day; and not only to me,
but also to all who have loved his appearing. And again elsewhere, showing
that the enjoyment of the good things is given to all in common, writing to
the Thessalonians he said: If indeed it is a righteous thing with God to give
back affliction to those who afflict you, and to you who are afflicted rest
with us. And again: That we who


are alive, who are left unto the presence of the Lord, shall not precede
those who have fallen asleep; through all these things showing that all in
common and having arrived together must enjoy the heavenly honors. This also
brings much pleasure to those who have arrived first, when they enjoy those
unspeakable good things together with their own members. For a father too,
sharing a splendid and costly table, will then enjoy the gladness of it with
more, when he shares the feast and the gladness with his own children. So
also Paul and all those according to him will feel a greater cheerfulness,
when being with their own members they enjoy it. For fathers do not show so
much affection toward children, as those men have care toward those who have
achieved the same things as themselves. So that we too may become of those
then being honored, let us be eager to overtake those saints. And how shall
we be able, he says, to overtake them? who will show us the road that leads
there? The Master himself of those saints, who not only teaches how we may
overtake them, but also how we may become under the same roof and
tent-fellows with them all; For make for yourselves friends from the mammon
of unrighteousness, he says, so that when you fail, they may receive you into
their eternal tents. He said well, Eternal.
For here, even if you have a splendid house, it will certainly perish, being
corrupted by time; or rather even before the corruption according to time,
death falling upon you casts you out of this splendid dwelling; and often even
before death difficulties of certain affairs and attacks of slanderers and
plots have prepared a falling out of it. But there none of these things is to
be suspected, not corruption, not death, not a fall, not the abuse of
slanderers, nor anything else, but the dwelling is unmoved and immortal. For
this reason he called them eternal. Make for yourselves friends, he says, from
the mammon of unrighteousness.
11. See how great is the love of man of the Master, how great the goodness and
gentleness; for he has not simply set this addition; but since for many rich
men wealth has been gathered from plunder and greed, Badly, he says, and you
ought not to have gathered the money thus; yet since you have gathered it,
stand off from the plunder and the greed, and use the money as is needed. I do
not say, that plundering you should pity, but that having stood off from
greed, you should use the wealth toward almsgiving and love of man. For if
someone should not cease from plunder, he will not even work almsgiving; but
even if he should cast down countless money into the hands of those in need,
plundering the things of others and being greedy, he has been reckoned equal
to murderers by God. Therefore one must first stand off from greed, and thus
pity those in need. =51.300= For great is the power of almsgiving, concerning
which we discoursed toward you also at yesterday's assembly, and now I will
discourse. But let no one think the continuity of the reminder an accusation
of the hearers. For in the contests the spectators stir up those of the
runners whom they see having come near the prize, and having many hopes of
victory. And I too then, since I always see you receiving the discourses
concerning almsgiving with much eagerness, for this reason I myself also more
continually move the exhortation about these things. The poor are physicians
of our souls, benefactors and protectors; for you do not give so much as you
receive; you give silver, and you receive the kingdom of heaven; you loose
poverty, and you reconcile the Master to yourself. Do you see that the
exchange is not equal? These things are on earth, those in heaven; these
perish, those remain; these are corrupted, those are above all destruction.
For this reason also before the doors of the houses of prayer our


fathers set the poor, so that even the most sluggish and most inhuman the
very sight of the poor may raise toward a reminder of almsgiving. For when a
choir of old men stands, bent over, wrapped in rags, squalid, filthy, having
staffs, scarcely able to stand, and often even having had their eyes cut out,
and the whole body maimed. who is so of stone, who so adamant, as, when both
the age, and the weakness, and the maiming, and the poverty, and the cheap
clothing, and simply all things, bend him toward compassion, to stand against
and remain unyielding toward all these things?
For these reasons they have stood before our doors, more powerful than any
word, drawing by the sight, calling those who enter toward love of man. For
just as it has been customary for there to be fountains in the courts of the
houses of prayer, so that those about to pray to God, having first washed
their hands, may thus stretch them up to prayer; so also the fathers set the
poor in place of springs and fountains before the doors, so that, just as we
wash the hands with water, so having first wiped the soul with love of man, we
may thus pray. 12. For not even so does the nature of water wash off stains of
the body, as the power of almsgiving wipes off the filth of the soul. Just as
then you do not dare, having entered with unwashed hands, to pray, although
that charge is less, so do not ever come to prayer without almsgiving. And yet
even often having the hands clean, if we do not first wash them with water, we
do not stretch them up to prayer; so great a thing is habit. This then let us
do also in the case of almsgiving; and even if we are conscious to ourselves
of no great sin, nevertheless let us wipe the conscience through almsgiving.
You have drawn many terrible things from the marketplace; an enemy provoked, a
judge forced you to do some of the things not fitting, you often threw out
absurd words, a friend shamed you into working something that has sin, you
have rubbed on many other things, such as it is likely a man being rubbed on,
turning in the marketplace, sitting at the courts, doing the affairs of the
city; for all these things you enter about to ask God for pardon and to make a
defense. Cast down then silver into the hands of the poor, and wipe off those
stains, so that with boldness you may call =51.301= asking the one who is able
to forgive you these sins. If you establish yourself in the habit never to set
foot on these sacred porches without almsgiving, never, neither willingly nor
unwillingly, will you fall short of this good work, for such a thing is habit.
And just as then always, whatever may happen, you do not endure to pray with
unwashed hands, since you have once been established in the habit; so also in
the case of almsgiving, if you set this law upon yourself, both willingly and
unwillingly you will fulfill it each day, being drawn by the habit. Prayer is
fire, especially when it is sent up from a sober and awakened soul; but this
fire also needs oil, so that it may touch the heavenly vaults themselves; and
the oil of this fire is nothing else but almsgiving. Pour on then the oil
abundantly, so that rejoicing at the achievement, with more boldness and
greater eagerness you may accomplish your prayers. For just as those who are
conscious to themselves of nothing good are not able even to pray with
bold=51.302=ness, so those who have achieved something, and after that
righteousness come to the prayer, rejoicing at the memory of the achievement,
make the supplication with more eagerness. So that in this respect also the
prayer may become more powerful for us, our mind being watchful in the
petitions from the memory of the achievements, let us come to the prayers with
almsgiving, and


let us remember with exactness all the things that have been said; and before
all the others keep for me continually that image, according to which I said
the poor stand before the doors of the houses of prayer, filling in the soul
this need which the fountain fills in the body. For if we are continually
mindful of this, continually washing the reasoning, we shall be able to
accomplish the prayers clean, and to draw much boldness from God, and to
attain the kingdom of the heavens, by the grace and love of man of our Lord
Jesus Christ, to whom be the glory and the power unto the ages of ages. Amen.
