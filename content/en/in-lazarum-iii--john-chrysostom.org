On Lazarus of four days. iii.
=62.777=
The light loosed Lazarus; but being so called he hastened, so as,
still wrapped in grave-bands, to display a running, he who had been
rotting a fourth day in the tomb, and much of his inward parts plundered
by worms, and all the harmony of the body torn apart, and possessing the
stench of the tomb as witness of the corruption. But the fragrance of
life, smelling the dead man, recast the dissolution of the inward parts;
the tomb became a womb for the one lying there, and as if sown in a
belly, not in a tomb, Lazarus leaped forth newly formed. O strange
wonder-working! A tomb brought forth a man for us into life, wrapped in
the grave-clothes, as an infant in swaddling-bands.
For the Master's voice, being recognized by the dead man, drew him from
the tomb together with the grave-bands. "Lazarus," he says, "come
forth"; and at the word the dead man quickly came out together, and the
members ran together to one another in the tomb; the eye returned to its
own rank, the nose took its own place, the cheeks pressed toward their
former shape, the neck was bound together with shoulders and head, the
pair of hands ready for motion, the harmonies of the joints were joined,
the order of the fingers was composed, the covenants of the sinews were
bound up again, the nature of the bones was clothed, the arteries were
stretched out with the veins, marrow was formed again, membranes were
woven, the hairs were set in bloom. I make the running of the members
slow by my speech. As a horse at a signal, so the dead man, pricked by
the Master's voice, leaped up. Lazarus, come forth. Before saying,
"Arise," he commands the going-out; =62.778= while he is still lying he
presses him toward a run. Raise him first, and then so command the one
who has risen to run.
Why, Master, do you proclaim a going-out to the one lying there before
the resurrection? "I command the run to one who is lying," he says, "so
that I may not, occupying the dead man with two things, have wasted time
on the going-out. I do not say to him, 'Rise, and come forth,' so that
he may run more sharply, having received one appointed time for rising
and for running." Hence Lazarus leaped out of the tomb winged, as a
guard rather than a dead man sitting beside the tomb. And Hades from
below, as it seems, giving back the dead man, cried out: Who is this who
by a voice raises the dead from the tombs as those who sleep? Who is
this who dissolves the ancient laws of the dead? Who is this who has set
forth so great a proclamation of return for the dead to rise up? Who is
this who trains the buried to run back toward life? Who is this who so
easily looses my dead? Who is this who by a command snatches away my
ancient dead? The scepter of my tyranny over men, as I see, is gone from
me; the prison of death has gone from me henceforth rotten. Elijah
raised a dead man, showing it as a favor to a woman; Elisha, coming
after, was made a double robber of my dead; now this one, a bitterer
helper on behalf of the dead, has marched against me, stealing the
rotten dead, and I am in danger henceforth of not even having authority
over the decayed dead. For I have been robbed of a dead man whom I had
consumed; a four-day body triumphs over me, dancing together with the
living, perceptible, with its grave-clothes; I shall be trampled
henceforth with the grave-bands, a dead man having run away from me.
For who henceforth =62.780= will fear me, me who have been conquered by
a decayed dead man, and who have become as a guardian of certain
deposits of the dead? For Lazarus was a kind of deposit of the tomb,
like some gold, being given back with the seals with which he was laid
away; bound with grave-bands he was laid in the tomb, and bound with
them again he was given back to life. For the people of the Jews is
unbelieving, and doubtful toward the magnificence of the wonders. They
would have said even now of Lazarus, as once of the blind man who was
healed: "This is he; this is not he." As therefore one about to give
back a deposit, and foreseeing the suspicion of the one who deposited
it, keeps the seals of the deposit, so that the sight of the seals may
persuade the depositor; so also the Master Christ, giving Lazarus back
to the Jews from the dead, the four-day deposit among the dead, brings
out together with him the signs of the grave-bands, as a kind of seal,
so that when they loose them they may see that it is not another, but he
himself whom they laid away.
But the incurable blindness of unbelief; for having recognized the dead
man they envied the wonder, and they planned a blind counsel. For the
chief priests, it says, took counsel that they might kill Lazarus also,
whom he raised from the dead; because on his account many of the Jews
were going over and believing in him; to whom be the glory unto the
ages. Amen.
