* Homilies on Exodus

217 He who looks beyond the present age and, having been "translated" by the
word of God to journey toward the age to come, went out from Egypt, from the
bodily and afflicting things, which would be a "house of bondage," seeking the
house of freedom, Jerusalem, wanting to stand in confessing the 218 Lord and
to see his peace; when God also becomes his Lord. For it is not in Egypt, even
if one eat the Passover as one ought, nor at any of the stations that have
been mentioned. For such a one is not yet free of bondage after so great a
demonstration, unless he come "to the mountain of God." This is the preface of
the following Decalogue. 221 The likenesses of things that exist, then, are
images of living creatures and of bodies, but the idols are fabrications of
things that have no existence. For 222 the idol is not existence; "for an idol
is nothing in the world," Paul says; as if someone should fashion
hippocentaurs or Pans or some monstrous natures. Every thought, therefore,
that arises according to a comprehensive imagination in a conceiving of the
divine nature fashions an idol of God, but does not proclaim God. And there
could also be likenesses of the things in heaven, of some painting or even
carving the sun or the moon; and perhaps he also speaks of the decans
according to astrology; for they describe certain strange demons, which
perhaps have been named "spiritual things of wickedness in the 223 heavenly
places" according to the word of God who says: "My sword has been drunken in
heaven." For they write these down in astrological tablets and in phylacteries
that turn demons away or that invite them, in which one might find some things
that are likenesses, and others that are idols. "You shall not worship them
nor shall you serve them." He who serves also worships, but one who worships
does not altogether serve; for the one is of a soul that reveres, and the
other even as far as the outward form; such as some at times pretend, courting
power or granting favors to passions, according to those who committed
idolatry in Numbers. For it has been written concerning them that they did not
serve nor were they slaves to 224 their idols. But God, wanting to be loved
"from the whole soul," does not even endure those who are impious as far as
the outward form; for one who has denied the alien gods must become a "portion
of God," having loved him "from the whole soul," and standing away from the
former evil lovers of the soul that fornicates from God, as also it has been
said concerning Jerusalem when her lovers became hostile to her, who also
says: "I will turn back to my former husband, for it was better for me then
than now." But if you do not thus 225 turn back, so that he himself receive
you and wash you and cleanse you, forgiving the sin, you have come into the
greatest danger. Therefore Joshua says to the sons of Israel: "You yourselves
choose whether you serve the gods of these places or you serve the God of
Israel"; and when they say: "We serve the Lord God," he says: "You are
witnesses against yourselves that you have chosen to serve the Lord the God of
Israel." For there is danger that, having withdrawn from those, one not come
genuinely to God; for many are the things that make war and want again to drag
one away toward their own portion, according to "the spirit that went out and
through the waterless places does not find rest, then finding the house from
which it went out standing idle, and fearing only to enter, but with seven
=226.20= more wicked ones making the last things heavier than the first." For
judge the one who turns back to the demons, and you will find the life after
these things to have become worse than before he came to God. For a demon
dwells in each of the Gentiles, "because all the gods of the nations are
demons." If, having cast this one out, you do not have the one who said: "I
will dwell in them and I will be their God," you have given yourself to that
one and to the seven more wicked; for while God is present they will not come;
"for what participation of righteousness and unrighteousness, or what
fellowship of light toward darkness; or what agreement of a temple of God with
idols; what portion of a believer with an unbeliever?" For we are a "temple of
the living God," as God said, that "I will dwell in them and walk among them
and I will be their 227 God and they shall be my people." "Therefore come out
from the midst of them and be separated and do not touch an unclean thing; and
I will receive you and I will be to you a father and you shall be to me sons
and daughters, says the Lord almighty." "For I am the Lord your God, a jealous
God." No man is jealous over a harlot, but over a wedded wife; for the one is
set forth for all, but the other belongs to the one who dwells with her alone,
over whom he is also jealous. "For the wrath of a man is full of jealousy,"
according to Solomon. Thus it is over a soul that has one word, one
bridegroom; but over a harlot =228.20= one went out, and another demon entered
instead, and there is not even one who is jealous. Whence the laws of the
nations commanded to honor every idol. But the bridegroom is also a good
jealous one, and even if he sometime take a harlot, he wants to make her
chaste. Therefore also Hosea, taking a harlot, said: "You shall sit for me
many days, and you shall not fornicate, nor shall you belong to another man,
and I upon you." God also made a great supper because of the marriage of his
son. And he is "a jealous God," not permitting another to have fellowship with
the soul, and at the same time with the fornication giving a "bill of divorce"
according to the one in the law that has pretextual reasons; which as to
Pharisees according to the letter Christ brought forward, according to Paul
saying: "I have become to the Jews as a Jew, that I might gain Jews." And he
makes this clear saying: "And this is the bill of divorce of 229 your mother,
with which I sent her away. Behold, you were sold for your sins and I sent
your mother away for your iniquities." But if she who is fornicating turns
back, he says concerning her, whom "God has cleansed," "do not you call
common." He holds, then, to every soul, but going out of the church she rather
gives to the bridegroom a "bill of divorce"; and to her: "You have
fornicated," he says, "among many shepherds and you have turned back to me,
says the Lord." But one who is repenting is to be received; but if anyone have
fellowship with one who is still a harlot, let him see that "he who is joined
to the harlot is one body." But God is no longer jealous over one who is
fornicating; for he says in Ezekiel to Jerusalem: "Therefore my jealousy shall
230 stand away from you and I will not be angry against you." These things are
of the goodness of God; having mercy he is angry and he strikes; and with the
mercy the wrath also ceases together.
