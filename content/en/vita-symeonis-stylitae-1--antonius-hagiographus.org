Life and Conduct of the Blessed Symeon the Stylite

1 A strange and paradoxical mystery has come to pass in our days. It seemed
good to me, the sinful and least Antony, to write what I comprehend. For the
composition is filled with benefit and with compunction. Therefore I beseech
you, incline your ear and hear the things I comprehend.

2 The one among the saints and most blessed Symeon, being a child in age, used
to tend the sheep of his father, in the same manner as the prophet David also.
And on the holy Lord's day he would go into the church toward the oracles of
God, and he would gladly listen to the holy scriptures, not knowing what he
was hearing. And as he advanced in age and was pricked by the word of God, he
goes away on one of the days into the holy church, and hearing the apostle
being read he asks a certain old man: Tell me, father, what is it that he is
reading. The elder says to him: Concerning self-control of the soul. Saint
Symeon says to him: What is self-control of the soul? The elder says to him:
Child, why do you ask me? For I see you young in age, but possessing an aged
understanding. Saint Symeon says to him: I am not tempting you, father; but I
am astonished at the name of the expression. The elder says to him:
Self-control is salvation of the soul, guiding into light, leading into the
kingdom of the heavens. Saint Symeon says to him: Teach me, honorable father,
the things you say, because I am unlearned.

3 The elder says to him: Child, if anyone fasts toward God unceasingly and
renders to him all the prayers with gentleness, that is, at the third hour one
prayer, likewise also the sixth, the ninth and the twelfth, and the things
that follow these; just as they are also performed in the monasteries. If then
you know, child, what you have heard, reckon these things in your heart; for
you must both hunger and thirst and be insulted and be struck and be reviled,
groan and weep and be afflicted and be unwell, recover and desire to renounce
the world and be humbled and suffer many things from men, and thus be
comforted by angels; see, you have heard all these things, may the Lord of
glory give you a good mind toward his will.

4 And having heard these things saint Symeon went out of the church and goes
away to a desert place and sets himself upon his face for seven days, weeping
and praying to God, partaking neither of food nor of drink. And after the
seven days, having risen, he comes running to a monastery. And he falls at the
feet of the archimandrite, crying out and saying: Have mercy on me, father, a
humble and wretched man, and save a soul that is perishing and that longs to
serve God. The archimandrite says to him: Who are you and from where? And what
is your name? And from where have you come? The blessed Symeon says: By birth
I am free, and the name is Symeon, but how I have come here or of what parents
I am, do not ask me, master, I beg you, but redeem for yourself one soul that
is perishing. Having heard these things the archimandrite raised him from the
ground and the archimandrite says to him: If you are from God, the Lord will
guard you from every wicked and deceitful deed, and you shall be doing the
service for all, that you may be loved by all.

5 But his parents did not cease weeping and seeking him; and the saint was
spending his time in the monastery serving all and being loved by all and
fulfilling the rule of the monastery. So on one of the days, having gone out
of the monastery, he finds upon the well a bucket, from which they used to
draw the water, having


a rope; and having loosed the rope he goes away to a private place and binds
himself about his whole body; and he puts on over the rope a hair tunic and
entered into the monastery and says to the brethren: I went out to draw water
and I did not find the rope on the bucket. The brethren say to him: Be silent,
lest anyone learn of it to the archimandrite. And no one knew that he was
bound about within with the rope. So he remained up to one year and more
having the rope wound in his flesh, and it ate up his flesh, so that the rope
was covered by the putrefied flesh of the righteous one, and from his stench
no one was able to stand nearest to him, and no one knew the mystery. And his
bed was filled with worms, and no one knew what had happened.

6 And he would take his food and give it to the poor, no one knowing. So on
one of the days one of the monks goes out and finds him giving his bread and
the pulse which he received to the poor. And all used to fast until evening,
but saint Symeon would taste from Lord's day to Lord's day. And one of the
monks, having entered, slandered him to the archimandrite saying: I beg your
holiness; this man wishes to dissolve the monastery, that is, the rule which
you handed down to us. The archimandrite says to him: How does he wish to
dissolve the rule? The monk says to him: We received to fast until evening,
but this one eats from Lord's day to Lord's day, and his bread, which he
receives, and the pulse he gives secretly to the poor each day; and not only
this, but also from his body an unbearable stench arises, so that no one is
able to stand nearest to him; and his bed is filled with worms, and we are not
able to bear it; But if you wish, keep this one here and we withdraw, or
dismiss him to go away, from where he came.

7 Having heard these things the archimandrite became astonished, and he
examines his bed, and finds it filled with worms, and from the stench he was
not able to stand there. And the archimandrite says: Behold even the young
Job. And having taken hold of him he says to him: Why have you done these
things, man? From where is this stench of yours, why do you lead the brethren
astray, why do you dissolve the rule of the monastery? Or are you a phantom?
Go away elsewhere and die without us; was it perhaps through you that I, the
wretched one, came to be tempted? For if you were a true man of understanding
parents, you would have reported to us who your father was and your mother and
your race, and from where you came here. Having heard these things the saint,
looking down toward the earth, was silent, saying nothing, and from his tears
the place in which he stood was filled; and having become furious for a long
time the archimandrite says to the monks: Strip him, that we may see from
where this stench is.

8 So they wished to strip him and they were not able; for his garment was
glued from the putrefied flesh. So for three days they did not cease soaking
him with warm water together with oil, and thus with much pain they were able
to strip him, so that together with the garment his putrefied flesh was flayed
off, and they find the rope wound about his body, so that nothing of it
appeared, except only the ends of the rope; and of the worms that were in him
there was no reckoning; then all the monks became astonished at him, seeing
that incurable wound, and they were considering among themselves how and by
what device they might take the rope from him; but saint Symeon was crying out
saying: Forgive me, my lords brothers, let me die thus, the stinking dog, for
according to my deeds I ought also thus to be judged; for all injustice and
greed has come to be with me, for I am the


sea of sins. And the monks and the archimandrite were weeping, seeing that
incurable wound; and the archimandrite says to him: You are not yet eighteen
years old, and what sins do you have? Saint Symeon says to him: The prophet
David says: Behold, I was conceived in iniquities, and in sins my mother
conceived me; and I have been clothed with the like of all these. And the
archimandrite was astonished at his intelligent answer, that being a rustic
how he was pricked unto the fear of God. And the archimandrite, having called
two physicians, with many toils and pains, so that he was thought to have
died, they tore the rope from him † from the flesh glued to it, and
having tended him for fifty days they benefited him in part. And the
archimandrite says to him: Child, behold you have become healthy; go away
where you wish.

9 Then saint Symeon goes out from the monastery; and behold there was a well
lying near the monastery, in which there was no water, but a multitude of
unclean wicked spirits dwelt in it; and not only of unclean spirits, but also
of asps and vipers and snakes and scorpions an incalculable multitude; whence
all were afraid to pass through that place. And saint Symeon went away there,
no one knowing, and throws himself into that well, having made the seal in
Christ, and hides himself in the side of the well.

10 So after seven days of his departing from the monastery the archimandrite
sees in a dream an incalculable multitude of men clothed in white and circling
the monastery, holding lamps and saying: Now we burn you here, unless you hand
over to us the servant of God Symeon. And for what cause did you drive him
out, and what did he do, that you cast him out from the monastery, and what is
his fault? Tell us, before we burn you; do you not know what you had in your
monastery? For this one will be found greater than you on that fearful and
terrible day. And having been awakened the archimandrite, having become
trembling, says to the monks: As I see, that man happens to be a genuine
servant of God; for behold I suffered many evils in a dream this night because
of him; but I beseech you, brothers, run about for me and find him for me,
since let none of you come here.

11 And having gone out they sought him in every place and not having found him
they came saying to the archimandrite: Truly, master, we did not leave a place
where we did not seek, except the place in which no one dares to travel
because of the multitude of the wild beasts. The archimandrite says to them:
Children, having made a prayer and having taken lamps, go down and search for
him. And having made a prayer upon the well for three hours they let down into
the well five monks with ropes holding lamps; and the reptiles, seeing them,
fled into the corners. And saint Symeon, having seen them, cried out saying: I
beg you, brothers and servants of God, grant me a little, that I may give over
my spirit, for it is enough for me, that what I undertook I did not fulfill.
But the monks, having forced him with much force, brought him up from the
well, dragging him as one who had done evil; and they led him to the
archimandrite. And he, having seen him, fell at his feet saying: Forgive me,
servant of God, become yourself my teacher and teach me the things which
endurance has and grants.

12 But saint Symeon did not cease weeping and praying to God, and having spent
three years in that very monastery he goes out, no one knowing, and goes away
to a desert place, in which there lay a good many villages, and nearest to it
was a


village called Gelasois, and he builds there for himself a small place of dry
stones, and he stands in the midst four years, being snowed on, rained on,
scorched, and many were going away to him; and his food was soaked lentils and
his drink water. And after these things he made for himself a pillar of four
cubits and stands on it seven years, and his fame was running everywhere; and
after these things the crowds build for him two enclosures of dry stone, and
they set a door on the inner enclosure and they make for him a pillar of
thirty cubits, and he stands on it fifteen years performing many healings; for
many who were demon-possessed would go there and be healed.

13 And saint Symeon was imitating his teacher Christ, whom also calling upon
he made the lame walk, cleansing lepers, making the mute speak, making
paralytics run, granting healing to the long-sick, admonishing and exhorting
each one: If someone says to you, who healed you, say you, that God healed me;
let it not seem good to you to say that Symeon healed me, since you will find
yourself again in the same pains. And this I say to you: never lie and swear
by God; and if also a necessity comes upon you of swearing, swear by me the
humble one, whether with truth or with falsehood; for it is a great sin and a
fearful thing to swear by God.

14 Hear a fearful and paradoxical wonder: his mother after twenty years learns
where saint Symeon is and having come running she wished to see him after so
many years and weeping much to look upon him, and she was not permitted to see
him; and as she for a long time wished to be sanctified by his holy hands, she
was forced to climb upon the wall; and climbing upon the wall of the enclosure
she was dashed to the ground, not having been able to see him. And saint
Symeon sends word to her: Forgive me, mother, for now, and if we are worthy in
that age we shall see one another. And as she heard these things, she was
burned all the more to see him. And saint Symeon sends word to her: Cease, my
lady mother, since you have come from afar and have toiled for me the humble
one, but set yourself down a little and resting take strength and after a
little I see you. And she, having heard, sets herself at his doorway and
immediately she gave over her spirit to God. And the attendants came to wake
her; and they found her dead and they announced to the saint concerning her.
And he, having heard, commands her to be brought in and he sets her before his
pillar, and having seen her with tears he began to say: Lord, the God of the
powers, the guide of the light and charioteer of the Cherubim, who guided
Joseph, who empowered your prophet David against Goliath, who raised Lazarus
four days from the dead, raise up your right hand and receive in peace the
soul of your servant. And as he was praying her holy relic was moved and she
smiled in her face, and all who were watching were astonished and were
glorifying God; and having given her funeral rites they buried her before his
pillar, that, as he prays, he may remember her.

15 Hear another strange and paradoxical mystery: some were coming to prayer
from a great distance, and a grazing hind meets them, having in the womb, and
one of them says to the hind: I adjure you by the power of the holy Symeon,
stand, that I may take hold of you. And immediately the hind stood, and having
taken hold of her he slaughtered her and they ate her flesh; but the hide
remained; and immediately their speech was hindered, and they began to bleat
as the irrational animals, and they come running and fall down before his
pillar begging to obtain healing, and they filled the hide of the hind with
chaff and for the recognition of many people the hide was set for a


sufficient time, and the men, having spent a sufficient time in repentance and
having been healed, withdrew to their own.

16 Hear another strange and marvelous thing: a certain woman, being thirsty
during the night, wished to drink water; and having taken hold of the
water-jar she drinks together with the water a small snake, and having been
nourished in her belly it became large, and her face became as the appearance
of green grass, and many physicians came, that they might heal her, and they
were not able; and those in her house, having learned the wonders and the
healings which the saint of God Symeon was doing, having taken her they led
her to the saint and they announce to him all the things concerning her and he
commands them saying: Put into her mouth from this water and from this spring.
And having done thus according to what was commanded, the beast was moved
before all, and she dashes herself to the ground, and it comes out and having
laid its head in the midst of the railings it made a noise, and all were
glorifying God.

17 And they changed his pillar, having made it of forty cubits, and he was
noised abroad into the whole inhabited world. Whence the multitude of the
Saracens came to him burning with faith, and he pricked them unto the fear of
God. So the man-hating devil, having a custom of tempting the saints and being
trampled by them, brought a plague upon his thigh in the manner of the blessed
Job, a pain of the so-called plague, and his thigh was wholly putrefied and
thereafter he was standing on one foot for a period of two years. And
incalculable worms were falling from his thigh upon the earth, whence those
nearest to him had no other work except only to collect them and carry them up
to the place from which they fell, the saint saying: Eat, from where the Lord
has given you.

18 And according to the will of God it comes to pass that the king of the
Saracens comes to prayer to him, and as soon as he entered nearest the pillar
to be blessed by saint Symeon, the saint of God having seen him began to
admonish him. And as they were speaking a worm falls from his thigh, and the
king sets his mind, not however knowing what it was that had fallen, and
having run he snatches it. And he sets it upon his eyes and upon his heart and
he goes out outside holding it in his hand. And the saint sends word to him
saying: Come in, put down what you took up, you are bringing a burden to me
the sinner. It is a stinking worm from stinking flesh. Why do you spoil your
hands, being an honorable man? And when the righteous one had said these
things the Saracen enters and says to him: This shall be for me unto a
blessing and unto remission of sins. And having opened his hand there was an
honorable pearl in his hand. And having seen it he began to glorify God and he
says to the righteous one: Behold what you said is a worm, it is a pearl such
as has no price, through which the Lord has enlightened me. And after these
things having heard the saint says to him: As you have believed let it be to
you all the days of your life, and not only to you, but also to your children.
And having been blessed the king of the Saracens went away to his own,
rejoicing in peace.

19 Hear another mystery. On the mountain on which he stands, toward the east a
great dragon was remaining. Whence not even herbage was coming up in that
place. And it happened to that dragon, as he was coming out to cool himself,
that a stick entered into his eye, and for a sufficient time from the pain no
one was able to bear his hissing. So on one of the days the dragon comes out
from his den dragging himself and comes with all watching and sets himself at
the doorway of the enclosure. And suddenly his eye was opened and the stick
comes out from his eye, and he spent three days there, until he was restored
healthy. And thus he went away to his place with all watching


having wronged no one, but as a sheep thus he was lying at the doorway of the
righteous one, and all were going in and going out being wronged in nothing by
him.

20 Hear another paradoxical wonder. There came to be a chief robber in Syria,
named Antiochus, and his surname was called Gonatas, whose affairs were spoken
of in the whole world. And from here and from there soldiers were sent to hunt
him and to bring him to Antioch, and no one was able to hunt him because of
the great power of his strength. So in Antioch bears and other beasts were
prepared, as he being about to fight with beasts, and the whole city of
Antioch was moved because of him. And they go out to hunt him and they find
him drinking, in a certain village in an inn, and the soldiers surrounded the
inn. And that one, having learned, began to act a tragedy, and a river was
lying by the village, and that chief robber had a mare and he was commanding
her as a man and having risen he throws his garments upon the mare and says to
her: Go away to the river and wait for me there, and the mare goes out from
the inn biting and striking, and having gone away to the river she was waiting
for him. And the chief robber also goes out from the inn having bared his
sword, crying out and saying to the multitude of the soldiers: Flee lest
anyone die, and none of the soldiers mastered him. And having escaped the
notice of all who were guarding him and having crossed the river together with
the mare, and having mounted her, he reached the enclosure of saint Symeon.
And having entered he threw himself before his pillar, and when the soldiers
had been gathered in the enclosure of the saint the saint says to them: With
our master Jesus Christ two robbers were crucified, the one received according
to his works, but the other inherited the kingdom of heavens. And if anyone is
able to withstand the one who sent him, let him come and himself snatch him
away, for I neither brought him nor am I able to dismiss him. For the one who
sent him here himself also claims him, so let no one any longer revile me the
humble one, who have toiled much because of my many sins. And having said
these things to them he dismissed them. And as these were going away the chief
robber says: My lord, I am going away. But the saint says to him: Are you
going away again into the same evils? And the chief robber says: No, master,
the Lord is calling me. And having stretched out his hands into heaven, he
said nothing more, except only: Son of God receive my spirit in peace. And he
wept for two hours, so as to make even the righteous one weep together with
those standing by. So setting himself before the pillar of the righteous one,
he immediately gave over the spirit, and the crowds wrapped him and buried him
near the enclosure of the righteous one. So on the next day there come from
Antioch more than a hundred men with swords to snatch him and they began to
cry out against the righteous one: Release to us the one you have. But the
holy one said to them: Brothers, the one who sent him here is stronger than
you, and having had need of him as being useful, having sent to him two
fearful soldiers in arms, who were able to strike your city with thunder with
those dwelling in it, he took him, and I the sinner, having seen their fearful
form, having been afraid I did not dare to speak against them, lest they kill
me the humble one also as opposing God. And those men, having heard these
things from the saint and having learned with what glory the chief robber gave
over the spirit, glorifying God and trembling went away again to Antioch.

21 Hear another fearful and glorious wonder. In the place in which the saint
was, water was not appearing. And the multitude of the animals and of the
people was perishing of those coming to the place of saint Symeon. And the
saint, having made a prayer for seven days, did not speak to anyone, but he
was having bent his knees and praying, so that all thought


that he had already died. And about the fifth hour of the seventh day suddenly
water burst forth from the east of his enclosure, and having dug they found
the form of a cave filled with water. And they built in it seven mouths. And
all were glorifying the God of heaven and of earth.

22 [And a certain queen of the Saracens, being barren, went away begging him
that she might bear. For she was being reproached by her husband each day and
falling down much and begging for a sufficient number of days she was
persevering at the pillar of saint Symeon. So the saint sent word to her: Go
away to your house and the Lord has the things that are beneficial to grant
you. And having gone away to her house she came together with her husband and
immediately she conceived. And she bore a daughter. And for five years she was
neither speaking nor walking. And she comes with her husband and her child
weeping, and saint Symeon answering said to them: Remain and God has to help.
And when they had spent seven days they were being benefited in nothing and
they were withdrawing grieved saying that In any case the Lord did not wish to
heal her. And as they were traveling and having gone not a little they turn to
see the column of saint Symeon. And behold their daughter suddenly cried out
saying: Glory to you, saint Symeon. And the parents of the child, having seen,
glorified God through saint Symeon.

what pretext to look upon him; she was devising then to take on the form of a

going away to the saint to pray. And having taken on the soldierly form she
also was traveling together with them. And having arrived at the place where
the pillar of saint Symeon was, she says to the soldiers with her: Brothers,
if you wish, having left me your animals go in and be blessed by the righteous
one, and when you come out I also go in again, that I may be counted worthy to
be blessed by the righteous one. And when they had entered and worshiped,
saint Symeon says to them: One soldier from you was left outside. And they

24 And another mystery if you hear. When a sufficient multitude had come
† according to custom with incense, crying to him: Servant of God, the
people are waiting to receive your blessing, command, dismiss them, for behold
they have been waiting a long hour, and they were receiving no answer. For his
spirit was not present. And as I was crying to him persistently and not
receiving an answer the deacon began to lament above by the column, having
thought that he had died. And the multitude of the people, having seen, began
to groan and to beat a great beating, mourning their sins. And when after a
sufficient hour his holy spirit returned again, and answering he said to the
people: Brothers, a great ship was being storm-tossed violently in the sea at
this hour, having about three hundred souls; and all the souls cried out


entreating the master God. And crying to me with terrible oaths, and having
seen what was happening to them I entreated the God who is always
long-suffering over our sins to calm the sea, and having given them a hand I
saved all. And having heard the mystery the multitudes of the people were
glorifying God through saint Symeon. And the righteous one, having blessed
them, dismissed them in peace.

25 A swelling came out of a female snake and from her pains she was going a
mile. And the male, sharing the pains of her toils, taking his female they
were coming toward lord Symeon and having arrived at the pillar they were
separated from one another. For the female, not daring to appear to the
righteous one, went away to the women's part, but the male, having entered
among that multitude, was worshiping the pillar, making his head up and down
and begging the righteous one. And the crowds, having seen the vast size of
the snake, fled from him. And saint Symeon having seen said to the crowds: Do
not flee, brothers, for truly having a prayer he came here. For his female is
very sick and she went away to the women's part. And he says to the snake:
Take mud from the earth and carry it up to your wife. And having put it on,
blow upon her and it has to heal her. And the snake, having taken from the
mud, was going toward his female. And the crowds, having seen, followed him to
see what he was about to do. And they see his female standing upright outside
the railings having a great swelling and the snake, having taken the mud, puts
it on her and having blown upon her in the presence of all he healed her. And
having taken her he withdrew. And the crowds, having seen the mystery,
glorified God. 26 And another wonder came to pass in his days.

26 For when the great threat of the fears had come upon the whole inhabited
world, the whole East and Antioch were being brought down to him begging him
to prevail upon God. For many falls of dwellings were coming to pass and
deaths of a good many people, so that even the righteous one together with the
pillar was being shaken as a reed by wind. And weeping together with them he
was saying to the crowds: Brothers, all have turned aside, together they were
made useless, there is not even one. You do not hear me, but as to who will
defraud whom your fornications and your injustices have overflowed. Truly I
say to you, brothers: it is easier for me to speak with the master than with
you the unbelieving. And having commanded the litany to cease, he made a
prayer, and again the threat was remaining in the same things shaking
everything. And he permitted them to cry Lord have mercy. And as they were
persisting for sufficient hours and crying: Give us peace, he made a great
prayer. And after he had completed the prayer, answering he said to the
people: Brothers, truly I say to you, that from the whole multitude except one
soul another was not heard; and that I may persuade you, behold I make him
come in the midst. And having commanded him to come in the presence of all he
questions him and says to him: Brother, believe me saying: from all these you
alone were heard; but give assurance to all and tell us what good has been
done by you. But that one was not wishing, saying: I am a sinner, forgive me.
And as he was being compelled for a long time a voice came to him from heaven
saying: Your petition has been heard. And as the rustic was standing among the
whole people he began to narrate to them that I am a farming man and it is my
custom, the toil of my day, whatever I work, I divide them into three parts.
And first I set aside the part of the poor, after the end of the public tax,
and thus that of my expenditure. Doing this until now I have not ceased. And
all greeting were worshiping him. And they were pressing the man, each one mad
to see him.

28 So the blessed one stood on various columns forty-seven years and after all
these things he sought him; and it was Friday; and he was shut up in prayer
and as he had a custom, he spent the whole of Friday and on the Sabbath and on
the Lord's day and he did not nod, as he had a custom, to bless those who had
bent their knees. And as I saw this, I went up to him and I saw his face and
it was flashing as the sun. And as he had a custom of speaking to me, he
answered me nothing. And I was saying in myself, that he has gone to rest; and
again I was disbelieving and I was afraid to draw near to him and having dared
I say to him: My lord, why do you not speak to me and complete your prayer?
The world is waiting, that it may be blessed, behold three days. And having
stood again one hour I say to him: You answer me nothing, my lord. And having
stretched out my hand I touched his beard and as I saw that his body was
rather soft, I knew that he had died; and having set my face in my hands I
wept bitterly and having bent I kissed his mouth and his eyes and his beard
and having lifted aside his garment I kissed his feet and having taken hold of
his hand I set it upon my eyes. And in his whole body and in his garments
there was a scent of myrrh, so as to be gladdened from that fragrance. And
having stood about a half-hour I attended to his honorable relic and behold
his body was shaken together with the pillar and I heard a voice saying: Amen
amen amen, and from the fear I said: Bless me, lord, and remember me in your
good rest.

29 And I went down and I did not reveal the mystery to anyone, lest a tumult
come to be, but through a faithful man I signaled to the bishop of Antioch
Martyrius and to the general Ardaburius. And on the next day the bishop of
Antioch comes with also six other bishops; and Ardaburius also comes with six
hundred names, lest when the villages had been gathered they snatch his
honorable relic; for thus they were also planning. And curtains came to be
around his pillar. And three bishops go up and kiss his garments having said
three psalms and they brought up a lead coffin and they placed his holy relic
and they bring it down by machines and then all knew that saint Symeon had
died, so that all the Saracens also were gathered in arms with camels wishing
themselves also to snatch the relic. And a confusion of the crowd comes to
pass so that the mountain was not appearing from the multitude and from the
smoke of the incenses and of the candles and lamps that were burning, of which
there was no number. And the voices of the weeping of the men and women and
children were going far, and the whole mountain was being shaken from the cry
of the birds that had been gathered and had circled the enclosure of the
saint. So when they had brought him down, they placed him upon the marble
altar, which he had before his pillar, already having the fourth day having
died. And thus his holy relic was stretched out as having gone to rest an hour
before. And all the bishops gave him the love; and his face


was very bright as the light; and the hairs of his head and of his beard were
as snow. And the bishop of Antioch wished to take a hair of his beard unto a
blessing and his hand was withered, and all the bishops made a prayer for him
weeping and saying to the holy relic that Nothing is lacking to you of the
members or of the garments nor again will anyone take from your holy and
honorable relic. And as they were saying these things with weeping the hand of
the bishop was restored healthy. And then with psalms and hymns they put him
in the coffin.

30 And I the sinner was seeing before five and six days of his rest a man in a
fearful robe, which I am not able to narrate, and his length being as of two
men and speaking with him a third of the day and giving him the love. And I
wished to narrate to some the things concerning him and my mind was being
snatched and I was hindered from speaking until he went to rest. So as the man
whom I mentioned was coming to him, I was seeing, he says, them eating; but
what they were eating, I did not know, and they were psalming; but what they
were psalming, I did not know, except only the amen. And fear was surrounding
me toward the sight of the man.

31 So they set the coffin of the saint upon the carriage and thus with candles
and incenses and psalmody they carry him away to their city Antioch. And as
they were coming about five miles from the city in a place called Merope the
mules stood and were not moving; for a paradoxical mystery was there; for
there was on the right of the road a tomb. And a certain man was remaining at
it; and the affair of the man was such. For he was loving a married woman for
twenty years and he was not able to master her. And the woman died, and they
place her in that tomb. And that somehow the hater of good might gain the soul
of the man, having gone away into the tomb he opens aside the slab of the tomb
and has intercourse with the dead body. And immediately he was made dumb and
was held in the tomb and he was no longer able to withdraw from that place.
And as the travelers were passing they were attending to him sitting on the
little steps of the tomb, and each for God's sake was bringing to him, one
water, one food. And according to the will of God as the honorable relic was
passing on that day and (read ὁ) neither speaking nor hearing, the carriage
standing and the crowd, he came out from the tomb crying out and saying: Have
mercy on me, saint of God Symeon, and having taken hold of the carriage,
immediately his jaws were opened and his wits came into understanding. And all
having seen what had happened glorified God, and from the cry of the people
that place was being shaken. And the man was crying out: I today through you
have been saved, servant of God, for I was lost in sin.

32 And the whole city went out to the meeting of the honorable relic clothed
in white with candles and lamps. And they bring him into the church called of
Cassian. And after thirty days the general Ardaburius commands and they place
him in the Great Church, and from there again according to a revelation of God
a prayer-temple of the holy and righteous Symeon was built and thus with much
glory and hymns they transferred his holy relic into that prayer-house.

33 And many entreated, having given gold and silver, that they might take a
blessing from his holy relics. And the bishop granted it to no one because of
the oaths which he had sworn. And many healings are performed in the place
where his honorable relics are laid through the grace given to him by our
master Christ. And the saint of God Symeon was perfected on the first of the
month September, as our Lord


Jesus Christ is reigning, to whom be the glory and the power unto the ages of
the ages. Amen.
