Rom 1:18 For the wrath of God is revealed from heaven. This causal account has
been said toward what is above. For since he said that a righteousness of God
is revealed from faith to faith, and righteousness is this, that which assigns
to each according to desert, for this reason he says that the wrath of God is
revealed from heaven, as if he were saying: do you seek the works of the
righteousness of God announced to us? You will find them from the things which
he renders to each afterward according to his works; but first he sets forth
the things awarded from the wrath of God to those who deserve them, because of
the wantonness and uncleanness then prevailing, of which he also goes through
the kinds, and then also the things from his goodwill, speaking somehow thus:
to those who by endurance of a good work. For taking up the word again he
renders more briefly what things are given to each according to desert.

Rom 1:19 He did not say the knowledge of God, but that which is knowable; for
through that which is knowable the knowledge concerning God comes to be. For
the knowable things are known simply by knowledge, I mean the sensible
creation, but from these God; for from the greatness and beauty of created
things the Maker is contemplated analogously. Rom 2:7 What follows is thus: to
those who

here too the "and" is superfluous; for the conscience is that which is tested
between one another of the reasonings and has given assent.

Rom 2:27 "Fulfilling": if it is separated by the stop from the things before
it, it means the uncircumcision living according to the law, not being subject
to law; but if with the things below, the stop being placed toward "the law,"
then "fulfilling" is taken i.e. showing forth.

Rom 3:9 The more accurate and more ancient of the copies do not have "are we
better?", but "what then do we hold extra?" "Are we better?" can mean two
things: either being condemned more, since we also call the condemned man
liable; and it can also mean having been preferred, as we say: this one excels
that one in virtue. According to the first, then, "are we better?" wishes to
make clear being condemned, in some such sense. What then? Are the Jews
condemned before every nation, inasmuch as they have been honored above all
nations by the giving of the law, yet have been improved by none of these
things? And he says: not at all; for all have sinned on equal terms, and those
not under law would not be able to boast against us, as themselves having been
better esteemed than we in virtue. But according to the second meaning he
wishes to purge the arrogance of the Jews upon the reception of the law, and
he shows that the Jews are not even to be preferred to the other nations
because of the law, since they themselves likewise are in need of the kindness
of God. 655 Rom 5:5 From here, I think, the blessed Gregory the Theologian
also, having taken occasion, says: "for to this the moderate light of the
truth here brings me" and the things that follow.

Rom 5:14 Kingship comes to be in the willing subjection of those under it,
just as tyranny in the unwilling. Since then no law was checking the sinning,
reasonably death also, having taken power through sin, is said to reign; for
when the law had come in besides and had pointed out the whole sinful thing
and of what sort it is, human beings were no longer sinning equally without
fear, nor indeed entirely according to the willing. The thing through the
willing having been taken away, which had established the kingship for death,
reasonably this one also began to be stripped of the power. How? Inasmuch as
the law also was not checking sin in vain, but preparing the way for the
destruction of death. For if sin in paradise sprouted death, it is altogether
clear that what hinders sin is eager to deprive of authority the one who grew
strong from it. Therefore after the law death was seen not as reigning, but
rather as tyrannizing. Rom 5: That it might abound. Nothing will harm the

the conjunction being set according to a causal account; for in addition to

Rom 6:10 It is possible also to understand the passage thus: for that which he
died to sin, he died once for all; but that which he lives, he lives to God.
Just as he who knew no sin did not die through his own sin, but paying the
debt common to the nature, the God joined to the flesh conceding to yield to
being brought under the condemnation of the forefather, so also now, he says,
he lives, the God united in substance to it in nature having restored to this
what was owed. 656 What is this? That just as it was kept untouched by sin, so
also it became unenslaved to the penalty from it, through which it also fled
the bonds of death and ran up to living again. And since of all these things
nothing else than the divine in Christ is the cause, for this reason he is
said to live to God, as if one should say: if because the God-man shared in
flesh the tyranny of the flesh ran down upon him, and this not justly (for he
was not subjected to the charge of death), by a fitting account also, because
God had clothed himself with this flesh, this inherits the ever-living of God.
And otherwise: also that he might be both an example and a firstfruits of
those according to that flesh, even if they do not put away sin entirely, who
will also come to life again as something owed, even if by the unspeakable
compassion of him who poured out his own blood as ransom, the grace of living
again is assigned also to those who have toiled nothing. Rom 6:11 I.e. those
who through sin are subjected to death, living through the righteousness in
Christ; for this is more consistent with the sense already given and with the
sequence of the argument. Rom 6:12 If the body is mortal, it is useless to
yield to its desires, since those who serve it toil for nothing, but suffer
the same as those who write on water and sow on the road.

Rom 6:17-19 This can also be as a praise of them, as also "your faith is
proclaimed in the whole world." For since, he says, you obeyed from the heart,
at your heels the worthy wage also follows. What is this? That your proven
character has been sounded forth throughout all the earth, and that you have
become a type for the rest and a canon well-fitted to the gospel of Christ.
657 "I speak humanly" is of this sort: since human life is not able to live
through wholly in inaction, but is balanced by the inclination toward either
of the things to be done, for this reason after bringing on the freedom from
sin the enslavement under righteousness, he added "I speak humanly." For he
also anticipated the objection in the argument. And what? Are we not left free
at all, but led again under slavery? And he says: you cannot live through in
idleness; for the time in the fleshly life is for work. Therefore, lest having
put away the works from sin, then being at leisure, you be again driven by
sin, because this heavy and unstable little flesh cannot be wholly still, it
is in truth most excellent that the enslavement of righteousness succeed the
freedom from sin, and that you show even for this as much zeal as also upon
sin. For I do not seek more, although it is due, but I speak considerately the
analogical thing and that to which human life, flowing as it is, is subjected.
Rom 6:23 "Wages" is the name of the soldier's pay. Since then those who submit
to sin, as being led under a general, the devil, accomplish what seems good to
him, reasonably they also carry off wages worthy of him, that is the opsonia.
Rom 7:5 One must supply in thought "having been made known to me." Rom 9:3
This does not separate, but rather binds him to Christ and fuses and unites;
for he who prays fervently to reconcile the kinsmen to the beloved would, if
somehow he himself should bear to stand away from this, not rather long to
herd together with the chain of his dearest and be ranked with them. What
therefore seems to lead him away from the love of the Teacher, this most of
all confirms it, in a novel manner of the separation binding together, and of
the being-separated fusing, and of the standing-apart making one.

Rom 9:6 The thought understood through these is of this sort: let no one,
seeing the bringing-in of the nations, think that the word of God has fallen
through, the word that raises up children to Abraham from Isaac alone 658 and
bequeaths the grace according to Christ to these alone. As a debt, if indeed
Christ himself is evangelized as having been sent to none other than the lost
sheep of Israel. For this is not possible, nor is it possible that the word of
God toward Abraham fall away; for not all who are of Israel, these alone are
Israel; and the things that follow. And "not such" i.e. I say nothing of this
sort, that if some disbelieved, the word of God has fallen away and the
promises toward Abraham and those toward the sons of Israel. And "not such"
i.e. but it is impossible that the word of God has fallen away, that is, this
is impossible to think, that the word of God has fallen away; for "such"
signifies not only likeness but also the possible. Plato: "as I am able to be
persuaded by none of my people, except by the word which appears right to me
as I reason."

Rom 9:22 Not fitted by God, but having fitted himself. Rom 9:24 One must

Rom 13:11 He says something of this sort: and this too, he says, do,

Rom 15:8 I.e. he ministered to the circumcision, having restored what was owed
to it. What is this? That through it all the nations be blessed; for "in your
seed," he says, "all the nations of the earth shall be blessed." In which
things then none of the others of the seed of Abraham has been able to bring
this to completion, for this reason Christ, having put on the circumcision and
through it having fulfilled the whole law, as it were served this, so that the
promises of God the Father might not fall through.
