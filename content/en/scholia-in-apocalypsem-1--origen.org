28 (After I had come to know, he says, that the root of David, the lion who
conquered from the tribe of Judah, has taken the book in order to loose its
seals) I saw in the midst of the throne and of the four living creatures and
of the elders a lamb standing as slain. After the resurrection and the
ascension the lamb that was seen was no longer seen slain, but as slain, and
still standing, that is, no longer being altered. If then according to a new
standing it has henceforth seven horns, it has a holy and blessed kingdom, for
the seven are a symbol of this. In addition to the seven horns it also has
seven eyes, which are no other than the seven spirits of God that go forth
upon the earth, overseeing the things done by human beings. With these agrees:
The seven eyes of the Lord are they that look upon all the earth.

29 It has been said somewhere: Let my prayer be set forth as incense before
thee. The bowls full of these incenses are the ruling faculties of those who
genuinely pray to Christ. And you will also say that the harps are their power
fitted musically and melodiously, according to which they understand and love
Christ. And what do they say in singing the new song, if not: Worthy art thou,
O Master, Savior, to take the book, and what follows? And these things are
plainly about the one who was crucified, who was led as a sheep to slaughter.
From the slaughter that came to be in this way the blood that flowed is given
as a price on behalf of those who have been saved. And since he was not
crucified on behalf of a part or of one nation of human beings, he purchased
with his blood from every tribe of Israel and from every tongue of human
beings. And further, a difference of people and nation can be taken in
different ways, by saying that those taken from the purer and wiser human
beings were purchased from a people, but those from the more common and the
many were purchased from a nation. The former hypothesis is confirmed as to
the number of the twenty-four elders from the present wording; for they
themselves confessed that they were purchased from among human beings and were
chosen. We then who read these things and have learned that the incenses are
the prayers of the saints, and that good deeds are spiritual sacrifices and
acceptable to God, see that from the sojourn of Christ incense is brought
forward in every place to the name of the Lord, and a pure sacrifice; for
great is his name among the nations, through the teaching of Christ, as the
prophet says.

30 From the scriptures it is possible to find that as it were certain
activities are a body of God, such as the serving hands, and the overseeing
eyes, and ears appointed over prayers, and feet of the providence of God that
drive away, for those who dwell upon the earth. Since therefore he is about to
speak of a great wrath of God, it is not the incidental passion that is named,
being called wrath of God, existing outside him, yet assigned for use to those
who need it, to which they are also delivered, as unworthy of God, in order
that they may long for the God whom they have despised when they come under
the authority of the worse. And the wrath of God is the devil. For also in the
second of Kingdoms it is said: And he set the wrath of the Lord to be kindled
against Israel, and it incited David, saying: Go and number Israel and Judah.
And the wrath of God that incited David is not "saying" in the feminine, but
"saying" in the masculine; therefore besides God himself, who is said to have
spoken many things to the saints, there is some other, his wrath, that says
these things and commands to sin a sin upon which punishment from God follows
for the one persuaded by the wrath that has spoken in this way. And how does
that which punishes for sins and justly punishes stir up toward sin, in order
that, having persuaded toward sinning, it may punish justly? For unjustly
would the cause of the sin punish the one who has sinned. But, as has been
said, I think the wrath of God is the devil, by his persuading to sin, wishing
to take the one who has sinned into his power from the sinning. For also in
the first of Chronicles, narrating the same cause concerning David, he says
thus: And the devil stood in Israel and incited David to number Israel. For he
used the designation "incited" both in the second of Kingdoms and in the first
of Chronicles, in the one of the wrath of the Lord, and in the other of the
devil. And if the "incited" is a cause of sinning, and the cause of sinning is
the devil, the devil was named through both designations, both through the
well-worn one and through the one that escapes most people, the one that names
him "wrath of the Lord," according to the Great Ode and elsewhere saying: I
sent my wrath and it consumed them as stubble, and what follows. For
everything that is sent by someone is other than the one who sends it forth.
Who then would the wrath be that is sent upon the Egyptians, if not, as we
taught in the first of Chronicles, the devil? If then sinners are said to be
delivered to the wrath of God, they must be understood as being delivered to
the devil, as Paul delivered the Corinthian, and those whom he delivered to
Satan, that they might be taught not to blaspheme.

We also have angels who watch over us and help us when we do well, and a
universal judgment comes about toward all these, as he says: for Arise, he
says, plead toward the mountains, and let the hills hear thy voice; hear, ye
hills, the judgment of the Lord. And in these things the Word of God seems to
be commanded to be judged with the powers entrusted with human affairs, in
order that someone may be able to show whether, through someone's neglect and
omission of the things that fall to him on behalf of human beings, a person
has come to be in sin or in blame. And we shall understand this by using an
example, say, a judgment of a people with the bishops, and a judgment of sons
with the father, and a judgment of learners with the teacher, when sometimes
the people will show that the cause of their sins has come from the bishops,
and sometimes the bishop, having shown that he has done all things on his own
part and has omitted nothing of the things that fall to a zealous ruler, will
prove the people liable to the charges. And understand the like also
concerning sons who blame the upbringing among the fathers, and who defend
themselves and prove the fathers to have become causes of such failings; and
likewise the contrary, the fathers defending themselves as having omitted
nothing toward the upbringing of the sons according to the word of God, and
the sons being convicted as having come to be in sins through their own sloth.
And it is not far from these things to understand also concerning disciples
and teachers.

31 When grim things are about to be brought on, some serving angel of God
calls to those entrusted with the toilsome things, not yet to bring them on
until the servants of God receive seals upon their foreheads. He commands this
same thing in other words in Ezekiel the prophet: Smite and spare not at all,
but upon those on whom is the sign come not near. Perhaps then, since those
who are punished suffer this through their own sins, a certain mark signifying
the righteousness that belongs to them is set upon the forehead of the
righteous, that is, upon their boldness in virtue, who, having obtained the
present sign, giving thanks to the one who has given it, say: The light of thy
countenance has been signed upon us, O Lord, and again: I have given to those
who fear thee a sign, to flee from before the bow. It must be inquired whether
it is possible that from Israel according to the flesh, while John was still
remaining in this life, there were so many thousands of virgin men. Since
therefore the sensible narrative carries much that is impossible, and the
things in the book, it being holy, must be true, it is necessary to take the
present things according to a spiritual sequence. For it is consistent that
the true Israel, that has no guile, be divided into tribes. If then we say
that of this Israel there is so great a multitude of virgin men, we say
nothing impossible, for all who have come to Christ, both Jews and Greeks,
fill up this intelligible nation. And since those who are Hebrews in this way
have much harmony and concord, being occupied about themselves in being
perfected in one mind and one judgment, equal divisions of it also underlie so
great a number. The number is therefore square, equally equal-sided from the
twelve. For twelve times 12 is 144. And the square figure bears a symbol of a
firm standing.

32 Going on he says that these 144 thousands are virgins. And if you take
these tribes as the bodily ones that are spoken of in Israel, where would you
find virgins, where 12 thousands of virginity, according to tribe? But in the
church we find this, virginity zealously pursued, the Word teaching it, not
according to commandment, nor that he may cast a snare upon those who hear,
but freely chosen in gladness and rejoicing, as it is written. These then are
they who were not defiled with women, as it has been said, for they are
virgins; and no lie was found in their mouth.

33 These white robes can indicate their undefiled purposes and deeds. In
addition to the white robes with which they have been clothed, they hold palms
in their hands, a symbol of the victory by which they have conquered the
world. These are they who come from the great tribulation, through martyrdom
and confession, clearly, and the other circumstances for the sake of Christ
which wicked human beings and demons bring upon the disciples of Jesus, having
whitened and washed the robes they wear with the blood of the lamb that was
slain on their behalf. And in order that we human beings may understand the
unceasing character of their service, the time among us, which is divided into
day and night, was named.

34 Attend whether the washed and whitened robes of those who have come up from
great tribulation can be their bodies, already contemplated beforehand as
having risen incorruptible and spiritual.

35 God, having judged to subject the sinners to plagues, left some human
beings inexperienced of these plagues, both those still remaining in the
things they were working and as many as were left without tasting of the
plagues, in order that they might have a place of repentance, so as no longer
to worship the golden demons and the images constructed from other matter. It
is made clear that some worship the golden and silver demons, and those of
bronze and of wood; so that demons are to be understood as the spirits that
sit upon the lifeless forms. And golden and the rest, not perceiving through
sight and hearing nor walking, are the sensible images. For they have a mouth
and will not speak, and what follows, as it is written in the psalms.

36 That the great words, when they are made clear, are thunders to the
righteous, the prophet perhaps also indicates, saying: the voice of thy
thunder was in the wheel. For you will seek a wheel there, and having seen the
wheel being turned in a circle you will see thunder there. And examining also
the things concerning the sons of thunder, James and John, whom Jesus called
Boanerges, which is sons of thunder, you will find them reasonably called sons
of thunder because of the greatness of voice of their thoughts and doctrines.
For I heard (he says) seven thunders, and as many things as the seven thunders
spoke I was about to write, and it was said to me: Write not the things which
the seven thunders spoke. Do you then understand concerning these that such
thunders spoke words that can be written and not written; and that the holy
John heard an articulate voice through such a voice. But perhaps the seven
thunders that spoke to John, if you attend to the scripture, you will find who
they are: one thunder is wisdom; another thunder understanding; a third
thunder counsel; a fourth thunder might; knowledge a fifth thunder; piety a
sixth; a seventh thunder fear. If I hear the thunders speaking these things, I
cannot write, for not even the world itself could contain the books written
from the voice of the holy thunders that speak in Christ Jesus, to whom be the
glory unto the ages. Amen.

37 And thy wrath came, and the time of the dead, according to the time of the
consummation, when all have been manifested at the judgment seat of Christ, in
order that each may receive worthily of the things he has lived. The wrath of
God is constituted, which each stored up for himself in a day of wrath and of
revelation of the righteous judgment of God; in which time also the reward of
the prophets and of the saints and of those who fear the name of God will be
rendered. Of those then who will receive a reward three orders are indicated,
of prophets and of saints and of others who fear the name of God. And see
whether those being introduced into reverence are signified by fearing the
name of God, but those who now fear him and not his name any longer by the
designation of the saints; for Fear the Lord, ye his saints, for there is no
want to those who fear him. And likely you will find the prophets as in a
species of these saints; for the saint is more than a prophet; for it is
necessary that the prophet of God also be a saint, but it does not convert:
many who are saints do not prophesy.

38 See whether the dragon, having warred with the angels and been afflicted,
cast down from heaven, as he fell, dragged the third of the stars, which
stars, being divine powers, likely revolted with him and were brought down
together with the dragon; as Isaiah says: How has the morning star fallen out
of heaven?
