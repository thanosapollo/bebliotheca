ORATION XXXVIII.
On the Theophany, or the Nativity of the Saviour.
A. Christ is born, glorify him; Christ from heaven, go out to meet him; Christ
upon earth, be lifted up. Sing to the Lord, all the earth; and, that I may say
both together, Let the heavens rejoice, and let the earth be glad, for him who
is heavenly, then =36.313= earthly. Christ in flesh, rejoice with trembling
and with joy; with trembling, because of sin; with joy, because of hope.
Christ from a Virgin; women, live as virgins, that you may become mothers of
Christ. Who does not worship him who is from the beginning? Who does not
glorify him who is the last?
B. Again the darkness is dissolved, again the light is made to stand, again
Egypt is punished with darkness, again Israel is enlightened by a pillar. Let
the people that sits in the darkness of ignorance see the great light of
knowledge. The ancient things have passed away; behold, all things have become
new. The letter yields, the spirit gains the advantage, the shadows run past,
the truth comes in upon them. Melchizedek is gathered up; he who is without
mother becomes without father; without mother formerly, without father in the
second place. Laws of nature are dissolved. The world above must be filled.
Christ commands; let us not strain against him. All you nations, clap your
hands, because a child has been born to us, a son also has been given to us,
whose government is upon his shoulder (for with the cross it is raised up),
and his name is called Angel of great counsel, that of the Father. Let John
cry: Prepare the way of the Lord. And I too will cry the power of the day: The
fleshless is made flesh, the Word is thickened, the invisible is seen, the
intangible is handled, the timeless begins, the Son of God becomes Son of man,
Jesus Christ, yesterday and today, the same also unto the ages. Let Jews be
scandalized, let Greeks mock, let heretics talk till their tongues ache. Then
they will believe, when they see him going up into heaven; and if not then,
yet when they see him coming from heaven, and sitting as judge.
C. These things later. But the things now are the Theophany, the festival, or
the Nativity; for both are said, two names being set upon one thing. For God
appeared to men through birth; on the one hand Being, and always Being from
the always Being, above cause and word (for there was no word higher than the
Word); on the other, becoming later for our sake, that he who gave being might
also grant well-being; or rather, that having flowed away from well-being
through wickedness, he might lead us back to it again through incarnation. And
the name, from the appearing, is Theophany; from the being born, Nativity.
=36.316=
D. This is our festival, this we celebrate today, a sojourning of God toward
men, that we might dwell with God, or return (for so it is more proper to
say), that putting off the old man we might put on the new; and as we died in
Adam, so we might live in Christ, being born with Christ, and crucified with
him, and buried with him, and raised with him. For I must undergo the
beautiful conversion; and as the painful things came from the more wholesome,
so from the painful the more wholesome must return. For where sin abounded,
grace superabounded; and if the tasting condemned, how much more did the
suffering of Christ justify? Therefore let us keep festival, not in a
panegyric way, but in a godly way; not worldly, but above the world; not our
own things, but the things of him who is ours, or rather the things of the
Master; not the things of weakness, but the things of healing; not the things
of formation, but the things of re-formation.
E. And how shall this be? Let us not crown porches, let us not set up dances,
let us not adorn streets, let us not feast the eye, let us not pipe upon the
ear, let us not make the smell effeminate, let us not prostitute the taste,
let us not indulge the touch, the roads ready at hand toward wickedness, and
the entries of sin; let us not be made soft in clothing, both delicate and
overflowing, and whose finest point is uselessness, nor with translucencies of
stones, nor with flashings of gold, nor with tricks of colors that belie the
natural beauty, and have been invented against the image; not in revels and
drunkenness, with which I know beds and wantonness are yoked; since the
lessons of evil teachers are evil, or rather the harvests of wicked seeds are
wicked. Let us not pitch high couches, making tents for the belly of the
things of luxury. Let us not honor the flower-scents of wines, the tricks of
cooks, the costliness of ointments. Let not earth and sea bring us as a gift
their precious dung; for so I know how to honor luxury. Let us not strive that
one outdo another in intemperance. For intemperance to me is everything
superfluous and beyond need; and this while others are hungry and in want, who
are of the same clay and mixture.
F. But let us leave these things to Greeks, and to Greek pomps and festivals;
who even name as gods those who rejoice in the reek of fat, and consistently
serve the divine with the belly, being evil of evil demons, both fashioners,
and mystagogues, and initiates. But we, whose object of worship is the Word,
if we must have some luxury, let us luxuriate in word =36.317= and in divine
law, and in narratives, both the others and those from which the present
festival comes; that the luxury may be akin, and not far from him who called
us together. Or do you wish (for I too am your host today) that I set forth
the word about these things to you, my fair diners, as abundantly and as
honorably as I can, that you may know how the stranger can feed the natives,
and the rustic the city-people, and he who does not luxuriate those who
luxuriate, and the poor and homeless those who are splendid with abundance?
And I will begin from here; and cleanse for me both mind, and ear, and
thought, as many of you as luxuriate in such things; since the word is about
God, and divine; that you may go away having truly luxuriated in the things
that are not emptied. And the same will be both most full and most concise; so
as neither to grieve by deficiency, nor to be unpleasant through satiety.
G. God always was, and is, and will be; or rather, he always is. For was, and
will be, are portions of our time, and of flowing nature; but he who is
always, and this he names himself, giving the oracle to Moses on the mountain.
For having gathered the whole in himself, he has being, neither having begun,
nor about to cease, like some sea of being infinite and unbounded, falling
beyond every thought both of time and of nature; sketched by mind alone, and
this very dimly and moderately, not from the things according to him, but from
the things about him, one impression being gathered from another, into some
one image of the truth, fleeing before it is grasped, and slipping away before
it is thought; flashing around our ruling part, even when it has been
purified, as much as the speed of lightning that does not stand still does to
sight. As it seems to me, that by what is graspable it may draw toward itself
(for what is perfectly ungraspable is beyond hope, and not to be attempted),
and by what is ungraspable it may be wondered at, and being wondered at may be
more desired, and being desired may purify, and purifying may make us godlike,
and to such as have become so, as to its own, it may already converse, the
word dares something youthful, God being united to gods and made known, and
perhaps so much as he already knows those who are known. Infinite then is the
divine, and hard to behold; and this alone of it is wholly comprehensible, the
infinity; even if someone should think that because it is of a simple nature
it is either wholly ungraspable, or perfectly graspable. For let us inquire
what it is that is of a simple nature. For simplicity is not this as a nature
for him, =36.320= if indeed neither for compounds is being compounds their
only being.
H. And infinity being contemplated in two ways, according to beginning and end
(for what is above these, and not in these, is infinite), when the mind looks
off into the depth above, not having where to stand and rest its impressions
about God, it named the infinite and unpassable there unoriginate; but when
into the things below and the things next, immortal and indestructible; and
when it gathers the whole, eternal. For an age is neither time, nor some part
of time; for it is not measurable; but what time is to us, being measured by
the course of the sun, this to the everlasting is age, that which is
coextensive with the things that are, like some temporal movement and
interval. Let these things have been philosophized by me concerning God for
now. For it is not the season beyond these, because the matter before us is
not theology, but economy. And when I say God, I say Father, and Son, and Holy
Spirit; the Godhead neither being poured out beyond these, lest we bring in a
people of gods; nor being bounded within these, lest we be condemned for a
poverty of Godhead, either Judaizing through the monarchy, or Hellenizing
through the abundance. For the evil in both is alike, even if it is found in
contraries. Thus then the Holy of Holies, which is also veiled even to the
seraphim, and is glorified with three sanctifications, coming together into
one lordship and Godhead; which has also been philosophized by some other of
those before us both most beautifully and most loftily.
I. But since this was not enough for goodness, to be moved only in the
contemplation of itself, but the good had to be poured out and to travel, that
there might be more who are benefited (for this was of the extreme goodness),
first he conceives the angelic and heavenly powers; and the conception was a
work, fulfilled by the Word, and perfected by the Spirit. And thus second
splendors came to be, ministers of the first splendor; whether intelligent
spirits, or a fire as it were immaterial and bodiless, or some other nature,
as near as may be to the things said, these must be =36.321= supposed. I wish
indeed to say that they are unmoved toward evil, and have only the movement of
the beautiful, as being about God, and first illumined from God; for the
things here, of a second illumination. But he persuades me not to suppose them
unmoved, but hard to move, and to speak so, he who through his splendor was
Lucifer, and both became and is called darkness through pride, and the
apostate powers under him, creators of wickedness by flight from the
beautiful, and procurers of it for us.
J. Thus then the intelligible world came to be for him, and for these reasons,
as far at least as I may philosophize about these things, weighing great
things with a small word. And since the first things were well with him, he
conceives a second world, material and seen; and this is the system and
compound out of heaven and earth and the things in the midst, praiseworthy
indeed for the fitness of each, but more worthy of praise for the harmony and
symphony out of all, one thing being well toward another, and all toward all,
unto the completion of one world; that he might show that he was able to bring
into being not only a nature akin to himself, but also one wholly foreign. For
akin to Godhead are the intelligent natures, and graspable by mind alone; but
wholly foreign, as many as are under sense, and of these themselves still
further, as many as are altogether soulless and unmoved. But what is this to
us, someone of those who are exceedingly fond of festival and more heated
might perhaps say? Spur the colt around the turning-post. Philosophize for us
the things of the feast, and those for which we sit in front today. This then
I will also do, even if I began a little from above, the longing and the word
having so compelled me.
K. Mind then already and sense, thus distinguished from one another, stood
within their own bounds, and bore in themselves the magnificence of the
Creator Word, silent praisers of the great work, and far-sounding heralds. But
there was not yet a mixture from both, nor some mingling of the contraries, a
token of greater wisdom, and of the lavishness concerning the natures; nor was
the whole wealth of goodness known. The craftsman Word wishing then to exhibit
this, and one living being from both, I mean from invisible and visible
nature, creates man; and taking the body from matter already existing, and
putting in a breath from himself (which the word knows to be an intelligent
soul and image of God), =36.324= he sets him upon the earth as a kind of
second world, great in a small one, another angel, a mixed worshipper,
overseer of the visible creation, initiate of the intelligible, king of the
things upon earth, ruled from above, earthly and heavenly, temporal and
immortal, visible and intelligible, midway between greatness and
lowliness; the same, spirit and flesh; spirit because of the grace, flesh
because of the pride; the one, that he may remain and glorify the
benefactor; the other, that he may suffer, and suffering may be reminded and
trained, being ambitious with greatness; a living being managed here, and
transferred elsewhere, and the end
of the mystery deified by the inclination toward God. For to this, I think,
the measured light of truth here tends, both to see and to suffer the splendor
of God, worthy of him who both bound, and will loose, and will bind again more
loftily.
L. This one he set in paradise, whatever this paradise was, having honored him
with self-determination, that the good might belong to the one who chose no
less than to the one who supplied the seeds, a tiller of immortal plants,
perhaps of divine thoughts, both the simpler and the more perfect, naked both
in simplicity and in the artless life, and without any covering and screen.
For such it was fitting that he who was from the beginning should be. And he
gives a law, matter for the self-determination. And the law was a commandment,
of which plants he was to partake, and which he was not to touch. And this was
the tree of knowledge, neither planted badly from the beginning, nor forbidden
enviously (let not the fighters against God send their tongues there, nor
imitate the serpent); but good when partaken of in due season (for the plant
was contemplation, as my contemplation has it, upon which it is safe for those
more perfect in habit alone to enter), but not good for those still simpler,
and greedier in desire, just as neither is perfect food profitable for those
still tender and in need of milk. But when by envy of the devil, and by the
woman's spite, both that which she suffered as being more tender, and that
which she brought as being more persuasive (alas for my weakness! for that of
the forefather is mine), he forgot the commandment that had been given, and
was defeated by the bitter tasting; and at once from the tree of life, and
from paradise, and from God, he becomes an exile through wickedness, and puts
on the coats of skins, perhaps the coarser flesh, both mortal and resistant;
and this first he comes to know his own shame, and hides from God. Yet here
too he gains something: death, and the cutting off of sin, that the evil may
not be immortal; and the punishment becomes a loving-kindness. For so I am
persuaded that God punishes. =36.325=
M. And having first been disciplined by many things, corresponding to the many
sins, whose root of wickedness sprouted according to different causes and
times, by word, law, prophets, benefits, threats, blows, waters, burnings,
wars, victories, defeats, signs from heaven, signs from air, from earth, from
sea, unexpected changes of men, of cities, of nations, by which the thing
aimed at was that wickedness be worn away. At last he needs a stronger drug
for the more terrible sicknesses, mutual murders, adulteries, perjuries, lusts
for men, the last of all evils and the first, idolatries, and the transfer of
worship from the maker to the creatures. These things, since they needed a
greater help, also obtain a greater. And this was the Word of God himself, the
pre-eternal, the invisible, the incomprehensible, the bodiless, the beginning
from the beginning, the light from the light, the fountain of life and of
immortality, the impress of the archetypal beauty, the unmoved seal, the
unchanging image, the Father's definition and Word; he comes to his own image,
and wears flesh because of the flesh, and is mingled with an intelligent soul
because of my soul, cleansing like by like. And he becomes, except for sin,
man; conceived from the Virgin, both soul and flesh having been purified
beforehand by the Spirit (for it was needful both that birth be honored, and
that virginity be honored more); and coming forth God with the assumption, one
from two contraries, flesh and Spirit; of which the one deified, and the other
was deified. O the new mingling! O the strange mixture! He who is becomes, and
the uncreated is created, and the uncontainable is contained, through a
mediating intelligent soul mediating between Godhead and the thickness of
flesh. And he who enriches becomes poor; for he is poor as to my flesh,
that I may be rich as to his Godhead. And he who is full, is emptied; for he
is emptied of his own glory for a little, that I may partake of that fullness.
What is the wealth of goodness? What is this mystery about me? I partook of
the image, and I did not keep it; he partakes of my flesh, that he may both
save the image, and make the flesh immortal. He communicates a second
communion, far more paradoxical than the former; by as much as then he
imparted of the better, now he partakes of the worse. This is more godlike
than the former; this is loftier to those who have mind. =36.328=
N. To these things what do the slanderers say to us, the bitter reasoners of
Godhead, the accusers of the things praised, the dark ones about the light,
the uneducated about wisdom, for whom Christ died freely, the ungrateful
creatures, the formations of the Evil one? Do you charge God with this, the
benefaction? Is he small for this, that he is lowly for you? Because the good
shepherd came upon the wandering one, he who lays down his life for the sheep,
upon the mountains and the hills, on which you used to sacrifice, and found
him wandering; and having found him, he took him upon the shoulders, on which
also the wood; and having taken him, he brought him back to the life above;
and having brought him up, he numbered him with those who remain? Because he
lit a lamp, his own flesh, and swept the house, cleansing the world of sin,
and sought the drachma, the royal image buried under the passions, and he
calls together the powers friendly to him upon the finding of the drachma, and
makes them partners of the gladness, whom he had also made initiates of the
economy? Because the exceedingly bright light follows the forerunner's lamp,
and the Word the voice, and the bridegroom the bridesman, who prepares for the
Lord a peculiar people, and purifies beforehand toward the Spirit through the
water? Do you charge God with these things? For these do you suppose him
worse, because he girds himself with a towel, and washes the feet of the
disciples, and shows the best road of exaltation, the humiliation? Because for
the soul bent to the ground he is humbled, that he may also raise up with
himself what was nodding downward under sin? And how do you not also accuse
that, that he even eats with tax-collectors, and at tax-collectors' houses,
and makes disciples of tax-collectors, that he too may gain something? What is
this? The salvation of sinners; unless one should also blame the physician,
because he stoops over the sufferings, and endures foul smells, that he may
give health to those who are ill; and him who bends down into a pit through
loving-kindness, that he may save the beast that has fallen in, according to
the law.
O. He was sent, but as man; for he was twofold; since he also grew weary, and
hungered, and thirsted, and was in agony, and wept by the law of a body; and
if also as God, what is this? Consider the good pleasure of the Father to be a
sending, to whom he refers his own things, both honoring a timeless beginning,
and that he may not seem to be an opposing god. Since he is also said to have
been handed over, but it is also written that he handed himself over; and to
have been raised by the Father, and taken up, but also that he raised himself,
and went up again. Those are of the good pleasure, these of the authority. But
you speak the things that lessen, and =36.329= pass by the things that exalt;
and that he suffered, you reckon; but that it was willingly, you do not add.
What things the Word suffers even now! By some he is honored as God and
confounded; by others he is dishonored as flesh and severed. With whom
shall he be more angry? Or rather, whom shall he forgive? Those who badly
join, or those who cut? For those also ought to have divided, and these to
have joined; the ones in number, the others in Godhead. Do you stumble at the
flesh? This the Jews also. Or do you also call him a Samaritan? And what
follows I will keep silent. Do you disbelieve the Godhead? This not even the
demons. O more unbelieving than demons, and more unfeeling than Jews! Those
thought the name of Son a voice of equal honor; these knew him who drove them
out to be God. For they were persuaded from what they suffered. But you
neither receive the equality, nor confess the Godhead. It would have been
better for you to have been circumcised and possessed, that I may say
something even ridiculously, than in uncircumcision and health to be disposed
wickedly and godlessly.
P. A little later then you will also see Jesus being purified in the Jordan,
my purification; or rather purifying the waters by the purification (for he
did not need purification, he who takes away the sin of the world), and the
heavens being split, and being witnessed by the kindred Spirit, and being
tempted, and conquering, and being served by angels, and healing every disease
and every infirmity, and making the dead alive (would that he would also you
who have been deadened by evil opinion), and driving out demons, some through
himself, some through the disciples, and feeding myriads with few loaves, and
walking the sea on foot, and being betrayed, and being crucified, and
crucifying with himself my sin; being brought as a lamb, and offering as a
priest, being buried as man, and being raised as God, then also going up, and
about to come with his own glory. How many festivals I have according to each
of the mysteries of Christ! Of all of which one heading is my perfection and
re-formation, and return to the first Adam.
Q. But now receive for me the conception, and leap toward it; if not as John
from the womb, yet as David at the resting of the ark. And reverence the
enrollment, through which you were written into the heavens; and worship the
birth, through which you were loosed from the bonds of birth; and honor little
Beth =36.332= lehem, which brought you back to paradise; and worship the
manger, through which being without reason, you were fed by the Word. Know, as
an ox, him who acquired you, Isaiah commands you, and, as an ass, the manger
of his lord; whether you are one of the clean, and under the law, and of those
who bring up the cud of the word, and fit for sacrifice; or of those still
unclean, and inedible, and unfit for sacrifice, and of the gentile portion.
Run with the star, and bring gifts with Magi, gold, and frankincense, and
myrrh, as to a king, and as to God, and as to one dead for you. Glorify with
shepherds, hymn with angels, dance with archangels. Let there be a common
festival of heavenly and earthly powers. For I am persuaded that those also
rejoice together and keep festival together today; if indeed they are lovers
of men and lovers of God, just as those whom David brings in after the passion
going up with Christ, and coming to meet him, and bidding one another the
lifting of the gates.
R. Hate one thing of those around the birth of Christ, the child-murder of
Herod; or rather reverence this too, the sacrifice of the same age as Christ,
offered beforehand of the new victim. If he flees into Egypt, eagerly share
the exile. It is a fair thing to flee with Christ when he is persecuted. If he
lingers in Egypt, call him out of Egypt, being well worshipped there. Travel
blamelessly through all the ages of Christ and powers, as a disciple of
Christ. Be purified, be circumcised, strip off the covering from birth. After
this teach in the temple, drive out the sellers of God; be stoned, if you must
suffer this; you will escape the notice of those who throw, I know well, you
will also flee through the midst of them, as God. For the Word is not stoned.
If you are brought to Herod, do not even answer for the most part. He will
reverence your silence also more than the long words of others. If you are
scourged, seek also the things that remain. Taste gall, because of the
tasting; be given vinegar to drink, seek spittings, receive blows, buffetings;
be crowned with thorns, with the roughness of the life according to God; put
on the scarlet, receive the reed, be worshipped by those who play at the
truth; at last be crucified with him, die =36.333= with him, be buried with
him eagerly, that you may also rise with him, and be glorified with him, and
reign with him, seeing God as far as he is, and being seen, him who in Trinity
is worshipped and glorified, whom we pray may even now be made clear to us, as
far as is reachable for those bound by the flesh, in Christ Jesus our Lord, to
whom be the glory unto the ages. Amen.
