Oration of Constantine the emperor to the generals of the East.

1 Even to speak with you often without some reasonable cause is desirable and
beloved, just as to be left without converse with you is painful and grievous,
according to me and my judgment. For I do not so love and embrace my soldiers
and count them worthy of every address and greeting, as not also to do this
same thing toward you by writing, you whom the only eternal and immortal King,
through boundless mercies, has granted to me as my lot and a people gathered
by God and the strongest measuring-line of the Lord's inheritance. But also to
exhort you by tongue in the things of goodwill and obedience is to me sweetest
of all and most eagerly sought, and to teach and sound in your ears through
syllables the things concerning wars, and to make those who are fit for this
more courageous toward courage, and to rouse and raise the more sluggish
toward boldness and firmness, is known and stands as sweeter to me than every
pleasure and every luxury.

The sacred oracles of the divine Gospel, wishing to show the greatness of the
love of God the Father toward men, say: "For God so loved the world that he
gave his only-begotten Son unto death"; but I give not the only-begotten Son,
but my whole self, in body and in soul, to you, and I nail and mix my flesh
with your flesh and bones with the bones, and I count each one of the members
as grown together and born together with you, and the soul itself, though it
is one, I divide and portion among you all, and in the part that is mine I
wish my people gathered by God to be both ensouled and given life by me.

Little children, whom I begot through the Gospel and planted in the
inheritance of God, whom God increased and brought to the measure of a most
robust stature, receive the present exhortation, sent up to you from the very
depth of the soul and from the hidden treasuries of the heart. For my heart
and my flesh, according to David who sings among the prophets, have rejoiced
over you. And how should one not rejoice and leap and be glad, when God has
given to his own inheritance such armies, such a courageous and most noble
people, such champions and defenders of the Romans?

Often we have anointed you toward courage through written recitations; very
often we have admonished you, and we have had no satiety of exhortation toward
you. Why is this? Wishing to set before you the burning and fiery and warm
longing that is in us toward you, we do not even set the briefest moment among
things done by the way, that we should fail to take the wings of a dove and
come to rest with you and make plain the affection toward you.

2 But now, as if not content with the former things and counting them small
beside the boiling of love that is in us toward you, we have sent out to you
those whom we had as the better of the servants, most well-disposed, most
faithful, most worthy, distinguished in prudence and experience and honored by
us more than the others, so that you might also have this to consider: that
having torn these men from our very inward parts and from the heart, we have
set them over you as leaders and governors. First, to choose out the most
courageous and most noble from among you and to separate them from the others,
so that your courage, with their cowardice overshadowing and covering it, may
not remain unseen and unrecognized as to the things of your virtue; and in
their place let those men bring in whom they themselves choose; and by such
preparation and selection and laborious and careful diligence let them lead
our Christ-loving tagmata and themes over to what is better and stronger, so
as to terrify the adversary by report alone.

3 And since through letters of these same most distinguished men and most
worthy servants of ours we have been taught that already, according to our
command, or rather according to the nod and inclination of God, they have cast
off all that is useless and unfit for wars, and have preferred and set apart
for wars all that is noble and useful and that runs into danger before the
others, and have used all care and zeal and laborious watchings for your good
order and establishment, and such servants of our Majesty are about to take
you up, as already fitted and prepared, and to move you out on expedition and
to rush against the enemies, wherever they have been appointed by our Majesty:
our joy has been increased beyond measure, and being seized at once by tears
and by pleasure we counted ourselves unworthy for the supplication of God, and
we called forth the most honored and holy fathers who sit in mountains and
caves and the holes of the earth toward petitions, and we urged them toward
supplications, and we ordered them to pray unceasingly and watchfully on your
behalf. But we also commanded that this same thing be done in the churches of
the God-guarded city and in the holy monasteries, so that the petition of all
these, going up into the ears of the Lord of Sabaoth and being mixed and
united with your eagerness, may also fall toward us, and the present way may
become easy for you and without stumbling.

Since therefore we have confidence both from the providence and help of God
the lover of mankind, and from the holy prayer of the sacred and holy fathers,
and from your praiseworthy courage and boldness, receive also our exhortation
as from an affectionate father, whose inward parts burn for you and who is
held every day by countless cares on your behalf.

4 Faithful and beloved little children, a holy army and one gathered by God:
now, if ever, the time has come for your courage to be shown, for your
boldness to be recognized, for the praiseworthy daring to become plain to all.
For even if in the times that have gone by you often showed yourselves brave
against the enemies and won glory against them, yet some things were done by
chance and by unstable and uneven fortune, and some by a practice and a craft
colored with the name of courage but known in the thing itself as cowardice,
and neither were the things of the courageous purely discerned, nor were the
things of the cowardly distinguished, but as in confusion and in a moonless
night or a night-battle your affairs were darkened and covered. But since the
present selection made through our most faithful servants and genuine
attendants has shown the courage and nobility of each of you, and has brought
into the light those who of old were covered (I do not know whether to say
enviously or truthfully) and unknown, and the courage of each of you, the
boldness, the endurance, has been recognized, and you were preferred for
selection like clean wheat, while the others, like tares grown up with the
wheat, were thrown out and driven off: show your irresistible charge and
endurance against the enemies. Go against them, and go not doubting, not
shrinking back and going to the rear, but stretching forward to the things in
front. You have those who will look upon your courage, the representatives of
our Majesty, who fill our place. You have these men also, who will wake your
eagerness both by words and by deeds. Show them the most noble and firm stand
that is in you by nature; let them see your strong arms fighting against
enemies, and let them wonder, and in this let them glorify God.

For I, being wholly given over to you and as it were walking in your very
hearts and making my dwelling there, have been so moved and mixed by love and
longing toward you that I have even made ready and prepared, God consenting
and nodding, to campaign together with you, and to be confirmed by the sight
itself of those things which I was long since sounding forth and hearing
through words.

5 If therefore there is in you also some longing to see us and our son as your
fellow-horsemen and fellow-armor-bearers and fellow-soldiers, now confirm such
a longing through the deeds themselves, increase through the facts the love
toward us that is in the heart, so that, being sinewed and made confident by
your feats and victories and mastery over the enemies and by the unconquered
trophies, I may become more eager to embrace the campaign with you.

For formerly a certain report ran about the most impious Chambdan and the
Christ-hating Tarsiots, that they are courageous and possess a people
unconquerable in wars, from which, being terrified and made slack, you shrank
from the clash with them. But now this is not so. For as you yourselves also
know, that so-and-so, sent with the rest of the generals some time ago against
the lands and fortresses of God-hated Tarsus, and having entered into the
inmost parts of them, and having armed the people with him with all eagerness
and with hortatory words, what kind of expedition he accomplished, and how
many kaites he overcame and a very great people of the Tarsiots, has escaped
none of you. But also the people sent a little while ago into Mesopotamia,
with the Patrician so-and-so and the rest, having thrown down the noble and
(as he thought) unconquerable array of Chambdan's army and having overcome it
without toil, will wholly persuade your souls to become bolder, and more
confident toward the clash against the enemies.

6 For a great and large report of your courage held the hearing of the
nations, that you have an irresistible charge, that you possess incomparable
courage, that you show an unbowed spirit toward wars. And since now certain
portions of such nations have campaigned together with you, they were struck
at the very sight by the courage and nobility of the others who won glory in
the former expeditions; now let them also be struck by your boldness, let them
wonder at the unconquered and unconquerable strength against barbarians.
Become for me both a wonder and a shock to the nations, and a strength and a
fortifying to those of the same race; sinew the souls, strengthen the arms,
whet the teeth like wild boars, and let no one try to give his back to the
enemies, since whoever puts this in mind will first renounce his own life. Let
your feats be spoken even in foreign lands; let the nations who are with you
also be struck by your successes; let them become heralds to their tribesmen
of the trophies and of the victory-making signs, as they see you having
accomplished them.

7 These things we say both to the Christ-loving and God-gathered armies of the
East and to those from Macedonia and Thrace who have campaigned with you, and
we make them known and declare them; for these too have become fellow-soldiers
and fellow-travelers of yours, and they have often appeared winning glory in
wars. For how great trophies they set up when sent into Langobardia against
the enemies, and how they overcame and subjected those who opposed our
Majesty, receive us as witnesses.

8 With them therefore, being slaves and soldiers of one kingdom and one
lordship, make the present campaign eagerly, being disposed toward them as
brothers and taking thought as fathers for their safety; for they have been
sent as your fellow-workers, and they have become partners of your dangers and
of your feats. To you all, as to my inward parts and members, exhorting the
same things and speaking through the present writing, I have trusted in Christ
the true God, the only immortal King, and I rest upon such hopes, that you
will not put my expectation of you to shame, you will not quench the hopes,
you will not blunt the goodwill, you will not adulterate the service, but as
genuine and most faithful servants and subjects of our Majesty and as firm and
unconquerable champions of the Roman race you have now shown such and so great
a courage and every other boldness and nobility, that appearing as victors and
trophy-bearers against the enemies and returning with victory and with glad
shouts we shall both embrace you and receive you, and we shall kiss your
bodies wounded for Christ as martyr members, and we shall boast in the stain
of the blood and be glorified in you and in your noble successes and contests.

And that you may know how much I am set on fire in soul concerning you and am
wholly burning and consumed on every side, devising for you the things that
tend toward safety and that make your way prosper, behold, from the undefiled
and all-holy signs of the passion of Christ our true God, both from the
precious woods and from the undefiled spear, and from the precious title, and
from the wonder-working reed, and from the life-giving blood that flowed from
his precious side, and from the all-holy tunic, and from the sacred
swaddling-clothes, and from the God-bearing shroud and from the rest of the
signs of his undefiled passion, having taken myron we have sent out to you
holy water, that it may be sprinkled upon you and that through it you may be
anointed all around and put on divine power from on high. For I believe in my
true God and Savior Christ, that just as through the blood and water that
flowed from his undefiled side he both refashioned and gave life to the race
of men, so also through the sprinkling of this holy water he will both give
you life and refashion you, and will furnish daring and strength and power
against the enemies.

And may Christ, the maker of the ages and the sustainer of all creation, our
true God, who is worshipped and glorified together with his beginningless
Father and with the co-natural and life-giving Spirit, who strengthens the
weak and empowers the lowly, who also covered the Pharaonic army in the depth
of the sea and saved a lowly people, the only High One and Ruler, who sits
upon the Cherubim and looks upon lowly things, who girds a sword on those
mighty in war and furnishes from on high help to those who call upon him, who
arrays himself against the proud, but brings sinners low to the earth, who
teaches hands for war and sets the arms of those who hope in him as a bow of
bronze, who gives the shield of salvation to pursue the blasphemous enemies
until they fail, who girds power for war, who binds the feet of all who rise
up against those who fight for him and pounds them fine as dust before the
face of the wind: may he himself, by his boundless and unspeakable goodness
and by his measureless and incomprehensible mercies, look upon you in mercy
and goodwill, look upon you from above with a gracious eye and prepare your
way before you; may he himself send out his angel and make straight your way;
may he himself encircle you with angelic camps and take you up and keep you
unconsumed from the harm of the enemies, so that returning to us in his power
and might with victory and trophies, you may have the praise from men,
ever-memorable and indelible, remaining and spoken to generations of
generations, and you may have our Majesty also rejoicing and taking delight in
your successes and in your feats, made beautiful by the intercessions of his
all-undefiled Mother the Theotokos and of all the angelic bodiless powers and
of the saints who from the age have served Him and been perfected for Him;
amen.
