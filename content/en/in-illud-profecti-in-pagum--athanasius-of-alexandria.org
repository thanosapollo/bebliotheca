* On that saying, Having gone into the village
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of Athanasius, archbishop of Alexandria, on the saying: "Go into the village
opposite and you will find a colt tied." 1.1 "Those who have set their mind on
earthly things" and have despised the commandment of God and have toiled in
this life, these before the finding of the good things spent their time in
ignorance. 1.2 For I think that the most wise Solomon also says on this
account, that "there is a time to seek and a time to lose." 1.3 That is: you
have sinned; be still. For even if once, spending time in ignorance, you
procured destruction for your own soul, yet a time has come for us to seek
this. 1.4 As also our Lord and Savior Jesus 1.4 Christ teaches in the gospels
saying: "Seek and you shall find." And when shall we find this if not now,
when the Lord being present has loosed us from the bonds of the devil? 1.5 As
also the blessed apostle teaches saying: "Behold now is an acceptable time,
behold now is a day of salvation." 1.6 A time then has followed for the sin
that darkens to be lost, and a time has called to seek the one that had gone
astray, that we might be loosed from the bonds. 1.7 For he has come "who
looses those who are bound and raises up those who are cast down." He has come
"who proclaims release to the captives and recovery of sight to the blind."
1.8 For "then shall the lame leap as a hart and the tongue of stammerers shall
be distinct." 2.1 And let us also apply ourselves otherwise to the saying. For
the little word of Solomon moves us to longing. For he says: "a time to seek
and a time to lose." 2.2 Since for a long time the human race was going astray
and being destroyed by the devil's wiles, a time has come for this to be
sought and recalled from captivity and set free from the bonds. 2.3 For there
was need for us to seek and to learn, that "the letter kills, but the spirit
gives life." 2.4 This then having been accomplished, a time has come for the
type and the shadow in the letter to be lost. 2.5 For "they were imposed until
a time of reformation." But when the more perfect has appeared, "that which is
in part has been done away." 2.6 For "when I was a child, I spoke as a child;
but when I became a man, I put away the things of the child." 2.7 Indeed the
Savior says: "He who finds his soul shall lose it, and he who loses his soul
for my sake shall find it." 2.8 That is, he who has sought his soul and found
it being in sins, let him make haste to lose it, not allowing it to linger in
wickedness, that he may receive a time, and having sought again he will find
it in virtue. 2.9 For if anyone wishes to find his own soul outside of
punishment, he will lose it for the present, that he may gain Christ; =2.10=
And the Savior teaches the losing here, not that we should procure destruction
for our own soul, but that in a time of martyrdom we should not spare money,
nor possessions, nor children, nor parents, nor body, nor even the soul
itself; but let us hand ourselves over even unto death, that, as I said
before, we may find our own soul in the kingdom of the heavens. =2.11= For
thus also the Savior teaching said: "He who loves father or mother or children
more than me is not worthy of me" and "he who loses his soul for my sake shall
find it." 3.1 If then anyone wishes to find his soul, he will lose it in the
present time. It is necessary therefore that wickedness be lost in us, that
having sought virtue we may find it. 3.2 For if one does not cease from anger,
how shall he practice meekness? If one does not lose fornication from himself,
how does he profess temperance? 3.3 For first it is fitting that all love of
money and idolatry be lost in us, and then thus to seek freedom from
possessions and piety. 3.4 For he says, "I sought him whom my soul loved, I
sought him and I found him and I brought him into my mother's house and into
the chamber of her who conceived me." 3.5 And this is Christ who is signified,
"Lord of hosts," who for our sins came down from heaven and was conceived and
born and laid in a manger, until he should save the human race. 3.6 For thus,
sparing us, he sends even now his own disciples into the village opposite,
saying to them: "Go into the village opposite and you will find a colt tied;
loosing it bring it to me." 3.7 And the holy apostles go and loose the colt
according to the things commanded them by the Lord. 3.8 And it belongs to
great men to loose this colt, beloved. And I say great, not in bodily age, but
great in faith, in love, in righteousness, great in prudence, in courage, in
virtue. 3.9 Of such a kind Moses also is witnessed to have been, as when the
apostle says concerning him: "By faith Moses, when he became great, refused to
be called the son of Pharaoh's daughter." But Isaac also, advancing, became
great. =3.10= For it belongs to those who are thus great to loose this colt.
And would that I too were of such stature, that I might be able to loose the
bonds of those present. =3.11= For each of us is bound with the bonds of sin,
as also the scripture bears witness saying, that "each is bound with the cords
of his own sins." =3.12= Let us then pray, that Jesus may send his own
disciples also to us, that they may loose us also from the bonds with which
each of us is bound. =3.13= For one of us is bound by love of money, and
another is entangled with fornication; and another is bound together with
drunkenness, and another is held by vainglory; and one does injustice, and
another seizes the things of his neighbor and of the poor man, and another
takes interest upon interest. =3.14= And in the end we have all grown weak in
our own injustices and we need the healing from the Savior; and we need him,
that he may send his own disciples also to us and they may loose us from the
bonds of the devil. =3.15= For thus he says to the disciples: "Go into the
village opposite and you will find a colt tied; loosing it bring it to me."
=3.16= And when you hear "the village opposite," understand the place upon the
earth; for the city is heavenly. =3.17= As also the blessed Paul writes
saying: "For we have not here an abiding city, but we seek the one to come,"
"whose craftsman and maker is God." =3.18= And again the same says: "For you
have not come to a mountain that can be touched and to burning fire, but you
have come to Mount Zion and to the city of the living God, the heavenly
Jerusalem, and to myriads of angels in festal assembly." 4.1 And such were all
the saints and holy ones living according to Paul; they reach after this. For
hear him saying: "For if they had remembered that from which they were cast
out, they would have had a time to return; but now they reach after a better,
that is, a heavenly." 4.2 And such were the holy apostles and disciples of the
Lord, who with Peter say: "Behold we have left all and have followed you." 4.3
They are sent then to loose the colt spoken of before. For the presence and
love of mankind of our Savior has called us back from the village opposite
into the heavenly city, the Jerusalem above. 4.4 For I think that because of
the disobedience of Adam that came to be we were cast out of paradise and were
settled in the village opposite. 4.5 For he says, "God cast out Adam and
settled him over against the paradise of delight." And here then the disciples
are sent by Jesus into the village opposite to loose the colt spoken of
before. 4.6 And see whether perhaps because of the one cast out over against
paradise the disciples are sent into the village opposite by Jesus to loose
this colt. 4.7 For on this account the Savior sojourned among us, leaving the
ninety-nine sheep that had not gone astray, that going he might seek the one
that had gone astray and finding it he rejoices over it. And the angels also
rejoice together "over one sinner who repents." 4.8 And it must be asked
whether the disciples were sent into the village opposite only for the sake of
the ministry of the colt. For I know that before the apostles invisible powers
were ministering to Jesus. 4.9 Perhaps then the Lord also sent those very
powers, being discipled to him, together with those about Peter into the
village opposite to loose the colt. =4.10= For hear the evangelist saying
concerning the Lord: "And behold," he says, "angels came and ministered to
him." And concerning humanity David cries saying: "Man ate the bread of
angels, he gave them bread of heaven." =4.11= Let no one then who spends his
time in the body and is drawn about into this life think that he is inside a
city. For he who "minds the things of the flesh" cannot be found in this great
city, but such a one still spends his time in a village. =4.12= For "the mind
of the flesh is enmity against God." One must know then, that he who dwells in
a village does not live as a citizen with his soul, does not command as a
general with his judgment, does not rule himself; for these things are truly
proper to a great city. =4.13= For when someone lives as a citizen in virtue
and commands temperance as a general and takes counsel of wisdom and rules
over self-control, then he will become an imitator of the saints. =4.14= For
the saints "have not here an abiding city, but" as I said before "they seek
the one to come," "whose craftsman and maker is God." =4.15= For hear the
apostle saying: "walking" upon the earth we have our citizenship in the
heavens. And let "your citizenship be in the heavens" also, beloved. =4.16=
For holy Paul signifies by this "walking" upon earth the progress that has
come to be for those who after the things here are going to live as citizens
in heaven. 5.1 The disciples then having gone loosed the colt. For it is the
work of disciples to loose this colt, and of disciples able to do the things
said by the teacher. 5.2 For many seeming to be disciples of Jesus, not only
do they not do the things said by him, but they even plot against the teacher,
as the thrice-wretched traitor Judas. 5.3 And of what size is this colt,
beloved, that it has not one lord but many? For the lords of the colt said to
the disciples: "Why do you loose the colt?" 5.4 Was it then, if this colt were
perceptible, not enough for one lord to sit beside it? But now it has many
lords according to the gospel. For he says, "His lords said to the disciples:
Why do you loose the colt?" 5.5 For perhaps they will say to them: Do you not
see how many of us sit beside it? How it is bound? How we have held it given
over? Why do you tear away from us our work? Why do you loose our expectation?
5.6 This one is still left to us, and do you wish to set us away from this
one? If this one is loosed from the bonds, we are bound in its place; for when
this one is set free we are condemned. 5.7 The guilty demons therefore were
afraid, seeing the colt being loosed. The opposing powers were troubled in
themselves, who had taken us captive. 5.8 But when our Lord and Savior Jesus
Christ sojourned among us and they recognized his presence, they were afraid
having heard: "Go into the outer darkness prepared for the devil and his
angels." 5.9 They were afraid having heard. "Behold I have given you authority
to tread upon serpents and scorpions and upon all the power of the enemy."
=5.10= They were afraid having heard: "Go, make disciples of all the nations,
baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy
Spirit." =5.11= They were afraid lest this be the one who also shines upon the
darkness. For they heard the prophet saying: "Galilee of the nations, the
people sitting in darkness, see a great light." =5.12= All then gape about the
colt, all sit about it; no one stands away from it. So great is this colt,
that all its lords keep it. =5.13= Was it then, if this were perceptible, that
its lords sat beside it, and a perceptible little ass becomes the possession
of many lords, and all who have acquired it sit beside it? Does none of them
trade? Does none of them walk in the marketplace? Have they no other work, but
all of them together are occupied about it? =5.14= Or is a perceptible little
ass bound in a street; it has no farmstead; it has no manger; it does not go
away to the marketplace; but a perceptible colt is bound in a street and all
its lords sit beside it keeping it? =5.15= Saying these things I do not take
away the history; for we do not take away the letter because of the spirit,
but we save the power of the spirit through the letter. 6.1 Truly the Lord sat
upon a perceptible little ass entering from Bethany into Jerusalem. For thus
also Zechariah prophesies saying: "Say to the daughter of Zion: Behold your
king comes to you meek and mounted upon an ass and a colt the foal of a beast
of burden." 6.2 The history then being true, I seek the things recorded in it;
we seek the treasure laid up for us, we seek the power of the spirit. For we
do not attend to a bare history; for such madness belongs to the Jews. 6.3
Indeed our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, convicting their mindlessness, said:
"Search the scriptures, for in them you think you have eternal life," but the
Jews, attending to the bare letter, were starved to death. 6.4 Knowing then,
beloved, that "the letter kills, but the spirit gives life," we do not follow
only the history of the scriptures, nor as Jews do we attend to the bare
letter, but as it is written we ought "to search the scriptures," that thus we
may be able to grasp the witness from them concerning Jesus. 6.5 For Greeks
also will laugh against us saying, that the God of the Christians, the one
called Christ, sat upon a little ass. 6.6 And we neither doubt nor deny his
presence in the body. For on this account he sojourned among us, that seeking
the lost he might find it and turn back the one going astray. 6.7 For thus
also through the prophet he cries saying: "Turn, you turning sons, and I will
heal your bruises." There are then realities that are signified in the divine
scriptures, beloved. 6.8 Great is this colt, who is also called elsewhere "a
younger son." For the colt is a young ass. And we then are the colt. 6.9 And
if I say "we," it is all humanity. Which man the lords who had taken him
captive before this themselves bound with unbreakable bonds and each of them
bound down bond upon bond. =6.10= And they secured him vehemently and, as I
think, they even bound down his feet, not allowing him to pass over to the
better things. But the lover of mankind, the Son of God, who on this account
sojourned among us, commands his disciples saying to them: =6.11= "Go into the
village opposite and you will find a colt tied; loosing it bring it to me."
7.1 The Lord then has furnished us many good things, beloved. For not only has
he loosed our bonds, those from sin. 7.2 But he has also "given us authority
to tread upon serpents and scorpions, and upon all the power of the enemy."
7.3 For the devil and "the world-rulers of this darkness," having taken us
captive and bound us, did not permit us to advance, and having bound us with
unbreakable bonds they did not allow us to walk in the good way, nor even so
did they stand away from us. 7.4 But even having us bound they sat around us
again, being some cruel and wild masters. But our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ
sojourned among us "to proclaim release to captives and recovery of sight to
the blind." 7.5 Indeed he sends his own disciples and they loose the colt.
Then having loosed it he also builds a manger for it and prepares pasture for
it. 7.6 For the prophet also, wishing to show this, says: "The Lord shepherds
me and nothing shall be lacking to me. In a place of green pasture, there he
encamped me, upon water of rest he nourished me, he turned back my soul." 7.7
And again: "Who makes grass to spring up for the cattle and green herb for the
service of men." 7.8 Having turned then, let us henceforth become more
perceptive and let us recognize our own benefactor and eagerly receive the
good things given us by him. 7.9 For thus giving thanks we are able to say
with David: "You have prepared a table before me over against those who
afflict me." =7.10= For such also is this: "The ox knew his owner and the ass
the manger of his lord." And perhaps on account of this colt Jesus is laid in
a manger. =7.11= Did Joseph then not have a place? Being a well-born man, a
man descended from a royal race, being called a son of David, and he did not
have a place except the lodging, was there no other place? Or clearly the
things being ordered were divine realities. =7.12= Indeed then also again our
adversary the devil, seeing the things accomplished for the race of men,
envied as is his custom. =7.13= For thinking the Lord to be one of all, he
prepares Herod to seek the true king who was born, that he might destroy him.
=7.14= For it is proper to the all-defiled and all-wicked one to destroy and
to kill; but it is proper to Jesus to save and to preserve. =7.15= For he
spares his own creatures, since also "all things came to be through him." Let
us therefore seek after the true king, our Lord Jesus Christ. =7.16= Let us
examine ourselves, whether our bonds have been loosed. And if they are loosed,
let us advance to the better things. But if the bonds have not yet been loosed
for you, give yourself over to the disciples of Jesus. =7.17= For take
courage: those who loose us are present even now. Having received this
authority from the Savior, that "whatever you bind on earth shall be bound in
the heavens, and whatever you loose on earth shall be loosed in the heavens."
=7.18= And again: "If you forgive the sins of any, they are forgiven them."
"Blessed" therefore "are they whose sins have been forgiven and whose
iniquities have been covered." 8.1 The lords of the colt then said to the
disciples: "Why do you loose the colt?" And they answering said, "that the
Lord has need of it." 8.2 You see the wise answer of the disciples. For the
false lords of the colt, having heard that the true and real lord has need of
it, do not answer a second time. 8.3 But they turn their backs, no longer
answering to the things said; they immediately then hasten to their own ruler
the devil to report to him the things that have happened. And you see there
tumult and deliberation and counsel coming to be against the Lord. 8.4 For all
the opposing power is gathered there and there comes to be an assembly of
those who work wickedness, that what was said through the prophet might be
fulfilled, saying: "The kings of the earth stood by and the rulers were
gathered together against the Lord and against his Christ." 8.5 For the guilty
demons say to the devil: What do we do? The colt has been loosed. For it has
gone away to its own lord; it is no longer under your hand, you no longer lord
it over it. 8.6 The wretched devil then was reasoning what he would do to
Jesus. Then the Pharisees and the scribes are gathered into the court of the
one called Caiaphas and they took counsel against Jesus, that they might
destroy him. 8.7 For the miserable ones did not know, that his death grants us
immortality and his descent furnished us the ascent. 8.8 For the Lord rose on
the third day from the dead, having spoiled Hades, having trampled the enemy,
having loosed death, having torn apart the bonds of the sin that held us, and
he redeemed those who were bound saying: "Rise, let us go from here." 8.9
Having been set free then from the slavery of the devil, let us recognize our
own benefactor. Let us glorify the Father with the Son and the Holy Spirit,
let us confess one Godhead. For thus living as citizens we shall inherit the
kingdom of the heavens in Christ Jesus our Lord, to whom be the glory and the
might unto the ages of ages. Amen.
