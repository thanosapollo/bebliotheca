* Address to the Alexandrians concerning Faith
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of Saint Cyril from the address toward Alexandrians concerning faith.
I do not accept the mixed-together; for it is alien to the apostolic faith and
to the right tradition of God; for the mixing-together works a disappearance of
the natures; and the unspeakable union confessed by those who think rightly
saves both natures unconfused, and completes one from both the Christ who was
seen, the same having become both God together and man, and not two christs, but
one only having been united and not mixed; for if the two natures have been
mixed into one mixture, happening to be of other substance, neither of the two
is saved, 539 but both having been confused have disappeared. and they neither
admit a separation from one another or a division having been mixed, and they
were divided at the time of the death and were separated, both a soul having
come down from a body into Hades with the Godhead, and a body from a soul having
been set in a new monument according to the unlying voices of Christ saying
"Loose this temple and in three days I will raise it." and the temple is loosed
in the time of the three-day burial, him wishing it, and again he raised it, and
it was united to him by a certain unspeakable and ineffable word, not mixed in
him, or having been enfleshed away, but saving in himself the property of the
two natures of other substance unconfused; for the natures have not surely been
mixed. 540 Then he himself adds this testimony of Athanasius among the saints
"Two natures of other substance in one Christ the Son of God being saved, and
neither being confused, nor being destroyed or having been divided." For he was
made like us in all things except sin; not of the divine nature having been
turned or mixed-together with the human nature, but having been united to it not
according to a turning, not according to a mixing, not according to a mixture,
not according to a confusion, not according to an alteration, not according to a
change, not according to a disappearance or an enfleshing-away, but according to
an unspeakable and inexpressible, and unnarratable union, of the two natures of
other substance in one Jesus Christ being Son of God being saved and neither
confused nor being destroyed or having been divided. 541 Of Cyril from the
discourse concerning faith. But he prepared for himself an ensouled and perfect
temple in the holy virginal womb; and having put it on and having been united to
it by an unspeakable word, both natures being saved and not mixed, he came forth
appearing a man, and being thought God Jesus Christ.
