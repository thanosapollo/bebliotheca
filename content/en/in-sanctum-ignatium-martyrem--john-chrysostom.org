ENCOMIUM On the holy hieromartyr Ignatius the God-bearer, who became
archbishop of Antioch the Great, was carried to Rome, and there suffered
martyrdom, and was brought back again from there to Antioch.

=50.587=

αʹ. The costly and ambitious of the entertainers make their feasts frequent
and successive, at once displaying their own abundance, and showing their
goodwill toward their friends. So also the grace of the Spirit, furnishing us
the proof of its own power, and displaying much goodwill toward the friends of
God, sets before us successive and continual tables of the martyrs.

Lately, at any rate, a maiden altogether young and unwedded, the blessed
martyr Pelagia, entertained us with much gladness; today again this blessed
and noble martyr Ignatius has succeeded to her feast. Different are the
persons, but one is the table; changed are the wrestlings, but one is the
crown; varied are the contests, but the prize is the same. For in the contests
outside, since the toils are of bodies, men alone are reasonably admitted; but
here, since the whole contest is of the soul, the stadium has been opened to
each nature, the theatre sits for each kind. And neither did men alone strip,
lest the women taking refuge in the weakness of nature should seem to have a
fair apology; nor did women alone play the man, lest the race of men be put to
shame; but from this side and from that many are proclaimed and crowned, that
you may learn through their deeds that in Christ Jesus there is not male, not
female, that neither nature, nor weakness of body, nor age, nor any other of
such things could hinder those who run the course of piety, if a noble
eagerness, and an awakened mind, and a fear of God warm and burning be rooted
in our souls. For this reason both maidens, and women, and men, and young, and
old, and slaves, and free, and every rank, and every age, and each nature
stripped for these contests, and from no side were they harmed at all, since
they brought a noble purpose into these wrestlings. The season then already
calls us to the narration of the achievements of this blessed man; but the
reasoning is disturbed and thrown into tumult, not having what to say first,
what second, what third; so great a multitude of encomia flows around us from
every side. And we suffer the same thing as if someone, having gone into a
meadow, and having seen much rose-garden, and much violet, and so great a
lily, and other spring flowers too, varied and different, should be at a loss
what he should look at first, what second, each of the things seen calling the
eyes to itself.

For we too, having entered this spiritual meadow of Ignatius's achievements,
and beholding not spring flowers, but the very fruit of the Spirit, varied and
different, in this man's soul, are thrown into tumult, and are at a loss, not
having where first to rest our reasoning, each of the things seen drawing us
away from its neighbors, and dragging the eye of the =50.588= soul toward the
contemplation of its own comeliness. For consider: he presided over the Church
among us nobly, and with such accuracy as Christ wishes; for that which that
One said to be the greatest definition and canon of the episcopate,



this man displayed through his deeds. For having heard Christ saying, The good
shepherd lays down his life for the sheep, with all courage he gave it over
for the sheep. He consorted genuinely with the apostles, and enjoyed the
spiritual streams. What sort of man then is it likely that he was who was
reared with those, and consorted with them everywhere, and shared with them
both things spoken and things unspeakable, and seemed to them worthy of so
great an office? A time again came on that sought courage and a soul despising
all things present, and boiling with divine love, and preferring the things
not seen to the things seen; and with such ease he laid aside the flesh, as
one would put off a garment. What then shall we say first? the teaching of the
apostles, which he displayed through all things, or the contempt of the
present life, or the accuracy of virtue with which he administered the
presidency of the Church? Whom shall we hymn first? the martyr, or the bishop,
or the apostle? For the grace of the Spirit, having woven a threefold crown,
thus bound that holy head; or rather a manifold one. For each of the crowns,
if one should unfold them with accuracy, he will find other crowns also
blossoming for us.

βʹ. And if you wish, let us come first to the praise of the episcopate; does
this seem to be one crown alone? Come then, let us unfold it in speech, and
you will see both two, and three, and more born from it for us. For I do not
wonder at the man only because he seemed worthy of so great an office, but
because he was also entrusted with this office by those saints, and the hands
of the blessed apostles touched the sacred head. For this is not a small thing
for encomium; not because he drew more grace from above, nor only because they
made the working of the Spirit come upon him more abundantly; but because they
also bore witness to him of all virtue among men; and how, I tell you. For
Paul writing to Titus once; and when I say Paul, I do not say this man only,
but also Peter, and James, and John, and all their choir.

For just as in one lyre the strings are different, but the harmony is one; so
also in the choir of the apostles the persons are different, but the teaching
is one, since the craftsman also was one, the Holy Spirit who moved their
souls. And Paul showing this said: Whether then they, or I, so we preach. This
man then writing to Titus, and showing of what sort the bishop ought to be,
=50.589= says: For the bishop must be blameless, as God's steward, not
self-willed, not soon angry, not given to wine, not a striker, not greedy of
shameful gain, but hospitable, a lover of good, sober, just, holy, continent,
holding to the faithful word according to the teaching, that he may be able
both to exhort others in the sound doctrine, and to convict those who
contradict. And to Timothy again writing about this subject, he says somehow
thus: If anyone seeks the episcopate, he desires a good work; the bishop
therefore must be without reproach, the husband of one wife, sober, temperate,
orderly, hospitable, apt to teach, not a striker, not given to wine, but
forbearing, not contentious, no lover of money. Did you see how much accuracy
of virtue he demands of the bishop? For just as some most excellent painter,
having mixed varied colors, if he is about to prepare an original image of a
royal form, works with all accuracy, so that all who imitate it and paint from
it may have an accurately drawn image; so indeed also the blessed Paul, as
though painting some royal image, and preparing its archetype, having mixed
the varied colors of virtue, sketched for us the finished features of the
episcopate, that each of those mounting to this office, looking to that, with



such accuracy may administer all his own affairs. Boldly then I would say that
the blessed Ignatius copied all of it with accuracy in his own soul, and was
without reproach and blameless, and neither self-willed, nor soon angry, nor
given to wine, nor a striker, but not contentious, no lover of money, just,
holy, continent, holding to the faithful word according to the teaching,
sober, temperate, orderly, and the rest of the things which Paul demanded. And
what is the proof of these things? says one. Those who said these things
themselves ordained him, and those who with such accuracy advise others to
make the testing of those about to mount the throne of this office would not
themselves have done this as a side-work; but if they had not seen all this
virtue planted in the soul of this martyr, they would not have entrusted this
office to him. For they knew accurately how great a danger lies for those who
make such ordinations simply and as it happens.

And Paul showing this very thing again, writing to the same Timothy, said: Lay
hands quickly on no one, nor share in others' sins. What do you say? another
has sinned, and I share the charges and the punishment? Yes, he says, the one
who furnished the authority to the wickedness. And just as one who has put a
sharpened sword into the hands of a madman and one beside himself, whatever
murder the madman works, that man undergoes the blame who gave the sword, so
also one who has furnished the authority from this office to a man living with
wickedness, draws all the fire of that man's sins and daring deeds upon his
own head; for the one who furnished the root is everywhere the cause of the
things that grow from it. Did you see how a double crown of the episcopate has
appeared to us so far, and the dignity of those who ordained him made the
office more brilliant, having borne witness to him of every proof of virtue?

γʹ. Do you wish that I also uncover for you another crown blossoming from this
very thing? Let us consider the time =50.590= at which he was entrusted with
this office. For it is not equal to administer a Church now and then, just as
it is not equal to travel a worn road, well constructed, after many travelers,
and one that is now first about to be cut, and has ravines and stones, and is
full of wild beasts, and has not yet received any traveler. For now by the
grace of God there is no danger to the bishops, but peace from every side is
deep, and we all enjoy a calm, the word of piety having been stretched to the
ends of the inhabited world, and those who reign over us keeping the faith
with accuracy. But then none of these things was, but wherever one looked,
precipices, and pits, and wars, and battles, and dangers, and rulers, and
kings, and peoples, and cities, and nations, and household, and strangers were
plotting against the believers. And this was not the only terrible thing, but
that also many of those who had believed themselves, as having now first
tasted strange doctrines, needed much condescension, and were still more
weakly disposed, and were often tripped up; and this was what grieved the
teachers no less than the wars from outside, or rather even much more; for the
battles from outside, and the plots, even furnished them much pleasure through
the hope of the rewards stored up.

For this reason also the apostles returned from the face of the council
rejoicing that they had been scourged, and Paul cries saying: I rejoice in my
sufferings, and he boasts in the tribulations everywhere; but the wounds of
their own and the falls of the brothers did not even let them breathe, but
like some heaviest pillory it pressed the neck of their soul and dragged it
down continually. Hear at any rate how Paul, who so rejoices in sufferings, is
bitterly pained over these things. For who, he says, is weak, and I am not
weak? who is made to stumble, and I do not burn? And



again: I fear lest somehow coming I find you not such as I wish, and I be
found by you such as you do not wish; and after a little: Lest when I come to
you again God humble me, and I mourn many of those who have sinned beforehand,
and have not repented of the uncleanness, and licentiousness, and fornication
which they practiced. And throughout you see him being in tears and
lamentations on account of his own, and fearing, and trembling always for
those who have believed. Just as then we marvel at a helmsman not when, the
sea being still and the ship being borne by fair winds, he is able to save
those sailing, but when, the deep raging, the waves rising, the passengers
within themselves being in faction, a great storm from within and from without
besieging those sailing, he is able to steer the vessel with all safety; so
also those then entrusted with the Church must be struck with awe and
marvelled at much more than those who administer it now, when the war was
great from without and from within, when the plant of the faith was still more
tender, and needing much care, when like a newborn infant the multitude of the
Church needed much providence, and some most wise soul that was going to nurse
it. And that you may learn more clearly, of how many crowns those then
entrusted with the =50.591= Church were worthy, and how great the toil and
danger it is in the preludes and beginning to take up a matter, and to come
first upon it, I bring forward a testimony of Christ voting these things for
you and confirming the judgment spoken by us. For seeing many coming to him,
and wishing to show the apostles that the prophets toiled more than they, he
says: Others have toiled, and you have entered into their toil. And yet the
apostles toiled much more than the prophets; but since those first laid down
the word of piety, and drew the souls of men, being untaught, toward the
truth, he votes the greater part of the toil to those. For it is not equal, it
is not, to come after many other teachers and to teach, and oneself first to
cast down the seeds. For what has already been practiced, and has come into
the custom of many, would easily become acceptable; but what is now first
heard throws the mind of the hearers into tumult, and furnishes many troubles
to those who teach. This at any rate also at Athens threw those who heard into
tumult, and for this reason they turned away from Paul, accusing him, that You
bring certain strange things to our ears. For if now the presidency of a
Church furnishes much labor and toil to those who steer it, consider how
double, and triple, and many times greater the toil was then, when there were
dangers, and battles, and plots, and continual fear. It is not possible, it is
not, to set forth in speech the difficulty which those holy men then
underwent; but this man alone will know it who has come through the experience
itself.

δʹ. Let me say also a fourth crown rising for us from this episcopate. What
then is this? That he was entrusted with our own fatherland. For it is
laborious even to preside over a hundred men and fifty only; but to be
entrusted with so great a city, and a people stretching to twenty myriads, of
how much virtue and wisdom do you think it is a proof? For just as in armies
the more royal phalanxes and those more abounding are entrusted to the wiser
of the generals; so indeed also in the case of cities, the greater and more
populous are entrusted to the more proven of the rulers. And otherwise this
city was of much account to God, as he also made clear through the deeds
themselves; at any rate he commanded Peter, the overseer of the whole
inhabited world, to whom he entrusted the keys of the heavens, to whom he
permitted to lead and to carry all things, to spend much time here; so much
was our city a counterweight to the whole inhabited world. And since I
mentioned Peter, I also saw a fifth crown being woven from him; and this is
that after that man this man succeeded to the



office. For just as if someone lifting a great stone from foundations is eager
in every way to bring in another counterweight to that one, if he is not going
to shake the whole building and make it more unsound; so indeed also when
Peter was about to sojourn away from here, the grace of the Spirit brought in
another teacher as a counterweight to Peter, so that the building already made
might not become more unsound by the cheapness of the one who would succeed.
We have then numbered five crowns, from the greatness of the office, from the
dignity of those who ordained, from the difficulty of the time, from the
measure of the city, from the virtue of the one who handed over the episcopate
to him. It was possible, having woven all these, to say also a sixth =50.592=
and a seventh, and more than these; but lest, having spent all the time on the
discourse about the episcopate, we fall out of the narrations about the
martyr, come now let us go to that contest. A harsh war was once fanned
against the Churches, and as when a most harsh tyranny has seized the earth,
all were snatched from the midst of the marketplace, being accused of nothing
unseemly, but that having been delivered from error they ran toward piety,
that they stood away from the worship of the demons, that they recognized the
true God, and worshipped his only-begotten Son, and for the things for which
they ought to have been crowned, and marvelled at and honored, for these they
were punished and were flooded with countless terrible things, all who
received the faith, but much more those who presided over the Churches.

For the devil, being a worker of evil and terrible at stitching such plots,
expected that, if he should take away the shepherds, he would be able easily
to tear the flocks apart. But he who catches the wise in their craftiness,
wishing to show him that it is not men who steer his Churches, but that he
himself is the one who everywhere shepherds those who believe in him,
permitted this to happen, that when those have been taken away he may see the
things of piety not diminished, nor the word of the preaching quenched, but
rather increased, he may learn through the deeds themselves, both he and all
who serve him in these things, that the things concerning us are not human,
but our subject of teaching has its root from above from the heavens, and God
is the one who leads the Churches everywhere, and that it is not possible ever
to prevail over God when he wages war. And the devil did not work this evil
only, but also another no less than this. For he did not allow the bishops to
be slaughtered in the cities of which they were presidents, but leading them
into a foreign land he killed them; and he did this at once hastening to take
them deserted of their friends, and hoping to make them weaker by the toil of
the journey; which indeed he also did in the case of this blessed man.

For from our city he called him to Rome, setting longer double-courses of the
race for him, and expecting by the length of the road and the multitude of the
days to cast down his mind, not knowing that having Jesus as fellow-traveler
and fellow-sojourner of so great a journey, he became rather stronger, and
furnished more the proof of the power that was with him, and he knit the
Churches together more greatly. For the cities along the road, running
together from every side, anointed the athlete, and sent him forth with many
provisions, contending together with him by prayers and intercessions. And
they themselves also received no ordinary exhortation, seeing the martyr
running toward death with such eagerness, as it was likely for one being
called to the palaces in heaven. And through the deeds themselves they
learned, through that noble man's eagerness and brightness, that
it was not death toward which he ran, but a sojourning and a translation, and
an ascent toward heaven. And teaching these things he went through every city
by words, by the deeds themselves. And what happened in the case of the Jews,
when they bound Paul



and sent him to Rome they thought they were sending him to death, but they
were sending a teacher to the Jews dwelling there, =50.593= this indeed also
happened in the case of Ignatius with a certain abundance.

For he went away as a wondrous teacher not only to those who dwell in Rome,
but also to all the cities lying in the midst, persuading them to despise the
present life, and to consider the things seen as nothing, and to love the
things to come, and to look toward heaven, and to turn toward none of the
terrible things in the present life. For teaching them these things and more
than these through the deeds he journeyed, like some sun rising from the east
and running toward the west; or rather even brighter than this; for this one
ran from above, bringing perceptible light, but Ignatius shone back from
below, sending intelligible light of teaching into the souls; and that one
going away into the parts of the west is hidden, and immediately brings on
night; but this man having gone away into the parts of the west rose more
brightly from there, and having benefited all along the road with the greatest
things; and when he set foot in the city, he taught that one also to
philosophize. For this reason also God permitted him to end his life there, so
that this man's end might become a school of piety for all who dwell in Rome.
For you by the grace of God no longer needed any proof, being rooted according
to the faith; but those who dwell in Rome, since there was then much impiety
there, needed more help. For this reason both Peter and Paul and after those
this man were all sacrificed there; this that they might cleanse the city,
polluted by the blood of the idols, with their own blood, and this that they
might furnish through the deeds the proof of the resurrection of the crucified
Christ, having persuaded those who dwell in Rome that they would not with such
pleasure have despised the present life, if they had not strongly persuaded
themselves that they were about to ascend to the crucified Jesus, and to see
him in the heavens. For truly the greatest proof of resurrection is that the
slain Christ displayed so great a power after death, as to persuade living men
to overlook fatherland, and house, and friends, and kin, and life itself for
the confession of him, and to choose scourges, and dangers, and death instead
of the present pleasant things.

For these were the achievements not of some dead man, nor of one who remained
in the tomb, but of one risen and living. Since how would it have reason, that
when he lived, all the apostles who were with him, having become weaker from
fear, betrayed the teacher, and went away fleeing, but when he died, not only
Peter and Paul, but also Ignatius who had not even seen him, nor enjoyed his
company, displayed such eagerness for him, as even to give over his life
itself for him?

εʹ. In order then that all who dwell in Rome might learn these things in deed,
God permitted the saint to be perfected there. And that this is the cause, I
will confirm from the very manner of the end. For not outside the walls
=50.594= in a pit, nor in a law-court, nor in some corner did he receive the
condemning vote, but in the midst of the theatre, the whole city sitting
above, he underwent the manner of the martyrdom, beasts being let loose upon
him, that having set up the trophy against the devil under the eyes of all, he
might make all the spectators zealots of his own contests, not only dying so
nobly, but also dying with pleasure. For not as about to be torn from life,
but as being called to a better and more spiritual life, so gladly he looked
at the beasts. Whence is this clear? From the words which he uttered when
about to die.



For having heard that this manner of the punishment awaits him, I would that I
might enjoy those beasts, he said. For such are those who love; whatever they
suffer for those they love, they receive with pleasure, and then they seem to
be filled with the desire, when the things that happen are much more harsh;
which indeed also happened in this man's case. For he was eager to emulate the
apostles not by death only, but also by eagerness; and hearing that those men,
having been scourged, withdrew with joy, he wished himself also to imitate the
teachers not by the end only, but also by the joy; for this reason, Of the
beasts, he said, may I have enjoyment. And he thought the mouths of these much
tamer than the tyrant's tongue, and very reasonably; for that called toward
Gehenna, but the mouths of these sent him on toward a kingdom. When then he
ended his life there, or rather when he went up toward heaven, he returned
thereafter a crowned one. For this too happened by the economy of God, that he
should be brought back again to us, and that the martyr should be distributed
to the cities. For that city received him dripping with blood, but you have
been honored with the relic; you enjoyed the episcopate, those enjoyed the
martyrdom; they saw him contending and conquering and being crowned, you have
him continually; God removed him from you for a little time, and with more
glory he granted him to you. And just as those who borrow money pay back with
interest what they receive; so also God, having borrowed this precious
treasure from you for a little time, and having shown him to that city, has
given him back to you with more brilliance. For you sent forth a bishop, and
you received a martyr; you sent him forth with prayers, and you received him
with crowns; and not you only, but also all the cities in the midst. For how
do you think they were disposed seeing the relic being brought back? how much
pleasure they harvested? how much they exulted? with how many praises they
struck the crowned one from every side? For just as when a noble athlete has
thrown all his antagonists, and has come out from the pit with brilliant
glory, the spectators immediately receiving him do not even let him set foot
on the earth, carrying him home in their arms, and striking him with countless
encomia; so indeed also then the cities in succession, receiving that holy man
from Rome, and carrying him on their shoulders, escorted him as far as this
city, praising the crowned one, hymning the president of the games, laughing
at the devil, because his sophism was turned round into the opposite for him,
and what he thought to do against the martyr, this happened on his behalf. And
then he benefited and set upright all those cities; and from that time even
until the present he enriches your =50.595= city; and just as a continual
treasure, being drawn upon each day, and not failing, makes all who partake
more well-supplied; so indeed also this blessed Ignatius, filling those who
come to him with blessings, with boldness, with a noble mind, and with much
courage, sends them home. Let us then not today only, but also each day go to
him, plucking spiritual fruits from him. For it is possible, it is possible
for the one who comes here with faith to harvest great good things; for not
only the bodies, but even the very coffins of the saints are filled with
spiritual grace. For if this happened in the case of Elisha, and a dead man
touching a coffin broke through the bonds of death, and returned to life
again, much more now, when the grace is more abundant, when the working of the
Spirit is more, it is possible even for one who has touched a coffin with
faith to draw much power from there. For this reason God also left us the
relics of the saints, wishing to lead us by the hand toward the same zeal as
those, and to furnish some harbor and a sure consolation of the evils that
always overtake us. Wherefore I exhort all of you, whether someone is in
despondency, or in diseases, or in despite, or in some other circumstance of
life, or =50.596= in a depth of sins, with



faith let him come here, and he will lay aside all those things, and will
return with much pleasure, having made the conscience lighter from the sight
alone; or rather not only those who are in terrible things must come here, but
even if someone be in cheerfulness, even if in glory, even if in power, even
if in much boldness toward God, let not this man either despise the benefit.
For having come here and having seen this saint, he will have the good things
unmoved, having persuaded his own soul to be moderate by the memory of this
man's achievements, and not letting the conscience be lifted up to some
swelling from the achievements.

And it is no small thing for those in prosperity not to be puffed up at their
good fortunes, but to know how to bear the prosperities with moderation. So
that the treasure is useful to all, the lodging is suitable, for those who
have stumbled, that they may be delivered from the temptations, for those who
prosper, that the good things may remain firm for them; for those in weakness,
that they may return to health, for those who are healthy, that they may not
fall down into weakness. Reckoning all which things, let us prefer the sojourn
here to every delight, every pleasure, that at once both rejoicing and
gaining, we may be able to become tent-mates with these saints and of one
table, by the prayers of the saints themselves, by the grace and
loving-kindness of our Lord Jesus Christ, with whom is the glory to the
Father, together with the Holy Spirit, now and always, and unto the ages of
ages. Amen.
