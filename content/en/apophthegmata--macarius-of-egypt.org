Concerning abba Macarius the Egyptian. Abba Macarius related concerning
himself, saying: When I was younger and was sitting in a cell in Egypt, they
seized me and made me a cleric in the village; and not wishing to accept it, I
fled to another place. And a devout man of the world came to me, and he would
take my handiwork, and he ministered to me. And it happened from a temptation
that a certain virgin in the village fell; and having conceived she was asked
who it was that had done this. And she said: The anchorite. And coming out
they seized me into the village, and they hung on my neck sooted pots and ears
of empty vessels, and they led me in pro =34.237= cession through the village
street by street, beating me and saying: This monk has corrupted our virgin;
take him, take him. And they beat me nearly to death. And one of the old men
coming said: How long are you beating the stranger monk? And the one who
ministered to me followed behind me being ashamed; for they were insulting him
much and saying: See the anchorite, whom you used to bear witness to, what he
has done. And her parents say: We will not release him, until he give a surety
of feeding her. And I said to my minister; and he stood surety for me. And
going away to my cell, I gave him all the baskets I had, saying: Sell them and
give my wife to eat. And I was saying to my thought: Macarius, see, you have
found yourself a wife; you must work a little extra, that you may feed her.
And I was working night and day, and sending to her. And when the time came
for the wretched woman to give birth, she remained many days being tormented,
and she did not bear. And they say to her: What is this? And she said: I know:
that I slandered the anchorite, and lying I accused him; and this man has
nothing in the matter, but so-and-so the young man. And the one who ministered
to me coming rejoicing said, that that virgin was not able to give birth,
until she confessed saying, that the anchorite has nothing in the matter, but
I lied against him; and see, the whole village wants to come here with glory,
and make repentance to you. And I hearing these things, that the people might
not afflict me, rose and fled here to Scetis. This is the beginning of the
cause, for which I came here. Macarius the Egyptian once came from Scetis to
the mountain of Nitria, to the offering of abba Pambo; and the old men say to
him: Speak a word to the brothers, father. And he said: I have not yet become
a monk, but I have seen monks. For once when I was sitting in the cell in
Scetis, the thoughts troubled me saying: Go away into the desert, and see what
you see there. And I remained fighting the thought five years, saying, lest it
be from demons. And as the thought persisted, I went away into the desert; and
I found there a lake of waters, and an island in the midst of it; and the
beasts of the desert came to drink from it; and I saw in the midst of them two
naked men; and my body was afraid; for I thought that they are spirits. But
they when they saw me being afraid, spoke to me: Do not fear; we too are men.
And I said to them: From where are you, and how did you come into this desert?
And they said: We are from a coenobium; and an agreement was made for us, and
we came out here; see, forty years. And the one is Egyptian, and the other is
a Libyan =34.240=. And they also asked me saying: How is the world? and
whether the water comes in its season, and whether the world has its
abundance, and I said to them: Yes. And I asked them: How can I become a monk?
And they say to me: Unless a man renounce all the things of the world, he
cannot become a monk. And I said to them: I am weak and cannot as you. And
they also said to me: And if


you cannot as we, sit in your cell, and weep
for your sins. And I asked them: When winter comes, do you not freeze? and
when heat comes, are your bodies not burned? And they said: God has made us
this provision; and neither in winter do we freeze, nor in summer does the
heat harm us. For this reason I said to you, that I have not yet become a
monk, but I have seen monks. Forgive me, brothers. When abba Macarius was
dwelling in the utter desert; and he was alone in it as an anchorite, and
below there was another desert of more brothers. And the old man was watching
the road, and he sees Satan coming up in the form of a man, to pass by him;
and he appeared as wearing a linen tunic full of holes; and from each hole a
little flask was hanging. And the great old man says to him: Where are you
going? And he said to him: I am going to remind the brothers. And the old man
said: And why these little flasks for you? And he said: I am bringing tastes
to the brothers. And the old man said: And all these? He answered: Yes; if one
does not please someone, I bring another; and if not this either, I give
another; and of them at any rate even one will please him. And having said
these things he went away; and the old man remained watching the roads until
that one returned again. And when the old man saw him, he says to him: Be
saved. And he answered: Where is it for me to be saved? The old man says to
him: Why? And he says: Because they have all become wild to me, and no one
puts up with me. The old man says to him: So you have no friend there? And he
answered: Yes, I have one monk there as a friend, and even he obeys me; and
when he sees me, he turns like a windlass. The old man says to him: And what
is the brother called? And he says: Theopemptus. And having said these things
he went away, and abba Macarius rising goes to the desert below, and the
brothers hearing, taking palm-branches came out to meet him. And then each was
making ready =34.241= thinking, that the old man was going to lodge with him.
But he was seeking who was called Theopemptus on the mountain. And finding him
he went into his cell. And Theopemptus received him rejoicing. And when he
began to be in private with him, the old man says: How are things with you,
brother? And he said: By your prayers, well. And the old man said: Do the
thoughts not war against you? And he said: For the present I am well; for he
was ashamed to speak. The old man says to him: See, how many years I have been
in ascesis, and I am honored by all, and even to me, an old man, the spirit of
fornication is a trouble. Theopemptus answering said: Believe me, abba, even
to me. And the old man pretended that other thoughts also warred against him,
until he made him confess. Then he says to him: How do you fast? And he says
to him: Until the ninth. The old man says to him: Fast until evening, and
practice ascesis; and learn by heart from the Gospel and the other
scriptures; and if a thought rise up to you, never look down, but always up;
and immediately the Lord helps you. And the old man having given the brother a
rule went out to his own desert. And watching again he sees that demon, and
says to him: Where are you going again? And he says: To remind the brothers,
and he went away.
And when he returned again, the saint says to him: How are the brothers? And
he says: Badly. And the old man says: Why? And he said: They are all wild; and
the greater evil, that even the one I had as a friend obeying me, even he I do
not know from where has been turned aside, and not even he obeys me, but he
has become wilder than all; and I have sworn no longer to set foot there,
except after a time. And having said thus he went away leaving the old man;
and the saint went into his cell. Abba Macarius the Great visited abba Antony

they made him a monk; and many of the Hellenes became Christians through him.
So abba Macarius used to say, that the bad word makes even the good bad; and
the good word makes even the bad good. They used to say concerning abba
Macarius, that while he was away a robber went into his cell. And when he had
come to the cell, he found the robber filling the camel with his vessels; and
he himself going into the cell was taking from the vessels, and filling the
camel together with him. So when they had filled, the robber began to beat the
camel, that it might rise; and it would not get up. And abba Macarius seeing
that it does not get up, going into the cell found a little hoe; and bringing
it out he put it on the camel saying: Brother, this is what the camel is
seeking. And the old man striking it with his foot says: Rise; and immediately
it rose, and went away a little because of his word =34.261=; and again it
sat, and did not rise, until they unloaded all the vessels; and thus he went
away. Abba Aio asked abba Macarius saying: Tell me a word. Abba
