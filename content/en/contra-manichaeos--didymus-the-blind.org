=39.1085= No opposition of contraries is opposed from wholes, because there
are also certain things common to the contraries. For example white, being
contrary to black, has the contrariety according to the differences alone,
color and quality being common to them; and none of such things has the
contrariety concerning them as prior; for all things are after the commons;
and in the commons there is no contrariety. For there must be common color and
quality, in order that thus the white and the black may be, likewise after the
qualified and a disposition, virtue and vice. If then two principles are
ungenerated and themselves contraries, either they have the contrariety from
wholes, or there are certain things common to them. But nothing admits
contrariety from wholes; they therefore also have commons, being, being
substances, and if anything else is found common concerning them. But what
makes them con =39.1088= traries is that the one is good, and the other evil;
and that the one is light, and the other darkness. But their being substances
is conceived prior, rather than their being good and evil. The contrariety
therefore is not according to the commons, but according to the particularity.
And they set them down as ungenerated; but nothing is before an ungenerated
thing. But before these there is something, that itself which I said belongs
to them in common; therefore the principles are not ungenerated. For these
contraries are ungenerated; but what belongs to them in common is before them.
Therefore the principles are not ungenerated; for nothing is before an
ungenerated thing. But if they should say that they do not have the
contrariety according to the ungenerated, this too will be found common of
them. But since the evil is evil ungenerately, and the good good, the
ungenerated evil of both will be found common, and the ungenerated good of
both. For if good cannot be found without the ungenerated, and the ungenerated
is common, then also that which of necessity follows the ungenerated is
common; and this is the good. But if they should say that the ungenerated is
not common to them: in that in which it belongs to the one, it will not belong
to the remaining; there will then be ungenerated either the good or the evil,
not the good and evil. If then the good should be found ungenerated, the evil
is generated. Besides, every evil is to be punished; nothing to be punished is
incorruptible; therefore no evil is incorruptible, because what does not exist
incorruptibly is corruptible. Nothing corruptible is ungenerated; but evil is
corruptible; therefore evil is not ungenerated. Nothing is evil according to
substance, because evil is a quality; and no quality is a substance; therefore
evil is not a substance. All contraries are destructive of one another; but
the ungenerated are incorruptible; therefore they are not contraries; for the
contraries are not incorruptible. Nothing ungenerated is changeable; but the
good is changed when overcome by the evil; therefore the good is not
ungenerated. The ungenerated is not appetitive of any corruption, nor yet
destructive; but evil is appetitive of corruption; therefore evil is not
ungenerated. The divine Scriptures say that not only evil human beings, but
also the devil himself, are delivered over to punishment. But everything
delivered over to punishment is changeable; and nothing changeable is
ungenerated; therefore none of the evils is ungenerated. =39.1089= Of things
that are contrary throughout, nothing is common. So that of all necessity that
which belongs to the one does not belong to the remaining; but to the good
belongs being ungenerated; therefore neither of these belongs to the evil.
Therefore evil is not-being. Everyone who reasonably desires something has the
desire attained, if he reasonably desires. But God desires to destroy vice;
therefore vice is of the corruptible things. And being of the corruptible
things, it will be made to vanish; and being made to vanish, it is not
ungenerated. If evil is ungenerated, it belongs to it by nature to be evil.
But no one acting according to nature sins. Therefore evil does not sin; and
that which does not sin is not liable. But Satan is liable; therefore he is
not ungenerated. For the divine Scripture of the deeds and practices which
human beings do, says that they are sons and

offspring. At any rate those who are wrathful and have the remaining sin, as
being on this account subject to the wrath according to punishment, it names
children of wrath, as Paul says: And we were by nature children of wrath, even
as the rest. These it is possible to change, so as no longer to be children of
wrath by nature. Paul at any rate concerning himself, and those who have
similarly become holy, says that We were by nature children of wrath, even as
the remaining human beings, who are still even now in sinning; but the "by
nature" attached does not signify that which is according to nature, but that
which is in truth. For he says, We were by nature children of wrath, making
clear that those who sin are in truth liable to wrath; and just as one becomes
a child of wrath according to nature by vice being with him, so having changed
he becomes a child of truth and of virtue. At any rate such things have been
said concerning certain ones by the Savior: Wisdom is justified from her
children; calling the wise children of wisdom. For since those who are
children of someone have their being from the one who begot them; and those
who are qualified also have their being from the quality that has been added
to them; reasonably those who are qualified are named children and sons of the
quality. For as such things have been said by the Savior, But these are the
wise, children of wisdom; so also he addressed others as sons of peace, saying
to the disciples: If you enter into a city, or into a house, and there is a
son of peace there, your peace given to the city, or to the house, will rest
upon the one who is in it a son of peace. Again the same Savior says: The sons
of this age are more prudent than the sons of light, in their own generation;
calling sons of light those who have been enlightened by the knowledge of the
truth and by the works of virtue. And to those who have also be =39.1092= come
light, because they are sons of light, he says: You are the light of the
world; but those who have loved the present living, and refer everything they
do to it, he named sons of this age. But even if he said these were more
prudent than the sons of light, he did not say them more prudent absolutely,
but in their own generation; that is, in the affairs that have been made
proper to this age, they are more prudent than the sons of light. For they are
wise to do evil; but to do well they have not understood. And concerning Judas
also it has been written that he was a son of perdition. For the Lord said
concerning him, that None has perished from my disciples, except the son of
perdition. And he called him a son of perdition, because he had done the
things of perdition. For he was not such when he was one of the apostles, and
served together with those men the Savior, but when he became a betrayer of
the Son of God and Teacher, having loved the silver of perdition. And that he
was not evil according to nature, being together with all the disciples he
heard: Behold I send you as sheep in the midst of wolves. But if he calls
those who sin wolves, and those who practice virtue sheep, if indeed Judas was
evil by nature, he was not a sheep of Christ; since the one who is evil
according to nature is not a practitioner of virtue. But not even the devil
himself is evil according to nature, but from a turning of his own free will.
As then not only of the base practices and things done, but also of the
excellent, those who accomplish them are called sons and children in the
aforementioned manner, according to which those who receive from them the
being which they have from the quality were said to be offspring of qualities;
reasonably flesh of sin, and body of sin, is not said of someone absolutely,
but because he serves sin. For the human being, misusing the body and the
flesh unto wantonness and the other such activities, makes his body a body of
sin; and the flesh, flesh of sin; just as the excellent man through temperance
and the following virtue sanctifies his own body and soul. The virgin at any
rate becomes holy in body and spirit, which is soul, as Paul writes, saying
that the

virgin must be undistracted from the affairs of life, That she may be holy in
body and spirit. But also in the Epistle to the Corinthians he writes: Glorify
God in your body. But if God is glorified in the body of human beings, and God
is not glorified in evil; the body is not evil by nature, even if the sinner
makes it evil, using it sinfully. And a little later again he addresses the
same: Or do you not know that your bodies are a temple of the Holy Spirit that
is in you, which you have from God? If anyone destroys the temple of God, God
will destroy this one; for the temple of God is holy, which you are. But also
in that to the Thessalonians the same Apo =39.1093= stle writes: May the God
of peace sanctify you wholly, and may your whole Spirit, and the soul, and the
body be preserved blameless in the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ. For how
can anyone be sanctified wholly, and have his own body whole, if the body,
being evil, is not naturally able to be sanctified? For if this is evil by
nature, it is not receptive of sanctification; but if it receives the good, it
is clear that it is not evil by nature, being receptive of good. For that
which is evil by nature is unreceptive of good. Just as that which is good by
nature is unreceptive of evil. The same Apostle writes in another Epistle to
certain ones: Let us cleanse ourselves from every defilement of flesh and
spirit, accomplishing holiness in the fear of God; showing also through this
saying that the flesh too is naturally able to be cleansed of the defilements
that attach to it from sin. But also the spirit and the soul; but if the flesh
were evil according to substance, and the spirit good according to substance,
neither would the spirit have defilement, nor yet would the flesh be cleansed
from defilement. But he commands to cleanse both from defilement, saying also
the manner of the cleansing. For accomplishing, he says, holiness in the fear
of God. And well too the saying: In the fear of God; showing that they have
the obedient from themselves, and not from necessity. But if, accomplishing
holiness in the fear of God, we cleanse our own flesh from defilement and the
spirit; it is clear that this comes to be when holiness comes to be in us. For
just as a soul is cleansed of ignorance, and of injustices, by the presence of
knowledge and of righteousness (for the flight from vice works an entrance of
virtue from the contrary); so by the presence of sanctification the flesh is
cleansed of defilement and the spirit. And spirit in these things he calls the
soul, just as also in the Acts of the Apostles Stephen the apostle, being
stoned, and about to die, said praying: Lord Jesus, receive my spirit. For
neither in these things did he say his spirit as anything else than the soul.
If then through more things the flesh and the spirit are said as having at one
time of sin, at another of sanctification, and toward glorifying God; none of
these is by nature evil, or good; but by free will receptive of both; or
according to the use of the one who has it, it receives in turn, at one time
to be of sin, at another of sanctification? But if, being ignorant also of the
saying from which they put forward that the flesh is of sin, they suppose
this; yet we, setting down the whole of it, will make the sense manifest. And
it has thus. God, having sent his own Son in the likeness of flesh of sin,
concerning sin condemned sin in the flesh. What he says is of this sort.
=39.1096= The flesh of all human beings has its being from coupling, apart
from the first-formed, and that which the Savior assumed. For otherwise a body
of a human being would not come to be, unless the male had been entwined with
the female
.
Since then the Savior took a body from the Virgin alone, not having the
beginning from intercourse, he called the Lord's flesh a likeness of the flesh
that is from intercourse. For he did not say absolutely that he has a likeness
of flesh, but a likeness of flesh of sin. And the likeness of the flesh of sin
is flesh, lacking of the other

fleshes in this alone, having had existence without a man. For it was
necessary that the one coming for the salvation of human beings should not
take a body from coupling. Nor again outside the succession of human beings,
as the first human being, lest his body be supposed of another kind. For if he
too had been formed from the earth, as originator of the race of human beings,
those who introduce the seeming through appearance would have taken a
suspicion, that therefore he did not in truth bear a body of a human being.
Again, if he had taken up a body from coupling, not having that which is
altered, he too would have been supposed liable to that sin, to which also all
those from Adam according to succession existed. But if they should say: If
flesh of sin is that which is born from intercourse of man and woman, marriage
is evil; let them hear that Before the sojourn of the Savior, who took away
the sin of the world, all human beings, just as they practiced the remaining
things with vice, so also they had marriage sinfully. Wherefore the bodies
born from marriage are to be understood; And thus, since after the sin the
coming-together of Adam and Eve came to be, on this account it has been called
flesh of sin. For being incorruptible before the transgression, he became
corruptible having transgressed; and thus the succession has the corruptible.
The Savior therefore had a likeness of this flesh, even if his flesh did not
see corruption. But when the Savior sojourned, as a taking-away of sin came to
be from the remaining affairs, so also from marriage. Now at any rate those
who live according to the Gospel are able to have honorable marriage and an
undefiled bed, as Paul writes to the faithful: Marriage is honorable, and the
bed undefiled; but fornicators and adulterers God will judge. For
distinguishing honorable marriage, and those who use it, from fornicators and
adulterers, he does not wish this to be fornication, nor adultery; whence
neither is marriage sin. And otherwise it will be said more naturally:
Virginity is a certain divine thing, and is as among virtues. If then anyone,
comparing marriage to this, should say it is sin, it is not sin absolutely.
For evil is not e =39.1097= vil by comparison of the good, being such in
itself. At any rate if ever something should be said evil as toward a good, it
too would be good, inferior to the good preferred to it. Those then who come
to the Gospel come to it primarily in order to be godly, and to become wise
according to God. These consequently have being warred upon by certain ones;
for they too must take up Christianity firmly. And the firmness is added from
the training according to the temptations. Whence of necessity they endure
contests. And just as those who set the contests, if they should take thought
of the reasonable, do not match this one with that one as it happens; an
athlete, but with much exactness, looking to the proportion of each one's
power; so also our prize-giver Christ does not yoke all with the same
antagonists, but according to the age and the power of each he gives the
opponent. To the Corinthians at any rate, being still infants, to be occupied
with the passions of the flesh Paul writes: Temptation has not taken you,
except a human one. For the temptations of these, and the contests, have their
reference toward human affairs. But writing concerning himself and those like
him, equally balanced and of equal strength, he says: Our wrestling is not
against blood and flesh, but against the principalities, and the authorities,
and the world-rulers of the darkness of this age; all but saying this: For us
who have gone beyond, the wrestling is no longer against blood and flesh, that
is human things, but against the ranks of the opponents that have gone beyond;
and these are species of rational beings, that have become wicked by choice.
And let no one suppose that, having said species of wicked rational beings, we
speak of a wicked substance. For there are species and genera also of things
that exist by choice. We say at any rate that species

of an excellent man are: just, temperate, prudent. For since virtue is a
genus, divided into species, into justice, temperance and the remaining, of
necessity the one who partakes of the genus, being excellent, is as it were a
genus of those who partake of the virtues according to species; likewise also,
science being a genus, of grammar, music and the remaining, we say that the
one named from the genus a man of science is a genus of those named from the
species of science. Even if then we speak of species of wicked rational
beings, we say that all those under these species are such by choice. At any
rate the names previously said as wicked are indicative not of substances, but
of things of choice. For the name devil does not make clear a substance, but a
choice. Immediately at any rate also Judas, being a human being =39.1100=, the
Lord named a devil, not because of the substance, but because of the choice,
saying to all the disciples together, numbering Judas also among them: Did I
not choose you twelve, and one of you is a devil? To which the writer adds:
And this he said concerning Judas, who was about to betray him. For if he was
called devil because of the coming betrayal, he was not then a devil when
together with all he obtained election. For he became elect together with all,
because of the virtue then present to him; but he was named devil then, when
he worked the things of the betrayal. If then in the case of Judas the name
devil is indicative of an act of choice, so even if the voice should be said
of some other, it signifies something of choice. And custom also knows the use
of this name. For we call those who accuse these to those, and those to these,
slanderers. Since then the devil also slanders God to human beings, and human
beings to God, and the human beings themselves to one another, he obtained
this naming. But if also the name satan should be predicated of the devil
himself, let them know that this too is indicative of something of choice. For
being a Hebrew voice, it signifies nothing other according to the Greek voice
than the opposing; and the opposing, taken of a human being, is equal to
enemy, harmful. As then the enemy is such by choice, so also the opposing;
just as also the contraries of these; for friend, and well-disposed, and of
one mind, and of one thought, are names indicative of choices. For this same
one is called friend and well-disposed, just as devil, and satan, and wicked.
As in the Gospel the Savior among other things also teaches the disciples to
say this in the prayer: And lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from
the wicked. For also the name of the wicked is indicative of choice. If then
through all the things which he signifies, the first-evil and first apostate
from virtue is such by choice, he cannot be evil according to substance; but
if they should say this, that if he were evil in substance he would also have
obtained the things of choice, on account of which he has been so named; let
them know that choice is not taken among the things of substance. For no one
says that one is an animal, or a stone, by choice; for these are of substance.
Whence neither is there anything of choice; for the things of choice are in
those in whom there is also choice. But perhaps they will say: Why did the
good God bring into being the one who was thus going to be harmful, or a
destroyer? And let them know, saying this, that they oppose not us alone, but
also all who set down providence. For many others also who set down providence
say that all rational living beings are from God, in whom vice also grows. If
then they say these things concerning the devil =39.1101=, they are compelled
to say this also concerning all rational beings. Nevertheless we, not
imitating those men, say: The one who made that which is able from its own
inclination to come into vice is not cause of vice. For he constructed a
rational living being, which he wished to be good from its own choice, having
the

free will to incline toward either. For he wished it to be good, voluntarily
choosing the good. For none of generated things is able to be good
unwillingly; but all who are good are such voluntarily. It was necessary then
that the one who would be good by his own choice should have the same power
with the fitness toward taking up the good. And it followed that what is
receptive of the good is also receptive of evil; the rational being having
been thus constructed, to have power toward virtue and vice, a commandment has
been given in order that it may have virtue according to actuality, and vice
according to fitness alone. For the law of the one who created exhorts, and
anoints, and cooperates toward virtue, turning away the nature of vice. Even
if then a power of vice exists in the rational being, this is not base because
of the power. For of both, I mean virtue and vice, there are the powers and
choices. For we choose to have a power of virtue, in order that we may also be
able to take it up according to actuality; we also choose to have a power of
vice. For then we have it, when we have virtue in actuality. And this opinion
is not ours alone, but also of all who have philosophized rightly concerning
rational living beings. Even if then God made the substance that is called
devil, he did not create it a devil, but receptive of virtue, in order that it
may have this; and receptive of vice, in order that it may keep away from
this. For since virtue is unsnatchable with vice, and virtue is dear to God,
putting the powers of both into the rational being he put them in for this
reason, in order that we may take the one over into actuality, and keep away
from the end of the other power, which is the actuality. So that every
rational being has come to be in order to be excellent, and not base. If then
certain of the rational beings have run the contrary to the one who made and
provides, the cause is not of the one who made and legislated, but of those
who strained against the laws given and provided. We see at any rate also in
ourselves, these things always coming to be, which we train the children,
wishing to bring them up according to a science, in order that they may also
actualize this; but they do not altogether actualize thus, as they were
taught; but there are times when many sons contrarywise, having been brought
up as physicians from fathers, do not use medicine as they ought, applying the
disease-making instead of the healthy to those who are ill; and surely neither
medicine, nor the one who trai =39.1104= ned this, is cause. For he did not
teach thus, in order that he might do thus. For he acts not as he learned to
do. Again we train certain ones in laws, in order that they may stand with the
just, and decline injustice. But some of them coming to the laws with bad
character, do the reverse. And we blame neither the law, nor the lawgiver. For
if they themselves were causes of the injustice, they would not punish the
unjust. For this too belongs to their will, That the unjust is to be punished;
and God punishes Satan, and those who have transgressed the laws set from God.
For to certain base ones he says: Go into the eternal fire, that which has
been prepared for the devil and his angels. Let those who contradict these
things know, then, that they say things that fight against themselves. For if
the devil were evil according to substance, and alien to the good God, able to
be of equal strength with him; he would neither have prepared punishment for
him, nor would he have been able to cast him into the punishment; but he says
that fire has been prepared for the devil. And this too must be said: If the
good God is unchangeable, as indeed he also is, and always has the same power,
so as not to be strong otherwise at one time, and otherwise at another; and he
was going, after much harm and much destruction, to deliver Satan over to
eternal fire, why did he not do this from the beginning, when he rose up
against him, and took a part of him? For more justly then rather he would have
appointed vengeance against him, as against a greater offense. For now he
harms human beings; then, according to them, he harmed the divinity itself,

as they also think. And a law and a just indignation is rather that against
those who dare against a king, than against certain of the subjects. For if
these things are thus to be thought, they will either attach ignorance to God
then, of how he will punish and hinder the evil, or inability. But neither of
these is about the good God; therefore it was not as an ungenerately enemy of
God that he rose up against him; but having come to be in order to be good, he
kept the power of virtue incomplete, and brought the fitness of vice into
actuality; wherefore also he is to be punished, and reasonably after a long
time he was subjected to vengeance. But if they should also put forward that
which was said by John the Baptist to those who came to the baptism with
hypocrisy, having thus: Serpents, offspring of vipers, who showed you to flee
from the coming wrath? Produce then fruit worthy of repentance, and do not
think to say in yourselves, that we have Abraham as father. For God is able
from these stones to raise children to Abraham. And already the axe is laid to
the root of the trees. Every tree therefore that does not produce good fruit
is cut down, and cast into fire. Let them hear that they cannot put forward
from things which they do not accept. =39.1105= For they do not receive John
the Baptist, who is one of the Old Scripture. Nevertheless even thus it must
be said to them: Through the whole context he does away with being good by
nature and being evil by nature. For he says to them: Produce then fruit
worthy of repentance. But repentance and its fruit do not fall either to the
one who is evil according to substance, or to the one who is good by nature.
For how does the one who is always good need repentance? or how will the one
who is evil by nature use the good of repentance? For the one who is evil
according to substance cannot be turned from sin; but neither does repentance
come to be for benefit of the good. Still also from the stones, those shown by
him, he says children will be raised to Abraham; and calling stones the
foolish, and human beings still remaining in vice. For if these should repent,
and believe God, they will be children of Abraham, who was named a human being
of God for this reason, that he believed in God himself, so that his faith was
reckoned unto righteousness. But since we have also heard that said by them
toward those born from Abraham and the other holy men, the, Serpents,
offspring of vipers, being said, it makes clear the vipers as those about
Abraham; let them know they say things that fight against the sequence of the
chapter. For he convicts them as not being of Abraham, but offspring of
vipers, pretending and vainly boasting to be of Abraham. For in another
chapter the Savior says to them: This Abraham did not do, whom you say to be
your father, lying. For if you were children of Abraham, you would do the
works of Abraham. But you are of your father the devil, and you wish to do the
desires of your father. For he was a man-killer from the beginning, and he has
not stood in the truth. And well too the saying, He has not stood, showing him
formerly standing in the truth. And he calls the devil their father, because
of the likeness of the turning and of the quality. So that he did not call
Abraham and the holy ones with him vipers. For they had this from a change,
being sons of vipers, because they used wicked teachers, as has been said,
according to their own choice. John then says to them: Since according to a
change from virtue you willed to become serpents (for they were not such
according to substance), cease from being offspring of vipers, whose quality
you willed to have. For you have been eager to be wicked from wicked, and
unjust from unjust. But since, because of coming to the baptism, you say you
bear a sign of repentance, but you do this pretensively, I say to you: Who
showed you to flee from the coming wrath? For the one who repents, for this
reason

repents, in order that no longer sinning he may flee the wrath that will
=39.1108= be against those who sin. And he inquires, Who showed you? in order
that he may say that the one who genuinely shows to withdraw from vice, this
does not come to be by pretence. But you do this with hypocrisy, coming to be
baptized. Therefore you will not be cleansed. For the one who truly wishes to
be cleansed stands away from sins, and bears fruit of such repentance, doing
the works of the disposition contrary to sin. But this you have not done,
having condemned yourselves, saying you have Abraham as father. But this will
not benefit you, thinking to be children of Abraham, but being his children,
both by the faith and by the flight from idols. Even if then you have at some
time become children of Abraham, this will not save you. For also those who
are not now his children God is able to make his children. So that the one who
is a child of Abraham according to virtue is saved, not the one who has become
his child according to flesh alone, and even if he becomes so, let him not
think great things; for such a thing is liable to fall. At any rate even those
who do not have it will be able to acquire it. For Paul also, those whom he
has not begotten in the flesh, says: For in Christ through the Gospel I begot
you. But hearing these things do not despise, as though the wrath against
those who sin were delaying. For already through the very sinning the base one
has the punishment present, being one to be punished. Wherefore know that the
axe is already drawing near to the roots of the trees. And he does not mean
sensible trees, but human beings made according to vice. And the root of these
trees is the worst disposition, from which fruit comes to be not edible, but
poisonous. He therefore called it not a good fruit. Even if then the axe
signifies the punishment, and the evil tree the sinning human being, these are
not natures that fight; for the punishment is threatened in order to change
the trees. For the one who threatens those who sin wishes through the threat
itself to transform. If then through the threat of the axe, which is going to
cut out the rotten trees, he persuades them to cease from bearing evil fruit,
but good; the tree is not evil by nature, nor is the punishment evil by
nature. But perhaps they will say: If the punishment transforms the base, it
is good; but if the punishment is good, and the promise is contrary to this,
and the contraries cannot be good at once; therefore the promise is evil. But
the promise being not of evil, but of good, the punishment is evil. But if the
punishment is evil, not God, but Satan brings it. But Satan brings the
punishment not upon his own; for they are, as he, wicked, and of necessity he

But this is impossible; therefore not the wicked one brings it, but God. And
everything brought by God is brought usefully =39.1109=, being either
productive of good, or preventive of evil. God therefore, wishing to prevent
the evil, brings this; and he would not attempt to prevent the evil, if it
were not possible for the evil to be prevented. And this being prevented
cannot be by nature and ungenerated. And the ignorant have also failed in
this, having supposed the punishment to be contrary to the promise; for each
is a requital, the one bringing a wage to the good, and the other vengeance
against those who have voluntarily transgressed the divine laws.
