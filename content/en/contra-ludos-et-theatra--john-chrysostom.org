* Against the Games and the Theatres
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of our father among the saints, John Chrysostom, archbishop of
Constantinople: a homily against those who have abandoned the church and
deserted to the hippodromes and the theatres.
=56.263=

1. Are these things to be endured? Are these things to be borne? For I wish
to appeal to you yourselves against yourselves. So too God dealt with the
Hebrews: appealing to them against themselves, he said: My people, what have
I done to you, and how have I grieved you, or how have I wearied you? Answer
me. And again: What iniquity did your fathers find in me? This then I also
shall imitate, and say to you again: Are these things to be endured? Are
these things to be borne? After so long a course of discourses and so much
teaching, some of you have abandoned us and deserted to the watching of
horse races, and have become so frenzied as to fill the whole city with
shouting and disorderly clamor, making it bear much laughter, or rather
lamentation. I therefore, sitting at home and hearing the shout breaking
forth, suffered more grievously than those tossed by storms. For just as
those men, when the waves break against the walls of their ship and they are
in peril of their lives, are afraid, so those cries broke upon me more
violently, and I bent to the ground and covered myself; while those above
were behaving so disgracefully, and those below, in the middle of the crowd,
were applauding the charioteers and shouting worse than they.

What =56.264= shall we say? Or what defense shall we make, if some stranger
from somewhere should bring a charge against us and say: Are these the
doings of the city of the apostles? Of her who received so great a teacher?
Of the people who love Christ, the unfeigned and spiritual theatre? You did
not even reverence that day on which the symbols of the salvation of our
race were being accomplished; but on the day of Preparation, when your
Master was being crucified for the world, and such a sacrifice was being
offered, and paradise was being opened, and the robber was being led back to
his ancient homeland, and the curse was being loosed, and sin was being
blotted out, and the long war was being abolished, and God's reconciliation
with men was taking place, and all things were being transformed; on that
day, when it was needful to fast and to give glory, and to send up prayers
of thanksgiving to him who did these things for the good things of the
world; then did you, abandoning the church and the spiritual sacrifice and
the assembly of the brethren and the solemnity of the fast, get carried off
a captive by the devil to that spectacle? Are these things to be endured?
Are these things to be borne? For I shall not cease saying these things
continually, and consoling my grief thereby, the remedy being not to press
it down with silence, but to bring it out into the open =56.265= and set it
before your eyes.

How shall we henceforth be able to make God propitious? How reconcile him
when he is angry? Three days ago a rainstorm burst and swept everything
away, snatching from the very mouth, so to speak, the table of the farmers,
laying low the waving ears of grain, and rotting all the rest through the
greedy excess of the moisture. There were litanies and supplications, and
our whole city ran like a torrent to the places of the apostles, and we took
as advocates the holy Peter and the blessed Andrew, the pair of apostles,
Paul and Timothy. After that, when the anger had been loosed, and we had
crossed the sea and dared the waves, we ran to the chief ones, Peter the
foundation of the faith, Paul the vessel of election, celebrating a
spiritual panegyric and proclaiming their contests, the trophies and the
victories over the demons. And yet, neither terrified by the fear of what
had happened nor instructed by the greatness of the apostolic achievements,
after all this, within the space of a single intervening day, do you caper
and shout, looking on while your own soul is dragged captive by the
passions? But if you wished to watch a race of irrational creatures, why did
you not yoke the irrational passions within you, anger and desire, and lay
upon them the yoke of philosophy, the kindly and light one, and set over
them upright reason, and drive them toward the prize of the higher calling,
not running from defilement to defilement, but from earth to heaven? For
this kind of horse race has, together with the pleasure, great benefit. But
you, abandoning your own affairs to drift simply and anyhow, sat watching
the victory of others, spending so great a day idly and in vain and for
evil.

2. Do you not know that, just as we, entrusting money to our own servants,
demand of them an account down to a single obol, so God will demand of us an
account of the days of our life, how we spent each day? What then shall we
say? What defense shall we make, when we are called to account for that day?
The sun rose for your sake, and the moon lit the night, and the varied dance
of the stars shone forth; winds blew for your sake, rivers ran; seeds
sprouted for your sake, and plants flourished, and the course of nature kept
its own order, and day appeared =56.266= and night passed by; and all these
things came to pass for your sake; but do you, while the creations serve
you, fill up the desire of the devil? And having rented so great a house
from God, I mean this world, you did not pay the rent. And you were not
satisfied with the first day, but on the second, when you ought to have
rested a little from the evil that had come upon you, you went up again to
the theatres, running from smoke into fire, casting yourself into another
pit, a worse one. Old men shamed their grey hairs, and young men threw away
their youth, and fathers led up their children, from the outset guiding the
age innocent of evil into the pits of wickedness, so that one would not err
in calling such men child-killers instead of fathers, destroying by
wickedness the souls of those begotten of them. "And what evil?" he says. It
is for this very reason that I am in pain: because even diseased, you do not
know that you are diseased, so as to seek the physician too. You have become
filled with adultery, and you ask, "What evil?" Have you not heard Christ
saying: Whoever looks at a woman in order to desire her has already
committed adultery with her? "What then, if I do not look in order to
desire?" he says. And how will you be able to persuade me? For he who does
not master his looking, but displays such eagerness for this very thing, how
after the looking will he be able to remain unstained? Is your body
perchance stone? Or iron? You are clothed in flesh, human flesh, which is
kindled by desire more readily than grass.

And why do I speak of the theatre? Often in the marketplace, if we meet a
woman, we are thrown into confusion; but you, sitting above, where there is
such encouragement toward shamelessness, seeing a harlot entering bareheaded
with all boldness, wrapped in golden garments, playing the wanton, yielding
to softness, singing harlots' songs, wanton melodies, uttering shameful
words, behaving so unseemly that if you took thought of these things you
would bow your head to the ground; do you dare to say that you suffer
nothing human? Is your body perchance stone? Or iron? For I shall not
refrain from saying the same things again. Are you perchance more of a
philosopher than those great and noble men who were overcome by mere sight?
Have you not heard what Solomon says: If anyone walks on coals of fire, will
his feet not be burned? If anyone embraces fire in his bosom, will his
garments not be burned? So is he who goes in to a strange woman. For even if
you did not couple with the harlot, you joined =56.267= with the desire, and
wrought the sin in your will. And not only at that moment, but also when the
theatre has been dismissed and she has gone away, her image lies laid up in
your soul: the words, the figures, the glances, the gait, the rhythm, the
elegance, the meretricious songs; and you depart, having received countless
wounds. Is it not from here the overturning of households? Is it not from
here the destruction of temperance? Is it not from here the divisions of
marriages? Is it not from here wars and battles? Is it not from here
aversions past all expression? For when, filled with her, you go up home a
captive, your wife seems more disagreeable, your children more troublesome,
your servants burdensome, your house superfluous, and your customary cares
seem to vex you in the management of necessary affairs, and everyone who
approaches you is tiresome and burdensome.

3. The reason is that you do not go up to your house alone, but have the
harlot with you; not coming up openly and manifestly, which would have been
lighter, for your wife would quickly have driven her out; but enthroned in
your thought and your conscience, and kindling within you the Babylonian
furnace, or rather one far more grievous: for it is not straw and naphtha
and pitch that become its fuel, but the things I have named, and everything
is turned upside down.

And just as those in a fever, having no charge to bring against those who
attend them, are through the malice of their disease displeasing to all,
rejecting their food, reviling their physicians, resentful toward their own
people, raging against those who minister to them; so those who are sick
with this grievous disease are altered and vexatious, seeing that woman at
every turn. O what grievous things! The wolf and the lion and the other
beasts, when struck by the arrow, flee the huntsman; but man, most rational
of all, when wounded, pursues the one who wounded him, so as to receive a
far more grievous dart and to nurse the wound in his flesh: which of all
things is most bitter, and makes the disease incurable. For he who does not
hate the sore, and does not wish to be freed of it, how should he seek the
physician? For this reason I am pained and cut to the heart, because you
receive such brutal outrage there, and for a brief pleasure endure continual
pain. For even before the gehenna and the punishment you exact of yourselves
here the last penalty. Tell me, is it not an extreme punishment, to nourish
such a desire, to be set on fire continually, and to carry about everywhere
the furnace of a strange love and the accusation of conscience?

For how will you mount those sacred steps? How touch the heavenly table? How
hear the discourse on temperance, being full of sores and =56.268= so many
wounds, and having your mind enslaved to the passion? And what need to say
more? From what is now happening among us it is possible to see the grief of
your mind. Even now I see you, in the midst of these words, smiting your
foreheads, and I have great thankfulness toward you, that you are a people
so tenderhearted. Indeed I think that many of those who have not sinned at
all are doing this, grieving for the wounds of their brethren. Therefore I
am pained and afflicted, because the devil ravages such a flock. But if you
are willing, we shall quickly block up his entrance. How, and in what
manner? If we see those who are diseased become whole; if, spreading the
nets of our teaching, we go about seeking those held captive by the beasts,
and snatch them from the very throat of the lion. Do not say to me: "Few
have been carried off from the flock." Even if there are only ten, the loss
is no ordinary one; even if five, or two, or one. Since even that shepherd,
for this reason leaving the ninety-nine sheep, ran after the one, and did
not return until he had brought it back again, and made whole through it the
number of the hundred that was lacking, by the restoration of the one that
had gone astray. Do not say that it is one; but consider that it is a soul,
for whose sake all things visible came to be; for whose sake laws and
penalties and punishments, and the myriad wonders and the manifold works of
God; for whose sake he did not spare even his Only-begotten. Consider how
great a price has been laid down even for the one, and do not despise his
salvation; but go and bring him back to us, and persuade him to fall no more
into the same things, and we have a sufficient defense. But if he will not
submit, either to our counsel or to your exhortation, I shall thereafter use
the authority that God gave us, not for destruction, but for building up.

4. For this reason I proclaim beforehand, and cry out with a clear voice,
that if anyone after this exhortation and teaching should desert again to
the lawless outrage of the theatres, I will not receive him within these
precincts, I will not let him share in the mysteries, I will not suffer him
to touch the sacred table; but as the shepherds keep back the sheep filled
with scab from the healthy ones, lest they communicate the disease to the
rest, so shall I also act. For if of old the leper was commanded to sit
outside the camp, and even if he were a king, was cast out together with his
diadem, far more shall we cast out of this sacred camp the one who makes his
soul leprous. For as at the beginning I made use of exhortation and counsel,
so now, after so much exhortation and teaching, it is necessary thereafter
to bring in also the cutting off. For it is now a year since I set foot in
your city, and I have not ceased often and continually exhorting you
concerning these things. Since then some have persisted =56.269= in the
corruption, come, let us from now on bring in the cutting off. For if I have
not steel, I have a word sharper than steel; and if I do not carry fire, I
have a teaching hotter than fire, able to burn more fiercely.

Do not then despise our decree. For though we are lowly and most pitiable,
yet we have been entrusted by the grace of God with an authority able to do
these things. Let such men then be cast out, that we who are healthy may
become healthier, and those who are sick may recover themselves from their
grievous infirmity. But if you have shuddered, hearing this decree (for I
see you all looking grave and downcast), let them change, and the decree is
dissolved. For as we received authority to bind, so also to loose, and to
bring them back again. For we do not wish to cut off our brethren, but to
repel the disgrace of the Church. For as it is now, the Greeks will laugh us
to scorn, and the Jews will hold us up to derision, whenever we thus
overlook our own members sinning. But then even they will greatly praise us,
and will admire the Church, showing reverence for the laws that are among
us. Let no one, then, of those who persist in the same fornication set foot
in the church; but let him be rebuked also by you, and let him be a common
enemy. For, he says, if anyone does not obey our word through the letter,
mark that one, and do not keep company with him. But do this: share no
conversation with him, receive him not into your house, have no share of
table with him, no going in, no going out, no marketplace; and so we shall
easily recover them. And as the hunters drive the hard-to-capture beasts not
from one quarter but from every side, and cast them into the net; so let us
also drive together those who have become like wild beasts, and quickly cast
them into the nets of salvation, we from this side, you from that.

That this may come to pass, do you also share our indignation, =56.270= or
rather grieve for the laws of God, and turn away a little from these
brethren who are diseased with such things and transgressing, so that you
may keep them continually. For it is no light judgment for you, if you
overlook so great a destruction, but you will incur the greatest punishment.
For if in the houses of men, when one of the servants is caught taking away
silver or gold, not only is the one caught punished, but also those who knew
and did not inform, far more so in the Church. For then God will say to you:
"You, seeing that not silver or a golden vessel was stolen from my house,
but that temperance itself was plundered, and that the one who had received
the precious body and shared in so great a sacrifice departed into the
devil's territory and committed such transgressions; how did you keep
silence? How did you bear it? Why did you not report it to the priest?" And
you will not be called to ordinary accounts. Wherefore I also, though I am
about to give pain, will spare none of the more grievous measures. For it is
far better that we, pained here, be delivered from the judgment to come,
than that we, flattering you with words, be punished with you then. For it
is not safe for us, nor without danger, to bear such things in silence. For
each of you will give an account for himself; but I am answerable for the
salvation of all. For this reason I shall not cease doing and saying
everything, even if I must give pain, even if I must appear burdensome or
tiresome, so that I may be able to stand at that fearful judgment seat,
having no stain or wrinkle or any such thing. And may it come to pass,
through the prayers of the saints, that those who have already been
corrupted may quickly return, and those who have remained unstained may
advance to greater comeliness and temperance; that you may be saved, and we
may rejoice, and God may be glorified, now and ever, and unto the unending
ages of ages. Amen.
