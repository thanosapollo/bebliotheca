* Homily I on Saint Lazarus
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

There was read of Hesychius, presbyter of Jerusalem, on James, the brother of
the Lord, and David the God-father.
1 If our Savior had not "humbled himself becoming obedient even to death,"
household servants would not have been named kinsmen, clay would not have been
called father of the potter. In Zion I greet Bethlehem as present, in the
daughter I see the mother. In you one torch-bearing star was lit, and in this
many. That one guided the Magi, this one torch-bore Parthians and Medes and
Elamites and those from all nations with the light of the lightning. You draw
milk from virgin breasts, this one brings down spirit from paternal bosoms.
You leavened the bread, but Zion showed a supper. You nourished the calf upon
the manger, but Zion led it up to the altar. You hid Jesus in the
swaddling-clothes, and this one bared the side to Thomas wishing to handle.
You have a virgin bearing with the organs of nature closed, this one a bridal
chamber receiving and having the bridegroom within with the doors closed. 2
How shall I encomium the servant and brother of Christ, the commander of the
new Jerusalem, the leader of the priests, the exarch of the apostles, the peak
among heads, the one shining above among lamps, the one appearing above among
stars? Peter harangues, but James legislates, and few sayings contracted the
greatness of the question: "I judge not to trouble those from the nations, and
the rest". "I judge": whose judgment it is not lawful to set aside, nor to
make cheap the vote; "For in me the judge of all both dead and living speaks.
Mine is the instrument, but the craftsman is above me. I furnish the tongue,
but the creating Word supplies the word. Do not attend to the reed, but to the
one writing. Let obedience succeed the hearing."
