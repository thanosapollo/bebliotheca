* On the Conception of the Venerable Forerunner

Of Hesychius, presbyter of Jerusalem, a discourse on the conception of the
honorable Forerunner.
1 All the leapings of the God-bearing prophets are great and most glorious of
all; for they move their feet with God and work their hands from the Spirit;
the Word and Son, both Christ and God, is presented immaterial on their
tongue, that Word who composed the beauties of heaven, and forged the
lightning of the solar chariots, ordered the monthly horns for the moon, yoked
the harmony of the stars and unyoked it, devising a proper course for each,
commanding to them one common road for all; he did not leave the clouds to fly
unbridled, but as the season calls, and the fruit of the fields seeks, and the
farmer demands and longs and prays, and all those below intercede toward the
one above.
2 He himself said to the sea: "Leap and rage; and let the waves be crested,
but let them be shattered in you." Let it therefore frighten, so that the
seafarer may not acquire forgetfulness of the Creator; but let it not swamp
the merchant, so that the element may not become injurious to the heir. He
himself distributes to the dry land the flowers, the plants, the fruits, the
shoots, and he honored each country with the goods that spring up
exceptionally, and he showed the whole earth as one body divided in many
places into many members. He himself distributes the pipes to the prophets and
divides the panpipes and forges the trumpets, works the cymbals and joints the
drums, distributes the lots and allots the gifts.
3 And John the Baptist has been allotted a double and more glorious country,
since being young he forced the times, he overran the bounds of nature, and
the belly was not able to hold the child, the womb was not able to shut in the
prophet. He had no utterance, and he filled the whole earth with glorious
words. The tongue had no pipe, and the melody reached the inhabited world. The
trumpet of the mouth was without a mouth, and the sound of the doctrines
overtook heaven, and the infant, having so much trusted the potter,
strengthened the clay. The time had not come, nor had the birth arrived, the
birth-pangs were not present, the months had not been completed, and the
infant without a tongue was speaking to the child, and he used the leapings
<in>stead of certain pipes, and he kicked the womb as a drum, and running
about the belly he struck it as a cymbal, and he found for himself a lyre
pure, broad, well-strung, belly-stringed, and he commanded the mother to say
to Mary: "Blessed are you among women, and blessed is the fruit of your womb."
4 "Blessed are you among women," because you loosed the bonds, you freed
nature, you slackened the birth-pangs of Eve, you set Adam above the
necessities. "Blessed are you among women"; for through you the barren woman
is a mother, and the infant speaks distinctly, and the child utters beyond
orators, and the one not yet a babbler has been shown forth as an evangelist.
"And blessed is the fruit of your womb." I am a voice and thunder, a bridesman
and a lamp, a mediator and a herald, a prophet and an apostle, a trumpeter of
the gift of life, a leveler of a road having no stumbling-block, a baptist as
regarded according to the flesh, a forerunner of a king whose platform is
everywhere, whose throne is borne by all the elements. All escort as a
bodyguard,

but no one has seen the face of the one escorted or knows the character. All
tremble as they reckon upon his glory, they become thunderstruck in mind,
trying to guess the essence or to imagine the power."
5 Let John therefore be hymned, the citizen of the desert, the wall-builder of
the Jordan, the spectator of the God-winged dove, the scout of the paternal
cloud, the hearer of a voice which presented the dignity of the Son,
manifested the glory of the Word, revealed the glory of the Only-begotten. Let
John be hymned, the piper of the song to which all run. For who, hearing, does
<not> repent, does not hasten to be washed, does not long to be purified, does
not bring a jar to the spring to draw and drink and wishing to cool the
burning heat? Who, having learned that the kingdom of the heavens has drawn
near, does not long to anticipate his brother and is not eager to get ahead of
his neighbor? Let John be hymned, whose first wonders and last are great and
exceeding-bright.
6 The first wonders: Gabriel announced the birth to him, an angel interprets
the conception to Zechariah. The priest, standing between the temple and the
altar, was receiving the gift from heaven, and so wretched were the children
of the Jews; for where the priest of God received the oracle, there they
sharpen the sword and arm the hands and rehearse the murder and work the
drama. And what did Gabriel say to Zechariah? "Do not fear, Zechariah, because
your petition has been heard, and behold, your wife Elisabeth will bear you a
son, and you shall call his name John. And there will be joy and gladness for
you, and many will rejoice at his birth. For he will be great before the Lord,
and he shall not drink wine and strong drink, and he will be filled with the
Holy Spirit even from his mother's womb, and he will turn many of the sons of
Israel to the Lord their God. And he will go before him in the spirit and
power of Elijah, to turn hearts of fathers to children and the disobedient in
the prudence of the righteous, to prepare for the Lord a people made ready."
7 You hear how great a wealth John the Baptist carries off before being born,
and of how many goods he becomes a sponsor before being conceived in the
organs of generation. "Do not fear, Zechariah," he says, that is, the Redeemer
is coming and the people ceases from sinning. "Your petition has been heard";
for it is time for heaven to be bent and for the earth to receive the Creator.
"And behold, your wife Elisabeth will bear you a son"; the barren woman gives
birth, so that there may be a time for the virgin toward the wonder and for
her to become Mother of God. "And you shall call his name John"; the name is
fittingly strange, since the thing also is strange to which the name is given;
the appellation is new; for new is the mystery that is about to be served
through him. "And there will be joy and gladness for you"; joy because you
beget this one than whom "there has not arisen a greater among those born of
women," and gladness because the one born from you will be the end of the law
and the prophets.
8 "And many will rejoice at his birth," those who receive his preachings of
repentance and are purified by the bath of baptism; but scribes do not rejoice
and Pharisees mourn with those; and to these he foretells the winnowing-fan
and threatens the axe and proclaims the cutting-out, because their thicket was
thorny, overgrown, beastly, untamed. And what else did Gabriel announce,
magnifying the Baptist: "He will be great before the Lord"? "He will be
great," because he points out the lamb who takes away the sin of the whole
world, but "before the Lord"; for there is not much of the heavenly things for
men

perception, and for Jews the mysteries of Christ are even a laugh, and they
make a comedy
of the heralds of the truth, thinking the orators of grace to be enemies.
9 "He shall not drink wine and strong drink," so that the spirit that chose to
dwell in him may not be cramped. "He will be filled with the Holy Spirit from
his mother's womb," he says, not simply nor in vain anticipating the gift and
arriving at the charisms beforehand, but so that he may preach the light
before conversing with the light, announce the Maker before seeing the
elements, declare the sun before seeing the course of the day, before spying
out the limits of the night. "And he will turn many of the sons of Israel to
the Lord their God." For he himself was the instructor of Andrew the great,
this one was the teacher of John the Theologian, before they offered
themselves to the net. The apostles, having learned from him the saying:
"Behold the lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world," say, following
him, "Rabbi, where do you remain?" eager to enter the tent of the uncontained.
10 "He will go before him in the spirit and power of Elijah," not in soul and
body. Away, away with the absurdity. These things the children of the Greeks
talk idly and fable, that souls pre-exist and pass from bodies to bodies, just
as those, being brain-struck, say, stringing upon themselves a crowd of
superfluous and vulgar words. But the Creator gave to each soul its own body,
whence John, even if he is called a second Elijah and because of zeal toward
that one, nevertheless is not in soul and body; but he who gave to that one
the energy in spirit, this one also gives to John the Baptist and Forerunner a
measure of gift such as that one received. He who supplied the power to the
ancient one, this one promises to supply it to the new.
11 This one "will turn hearts of fathers to children and the disobe 11 dient
in the prudence of the righteous." "Fathers" is Israel, "children" the nations
are called because of the time, but the fathers turn toward the children,
because the children take to themselves the fountain of life, and as
well-disposed and faithful they appropriate the supper of joy, and having
anticipated the table of the light they sit around it. Whence those, though
called "fathers," are fittingly styled "disobedient"; for concerning them it
has been proclaimed to Isaiah the prophet: "I know that you are hard, and your
neck is an iron sinew, and your forehead bronze," so that he may interpret the
unbending of the synagogue and the extreme disobedience of its heirs. And
"righteous" are those from the nations; for even if for a little they were
ignorant of righteousness, yet now they are taught it with zeal; even if they
did not know the door of piety, yet they <now> running into the bride-chamber
bring themselves in with joy; even if they were far from the light, yet now
they behold it, the whole sun, with pure eyes.
12 And what else does Gabriel promise that John will accomplish, "to prepare
for the Lord a people made ready"? Whom did anyone make ready, if not
altogether Christ? For he who formed re-formed; he who rolled the wheel from
the beginning, this one rolled it back later; he who said: "Let man be a
living soul," this one said: "I do not wish the sinner to die." And these
things Gabriel, having come down from the heavens, announced; and in these
words John, having been sown, came to be inside the belly of Elisabeth, and
anticipating the one in the womb he greets him from the womb, and he
theologizes the one in the womb while still held by the bonds of the womb, and
these things while being confined; but having been born he is sent into the
desert, so that he may be likened to the angels, so that he may converse with
God, so that he may become free of the smokes and tumults of life, so that he
may exercise manliness, so that from the perceptible beasts he may be taught
in what manner one must box against the hidden ones and fight against those
that escape notice.

13 And when age had been added and time was advancing, he becomes a diner at a
table that has no cook, nor hiring a cook; for he had wild honey and
altogether unseasoned; and locusts were relishes whose sacrifice was without
iron, whose slaughter without a knife had no need of cauldrons, did not want
coals. He had no need of seasoning from outside, but he possessed the
seasoning from himself. And camel's hairs prepared the garment for the
Baptist, so that the spirit may be warmed while the body is worn down, and as
the flesh is melted the worms of the pleasures may be put to death. But he
also had a leather belt about his loin. And the mortification of the pleasures
shone above gold, the sanctification flashed above electrum, the chastity hid
the rays of the many-shining stones. Having thus practiced philosophy he was
completed as a true ascetic and a harmless runner and a blameless soldier and
a just bridesman and a pious herald and a mediator and forerunner, "abstaining
from every evil thing whatsoever."
14 How many times did the devil, having seen him philosophizing such things,
become jealous? But nevertheless he was not able to slander him. And what,
though thirsting and longing and needing so greatly? What was he going to
slander of the athlete, what to scoff of the soldier, what to mock of the one
holding nothing in his hands? What plenty was he to relate of the one having
no kinship with the prosperity of life? He did not have sons to slander whom
he did not sow, nor daughters of whom he was not father, nor fields which he
did not have, nor houses which he did not raise, asses or oxen or camels of
which he acquired nothing, a wife whom he did not know, did not have, did not
marry. Let John therefore be hymned, I say again, because he distributed to
all fittingly the preaching of salvation. For he commanded the soldiers to be
content with their wages, and the tax-collectors to take nothing more than the
appointed taxes, the people to be shown a short road of life toward Christ,
the one who has to give to the one who has not, the one who abounds to provide
the surplus to the one who lacks, so that the body may not lack, the members
contributing to one another and supplying the things needed.
15 This people was hastening in a throng to the spring of baptism, and after
them the Redeemer himself, wishing as a shepherd to follow the sheep and to
share in the spring which he himself showed to the lambs. The Baptist's
fingers quivered, the hands grew numb, each of the members of his array from
head to feet trembled. And neither being able as a servant to contradict the
master, nor having room to minister to so great a command, he did not dare to
refuse the one commanding great things. "I have need to be baptized by you,
and do you come to me?" How, he says, shall I wash the clean? And in what
manner shall I hold the uncontained? How shall I whiten the light? How shall I
scour the sun? What need has he of my hand? What need have you of a tongue
which you yourself fashioned of clay? What shall I say to you as I baptize?
What shall I utter to the Jordan itself? What to the dove of the Spirit coming
over of its own accord from the heavens? What to the cloud on which the
proclamation of your Father is carried? Do you not know what I am, Master? And
do you not know why I have come?"
16 "I have need to be baptized by you, and do you come to me?" For were you
"conceived in lawlessnesses," as I was? For did "your mother conceive you in
sins," or did the birth have seed? Did the pregnancy have corruption or the
conception a coupling? Did the womb have a bridegroom or the belly a farmer?
Was the milk from a man? Did anyone handle the breast of your mother? Did the
virgin fall under the curse of Eve?" But what did the Redeemer add to him?
"Let it be now; for thus it is fitting for us to fulfill all righteousness.
Let it be now," that is, why do you strip before the time the things that are
hidden? Why do you uncover the things that still need to be hidden until the
time? Why

do you trouble the hearing of the Jews? Why do you shoot the ears of the
devil? Why do you drive the demons from the earth before the cross? Why do you
make cowardly those who are going to minister to the sacrifice of the cross
even against their will?"
17 "Let it be now." Why do you greatly damage yourself? Why do you deprive the
exceeding nature? If I am not baptized, how will you hear a voice? If you do
not hear a voice, how will you learn the will of the Father, how will you be
bold for the mysteries of the Son, how will you behold the arrival of the
Spirit, how will you see the heaven being opened? If I am not baptized, who
will sanctify the water of baptism, who will crush the heads of the dragon,
how are the elements made white, how is the creation purified, how are the
soiled vessels of Adam washed off? "Let it be now; for thus it is fitting for
us to fulfill all righteousness," which the law commands and the prophets
preach and grace introduces and the new proclamation of the kingdom is sought.
I was born of a woman and the debt of Eve has been paid; I wore the form of a
slave and I freed Adam from the obligation; I sat at a manger and the one who
had become beast-like was loosed from the curse and has been released from the
shame; I put on swaddling-clothes and I healed the wound of the nakedness. For
the sake of Israel I was circumcised; behold, again I am baptized for the
nations, and thus all righteousness is sealed by my laws together and by my
works."
18 Such then was the power also of the things uttered by the Word and God; and
he himself, having taken courage at the ministry of the mystery and having
become higher at the vision of the dove and having acquired still more
brilliant boldness by the voice of the Father, both moved his tongue against
tyrants and convicted Herod as he was acting lawlessly, and he speaks to the
king with a great voice: "It is not lawful for you to have the wife of Philip
your brother." If you do not keep the law, you will not be able to have the
scepter. If you transgress the commandment, put off the purple. If you despise
God, come down from the throne which you received from him. "Do not exalt
yourself in the arms," he says; "for the laws guard the king. Do not neglect
virtue; for it is more seemly than a chrysolite platform." "It is not lawful
for you to have the wife of Philip your brother."
19 "Spare the succession of the race," he says. "Do not corrupt the order of
the royal seed. Tell me, of whom do you wish the one born from Herodias, who
is being committed adultery with by you, to be called, of the former
bridegroom or of the second adulterer, of the one who went up the bed lawfully
or rather of the one who seized it unlawfully? Shall we call the one born from
her a son of Philip? The one born is alien to him. Do you then wish him to be
named an offspring of Herod? But the field is not yours, the tilth sown by you
unlawfully is another's." Yet the tyrant does not endure the counsel, but he
gives bonds as a wage to the teacher. And he sent the Baptist to the prison
and was in birth-pangs of the murder. He wished to thrust the sword and he was
rehearsing the slaughter and, being ashamed of the people, he delayed. He was
therefore seeking, as is likely, a time for the unlawful drama and imagining a
timely day for the untimely cutting.
20 And when there came the birthday of Herod, who ought in no way to have been
born into the world nor to have come forth into this common stadium of the
human polity, nor to have enjoyed the elements, nor to have been well-treated
by the creation, nor to have used the moon for wantonness, to consume the sun
besides, to insult the course of the stars, to slander the measures of the day
and the limits of the night; for neither was he ashamed as he acted lawlessly
by day, nor did he spare the night as he was wanton, he was not abashed at the
solar course, overpassing the bounds of the Maker; and when the birthday was

nevertheless being kept of the one who harmed the races and insulted the royal
birth, "the daughter of Herodias danced in the midst and pleased Herod; whence
with an oath he swore to give her whatever she should ask. And she, being put
forward by her mother: 'Give me,' she says, 'here on a platter the head of
John the Baptist.' And the king was grieved; but because of the oaths and
those reclining with him, he commanded it to be given and, having sent, he
beheaded John in the prison. And his head was brought on a platter and was
given to the girl; and she brought it to her mother. And his disciples, having
come, took the body and buried it, and having come they announced it to
Jesus."
21 Alas, of how many evils have women filled the life of men, how many thorns
they sowed for us and farmed thistles. A woman closed paradise to the
first-formed, women brought the flood upon the race of the giants; "For
seeing," he says, "the sons of God the daughters of men that they are fair,"
they went in to them and the Lord was angered in wrath and said: "I have
repented that I made man and I will blot out every stature from the face of
the earth." A woman tempted the great patriarch Joseph, and she made the
prophet keep company with bonds, and she prepared the God-bearing one to sit
in the prison as an adulterer, to carry a heavy collar on the neck as a
corrupter. A woman delivered Samson captive to the foreigners. A woman forced
Elijah to become a runaway of the whole land of Israel.
22 A woman now dances and blinds the banquet and stupefies the tyrant with the
unlawful spectacle. For she had a plaiting of hair netting the young, a
painting of the eyes tripping those who looked. She sprinkled rouge upon the
virtues, and for those who met her the hook was not bearable. She opened the
mouth, and she belched death from the tongue. She bent back the neck, and the
spectacle was a sponsor of corruption. She bared the breast, and she offered
the foregates of Hades to be seen by those who looked curiously. She bared the
thighs, drawing up from the feet the little tunic of the varied spectacle, and
serpents hissing from the flanks came forth to corruption for the theater. And
the nod was deceitful, the glance filthy, the utterance corruption-making, the
movement soul-harming, the kiss death-bearing.
23 Yet Herod, having been caught, swore to give whatever the wicked
dancing-girl should ask. "And she, being put forward by her mother: Give me,
she says, here on a platter the head of John the Baptist." You see how great
is the woman-mad pleasure. You perceive how much the corruption enslaves the
one caught, how the one slackened by drunkenness no longer knows the scepter,
does not remember the authority, lies a plaything for all. For what did the
dancing-girl utter to him? "Give me here at once on a platter the head of John
the Baptist." And these are not the words of one entreating, but of one
commanding; for she does not approach the king as a girl with fear or shame or
some shrinking, but as a mistress to a servant, as one having authority of
bonds to a prisoner. "Give me, she says, on a platter the head of John the
Baptist." Do not delay the request. Here I need the slaughter of the prophet,
here let the murder of the one convicting the adultery be done. Where you
beheld my nakedness, here I exact punishment of the one who made a triumph of
it."
24 "Give me the head of John on a platter"; for Herodias commanded, the
adulteress ordered: "If you do not give, you do not have the one committing
adultery together with you; the bed after the wine will be widowed for you.
Withdrawing from the couch, with whom will you come together? The mother is
ashamed of the corruption, while John trumpets, she respects the servants, she
is abashed at the bodyguards. To all the queen is a byword because of the
boldness of John

becomes. All make a tragedy of her drama, and the Baptist, the cause of the
tragedy,
having leapt forward moved the tongue of all. This one put forth the trumpet
against us; first, not shrinking, he began to post the queen as an adulteress.
Moses sleeps, and he speaks through him; the law is asleep, and John awakens
it against us; he calls the queen an adulteress, and he urged many to follow
and to become bold."
25 "Give me therefore the head of John the Baptist." "Give"; for if you do not
give, we do not need to take; and the executioner, having been ordered by us,
will go over of his own accord to the murder. All know you as a slave of the
adulteress, a captive of the dancing-girl. The couch bears witness to these
things, the banquet bears witness, the wine, the drunkenness, the compact of
the oaths, the unbridled promise of the king. "Give me therefore here on a
platter the head of John the Baptist"; for I thirst to drink his blood and to
chew the members, to see with the royal relishes the head of the one railing
at the palace. I must bring this still gasping to my mother as a guest-gift
from the royal table, since Herodias awaits this portion of the dinner from
your birthday." And these things the girl.
26 "And the king, having been grieved because of the oaths and those reclining
with him, commanded it to be given." Will you accept the grief of Herod? Will
you also deem the tyrant worthy of pardon? For it was possible, if he wished,
to turn back the dancing-girl. It was possible, if he wanted, to refuse the
oath. What then? If Herodias happened to be demanding the eyes of the tyrant,
was the king going to sit on the throne blind? What then? If she wished to
dock the ear and to cut off the nostrils, did the tyrant become a monster all
at once? And what again? If she ordered the hands to be cut off, did the
scepter also fall from the fingers? By no means.
27 But to the girl he ought, as was likely, to say: "I swore to provide
whatever you ask that is mine; for I thought to expect bridal pearls, or that
you would ask a field planted with ten thousand plants, or that you would long
for a city which I have as choice of my kingdom. But now you ask nothing of
mine, and you seek what I am not able to provide, you seek a prophet's head.
And how can the king provide you the things of the Maker? If you asked for the
heaven, the king is not liable to the oaths, or the earth or the sea? By no
means; for it is not in me to give the things above me, which do not belong to
my authority and lie above my hands." None of these things does Herod utter,
but he is grieved; for the conscience was goading him. And the natural law
within was troubling him. Besides this he was in agony at the crowd and he
feared the multitude; for they held the Baptist in wonder for many reasons.
But nevertheless, since he was in birth-pangs of the murder and thirsted for
the slaughter and thought the death of the prophet his own freedom, being put
to shame by the oaths he plays a part and he sends the executioner to minister
to the command of the girl.
28 And thus the head of the Baptist is brought on a platter, the beauty of the
prophets, the boldness of the word, the grace of repentance, the many-membered
pipe, the God-sounding trumpet, a tongue that moves the clouds, eyes that see
the unseen things, ears that hear purely the mysteries of God, a head that
touched the head of all; and which, as it was given to the girl, a leopardess
carried the sacrifice of a lamb, a she-wolf carried about the head of a royal
lion. And she brought to her mother the accuser who did not even after death
cease from the convictions, nor after the slaughter let go of the boldness.
But the prophet has fallen asleep, and the word has been distributed
everywhere; the trumpet ceased, and the theaters of the inhabited world are
full of the sounds from it; the tongue is gone into earth, but the utterances
from it filled the tribes; it thundered upon the nations, sounding stronger
than thunders in each generation; for from that

having begun they ferried across into all the earth the saying: "Behold the
lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world." But do you by no means run
down the whole world from one woman, nor, because some one vessel is now
filthy, throw down the whole pottery of the Maker.
29 For even if a woman closed paradise, yet a woman again opened the
bride-chamber of the kingdom. Women brought the flood upon the race of the
giants, but women opened the royal platform to the choir of the apostles. A
woman tempted Joseph, but a woman nourished Elisha. A woman bound Samson, but
a woman was general to Barak. A woman sent Elijah a fugitive into the desert,
but a woman fed the same man as he was worn down by famine. A woman tripped
Peter,
and a woman became a patroness of Paul, as he himself boasts. Do you think the
word grows weary wanting to advocate for a woman? But it does not grow weary,
and with a swift foot it carries off the prizes. A woman was in birth-pangs of
our Redeemer. She bore and nursed the Maker of the race, she gave drink to the
nourisher of the creation, she carried in her arms the one who improvised
springs for the creation, "who measured the heaven with a span and the earth
with a handful," who ruled and sealed the sea with the palm; to him the glory
and the might unto the ages of the ages. Amen.
