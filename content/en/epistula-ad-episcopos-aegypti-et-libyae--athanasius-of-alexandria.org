* Letter to the Bishops of Egypt and Libya
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of our father among the saints Athanasius, Archbishop of Alexandria, to the
bishops of Egypt and Libya, an encyclical letter against the Arians.

All things that our Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ, as Luke wrote, both did and
taught, he accomplished having appeared for our salvation; for he came, as
John says, not that he might judge the world, but that the world might be
saved through him. And among all things this too is to be admired of his
goodness, that even concerning those who fight against us he did not keep
silence, but even strongly foretold, so that whenever those things come, we
may at once be found having our mind secured from his teaching, as he says:
There shall arise false =25.540= prophets and false christs, and they shall
give great signs and wonders, so as to deceive, if possible, even the elect;
behold, I have told you beforehand. For the lessons and gifts stored up in us
from him are many and beyond man; for they are a type of heavenly citizenship,
and power against demons, and adoption as sons, and the gift above every gift
and exceptional, the knowledge of the Father and of the Word himself, and the
gift of the Holy Spirit. But the mind of men is carefully set upon evil
things; and moreover our adversary the devil, envying the so many goods that
have come to us, goes about seeking to snatch the seeds of the Word that are
in us. For this reason then, sealing the lessons in us as his own treasures
through the prediction, the Lord said: See that no one deceive you. For many
will come in my name, saying: I am he; and the time has drawn near, and they
will deceive many; do not therefore go after them. The Word has given us a
great gift, so as not to be deceived from what appears, but rather, even if
these things happen to be veiled, to discern by the grace of the Spirit. For
since the inventor of wickedness and great demon, the devil, is wholly
hateful, and merely appearing he is struck down by all, as a serpent, as a
dragon, as a lion seeking to snatch some and swallow them; for this reason
what he himself is, he hides and covers, and the name desired by all he
assumes with cunning, so that, having deceived by the appearance, he may then
pierce those who have been led astray with his own bonds. And as if someone,
wishing to kidnap others' children, while the parents are away, were to assume
their faces, and deceive the sons who long for them, and then leading them far
away, destroy them; in the same way the malignant and crooked devil, having no
boldness of his own, but knowing men's love for the truth, assumes this in
appearance, and casts his own poison on those who have followed him. Thus he
also deceived Eve, not speaking his own things =25.541= but assuming the words
of God, while falsifying their meaning; thus he also put forward the wife of
Job, persuading her to assume love of her husband, while teaching her to
blaspheme against God; thus the deceitful one plays with men by appearances,
stealing and dragging each into his own pit of wickedness. Long ago then,
having deceived the first man Adam, and thinking through him to have all under
his hand, he leaped about boldly saying: I will take the whole inhabited world
in my hand as a nest, and as eggs left behind I will take them up, and there
is none who will escape me, or speak against me. But when the Lord sojourned,
and the enemy had experience of his dispensation in humanity, not being able
to deceive the flesh worn by him, then indeed

he who promised to take the whole inhabited world is from that time and
because of him played with even by children, as a sparrow, the proud one. For
a little child now putting his hand into a hole of asps laughs at him who
deceived Eve; and all who believe rightly in the Lord trample him who said: I
will set my throne above the clouds, I will go up, I will be like the Most
High. Thus then he suffers with shame; nevertheless, even if the shameless one
dares again to disguise himself, yet even now more the wretched one is known
by those who bear the sign on the forehead, and more terribly he is turned
away, humbled and put to shame. For even if now creeping as a serpent he
transform himself into an angel of light, yet he will not have the hypocrisy
for profit; for we have been taught that even if an angel from heaven
evangelize us contrary to what we received, he is anathema. And if again he
hide his own lie, and assume to speak the truth through lips; yet not being
ignorant of his thoughts, we can say the things said against him by the
Spirit: But =25.544= to the sinner God said: Why do you declare my statutes?
and, Praise is not seemly in the mouth of a sinner. For not even when speaking
the truth is the cunning one trustworthy. And this Scripture showed,
recounting his craft against Eve in paradise; and the Lord also convicted him,
first on the mountain, dividing the folding of his breastplate, and showing
who the deceitful one is, and convicting that he is not one of the saints, but
is Satan who is tempting, in saying: Get behind me, Satan; for it is written:
You shall worship the Lord your God, and him only shall you serve; and also
when he silenced the demons crying from the tombs. For what was said by them
was true, and they did not then lie saying that You are the Son of God, and
the holy one of God; but he did not wish the truth to be brought forth through
an unclean mouth and especially through those, lest, mixing their own will of
wickedness under pretext of this, they sow it among men who are asleep. For
this reason he himself did not endure them speaking such things, nor wishing
us to endure such, he commanded through himself, Beware, saying, of false
prophets, who come to you in sheep's clothing, but inwardly are ravening
wolves; and through the holy apostles: Do not believe every spirit. For such
is the manner of the opposing energy; and such are the gatherings of the
heresies; for each having as father of its own invention the devil who from
the beginning turned and became a man-slayer and liar, and being ashamed to
bring forward his hateful name, assumes the good name of the Saviour, which is
above every name, and wraps itself in the words of the Scriptures. And it says
the words, but steals the true meaning; and then, having covered the invention
it fashioned as in some deceit, it too becomes a man-slayer of those who are
led astray. For from where does Marcion and Manichaeus have the Gospel,
denying the law? For from the old the new, and the new bears witness to the
old. Those then who deny these, how will they confess the things from them?
For Paul became apostle of a Gospel =25.545= which he promised beforehand
through his prophets in holy Scriptures; And the Lord himself said: Search the
Scriptures, for they are they that bear witness concerning me; How then will
they confess the Lord, not first searching the Scriptures concerning him? For
him whom Moses and the prophets wrote, this one the disciples say they have
found. And what is the law to Sadducees who do not receive the prophets? For
the God who gave the law, himself promised in the law also to raise up
prophets, so that the same is Lord both of the law and of the prophets, and he
who denies one of them denies entirely the other also. And what is the Old to
Jews any longer, who have not

recognized the Lord expected from it? For if they believed Moses' writings,
they would have believed the Lord's words. For concerning me, he says, he
wrote. And what are the Scriptures to the Samosatene, denying the Word of God,
and the incarnate presence of the Word signified and shown from both the Old
and New Covenant? And what are the Scriptures to the Arians, and why do these
put them forward, men who say the Word of God is a creature, and, as Greeks,
worshipping the creation rather than the God who created? For toward the
particular impiety of its invention each of these heresies has nothing in
common with the Scriptures; and this even those who champion their things
know, that they are much, or rather wholly, contrary to the mind of each of
those; but for the deception of the simpler, such as those of whom it is
written in Proverbs: The innocent believes every word; they pretend to study
and speak the words, as their father the devil, so that from the words they
may seem to have also the mind right, and then persuade the wretched men to
think contrary to the Scriptures. Indeed in each of the heresies the devil,
having thus disguised himself, put forward words full of deceit; for
concerning these the Lord said that There shall arise false christs and false
prophets, so as to deceive many; therefore the devil came, saying through
each: I am the Christ, and with me is the truth; and the slanderer has made
all of them, privately and in common, to lie. And the =25.548= paradox is that
all the heresies, fighting against one another concerning the evils each
fashioned, have been bound to one another in lying alone; for they have one
father who sowed the lie in all. The faithful then and disciple of the Gospel,
having grace to discern spiritual things, and having built the house of his
faith upon the rock, stands firm and remains safe from their deceit; but the
simple, as I said before, and not strongly catechized, such a one looking only
at what is said, and not considering the meaning, is at once drawn away by
their methods. For this reason it is good and necessary to pray to receive the
gift of discernment of spirits, so that each may know according to John's
command whom he ought to reject, and whom to receive, as friends and being of
the same faith. One might write much, if he wished to work these things out in
detail, for the impiety and ill-mindedness of the heresies will appear much
and various, and very terrible the cunning of those who deceive. But since the
divine Scripture is more sufficient than all, for this reason, having advised
those who wish to know much about these things to meet with the divine words,
I myself have now been eager to make clear the urgent matter, for which
especially I have thus written. I heard while staying in these parts, for
genuine brethren and of the right opinion reported, that some of those who
think the things of Arius, having come together, have written ostensibly
concerning faith, as they wished, and wish to send it to you, so that either
you may subscribe according to what seems good to them, or rather according as
the devil inspired them; or the one who contradicts may become an exile. For
they are already beginning to harass the bishops of these parts also. From
this then the manner of those who write becomes clear. For those who =25.549=
write thus, so that the end of their letters has exile and other punishments,
what would such men be but strangers to Christians, and friends of the devil
and of his demons? especially that, while the most God-fearing emperor
Constantius is humane, they spread abroad what they themselves wish contrary
to his mind; for they do this too with much cunning, and especially for two of
these reasons, as it seems to me; one, so that, if you also subscribe, they
may seem to put away the ill-name of Arius, and themselves escape notice as
not thinking the things of Arius; and the other, so that writing these things
they may seem again to hide the synod that took place at

Nicaea, and the faith set forth in it against the Arian heresy. But this too
rather convicts their malice and heterodoxy; for if they believed rightly,
they would have been content with the faith set forth at Nicaea by the whole
ecumenical synod; and if they thought themselves slandered and called Arians
in vain, they ought not to have been eager to change the things written
against Arius, lest the things written against him be defined as against them.
But now they do not do this, but as themselves being Arius they fight for
themselves. For consider how truth is not a care to these, but they both say
and do all things for the Arian heresy. For those who dare to slander the
things well defined, and attempt to write other things besides those, what
else do they do but accuse the Fathers, and champion the heresy against which
those both came together and declared? For even now what they write, they
write not caring for truth, as I said before, but rather playing and doing
this by art, for the deception of men, so that in the sending about of the
letters they may occupy the ears of the peoples with these things, and
themselves gain the time of being accused, and having escaped notice as being
impious, may have room to extend the heresy, as a gangrene, having pasture
everywhere. They move and disturb all things, then, and not even so are they
content with their own; for yearly, as those who write wills, they themselves
also coming together, pretend to write concerning faith, so that in this too
they may incur laughter and shame =25.552= rather, because not by others, but
by themselves their own things are thrown out. For if they trusted what they
wrote first, they would not have sought to write second things; nor again
leaving those would they now write these which, having practiced this
thoroughly, they will again change, if a very little time pass, and they take
a pretext for plotting against some as is their custom. For then, whenever
they plot, they especially pretend to write concerning faith, so that, as
Pilate washed his hands, so these too by their doing may kill those who are
pious toward Christ; and so that, seeming to define concerning faith, as I
have often said, they may seem to flee the charge of heterodoxy. But they will
be able neither to escape notice, nor to flee; for as long as they apologize
to themselves, they always become accusers of themselves, and justly; for they
do not answer those who convict them, but persuade themselves as they wish.
When then, the one liable judging himself, is there a release of the charge?
For this reason then they always write, and always changing their own things,
they have an unclear faith, or rather they have a manifest unbelief and
ill-mindedness. And they seem to me to suffer this reasonably. For since,
having departed from the truth, and wishing to overturn the faith written well
at Nicaea, they have loved, according to what is written, to move their own
feet; for this reason these also, as Jerusalem of old, have toiled and labour
in the changes, writing different things at different times, only that they
may gain the times, and remain Christ-fighters deceiving men. Who then, to
whom truth especially is a care, will still wish to endure these? Who writing
will not justly turn away from them? Who would not condemn their daring? For
being few in number, they wish their own things to prevail over all; and
wishing the gatherings of themselves that take place in corners and are
suspect to prevail, they force to dissolve and annul the ecumenical, genuine
=25.553= and pure synod that took place; and men, advanced by those around
Eusebius because they advocate the Christ-fighting heresy, dare to define
concerning faith, and owing to be judged as liable, they themselves also
attempt to judge, as those around Caiaphas; and they wish the Thalia to be
believed, they who do not even know how they believe. For who does not know
that because of the madness of Arius Secundus the

Pentapolitan, who of old was often deposed, was received by them, and George,
who is now in Laodicea, was put forward, and Leontius the eunuch, and before
him Stephen, and Theodore who is in Heraclea? For also previously deposed from
the presbyterate, later because of the impiety Ursacius and Valens were called
bishops, who also at the beginning, as younger men, were catechized by Arius;
and Acacius and Patrophilus and Narcissus, the most daring toward all impiety;
these then were also deposed in the great synod that took place at Sardica;
and Eustathius who is now in Sebasteia, and Demophilus and Germinius, and
Eudoxius, and Basil, being advocates of the impiety, were advanced to this.
For concerning the present Cecropius, and the so-called Auxentius, and
Epictetus the hypocrite, it is even superfluous to speak, it being clear to
all how and on what pretexts and by which enemies these also were put forward
to assume the slanders against the orthodox bishops who were plotted against;
that these also, although being from eighty monasteries, and not known by the
peoples, nevertheless because of the impiety procured for themselves the name
of bishop. For this reason then, having hired a certain George from
Cappadocia, they now wish to send him to you. But there is not even any
account of this man. For there is a report in these parts =25.556= that this
man is not a Christian at all, but rather has been zealous for the things of
the idols, and is in manner an executioner; wherefore they took him on being
such, so that he might be able to wrong, to snatch, to murder; for these
things he has practiced more, and he does not know at all the proper things of
the faith in Christ. Those men then contrive such things against the truth;
but the mind of these will be evident to all, even if ten thousand times they
attempt to slip away like eels and to escape notice as Christ-fighters. For
this reason I exhort, let none of you be deceived, let none of you be snatched
away; but rather, as Jewish impiety is coming against the faith in Christ, all
be zealous for the Lord; and each holding the faith from the Fathers, which
also those who gathered at Nicaea recalled in writing, let him not endure
those who attempt to innovate against it. For even if they write the words
from the Scriptures, do not endure those who write; even if they utter the
words of orthodoxy, not even so give heed to those who speak. For they do not
speak with a right mind, but wrapping the words about themselves as a sheep's
clothing, they think within the things of Arius, as the leader of the
heresies, the devil. For he too spoke the things from the Scriptures, but was
silenced by the Saviour. For if what he said he also thought, he would not
have fallen from the heavens; but now, having fallen in mind, the cunning one
assumes in words; and often even through Greek cleverness, and with its
sophisms the ill-willed one tries to deceive. If then the things written were
from orthodox men, such as would have been from the great confessor Hosius,
and Maximinus of Gaul, or from him who succeeded him, or from Philogonius and
Eustathius of the East, or Julius and Liberius the bishops of Rome, or
Cyriacus of Mysia, or Pistus and Aristaeus the bishops from Greece, or
Silvester and Protogenes of Dacia, or Leontius and Eupsychius the =25.557=
bishops of Cappadocia, or Caecilianus of Africa, or Eustorgius of Italy, or
Capito of Sicily, or Macarius of Jerusalem, or Alexander of Constantinople, or
Paederotus of Heraclea, or the great Meletius and Basil, and Longianus, and
those with them from Armenia and Pontus, or Lupus and Amphion from Cilicia, or
James and those with him from Mesopotamia, or our own blessed Alexander, and
those of like mind with them, there would have been nothing to suspect in the
things written; for guileless and simple is the manner of apostolic men.

But since the things written are from those hired for a fee to advocate the
heresy, and according to the divine proverb, The words of the impious are
deceitful, and, The mouth of the impious answers evils, and, The impious steer
deceits; it is necessary to watch, as the Lord said, brethren, and to be
sober, lest some deceit come from the cleverness and cunning, lest someone
come in the name saying, I too proclaim Christ, and after a little be known as
Antichrist. And these are antichrists, as many as come to you because of the
madness of Arius. For what is lacking among you, that someone from outside
should sojourn with you? Or of what do the Churches throughout Egypt and the
Libyas and Alexandria have need, that, trafficking the episcopate instead of
timber and cargoes, they should invade Churches that do not belong to them?
Who does not know, who does not see clearly, that they do all these things for
the establishment of the impiety? For this reason, even if they make
themselves mute, even if they bind about themselves greater fringes than the
Pharisees, and broaden themselves with utterances, and practice the pitch of
the voice, they ought not to be believed; for not the diction, but the mind
and the conduct with piety constitute the faithful. For this reason the
Sadducees =25.560= and the Herodians, although having the law through lips,
were put to shame hearing from the Saviour: You err, not knowing the
Scriptures nor the power of God. And it has been shown to all, that even those
who seemed to speak the law were convicted of being heretics in mind and
God-fighters. Others then they deceived saying such things; but the Lord
become man they were not able to deceive; for the Word became flesh, who knows
the reasonings of men, that they are vain; for thus he also convicted Jews
snatching, saying: If God were your Father, you would love me; for I came
forth from the Father, and have come to you; Thus these too seem to me to do
now; for they hide what they think, and then for writing they use the words
from the Scriptures, so that having baited with them they may draw those who
do not know into their own wickedness. See whether it is not so. For if, with
nothing underlying, they write concerning faith, the attempt is superfluous,
and perhaps even harmful, because, there being no inquiry, they themselves
furnish a pretext for word-fighting, unsettling the innocent hearts of the
brethren, and sowing beside what never even came into their mind; but if they
attempt to write apologizing because of the Arian heresy, they ought first to
take away the seeds of the evils that have grown, and to pillory those who
furnished the seeds, and thus write rightly the things instead of those, or
openly avenge the things of Arius, so that not hiddenly but openly they may be
shown Christ-fighters, and all may flee them as from the face of a serpent.
But now they hide those things too, and pretending they write concerning
others; and as a physician called to one wounded and ill, and entering should
say nothing concerning the wounds, but should converse concerning the healthy
members, he would be condemned of much stupor, because what he entered for, he
is silent, and of the others, of which there is no need of him, he converses;
in the same way these also leave the things of their heresy, and attempt to
write other things. But they ought, if they were caring concerning faith, and
loved Christ, first =25.561= to take away beforehand the blasphemous words
against him, and thus instead of those to speak and write the healthy words.
But neither do they themselves do this, nor do they allow those who wish to do
it, either being ignorant, or using an art of cunning. If then being ignorant
they suffer this, they would be charged with rashness, because they affirm
concerning what they do not know; but if knowing what they pretend, the
condemnation against them is greater, because when deliberating concerning
their own affairs they set nothing as a side-work; but when writing concerning
the faith in our Lord they play, and do all things rather than speak truth;
and they hide the things for which their heresy is charged, and they speak the
words from the Scriptures. And this is

theft of the truth outright, and full of all injustice; and this I well know
that your piety also will be able to see well from these things. For no one
charged concerning adultery apologizes concerning theft, nor does anyone
prosecuting a charge of murder endure the accused, if they should apologize
saying, we did not swear falsely, but we also kept the deposit; for this is
rather a game than a release of a charge and a demonstration of truth. For
what is murder to a deposit? or what is adultery to theft? For even if the
evils hold to one another, going out from the same heart, yet as toward
releasing the offense, they have no communion with one another for this. Achar
then, as it is written in Joshua the son of Nun, being charged concerning
theft, did not apologize putting forward goodwill in the wars, but being
convicted in the theft, was stoned by all. And Saul too, being charged with
neglect and lawlessness, gained nothing by making excuses in other things. For
neither does the apology in other things work a release of other charges; but
if indeed one must do all things lawfully and justly, it is fitting that
concerning what one is charged, concerning these he also apologize, and either
show that he has not done it, or confess, if he should be convicted, no longer
to do nor to do the same things. But if he has done and committed, and
=25.564= should neither confess, but even hiding, should say other things
instead of others; it is clear that he has not acted rightly, but even himself
is conscious of acting unlawfully. And what need of long words, when these
themselves are accusers of the Arian heresy? For if they do not dare to say,
but rather hide those words of blasphemy, they are clearly knowing this heresy
to be foreign and alien to the truth. But since they themselves hide and fear
to speak, it is necessary for us to strip off the covering of the impiety, and
to exhibit the heresy, knowing what those around Arius then said, and how they
were cast out from the Church, and deposed from the clergy; having first asked
pardon, however, for the filthy words we are about to put forward; because not
thinking them, but convicting the heretics, we say these things. The blessed
Alexander the bishop then cast Arius out of the Church thinking and saying
these things: "God was not always Father, the Son was not always; but all
things being from things that are not, the Son of God also is from things that
are not; and all things being creatures, he also is a creature and a work; and
all things not being formerly, but coming into being later, the Word of God
himself also was once when he was not before he was begotten, but he has a
beginning of being; for then he came to be, when God willed to create him; for
he too is one of all the works; and that by nature he is changeable, but by
his own free will, as he wishes, he remains good; whenever however he wishes,
he too can change just as all things also. For this reason God, knowing
beforehand that he would be good, giving this glory to him beforehand, which
he would also have had from virtue afterwards; so that from his works, which
God knew beforehand, he has now become such." They say then that "Christ is
not even true God, but by participation he too, just as all the others also,
is called God." And they add this too, that "He is not himself the Word in the
Father by nature, and proper to his essence, and the proper Wisdom, in which
he has also made this world; but there is another Word proper to him in the
=25.565= Father, and another Wisdom proper to him in the Father, in which
wisdom he has also made this Word; but this Lord himself is called Word
notionally because of the rational beings, and is called Wisdom notionally
because of the things made wise." Indeed they say that "All things being
foreign and alien according to essence from the Father, so he too is foreign
and alien in all respects from the essence of the Father, and is proper and
one of the generated and created things; for he is a creature and a work and a
deed." And again they say that "He did not create us through him,

but him through us. For, they say, God was alone, and the Word was not with
him; then wishing to create us, then he made this one, and from when he came
to be, he named him Word, and Son, and Wisdom, so that he might create us
through him; and just as all things by the will of God, not being formerly,
came to subsist; so he too by the will of God, not being formerly, came to be.
For the Word is not a proper and natural offspring of the Father, but he too
has come to be by grace. For the God who is made the Son who is not by the
will, in which he has also made all things, and created, and formed, and
willed them to come to be." For they also say this, that "Christ is not the
natural and true power of God; but just as the locust and the palmerworm are
called power, so he too is called power of the Father." In addition he said
that "The Father is ineffable to the Son; and neither to see, nor to know
perfectly and accurately can the Son the Father. For having a beginning of
being, he cannot know the beginningless; but even what he knows and sees, he
knows analogously to his own measures, and sees, just as we also know and see
according to our own power." And he added this too, that "The Son not only
does not know his own Father accurately, but he does not even know his own
essence." Saying these and such things Arius was shown a heretic. And I, even
only writing, was purifying myself considering the things against these
=25.568= and holding the mind of piety. For =25.568= also in the synod at
Nicaea all the bishops who gathered from everywhere stopped their ears at
these things, and with one vote all also because of these condemned this
heresy, and anathematized, saying this to be alien and foreign to the
ecclesiastical faith. Necessity did not lead those who judged to this, but all
by choice avenged the truth; and they have done this justly and rightly. For
atheism is introduced from these things, and rather than the Scriptures a
Judaism, having Hellenism following close, so that he who thinks these things
is no longer even to be called a Christian; for all these things are contrary
to the Scriptures. For John says: In the beginning was the Word; but these
assert: He was not, before he was begotten. And he himself again wrote: And we
are in the true one, in his Son Jesus Christ; this is the true God and the
eternal life; But these as fighting against assert that Christ is not true
God, but by participation he too as all things is called God. And the Apostle
blames the Greeks, because they worship creatures, saying: They worshipped the
creation rather than the God who created; but these saying the Lord to be a
creature, and worshipping him as a creature, how do they differ from Greeks?
For how, if they think this, is the reading not also against them, and is the
blessed Paul not blaming them as well? And the Lord says: I and the Father are
one; and, He who has seen me has seen the Father; and the Apostle sent by him
to preach writes: Who being the radiance of the glory, and the impress of his
hypostasis; but these dare to divide, and to say him alien from the essence
and eternity of the Father, and the godless introduce him as changeable, not
seeing that, saying these things, they no longer make him one with the Father,
but with the creatures. For who does not see that the radiance is indivisible
from the light, and proper to it coexisting with it by nature, and has not
come into being later =25.569=? Then the Father saying, This is my beloved
Son, and the Scriptures saying this to be the Word of the Father, in whom also
the heavens were established, and simply all things came to be through him;
these, becoming inventors of new doctrines and fictions, introduce another
word, and

another wisdom of the Father to be, and this one to be called Word and Wisdom
notionally because of the rational beings, not seeing from this the absurdity.
For if because of us he is called notionally Word and Wisdom, what he himself
would be, they would not be able to say. For if the Scriptures say these
things he is, but these do not wish the Lord to be these, clearly they do not
even wish him to exist, the godless and contrary to the Scriptures. And this
the faithful can learn from the paternal voice itself, and from the angels who
worship him, and from the saints who wrote concerning him; but these, since
they do not have a pure mind, nor can they hear divine and theological men,
can at least learn from the demons like themselves, that they did not cry out
as of many being, but knowing this one only they said: You are the holy one of
God, and the Son of God. For even he who put the heresy under them, tempting
on the mountain, did not say: If you also are a son of God, as of others
being; but, If you are the Son of God, as of him alone being. But the
admirable ones, just as Greeks, having fallen from the conception concerning
the one God, descended into polytheism, so these too, not believing there is
one Word of the Father, have fallen into a conception of many; and they deny
the God who truly is and the true Word, and have dared even to think him a
creature, not seeing of how much impiety the mind is full. For if he is a
creature, how is he himself creator of the creatures =25.572=? or how is he
himself Son and Wisdom, and Word? For a word is not created, but begotten; and
the creature is not a son, but a work. And if the creatures came to be through
him, and he too is a creature; through whom then did he himself also come to
be? For it is necessary that the works come to be through someone, as indeed
they also came to be through the Word, because he was not a work, but Word of
the Father. And again, if there is another wisdom in the Father besides the
Lord, then Wisdom came to be in wisdom. And if the Word of God is Wisdom, the
Word would also have come to be in a word; and if the Word of God is the Son,
the Son would also have been made in a son. How then did the Lord say: I am in
the Father, and the Father in me, there being another in the Father, in whom
the Lord himself also came to be? And how does John, leaving that one, recount
concerning this one saying: All things came to be through him, and without him
came to be not even one thing? And if all things that came to be by will came
to be through him, how is he himself also one of the things that came to be?
And how, the Apostle saying, For whom are all things and through whom are all
things, do these say: Not we through him, but he came to be through us? For he
ought, if it were so, to have said, For whom the Word came to be. But now not
saying this, but, for whom are all things and through whom are all things, he
shows these to be heretics and slanderers. Besides, if they have dared that
there is another Word in God, since they do not have clear proofs from the
Scriptures, let them show even one work of his, or a work of the Father that
has come to be without this Word, so that they may seem to have some pretext
for their own invention. For of the true Word the works are clear to all, so
that from them he himself is analogously contemplated; for =25.573= as seeing
the creation, we think of the God who created it, so seeing nothing disorderly
in the things that have come to be, but all things moved and remaining in
order and providence, we consider there to be a Word of God who is over all
and governing. This the divine Scriptures also witness, saying that he himself
is the Word of God, and that through him all things came to be, and without
him came to be not even one thing. But of that one, concerning whom they
speak, neither a saying nor a work is shown by them. For even the Father
himself saying, This is my beloved Son, signifies there is no other besides
him. Therefore according to this the admirable ones have now also been added
to Manichaeans. For those too name a good God only as far as a name, and can
show neither a visible nor an invisible work of his; but denying the true God
who truly is, the maker of heaven and earth, and of all the invisible things,
they are altogether myth-tellers. And this these ill-minded ones too seem to
me to suffer.

For of the true Word and only one being in the Father they see the works, and
they deny this one, and they fashion for themselves another word, whom they
can demonstrate neither from works nor from hearing; unless perhaps they
mythologize, suspecting God as composite, as a man, speaking and exchanging
words, and again thus thinking and being made wise. They do not see into how
great unreason they have fallen saying these things. For if he has words that
supervene, they think this much a man; and if they are also brought forth from
him and then disappear, they are more impious, because they dissolve the
things from him who is into not being. For if they altogether suspect him to
generate, it is better and more pious to say that God is begetter of one Word,
who is the fullness of his godhead, in whom also the treasures of all
knowledge are hidden, and =25.576= to say this one both is and coexists with
his own Father, through whom also all things came to be; than to think God
Father of many who do not appear, or as composed from many, as subject to
human passions and various, to imagine him who is simple in nature. Then the
Apostle saying: Christ the power of God and the wisdom of God, these number
this one also among the many powers, and what is worst, that they compare him,
the lawless, to the locust and the other irrational things sent through him
for punishment. Then, the Lord saying: No one knows the Father except the Son,
and again, Not that anyone has seen the Father except he who is from the
Father; how are these not truly God-fighters, saying that the Father is
neither seen nor known perfectly by the Son? For if the Lord says, As the
Father knows me, I also know the Father; and the Father does not know the Son
in part; how are they not mad babbling, that in part and not fully the Son
knows the Father? Then if the Son has a beginning unto being, and all things
also have a beginning unto having come to be; which is first of which, let
them say. But they have nothing to say, nor can the cunning ones show such a
beginning of the Word. For he is a true and proper offspring of the Father,
and In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was
God. For concerning their saying that the Son does not know his own essence it
is superfluous to answer, except only to condemn their madness, if indeed the
Word does not know himself, who grants to all the knowledge concerning the
Father and himself, and blames those who do not know themselves. But it is
written, they say: The Lord created me a beginning of his ways for his works;
uneducated and thunderstruck in their wits! And he was called a servant in the
Scriptures, and son of a handmaid, and lamb and =25.577= sheep, and that he
has toiled, and thirsted, and was struck, and has suffered. But such things
again have the pretext and the reasonable cause near, signified from the
Scriptures. And this is, because he became man and Son of man, taking the form
of a servant, which was the human flesh. For the Word, says John, became
flesh. And if he became man, some must not be scandalized at such utterances;
for it is proper to man to be created, to come to be, to be formed, to toil,
to suffer, to die, to be raised from the dead. And just as being Word and
wisdom of the Father, he has all the things of the Father, the eternal, the
unchangeable, the like in all things and in all, the not former and later, but
the coexisting with the Father, and that he himself is the very form of the
godhead, and the creative, and the not being created; for being like according
to essence of the Father, he would not be created, but creator, as he himself
has said: My Father works until now, and I work; so having become man, and
wearing flesh, of necessity he is said to be created and made, and all the
proper things of the flesh, even if these, as Jewish hucksters, mix the wine
with water, humbling the Word, and

subjecting his godhead to the conceptions of generated things. For this reason
then reasonably and justly the Fathers were indignant, and anathematized this
most impious heresy, but these as easily convicted and everywhere unsound fear
and hide it. For we have written these things, few though they are, for their
condemnation; but if someone should wish to use a broader refutation against
them, he will find this heresy not far from Greeks, and last and dregs of the
other heresies. For those either wander concerning the body and the
incarnation =25.580= of the Lord, some thus, others thus lying, or thinking
the Lord not to have sojourned at all, as Jews thinking they have wandered;
but this alone more madly has dared against the godhead itself, saying the
Word not to be at all, nor the Father always to be Father. Whence reasonably
one might say that psalm to have been written against them: The fool said in
his heart, There is no God; they were corrupted and became abominable in their
practices. But we are strong, they say, and we are able by cunning to champion
the heresy. But their apology would become better, if they can champion this
not by some art nor by Greek sophisms, but by simple faith. Therefore if they
have dared and know this to be ecclesiastical, let them say the mind openly;
for no one having lit a lamp puts it under the bushel, but on the lampstand,
and it shines for all who enter. If then these too can champion, let them
write the things said before, and let them show to all, as a lamp, their own
heresy naked; and let them openly also accuse the blessed bishop Alexander, as
having unjustly cast out Arius saying these things, and let them blame the
synod at Nicaea, which instead of the impiety recalled and wrote the pious
faith. But they will not do it, I well know, because they are not so ignorant
of the evils they have fashioned and are contentious to sow; but they know
very well that, even if at the beginning they snatch the innocent through
empty deceit, yet at once their thought as a light of the impious is quenched,
and they are pilloried everywhere, as enemies of the truth. For this reason
then doing all things foolishly, and speaking as fools, this alone, as being
sons of this age, they have done prudently, hiding their own lamp under the
bushel, so that it may be thought to shine, and not appearing may be quenched
being condemned. For even Arius himself, the leader of the heresy, and
Eusebius' =25.581= partner, being then called by the zeal of those around
Eusebius before the blessed Constantine Augustus, and being required to say
his own faith in writing, the deceitful one wrote, hiding the proper words of
the impiety, and himself also assuming, as the devil, the words of the
Scriptures simple, and as they are written. Then the blessed Constantine
saying: "If you have nothing else besides these in your mind, give truth as
witness; for the Lord avenges one who has sworn falsely against you;" the
wretched one swore that he neither has, nor have other things besides those
now written even been said ever by him or been thought. But immediately going
out, as paying a penalty, he fell, and becoming prone he burst in the middle.
Now death is a common end of life to all men, and one must not insult, even if
the one who has died is an enemy, it being unclear lest until evening this
overtake them also; but the end of Arius, since it did not come to be simply,
for this reason is also worthy of a narrative. For those around Eusebius
threatening to bring him into the church, the bishop of Constantinople
Alexander was contradicting, but Arius was confident in the force and the
threats of Eusebius; for it was sabbath, and he expected to be gathered on the
next day. There was then much struggle, those threatening, and Alexander
praying; but the Lord becoming judge awarded against those who were wronging.
For not yet had the sun set,

and a necessity drawing him to a place, there he fell, and was immediately
deprived both of the communion and of living. And the blessed Constantine
immediately hearing wondered, seeing this one convicted as a perjurer; and it
became then clear to all, that the threats of those around Eusebius were
weakened, and the hope of Arius became vain. And it was shown again, =25.584=
that from the Saviour the Arian madness has become without communion both here
and in the Church of the firstborn in heaven. Who then would not wonder seeing
these unjustly contentious for those whom the Lord condemned, and seeing them
avenge the heresy, which the Lord convicted as without communion, not allowing
the leader of it to enter into the church, and not fearing what is written,
but attempting impossible things? For what the holy God has purposed, who will
scatter; and whom God has condemned, who is he that justifies? Let those then,
as avenging their own fantasies, write what they wish; but you, brethren, as
bearing the vessels of the Lord, and avenging the doctrines of the Church, I
exhort, become testers of the matter; and if they write other words besides
the aforementioned words of Arius, from this condemn them as hypocrites,
hiding the poison of the mind, but fawning like the serpent with the words
from lips. For writing these things they have with them those then cast out
with Arius, Secundus the Pentapolitan, and the clerics once convicted in
Alexandria, and they write to these in Alexandria; and what is wonderful, they
have made us and those with us to be persecuted, although the most pious
emperor Constantine sent us with peace to the fatherland and the church, and
caring for the concord of the peoples; but they have now caused the churches
to be handed over to these, and have shown to all, that for the sake of these
the whole plot from the beginning against us and against the others has come
to be. How then, doing these things, can they writing these things be
believed? For if they wrote rightly, they would blot out the Thalia which
Arius wrote, and =25.585= they would have cast out the offshoots of the
heresy; for these have become disciples and partners both of the impiety and
of the censure of Arius; therefore, not casting these out, they are evident to
all as not having a right mind, even if they write ten thousand times. For
this reason it is fitting to watch, lest some deceit come from the wrapping of
the letters, and they carry some away from the pious faith. But if having
dared they write the things of Arius, seeing themselves succeeding and
flourishing, there is nothing remaining but to use much boldness, remembering
the apostolic predictions, which he wrote giving warning beforehand because of
such heresies, and it is fitting for us to say: We know that, as it is
written, In last times some will depart from the healthy faith, giving heed to
spirits of error and teachings of demons, turning away from the truth; and: As
many as wish to live piously in Christ will be persecuted; but wicked men and
sorcerers will advance to the worse, deceiving and being deceived. But none of
these things will persuade us, nor will it separate us from the love of
Christ, even if the heretics threaten us with death; for we are Christians,
and not Arians. Would that not even those who have written these things
thought the things of Arius! Yes, brethren, of such boldness there is need
now; for we have not received a spirit of slavery again unto fear; but God has
called us upon freedom; and it is truly shameful, and exceedingly shameful, if
the faith which we had from the Saviour through the apostles, this we should
lose through Arius or those who think and champion the things of Arius.
Already then most even of these parts, having learned the cunning of those who
write, are ready even unto blood to stand against their methods, hearing
especially concerning your firmness; but since from you the conviction against
the heresy has sounded forth, and as a serpent

from the dens the heresy has been uncovered; among you the child has been
kept, which Herod sought to destroy, and the truth lives in you, and the faith
is healthy among you. =25.588= For this reason, I exhort, having in hand the
faith written at Nicaea by the Fathers, and avenging this with much eagerness,
and with confidence in the Lord, become a type to those everywhere, showing
that a struggle now lies before against the heresy for the truth, and that the
methods of the enemy are various. For not only not sacrificing frankincense
shows martyrs; but also not denying the faith makes the martyrdom of the
conscience bright. And not only those who bent to idols were condemned as
aliens, but also those who betrayed the truth. Judas then was not cast out
from the apostolic honor for sacrificing, but becoming a traitor. And
Hymenaeus and Alexander did not leap away turning to idols, but because they
made shipwreck concerning the faith. And again Abraham the patriarch was not
crowned by being killed, but because he became faithful to God. And the other
saints, of whom Paul says, Gideon, Barak, Samson, Jephthah, David also and
Samuel, and those with them, were not perfected by blood poured out, but
through faith they were justified, and until now they are admired, because
they were ready even to endure death for piety toward the Lord. And if one
must add also what has come to be among us, you know how the blessed Alexander
struggled even unto death against this heresy, and how many afflictions and
how great labours, although being an old man, having endured, in the
presbyteral age he too was added to his fathers. And how many others also have
had how many toils, teaching against this impiety, and have the boast of the
confession in Christ. Therefore we too, as the struggle concerning all being
ours, and it now lying before either to deny or to keep the faith, let us have
this zeal and purpose, to guard what we received, having for a reminder the
faith written at Nicaea; and to turn away from the innovations, and to teach
the peoples not to give heed to spirits of error, but altogether to turn aside
from the impiety of the Ariomaniacs, and from the conspiracy of the Meletians
with them. =25.589= For you see, how formerly fighting against themselves,
now, as Herod and Pontius, they have agreed upon the blasphemy against our
Lord Jesus Christ; for which one might also justly hate them, because for
their own sakes they were hostile to one another, but for the enmity against
the truth and the impiety toward God they have become friends and greet one
another, and they endure to suffer all things contrary to their mind, each
because of the pleasure of his own purpose; Meletians because of the
presidency and the madness of love of money, Ariomaniacs because of their own
impiety, so that in such a conspiracy they may be able to lend one another
their own ill-will, and they themselves may assume the impiety of these, and
these may run together with the wickedness of those in the evil, and then in
common their own wicked deeds, as having mixed the cup of Babylon, they may
plot against those who are rightly pious toward our Lord Jesus Christ. The
wickedness then and the slander of the Meletians even before these things was
clear to all; and the impiety and God-fighting heresy of the Arians was long
ago everywhere and to all evident. For the time is not little; but the one
have become schismatics before fifty-five years; and the others before
thirty-six years were shown heretics, and were cast out of the Church by
judgment of the whole ecumenical synod. And from what they have now done, it
has been shown then even to those who seem to pretend their things openly,
that for no other reason have they made the plot from the beginning against us
and against all the orthodox bishops, than because of their own heresy of
impiety. For behold what was long ago being pursued by those around Eusebius
has now come to be; and they have caused the churches to be snatched from us,

and bishops and presbyters not communicating with them they have exiled, as
they wished, and the peoples =25.592= turning away from them they have shut
out of the churches, and they have handed them over to the Arians condemned so
long a time ago, so that, the hypocrisy of the Meletians also being with them,
they may have license in them to spit out the words of their impiety, and to
prepare beforehand, as they think, for the Antichrist who sowed the heresy in
them, the way of deceit. Let those then, nodding so much, imagine in vain; for
we know that the humane emperor also, hearing, will hinder their wickednesses
and they will not last, but according to what is written, the hearts of the
impious will quickly fail; but we as against apostates and those wishing to
plant madness in the house of the Lord, having clothed ourselves, according to
what is written, with the words of the divine Scriptures, let us stand
against, and let us neither fear bodily death, nor emulate their ways; but let
the word of the truth be preferred before all. For we also, as you all know,
being then asked by those around Eusebius either to assume the impiety with
them, or to expect a plot from them, did not wish to consent to them, but
rather chose to be persecuted by them, than to imitate the manner of Judas.
Indeed they have done what they threatened, and imitating Jezebel, they
prepared the Meletians as planted agents for themselves; knowing how they have
been against the blessed Peter the martyr, and after him the great Achillas,
then against the blessed Alexander, so that thus also against us, as having
practiced this, these may assume whatever is put under them, and those may
take a pretext to persecute and seek to kill us. For this they thirst, and
they do not cease even until now wishing to pour out our blood. But of these I
have no care; for I know and am persuaded that for those who endure there will
be a reward from the Saviour, and that you also having endured, as the
fathers, =25.593= becoming types to the peoples, and overturning this foreign
and alien invention of the impious, will have the boast, saying: We have kept
the faith; and you will receive the crown of life, which God has promised to
those who love him. And may it be that I too with you inherit the promises,
those spoken not only to Paul, but also to all who have loved the appearing of
our Lord, and Saviour, and God, and all-king Jesus Christ, through whom to the
Father is the glory and the power in the Holy Spirit both now and unto all the
ages of the ages. Amen.
