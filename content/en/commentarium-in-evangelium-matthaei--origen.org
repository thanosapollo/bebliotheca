OF ORIGEN, FROM THE EXPLANATORY TREATISES ON THE GOSPEL ACCORDING TO MATTHEW
BOOK 10
10.1 Then having dismissed the crowds he came into his house. And
there came <to him> his disciples saying: Explain to us the parable of the
tares of the field (13, 36[–43]). When Jesus is with the crowds, he is not in
his house, for the crowds are outside the house; and it is a work of his love
of humanity to leave the house and go away to those who are not able to come
to him. And having discoursed sufficiently with the crowds in parables he
dismisses them and comes into his own house, where his disciples come to him,
not remaining behind with those dismissed by him. And as many as hear Jesus
more genuinely first follow him, then inquiring about his lodging are
permitted to see it, and having come they see and remain "with him," all "that
day," and perhaps some of them even longer. And such things I think have been
indicated in the Gospel according to John through these: "The next day again
John was standing, and two of his disciples." Still, to exhibit that of those
permitted to go with Jesus and see his house the one who is distinguished also
becomes an apostle, there is added to these: "Andrew the brother of Simon
Peter was one of the two who heard from John and followed him." We too,
therefore, if we wish not to hear Jesus as crowds, whom having dismissed he
[also] comes into the house, taking up something exceptional beyond the crowds
let us become intimate with Jesus, so that as his disciples we may come to him
when he has come into the house, and having come may request an explanation of
a parable, whether of the tares of the field or of any whatever. And in order
that it may be understood more accurately of what reality the house of Jesus
is indicative, let someone gather from the Gospels whatever has been said
about the house of Jesus and what things have been spoken in it or done by
him; for these things, collected to the same point, will persuade the one
attending to this reading that the letters of the Gospel are not only simple,
as some suppose, but for the simple they have been made as simple according to
economy, while for those who wish and are able to hear them more sharply there
are hidden wise things and things worthy of the word of God. 10.2 After these
things answering he said, from: the one who sows the good seed is the Son of
man ***. Concerning which, even if we have discussed according to our ability
in the things before these, nonetheless now too will be said the things that
can be fitted to those, even if they have a meaning according to another
interpretation. And consider whether you can take the good seed, the sons of
the kingdom, besides the things already given, also in another way: that
whatever good things grow in the human soul, these <are sown> by the Word of
God who was "in the beginning with God," being offspring of the kingdom of
God, so that the sound reasons concerning each thing are the sons of the
kingdom. But while those who do not act according to the commandment of Jesus
are sleeping, who says: "Watch and pray that you may not enter into
temptation," the devil watching over sows upon them the so-called tares, the
wicked doctrines, upon the so-called by some natural notions and good seeds
that are from the Word. And according to this the field might be said to be
the whole world also and not only the church of God; for in every world the
Son of man sowed the good seed, and the evil one the tares, which are the
wicked words, the sons of the evil one from wickedness. And at the end of
things there will need to come to pass what is called the consummation of the
age, a harvest, so that those appointed for this


angels of God may gather up the base doctrines that have grown onto the soul
and deliver them to consumption, overturning them by the so-called burning
fire. And thus the angels and ministers of the Word will collect from all the
kingdom of Christ all the stumbling-blocks that exist in the souls and the
reasonings that work lawlessness, which consuming they will cast into the
furnace of fire that is burning; where also those who have come to awareness
of themselves, because through sleeping they have received the seeds of the
evil one in themselves, will weep and as it were be angry with themselves. For
this is the gnashing of the teeth, wherefore also in the Psalms it has been
said: "they gnashed their teeth at me." Then especially the righteous will
shine no longer differently, as at the beginnings, but all as one sun in the
kingdom of their Father. As indicating a mystery therefore the Savior, perhaps
even through all the things in the explanation of the parable, but perhaps
especially through then the righteous will shine as the sun in the kingdom of
their Father, adds: "He who has ears to hear, let him hear," teaching those
who suppose that in the explanation of the parable it has been set forth most
clearly, so that it can be understood even among chance people, that even the
very things of the narrative of the parable need clarification. 10.3 But since
above we said on then the righteous will shine as the sun, that the righteous
will not shine differently as before, but all will be as one sun, we will
necessarily set forth what has appeared to us on the place. Daniel, knowing
the intelligent as "light of the world" and that the many of the righteous
differ in glory, seems to have said: "and the intelligent will shine as the
brightness of the firmament, and from the many righteous as the stars forever
and yet." And the apostle too, in "there is one glory of the sun, and another
glory of the moon, and another glory of the stars; for star differs from star
in glory, so also is the resurrection of the dead," says the same things as
Daniel, having taken this mind from his prophecy. Someone will therefore
inquire how some speak of a difference of the light among the righteous, but
the Savior the opposite: they will shine as one sun. I suppose then that at
the beginning of the beatitudes among those being saved, because those who are
not such have not yet been purified, the things of the difference of the light
of those being saved take place; but whenever, as we have given, all the
stumbling-blocks are collected from the whole kingdom of Christ and the
reasonings that work lawlessness are cast into the furnace of fire and the
worse things are consumed, and as these things happen those who received the
words that are sons of the evil one come to awareness, then having become one
solar light the righteous will shine in the kingdom of their Father. But for
whom will they shine, if not for those inferior who will enjoy their light,
analogously to the sun now shining for those on earth? For they will not
surely shine for themselves. And perhaps also "let your light shine before
men" can be copied "upon the breadth of the heart" according to what was said
to Solomon in three ways, so that even now the light of the disciples of Jesus
shines before the remaining men, and after the departure before the
resurrection, and after the resurrection, until all attain "unto a perfect
man" and all become one sun; then they will shine as the sun in the kingdom of
their Father. 10.4 Again the kingdom of the heavens is like a treasure hidden
in the field, which a man having found hid (13, 44). The former parables he
spoke to the crowds; but this and the two after it, being not parables but
likenesses toward the kingdom of the heavens, he seems, having come into the
house, to have said to the disciples; concerning which let the one attending
to the reading examine also of the two following whether they are not even
parables; for on those the scripture did not hesitate before each to prefix
the name of the parable, but on these it has not done the same. And reasonably
it has done this. For if he spoke to the crowds "in parables" and "all these
things he spoke in parables and without a parable


he did not speak to them," but having come "into the house" he discourses not
with the crowds but with the disciples who came to him in it, it is clear that
the things spoken in the house are not parables; for to those "outside" he
speaks in parables and to those to whom "it has not been given to know the
mysteries of the kingdom of the heavens." Someone will therefore say: if these
are not parables, what are they? Perhaps then following the wording of the
scripture we will say that they are likenesses. And a likeness differs from a
parable; for it has been written in Mark: "To what shall we liken the kingdom
of God, or in what parable shall we put it?" For from this it is exhibited
that there is a difference of likeness and parable. The likeness therefore
seems to be generic, and the parable specific. And perhaps also the likeness,
being most generic of the parable, has in species, as the parable, so also the
likeness homonymous with the generic. Which has also happened in the case of
the others, as those skilled concerning the positing of the many names have
observed; who say that impulse too is most generic, <encompassing> many
species, as both of aversion and of impulse, saying that in species impulse is
taken homonymously with the generic in contradistinction to aversion. 10.5 And
in this one must seek separately the field and separately the treasure hidden
in it, and in what way the man, having found this treasure hidden, from joy
goes away and sells whatever he has, in order that he may buy that field. And
one must seek also what the things are that he sells. It seems to me then
according to these that the field is the scripture planted with the manifest
things of the sayings of the history and of the law and of the prophets and of
the remaining thoughts (for much and varied is the planting even of the
sayings of the whole scripture), and the treasure hidden <in> the field is the
thoughts of the wisdom hidden "in a mystery," which are concealed and underlie
the manifest things, and in Christ, "in whom are the treasures of wisdom and
knowledge hidden." But another might say that the "field" is the one truly
"full, which the Lord blessed," the "Christ of God," and the treasure hidden
in him the things said by Paul to be hidden in Christ, who says concerning
Christ: "in whom are the treasures of wisdom and of knowledge hidden." The
heavenly things then and the kingdom of the heavens have been written as in an
image in the scriptures, which are the kingdom of the heavens, or Christ
himself the king of the ages is the kingdom of the heavens, likened to a
treasure hidden in the field. 10.6 And having come to the place you will
inquire whether the kingdom of the heavens has been likened only to the
treasure hidden in the field, so that one understands the field as other than
the kingdom, or it has been likened to this whole, <the field and the>
treasure hidden in the field, so that according to the likeness the kingdom of
the heavens is both the field and the treasure hidden in the field. And a man
having come into the field, whether the scriptures or Christ who is composed
of manifest and hidden things, finds the hidden treasure of wisdom, whether in
Christ or in the scriptures (for going about the field and searching the
scriptures and seeking to understand Christ he finds the treasure in him), and
having found he hides it, thinking it not without danger to reveal the secret
thoughts of the scriptures or the treasures of "wisdom and knowledge" in
Christ to chance people, and having hidden he goes away working how he will
buy the field, that is the scriptures, in order that he may make them his own
possession, having received from those of God "the oracles of God," which
first the Jews "were entrusted with." And when the one discipled to Christ has
bought the field, "the kingdom of God" is taken away from those, which
according to another parable is a vineyard, and it is given "to a nation
producing its fruits," to the one who through faith bought the field from
having sold all that belonged to him and no longer having with himself any of
the things that formerly belonged (but wickedness belonged to him). And you
will apply the same, even if the field that has the hidden treasure is Christ;
for those who "have left all" and follow him as it were according to another


account have sold their belongings, in order that, through having sold and
given those things away and instead of those having received from God that
they are helped by a good purpose, they may purchase at the great price and
worth of the field the field that has a treasure hidden in itself. 10.7 Again
the kingdom of the heavens is like a man, a merchant, seeking beautiful pearls
(13, 45[46]. Since the kingdom of the heavens is like, there being many
merchants trafficking in many things, not any of those, but the one seeking
the beautiful pearls and having found one pearl of great price worth the many,
which he bought instead of all, I think it reasonable that the things
concerning the nature of the pearl be examined. And observe carefully that he
did not say that he sold all that he had; for he did not sell only those which
the one seeking beautiful pearls had bought, but also all that he had, in
order that he might buy that beautiful pearl. We found then among those who
have treated of stones concerning the nature of the pearl these things: that
of pearls some are land pearls, and some marine. And the land ones occur among
the Indians alone, being suitable for seals and bezels and necklaces. And of
the marine, the distinguished ones are found among the same Indians, which are
also the best, occurring in the Red Sea. Second as among pearls are those
taken from the Ocean around Britain. And third and falling short not only of
the first but also of the second are those found around the Bosporus about
Scythia. And further these things were said concerning the Indian pearl, that
it occurs in shells resembling in nature rather large conchs. And these are
reported as making their marine pasturage as it were in troops, some leader of
the herd going before, conspicuous in color and size and distinguished from
those under him, so that he has an analogy to the so-called king of bees. And
concerning the hunting of the distinguished ones, that is those in India, some
such thing has been recorded: that the natives, enclosing a great circle of
shore with nets, dive down, taking care from among all to catch the one that
leads, for when this one is caught they say the hunting of the herd under him
becomes without toil, none of those from it still remaining still, but as if
bound by a strap and following the leader of the herd. And it is said also
that the generation of the pearls in India is constituted over times, the
animal taking more turnings and changes until it is perfected. And this too
has been recorded, that the shell of the animal that bears the pearl is opened
similarly to a yawn, and having opened it receives into itself the heavenly
dew, of which having been filled, if it is pure and unmuddied, it becomes
radiant and brings forth the stone large and well-formed. But if ever it
partakes of a dew that is clouded and uneven and wintry, it conceives a misty
pearl and blameworthy with stains. And this too we found, that if while going
toward the completion of the stone which it conceives it is intercepted by
lightning, it shuts and as it were *** from the terror scatters and pours out
the offspring into the so-called bubbles. And there are times when as if
abortive they are born small and having something of mist, yet well-formed.
And this too the Indian pearl has beyond the others: it is white in color,
resembling transparent silver, and it shines through with a gleam that is
gently yellowish, and for the most part it has a round shape. And it is also
tender-skinned and softer than according to a stone. And it is so delightful
to see that even among the more notable, as the one who wrote about the stones
said, it is celebrated. And this too is a sign of the best pearl, to have the
circumference as if turned on a lathe and the color most white and most
translucent and in size the greatest. These things then concerning the Indian.
And the one around Britain (they say) is golden in surface-color, but somewhat
misty and rather dull in its sparkles. And the one in the strait around the
Bosporus is more dusky than the British and livid and altogether dim, and soft
and long in size. And the one in the Bosporus


strait is not generated in the pinnae, which is a kind of oyster that bears
pearls, but in those called mussels. And for these, I mean those around the
Bosporus, the pasturage is in marshes. And it has been recorded that there is
also a fourth kind of pearls around Acarnania in the pinnae of the oysters;
but these are not excellent, anan, but also ill-formed both in shape and in
color, altogether muddied and squalid. And there are others besides these
around the same Acarnania, rejectable in every respect. 10.8 And having
gathered these things from the treatise concerning stones, I say that the
Savior, knowing a difference of pearls, of which in the genus there are both
beautiful and others base, said: the kingdom of the heavens is like a man, a
merchant, seeking the beautiful pearls; for if there were not also some base
among pearls, it would not have been said seeking beautiful pearls. And among
the words of every kind that profess truth and among those who bear them seek
the pearls, and let them be, that I may so name them, the shells that conceive
from the heavenly dew and that bring forth from heaven a word of truth, the
prophets, the beautiful pearls, which the merchant man according to the
present wording seeks. And the leader of the herd of the pearls, with whom
when he is found the rest also are found together, is the pearl of great
price, the Christ of God, the Word above the precious letters and thoughts of
the law and of the prophets, of whom when he is found all the others too are
easily received. And the Savior discourses with all the disciples as with
merchant men, not only seeking the beautiful pearls but also having found them
and possessing them, in which he says: "Do not cast the pearls before the
swine." And it is clear that these things have been said to the disciples from
the fact that there has been prefixed to the words: "And seeing the crowds he
went up into the mountain; and when he sat his disciples came to him"; for in
the sequence of those words it has been said: "Do not give the holy to the
dogs, nor cast the pearls before the swine." Perhaps then the one who does not
have pearls or the pearl of great price is not even a disciple of the Savior,
and *** the beautiful pearls, not the cloudy nor the misty, such as are the
words of the heterodox, not generated toward the east but toward the west or
toward the north, if one must take these things also in addition because of
the difference of pearls which we found occurring in different places. And
perhaps the muddied words and the heresies wrapped up in the works of the
flesh are the misty ones and the pearls that occur in marshes, which are not
beautiful. 10.9 And you will join to the one seeking beautiful pearls the
"seek and you will find" and the "everyone who seeks finds." For what do you
"seek," or what does "everyone who seeks find"? Venturing I will say the
pearls and the pearl which the one who has given all and suffered loss
possesses, on account of which Paul says: "I suffered the loss of all things,
that I might gain Christ," saying "all things" the beautiful pearls, and "that
I might gain Christ," the one pearl of great price. A lamp then is precious to
those in darkness, and there is need of a lamp until the sun rises; and
precious also is the glory upon the face of Moses (and I think that of the
prophets too) and a beautiful spectacle, through which we are introduced so as
to be able to see the glory of Christ, to which the Father bearing witness
says: "This is my beloved Son, in whom I have been well pleased." But "that
which has been glorified has not been glorified in this part on account of the
surpassing glory," and we need first a glory that admits of being done away,
for the sake of "the surpassing glory," as there is need of the knowledge that
is in part, which "will be done away, when the perfect comes." Every soul then
that comes into infancy and journeys "unto perfection" needs, until "the
fullness of time" is present to it, a pedagogue and stewards and guardians, in
order that after all these things the one who formerly "differed in nothing
from a slave, being lord of all," having been set free from pedagogue and
stewards and guardians, may receive the paternal things, the things analogous
to the pearl of great price and to the perfect that comes, doing away "that
which is in part," when "the surpassing of the knowledge


of Christ" someone is able to contain, having been previously exercised in
the, that I may so name them, knowledges surpassed by the knowledge of Christ.
But the many, not having understood the beauty of the many legal pearls and
the still "in part" knowledge that is all prophetic, think they can, without
those being made distinct and grasped through and through, find the one pearl
of great price and behold "the surpassing of the knowledge of Christ Jesus,"
by comparison with which all the things before knowledge so great and of such
a kind, though not being "refuse" in their own nature, appear as refuse, which
things are perhaps the dung thrown to the fig tree by the vinedresser, being
causes of its bearing fruit. =10.10= "To all things then there is a time and a
season for every matter under heaven" and there is a "season for gathering
stones," the beautiful pearls, and a season after the gathering of them for
finding one pearl of great price, when it is fitting, having gone away, to
sell all that one has, in order to buy that pearl. For just as everyone who is
to be wise in words of truth will need first to be grounded in elements and to
pass beyond the elementary instruction still more and to make much of the
elementary instruction, yet not to remain in the elementary instruction as
having honored it according to the beginnings, but having passed "unto
perfection" to have gratitude for the introduction as having been useful
according to the former things, so the legal and the prophetic things,
perfectly understood, are elementary instruction toward the Gospel perfectly
conceived and every mind concerning the works and words of Christ Jesus.
=10.11= Again the kingdom of the heavens is like a net cast into the sea (13,
47[–50]). Just as in the case of pictures and statues the likenesses are not
likenesses from all points of those toward which these are made, but the image
painted in wax, say, on one plane of wood has the likeness of the surface
together with the color, but no longer preserves the hollows and the
projections but only an impression of them, and the modeling according to
statues tries to preserve the likeness according to the hollows and the
projections, but no longer also the things according to color, and if a wax
impression also comes to be, it tries to preserve both (I mean both the things
according to color and the things according to the hollows and projections),
yet it is not also an image of the things in depth, so understand also in the
case of the likenesses according to the Gospel that the kingdom of the heavens
being likened to something is likened not through all the things that belong
to that toward which the likeness is, but through certain things which the
argument taken up needs. And here then the kingdom of the heavens is like a
net cast into the sea, not as some suppose who present by the argument that
there are different underlying natures of the wicked and righteous who have
come under the net, so as to think that because of its having gathered from
every kind there are many and different natures of the righteous, and
similarly also of the wicked; for to such an interpretation all the scriptures
that exhibit free will are opposed, both blaming those who sin and approving
those who do right, blame not justly following those who are such by nature
from the base kinds, or praise those from the more excellent. For of base and
beautiful fishes the cause is not about the souls of the fishes, but about
that which the Word, knowing, said: "Let the waters bring forth creeping
things of living souls," when also "God made the great sea-monsters and every
soul of creeping animals, which the waters brought forth according to their
kinds." Therefore there "every soul of creeping animals" "the waters brought
forth according to their kinds," the cause not being about it. But here we are
the ones responsible for being beautiful kinds and worthy of the so-called
vessels, or rotten and worthy of being cast outside; for a nature in us is not
the cause of wickedness, but a purpose that is voluntary, being evil-doing.
And thus neither is a nature the cause of righteousness as being incapable of
injustice, but a word which we received is what constructs the righteous; for
indeed the kinds of the water-creatures it is not possible to see changing
from


base kinds as among fishes to the excellent, or from the better to the worse,
but those among men who are righteous or wicked it is always possible to
behold either arriving at virtue from wickedness or dissolving from the
progress toward virtue into the flood of wickedness. Wherefore also in Ezekiel
concerning the one who turns from lawlessness to the keeping of the divine
commandments such things have been written: "And if the lawless one turns from
all his lawlessnesses which he did" and the things following as far as "as his
turning from the wicked way and his living," and concerning the one dissolving
from the progress toward virtue into the flood of wickedness such things: "And
in the turning of a righteous one from his righteousness and doing injustice"
and the things following as far as "and in his sins which he sinned, in them
he will die." Or let those who introduce natures from the parable of the net
tell us, "the lawless one" later turning "from all his lawlessnesses which he
did" and keeping "all the commandments" of the Lord and doing "righteousness
and mercy," of what nature he was when he was lawless? For it was not of the
praiseworthy. But if of the blameworthy, of what nature would he reasonably be
said <to be>, when he turns "from all his lawlessnesses which he did"? For if
of the base because of the former things, how did he change to the better? But
if of the excellent because of the second things, how, being of an excellent
nature, was he lawless? And you will raise the similar difficulty also
concerning the righteous one who turns "from his righteousness" and does
"injustice according to all the lawlessnesses." For before he turned from
righteousness, being in righteous works, he was not of a base nature; for a
base nature would not come to be in righteousness, since a wicked tree,
wickedness, cannot produce good fruits, those from virtue. And again, being of
an excellent and unchangeable nature, he would not have turned from the good,
after being called righteous, "from his righteousness" to "doing injustice
according to all his lawlessnesses which he did." =10.12= And these things
having been said, one must think that the kingdom of the heavens has been
likened to a net cast into the sea and gathering from every kind, for
exhibition of the variety of the purposes among men, which have toward one
another as great a difference as possible, so that the "gathering from every
kind" is of praiseworthy and blameworthy in the inclinations toward the
species of the virtues or of the vices. And the kingdom of the heavens has
been likened to a varied weaving of a net according to the old and new
scripture woven from thoughts of every kind and varied. And just as of the
fishes falling under the net some are found according to these parts of the
net, and some according to others, and each under the part by which he has
been held, so you would find also in the case of those who have come under the
net of the scriptures that some have been held under the prophetic weaving,
say, of Isaiah according to this saying or of Jeremiah or of Daniel, and
others according to a legal one and others according to a gospel one and some
according to an apostolic. For first someone being caught by the Word, or
seeming to be caught, is taken from some part of the whole net. And it is not
strange if some of the fishes that have been caught are encompassed by the
whole weaving of the net in the scriptures and are held together and held from
every side, not being able to run through, but as it were being enslaved from
all sides and not allowed to fall out of the net. And this net has been cast
into the sea, the life of men everywhere in the inhabited world that is tossed
with waves, *** and of those swimming in the salty affairs of life. And this
net before our Savior Jesus was not wholly filled; for there was lacking to
the legal and prophetic weaving the one who said: "Do not think that I came to
destroy the law or the prophets; I came not to destroy but to fulfill." And
the weaving of the net has been filled in the Gospels and in the words of
Christ through the apostles. On this account therefore the kingdom of the
heavens is like a net cast into the sea and gathering from every kind. And
apart from the things given, the "gathering from every <kind>" can indicate


the calling from every race of nations. And those who served the net cast into
the sea are the Lord of the net, Jesus Christ, and the angels who came and
will serve him, who do not draw up the net from the sea nor bring it upon the
shore outside it, the affairs outside of life, unless the net is filled, that
is unless "the fullness of the nations" "enters" into it. And when it has
entered, then they draw it up from the affairs here and below, and they bring
it upon the tropically so-called shore; where it will be a work of those who
drew it up both to sit beside the shore and to establish themselves there, in
order that they may set each of the beautiful ones under the net into its own
order according to their vessels named here, and may cast outside those that
are in the contrary state and are called rotten. And the outside is the
furnace of fire, as the Savior interpreted saying: Thus it will be in the
consummation of the age; the angels will go out and will separate them, the
wicked, from the midst of the righteous and will cast them into the furnace of
fire. Yet one must observe that already through the parable of the tares and
the present likeness we are taught that angels are going to be entrusted with
the distinguishing and separating of the base from the righteous; for above it
is said that "the Son of man will send his angels and they will collect from
his kingdom all the stumbling-blocks and those who work lawlessness and will
cast them into the furnace of fire; there will be the weeping and the gnashing
of the teeth." And here, that the angels will go out and will separate the
wicked from the midst of the righteous and will cast them into the furnace of
fire. =10.13= And from these there does not follow what some suppose, that
even than the holy angels those men who are saved in Christ are better; for
how can those who are cast into vessels by the holy angels be compared with
those who cast into the vessels, being ranked under their authority? And we
say these things not being ignorant that the men who will be saved in Christ
differ from some angels who have not been entrusted with such an economy, but
not from all of these; for we have read also "into which things angels desire
to look," where it has not been said: "all angels." We know also "we will
judge angels," where it has not been said: "all angels." And these things
having been written concerning the net and those under the net, the one who
wishes, before the consummation of the age and before the angels going out
