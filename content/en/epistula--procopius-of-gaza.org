Well done to you for the wonderful letter; how it struck us as a spring of
reason, and sounds something very musical instead of the swallows. How I am as
one possessed by pleasure, admiring everything, the diction, the thought, the
harmony, the writer. And I seem to myself suddenly to be a Lydian, having
imitated the Lydians, who, devoted to Dionysus, kept spring only whenever
Dionysus happened to be among them. But perhaps you will laugh at these
things, as the sophistic itself blooms on them, and you might perhaps call me
a sophist as in reproach; but I cannot help admiring the beautiful things,
even if you are silent, my good man, we speak the opposite; but do not give me
trouble, when I seek to present by letters the grief from silence.
