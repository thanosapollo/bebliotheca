* Passion of the Ten Holy Egyptian Martyrs
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Fair, O beloved, also is the company that bore witness to God in Egypt, fair
and worthy of narration. For men together with women, found of one mind,
having chosen to serve the Lord, and having trained mind and body to endure
anything
whatsoever, they all are subjected to one punishment, and they travel together
in common with one another, having won a fair contest and bound on a bright
crown. For when those who were killing had given out, and those who were
handing them over hesitated, and they now loathed making them stoned to death,
the duke of Egypt invented a punishment even beyond human invention, taking
the situation of the place as helper; for as the sun was burning and sending
forth much peak of fire upon the plains, Egypt's situation being such by
nature that it is heated sooner than other places, and is more inflamed even
than the changes; he builds a wall =20.1536= upon the plain, raising it to
such a height that those who wished could not run away; and having shut in
those of one mind: "Pay the penalty," he says, "all you polluted ones; since
you have also had the same mind." And having set guards, and having ordered
them to ask little by little, "There is abundant water overflowing the
provisions; if you thirst, deny your God and be saved; if you need food, do
this, and you shall have it." But they, having well recognized martyrdom to
be the one freedom, the confession toward God the one salvation, death here
the one life, neither gave out before the sun that scorches and burns, nor
turned toward the wearing hunger, nor endured to treat the drying thirst; but
psalm was their food, and praise of the Lord their drink. And their faith
concerning these things was not out of tune with the voice of the Gospel.
For the devil said on the mountain, "Say to this stone that it become bread,"
and Jesus answering said to him: "It is written thus: Man shall not live on
bread alone, but on every word of God"; and again in another, Jesus answering
the Samaritan woman said: "Everyone who drinks of this water will thirst
again; but whoever drinks of the water that I shall give him will never thirst
unto the age; but the water that I shall give him will become in him a spring
of water leaping up into eternal life." Thus the most holy ones were fed,
thus a persistent fair drink truly, and like the spring toward which they were
hastening; this psalm, this praise had made them rather enjoy paradise, they
rather wished to draw from the holy spring.
And after these things, when it was time for the soul to hasten to its own
places toward their God, they have handed it over, full of many hymns and
praises of God, and rendering it most pure. When these things had been
announced to the duke, he also ordered the wall built for them as a guard to
become a tomb; but we Christians, let us send up glory to the Father, and to
the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and always and unto the ages. Amen.
