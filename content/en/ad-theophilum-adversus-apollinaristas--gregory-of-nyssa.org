against us they attempt by a charge to strengthen their own, making the Word
fleshly and the creator of the ages the son of man, and constructing the
divinity of the Son as mortal. For they bring forward that some of those in
the catholic Church advocate two sons in the doctrine, the one being so by
nature, and the other added later by adoption (I do not know from whom they
have heard such a thing 3,=1.121= and against what person they are sparring;
for I have not yet come to know the one who uttered these things amiss);
nevertheless, since they have set this thesis against us in front of
themselves and, by seeming to wrestle with this absurdity, they strengthen
their own opinions, it would be well for your perfection in Christ, as the
Holy Spirit may lead your mind, to cut off the pretexts of those who seek
pretexts against us and to persuade those who slanderously charge these things
against the Church of God, that neither is there such a doctrine among
Christians nor will it be proclaimed. For it is not because the maker of the
ages appeared upon the earth in the last days and consorted with men that two
sons are therefore numbered according to the Church, one the maker of the ages
and another who at the end of the ages was manifested through flesh to human
life. For if anyone should take the economic manifestation through flesh of
the only-begotten Son of God as a construction of another son, this one,
having numbered also all the theophanies that have come to pass for the
saints, both those before the 3,=1.122= manifestation according to the flesh
of the only-begotten Son of God and those that again after these things have
come to pass for the worthy, will construct a multitude of sons according to
the number of the divine manifestation, and there will be for him one son who
spoke to Abraham, another who appeared to Isaac; and another who allied with
Jacob; and another who appeared to Moses in various manifestations, in light,
in gloom, in a pillar of cloud, in the face-to-face vision, in the hinder
parts. Another son again who arrayed himself with the successor of Moses; and
the one who spoke to Job through a whirlwind; and the one who appeared to
Isaiah upon the high throne; and the one described in the account by Ezekiel
in human form; and after these the one who flashed upon Paul in the light; and
before this the one who appeared upon the mountain to those about Peter in a
loftier glory. But if this is absurd and altogether impious, to portion out
the various theophanies of the Only-begotten into a number of sons, it has
equal absurdity also to make the manifestation through flesh a pretext for the
construction of another son. 3,=1.123=
For we think that according to the measure of the capacity of each of those
receiving the divine manifestation the vision of the surpassing nature has
always come to pass, greater and more God-befitting for those able to reach
the height, but lesser and more paltry for those who cannot contain the
greater; for which reason he does not appear in the economy through flesh to
the life of men in the same way as the former manifestations, but since all,
as the prophet says, have turned aside and become useless, and there was not,
as it is written, one able to understand and to seek out the height of the
divinity, for this reason the only-begotten Son, appearing to the more fleshly
generation, becomes flesh, contracting himself according to the smallness of
the receiver, or rather, as the Scripture says, emptying himself, in order
that nature might receive as much as it can contain. For the fact that that
generation is condemned beyond the former ones we have clearly learned from
the Lord's voice, in which he declared the men of Sodom more tolerable than
they, and the Lord determined that the Ninevites and the queen of the south
would condemn that generation at the resurrection.
If then all had been able, after the manner of Moses, to come within the gloom
in which Moses saw the 3,=1.124= unseen things, or after the manner of the
lofty Paul to be lifted above three heavens and in paradise to be taught the
unspeakable things concerning matters beyond speech; or after the manner of
the zealot Elijah to be lifted together with the fire toward the ethereal
region and not to be weighed down by the trailing-boat of the body; or after
the manner of Ezekiel and Isaiah to see upon the throne of glory the one
lifted upon the Cherubim or glorified by the Seraphim, there would altogether
have been no need of the manifestation of our God through flesh, all being
such. But since, as the Lord says, that generation was an evil and adulterous
generation (evil because, as the Gospel says, the whole world then lay in the
evil one, and adulterous because, having stood away from the good bridegroom,
it was mixed with the one who through wickedness commits adultery with souls),
for this reason the true physician, who heals those who are ill, as the
disease sought the treatment, so he brought the care to the sick one, in a
certain way becoming weak together with the sickness of our nature and
becoming flesh, which in its own nature has weakness co-essential with it, as
the divine voice teaches that The spirit is willing, but the flesh is weak. If
then the divine, having come to be in the human, and the immortal in the
mortal, and the powerful in the weak, and 3,=1.125= the unchangeable and
incorruptible in the changeable and the decaying, had permitted the mortal to
remain with the mortal, or the corruptible with corruption, and the rest with
the rest in the same manner, reasonably might someone have contemplated a
duality in the Son of God, numbering each of the things contemplated according
to the contrary as peculiarly upon itself. But if the mortal, having come to
be in the immortal, became immortality, and likewise also the corruptible
changed into incorruption, and all the rest in the same way were recast toward
the impassible and the divine, what argument still remains for those who split
the one into a dual difference? For the Word both before the flesh and after
the incarnate economy was and is Word; and God both before the form of the
servant and after this is God; and the true light both before it shone in the
darkness and after these things is true light. If then every God-befitting
conception is seen throughout as unfallen and unaltered concerning the
Only-begotten, and he is always the same, being toward himself in the same
way, how does anyone compel us to name his manifestation that came to pass
through flesh a duality of sons, as if one were the pre-eternal Son, and
another son were begotten to God through flesh? For we have both learned from
the mystery and 3,=1.126= believed that human nature, united to the Word, was
saved; but a fleshly son of God contemplated peculiarly upon himself we have
neither learned nor are we led by any consequence to think. For he did not,
having become sin and curse for us, as the apostle says, and having taken up
our weaknesses, according to the voice of Isaiah, about himself, leave the sin
and the curse and the weakness unhealed; but the mortal was swallowed up by
life, and he who was crucified from weakness lived from power, and the curse
was recast into blessing, and everything as much as is weak and perishable of
our nature, mixed with the divinity, became that which the divinity is. From
where then would anyone conceive the duality of sons, as if led by some
necessity through the economy according to the flesh toward such a
supposition?
For he who is always in the Father and always has the Father in himself and is
united to him, as he was even formerly, so also he is and will be, and another
son besides that one neither was nor came to be nor will be; but the
first-fruits of human nature that was assumed, mixed by the all-powerful
divinity (as one might say using an image) like some drop of vinegar in an
infinite sea, is in the divinity, yet not in its own properties. For thus the
duality of sons 3,=1.127= would be inferred as a consequence, if in the
unspeakable divinity of the Son some heterogeneous nature were recognized in
peculiar marks, so that the one would be weak or small or corruptible or
temporal, and the other powerful and great and incorruptible and eternal; but
since all the things contemplated together with the mortal have been recast in
the properties of the divinity, the difference is grasped in nothing (for
whatever one may see of the Son is divinity, wisdom, power, sanctification,
impassibility), how would the one be divided into a dual signification, no
difference portioning the number? For the divinity highly exalted the lowly;
to the one named in a human way it granted the name that is above every name;
it made the subject and servant Lord and king, as Peter says that God made him
Lord and Christ (for we understand through Christ a king). And because of the
exact unity of the assumed flesh and of the assuming divinity the names are
exchanged, so that both the human is named with the divine and the divine with
the human. Wherefore also the crucified one is called Lord of glory 3,=1.128=
by Paul, and the one worshipped by all creation, of the heavenly and the
earthly and those under the earth, is named Jesus; for through these things
the true and undivided union is interpreted, from the fact that even the
unspeakable glory of the divinity is signified by the appellation of Jesus,
when every flesh shall confess and every tongue that Jesus Christ is Lord, and
that the one who received the sufferings of the cross and was pierced through
with the nails and was run through with the lance is addressed by Paul as Lord
of glory. If then even the human is not shown in the properties of the nature
but is Lord of glory, and no one would dare to say two Lords of glory, having
learned that There is one Lord, Jesus Christ, through whom are all things,
from where do they charge against us the duality of sons, who put forward the
accusation against us as a plausible pretext for the construction of their own
opinions? Such then
