633 1 Thess 2:4-6 But as we have been approved. As to their not coming in a
word of flattery he calls them as witnesses, and reasonably; for they
themselves knew the things concerning themselves more than others, but that
they did not come even in a pretext and with the aim of taking, he calls God
as witness; for the things practiced in each one's mind are unclear to men.
For to take or not to take becomes known to men and is witnessed, but never
even to have acquired the aim of taking is known to God alone, and in time is
revealed also to men; for the one who has this aim will certainly, unless he
should change the aim, take at some time. But to Paul it did not even come
into mind to preach on the pretext of gain. Therefore reasonably of the things
in the mind he calls the Lord as witness. And the same is witness, he says,
that we do not preach seeking glory from men either. And well: from men; for
the glory from God they both sought and received. And very wisely also the
"seeking"; for even if they were glorified by men, yet not seeking nor
practicing to be glorified. Therefore of this too God is witness; for it is to
be taken from the common construction. 1 Thess 2:10 You are witnesses of what
you saw; but of what you did not see, God who knows all things. Thus he did
not even wish to leave them a handle for suspicion. 634 1 Thess 2:13 Word of
hearing. Well; for not all were able to become eyewitnesses of what was being
preached. And the Lord also said: blessed are those who have not seen and have
believed, having received piety through hearing, that is, and word. 1 Thess
2:15-16 See the intensified accusation of the Jews, and one worthy of their
blood-guilt. For he says that all that the Jews did, they did with the aim of
sinning, that is, they knew that they sin, and they sinned; for to fill up, he
says, their sins, as if they sinned as those owing to sin; for if they killed
the Lord not knowing him, why the prophets? in whom they take pride. Why do
they persecute us? And why do they maltreat you? Why do they hinder the
nations from being saved? For if it is a good thing that the nations come to
the faith of Christ, why do they themselves not run toward it also, but even
attempt to hinder those who run toward it? But if they do not consider it
good, why do they begrudge those who approach? And what concern have they even
of the nations? You see how they sin willingly and do all things so as always
to fill up their sins, that is, for the sake of never departing from sinning.
For this reason he says: and wrath has come upon them to the end. For while
they were always sinning the wrath followed, but it was deferred, giving room
for repentance, but now it has overtaken them. 1 Thess 4:11 To be ambitious.
For what? For being quiet and doing one's own things, as if you should say: do
not be ashamed that you have become poor for Christ; but even consider this an
ambition, to be quiet and to do your own things, and so on. Or he says "to be
ambitious" is to benefit the brothers and to show kindness. Then: but we are
poor; but in being quiet and doing, he says, your own things there will be for
you a means and power through which you will be able to be ambitious toward
those also and to benefit them. 1 Thess 4:16 The Lord commands, and the
archangel sounds the Master's command, and the other heavenly ranks sound,
which he tropically calls trum 635 pets, just as also the "sound"; but the
Lord, as we said, commands, and the archangel announces the Master's command
to those under him, and they minister it and sound it through to the ends of
the world. And the dead from all the inhabited earth rise and are gathered.
And see how skillfully and in detail he sketches the resurrection of the dead,
so that it may be more impressed on the minds of the hearers. He calls the
angelic powers trumpets, because just as the trumpet sounds nothing of its own
but whatever the craftsman may blow, so also the trumpets of God dare to sound
nothing of their own but whatever the common Master and creator may command
them through the archangel. 1 Thess 4:17-18 You will note that in describing
the resurrection he did not mention expressly those rising for punishment and
chastisement. And this, I think, he does for three reasons; first, because he
mentions the resurrection while consoling the Thessalonians. For I do not want
you, he says, to be ignorant concerning those who have fallen asleep, so that
you may not grieve. Therefore he ought not, intending to console, to mix in
the gloomy things; for what sort of consolation is punishment and unending
chastisement and darkness and worm and fire? Rather, if he wrote thus, he
would have intensified the grief, but would not have consoled their souls from
anywhere. Second, he was narrating the resurrection to the faithful.
Reasonably therefore he does not mention those who will be led away to
punishment; for the faithful who live according to the Master's commandments
receive experience of no punishment, but continue in everlasting joy and
gladness. There was therefore no need to make mention of others' sufferings
and a distress in the glory and delight and blessedness being prepared for the
faithful, and then while writing to the faithful. Third, because the thing
itself, the resurrection, for which it came to be, he now explains according
to itself. For God made the resurrection for the salvation of all men and
incorruption and unspeakable glory and joy and enjoyment of the eternal goods;
for the chastisement he prepared for the devil and his angels, as he also says
in the Gospel, but not for men. But if some by their own wickednesses choose,
instead of the goods prepared for them, the things prepared for the devil,
this does not happen from the Master's philanthropy and providence. Therefore
also the divine Paul, speaking of the very nature of the resurrection and for
what the creator establishes it, reasonably does not mention those who will be
led to punishment; for it comes to be not for punishment but for recompense.
636 1 Thess 5:3 Concerning Christ's coming suddenly, so that one must prepare
oneself with zeal, faith, hope, love, honor, peace, long-suffering, love of
learning, and other things, lest the punishment of those being wronged become
wholly inconsolable, and further lest those more powerful, knowing the end
beforehand, trampling wholly without mercy and without philanthropy upon the
weaker, drive them together into blasphemy and helplessness.
