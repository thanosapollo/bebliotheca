An encomium of Hesychius, presbyter of Jerusalem, on Saint Luke the apostle
and evangelist.

1 Not enduring to keep silence concerning the present feast, for fear of
silence, even if I, the wretched one, am exceedingly unlearned in speech and
have no worthiness to speak, nevertheless, drawn by longing, I pay the debt
according to my power. And since I see a contest of both words and works at
the present assembly, the words being forced to set forth the greatness of the
works, and the works again contending, through excess of wonders, to outstrip
the speed of the words, nevertheless I judge such a victory rather the better,
if I should appear in praises defeated by the blessed Luke, who in teaching
and wonders conquered all creation in heaven and on earth.

2 For what is greater than an evangelist? And what is equal to that grace of
his, who by thought surpasses every sensible motion, who with the tongue
searched the paternal bosom, who became thunder of the ineffable mystery?
Being a man of three cubits, compacted with clay, boxing with passions,
subject to diseases, allotted to death, he went up into heaven by his
thoughts, saw by faith the beginningless generation, beheld the Word
proceeding impassibly from the paternal bosom and God begotten from God and
the uncreated essence not divided into two gods, saw the consubstantial
Trinity upon the throne being worshipped, divided in hypostases and united in
nature, neither the Trinity being shut into one person, nor the essence being
stretched into number. And the evangelist was found bolder than the seraphim;
for those cover the sight because of the lightning, but this one, because of
the grace, theologized the generation.

3 And again, having come down from heaven to earth and stooped into the
fearful cave, with the eyes of faith he saw a mystery of God higher than
change and alteration, he saw a virgin unwedded mother, he saw a womb broader
than heaven, he saw a birth not a beginning of God but an appearing, he saw an
infant, the Creator of all the ages become flesh, he saw God emptied in the
form of a slave, he saw a manger as the bed of the mystery, he saw
swaddling-clothes as the ransom of the world, he saw a cross as a bridge into
heaven, he saw a tomb as a weapon against death, he saw the eternal and
incomprehensible wealth of the Holy Spirit, he saw him who holds the earth in
his grasp fleeing into Egypt in the flesh. And the mystery escaped the
seraphim, but the evangelist theologized the wonder; therefore Paul,
proclaiming the dignity of the evangelists, cried: "that now to the
principalities and the authorities in the heavenly places there might be made
known through the church the manifold wisdom of God."

4 And look, I pray you, at the wisdom of the Master; from every science and
craft he chose disciples for himself, from fishermen Peter, from
tax-collectors Matthew, from tentmakers Paul, from physicians Luke. And he did
not do this at random nor simply; for human nature was in the depth of error
and there was need of fishermen. Again the devil exacted a tax from Adam,
snatching away immortality; there was need of a tax-collector evangelist.
Disobedience clothed man in the coats of skin; there was need of the tentmaker
and of grace, that he might cut with the knife of the Word the deadness of the
passions.

5 The race of men was variously sick with the fever of incontinence, with the
envy of love of money and with pleasure, with the despair of grudging, with
the unreason of idolatry. Therefore he set Luke as an experienced physician
for them, and Paul cries: "Luke the physician greets you";


for in truth Luke is a physician of the soul. Do you wish to see this man's
workshop? Look at heaven. Do you wish to see his healing? Look at his
worldwide circuit. Do you wish to see his medicine-chest? Look at the surgery
of the Jewish tongues. Do you wish to see his herbs? Look at the various
healings. Do you wish to see the sick whom he visits? Look at the neighboring
heretics. For from this side those who reject the Spirit are sick with
blasphemy, and from that side those who reject the Son are ill with atheism,
but to both, as a physician, he orders what is fitting, saying to these: "Do
not grieve the Holy Spirit," and crying to those: "He who does not honor the
Son does not honor the Father either." Truly Luke is a great physician.

6 And do you wish to see his art? Look at the divine Gospel; for the other
evangelists made the narrative of one kind. I mean something of this sort:
Matthew began from the genealogy according to the flesh, saying: "The book of
the generation of Jesus Christ, son of David, son of Abraham." Mark also
himself proclaimed the economy according to the flesh, saying: "Jesus came
from Nazareth of Galilee." But John, the evangelical eagle, the theologian
lyre, proclaimed only the beginningless and unwitnessed generation and the
co-eternal existence. And let the mouths of those who love slander be stopped,
because he was begotten only from only, and the generation above is
unwitnessed. For time was not present; for he himself is the Creator of all
the ages. An angel was not present; for he himself is "he who makes his angels
spirits and his ministers a flaming fire." Heaven was not present; "For by the
Word of the Lord the heavens were established." The sun was not present; for
he himself is "the sun of righteousness." The moon was not present; "For he
founded the moon and the stars." Earth was not present; for he himself said:
"Let the waters be gathered and let the dry land appear." Unwitnessed then is
the generation above, divine and incomprehensible, since the Father alone
knows how he begot, and the Son how he was begotten. John then alone
proclaimed the beginningless generation and the co-eternal existence.

7 But the blessed Luke, the excellent among the evangelists and merchant of
this wondrous stone and precious pearl, the unsleeping guardian of the city,
the experienced physician of souls, the impregnable wall of kings, narrated
the whole mystery together: the good news of Gabriel, the reverence of the
virgin, the coming-upon of the Holy Spirit, the form of God, the impassible
emptying of the Godhead, the unchangeable and unconfused character of the
incarnation, the inviolability of virginity, the uncontrived character of the
conception, the incorruption of the birth, the annunciation of the angel to
the virgin saying: "Hail, full of grace, the Lord is with you."

8 O greeting that fitted no other, except the holy virgin alone. Eve bore, but
in sorrows; Sarah bore, but in toil; Rebecca bore, but in groanings; Anna
bore, but in tears; Elizabeth bore, but in corruption; all bore in sorrows
because all conceived with corruption. But you alone, rejoice, the gate of God
and cloud of the mystery. You alone, rejoice, the unconfined temple of the
uncircumscribed. You alone, rejoice; for he who is made flesh from you "took
away every tear from every face." You alone, rejoice, the mother and heaven,
the maiden and cloud, the virgin and throne; for as mother you bore him who is
from you and before you and with you, and as virgin you conceived the Word and
travailed with a mystery, as a throne you bore in the womb him whom no one
circumscribes according to essence.

9 Therefore the prophet Isaiah, astonished at the virginal mystery, cried: "A
voice of a cry from the city, a voice of a cry from the temple" was heard. And
what do you say, O prophet? Sure is the riddle and deep the meaning. "A voice
of a cry from the city, a voice


from the temple" was heard; he calls John a voice, he names the leapings a
cry, he names Elizabeth a city, he proclaims the cave a temple. "A voice of a
cry" was heard saying: "Before she who was in labor gave birth, before the
pain of the labor-pangs came, she escaped and bore a male child." And he took
of the mystery all together, both speed and birth and virginity and wonder,
because the infant is not from intercourse. "Before," he says, "the pain of
the labor-pangs came"; for holy Mary did not suffer the things of Eve. "For
she escaped and bore a male child"; grace did not wait for the bounds of
nature; for the mystery of the economy is without appointed time. "Hail then,
full of grace, the Lord is with you"; for our Lord Jesus Christ opened the
bosom of hospitality, lodges apostles as in a chamber, brings evangelists to
perfection, from among whom he also set the blessed Luke as orator and teacher
of the church.

10 Now this thrice-blessed evangelist Luke is a Syrian by race, a physician by
craft, and having become a disciple of the apostles, and later having followed
Paul until his martyrdom, and after these things, having served the Lord, he
proclaimed the word without distraction. Adorned with virtues of every kind
and shining with divine gifts, having become eighty-four years old he fell
asleep in Thebes, the metropolis of Boeotia, full of the Holy Spirit. This man
wrote the Gospel, making clear through the prologue this, that before him
other things have been written, and that it was necessary to set forth for the
faithful from among the Greeks the accurate narrative of the economy. And
Saint Luke the evangelist rested on the eighteenth of the month of October, by
the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, to whom be the glory and the power with
the immortal Father and the all-holy and life-giving Spirit, now and always
and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
