* On the Kalends
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

A discourse of the same, on the Kalends, Flavian the bishop of Antioch not
having come forth. It was spoken against those who observe the new moons, and
who celebrate dances throughout the city, and on the saying of the Apostle: Do
all things to the glory of God.

=48.953=

αʹ. Just as a chorus seeks the chorus leader, and a ship's crew the helmsman,
so also this gathering of priests today seeks the high priest and common
father. But in the case of the chorus and the ship, the absence of those who
preside often takes away much both of order and of safety; here it is not so.
For even if he is not present in the flesh, yet he is present in the spirit,
and now he is with us sitting at home, just as we too are with him standing
here. For such is the power of love: it is accustomed to gather together and
bind those who are at a great distance. At any rate, if we love the one who
sojourns in a foreign land and is separated from us by long seas, we picture
him every day; just as, when we are ill-disposed toward someone, we often seem
not even to see him standing near. Thus when love is present there is no harm
from distance of place, but when it is absent there is no benefit from
nearness of places.

The other day, at any rate, when we were praising the blessed Paul, you leaped
so as seeing him present; and yet the body lies in imperial Rome, and the soul
is in the hands of God: For the souls of the righteous are in the hand of God,
and torment shall not touch them; but nevertheless the power of love set him
before your eyes. And I wished to come down to the same subject today also,
but the word has rushed toward other things that press upon us, the sins
committed today by the whole city. For those who hear the praises of Paul must
first be zealots of the virtue of Paul, and worthy of such a hearing. Since
then the father is not present with us, come, taking courage from his prayers,
let us take hold of the teaching toward you. For Moses too, not being present
in the body with those who were fighting, contributed to that battle no less
than those who were fighting, but even much more, by the stretching out of his
hands raising the affairs of his own people, and making them terrible to the
adversaries. For just as the power of love is not cut off by distance of
place, so neither is the working of prayer, but just as that binds those who
are apart from one another, so this also is able to =48.954= benefit those who
are far off in the greatest things. Taking courage then, let us go forward to
the battle. For a war has also now been set up for us, not of Amalekites
attacking, as then, nor of any other barbarians overrunning, but of demons
parading in the marketplace. For the devilish all-night revels that take place
today, and the jests, and the insults, and the night dances, and this
ridiculous comedy, have taken our city captive more harshly than any enemy,
and whereas we ought on these accounts to be subdued, to mourn, to sink down,
both those who have sinned and those who have not sinned, the former for what
they transgressed, the latter for having seen their brothers behave unseemly;
our city has become overjoyed and bright, and has been crowned, and just as a
woman fond of adornment and luxurious, so the marketplace has adorned itself
ambitiously today, putting on gold, and costly garments, and shoes, and other
such things, each of those in the workshops striving by the display of his own
works to outrun his fellow craftsman. But this ambition, even if it is of a
childish


mind, and of a soul that imagines nothing great or lofty, nevertheless does
not drag after it so great a harm, but is only some foolish zeal, pouring
laughter upon those who have been eager about these things. For if you wish to
adorn, adorn not the workshop, but your own soul; not the marketplace, but the
mind; so that angels may marvel, and archangels may approve what is done, and
the Master of the angels may reward you with gifts from himself; for this
display that is now taking place brings both laughter and envy, from those of
loftier mind laughter, and from those who are sick with the same things envy
and much jealousy.

βʹ. But, as I said, this ambition is not worthy of so many accusations; but
the contests that take place today in the taverns, these especially cause
pain, and are filled with much dissipation and impiety: impiety, because those
who do these things observe days, and take omens, and think that if they
should accomplish the new moon of this month with pleasure and cheer, they
will have the whole year also such; dissipation, because toward dawn women and
men, filling bowls and cups, drink the unmixed wine with much dissipation.
These things are unworthy of =48.955= our philosophy, whether you do them, or
overlook others doing them, whether slaves, or friends, or neighbors. Have you
not heard Paul saying: You observe days and months and seasons and years; I am
afraid lest somehow I have labored in vain among you. Besides, it would also
be of extreme folly to expect this of the whole year from one day, if it
should turn out favorable; and this judgment is not of folly only, but also of
devilish working, to commit our affairs of life not to our own zeal and
eagerness, but to the cycles of days. The year will be favorable to you
throughout, not if you get drunk at the new moon, but if both at the new moon
and on every day you do the things that seem good to God.

For a day becomes evil and good, not from its own nature, for one day has
differed in nothing from a day, but from our zeal and negligence. If you work
righteousness, the day has become good for you; if you work sin, it is evil
and full of punishment. If you philosophize these things, and are so disposed,
you will have the whole year favorable, working prayers and alms every day;
but if you neglect your own virtue, and trust the cheer of your soul to
beginnings of months and numbers of days, you will be destitute of all the
goods that are your own. Which thing the devil also having perceived, and
being eager to dissolve our labors concerning virtue, and to quench the
eagerness of the soul, has taught us to ascribe successes and failures to the
days. For he who has persuaded himself that a day is evil and good will
neither in the evil day take care of good works, as doing everything in vain,
and benefiting nothing because of the necessity of the day; nor in the good
day again will he do this, as suffering no harm from his own negligence,
because of the good fortune of the day again; and thus from both sides he will
betray his own salvation; and sometimes as toiling without profit, sometimes
as doing what is superfluous, he will live in idleness and wickedness. Knowing
this then, we must flee the devices of the devil, and cast this vote out of
the mind, and not observe days, nor hate one and love another. For not only
that he may cast us into negligence, but also that he may slander the
creations of God, that wicked demon plots these things, wishing to drag our
souls down at once into impiety and into negligence. But we must leap away,
and know clearly that there is nothing evil but sin only, and nothing good but
virtue only, and pleasing God through all things.

Drunkenness does not make cheer, but spiritual prayer; not wine, but a
teaching word. Wine works a storm, but the word a calm; that tumult


introduces, this casts out disturbance; that darkens the mind, this enlightens
what is darkened; that injects despondencies that are not, this drives out
those that are. For nothing is accustomed to produce so much good cheer and
gladness as the doctrines concerning philosophy, the looking down on present
things, the gaping toward the things to come, the thinking that none of human
things is firm, not wealth, not power, not honors, not services. If you know
how to philosophize these things, even if you see someone growing rich, you
will not be stung by envy, even if you fall into poverty, you will not be
brought low =48.956= by beggary; thus you can keep festival continually. For
the Christian must not keep festival by months, nor by new moons, nor by
Lord's days, but throughout the whole of life keep the feast that is fitting
to him. And what is the feast fitting to him? Let us hear Paul saying: So that
we may keep the feast, not with old leaven, nor with the leaven of malice and
wickedness, but with unleavened bread of sincerity and truth. If then you have
a clean conscience, you have a feast continually, being nourished together
with good hopes, and delighting in the expectation of the goods to come; just
as if you have a conscience without boldness, and are liable for many sins,
even if there be ten thousand feasts and assemblies, you will be disposed no
better than those who mourn. For what benefit to me of a bright day, when my
soul is darkened by my conscience? But if you wish also to harvest something
from the new moon, do this. When you see a year fulfilled, give thanks to the
Master, that he has brought you into this cycle of years; pierce your heart,
number the time of your life, say to yourself: The days run and pass by, the
years are fulfilled, we have advanced far along the road; has any good been
worked by us? Shall we not go away from here empty and destitute of all
righteousness, the judgment seat at the doors, our life henceforth hastening
toward old age.

γʹ. Philosophize these things from the new moon, recall these things from the
cycle of years; let us reckon the coming day, lest someone say also toward us
that which was said by the prophet toward the Jews: Their days failed in
vanity, and their years with haste. This feast which I spoke of, the continual
one, which does not wait for cycles of years, nor is bounded by days, both
rich and poor will be able to keep alike; for there is no need of money here,
nor of abundance, but of virtue only. You have no money? But you have the fear
of God, a treasure more abundant than all money, not consumed, not changing,
not spent. Look to the heaven, and the heaven of heaven, the earth, the sea,
the air, the kinds of animals, the plants of every sort, the whole nature of
men; think of angels, archangels, the higher powers; remember that all these
are possessions of your Master. And it is not possible for a slave of so
wealthy a Master to be poor, if he has his own Lord gracious. Observing days
is not of Christian philosophy, but of Hellenic error. You have been enrolled
in the city above, you belong to the citizenship there, having mixed yourself
with angels; where light does not end into darkness, nor day finish into
night, but continually day, continually light.

Let us look then toward those things continually. Seek the things above, he
says, where Christ is, sitting at the right hand of God. You have nothing in
common with the earth, where there are courses of the sun, and cycles, and
days; but if you live rightly, the night becomes day for you; just as for
those who live in wantonness and drunkenness and incontinence their day is
transferred toward the darkness of night, not because the sun is quenched, but
because their mind is darkened by drunkenness. To be fluttered toward =48.957=
such days, and to receive more pleasure in them, and to light lamps in the
marketplace, and to weave garlands, is of childish folly. But you have been
freed from this


weakness, and you belong among men, and you have been enrolled in the
citizenship of the heavens. Do not then kindle a sensible fire in the
marketplace, but kindle a spiritual light upon the mind. Let your light shine,
he says, before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify our Father
who is in the heavens. This light brings you much recompense. Do not crown the
door of the house, but display such a manner of life that you may receive the
crown of righteousness upon your head from the hand of Christ. Let nothing
come to be in vain, nor simply; thus Paul commanded to do all things to the
glory of God. For whether you eat, he says, or drink, or do anything, do all
things to the glory of God. And how is it possible, he says, to eat and to do
to the glory of God? Call a poor man, make Christ a partner of the table, and
you have eaten and drunk to the glory of God. But he commanded not this only
to do to the glory of God, but also all the rest, such as going into the
marketplace, and remaining at home; let both of these come to be through God.
And how is it possible for both of these to come to be through God? When you
walk to church, when you take part in prayer, when in spiritual teaching, the
going forth has come to be to the glory of God. Again it is possible to remain
at home through God. How and in what manner? When you hear tumults, disorders
and devilish processions, the marketplace filled with wicked and incontinent
men, remain at home, and be freed from this disturbance, and you have remained
to the glory of God. And just as it is possible to make the sojourns at home
and the goings out through God, so also praises and censures. And how is it
possible, he says, to praise someone to the glory of God, and to blame? You
often sit in the workshops, you see wicked and worthless men passing by,
drawing up their eyebrows, puffed up, dragging after them many parasites and
flatterers, wearing costly garments, clothed with much ostentation, seizing
the things of all, overreaching.

If you hear someone saying, Is this man not enviable, is he not blessed?
rebuke, accuse, muzzle, call him wretched, weep; this is to censure through
God. Such censure is a teaching of philosophy and virtue to those sitting
together, no longer to gape after the things of this life. Say to the one who
has said these things: Why is this man blessed? because he has a marvelous
horse and a gold bit, and possesses many slaves, and has put on a bright robe,
and bursts with drunkenness and luxury every day? But for this he would be
wretched and miserable, and worthy of ten thousand tears. At any rate I see
that you are able to praise nothing of his own, but all the things outside
him, the horse, the bit, the garments, none of which is anything toward him.
What then, tell me, could be more wretched than this man, when his horse, and
the bit of the horse, and the =48.958= beauty of the garments, and the bodily
good condition of the slaves are admired, but he himself runs past unpraised?
What then could be poorer than this man, who has nothing good of his own nor
what he will be able to carry away from here, but is adorned with all things
that belong to others? For our own adornment and wealth are not slaves and
garments and horses, but virtue of soul, and wealth of good works, and
boldness toward God.

δʹ. Again if you see another poor man, cast aside, despised, living together
with poverty and virtue, and being called wretched by those sitting together,
praise this man, and the praise of the one who passed by has become an
exhortation and counsel of a good and excellent manner of life. If they say,
wretched and miserable, say that this man is most blessed of all, having God
as friend, living together with virtue, possessing wealth that never fails,
having a clean conscience. For what harm to him from the lack of money, when
he is about to inherit heaven and the goods in heaven? If you thus
philosophize yourself, and teach others, you will receive much both of the
censure


and of the praises the recompense, having done both to the glory of God. And
that I am not vainly entertaining you saying these things, but that some great
recompense from the God of all is laid up for those so disposed in mind, and
the thing is accounted a virtue, this voting such things concerning those who
live thus, hear what the prophet says, and how he sets in the rank of
achievements the despising of those who do wickedness, and the glorifying of
those who fear God. For after enumerating the rest of the virtue of the one
who is about to be honored by God, and saying what sort of man he must be who
dwells in the holy tabernacle, that he is blameless, and working
righteousness, and without wickedness, he added this also; for having said:
Who has not used deceit in his tongue, and has not done evil to his neighbor;
he added: The one doing wickedness is set at naught before him, but he
glorifies those who fear the Lord; showing that this also is one of the
achievements, [that is], to despise the wicked, and to praise and bless the
good. And again elsewhere making this same thing clear he said: But to me your
friends have been exceedingly honored, O God, their beginnings have been
exceedingly strengthened. Whom God praises, do not blame; and he praises the
one living in righteousness, even if he is poor. Whom God turns away from, do
not praise; and he turns away from the one living in wickedness, even if he is
clothed with much wealth. But whether you praise or whether you censure, do
both as God wills. It is possible also to accuse to the glory of God. How? We
are often angry at slaves. How then is it possible to accuse through God? If
you see one drunk, if stealing, whether the slave, or the friend, or some
other of those belonging to you, if running to theaters, if neglecting his own
soul, if swearing, if perjuring, if lying, be angry, punish, turn, correct;
and you have done all these things through God. If you see him sinning against
you, and falling short of service toward you, forgive =48.959=, and you have
forgiven through God. But now many do the opposite, both in the case of
friends and of slaves. For when they sin against them, they become bitter and
unforgiving judges; but when they insult God, and destroy their own souls,
they make no account. Again, must one make friends? Do it through God. Must
one make enemies? Do it through God. And how is it possible to make friends
through God and enemies? If we do not draw those friendships from which it is
possible to take money, from which to share a table, from which to obtain
human patronage, but hunt and make friends of those who are able always to
train our soul, to counsel the things needed, to rebuke when we sin, to
convict when we transgress, to set us upright when we are tripped, and by
counsel and prayers helping to bring us to God. Again it is possible to make
enemies through God.

If you see someone incontinent, polluted, full of wickedness, filled with
unclean doctrines, tripping you and harming you, stand off and leap away, just
as Christ also commanded saying: If your right eye scandalizes you, pluck it
out and cast it from you, commanding us to cut off and cast out those friends
who are longed for in the rank of eyes, and necessary in the affairs of this
life, if they harm us toward the salvation of the soul. If you share in
gatherings, and stretch out a long word, do this also through God, and if you
are silent, be silent through God. And how is it possible to share in a
gathering through God? If sitting with some, you converse about nothing of the
affairs of this life, nor of the simple and vain things that do not even
belong to us, but about our philosophy, about Gehenna, about the kingdom of
heaven, but not the superfluous and unprofitable things, such as: who has come
into office? who has been released from office? for what reason then was
so-and-so fined? from where did so-and-so gain and become more wealthy? what
did so-and-so leave to so-and-so when dying? how did so-and-so fail, to be
enrolled among the first of the heirs


having expected? and many other such things. Let us not then converse about
such things, nor put up with others conversing; but let us consider what
doing, or what saying, it is possible to please God. Again it is possible to
be silent through God, being insulted, being reviled, suffering ten thousand
evils, if you bear it nobly, and utter no blasphemous word against the one
doing these things. And not only to praise and to censure, nor to remain
within and to go out, nor to speak and to be silent, but also to weep and to
be grieved, and to be glad and to rejoice, is possible to the glory of God.
For when you see either a brother sinning, or yourself fallen into a
transgression, then groan and are grieved, you have gained from the grief a
salvation not to be regretted, just as Paul says: For the grief according to
God works a salvation not to be regretted. If you see another succeeding, then
do not envy, but as at goods of your own give thanks to God, who has made your
brother illustrious, and you have received a great wage of this joy.

εʹ. What then, tell me, could be more pitiable than the envious, when it is
possible both to rejoice and to gain through the joy, they themselves choose
rather to be grieved at the successes of others, and with the grief still also
to drag upon themselves punishment from God, and unbearable vengeance? And why
must one speak of praises, and censures, and griefs, and joys, when even from
the smallest and cheapest things themselves it is possible to harvest the
greatest, if we do them through God? For what is cheaper than being shorn? But
it is possible to do this also through God. For when you do not arrange the
hair, nor adorn the face, nor dress yourself for bait and deceit of those who
see, but simply and as it happens and only as much as need requires, you have
done this through God, and you will certainly have the wage, because you have
chastened a wicked desire, and have cut short an untimely ambition. For if he
who has given only a cup of cold water through God will inherit the kingdom of
heaven, consider of how great a recompense he will enjoy who does all things
through God. It is possible also to walk through God, and to look through God.
How is it possible to look through God, and to walk? When you do not run
toward wickedness, when you do not busy yourself with the beauties of others,
when seeing a woman coming to meet you, you bridle the eye, you wall the sight
with the fear of God, you have done this through God; when we put on not the
costly things, that make us softer, but only those that are able to cover us.
It is possible to carry this law even as far as shoes. For many have slipped
into this slackness and dissipation, so as even to adorn the shoes, and to
variegate them from every side, no less than others do their faces; which is
of an unclean and corrupted soul. For even if the thing itself seems small,
yet this would be a proof and conviction of great destruction, both in men and
in women. It is possible then also to use shoes through God, when we seek need
everywhere, and make this the measure of enjoyment. And that it is possible
also through walking and through dress to glorify God, hear what a certain
wise man says: The attire of a man, and the laughter of teeth, and the step of
a foot, announce the things concerning him. For when we appear composed [and
solemn], and full of solemnity, and displaying much self-control from every
side; even from the mere meeting both the unbeliever, and the incontinent, and
the turbulent, seeing such a one, will marvel, even if he be more insensible
than all. And if we take a wife, let us do this through God, that we may be
self-controlled, not that we may make our substance more wealthy, that we may
seek nobility of soul, not abundance of money, nor distinction of ancestors,
but virtue of manners and gentleness; let us take a partner of life, not a
partner of huckstering. And why must one enumerate all things? For it is
possible henceforth for us from the things that have been said to go over each
of the things that come to be and are done, and to do all things through God.


And just as merchants sailing over the sea, and putting in to cities, do not
first leave the harbor, nor go up into the marketplace, until they learn that
some of the things stored there has a profit; so also you, do nothing, nor say
anything, if it does not have the profit according to God. And do not tell me
that it is not possible to do all things through God. For when putting on
shoes, and shearing, and the dress of garments, and walking, and looking, and
words, =48.961= and assemblies, and goings in and goings out, and jests and
praises, and censures and encomia, and friendships and enmities are able to
come to be through God, what is there left that is not possible to come to be
through God, if we wish? What is worse than a jailer? Does not his life seem
to be the most wicked of all? But it is possible for the one who wishes to
gain even from there, when he spares those bound, when he tends those thrown
in unjustly, when he does not traffic from another's misfortune, when he is
set forth as a common harbor to all who are in need. Thus that jailer in the
time of Paul was saved; whence it is clear that from everywhere, if we wish,
we are able to traffic.

ς ʹ. What, tell me, is more harsh than murder? But it was able once to beget
righteousness for the one who did this daring deed; so great a thing it is to
do something through God. And how was murder able to work righteousness? The
Midianites once wishing to make God war against the Jews, and expecting to
prevail over them in this way, if they should deprive them of the goodwill of
the Master, having adorned girls, and set them before the camp, baited them,
and led them into fornication, then from there into impiety. Phinehas seeing
this, taking a sword in hand, and catching two who were fornicating, pierced
both in the very sin, and by his purpose stopped the wrath of God. And the
thing that came to be was murder, but the achievement from that was the
salvation of all who were being destroyed, whence it also brought
righteousness to the one who did it. And not only did that murder not defile
the hands, but it even made them cleaner, and very reasonably; for he did this
not hating those who were slain, but sparing the rest; he killed two, and
saved countless myriads. For just as physicians do, cutting out the rotten of
the members, they save the whole body [and healthy]; so also that man did.

Therefore the psalmist says: Phinehas stood and made propitiation, and the
slaughter ceased, and it was reckoned to him for righteousness to generation
and generation, unto the age. At any rate the memory of the achievement
remains immortal. Again another prayed, and struck against God; so great a
thing it is not to do something through God; I mean the Pharisee. But just as
Phinehas having done a murder =48.962= succeeded, so also this man not because
of the prayer, but because of the mind with which he prayed, fell having
struck against. Thus when something does not come to be through God, even if
the thing that comes to be is spiritual, it would harm in the greatest things;
just as when it comes to be through God, even if the thing that comes to be is
of this life, it benefits in the greatest things the one who does it with a
God-loving mind. For what is worse and more harsh than murder? yet it made
righteous the one who dared it. What defense then shall we have, we who say
that it is not possible to gain from everywhere, and to work all things
through God, when someone is found even having gained from murder? If we wish
to attend, throughout the whole of life we shall traffic this spiritual
business, whether we must buy something, or sell; as when we do not demand a
price more than what is customary, when we do not observe the times of
scarcity, and then share with those in need; For he who hoards grain is
accursed of the people, he says. And why must one speak of each thing, when it
is needful to gather the whole through one example? For just as builders, when
they are about to raise a wall, stretching a cord from corner to corner, thus
weave the building, so that its surface may not become uneven; so


also we, stretching this saying instead of a cord, which says: Whether you
eat, or drink, or do anything else, do all things to the glory of God.

Whether we pray, or fast, or accuse, or forgive, or praise, or censure, or go
in, or go out, or sell, or buy, or are silent, or converse, or do anything
else whatever, let us do all things to the glory of God, and if something does
not come to be to the glory of God, let it neither come to be, nor be said by
us; but instead of a great staff, instead of a weapon and safety, instead of
unspeakable treasures, wherever we are, let us carry this saying about with
us, having written it upon the mind, so that having done and said and
trafficked all things to the glory of God, we may obtain the glory from him
both here and after the departure from here. For those who glorify me, he
says, I will glorify. Let us then not by words, but also through the deeds
themselves glorify him continually with Christ our God, because to him belongs
all glory, honor and worship, now and always and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
