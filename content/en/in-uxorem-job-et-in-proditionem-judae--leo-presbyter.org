Of Leontius, presbyter of Constantinople, a discourse on the betrayal of
Christ.
Just as when a rule is set beside them the crookedness of the timbers is
exposed, so when this Master's Thursday is present those who loose what is not
to be loosed are made public. Where are the leaps of the daily tightening?
Taverns are preferred to churches, and baths to the altar. At once a name
confirms the intention of the undiscriminating. Are you dissolving the fast,
tell me? Where the good is, tighten and do not loose; where the worthless is,
loose, do not tighten. What is it that moves you toward dissolving the fast,
friend? If because of weakness of the flesh you partake of wine, enjoy it
today also; for God asks nothing of you beyond your strength. But if in the
days gone by you drank water and practiced hard fare, endure today also, lest
you be shipwrecked in the harbor. What you do not want to suffer, do not do.
What I mean is this: often you set forth a sealed purse filled with pearls; if
the one who received it as a deposit, having loosed the purse from the sides,
takes away a pearl, do you not hand him over, do you not punish him, do you
not make him public as unfaithful? In the same way the God of all entrusted to
you the purse of the fast, in which the pearls of enjoyment are sealed with
the finger of the Holy Spirit; do not take away, do not dissolve, do not
through one day or hour miss the crowns. What moves you toward dissolving it?
No one rejoices today, except the devil and his companions, the Jews. Did you
not hear just now how the children of the Jews, gathered in the court of the
high priest together with the elders, took counsel how they might seize Jesus
and kill him by deceit? Today the Master Christ is held by grief, for in all
things he fulfills the human measures; today the disciples become anxious, for
they are men clothed in cowardice; today the creation is in mourning, the
Creator of all being insulted, and you continue in drunkenness and in revelry
and in contrary things? Do not insult the feast, O friend; do not set aside
the resurrection, do not desire two Paschas, do not make a pretext of the
mystery; what you see is not, it is not food and drink. For this reason it is
called a mystery, because it is not given unto satiety. The mystery is a
tempering of the soul, an earnest of temperance, a tightening of friendship, a
union of those who were apart; you eat flesh, you do not eat bread; you drink
blood, you do not drink wine. You heard just now: the woman who was read to us
in the divine Gospels, she poured out ointment; for she did not pour out wine,
did she? Just now the Master Christ handed over the mystery; for it was not a
drunken-mystery, was it?
No one got drunk today, except only Judas, with a soul-destroying drunkenness.
You heard just now the evangelist saying: And when evening had come, Jesus
reclined with his twelve disciples. O the strange things, O the Lord's love of
mankind. He even made Judas recline with himself. Ought he not to have thrust
the traitor away? He reclined with him. Why? That he might not find a pretext
for the betrayal. If he had not made him recline with the rest of the
disciples, Judas would have been about to say: I was set aside, I was
despised, and for this reason I drove on to the selling of the Lord. So that
he might everywhere cut off his pretext, he makes him too recline with the
disciples. And in this the Lord, making known also to all who reclined with
him, as God, his foreknowledge, said to the disciples: Amen I say to you, one
of you will betray me. When the disciples first heard this bitter voice they
were brought down by grief and they left the bread they were holding; each one
of them, driven by conscience, came to the Lord: Shall I betray you, Master? I
wake the dead, using your name; I grant the lame to run; I open the cataracts
of the blind; I walk the sea as dry land; I overturn a crowd of sufferings; I
put to flight a herd of demons; and for so great a benefaction shall I bring
you betrayal as a wage? Nevertheless tell me, Master, since I do not know what
the coming day will bring forth; for a man's way is not in himself, but to you
the things that are to come are known as present: tell us who it is that
betrays you, lest we all faint from grief. But the Lord to the disciples: Who
is it that betrays me? He who dipped with me in the dish, he will betray me.
Already he despises me, already he has risen up against me. But Judas, having
heard these things, though he ought to have fallen down, confessed, and
received repentance, he did none of these, but comes to the Lord as to one who
does not know, saying, as you heard just now: Is it I, Rabbi? But the Lord to
him: If you are ignorant, ask; if you do not know, learn; if you do not dip in
the dish, make trial. You carry my price and you ask: Is it I, Rabbi? Do you
not even compare me to the prophet Elisha? Elisha the prophet, questioning his
own servant Gehazi when he returned from Naaman, when he was taking a wage for
the healing, and Gehazi, thinking to deceive a man, said to him: Your servant
has not gone hither and thither; but Elisha, making him public, said: "Was not
my heart with you when you went down with Naaman?" Elisha's heart was not
deceived, and my divinity is at fault? You came to me as to one who does not
know, saying: Is it I, Rabbi? You have made a compact with the Jews, you gave
them a sign, saying: Whom I shall kiss, he is the one; seize him. If I do not
will it, I am not seized. Do you not know what the fig tree suffered? Do you
not know where I sent the legion? Did you not hear the demons confessing the
lordship? Did you not see the Jordan checking itself? Did you not behold the
sea fleeing? Did you not know the winds cowering? Did you not see the waves
growing calm? Creation knows, and you sell? Nevertheless, Judas, go to the
proposal, make compact with the Jews, return to me, give me the kiss. I
consent to your kissing me, not because you are worthy of the kiss, but
because I must receive what I gave. But let the things concerning Judas have
been said sufficiently.
But that we may also take hold of the rest of the passage, you who have just
now heard the things that were read, cry out in season the words of blessed
Paul: Where sin abounded, grace superabounded. And someone will surely say:
What is "Where sin abounded, grace superabounded"? What is it? As I said
before, take the proof from the things just now read. On the evening before
this, Job's wife was urging her husband to blaspheme, but the woman just now
read to us was anointing the Lord with myrrh and making the inhabited world
fragrant. Where sin abounded, grace superabounded. That one a woman and this
one a woman, one nature but a different manner. That one a companion of Eve,
this one a fellow-runner of the virgin; that one a servant of the devil, this
one a minister of the Lord; that one sharpened her tongue toward blasphemy,
this one poured out the alabaster of ointment upon the Lord. What then of the
evangelist Matthew? You heard him just now saying: And when Jesus had come to
Bethany in the house of Simon the leper, a woman came to him having an
alabaster flask of precious ointment and poured it upon his head. Did you see
a woman's work? Every tongue is small toward her praise. She surpassed
Abraham; Abraham washed the Lord's feet, she anointed the Lord's head. Where
sin abounded, grace superabounded. She surpassed all the women; the Canaanite
woman received crumbs of compassion, the Samaritan woman drew water of
benefaction, the woman with the issue of blood plucked a wing of healing, but
this one poured upon the Master's crown, where the treasure of the inhabited
world is, where the root of the resurrection is. The woman is incomparable;
she touched the Lord's head, she took the rank of a priest, she imitated an
altar, she filled the place of the cherubim, she rivaled the powers above.
Anyone who called her a rational dove would not miss the mark; she flew well
about heaven, she ran well with the sun, using her hands as wings. This woman
surpassed all, not only women but also men. And let none of the men present be
gloomy, seeing a woman preferred to a man; for the gain is one, whether a
woman is praised or a man; one leaven, one and the same lump. And of this
blessed Paul is witness, saying: The head cannot say to the feet: I have no
need of you. If then the head has need of the feet and the feet need the
head, much more does a head need a side; for the substance and the
fellowship are one. And someone will surely say: And how did this woman who
poured the ointment on the Lord surpass all? How? From the Lord's testimony.
You heard just now the evangelist saying: And when Jesus had come to Bethany
into the house of Simon the leper, a woman came to him having an alabaster
flask of precious ointment and poured it upon his head. And when his disciples
saw it they were indignant, saying: To what purpose has this waste of the
ointment come about?
Could it not have been sold for much and given to the poor? But Jesus,
knowing, said to his disciples: Why do you give trouble to the woman? For she
has worked a good work upon me. Did you see a woman having the Lord as
advocate? She acquired a good pleader; she is silent and the Lord pleads on
her behalf, saying to his own disciples: Why do you give trouble to the woman?
She has done nothing untimely; she paints the Church in advance, she announces
the resurrection in advance, she grieves the devil in advance, she beholds the
salvation of the world in advance, she punishes Judas in advance, she gladdens
the women in advance.
Why do you give trouble to the woman? She knows what she has done; before you
she recognized the mystery. Judas sells and she anoints; you flee my suffering
and she urges it on. She knows what is profitable for humanity. Why do you
give trouble to the woman? She has worked a good work upon me; for you always
have the poor with you, but you do not always have me; for she poured the
ointment upon my body; she kept it for my burial. Amen I say to you, wherever
this gospel is preached, what she has done will also be spoken of, for a
memorial of her. Did you see, O friend, how she surpassed all the women
together and the men as well? Peter, the chief of the apostles, so great and
of such stature, when he heard the Lord saying that I must go up to Jerusalem
and suffer many things from the scribes and high priests and be killed and on
the third day rise, said: Be it far from you, Lord; this shall not be to you.
But she was carrying the things for the tomb, that when the Lord nodded, she
might at once bury him in advance. Therefore the Lord also, seeing her work as
great, answered saying: Amen I say to you, wherever this gospel is preached,
what she has done will also be spoken of, for a memorial of her. O the strange
things. The gospel was not yet either published or composed or written or made
public, and she was already being proclaimed beforehand. Where sin abounded,
grace superabounded. But to God be glory unto the ages. Amen.
