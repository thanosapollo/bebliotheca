OF OUR FATHER AMONG THE SAINTS ATHANASIUS ARCHBISHOP
OF ALEXANDRIA TO JOVIAN CONCERNING THE FAITH.
A studious purpose and a desire of the heavenly things is fitting for a
God-loving king; for thus truly you will also have the heart in the hand of
God. Since therefore your piety has willed to learn from us the faith of the
catholic Church, having given thanks to the Lord for these things, we resolved
above all to remind your piety of the faith confessed by the Fathers at
Nicaea. For some, having set this aside, have plotted against us in many ways,
because we were not persuaded by the Arian here =26.816= sy; and they have
become causes of heresy and of schisms to the catholic Church. For the true
and pious faith toward the Lord has been made manifest to all, being known and
read from the divine Scriptures. For in this also the saints, having been
perfected, bore witness, and now having departed they are in the Lord; and the
faith would have remained unharmed throughout, if the wickedness of certain
heretics had not dared to falsify it. For a certain Arius and those with him
attempted to corrupt it, and to bring in impiety against it, asserting that
the Son of God is from things that are not, and a creature, and a thing made,
and changeable. And they deceived many with these words, so that even those
who seemed to be something were carried away with their blasphemy; and yet our
holy Fathers, having come together beforehand, as we said before, at the synod
in Nicaea, anathematized them; and they confessed the faith of the catholic
Church in writing, so that, this being proclaimed everywhere, the heresy
kindled by the heretics might be quenched. This then was everywhere throughout
every Church known and proclaimed without guile; but since now some, wishing
to renew the Arian heresy, have dared to set aside this faith confessed at
Nicaea by the Fathers, and they pretend to confess it, but in truth they deny
it, misinterpreting the homoousion, and themselves blaspheming against the
Holy Spirit, in that they assert it to be a creature, and a thing made that
has come to be through the Son; of necessity, having seen the harm coming upon
the peoples from such blasphemy, we have been eager to give over to your
reverence the faith confessed at Nicaea, that your godliness may know the
things written with all exactness, and how far they wander who teach besides
this. For know, most God-loving Augustus, that these things have been
proclaimed from of old, and that the Fathers who came together at Nicaea
confessed this, and that all the Churches everywhere in each place are of one
vote with this, both those throughout Spain, and Britain, and the Gauls, and
all Italy, and Dalmatia, and Dacia and Moesia, Macedonia, and all Greece, and
throughout all Afri =26.817= ca, and Sardinia, and Cyprus, and Crete, and
Pamphylia, and Lycia, and Isauria, and those throughout Egypt and the Libyas
and Pontus and Cappadocia, and those near us, and the Churches throughout the
East, except a few who think the things of Arius. For of all those aforesaid
we have known the mind also by experience, and we have letters. And you know,
O most God-loving Augustus, that even if some few contradict this faith, they
cannot make a prejudice, since the whole inhabited world holds the apostolic
faith. For those men, having been harmed for a long time by the Arian heresy,
now more contentiously stand against piety. And for your piety to know,
although it already knows, nevertheless we have been eager to subjoin the
faith confessed at Nicaea by the bishops.

This then is the faith confessed by the Fathers at Nicaea: We believe in one
God, Father almighty, maker of all things visible and invisible; and in the
one Lord Jesus Christ, the Son of God, begotten of the Father as
only-begotten, that is, of the essence of the Father, God from God, light from
light, true God from true God, begotten, not made, homoousios with the Father,
through whom all things came to be, both those in heaven and those upon the
earth; who for us men and for our salvation came down, and was made flesh,
became man, suffered, and rose on the third day, and went up into the heavens,
coming to judge living and dead; and in the Holy Spirit. But those who say,
There was once when he was not, and, Before he was begotten, he was not, and
that He came to be from things that are not, or those asserting that he is of
another hypostasis or essence, or created, or changeable, or alterable, the
Son of God, these the catholic and apostolic Church anathematizes. In this
faith, Augustus, it is necessary that all remain, as divine and apostolic, and
that no one move it by persuasions and word-fights, which the Ariomaniacs have
done, saying that the Son of God is from things that are not, and that there
was once when he was not, and that he is created, and made, and changeable.
For this reason, as we said before, the synod at Nicaea also anathematized
such a heresy, and confessed the faith of the truth. For they did not say the
Son is simply like the Father, lest he be believed simply like God, but
=26.820= from God true God; but they wrote homoousios, which was proper to a
genuine and true Son, of a true and natural Father. But they also did not
alienate the Holy Spirit from the Father and the Son, but rather they
glorified it together with the Father and the Son in the one faith of the holy
Trinity, because there is also one Godhead in the holy Trinity.
