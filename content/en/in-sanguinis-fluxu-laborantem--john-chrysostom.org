* On the Woman with an Issue of Blood

V. Of the same, on the woman with an issue of blood, from the Gospel according
to Luke.
=64.17=
The aim of life has assigned different trades to different people, dividing to
each what seems needful and profitable; but to us God has allotted one trade,
the commerce of the Spirit, which is manifold through faith, and which
accomplishes the greatest things according to what is written: The same
Spirit, distributing to each one individually as he wills. For faith alone
remains with those who have it; it does not deceive the one who receives it,
does not set aside the one who has acquired it, does not fear thieves, does
not suspect plunderers, does not need seals, does not seek watchmen; for it
has God as a guardian, who through it readily grants all things to all. Kings
desire to have it, but private persons possess it; rulers seek it, but it runs
to the humble and embraces the lowly; it comes to the sick, and taking hold of
them it leads them by the hand, granting healings without pay.
When therefore you see, beloved, Christ coming down from a mountain, =64.18=
and again going up a mountain, crossing a lake, sailing a sea, visiting fields
and cities and villages, remember with the lower journey also the higher; do
not forget the throne of the Cherubim, do not pass over the divine because of
the human, do not give heaven over to forgetfulness, do not fail to remember
that the Word became flesh, as he willed. For the flesh became the Word; as
flesh he walks, as Word he gives way; as flesh he goes up a mountain, as Word
he sits above the Cherubim; as flesh he endures my things for my sake, as Word
he works the things of the Father without toil, having produced them from
water at Cana; he opens windows of light, making the eyes new with clay and a
finger. He said to the leper, I will; be cleansed, and after the voice the
disease was not; Rise, he says to the paralytic; and the word became instead
of feet, and the sufferer received walking more swiftly than the command; he
reached the house of Jairus, and the =64.19= mourning was suddenly changed
into joy. See, today too, hemmed in by a crowd, the hem shines forth, and with
the rain of faith he flooded the issue. For what has the evangelist just now
set before us, bringing in the many-sided gains of faith? For there was a
ruler of the synagogue, a servant of the laws of Moses and a teacher of the
people, and his name was Jairus. To him nature had given one gift, an
only-begotten daughter, about whom a necessary crowd was occupied, as is the
custom with a father who loves his child. But, as it seems, he did not know
it: he was caring for her not for succession, but for an untimely burial. For
a hard disease came upon her, making her a neighbor of death, and cutting the
father off from the good hope he had in her.
But the suffering did not conquer faith; for Jairus goes at once to the Savior
and begs him to reach the house. But the Lord outstrips the one who calls him
by his eagerness; for rising, he followed him. But see, faithful one, what
just violence is newly invented by a woman, what wise theft is managed, what
harmless plunder is devised by a female; for he, pressing on toward the
movement, must also finish that road quickly. A woman, he says, with an issue
of blood for twelve years, coming up behind, touched the hem of his garment;
for she said within herself, If I only touch, I shall be saved. For this
physician, she says, has come from heaven; he does not order a costly store of
drugs, he does not cut with iron, he does not bring on the pains; he knows the
things of the female; for from a virgin mother, as he willed, he came to dwell
among us; he is not defiled when he searches out hidden sufferings; he himself
is the overseer of the


hidden things. I am a woman, and I carry about a shameful and hidden
suffering; for I have unemptied streams of blood, and unfailing springs of
unclean channels. I need therefore a spring, which he himself is, that he may
secretly cleanse my unseemly issue; for the law does not admit such a disease.
The crowd thrusts me, the wretched one, away as abominable; but the physician
who is present, being the craftsman of nature, who formed woman from a side,
would not refuse to free me from corruption; for with a word alone he
commands, and the suffering does not remain; he is stirred by the Spirit, and
he exacts the dead from Hades; from a few loaves he fills thousands; he is
washed with tears and causes forgiveness to spring forth.
But no one dares this, except undoubting faith alone; taking her then as a
guide according to the soul, I come secretly, and in silence I steal the
healing by a touch; and if I escape notice, the whole is gain, for I know that
I shall be saved; but if, not escaping notice, I am called into the midst, I
would not be accused for daring for the sake of healing, nor shall I be made
to give back what faith has plundered; for all her gifts, as being of God, are
without regret; for he who said to the sea, Be silent, be muzzled, will also
stop up my unseen spring. Come then, faith, steer what is to come, and cut
quickly through the wave of the crowds, that I may bring the rotten boat of my
body to harbor in that calm haven; for the drop of his help is able without
toil to drown the deep of bloods that is in me; and having planned these
things well she dares the undared, and she neither fears time, nor reckons
poverty, nor counts the multitude; but leaping over all these things through
faith, or rather with faith, secretly touching the Master's tunic, she
plundered the gain of healing at the right time, believing that union with him
is deliverance from the suffering; truly a deliverance; for she was healed
Immediately from the plague.
For twelve years, boxing with the =64.20= disease day by day and year by year,
she was stealing life, being slaughtered always, and yet not dying; for the
first year, and she was beginning to perceive certain pains and sufferings; it
was the second year, and it was throwing the hidden parts of this unseen city
into turmoil; the third year was at hand, and she was held by the swelling of
the sinews and veins; it was the fourth year, and a spring of bloods was
coming forth from her; the fifth year, and from every side physicians were
running together, laying bare members which it is not lawful for males to see;
the sixth year was being completed, and the terror was stretching still more;
the seventh year, and while so much wealth was being emptied, the wasting of
the bloods remained inexhaustible; the eighth year came on, and those who were
treating her confessed defeat; it was the ninth year, and leaving her wholly
given up they went away; the tenth year drew near, and the woman was utterly
without support in substance and in health; the eleventh year, and no helper
stood by the one in pain; the twelfth year was being numbered, and the
physician of souls was present at the last hour, healing the diseases by touch
and word and faith. Meeting with him, and believing what was to come, she
received what she wanted; but she trembled, knowing that she had not escaped
notice. For what does the Savior say? Who is the one who touched me? for I
knew power going out from me. He did not say, Who is the woman who touched me?
lest somehow those present should occupy themselves with a doubtful search for
a little woman; but he said, Who is the one who touched me, leaving the voice
in mid-air, so that each who heard might think such an attempt to be a man's;
Who is the one who touched me? for I knew power going out from me. What are
you saying, O Master? Have you come that your things might be plundered?
Willing it, have you set the kingdom before the faithful for seizure? And how
do you ask what you know, and seek what you are not ignorant of? If you were
not willing that the power that went out should be stolen, you ought to have
held it back; but if you were plundered because you willed it, why are you
displeased, giving what faith forces to take from you?


You granted without being asked, and after the giving you bring a charge? You
healed invisibly, and after the healing you assemble a court? Yes, he says; I
know her who plundered me, I am not ignorant of her who robbed with good
repute, but I wish her to come into the midst, that she may become a herald of
the grace she obtained. For having been so gladly plundered by her, I have
chosen to confirm her gift before all: Take courage, daughter, your faith has
saved you. Keep, he says, what you stole with good skill; hold, woman, what
you seized unseen; acquire a genuine possession, which faith has furnished
you; for instead of a slave you were named daughter today, instead of defiled,
anointed: Be whole from your plague. Do you see, beloved, how much faith can
do, how in one moment it accomplished wonders? encouraging the trembling woman
in silence, prevailing upon the physician.
So the Church from the nations, imitating the woman with the issue of blood,
and drawing near to the Master in silence by faith, stopped up the abominable
spring of the legal bloods, and being called daughter she is mother of many,
having as witness him who of old cried out: For the children of the desolate
are more than of her who has the husband; I mean of the synagogue, whose
husband is the law, a ruler of the synagogue calling the Master today to the
raising of the child who has died. But as the hour presses, I promise myself a
debtor of the miracle concerning her, setting the wealth-giving Lord as surety
for you; to whom glory is due unto the ages of ages. Amen.
