* On the Lake of Gennesaret and on Saint Peter the Apostle
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Homily of our father among the saints John, archbishop of Constantinople,
Chrysostom, on the lake of Gennesaret, and on Saint Peter the apostle.

=64.47= When the teacher is illustrious, then the disciple is obedient, then
the disciple is of good hope; when the teacher is eager, then the teacher
reaps a profit; when the disciple shows diligence, then the disciple receives
the teacher's craft, because he embraces the occupation as a friend. For
otherwise it is impossible for a teacher to shine, unless he finds the
disciple's lamp watered with the oil of love of learning; and otherwise it is
impossible to shine, unless the teacher pours out the channels of wisdom
richly. For both sides have need of running and of zeal, and both must
contribute their strength toward the building-up of betterment; the one
sharpening the tongue, the other preparing the hearing; the one having the
mind made complete, the other offering a soul at peace.

These things are necessary not only in bodily matters, but also much sought
after in spiritual ones. For just as in bodily studies a child is not
otherwise freed from the rod, unless he keeps watch over the lessons, so also
in spiritual teachings a mortal is not otherwise released from condemnation,
unless, according to the prophet, he meditates in the law of the Lord day and
night. Since then these things stand thus, and all of us present are
fellow-disciples in Christ; for we all have one and the same guide and
teacher, Christ, according to the divine saying: And they shall all be taught
of God; come, today too let us prepare more spaciously the twofold hearing, I
mean that of the soul and that of the body, for the Lord's teaching, so that,
receiving the word as seed, we may be able to bear fruit, by the Lord's care,
in thirty, and in sixty, and in a hundred, the many-grained ear of obedience.
For you heard just now the evangelist Luke crying out: And Jesus, having
entered into one of the boats, which was Simon's, asked him to put out a
little from the land. And sitting down he taught the crowds from the boat. Did
you see a teacher's God-announced teaching? Did you see a crowd's lovable
pressing? Did you see the disciples' soul-profiting zeal? Did you see a paid
remaining of men? Did any of those hearing the word of God grow listless, or
leave the lake, or come down from the mountain, or ask saying, as some of
those present do: Why do you stretch out the word, teacher? I have something
to transact, an appointment waits for me, I have not yet bought anything for
the meal, the hour of the bath has passed, besides the heat holds me. Did any
of those present at that time say any of these things to the Master? No, by no
means. Why? Because they knew the one who has said that all things are good in
their time.

You have entered the church of God, =64.48= you have been counted worthy of
Christ's meeting; unless you are dismissed, do not go out; for if you go out
before the dismissal, you make your way as a runaway. You spend six days on
bodily things, and occupy yourself two hours in spiritual things. Do not scowl
here and beam outside; do not have the nod here and the thought outside; do
not praise here and revile outside. Let us present the things of the Lord; for
we are not speaking myths, are we? We read the things of the prophets; for we
do not abhor the righteous, do we? We remember the apostles; for we do not
despise the fathers, do we? You often go up to the theater, and you do not
come down until the dismissal; and you enter the church, and go out before the
divine mysteries? Fear the one who said: He who despises a matter shall be
despised by it. What then does the evangelist Luke say? It came to pass, while
the crowd pressed upon Jesus, that he himself was standing by the lake of
Gennesaret. O the well-crafted teaching of the Lord; the river was standing by
the lake; or rather the fisher of men came to the lake, that he might hunt the
hunters of fish. For nothing is idle with the Master Christ, not place, not
word, not riddle, not step. For since Peter and Andrew, James and John were
tracking at the lake of Gennesaret, practicing the fishing craft, having their
hope in uncertain things, letting down ruined nets, striving with an
unmeasured deep, not seeing from where they must be fed, stealing rather than
toiling, baiting rather than persuading; for without deceit a fish is not
caught; these the Lord, having perceived with the divine and unerring gaze as
being fit for the catch of fish, came to the lake to seine them in turn, not
using a dipped net, but a guiding word. And because the aforementioned
apostles, trusting in the boats that run the lake, did not know the Master of
the lake and of all things; for this reason the Lord was coming to the lake,
that he might teach the crowds, and hook the fishermen. What then does the
evangelist say? And Jesus, having entered into one of the boats, which was
Simon's, asked him to put out a little from the land.

Do you see how he who has no need of a boat enters a boat, that he may gain
the master of the boat. And why does the Lord also entreat Simon? Why? Listen
intelligently. Simon, as you heard just now, having toiled through the whole
night and accomplished nothing, remained without result. And he was greatly
distressed, washing the nets with empty hands, joining toil to toil, and
seeing no profit. For this reason the Lord, seeing Peter downcast, entreats
him; for he is the comforter of the afflicted. For as soon as the Lord saw
Simon afflicted, answering him with a hortatory word =64.49= he said: Lend me
your little boat a little, sailor, that entering I may teach and instruct the
multitude which you see following me; let your little boat become a church for
me, sailor; hear the word of God. Do not think me burdensome; for I came to
make you better. Leave the vain bodily hope, and look around at the spiritual
portion; lend me your little boat a little, sailor. But Simon, still baptized
in the grief of failure, answering the Lord said: From where have you come,
stranger-faced man? Why do you become a trouble to me? Look around for some
other skiff, if you wish to linger about the lake. You see me mourning, as one
not well supplied with bread, and you promise to furnish the word of God?
Shall I not look around from where I may borrow money, that I may feed my
mother-in-law and my wife, but shall I spread the little boat under you, that
I may labor in vain by day as by night? If you bring a fare, board the boat;
but if you are not carrying any, go away, I beg; for a gift feeds me, not a
word. But the Lord to Simon: Do not be vexed, Simon; truly you speak as a
sailor; the word of God is more nourishing than bread; I will feed you
abundantly; for I am the bread of life. Do not count my presence a loss; take
as much as you wish; for I am a well-supplied child of a rich Father. Hearing
these things, Simon, a little brightened in face, receives the Lord in the
boat, driving with oars, and said to him: Sit in the middle, stranger man. He
was disposed as one carrying a passenger, not perceiving that he carries the
one who carries all things. And Jesus, having entered into the boat which was
Simon's, asked him to put out a little from the land. And when Simon had put
out in hope of receiving a wage, Jesus sat down and taught the crowds from the
boat.

And what did he teach? Unfeigned love, undoubting faith, guileless peace,
undivided brotherly love, piety not peddled, knowledge of God without love of
money, unsullied virginity, unbending discipline, undivided marriage; to
undergo every toil for the truth, not to fear those who kill the body but
cannot kill the soul. While the Lord was teaching these things and still
saying with addition: He who loves his life shall lose it; and again: He who
wishes to come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and
follow me; and again: Do not acquire gold, nor silver, nor copper in your
belts, nor a bag, nor a purse, nor two tunics; while the Lord was teaching
these things, Simon hearing, who thought he had hired the boat to the Lord,
began to murmur and say in himself: Shall we expect to receive a wage from
this man? I have fallen into a loss by day and night; who has met me today?
This man is discoursing about possessing nothing; he teaches that no one
should have anything. Perhaps he wants me to sell even the boat, that I may
distribute to the poor; do I expect to receive a wage from him? While Simon
was saying these things in himself, the Lord completes the teaching, that he
may display the wonder-working, and fully persuade Simon. For it is labor ever
to persuade a simple man by word.

Then when the Lord had finished the word, and the whole multitude was pleased,
Simon coming to the Lord said: Your words are good, and your voice is sweet,
and your teaching is precious, but nonetheless fulfill for me the matter of
the agreement; bid me receive the wage of the sailing, so that I too,
rejoicing with the multitude, may go away to my house. But the Lord to Simon:
as you heard just now, do you want to receive your wage, Simon? Put out into
the deep, and let down your nets for a catch, and take the wage promised you
by me. But Simon to the Lord: Shall I fish again and let down nets, and
undergo toil? Would that I had not even groped through the night with empty
hands. Are you more skilled than my sailing? Or do you possess something in
the unseen deep? You can be a teacher; are you also a hunter of fish? Go out
from me; for I see you carrying nothing. Why do I also lose the time? Did I
not testify these things to you from the beginning? But the Lord to Simon: Do
not regard me as destitute, Simon; do not look at my hands and my bosom;
=64.50= I carry your wage not in the hands, but in my mouth. Having toiled the
whole night, you received nothing; for the Jewish thoughts are a night of
want. In the day of grace let down the nets; for the Christian obediences are
a light of good fruit. Put out into the deep, and let down your nets for a
catch. Then Simon to the Lord: As you heard just now, Master, having toiled
through the whole night, we took nothing, but at your word I will let down the
nets.

And having done this, they enclosed a great multitude of fish; and the net was
breaking. O the paradoxical things! Care does not leave Peter; The net was
breaking. And he looked around at Christ, and considered the fish, and thought
on the matter. He could not undo the load, and he drew helpers to himself; For
they beckoned, he says, to the partners in the other boat, in which were James
and John, to come and help them. They beckoned, they did not cry out, sailors
who accomplish nothing without cry and tumult. Why? Because the strangeness of
the thing raised them to speechlessness.

For what could they cry, except only to beckon, as seeing a divine mystery?
And those of the other boat also running together, where James and John were,
began to gather the fish, and as much as they gathered, so much they were
multiplied. For the fish were hastening which of them first would serve the
Master's command; the small outran the large, the middle outflanked the
greater, the large leaped over the small; they did not wait for the
fishermen's hands, they dove themselves onto the boat. The bottom of the lake
was not being moved, no one of the fish endured to remain below; for they knew
the one who said: Let the waters bring forth creeping things of living souls.
What then of Simon, who had fallen into two things, who toiled through the
night and caught nothing, and by day at the word of Jesus hunted all things?
When he saw the nets broken, and many other fish standing outside and gaping
to be caught, and not enduring to remain far from the nets, as one undergoing
force, he fell before the Lord saying: Go out from me, Lord, for I am a sinful
man. I have come to know at last who you are; I have never seen such a catch
of fish. It is custom, Master, to shut the fish inside the seine; but do I now
see the contrary? From outside the fish hold the seine. Go out from me, I beg.
Hold back now your divine command; leave the lake at least two fish for
generation. Go out from me; I am not thrusting you away, but I am calling
myself wretched.

Go out from me, for I am a sinful man, Lord. But the Lord to Simon: Let me go
out, from your little boat, Simon, I am going out; but I will enter into you;
for this reason I also came to you. For hunting you have been hunted; what you
do, you have suffered. For you draw from life to death; but I from death to
life. Do not fear, Simon, from now on you shall be catching men. Leave the
lake, net the inhabited world; break the reed, and prepare the cross; tear
apart the line, and preach piety; throw away the hook, and heal diseases; hate
the nets, and forge the heavenly keys; go out from the boat, and enter into
the Church.

Do not fear, Simon, from now on you shall be catching men. O the power of the
Lord! O the understanding of Peter! The caught fisherman did not say, What is
the strangeness of the teacher's counsel? I shall catch men, and what net
shall I prepare? And where even nithonta these man-hunting nets? Does a word
that has hooked me seize me? Simon said none of these things, but only heard,
immediately obeyed; he did not sell the fish, he did not ask for the plenty,
he did not arrange with his wife, he did not say to the Master, Save my modest
things, Master; I will settle accounts with the partners, I will leave small
expenses for those in my house. Simon uttered none of these things; for he was
Peter wholly. And because not only Peter obeyed the Master's command, but also
Andrew, and James, and John; for these are the two faithful brother-pairs. Did
you see the Lord's business? For what reason did he come to =64.51= the lake?
That he might teach the crowds, and hook the fishermen. You heard then just
now the evangelist saying: And having brought their boats down to the land,
leaving all things, they followed Jesus.

And what of the Lord? For we must bring the word to a close. When he saw the
apostles' eagerness, that leaving all things they followed him, he hastens to
a healing, lest the devil find a spark of unbelief; for the race of men is
easily changing its mind, and ready to swing the other way. For the apostles
of the Lord were about to say, if they had seen no other sign: Have we perhaps
followed him in vain, having left our substance? and the running-together of
the fish happened in some manner, and not according to a divine command. So
that the apostles might not be defiled by such devices, as soon as he left the
lake, and in one of the nearby cities, a crowd having run together, he opens a
God-healing infirmary, and at once by will alone he strips the leper, and
cleansed his spotted sheepskin of the leprosy; that Peter and those with him
might know that not only the living fish obey the Master's command, but even
the soulless affliction is put to flight by his nod. And you heard just now
the leper crying: Lord, if you will, you can make me clean.

O a leper's =64.52= knowledge of a pious dogma! The wholly filthy hide of the
leprosy did not stain his orthodox mind; from without the affliction crept;
for within it found no pasture; it was grazing down the body, but it did not
touch the soul; he bore an insulting title, but the fellow-traveler did not
draw near; he was full of leprosy, but he hated the lawless Uzziah; the
synagogue was thrusting him away, but the Church was embracing him; Moses was
insulting him, but Christ healed him. Here he is healed, and there he is
commanded to offer the gifts of the healing. Why? That both the Master
Christ's love of mankind may be known, and that you may show the money-loving
Jews' love of trade. What then does the leper, the refutation of the
heterodox, say? Lord, if you will, you can make me clean. I do not say,
entreat, but act with authority; I do not say, pray, but heal. For it is
proper to God to heal by his own will, and not by another's nod.

Lord, if you will, you can make me clean. For I heard the prophet saying: Our
God in heaven and on earth has done all things whatsoever he willed. Since
then by will you brought all things into being, and stretched out the heavens
as a skin, and spread the earth as a linen cloth, and clothed the things
between them in many forms; give back to me also the garment of nature, that I
may turn aside from the law as burdensome, and glorify grace as loving
mankind, in Christ Jesus our Lord, with the Father and the Holy Spirit, unto
the ages of ages. Amen.
