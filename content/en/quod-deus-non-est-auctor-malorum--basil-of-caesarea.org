HOMILY THAT GOD IS NOT THE AUTHOR OF EVILS.

Many are the modes of teaching, those shown to us through the sacred psalmist
David by the Spirit who worked in him. For sometimes the prophet, recounting
to us his own sufferings, and how he bore the things that befell him nobly,
leaves us through his own example a most vivid teaching of endurance, as when
he says: Lord, why are they multiplied who afflict me? And sometimes he sets
forth the goodness of God, and the swiftness of his help, which he grants to
those who truly seek him, saying: When I called, the God of my righteousness
heard me; uttering things of equal force with the prophet who said: While you
are still speaking, he will say: Behold, I am here. That is: I had not yet
called, and the hearing of God anticipated the end of the invocation. Again,
offering supplications and petitions to God, he teaches us in what manner it
is fitting that those who are in sins should propitiate God: Lord, rebuke me
not in your wrath, neither chasten me in your anger. And in the twelfth psalm,
having indicated a certain stretching-out of trial in saying: How long, Lord,
will you forget me unto the end? and having taught us through the whole psalm
not to grow weary in afflictions, but to wait for the goodness of God, and to
know that by a certain economy he delivers us over to afflictions, bringing on
the measure of the torments according to the proportion of the faith that
exists in each.

Since then it has been said, How long, Lord, will you forget me unto the end?
and, How long will you turn your face away from me? he immediately passes over
to the evil of the godless; who, whenever some small thing of those in life
strikes against them, not bearing the more difficult circumstances of affairs,
immediately become doubtful in their thoughts, whether there is a God who
cares for things here, whether he oversees each thing, whether he distributes
to each according to desert.

Then, when they see themselves stretched out still longer in the things they
did not wish, they confirm in themselves the wicked doctrine, and they declare
in their hearts that There is no God. The fool said in his heart: There is no
God. And having thrown this into his own mind, he thereafter proceeds
unsparingly through every sin. For if there is none who oversees, if there is
none who renders to each according to the desert of the things lived, what
prevents one from oppressing the poor, murdering orphans, killing widow and
sojourner, daring every unholy deed, being defiled with unclean and abominable
passions and =31.332= all brutish desires? Therefore, as following upon there
being no God, he added: They were corrupted, and became abominable in their
practices. For it is impossible to turn aside from the just path, without
having sickened in the souls with forgetfulness of God. Whence have the
nations been given over to a reprobate mind, and do the things that are not
fitting? Is it not because they said: There is no God?

Whence have they fallen into the passions of dishonor, and the females among
them exchanged the natural use for that against nature, and males among males
work the unseemliness? Is it not because they changed the glory of the
incorruptible God into the likeness of beasts and four-footed things and
reptiles? A fool then, as truly deprived of mind and understanding, is he who
says that There is no God. And close to this one, and falling short of him in
no way as to folly, is also he who says that God is the cause of evils. For I
set their sin as of equal honor, because both alike deny the good: the one
saying that he does not exist at all, the other

defining that he is not good. For if he is cause of evils, he is clearly not
good; so that from both sides there is a denial of God. Whence then, he says,
are the diseases? Whence the untimely deaths? Whence the utter destructions of
cities? the shipwrecks, the wars, the plagues? For these too, he says, are
evils, and all are works of God. So whom else do we have to blame for the
things that come to be, and not God? Come then, since we have fallen into the
much-talked-of question, having led the argument up to some agreed principle,
and having treated the problem more laboriously, let us try to make the
explanation concerning it clear and unconfused. This one thing then we must
have taken beforehand in our thoughts, that, being a work of the good God, and
being held together by him, who orders both small and greater things
concerning us, we would neither suffer anything unless God willed it, nor
indeed is anything of what we suffer harmful, or such that something better
could be taken even in thought.

For deaths are from God; yet death is not altogether evil, except if someone
should speak of that of the sinner; because the release from the things here
is the beginning of the punishments in Hades. But again the evils in Hades do
not have God as their cause, but ourselves. For the beginning and root of sin
is that which is in our power and the self-determining. For those who, by
abstaining from evil, might have had nothing terrible, these, having been
baited through pleasure =31.333= into sin, what decent account could we give,
that they have not themselves become causes of their own pains? Evil then is
one thing as toward our sensation, and another as toward its own nature. That
which is evil by nature depends on us: injustice, wantonness, folly,
cowardice, envies, murders, poisonings, knaveries, and as many kindred
passions as these, which defile the soul that has been made according to the
image of the Creator, and are by nature wont to overshadow its beauty. Again
we call evil that which is toilsome and painful to us toward sensation:
disease of body, and blows of body, and lack of the necessities, and
dishonors, and losses of money, and losses of kin; each of which is brought
upon us by the wise and good Master toward what is beneficial. For he takes
away wealth from those who have used it badly, destroying their instrument
toward injustice; and he brings on disease to those for whom it is more
profitable to be fettered in the members than to have the impulses toward
sinning unhindered; and deaths are brought on when the bounds of life have
been fulfilled, which from the beginning the just judgment of God fixed
concerning each, looking far ahead to what is beneficial concerning each of
us; and famines and droughts and floods are certain common blows of cities and
nations, punishing the excess of evil.

As then the physician is a benefactor, even if he produces toils and pains in
the body (for he fights with the disease, not with the one who is ill); so God
is good, who administers the salvation of the whole through the particular
chastisements. But you bring no charge against the physician, who cuts some
things, and burns some, and some he removes entirely from the body; but you
even pay him money somewhere, and you address him as savior, because in a
small part he stops the disease, before the passion is poured out into the
whole body. But when you see a city shaken down upon those who dwell in it, or
a ship with all its men broken up upon the sea, you do not hesitate to move a
blasphemous tongue against the true physician and savior. And yet one ought to
understand that, when the illnesses of men are moderate and curable, the helps
from care are taken; but when the passion has been shown stronger than the
treatment, the alienation of the thing that has become useless becomes
necessary, so that the disease, advancing continuously, may not go on to the
vital parts.

As then of the cutting or of the cautery the physician is not the cause, but
the disease; so also the disappearances of cities, having their beginning from
the excess of the things sinned, acquit God of every blame. But if God is
not the cause of the evils,

he says, how =31.336= has it been said, I who constructed light, and made
darkness, making peace and creating evils? And again: Evils came down, he
says, from the Lord upon the gates of Jerusalem; and: There is no evil in a
city, which the Lord did not make. And in the great song of Moses: See, see
that I am, and there is no God besides me; I will kill, and I will make to
live; I will strike, and I will heal. But none of these, to him who
understands the mind of Scripture, has an accusation of God as cause and maker
of evils; for he who said, I who construct light, and make darkness, presents
himself through these as craftsman of the creation, not as maker of some evil.
In order then that you may not think one to be the cause of the light, and
another of the darkness, he called himself maker and craftsman of the things
that seem to stand oppositely in the creation, so that you may not seek
another craftsman of fire, and another of water, nor another of air, and
another of earth; since they seem somehow to be opposed to one another
according to the contrariety of the qualities. Which already some, having
suffered this, turned to polytheism. And he makes peace, and creates evils.
Especially he makes peace in you, when through the good teaching he pacifies
your mind, and reconciles the passions that are in revolt against the soul;
and he creates evils: that is, he recasts them and leads them to betterment;
so that, having put off being evils, they may take on the nature of the good.

Create a clean heart in me, O God. Not now create it as a craftsman, but renew
that which has grown old from wickedness. And, That he might create the two
into one new man. The, he might create, is not that he might bring them from
not-being, but that he might recast those who are. And, If anyone is in
Christ, a new creation. And again Moses: Is not this one your father who
acquired you, and made you, and created you? For clearly here the creation,
being placed after the making, teaches us that the name of creation has been
appointed, as in most cases, upon the betterment. So that Making peace, thus
he makes the peace, from creating the evils: that is, from recasting them, and
leading them to betterment. Then however, even if you should understand peace
as the security from wars, and should call evil the toilsome things that
follow those who wage war, expeditions beyond the borders, toils,
sleeplessness, agonies, sweats, wounds, slaughters, captures of cities,
enslavements, leadings-away, the pitiable spectacles of those taken, and in a
word as many grievous things as follow wars, we say they come to be by the
just judgment of God, who brings the =31.337= punishments through wars upon
those worthy of chastisement. Or did you wish that Sodom not be burned after
those unholy deeds? or that Jerusalem not be overthrown, nor the temple made
desolate, after the fearful madness of the Jews against the Lord? And how else
was it just that these things come to be, and not through the Roman hands, to
which the Jews, enemies of their own life, delivered our Lord? So that it is
possible at times also for the evils from war to be brought justly upon those
who are worthy. And the, I will kill, and I will make to live, receive, if you
will, according to the ready sense. For fear builds up the simpler. I will
strike, and I will heal. And this too is useful understood from itself; the
blow producing the fear, and the healing turning one toward love.

It is possible for you, however, also to think more loftily concerning the
things said. I will kill, by sin; and I will make to live, by righteousness.
For as much as our outer man is being corrupted, so much is the inner being
renewed. He does not then kill one, and make another live; but the same one,
through the things by which he kills, he makes live; and through the things by
which he strikes, he heals, according to the Proverb that says, You shall
strike him with a rod, but you shall deliver his soul from death. Therefore
flesh is struck, that soul may be healed; and sin is put to death, that
righteousness may live. And the, Evils came down from the Lord upon the gates
of Jerusalem, has the explanation from itself. What evils? A noise of chariots
and

of horsemen. And when you hear, There is no evil in a city, which the Lord did
not make; understand the name of the evil; that the word hints at the
maltreatment brought upon those who sin for the correction of the offenses.
For I maltreated you, he says, and I starved you, that I might do you good;
stopping the injustice before it is poured out into excess, as a stream held
by some strong fence and barrier. For these reasons diseases of cities and
nations, droughts of airs and barrennesses of earth, and the rougher
encounters in life for each, cutting off the increase of wickedness. So that
such evils come from God, taking away the generation of the true evils. For
both the maltreatments according to the body, and the toilsome things from
without, have been devised toward a check of sin. God then takes away the
evil; and the evil is not from God; since also the physician takes out the
disease, but does not put disease into the body. And disappearances of cities,
earthquakes and floods, and losses of armies, and shipwrecks, and all
many-peopled destructions, whether from earth, or from sea, or from air, or
fire, or coming on from whatever cause, come to be for the sobering of those
who remain, God sobering the public wickedness with public scourges. That
which is properly evil then, sin, which is especially worthy of the name of
evil, =31.340= depends on our choice, it being in our power either to abstain
from wickedness, or to be depraved; and of the remaining things, some are set
forth as contests toward a display of courage, as to Job the deprivation of
the children, the disappearance of all the wealth in one turn of a moment, the
blow through the sore; and some as healing of the things sinned, as to David
the shame concerning the house, paying the penalties of a lawless desire.

And again we have considered another kind of the fearful things brought on by
the just judgment of God, toward making more sober those who are easy to slip
toward sin; as Dathan and Abiram were swallowed by the earth, chasms and
openings having broken under them. For here they themselves did not become
better by such a manner of chastisement (for how could those who went down
into Hades?), but they made the rest more sober by the example. Thus also
Pharaoh was drowned with all his army. Thus those who formerly dwelt in
Palestine were wiped out. So that, even if the Apostle should ever say,
Vessels of wrath fitted for destruction, let us not think there is some wicked
construction of Pharaoh (for thus the cause would more justly be transferred
upon the one who constructed him); but when you hear vessels, understand that
each of us has been made toward some use. And just as in the great house one
vessel is of gold, and one of silver, and one of earthenware, and one of wood
(the choice of each furnishing the likeness toward the materials; and a golden
vessel is he who is pure in manner and without deceit; and of silver, he who
is lesser than that one according to worth; and of earthenware, he who thinks
the earthly things and is fit for being shattered; and of wood, he who is
easily defiled through sin and becomes fuel for the eternal fire); so also a
vessel of wrath is he who has received the whole working of the devil, as a
vessel, and through the stench that has come to be in him from the corruption
is no longer able to be brought to use, but is worthy of disappearance and
destruction only. Wherefore, since he needed to be shattered, the prudent and
wise steward of souls =31.341= ordered that he become conspicuous and heard of
by all, so that he might at least become useful to others through the
suffering, since he himself through the excess of wickedness was incurable.
And he hardened him, by long-suffering and by the delay of the punishment
stretching out his wickedness, so that, his wickedness having been increased
to the last bound, the justice of the divine judgment upon him might be made
manifest. Therefore, always adding from smaller blows and stretching the
scourges, he did not soften his insubordination, but

found him both despising the forbearance of God, and by habit having practiced
himself in the terrible things brought upon him.

And not even so did he deliver him to death, until he made himself submerged,
in the pride of his heart having dared the passage of the just, and thinking
that just as for the people of God, so also for himself the Red Sea would be
passable. These things then knowing from God, and having divided with yourself
the kinds of evil, and knowing what is the truly evil, that it is sin, whose
end is destruction; and what is that which seems evil because of the pain of
sensation, but has a power of good, as the maltreatments brought on toward a
check of sin, whose fruits are eternal salvation of souls; cease being
displeased with the divine economies; and wholly neither consider God cause of
the existence of evil, nor imagine that there is a proper subsistence of evil.
For wickedness is not something standing as a living thing; nor can we present
a hypostatic essence of it. For evil is a privation of good. An eye was
created; but blindness came on by the destruction of the eyes. So that, if the
eye were not of a corruptible nature, blindness would not have had an
entrance. Thus also evil is not in a proper existence, but comes upon the
maimings of the soul. For it is neither unbegotten, as the word of the
impious, who make the wicked nature of equal honor with the good nature, if
indeed both are without beginning and above generation; nor indeed is it
begotten.

For if all things are from God, how is evil from the good? For neither is the
shameful from the beautiful, nor wickedness from virtue. Read the making of
the world, and you will find that there All things were good, and very good.
Evil then was not co-created with the good things. But neither was the
intelligible creation, coming to be from the Craftsman, brought into being
mixed with wickedness. For if the bodily things did not have in themselves the
evil co-created =31.344=, how would the intelligible things, so far differing
in purity and sanctification, have had a common subsistence with evil? But yet
evil is, and the working shows it poured out much against the whole of life.
Whence then does it have being, if it is neither without beginning, he says,
nor has been made? Let those who inquire after such things be asked in return:
Whence diseases? whence the maimings of the body? For neither is disease
unbegotten, nor indeed a work of God as craftsman. But the living things were
created with the construction fitting them according to nature, and were
brought to life completed in the members, and they fell ill when they were
turned aside from what is according to nature. For one stands out of health
either through a wicked diet or through some disease-making cause whatsoever.
God then created a body, not disease; and God then made a soul, not sin; and
the soul was maltreated, having been turned aside from what is according to
nature.

And what was its preceding good? The attendance upon God, and the conjunction
through love; having fallen from which, it was maltreated with the various and
many-formed infirmities. And why was it at all receptive of evil? Through the
self-determining impulse, especially fitting to a rational nature. For being
loosed from every necessity, and having received a self-chosen life from the
one who created it, because it has been made according to the image of God, it
understands the good, and knows the enjoyment of it, and has authority and
power, remaining in the contemplation of the beautiful and in the enjoyment of
the intelligible things, to keep its life according to nature; and it has
authority also to decline at some time from the good. And this happens to it,
when, having taken a satiety of the blessed delight, and as if weighed down
with a certain drowsiness and having slipped from the things above, it is
mixed with the flesh through the shameful enjoyments of the pleasures. Adam
was once above, not in place, but in choice, when, just ensouled and having
looked up toward heaven, having become overjoyed at the things seen, loving
exceedingly the benefactor, who had granted enjoyment of eternal life, and had
given rest

in the delights of paradise, and had given him a rank after that of the
angels, and had made him a fellow-dweller with archangels, and a hearer of a
divine voice; being shielded in all these things by God, and enjoying his good
things, quickly having been filled with all, and as if insolent through the
satiety, he preferred the pleasant that appeared to the fleshly eyes over the
intelligible beauty, and he set the filling of the belly more honorable than
the spiritual enjoyments. =31.315= Straightway he was outside paradise, and
outside that blessed way of life, having become evil not from necessity, but
from ill counsel. Therefore also he sinned through a depraved choice, and he
died through the sin; For the wages of sin is death. For as much as he stood
away from life, so much he drew near to death.

For God is life; and privation of the life is death. So that Adam prepared
death for himself through the withdrawal from God, according to what is
written, that Behold those who put themselves far from you shall perish. Thus
God did not create death, but we drew it upon ourselves from a wicked
judgment. Yet he did not prevent the dissolution for the reasons said
beforehand, lest he should keep the infirmity immortal for us. As if someone
should not accept to give over a clay vessel that has leaked to the fire,
until he should heal the passion existing in it through the remolding. But why
did we not have the inability to sin in the construction, he says, so that
even if we wished it, sinning would not exist for us? Because you too, when
you have household servants, do not take them as well-disposed when you have
them bound, but when you see them fulfilling the things due to you willingly.
And to God then it is not the forced that is dear, but that which is
accomplished from virtue.

And virtue comes from choice, and not from necessity. And choice depends on
the things in our power. And that which is in our power is the
self-determining. He then who blames the maker as not having constructed us by
nature unable to sin, prefers nothing other than the irrational nature to the
rational, and the unmoved and without-impulse to the choosing and practical.
These things, even if by way of a digression, have yet been said of necessity,
lest, having fallen into a depth of reasonings, in addition to the deprivation
of the things much-sought, you should still also undergo a deprivation of God.
Let us then cease setting the wise one right. Let us cease seeking the better
of the things that come to be from him. For even if the reasons of the things
ordered in particular escape us, yet let that one doctrine at least exist in
our souls, that nothing evil comes to be from the good. And there comes in
upon this, according to the sequence of the thought, also the question
concerning the devil. Whence the devil, if the evils are not from God? What
then do we say? That the same account will suffice for us also concerning this
question, which was given also concerning the wickedness in men. For whence is
man wicked? From his own choice. Whence is the devil evil? From the same
cause, having himself too a self-chosen life, and the authority lying upon
him, either to remain with God, or to be alienated from the good.

Gabriel is an angel, and he stands by God continually. Satan is an angel, and
he fell from his own rank entirely. And that one the choice kept =31.348= in
the things above, and this one the self-determining of the judgment threw
down. For that one too was able to revolt, and this one not to fall. But the
insatiable love of God saved the one; and the withdrawal from God
showed the other rejected. This is the evil, the alienation from God. A small
turning of the eye either makes us be with the sun, or with the shadow of our
body. And there, to the one who has looked up, the being enlightened is ready,
and to the one who has declined toward the shadow the darkening is necessary.
Thus the devil is wicked, having the wickedness from choice, not a nature set
against the good. Whence then is his war toward us?

Because, being a receptacle of all wickedness, he received also the disease of
envy, and he begrudged us the honor. For he did not bear our painless life in
paradise; but having deceived the man with wiles and devices, and having used
for the deceit this desire of his which he had toward being made like God, he
showed the tree, and promised that through the eating of it he would set him
like God. For if you eat, he says, you will be as gods, knowing good and evil.
He was not then constructed as an enemy to us, but from jealousy he stood
against us into enmity. For seeing himself thrown down from the angels, he did
not bear to see the earthly one being raised by progress to the dignity of the
angels. Since then he has become an enemy, God kept for us the opposition
toward him, through the things by which he was speaking to the beast that had
served him, referring the threat to him: that I will put enmity between you
and between her seed. For in truth the reconciliations toward wickedness are
harmful; since this law of friendship is by nature to come to be in those who
are joined through likeness. Whence it is right, Evil communications corrupt
good manners. For as in the disease-making places the air breathed in little
by little deposits a hidden disease in those who dwell there; so the custom
toward the base lets great evils into the souls, even if the harmful should
escape the sensation at the moment. Therefore the enmity toward the serpent is
irreconcilable. And if the instrument is worthy of so great a hatred, how much
ought we to be hostile to the one who worked it? But also why was the tree in
paradise, he says, through which the attempt against us was going to go
forward for the devil? For if he did not have the bait of the deceit, how
would he have led us through the disobedience into death? Because there needed
to be a commandment that tests our obedience.

Therefore there was a plant abundant in beautiful fruits, so that, having
shown the good of self-control in the abstinence from the pleasant, we might
justly be counted worthy of the crowns of endurance. And there followed the
eating not only disobedience of the commandment, but also the recognition of
the nakedness. For they ate, he says, and their eyes =31.349= were opened, and
they knew that they are naked. And it was necessary not to know the nakedness,
lest the mind of the man be drawn around toward the filling of what was
lacking, inventing coverings for himself and a consolation of the nakedness,
and altogether being dragged away by the care of the flesh from the gazing
toward God. And why were the garments not constructed together with him at
once? Because it was fitting that these be neither natural, nor from art. For
the natural things are proper to the irrational; such as feathers, and hairs,
and thicknesses of skins, able to keep off winters, and to bear burning heats.
In which none has differed from another, the nature being of equal honor for
all; but for man it was fitting, according to the proportion of the love
toward God, that the exchanges of the good things be varied. And the practices
from art would have become procurers of occupations; which was especially to
be fled as harmful to the man. Wherefore also the Lord, calling us back to the
life in paradise, casts out the care of the souls, saying: Do not be anxious
for your soul, what you shall eat; nor for your body, what you shall put on.
Neither then was it fitting for him to have the coverings from nature, nor
from art; but other things had been prepared, for him who had shown the
virtue, things from the grace of God going to bloom upon the man, and to shine
around with certain luminous wraps, such as those of the angels, beyond the
variety of the flowers, beyond the brightness and gleam of the stars.
Therefore then not the garments at once for him, since they were prizes of
virtue laid up for the man, which he was not permitted to reach because of the
spite of the devil. The devil then has been set as a contestant for us because
of the fall that came to us of old from spite, the Lord having ordered for us
the wrestling toward

him, so that through the obedience we may wrestle again, and be crowned over
the adversary. Would that the devil had not come to be, but had remained in
the rank in which he was ranked from the beginning by the marshal of the
ranks! but since he has become an apostate, an enemy of God, and an enemy of
men who have been made according to the image of God (for this is why he is a
hater of men, because he is also a fighter against God; and he hates us as
possessions of the Master, and he hates us as likenesses of God); the one who
orders human things wisely and with foresight used his wickedness then as a
gymnasium of our souls, as a physician using the venom of the viper for the
preparation of saving drugs. Who then was the devil? and what was the rank?
and what the dignity? and whence at all has he been named Satan? Satan then,
because of being set against the good; for so the voice of the Hebrews
signifies, as we have learned in the King =31.352= doms; For the Lord, he
says, raised up a satan to Solomon, Ader the king of the Syrians; and devil,
because the same one becomes both a fellow-worker of our sin and an accuser;
rejoicing in our destruction, and making a spectacle of us upon the things
done. And his nature is bodiless, according to the Apostle, who said: Our
wrestling is not against blood and flesh, but against the spiritual things of
wickedness.

And a ruling dignity; For against the principalities, he says, and the
authorities, and the world-rulers of this darkness. And the place of the rule
is the aerial, as the same one says: According to the ruler of the authority
of the air, of the spirit now working in the sons of disobedience. Therefore
he is also called ruler of the world, since his rule is around the earth. And
thus the Lord says: Now is the judgment of this world, now the ruler of this
world shall be cast out. And again: The ruler of this world comes, and in me
he will find nothing. And since it has been said concerning the army of the
devil, that They are spiritual things of wickedness in the heavenlies, one
must know that Scripture is accustomed to call the air heaven, as the, The
birds of the heaven; and the, They go up as far as the heavens; that is, they
are raised high upon much of the air. Therefore also the Lord Saw Satan as
lightning fallen from heaven; that is, fallen from his own rule, and having

For he gave power to his own disciples to trample upon serpents and scorpions,
and upon all the power of the enemy. Since then his wicked tyranny has been
cast out, and the place around the earth has been cleansed through the saving
passion, which made peace the things upon the earth and the things in the
heavens, the kingdom of the heavens is thereafter proclaimed to us; John
saying: The kingdom of the heavens has drawn near; and the Lord everywhere
proclaiming the Gospel of the kingdom; and still earlier the angels crying:
Glory in the highest to God, and on earth peace; and those rejoicing at the
entrance of our Lord into Jerusalem, and crying: Peace in the heavens, and
glory in the highest. And in a word, countless are the voices of the
victory-songs, presenting the final overthrow of the enemy, as no wrestling,
nor contest remaining for us in the things above, nor anyone standing against
and turning us aside from the blessed life, but, =31.353= having our painless
passing on toward what is next, and enjoying the tree of life continually, of
which we were prevented from partaking from the beginning through the plot of
the serpent. For God set the flaming sword to guard the way of the tree of
life; which having passed unhindered, may we come within the enjoyment of the
good things in Christ Jesus our Lord, to whom be the glory and the power unto
the ages. Amen.
