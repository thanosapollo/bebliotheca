BY THE SAME. A discourse on "Pray, and pay to the Lord our God."

=55.593=

The teachers of this world lead children up to the knowledge of letters; but
the teachers of the Church instruct their disciples toward the perfection of
virtuous deeds. And it is possible, beloved, =55.594= to learn accurately from
the things just now read out to us in the psalm the most exact truth of what
has been said.

For what was the prophet of the Spirit, the great writer David, saying now?
Pray, and pay =55.595= to the Lord our God. As, for instance, the blessed
Judith, when once the Babylonian Holofernes with his own army had encircled
Jerusalem, and had cut off all the water-bearing springs that led into the
city, and the Hebrews were perishing by famine of water, f. so that they were
ready to hand over both themselves and the city to the one who was laying the
necessity upon them: but that blessed woman, coming forward with a manly mind
and a faithful purpose, hindered the surrender of the city from taking place;
and having stirred herself up to fight and make war against so great an army,
wishing to avenge her own race and to save the city, having trusted in God,
having given herself over by the strength of her body to the war against the
adversaries, she said to the priests and to all the faithful people: Pray, and
pay to the Lord our God. And she, going out of the city, when evening had
already come on, with one maidservant, taking provisions measured by faith
(for she did not wish to make use of Greek foods, being a Jewess, and being
forbidden by the law), and having come to Holofernes as a refugee, in a clean
robe and becoming womanly adornment, having enslaved the foreigner by desire
toward a woman, kept herself undefiled; for by chastity she was not defiled.

For at night, when the camp was sleeping, and Holofernes was stupefied with
much wine, taking his two-edged sword with good courage, she beheaded him; it
being clear that God was strengthening the right hand of the young woman. And
in that same night in which she had done the brave deed, running back as
victor to the city, and letting down his head through the wall, she displayed
him as a triumph to all the foreigners, so that on the next day the
foreigners, seeing their own camp-commander slain by one woman, pressed by
much cowardice and much fear, fled from the face of the Hebrews, and the
written word was fulfilled: How shall one chase a thousand, and two put ten
thousands to flight? For the most valiant Samson is found to have chased a
thousand alone, and the most valiant Judith with her own maidservant to have
put the ten thousands of the foreigners to flight.

You see how great a good it is to pray, and to pay to the Lord our God. And
Hezekiah the king, being a faithful man, and relying more on faith and prayer
than on arms [who] and military power, was working the victories over the
enemies beyond hope. At any rate, when Sennacherib had come on with a great
bulk of barbarians, and had whirled about Jerusalem, and was reproaching God
and the people who believed in him, and had sent written insults to Hezekiah
against God, and Hezekiah, having received the book and having read it before
God, prayed and paid to the Lord our God; whence at once he was set free from
the danger that was encompassing him; so suddenly and wondrously was he
receiving the help from God.


For one angel from heaven, rushing down sword in hand in the night, slew a
hundred and eighty-five thousand of the foreigners, none of them being able to
withstand the onset of his right hand, that the races of the foreigners might
be taught not to sharpen a blasphemous tongue against God. So great a good it
is, beloved, to pray, and to pay to the Lord our God. And David the most wise,
in the time of Saul the king of Israel, when the foreigners had encamped
against the race of the Israelites, and had put forward for single combat one
man a giant in strength of body, beyond a giant in the purpose of his mind,
speaking great things and promising great things; and this man was Goliath by
name, whose size the children of the Hebrews saw, and being pressed by
cowardice and fear they were held fast, fleeing the encounter of the war; but
the sharp-witted David, knowing that bulk of body has no power toward victory,
nor an array of arms and horses (For a king is not saved by much power, and a
giant shall not be saved by the multitude of his strength; a horse is false
for salvation, and he shall not be saved by the multitude of his power),
taking five stones, a symbol of the Pentateuch given them from God, the small
one, the younger, the one despised among his own and among the foreigners,
striking the proud giant with one cast of a stone upon the forehead, brought
him down upon his face.

And it is, beloved, worth inquiring how Goliath, having taken the blow
=55.596= upon the forehead, was not turned backward, but was dashed upon his
face. Surely it is clear that we must understand that an angel was standing
behind the God-fighter, hurling him onto his face. So great a good it is to
pray, and to pay to the Lord our God.

2. And Moses the blessed, having moved the people of the Hebrews out of Egypt,
and having come by the streams of the Red Sea, and the Egyptians having rushed
upon them with a strong and numerous crowd, and the Hebrews being able neither
to stand against the war, nor to procure their own salvation by flight; for in
front a sea was spread out preventing them from fleeing, and behind the dark
people of the Egyptians was being shaken against them sword in hand; and as
much as the Hebrews were overjoyed at the thought of the things that had been
done, so much, suddenly seeing Pharaoh and the Egyptians, they were held by
fear, and were driven in a flood in their thoughts, as a sea is driven when
violent winds have blown upon it, so that they even dared, because of the
excess of fear, to say to Moses: Do you see how far the matters have come,
even to the very end, and how fear crawls into our souls? For already they
threaten that we shall become a feast for birds, perceiving that we have come
to the last point, and that our affairs are altogether without resource. Why
do you delay, Moses? It were better for us to have died in Egypt, than having
come out here to see our infants and our wives and ourselves becoming a feast
and torn pieces for birds and land-beasts.

For Pharaoh, having trusted these things most of all, is full of pain, and as
having the authority in his hands, is insolent, and speaks boldly even against
God himself. Then what does Moses say to them? Do not fear, nor be cowardly;
Pray and pay to the Lord our God, and you shall see the help from God being
given to you. The Lord will fight for us, and you shall be silent. And when
such a prayer had been sent up to God, at once the wet sea was being turned
into dry land, and was serving them without hindrance. And it was possible to
see, beloved, a wondrous spectacle, the sea that once was foam-formed and
flowing, then being split, and a wall wet through, dark-blue, black-hued,
unbroken, being fenced about Israel on either side. And the water was hanging
suspended so far, until Israel, as when a man goes quietly and on foot, was
traversing the breadth of the sea.


And they themselves ran rejoicing as through a grass-bearing plain, but their
enemies the Egyptians were being sunk under water like lead. So great a good
it is to pray and to pay to the Lord our God. Or were they not also in the
wilderness in want of water and bread? For how were they not going to be
lacking bread and water in the wilderness, where there is no tilled field, no
vine, no tree, no herb, no seed, no harvest? But holy prayer was opening
heaven to give them manna, and air to give them meat, and was preparing the
rock to put forth steep-cut fountain-gushing streams. For they were being
served by these three elements, heaven, air, and rock. For heaven was bubbling
for them snow-colored water, and air was bringing them the fattened quail
without toil. And even the time of the day will fail me as I recount the
victories of the fathers, both prophets and kings, that came about through
prayer beyond hope, the good and life-bearing outcomes that came about through
prayer in diseases and dangers and the other more grievous affairs.

For so great a thing it is to pray and to pay to the Lord our God. If any of
you is held in some affair despaired of among men, or is pressed by some
necessity or danger, let him remember this scripture; let him pray and pay to
the Lord our God, and he will find the life-bearing and blessed end of the
good teaching. But if someone wishes, beloved, to take the verse of Scripture
otherwise also, and to say that to pray is to promise, in this way too the
word will be useful. For in many ways we have found in Scripture to pray, i.e.
to promise; as Wisdom says: =55.597= It is good that you not vow, rather than
that having vowed you not pay; that is, rather not to promise to God, than
having promised to lie. Therefore it commands, And do not lie in a word in the
time of your prayer. Did you vow that, receiving your daughter or your son
healthy from a heavy disease, you would dedicate them to God? Do not lie in a
word in the time of your prayer. But as the blessed Anna, being barren before,
having vowed that whatever she should bear she would dedicate to God, and
having done this, she herself became heir of an eternal blessing; and her son
Samuel is counted first and chosen among the prophets, and was so honored
before God that after Moses he was numbered together with Aaron: For
Moses and Aaron among his priests, and Samuel among those who call upon his
name. The first good is to pray, that is, to promise; to pay quickly with a
cheerful and good purpose.

For God loves a good and cheerful giver. Thus Abel was well-pleasing to God,
since from a cheerful and good purpose he brought the gifts to God; but Cain
was displeasing to God, since not from a cheerful heart, but as from grief and
necessity he was offering the gifts to God, being grieved at his brother's
good repute. And having terribly stirred up the spark of envy in his heart, he
was burning toward treachery, whereas he ought, having imitated his brother's
God-loving mind, to have brought away from the Lord an honor equal to his
brother's. But now, looking neither to kinship and family, nor reckoning that
God was watching his own unlawful deed, having drawn the young man onto the
plain with deceitful words, he undid the bright creation of God by murder.
This man first committed fratricide, first defiled the earth with blood, first
opened the gates of death. For death had not yet tasted a human being, except
that Cain through envy conceived the murder. For how did Abel's life harm you,
Cain? or how did that man's death profit you? or rather, did it not also harm
you? For behold, you completed your life with an unsteady mind and with
trembling.

3. O envy, brother of hypocrisy, craftsman of deceit, sower of murder,
serpentine seed, death-breathing flower. What is more defiled than envy?
Nothing. And what


also conceived death? Nothing else, unless envy, as Scripture testifies to me
saying that God did not make death, nor does he delight in the destruction of
the living; but by the envy of the devil death entered into the world. It
seems to me, beloved, that envy is an invention of the dragon; and it is
impossible for anyone to have envy, unless the serpent, having crept up to
someone secretly, and having vomited the poison from his own heart into the
heart of the one who receives it, casts envy in. For thus the envious man ends
in the same rank and power as the serpent. Have you seen the envious man? have
you seen the serpent? He speaks to you his own things as a friend, and he
learns from you your things as your enemy. The serpent comes to Eve as a
friend, bewitching her with sweet words, but snaring her with bitter thoughts;
bearing honey on the mouth, hiding the sword in the heart; turning the head
aside one way, and twisting the tail at you another way. Cain comes to Abel as
a friend; he speaks peaceful things, and thinks warlike things; deceit was
borne upon his mouth, and envy was being shadowed in his heart. The envious
man comes to you; he speaks to you his own things, conversing with you in
deceitful words and a lukewarm laugh, he brightens your face, he opens your
mind, that he may empty out the hidden store of your mind. He learns your
mysteries as he speaks, and he brings your hidden things before all. And if
you flatter him, he no longer endures, he no longer laughs, he no longer
relaxes his face =55.598= with a smile, but he stands accusing harshly, and
not moving, until he sees your disgrace, and then he will laugh. If in a
banquet something is said against you, that man opens his ears. Well done. If
something is said for you, that man, throwing in other words, sprinkles the
mind of those who hear, that the things being said about you may not bring
glory to you even in your absence. What is more wicked than an envious man?
Nothing. Among what sort of beasts shall such a one be numbered? If you liken
him to a lion or a leopard, you will find the wickedness in the envious man.

For every living thing loves what is like itself; but the envious man hates
his own brother, and murders him. The ravens were feeding Elijah on the
mountain, and the brothers, selling Joseph through envy, were eating. The
serpent deceived Eve through envy, and Cain murdered Abel through envy. The
whale hosted Jonah in the sea, but Saul through envy was pursuing David. The
lions stood in awe of Daniel, but the Jews through envy crucified Christ. Asps
when flattered are tamed, but the envious when benefited are more inflamed.
But that we may not seem, beloved, to chatter at length and to go outside the
things just now read, let us return to the matter at hand. You also who are
envied, pray, that you may not suffer any of the incurable things from those
who envy, nor fall into the same disease. For he who thinks he stands, let him
look lest he fall. You also who envy, pray, that having vomited out the poison
of the dragon, you may receive the heavenly bread in the clean witness of
conscience. Pray, and pay to the Lord our God.

Prayer has much power, beloved, making the things impossible among men
possible. Moses prayed, and dried the sea, and led the Hebrews through as
through a grass-bearing plain. Joshua the son of Nun prayed, and checked the
streams of the Jordan, and the written word was fulfilled: What ails you, O
sea, that you fled, and you, O Jordan, that you turned back? Elijah the
charioteer prayed, and bridled the rain-bearing clouds, and opened them again
through prayer. The same Elijah prayed, and was giving to the poor widow the
flour and the oil measured by faith each day. His disciple Elisha prayed, and
the Shunammite, being barren before, was bearing beyond hope. Daniel prayed,
and overthrew Bel, and killed the dragon, and the lions to look up to heaven


he taught. The three children prayed in the furnace, and the bubbling flame of
the furnace was being quenched. Hezekiah prayed, and having healed his own
sickness he undid death, and was receiving fifteen years of life. Jonah
prayed, and tamed the whale, and taught it to host him for three days, that it
might be shown that for the saints hospitality is found even in the depths of
the sea. The most pious Tobias prayed, being blind, and was receiving the
light from an angel's hand. Sarah the daughter of Raguel in Ecbatana prayed,
and put the man-killing demon to flight. Silas and Paul prayed, being in the
prison, and the earth trembled, the doors of the prison were opened, the iron
chains of the bonds fell off, and all who were liable to bonds were shown to
be free. Peter prayed, and raised Tabitha from the dead. The same Peter prayed
in Rome, and brought Simon flying down from the air, and no longer allowed him
to mock the Romans. Prayer raises the dead, puts demons to flight, heals
diseases, undoes deaths. O prayer, giver of life, store of health,
hope-bearing flower. Pray then you also, beloved, and pay to the Lord our God;
for to him belongs all glory, unto the ages of ages. Amen.
