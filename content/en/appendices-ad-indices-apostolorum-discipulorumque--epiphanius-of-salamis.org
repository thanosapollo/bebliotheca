126 Timothy, appointed bishop of Ephesus by the blessed Paul, was from the
Gentiles, not from the circumcision, to whom Paul also bears much witness,
admonishing him to keep the good confession of the gift given to him, who also
was martyred there gloriously. 127 Titus, bishop of Crete, preached the gospel
of the Lord to the Cretans themselves and to the islands round about, and
having died there was buried. Crescens preached the gospel of the Lord in Gaul
and is buried there. The eunuch of Candace the queen of the Ethiopians
preached the gospel of the Lord Jesus Christ in Arabia Felix and in the island
of Taprobane and in all the Red Sea, and report has it that he was also
martyred gloriously and buried there; and his resting-place is an
unconquerable weapon for the faithful against guilty barbarians, a place of
refuge driving away diseases and working healings even unto this day. Of the
seventy apostles of the Savior there have been, as Clement records in the
fifth of the Hypotyposes, Barnabas, 128 Sosthenes, Cephas namesake of Peter,
Matthias who was numbered with the eleven, Eubulus, Pudens, Crescens, in the
2nd, Barsabbas and Linus, whom Paul mentions writing to Timothy, Thaddaeus,
Cleopas and those with him.
These are the names of the appointed deacons. 1. Stephen the protomartyr. 2.
Philip. 3. Prochorus. 4. Nicanor. 5. Timon. ʹ. Parmenas. 7. and Nicholas. And
of the 70 the foremost are these? Paul, Mark, Luke, Titus, Candace the eunuch,
John the Baptist.
It is necessary to know how the 4 Gospels were composed. That after the
ascension of our Lord Jesus Christ 129 that according to Matthew after 8
years, that according to Mark after 12 years, that according to John after 30
years under Domitian the emperor, that according to Luke after 15 years. The
Gospel according to Matthew, written by him in the Hebrew dialect, was
published in Jerusalem, and was interpreted by John. That according to Mark
was dictated by Peter in Rome. That according to Luke was dictated by Paul in
Rome. That according to John in the times of Trajan was dictated by John
himself under Commodus, and was written on the island of Patmos. And of that
according to Luke, Luke being a disciple of the apostle Paul, whom the same
apostle mentioning in a certain epistle writes: Luke the beloved physician
greets you, and the same evangelist also composed the Acts of the holy
apostles. Neither more than the numbers, nor fewer, is it possible for them to
come to be. For the Gospel according to John recounts his sovereign and
practical and glorious generation 130 from the Father: In the beginning was
the Word, and the Word was with God and the Word was God, all things came to
be through him; therefore in the likeness of an eagle. And that according to
Luke, inasmuch as being of a priestly character, began from Zechariah the
priest, saying: There was in the days of Herod, king of Judaea, a certain
priest named Zechariah of the course of Abia, since according to the ancient
tradition of the law the priests used to bring bulls; therefore also his form
is depicted as calf-shaped. And Matthew sets forth his generation according to
man, The book of the generation of Jesus Christ son of Abraham, and again Now
the birth of Jesus Christ was thus, whence also his Gospel is portrayed as
man-shaped. And Mark made the beginning from the prophetic Spirit that comes
from on high in the heavens, saying the beginning of the Gospel, as it is
written in the prophets: A voice of one crying in the wilderness, the
prophetic image of the Gospel, therefore showing



and depicting also the image of the lion in the wilderness: Holy Trinity our
God save us by your grace. Mark the evangelist, bishop of the Alexandrians.
Luke the evangelist, these too of the 70 happen to have been scattered at the
preaching at which the Lord said: Unless someone eat not my flesh and drink my
blood, he is not 131 worthy of me. But the one through Peter, the other
through Paul, having returned again to the Lord, are deemed worthy to preach
the gospel, for which they also were martyred, the one burned with fire, the
other crucified upon an olive tree.
