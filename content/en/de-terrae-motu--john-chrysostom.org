HOMILY AFTER THE EARTHQUAKE.
=50.713=
Even if sickness kept us from dancing with you the spiritual dance, yet
the toil of the journey did not unstring you. For though the labor
brought you here streaming with much sweat, the teaching of the word
both turned our sickness into health and comforted your toil through the
psalmody. For this very reason I myself, though ill, did not bind my
tongue with silence, nor did you, though wearied, withdraw from the
hearing; but as soon as the word appeared, the toil receded, and as soon
as the teaching appeared, the labor fled. For sickness and toil belong
to the body, but teaching is an achievement and a healing of the soul.
And as much as the soul is better than the body, so much more precious
are its achievements.
For this reason, not only when sickness hindered, but even when
countless other hindrances arose, I did not cease from wrapping myself
in your love, nor was I deprived even today of this fair festival. But
until just now we were nailed to the bed, yet God did not allow us to be
utterly destroyed by famine. For as it is famine for you not to hear, so
also for me it is famine not to speak. So too a mother, often when she
is weak, would rather have her breast strained by the child than see him
wasting with hunger; let my body too be strained. For who would not
gladly even pour out his blood for you, men so boiling with reverence,
so boiling with love of hearing, who from so slight a turn of the moment
have displayed repentance? You do not know day and night, but you make
either season into day, not by changing the air, but by lighting the
nights with your all-night vigils; your nights are sleepless, and the
tyranny of sleep has been overthrown; for the longing for Christ has
conquered the defect of nature. You have been freed from being men as to
the bodies by imitating the powers, displaying vigil, intense fasting,
so great a labor of journeying, labor according to nature, rest
according to purpose. This is the fruit of the fears, this is the
progress of the earthquake, a progress never spent, a progress that both
makes the poor more well-provided and enriches the rich; it knows no
poverty, it knows no wealth. The earthquake came, and it has put the
unevenness of life outside.
Where now are those clothed in silks? Where is the gold? All those
things are gone, and have been torn more cheaply than a spider's web,
and have been exposed more than the flowers of spring. But since I see
your mind prepared, I wish to set before you a more abundant table. I
see your bodies worn out, but the soul young. Many are the springs of
sweat, but they wipe the conscience clean. For if athletes even stream
with blood for leaves of laurel, given today and withering tomorrow,
much more must you who have entered the contest of achievements not give
in from the labors for virtue, nor grow soft. Your theater is a crown to
me, =50.714= and one hearer among you is a counterweight to the city.
For some have crowned mixing-bowls, others have gathered Satanic
drinking-parties, others have prepared a lavish table; but you have
accomplished so great an all-night vigil, and you have cleansed the
whole city by the stepping of your holy feet, measuring out the
marketplace by your walking, and making the air holy. For the air too
becomes holy from psalmody, as you heard today
God saying to Moses: For the place on which you stand is holy ground.
You have hallowed the pavement, the marketplace; you have made the city
a church for us. And just as a torrent flowing past, and carried with a
great stream, overturns everything, so also the spiritual torrent, the
river of God, which gladdens the city of God, was filled with waters,
and it cleansed out the mire of impiety. No one is wanton; or rather,
even if someone is wanton, he is changed. He hears the voice, and the
mind is recast; the melody enters, and impiety is changed; the passions
of greed flee.
For even if they do not flee, yet just as wild beasts hole up in winter,
so the wanton mind is buried, and just as snakes, when the frost freezes
their bodies, sink downward, so these passions, the ignoble and slavish
ones, are covered as into some deep. Indeed even those who carry them
about are themselves ashamed; for they carry them, but they are dead.
For instead of winter your melody becomes for them. The voice enters the
hearing of the greedy man, and even if it does not cast out the passion,
yet it deadens the passion; it enters the hearing of the wanton and the
proud, and even if it does not kill wantonness and pride, it buries
wantonness and pride. And it is no small thing that vice should not
speak with boldness. I said yesterday also that great is the fruit that
comes from earthquakes. Did you see the Master's loving-kindness,
shaking a city and establishing a mind? shaking foundations, and fixing
thoughts? making the city unsound, and strengthening the purpose?
Consider his loving-kindness: he shook a little, and he fixed lastingly;
the earthquake for two days, but let the reverence remain for all time;
you were grieved for a little, but you were rooted lastingly. For I know
well that by the fear of God your reverence has taken root; and even if
there comes relief, the fruit remains.
No longer are there thorns that choke, nor does a flooding rain wash
away; fear has farmed you well, it has become an ally of my words. I am
silent and the foundations speak; I keep silence and the earthquake
sends forth a voice brighter than a trumpet, saying these things: The
Lord is compassionate and merciful, long-suffering and of much mercy; I
came, not that I might bury you, but that I might sinew you. These
things the earthquake says, and it sends forth a voice: I frightened
you, not that I might grieve you, but that I might make you more exact.
Attend with exactness to the word; since the word grew faint, punishment
cried out, since the teaching grew weary, =50.715= fear fought as an
ally. Conversing these things with you I come for a little, and I do my
part; when I have tightened you, then I hand you over to the word, so
that the word may not grow weary; finding stones and thorns growing, I
make the field clean, so that the word may with a lavish hand cast down
the seeds. What harm did you suffer by being grieved for a little? You
became angels instead of men; you were transferred toward heaven, if not
in place, yet in manner. And that I do not say these things in flattery,
the facts bear witness. For what did you lack in the account of
repentance? You cast out envy, you banished the slavish passions, you
planted virtue, having endured the whole night in holy vigils, with much
love and intense disposition.
No one remembers usury, no one discourses about greed, not only are the
hands clean of sins, but the tongue too is freed from lawlessness and
reviling; no one insults his neighbor, no one goes off to Satanic
drinking-parties; the houses are clean, the marketplace wiped; evening
comes on, and nowhere are there choruses of young men singing the songs
of the theater. But there are choruses, yet not of wantonness, choruses,
but of achievements; and in the marketplace one may hear hymnody, and of
those sitting at home, one psalming, another hymning; night comes on,
and all
to the church, the harbor without surge, the calm free of waves. I
thought that after one and a second day the vigil had unstrung your
bodies; but now, the more the vigil is intensified, the more your
longing increases. Those who psalm for you have grown weary, and you are
young; those who psalm for you have grown faint, and you have been
sinewed. Where now are the rich, tell me? Let them learn the philosophy
of the poor. Those sleep, but the poor do not sleep on the ground, but
they bend the knee, imitating Paul and Silas. But =50.716= those
psalmed, and they shook the prison; you psalm, and you have stood the
city that was being shaken.
The ends of the matters are contrary, but both are for the glory of God.
For he shook the prison, that he might shake the mind of the
unbelievers, that he might loose the jailer, that he might proclaim the
word of God; you stood the city, that you might loose the wrath of God;
both those things and these were ordered differently. Yet I rejoice, not
that the city stood, but that it stood through your prayers, that your
psalmodies have become foundations. From above the wrath, and from below
your voice; the voice sent up from below checked the wrath flowing from
above. The heavens were opened, and a sentence was brought down, the
sword sharpened; the city upon the ground, the wrath inexorable. We had
need of nothing but repentance, but of tears and groanings, and all was
loosed; God pronounced, and we loosed the wrath. One would not err in
addressing you as guardians and saviors of the city. Where are the
rulers? Where the great saviors? Truly you are both towers of the city,
and wall, and security. For those by their own wickedness made the city
unsound, you by your own virtue stood the city. And if someone is asked,
from where was the city shaken, even if he does not say it, it is
confessed that it was through the sins, through the greed, through the
injustices, through the lawlessnesses, through the prides, through the
pleasures, through the lie. And whose? The rich.
Again if someone is asked from where the city stood, it is confessed
that it was through the psalmodies, through the prayers, through the
all-night vigils. And whose are these? The poor. The things that shook
the city were theirs, but the things that stood it were yours; so that
you have become saviors and guardians. But here let us dissolve the
word, remaining in the vigils, in the psalmodies, sending up glory to
the Father and to the Son and to the Holy Spirit, now and always, and
unto the ages of ages. Amen.
