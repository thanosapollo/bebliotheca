=28.712= SAYINGS AND INTERPRETATIONS OF THE PARABLES OF THE HOLY GOSPEL OF OUR
FATHER AMONG THE SAINTS ATHANASIUS. Question 1. "Go away into the village
opposite, and you will find a man carrying a jar of water." What is the city?
Church. What is the jar of the water? Holy baptism. Who is the man? The
Forerunner. What is the upper room? The kingdom of the heavens. Question 2.
"The kingdom of the heavens was likened to a man, a king, who wished to take
account with his servants." Who is the king? God. Who is the debtor of the ten
thousand talents? The man who in many sins has enjoyed the long-suffering of
God. Who is the one of the hundred denarii? The one under the hand of him who
owes the ten thousand talents; and he himself obtained long-suffering, but
this one appeared raw and hard toward his fellow-servant. Another parable 3.
"A certain man had two children, and he said to the one: Go away into the
vineyard; and he said: I do not go, and he went away. And coming he said also
to the other; and he said: I go, and he did not go away." Who is the man? God.
Who is the one who said, I go, and he did not go away? the Jews. Who is the
one who said, I do not go, and he went away? The people from the nations.
Another parable 4. "The land of a certain rich man brought forth well." Who is
the man? Judas, and the greedy and money-loving after him. Another 5. "Go away
into the village opposite, and you will find an ass and a colt bound." What is
the village? The world. And ass the Jews; colt the Christians. The one who
bound? The Mosaic law. The one who loosed? Christ. For a colt is the unreason
and the ignorance of the nations. Another 6. "Go away into the village
opposite to such a one, and say to him: The Teacher says: Toward you I make
the Passover with my disciples." What is the city? Hades. The such a one?
Adam, for he was about to raise him from the dead. Another question 7. "And
already the axe also lies toward the root of the trees." Answer. Root is the
faith; the tree, the man; the axe, the end of the man. Another 8. "A certain
man built his house upon the rock." Who is the man? Holy Peter; rock, Christ;
house, the faith; wind =28.713= the devil. And the one who built upon the sand
Judas; for sand is the unfruitfulness. Another 9. "The kingdom of the heavens
was likened to a man, a king, who made a marriage for his son." Who is the
king? God. Who is the son? Christ. Who are the called? prophets and apostles.
Who are those who demanded? the Jews. Who are those who arrived? those from
the nations. Who is the one not having a marriage garment? Judas, and those
after him leaping unworthily upon the holy things. Another 10. "The Lord said
toward Peter: Go away into the sea, and cast a hook, and take up the first
fish that has come up, and opening its mouth, you will find a stater. Give
this instead of me and you." What is the sea? the world. What is the hook? the
word of God. Fish Christ. Stater the cross of the Lord, on which that one also
was about to be crucified, and Peter. Another 11. "A certain man had two sons,
and the younger of them said to the father: Father, give me." Who is the man?
God. The two sons? the righteous and the sinners. Younger son the one having
the unstable mind. The portion that falls, the oracles of God and his
commandments. Another 12. "A certain well-born man went into a far country, to
take a kingdom." Man, God; far country his presence upon earth; kingdom the
second presence; the servants the prophets, the apostles, and the martyrs. And
the mina is the grace, which Judas received, likewise also the talent. Another
13. "A man


planted a vineyard, and put a hedge about it." Man God; vineyard the world;
the winepress the baptism; the tower the faith; the husbandmen the Jews; the
servants prophets, apostles, and martyrs. And he sent them to preach, and they
killed them; likewise also the only-begotten Son and Word of God, and the
kingdom was taken up from them, and was given to Romans, who do its fruits.
And they themselves, being evil, perished evilly through their unbelief and
hardness of heart. Another 14. "The sower went out to sow his seed." The seed
is the word of God; the one who fell into the thorn Judas; for having gone
away he hanged himself, and the birds of the heaven ate him down; and that
upon the good earth the prophets and apostles. Another 15. "A certain man was
going down from Jerusalem to Jericho, and fell among robbers." Man Adam;
Jerusalem the paradise; Jericho the world; robbers the evil reasonings and the
transgression; priest the prophets; Levite the Mosaic law; Samaritan Christ;
oil and wine the precious body and blood, and the holy baptism; and the inn
the Church; the innkeeper holy Paul; the two denarii, the Old and the New
Testament. =28.716= Another 16. "A certain man went out early to hire
workers." Man God; vineyard the world; workers the apostles. And those who
came about the eleventh hour is Matthias, who was also voted in with the
eleven apostles. Another 17. "A certain woman had a drachma, and lost it." Who
is the woman? the Church. What is the drachma? Adam. Another 18. "The kingdom
of the heavens was likened to a treasure hidden in a field." What is the
treasure? Christ. Who are those who bought? Joseph and Nicodemus, or rather
the holy Theotokos. Question 19. "The kingdom of the heavens was likened to
leaven, which a woman taking hid into three measures of meal." What leaven?
the faith. And the three measures of meal? the three-day resurrection of the
Savior. Question 20. For what reason does the Baptist call the Jews offspring
of vipers? Answer. Because this beast eats the belly of its mother, and thus
comes out; just as these also did, having become father-smiters, and consuming
their teachers and the prophets by envy. For they also stoned Moses, and they
cut Isaiah in two with a wooden saw, sawing him. Question 21. For what cause,
when John had been given over, did Jesus withdraw into Galilee? Answer.
Teaching us to flee the temptations, but those who have fallen in to stand
nobly. Question 22. What did the fig-tree sin, that Christ dried it? and
through what matter? and in what manner? Answer. Since the Lord was about to
undergo the passion, in order that the disciples may not suppose that he has
inability and weakness, and through this suffers, in the soulless thing he
shows the power of himself; in order that he may show that he was able to dry
all those who speak against. But since he did not come to judge the world, but
to save, as he himself said, he shows the power upon the soulless fig-tree,
and he stores up the love of man for men with measureless forbearance of evil.
And sin also is said to be a fig-tree, which Christ our God cursed and dried.
And there is also this, because Adam took the leaves of the fig-tree, and
covered the sin of the shame after the transgression; having dissolved the
fault of the transgression, and having nailed and wiped it out on the cross,
he dried the covering of the sin, as the leaves of the fig-tree which Adam had
been clothed with after the disobedience. Question 23. What does he say, that
"You had five men, and now he whom you have is not your husband?" Answer. The
woman had men according to the law of the =28.717= Samaritans; they died,
after those she fornicated; for no one wished to approach toward her as a
lawful wife. And she, not bearing to bridle the desire, had secretly the one
fornicating with her, and he was neither conspicuous nor lawful. And she had


him as a husband hiddenly. Question 24. Who are true worshippers, who worship
God neither in that mountain, nor in Jerusalem, but in spirit and truth?
Answer. These are those dwelling in deserts and mountains, and caves and the
holes of the earth, who, without an ecclesiastical gathering, being illumined
by a divine spirit through good works, worship in spirit and truth God and our
Father who is in the heavens, living blamelessly and serving God with a pious
mind, shining in all piety and solemnity of the virtues, and they have no need
of a church or a place, but making themselves temples by the good workings, in
every place and everywhere they please the Divine, treating him without
ceasing and purely all the days of their life. Question 25. What does he say:
"If two of you agree upon the earth, upon every matter, whatever they ask, it
will come to be for them?" Answer. That is, concerning every matter, whatever
the soul agrees with the body, of a pious and virtuous seeking, God comes to
be in the midst of them, just as having conversed with Elijah on the mountain,
and Daniel in the pit, and having fulfilled their requests. Question 26. What
does he say: "Be well-minded to your adversary, until you are on the way with
him?" Answer. An adversary is the body to the soul. For these lie against one
another, as Paul says. For we bruise and lead the body as a slave, being
well-minded through fasting and vigil and the rest of the hardship, in order
that it may no longer rise up against the soul through passions of dishonor.
He says way, this vain and wandering life; for if the soul is loosed from the
body, and comes out of this life and of the way, it is no longer able to work
good. And a farthing is said even the leftover itself of the thought. And the
conscience also is said to be an adversary; for it convicts us in the heart
hiddenly, and holds us back from the evil, and if we do not hear it, last it
gives us over in the judgment-seat of God and we will give accounts both for
the great faults, and for the cheap and very least. Question 27. What is: "The
kingdom of the heavens was likened to a grain of mustard?" Answer. He says it
through the humanity, that "Being rich he humbled himself as far as death, and
death of a =28.720= cross." And great and luxuriant with the leaves through
the divinity. And birds of the heaven in its branches prophets and apostles,
and the evangelists themselves sitting. Question 28. What is the: "You shall
not make a sisoe from the hair of your head?" Answer. Some have interpreted
the sisoe otherwise; but I think the law forbids the shearing. For the Greeks
are accustomed not to shear the crowns of the children, and to let the locks
be, and after time to dedicate these to the demons. Question 29. For what
reason did Christ breathe into his disciples saying: "Receive Holy Spirit?"
Answer. Just as upon the blind man he made clay, and anointed his eyes, in
order that he may show that he himself is the one who formed at the beginning
the man from the clay; in the same manner also he breathed into the disciples,
showing that he himself is the one who gave breath and life to men through the
old inbreathing toward Adam. And having said to them: "Receive Holy Spirit;"
he gave them authority and grace through the inbreathing toward the forgiving
of sins. Through the coming-upon at the holy Pentecost he was furnishing the
working of the baptism and of the signs. Therefore he says: "And you will be
baptized in Holy Spirit, and you will receive power when the Holy Spirit has
come upon you." For before the passion the apostles were not doing the
healings in Holy Spirit, but by the authority and command from Christ. "For
Holy Spirit was not yet, because Jesus was not yet glorified." And he has been
glorified having been raised from the dead. And naming Holy Spirit not the
hypostasis of the Holy Spirit; for it was


always and is and will be, but the gift of the Holy Spirit, which according to
the tenth day of the taking-up, and Pentecost of the resurrection, came down
upon the disciples of Christ. Question 30. Who is the judge of the injustice,
who neither fears God, nor is ashamed before a man? Answer. God; for he
neither has a God whom he will fear, nor will he be ashamed before a man. "And
there was a certain widow in the city." And who is that widow? the soul of
every man, who once having the devil as a husband, and having thrown him off,
and having taken Christ into the breast, is a widow, and beseeches according
to each day and night, calling upon the judge and lamenting and saying:
"Avenge me from my adversary." Question 31. And who is the adversary? Answer.
The devil, who "as a lion roaring walks about, seeking whom he may swallow
down." Question 32. And what did the judge say: "Even if I do not fear God,
and I am not ashamed before a man, =28.721= yet I will avenge her, in order
that she may not coming bruise me." And the Lord brings on saying: "Hear, what
the judge of the injustice says," God; since he judges and condemns the
unjust. "For a law is not laid down for a righteous man." Question 33. How
must it be understood, that "He who has a garment, let him sell it, and let
him buy a sword?" Answer. The Lord said this wishing to make known to the
apostles his betrayal, and the onset of the Jews. He neither therefore was
wholly silent, in order that they may not be troubled by the sudden onset, nor
again did he wholly make it known, in order that they may not be held by some
cowardice. Question 34. How will the one who loves his soul lose it? Answer.
The one who loves his soul is the one who does his out-of-place desires; and
the one who hates it is the one who does not yield to it when it commands the
things that harm; and the one who wishes to lose it is the one desiring to
flee beyond what is needed from the dangers and afflictions and temptations
that come upon it. As therefore he also named the loss that comes to be in the
dangers on his behalf salvation; so he will find it into the eternal life.
Question 35. What is: "If your right eye, or your hand, scandalizes you, cut
it out, and cast it from you?" Answer. The Lord has not said this concerning
the members of the body; away! but concerning friends and kinsmen; for we are
members of one another. If therefore, he says, you have a friend and kinsman
being necessary to you, as a right eye or hand; according to what has been
said by Job, that "I was an eye of blind men, and a foot of lame men;" and you
should see yourself being harmed in the soul by him, cut him out from you; for
there is no communion of light toward darkness, especially if they partake of
some hypocrisy and deceit. Of Chrysostom 36. A certain one had a fig-tree in
his vineyard, and he came seeking fruit in it, and he did not find. Answer.
Scripture is accustomed to call a fig-tree the soul in sins; and a vineyard
the Church of the faithful; and vine-dressers the angels. Since therefore
living in the Church unworthily of the faith, they are named not a vineyard,
but a fig-tree. Having been planted in it from young age, as is likely,
through the baptism, and until old age changing the manner; reasonably
long-suffering God says to the vine-dresser, that is to the angel of the
Church: "Behold three years I come seeking fruit in the fig-tree, and I do not
find. Cut it out; why does it also make the earth idle? And he says: Lord,
leave it also this year, until I dig about it and throw dung." Three years
therefore are among men, in which God comes seeking fruit, the three changes
of age of the =28.724= man, or as children, younger men, andelders. For many,
having repented in old age itself, having been dug through afflictions, and
having received dung through temptations, bore the fruit of the virtue. Of
Cyril 37. For what reason is the Church called Church, and for what reason
catholic? Answer. Church, through calling all out; and catholic, because it
exists poured out through the world as a whole. Question 38. From where is
heresy said? Answer. From choosing something


private and following this out. Text 39. "Pray, in order that your flight may
not come to be in winter, or on a sabbath." Interpretation. It is to be noted,
that God did not make winter. For it has been written: "Summer and spring, you
formed them." And he says to flee, whenever the soul is separated from the
body. And he calls winter the sins; and sabbath the neglect; and woe the soul
in sin and despondency and neglect. Another text 40. "A stone which the
builders rejected." Interpretation. Stone Christ, who was rejected by the
scribes and Pharisees and elders of the people of the Jews, and became head of
the Church of the nations. Which is said through holding together the nations
in the faith into one concord. For since the offspring of the Greeks and of
the Jews have believed in Christ, through this Christ also was likened to a
corner, that he joined the offspring of those in the faith. And Christ is said
also according to some other manner a chief-corner stone, since, when a house
is founded, the builders add in the corner of the wall a chief-corner stone,
that is the one having a corner in its extremity. And such a one having fallen
in the extremity of the wall, joins and holds the two walls at once. According
to such a manner therefore Christ also is said a chief-corner stone, that he
joined the two peoples at once, both the people of the Jews and the one from
nations; and through this he has been named a chief-corner stone. Question.
What is that which Christ said to Peter ...? ηιξ δεσυντ θυαεδαμ. the crowds
coming, and having Christ in their midst some were coming secretly behind him,
and they were striking him, or others were asking him testing, Tell us, who
then struck you from the people hiddenly? and if you know, prophesy also and
say; show us from the crowd the one who struck you, and we will know that you
are a prophet. Question 41. What is: "And having inclined the head he gave
over the spirit?" For also all those who die first come to an end, and thus
they incline their own heads. =28.725= Answer. Since the Lord completed all
the things written, the incarnation, the birth, the baptism, the showing of
the signs, the working of the teaching, the appropriation of the disciples,
all the human things, the passion, the crucifixion, the mockings, the wounds
of the nails, after filling these things, which he ought to suffer; only the
death was left. And fearing it did not dare to draw near to him; through this
Christ inclined the head, crying to it. For until he did not incline the head,
it did not dare to draw near to him. Through this the evangelist says: "And
having inclined the head he gave over the spirit." Question 42. For what cause
did Christ cry out upon the cross with a great voice? Answer. In order that he
may show his power, and may frighten the Jews, and may lead them in toward
faith. For this voice also split the rocks, and tore the veil of the temple,
and shook the earth, and opened the tombs. And not even thus did the unfeeling
Jews understand. Question 43. How did Mary see the Lord, and in what manner?
For she was standing weeping in the tomb before the angels with a sullen
manner, and with a downcast look. How therefore did she see him walking behind
her and coming? Answer. And it has thus, that the angels were sitting before
her, and they were looking at Christ coming behind her; and she was not seeing
behind her; and the angels sitting opposite her were beholding Christ coming
behind her, and immediately they stood up. And the woman seeing the angels
leaping away was astonished, and again she turned into the things behind to
see what the angels beheld, and she saw Jesus coming. This is the cause of
Mary seeing the Lord having turned into the things behind. Question 44. For
what reason did Christ seek to eat with his disciples after the resurrection,
and another part was not found, but only a part of roasted fish, and from a
honeycomb? Answer. Some the from a honeycomb


interpreted to be salt; mythology of babblers these words. For the from a
honeycomb and a part of roasted fish are these such as Scripture says. And it
has some paradoxical interpretation and a very deep and most anagogical
exactness. The mind of this reading is this: the fish is likened to the hard
way of life of the ascetics, who have tamed the flesh by fastings and vigils
and the rest of the hardships, and have crucified themselves to the world and
to the pleasures, and have renounced the moistures and the foods of the life,
and have tamed themselves by the dryness, and go on the ascetic way, as Christ
commanded: "He who wishes to save his soul will lose it," in fastings clearly
and vigils, and the rest of the hardships. And how the fish is likened to the
ascesis, or the hardship, hear. A man is like the fish; =28.728= for just as
the fish, until it is in the sea, has all the moisture, and lives with its
blood and all mud; but when it is held outside the water, it is made dead; and
being placed in the fire it is roasted and dried, and loses all its
uncleanness; in such a manner is the man also; until he is in the midst of the
life, he lives passionately toward the pleasures and unbridled desires. But
when he stands away from the world, and lives in ascesis and hard training in
the desert, being warmed by fire of the Holy Spirit, then he is dried from the
passions, and is cleansed from all uncleanness, and becomes to God a
well-pleasing sacrifice through the virtues. Just as the fish becomes fit for
food having been roasted upon coals; so also the man, having been cleansed
through the warmth of the Holy Spirit, becomes an acceptable sacrifice to the
Lord. And the from a honeycomb again has such an interpretation. It is to be
sought therefore how the honey was not without a comb. And there is also a
comb without honey; and there is also honey without a comb; and there both
were found; and according to what manner, hear. The honey through the
sweetness of the Holy Spirit, which he was about to pour out toward the
apostles and all the faithful; and the comb through the seal, which he
furnishes to all the faithful through the baptism. For also the treasure, as
much as it is stopped up through a comb, so much is it safer. In such a manner
therefore also the wealth of the divine grace exists in the man inviolable,
having the seal of the holy baptism, and the sign of the cross, and the faith
into Christ. Concerning glory. Question 45. And know, that our Lord and God
Jesus Christ, after being incarnate from Holy Spirit and from the ever-virgin
Mary, and becoming man, and taking up all human things without sin and
weakness, and after working wonders and being crucified, and on the third day
rising according to the Scriptures, afterward was taken up, and sat at the
right of God. And hearing a right of God, do not circumscribe places and
figures of glory. For right and left are of the things circumscribed; but God
is unformed and unbounded and unfigured, and uncircumscribed. And when you
hear a right of God, understand the glory and the honor of God to be. And the
Son of God and God, as has been said, after filling all economy, afterward was
taken up; and the Holy Spirit, having come down upon the holy disciples at the
holy Pentecost, and having been poured out upon all flesh, was not taken up,
but according to generation and generation it goes through into all, and as it
finds each one of men, it tabernacles upon him according to the analogy of the
cleansing of each, sweetening beyond honey in the soul. Question 46. What is
that which Christ said, that "Whoever says a word against the Son of man, it
will be forgiven him; but whoever says against the Holy Spirit, it will not
=28.729= be forgiven either in the now age or in the one about to be?"
Interpretation. Christ has said this, since being double according to essence,


God and man, everyone who will set aside blaspheming into his flesh, it will
be forgiven him having repented, but to the one who has blasphemed into the
... Caetera desiderantur ... showing the third person like a man writing most
clearly his presence according to man; and the fourth like an eagle flying,
making clear the gift of the Spirit flying upon the Church, and the Gospels in
concert with these, in which Christ sits together. For that according to John
narrates his ruling and glorious generation from his Father saying: "In the
beginning was the Word;" therefore also he has been renamed eagle, as the
heights ... having stepped on emptiness, and from there having been initiated
into the unspeakable things, and the hidden things of theology ... having
drawn up, and having written the unspeakable and incomprehensible generation
of the Son of God. And that according to Luke begins from Zacharias the
priest; and that according to Matthew you write his generation according to
man "Book of generation." And that according to Mark: "Beginning of the
Gospel." Through this also four catholic covenants were given to men: one
according to the flood of Noah upon the bow; second upon Abraham upon the sign
of the circumcision; third that through Moses in the legislation; fourth that
through the holy Gospel, the grace and gift of our Lord Jesus Christ. VARIOUS
INTERPRETATIONS FROM THE OLD. Question 47. What is: "And the earth was
invisible and unfurnished?" Answer. That it had not yet been adorned. For with
the water lying upon it, it was invisible, that is unseeable and unapparent;
and unfurnished, that is unbeautified, unfinished; for it did not yet have
either flowers, or meadows, or plants, or a shoot. Question 48. What spirit
was being borne upon the water? Answer. To some the all-holy Spirit seems,
giving life to the nature of the waters; but I fittingly say that the "was
being borne upon" indicates the kinetic essence of the air, as also David says
concerning the God of all: "His spirit will blow, and waters will flow."
Question 49. For what reason, having said one the gathering of the =28.732=
waters, after these things did he name many? For "The water under the heaven
was gathered," he says, "into their gatherings." Answer. One is the nature and
gathering of the waters; and he named the gatherings in the plural, since
other is the Indian sea, and other the Pontic, and the Atlantic another, and
other the Propontis, and Aegean another, and another again the Ionian gulf.
And from outside again the greatest sea lies upon, which some name Ocean. For
this cause, as gathered, he named one gathering; and as being divided,
gatherings. Question 50. What light was dividing the day and the night, before
the sun came to be? For also the sun and the remaining luminaries came to be
later. "And evening came to be, and morning came to be, day," how must it be
understood? for not yet a sun. Answer. God said the light, and it came to be.
That therefore was the thing dividing the day and the night. And God said,
that "Let light come to be, and light came to be." This is not sun; and what
this light is, hear. This light is the morning one, which rises and lights the
world from morning before the sun rises. And let no one suppose, that that
shining is of the sun, which gleams quickly and lights the world before the
sun; but it itself is what Scripture says: "And God said: Let light come to
be, and light came to be." This light being much and over-shining, then God
divided it into the luminaries, both the sun and the moon and the remaining.
Question 51. What does he say, that "Let them be into signs and into seasons,
and days and years?" Answer. He calls signs the knowing a time of sowing and
of summer, and of vintage, the planting in


what time, when to cut wood. For each season has its own working. For the year
has four seasons; and each season has its own working. Question 52. For what
reason did God make the plants before the luminaries, and the animals after
these things? Answer. Because the plants are soulless, and neither have eyes,
and they were created before the luminaries; for they were bearing the
brightness of the light; and after the luminaries later the animals came to
be; since God divided the first great light which he made into the luminaries,
and it was scattered, and became lighter; for if it had not come to be thus,
the animals were not able to bear the much lightning and the brightness of all
that fire. And through this the animals came to be later, in order that they
may be able to bear the brightness of the fire. Question 53. For what reason
God having formed the man from the earth, did not form Eve also, but from his
side he formed her? Answer. In order that they may not have a starting-point
to say, that other is the =28.733= soil of Adam, and other of Eve; and
stronger was the soil which formed Adam, and from that are the good and
virtuous men; and from Eve are the evil and wicked, and the crooked men.
Through this God formed them from one soil. Question 54. For what reason, as
the man is a stronger and more paradoxical work of God (for he has made him a
king upon the earth, and he made him according to image and according to
likeness of him), how did God form him later than all? Answer. Since just as a
king, when he is about to go away into some city, first they prepare his
preparation, and furnishing his gift-bearing, and all his service and rest,
and after this the king arrives, so it has come to be also upon the man. First
God took thought of the things of his rest, and he worked his dwelling and all
his need and rest, and after these things he formed him. Question 55. What is
the: "Let us make man according to our image, and according to likeness?" What
is the "according to image?" and what is the "according to likeness?" Answer.
Not as men are, so also God is man-formed; away! But the "according to image"
is this: for just as God reigns in every world, and rules, and has authority
over all the things in heaven and the earth; so also the man has been set as
ruler and king of all the earthly matters, and independently he does what he
wishes, just as also God; and this is the "according to image." And the
"according to likeness" is the good and virtuous. For just as God is good and
merciful, and compassionate, and just, both long-suffering and meek, both
loving and sympathetic, both peaceful and reasonable, having mercy and
nourishing all men, and upon all showing the good; in such a manner he also
worked and formed, and wishes the man to be, in order that he may exist
adorned with every virtue, and ... he imitates God according to what is

the plot of the enemies, and in the desert they were nourished with the manna;
so indeed also those being baptized are ransomed from the noetic Pharaoh and
the Egyptian demons, and they are nourished with the heavenly bread of the
Holy Spirit. And since the word is concerning the baptism, let us say more
clearly concerning it. Concerning the holy baptism. First baptism that of the
flood into a cutting-out of sin; second that of Moses, when he passed through
the Red; for it was typical; for just as


there the sea, so the water in the baptism; and just as there the cloud, so
here the Holy Spirit. And what that cloud was, hear: When the Hebrews came out
of Egypt with Moses, he sent a cloud, as a pillar of fire, and it was lighting
them the night, so as for them to walk also the night, in order that the
Egyptians may not overtake them. And behind them a cloud was walking, and it
was shadowing them, in order that the Egyptians may not see them. And this is
the second baptism. And third the legal one, which the Hebrews had. For every
unclean one was washed with water, his garments were rinsed, and thus he was
coming in into the camp. Fourth baptism that of John, introductory, and not
yet wholly spiritual, but into repentance only. Fifth that through our Lord
Jesus Christ, which he himself also was baptized. And this wholly spiritual;
this we also are baptized. Sixth that through tears, truly toilsome, as the
one washing according to each night his bed, and repenting and wailing over
the things in which he sinned. Seventh baptism that through martyrdom and
blood, which Christ himself also was baptized, and much more reverend than the
others, by as much as it is not defiled with second filths. Eighth the last
not saving, but burning, and punishing unendingly and eternally the sinners.
Question 102. What does he say: "Flesh and blood is not able to inherit a
kingdom of God?" Answer. That everyone who nourishes his own flesh into
satiety, is not able to inherit a kingdom of God. Question 103. What does he
say: "If someone does not love our Lord Jesus Christ, let him be anathema,
Maran atha?" Answer. That is: Separate him from the Church and the faithful,
and let him be separated from the people the one who does not believe. For the
Lord came; for this interprets the Maran atha. Question 104. "If someone in
Christ a new creation, is he renewed?" Answer. That is: If someone believed
into Christ, he came into another way of life. For from above he was generated
through the Spirit, and he lives not the one in sins, but the life in spirit.
=28.761= Question 105. "Three times I was shipwrecked." Answer. Speaking
riddling mystically. For a third time the humanity was shipwrecked; first
through the transgression; second in the flood of Noah; third, after receiving
the law, the people fell out into idolatry, until Christ the steersman of the
souls having come, and having set the sail of the cross, built our unstormy
voyage into heaven. Question 106. "A night-and-day I have made in the deep?"
Answer. He says a night-and-day the spending before the presence of Christ in
a dark error; and he says a day the way of life after the presence of Christ
in the light-bearing baptism. Another. And some say, that sailing in the midst
of a sea; and others, that swimming and diving, where there was neither earth
nor a mountain to see. Text 107. "A thorn was given to me in the flesh, an
angel of Satan, in order that he may buffet me." Interpretation. Satan is said
the opposing one. An angel of Satan therefore he says Alexander the
coppersmith, and Hymenaeus and Philetus, who were tempting him, and were lying
against saying, that a resurrection has come to be; and holding him, they
threw him into prisons, and they showed many evils to him. Text 108. "My grace
is sufficient for you." Answer. That is: My grace is sufficient for you, that
you raise dead men, and you heal blind men, and you do other of the signs. And
being driven and being pursued you conquer those who pursue. Question 109.
What does he say: "To me a world has been crucified, and I to the world?"
Answer. The coming out from the life and cares of it, and becoming dead to the
passions, and to the desires and to fast, and to keep vigil, and to suffer
hardship in the ascesis, as also elsewhere he says: "And those of Christ
crucified the flesh with the passions and the desires." Question 110. What
does he say: "Having loosed the middle-wall of the hedge?" Answer. That is,
the enmity, which we men had toward God through our sins. Having been
incarnate therefore the


Lord and God, and having died he loosed the sin, that which separates us from
God, he made peace of all things, and he worked us sons of God, having torn
the handwriting against us upon the cross. And what the handwriting was, hear:
When Adam transgressed the commandment, and took from the tree through his
hand, and ate, the sin was written by his hand, and the devil was holding the
written thing. And when Christ came upon the tree, that is on the cross, and
was wounded in the hands with the nails, then he nailed it on the cross and
wiped it out. For the devil himself who was holding the handwriting of the
=28.764= transgression of Adam, he himself split it, since he was bound by
Christ upon the cross. Question 111. What does he say: "Rise, you who sleep,
and stand up from the dead, and Christ will shine upon you?" Answer. He says
sleeping the one lying in sins. For also the one living in sins is said to be
dead. And just as the dead man is unmoved, so also the one made dead by the
sins remains unmoved toward the good. Text 112. "Put on the panoply of God."
Answer. He says the Holy Spirit. For the one without share of the Spirit is
not able toward the opposing powers. "For also the wrestling is not, he says,
toward blood and flesh;" that is, the war is not toward men, "but toward the
beginnings." And he says beginnings the demons, since in this world they rule,
but of the works of the darkness. And the "in the heavenly things," since the
demons do not war us through a small work, but through heavenly good things.
For since we contend through the kingdom in the heavens, they war us harshly.
Through this it fits always to watch, and to conquer them by the power and
grace of Christ. And a shield of the faith he says the sincere and fiery
faith. Question 113. What does he say: "Having put off the old man, and having
put on the new, the one being renewed?" Answer. He says an old man the first,
the one generated from the fall of Adam, or the wickedness from him and every
evil; and a new man the citizenship according to Christ, that is a virtuous
and good citizenship. Question 114. What does he say: "In order that you may
not grieve, as also the remaining, who do not have hope?" Answer. Those who do
not have hope of a resurrection, it fits those to grieve. And attend, how upon
Christ he said, that he died and rose. And upon the common people, he said,
"of those who have been laid to sleep," in order that he may show, that those
who sleep will rise. Question 115. What does he say: "He did not consider
being equal to God a snatching?" Answer. That which he says is such: That the
Son of God, and God, having wished to come down upon the earth and to be
incarnate, did not fear to come down from the worth. For he did not have the
divinity from a snatching, as someone has a matter from a snatching, and fears
to lose it. Christ was not such, let it not be! but being perfect God, he
wished also to become man. Question 116. "For this we say to you in a word of
the Lord." =28.765= Answer. The word of the Lord, which he has said, that they
will hear the voice of the Son of God and those who have heard will live.
"This we also say; that we the living, those left around into the presence of
the Lord, will not overtake those who have been laid to sleep." Living are
those found then in the resurrection. For also men will be found in that day,
and they will not see death, except they will not overtake those who have been
laid to sleep. For first those who have come to an end will rise, and after
these things the living will be changed, and they will be snatched at once
into a meeting of the Lord into air. And how the snatching will come to be,
hear. As the Lord in a command, that is in a commanding, and the angels cry:
Rise, the Bridegroom came; then he will come down from heaven. And first the
dead will be raised; and after these things the living also will be changed,
and they will become incorruptible. And after this all the saints and worthy
will be snatched by clouds, and they will receive the Lord


our coming from the heavens, as he says: "And we will be snatched in clouds

the righteous. And the sinners wait for him to come down. And this comes to
be, just as upon a king going away into some city; the first and glorious of
the city come out into a meeting of him; and the poor and cheap wait, until he
arrives into the city. So understand for me also upon Christ, that, when he
comes down from the heaven, all the righteous and good will be snatched into
the heaven, to meet our Lord Jesus coming down; and the sinners stand upon the
earth receiving. Text 117. "No one serving as a soldier is entangled with the

will not please, if he does not leave all the cares of the life; how much more

Interpretation. For if someone serves as a soldier, and comes in into a war,
