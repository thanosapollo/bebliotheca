ON THE 115TH PSALM.
I believed, therefore I spoke; but I was humbled exceedingly; and I said in my
ecstasy: Every man is a liar. Faith must lead the words concerning God; faith,
and not demonstration. Faith, which draws the soul to assent beyond the
methods of reasoning; faith, not that which comes by geometric necessities,
but that which is engendered by the operations of the Spirit. In the name of
Jesus Christ the Nazarene, rise and walk. The deed followed this utterance
through the Spirit. From the miracle the acceptance of the divinity of the
Only-begotten became necessary for those who saw. What is more compelling
toward assent (tell me)? Intertwinings of premises drawing after themselves
the conclusion that follows from them, or so great a miracle seen plainly,
surpassing every power of man? But these things are not in honor even now, nor
do the works of the Spirit confirm the word concerning God; but artful
demonstrations, having their hope in the persuasiveness of the wisdom of the
world, not in the strong and evident revelation of the Spirit, which, through
those who in simplicity of heart have trusted God without curiosity, is
displayed for the salvation of the many. But would that it might be granted
even to me to believe worthily, that I might now speak to this great Church of
God five words in my mind.
And let no one mock the word, as if we were leading the hearers without
scrutiny into assent concerning the things of which we speak. For of every
learning the principles must be unexamined =30.105= for those who learn; since
it is impossible for those who wrangle about the first things to be able to
proceed in order and sequence to the end. And this you may learn from the
outsiders themselves. For if you do not grant the first principles to the
geometer, it is impossible for him to conclude the things that follow. And he
who opposes the first and elementary things of arithmetic cuts off the way
forward. Likewise also medical principles are undemonstrated for physicians.
And in general, in whatever pursuit of those who proceed in order and sequence
to the end, it is impossible to seek demonstrations of the first hypotheses;
but one must, having received the principles of the rational arts unexamined,
look in what follows to the consequence from the things assumed. So then also
the mystery of theology seeks the assent that comes from unexamined faith. For
one must believe, it says, that God is, not inquire, nor wrangle over what he
is. And in general, if faith is the substance of things hoped for, and the
proof of things not seen, do not contend to see already the things that lie
far off, nor make the things hoped for doubtful, because you are not yet able
to grasp them according to knowledge. These things then in general concerning
faith, on account of those who fight with words and have their hope in empty
phrases. But the psalm in hand seems to me to have a sequence in relation to
the thought that went before in the previous psalm. For he said there: I will
be well-pleasing to the Lord in the land of the living. Since therefore he
discourses about a land that is not seen, as one who clearly knows it, he
gives the cause of his determination concerning things unclear, saying: I
believed that there is a land of the living; therefore I spoke, that I will be
well-pleasing in it to the Lord. And in general, the soul that comes to
speaking without faith will talk nonsense in vain, advancing speech upon no
underlying subject. The beginning then of a sensible word is faith firmly
seated in the heart of the speaker. Then, whence it is possible for us to
accomplish the perfection concerning faith, he adds next, saying: But I was
humbled exceedingly. For he who has not humbled his own mind, nor says in
imitation of the Apostle, Brothers, I do not yet reckon myself to have laid
hold, but who speculates about a comprehension of the essence of God, and
measures the unattainable by his own reasoning, and reckons God to be only so
great as he himself has encompassed by reasoning; and in general, he who makes
his own mind the measure of the things that are, not reckoning that it is
easier to measure the whole sea with a small cup than for the human mind to
grasp the unspeakable majesty of God, puffed up in vain and lifted up by the
vanity of his mind, cannot say, I believed, therefore I spoke; since it is not
even possible for him to add what follows, But I was humbled exceedingly; for
he lives with conceit and boastfulness, and =30.108= delights in empty words,
not having acquired the strength that comes from faith. Then he does not
humble himself only, but also all humanity together, akin to himself and
sharing the same nature.
For I said, he says, in my ecstasy: Every man is a liar. So that he who is not
helped by faith, but comes to the word from himself, trusting in human
preparations toward the comprehension of the truth, is a liar, falling far
from the truth. And he says that he spoke this word in the ecstasy. And what
is the ecstasy? Either that, having looked around at human nature, and having
searched whether there is anywhere any truth among men, being unable to find
it anywhere without the help from God, becoming astonished at the paradoxical
nature of the matter, I cried out that Every man is a liar; or in that ecstasy
which I feigned before Achish, pretending epilepsy and derangement, since I
saw myself come into the necessity of lying against myself, and of inventing
things that are not, in order to escape the danger from the enemies, then I
said that Every man is a liar; even if not by choice, nor for the harm of
neighbors, but in any case taking refuge in lying through certain
circumstantial causes. Yet the Prophet does not fall into contradiction in the
saying, as some of the sophistic sort attempt to refute him, saying that the
Prophet has fallen into the refutation by reversal. For if every man is a
liar, and David also is a man, he too was a liar, clearly; and if a liar, he
is not to be believed concerning the things he declares. And if this saying is
not true, and not every man is a liar, we will also release David from the
charge of falsehood. But if he does not lie, let us believe him concerning the
things he declares; and believing, we will again be brought round into the
necessity of not believing. So that if David speaks truly, he undoes his own
saying, being both a man and not lying; and if he lies, from that very
fact he is not to be believed; for who will attend to one who speaks
falsely? So that since he once declared every man to be a liar, let David lie,
that he may confirm the saying; or, himself speaking truly, he has undone the
assertion. But these things belong to those who play, and who delight in the
turns of words against the inexperienced; but the truth is not so. For men are
called those who are still held by human passions; but he who has already
become higher than the passions of the flesh, and through the perfection of
the mind has passed over to the condition of the angels, this one, when he
discourses concerning human things, clearly excepts himself from the rest. For
he who said is without falsehood: =30.109= I said, You are gods, and all sons
of the Most High; but you die as men. You are gods. And certainly if to anyone
else, such a title is also fitting for David. For he is also a son of the Most
High, who through virtue has been made akin to God, and does not die as a man,
but has the living God in himself. I therefore said, he says, in my ecstasy:
Every man is a liar. Let those hear who are mixed with the flesh through the
passions, but lift their own mind above the things beyond the world; and it
is to be a common aim for all who claim piety to pull down their reasonings,
and the height of their heart that exalts itself against God.
What shall I give back to the Lord for all that he has given back to me? I
will take the cup of salvation. Having come to a perception of the countless
gifts from God, that he was brought from non-being into being; that having
been formed from the earth, he has been honored with reason, in which he is
also able to bear the image of the heavenly; then also looking toward the
economy on behalf of the race of men, that the Lord gave himself as a ransom
for all of us, he is at a loss, and seeks among all that belong to him what
worthy gift he may find for the Master. What then, he says, shall I give back
to the Lord? Not sacrifices, nor whole burnt offerings, nor the service from
the legal worship, but my whole life itself; and for this reason, he says, I
will take the cup of salvation, calling a cup the suffering in the contests on
behalf of piety, the standing against sin even unto death. And this would be
what the Savior himself taught in the Gospels: Father, if it is possible, let
this cup pass; and again to the disciples: Are you able to drink the cup which
I am about to drink? and he was indicating the death which he was accepting on
behalf of the salvation of the world.
For this reason, he says, I will take the cup of salvation; that is, thirsting
I come to the perfection through martyrdom, counting as rest of soul and body,
not as pains, the punishments brought in the contests on behalf of piety. I
will therefore, he says, offer myself as a sacrifice and offering to the Lord,
since I set all things as lesser than the worth of the one who has done good.
And these promises I am ready to pay with all the people as witness. For I
will pay my vows before all his people. Then an exhortation to the hearers not
to be struck with terror at death. Precious, he says, in the sight of the Lord
is the death of his holy ones. Do not shrink, he says, from the good contest,
you men, nor be cowardly toward =30.112= death; for it is not corruption, but
a starting-point of life; it is not complete disappearance, but a passage
toward honor. Among men who love riches, certain little stones, gleaming with
bright colors, are accustomed to be named precious; but truly precious is the
death of his holy ones. When a soul purified by the life, free of the
defilements of the flesh, having no spot or wrinkle, glorified from the
contests on behalf of piety, and having bound on the crown of righteousness,
shining out from all these things with the beauty of virtue, stands beside the
Master and judge of all, possessing the brightness from grace more translucent
than every costly stone, how is the death of such a man not precious in the
sight of the Lord? Let us not then lament the departures from here of the holy
men, but rather the births and the entries into this life. For the entrance
into this world is through the dishonor of filth and stench, and of things
whose very sight one would not easily endure, they being the things that lead
the way of our life; for through such things the passage of fleshly birth has
been legislated by the necessity of nature. But the exit and the release from
here is precious and illustrious; not of all men, but of those who have passed
through the life here in holiness and righteousness. Precious then is death,
not precious is the birth of men. For it is sown, he says, in dishonor, it is
raised in glory; it is sown in corruption, it is raised in incorruption.
Compare then death with birth, and cease lamenting the one who is being
released from dishonor.
When they were dying in the Jewish manner, the corpses were abominable; when
the death is on behalf of Christ, the relics of his holy ones are precious.
Before this it was said to the priests and to the Nazirites: You shall not be
defiled upon any dead person; and: If anyone touches a dead body, he shall be
unclean until evening; and: He shall wash his own garments. But now he who has
touched the bones of a martyr receives a certain participation of
sanctification from the grace that attends upon the body. Precious then in the
sight of the Lord is the death of his holy ones. Do not prefer the most
dishonorable to the most precious, nor become bad traders, preferring the life
that is being corrupted to the incorruptible and blessed condition. For apart
from the passions of dishonor, to which most of those who love pleasure are
subject, still also the needs according to the life, bending down the
high-natured quality of the soul, as it were enslave it, dragging it down to
the service of the flesh. And where there is slavery, there clearly is also
dishonor. One must therefore flee the life to which the dishonorable is
attached. I do nothing great, he says, giving myself back to you the Master.
For being your servant, I bring to you =30.113= your own possession. For in
every way the thing created belongs as a servant to the one who created. So
that he is a servant not only by choice, but also by nature. For I am a son of
your handmaid, whom the enemy, leading astray while she was still an infant
and altogether a child, and making her stand away from your service, made a
slave of sin. But I turn back and run again to the Master from the beginning,
and I recognize the ancient service. You have broken my bonds.
Since you have freed me from the bonds of sin, having gone down into Hades,
and having released humanity that was a prisoner of death, and held in the
inescapable prisons of Hades, for this reason I will sacrifice to you, not
four-footed animals that are cloven-footed and that chew the cud, nor clean
birds, or fine flour mixed with oil, or transparent frankincense, or that
incense of the composition (for these are offered to you, the Master, from
the earth); but what is my own possession, and an offspring of my own heart,
your doxology I will offer up from it, as from a certain altar of my own
mind. And I will minister as a priest to you the sacrifice of praise, more
precious than countless whole burnt offerings to you the God who has no need
and is perfect, who do not demand bodily sacrifices, in which the wealthier
have the advantage, but the confession from a disposition and a true heart,
of which all who will have an equal share. I will pay my vows to the Lord
before all his people. And sacrificing to you the praise, I will not, as one
ashamed, sacrifice somewhere in secret and in a hidden place, fleeing the
censures of the impious; but I will accomplish a public sacrifice, taking
the whole people as partner of the worship. And the place of the sacrifice
for us will be shown as the midst and the most conspicuous parts of
Jerusalem. Hear, you who abandon the Church and gather in common houses, you
wretched fragments of the precious body, that one must pay the vows in the
midst of Jerusalem; that is, of the Church of God. For not even according to
the ancient worship was it permitted for each to set up his own altar, but
one had been appointed for those who chose to sacrifice. But you raise an
altar against the altar of the fathers, and kindle alien fire upon it, not
even being sobered by the ancient example, drawing to yourself men corrupted
in mind, and defiling yourself together with them. From whose imitation may
you flee, you the people of the Lord, =30.116= and may you not be separated
from the portion of those who are being saved, remaining in the faith, being
perfected in the commandments of the Lord, to whom be the glory and the
power, unto the ages of ages. Amen.
