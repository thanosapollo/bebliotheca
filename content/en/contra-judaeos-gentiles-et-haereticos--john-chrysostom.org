* Against Jews, Gentiles, and Heretics
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

To the Jews and Greeks and heretics; and on the text, Jesus was invited to a
marriage.

=48.1075=

The nestlings of the swallows sit in the nest awaiting the presence of the
parents, who bring them food; and when they see them coming, they are winged
with the light wing of the mind. Likewise I also, being about to draw near to
this spiritual nest, crying out beforehand glorify God, that he has given me
such affectionate children. But indeed among the swallows the food is given
from mouth to mouth, but among us it is not so, but from mouth to hearing. For
those things proceed toward corruption, but these toward incorruption; those
nourish bodies, but these fatten the soul. And all these things have not been
said by me simply nor at random; but since the portion of the Gospel that was
read today leads us by the hand to this teaching, it is necessary to instruct
you. Jesus, he says, was invited to a marriage. But stretch yourselves, I
beseech you, attend to the things being said. Since some make marriage a
pretext, others children, others military service, others trade, others a
craft, others slavery, others wealth, others poverty, and certain other things
as many as there are; I wish therefore to show you that neither wealth
hinders, nor poverty, nor military service, nor trade, nor marriage, nor
children, nor slavery, nor a craft, nor anything of such things. Jesus was
invited to a marriage; did he go, or did he not go? It is clear that he went.
If then he went, how is marriage evil? Have you no defense? And I not only say
that he went, but that he also brought gifts; for he changed the water into
wine. Do not then make marriage a pretext. Your Master came to a marriage, and
glorified the marriage, and you insult the marriage, and say that marriage is
a hindrance? There is no hindrance toward piety. Do you wish to learn that it
does no harm at all to have a wife and children? Did not Moses have a wife and
children? Was not Elijah a virgin? =48.1076= Did not Moses bring down manna
from heaven? Did not Elijah bring down fire from heaven? Did not Moses spread
out quail? Did not Elijah by the word shut the heaven? Did not God converse
with Moses? Did not God also converse with Elijah? Did not Moses split the
sea, and lead the people through? Was not Elijah taken up in a fiery chariot?
Did virginity harm this one at all? Did the wife and the children hinder this
one at all? You saw Elijah the charioteer of the air, you saw Moses the
traveler of the sea; look also at Peter the pillar of the Church, that he too
had a wife. It is not written that Peter had a wife; from where then is it
clear that he had a wife? I say: Jesus, he says, entered toward the
mother-in-law of Peter; and she was sick, and he touched her, and the fever
fled from her, and she was serving. Do you see that Peter also had a wife? For
where there is a mother-in-law, it is quite clear that there is also a wife.
Do you see that Peter also had a wife? Do not slander marriage. And I say
these things, so that no one may make a pretext of either wife, or children,
or military service, or trade, or a craft, or slavery, or wealth, or poverty;
these are pretexts, these are excuses, these are plots of the devil. You make
military service a pretext, and you say, I am a soldier, and I cannot be
devout. Was not the centurion a soldier? And he says to Jesus, that I am not
sufficient that you should enter under my roof; but say a word, and my servant
will be healed.

And Jesus marveling says: Amen, amen, I say to you that not even in Israel
have I found so great faith. Did military service hinder him at all? But what
are you? Are you a craftsman? And Paul, he was even a plunderer of the
Churches, and after these things he became a herald and apostle. But what are
you? Rich? And Abraham, he too had a wife and children; And Abraham believed,
he says, =48.1077= God, and it was reckoned to him for righteousness, and he
was called friend of God. Did wealth and the wife, or the children, hinder him
at all? Have you no defense? Behold, the three things which you make a
pretext, this man has, both wife and children and wealth. But what are you? A
slave? And Onesimus; and Paul writing to Philemon was saying: Onesimus, who
was once useless, but now useful both to me and to you. But what are you? A
king? And David; and Nathan the prophet entered toward him, and rebuked him
concerning the sin which he had done. Did the king play the tyrant at all? Did
he pass sentence against the prophet at all? Did he not even in the kingship
confess the servitude, and say, I have sinned against the Lord? And the
prophet seeing his repentance says to him: The Lord has taken away your sin.
Sharp was the repentance, sharper the forgiveness. Did the king attend to
himself at all, and say, that I am a king, I am clothed with purple, and I
wear a diadem, and I have bows and arrows and shields and helmets and
soldier-defenders; and I fear this poor man, and I do not kill him, but I put
up with him? But he said none of these things; but even if he was a king, he
knew that he had sinned, and for this reason he repented, since he knew that
he had done a grievous sin. Did the kingship hinder him at all? But what are
you? A child? And Daniel, he both muzzled lions, and tore apart a dragon,
overthrew Bel, and condemned the elders, but saved Susanna, and the
incompleteness of youth hindered such virtue not at all. But what are you? A
lover of brothers? And Joseph; and how much philosophy and temperance did that
man display toward the Egyptian woman? He tore his tunic, but he did not tear
apart his faith; he conquered her shamelessness, but he did not fulfill her
desire. But what are you? A widow? And the Sidonian woman, who received Elijah
the prophet, and nourished him with bread and water; and famine did not waste
away the cause of the famine. But what are you? A harlot? And Rahab, who hid
the spies, and after the sons of Israel took the land of the promise, she
herself was saved and her house and her race. But what are you? Poor? And
Lazarus, and in poverty how much philosophy and patience he displayed, and he
was not discontented being in such necessity! But what are you? A lover of
women? And Samson; for even if he was betrayed by a woman, yet God furnished
him the vengeance, and he was avenged upon the foreigners. But what are you? A
lover of children? And Jacob. Do you see how many were saved? Do you have
faith? You have all things; do you not have faith, but are a king? The
kingship benefits you nothing. But I have not forgotten the promise. Jesus
then was invited to a marriage. And when he was present, they did not have
wine; his mother says to him: They have no wine. Jesus says to her: Go, woman;
for my hour has not yet come. And having said these things Jesus says: Fill
the water-jars with water. And they filled the water-jars with water, and he
made the water wine. And why did he say, Fill the water-jars with water? Was
then he who made the heaven by a word, and founded the earth, and all the
things in them, whose glance dries up abysses, at whom all the creation of
heavenly and earthly and subterranean things shudders and trembles, not able
to fill the water-jars by a word? But he says: Draw water. Why? I say, so
that, if they should deny, their hands that drew, and their shoulders that
carried the water, may refute them; since also in the case of Lazarus he did
the same. Before coming to the place, he says to the disciples: Lazarus our
friend has fallen asleep; then when the sisters of Lazarus came, and they say
to him, that Lazarus has died, he says to them: Where have you laid him? And
they say: Come and see. O new and paradoxical things! A little before you were
saying to the disciples that Lazarus has died, and now you say, Where have you
laid him?

You declare the death beforehand to your disciples, and you do not know the
place? He knew, but he was saying it because of the Jews, since they were
ungrateful; so that the saying, Where have you laid him? was not of ignorance;
since also in paradise he was saying to Adam: Adam, where are you? Did he then
not know where he was? But since the =48.1078= devil was saying to them, that
In the day in which you eat from this tree, you will be as gods, those men
were supposing that they were about to become gods.

And for this reason God was saying to him: Adam, where are you? from which you
ate, and you remained a man; for this reason God was saying to him: Adam,
where are you? Thus he also did in the case of Lazarus. Having drawn near to
the tomb he says to the Jews: Take away the stone from the tomb; and they took
it away. The sister of Lazarus says to him: Lord, he already stinks; for he
was four days. And crying with a great voice he says: Lazarus, come out; and
he came out bound with grave-bands. Was then he who transfers mountains into
the hearts of seas not able by a word to move the stone? He who broke the
stings of death, he who crushed the doors of Hades, was he not able also to
tear apart the grave-bands? And he says to them: Loose him. Why? So that, if
they should deny, their hands and their eyes may refute them; for this reason
he said: Loose him. And at the marriage he did the same, in that he said to
them: Fill the water-jars with water; and they filled the water-jars, and he
made the water wine. For this reason then from every side he was securing
them; for the Jews were always ungrateful and shameless. Do you wish to learn
how many things God furnished them, and they became forgetful? Learn; but
stretch yourselves for me, I beseech you, attend to the things being said; for
I wish to show you that from the beginning the Jews were ungrateful: Seventy
and five souls went down into Egypt, and they became six hundred thousands.
Joseph died, and another king arose for them, who did not remember Joseph; and
he was afflicting the sons of Israel exceedingly. They cried out then to God
saying: Lord, you swore to our fathers, that I will give to your seed a land
flowing with milk and honey; and now, Lord, remember the covenant which you
made with our fathers, and deliver us from the hand of Pharaoh king of Egypt,
and from the slavery of the Egyptians. God then heard their voice, and sent
Moses to them. But stretch yourselves for me, I beseech you. God says to
Moses: Take a lamb unblemished of a year, firstborn, and sacrifice, and each
shall anoint its blood upon the door, so that, when the angel comes having the
sword drawn, and sees that the blood has been anointed upon that house, he may
withdraw; but if he sees that it is not there, he may enter, and destroy all.

Moses then did thus, and gave it to the people; and they taking it, each
anointed their doors. And the angel was coming having his sword drawn, and he
was seeing the blood on the doors, and withdrawing; but where he was not
seeing it, he was entering, and he slew all. And all these things were not
happening simply; but since we were about to be saved through blood and wood,
for this reason God said, that Each shall anoint the blood upon the doors. Why
did he not say, Anoint the blood upon the wall? Because we were not about to
be saved, except through wood. Moses then cast them out of Egypt, and brought
them into the wilderness, and they say to Moses, Because there are no tombs in
Egypt, has he brought us into the wilderness to kill us? Was it not well with
us in Egypt, when we were eating the cucumbers and the melons and the onions
and the garlic? Did you see ungratefulness? They themselves were saying to
God, they themselves were asking, that Lead us out of the land of Egypt; and
having come into the wilderness they were ungrateful. Did you see their
ungratefulness? But learn the greater and exceptional thing. They spent forty
years in the wilderness; their hair did not grow, nor a nail; their garment
was not soiled, their sandal was not worn through; they did not labor, they
did not dig; they did not yoke oxen, they did not drag a plow, they did not
cut furrows, they did not pull up thorns, they did not cast down seeds, and
they did not wait for rain, they did not expect the hot rays of the sun, they
did not receive a time of harvest, they did not sharpen a sickle, nor did they
harvest, they did not bind sheaves and carry them, nor did they bring them to
a threshing-floor and toil, nor did they thresh, nor did they clean, =48.1079=
nor did they mix flours, nor did they bake and make breads, nor even any of
such things; but they were eating bread not farmed by hand, an improvised
table was set before them, in impassibility they were imitating the angels;
nature forgot its own weakness. And after so many benefactions they were
saying to Aaron: Give us gods, who will go before us; for this Moses, who led
us out of Egypt, we do not know what has become of him; and they did great
violence to Aaron. That man seeing that they were hard-hearted and
uncircumcised, says to them: Take off your gold things, and cast them in fire,
and if anything comes up, this is god. And the ox-head came up. Here for me
the ungrateful heretic, the parasite Jew who says that God begot, and
underwent a flux. Corrupt, wretched and miserable man, do you suppose these
things concerning God? But we do not so, may it not be, but that Father begot
Son, only the only, God God, perfect the perfect; for these are thoughts of
Christians. Since then you seem to busy yourself about the things above, tell
me your own, if you seem to know: How, when the gold things were thrown into
the pyre, did the ox-head come up? A bronze-worker did not work it, there was
no smelter; how, did the ox-head come up, do you dare to say? If you do not
know the things below, how do you busy yourself about the things above? He who
does not know his own, how does he busy himself about the things of others?
And they bring forward to us an evangelical voice of the Son himself saying to
the disciples: I am the vine, you are the branches, my Father is a husbandman.
Do you see, he says, that he worked the Son? For if a husbandman does not work
a vine, it cannot be useful. And they think that they are saying something
great and virtuous. But stretch yourselves for me, I beseech you.

I spoke toward the ungrateful Jews; let me speak also toward the shameless
heretics: Can you tell me, ungrateful and shameless heretic, that the
husbandman is not nourished from the vine? From your own weapon I master you.
As then there is a husbandman working a vine, so also the Father worked his
own Son. Let us see then, who is nourished from whom, the vine from the
husbandman, or the husbandman from the vine. According to you the Father has
being from the Son; for there is no husbandman not being nourished from his
works. What then? Does the Son nourish the Father? Not so, may it not be; for
these things are of extreme mindlessness. It is better to say, as a husbandman
rejoices over a vine, so also the Father rejoices over the Son; for he did not
say, that My Father who worked me; but, My Father the husbandman; for he was
rejoicing over the Son. Did you not hear him saying, that This is my Son the
beloved, in whom I was well pleased, when he stood in the Jordan streams? Did
he say, that This is my Son, whom I created, or whom I made? But he said: In
whom I was well pleased. Do you also strike out the voice? And again he says:
I have come down from heaven, not so that I may do my own will, but that of
the Father who sent me. To whom was he saying it? Altogether to the Jews;
since they were saying to him: This is the son of the carpenter, and they were
attending to him as to an adversary of God, for this reason he said, that I
have come down from heaven. And since they were calling him an adversary of
God, for this reason he was saying to them, that I did not come to do my own
will, but that of the Father who sent me. Since they seemed to honor the
Father, but were denying him, for this reason he was condescending to their
weakness; since how was he sent who is everywhere, who fills all things? Do
you see then that because of the Jews he said, that I have come down from
heaven. Did he say it at all because of himself? =48.1080= Have you no
defense? But they also bring upon us another testimony of the Apostle saying:
One God the Father, from whom are all things, and one Lord Jesus Christ,
through whom are all things. You say that there is not another God according
to the Father; and I say to you that there is not another Lord according to
the Son of such a kind. Why then do we contend? Not so, may it not be! Let us
see then to whom Paul was discoursing, and because of whom this has been said.
But attend with accuracy to my word. Since Greeks were worshipping polytheism,
but the Jews monarchy, so that he might both take away the polytheism and the
monarchy, for this reason he was saying: For even if there are many gods, and
many lords, whether in heaven, or upon earth, yet for us one God the Father.
And having said the, one, he took away the polytheism; for where there is one,
there are not many; and having said Father, he took away the monarchy; for
where there is a father, it is clear that there is also a son. For this reason
then he said: One God the Father, from whom are all things, and one Lord Jesus
Christ, through whom are all things. Do you see that Paul was stripping for
combat against Greeks and Jews; for this reason he said: One Lord Jesus Christ
through whom are all things; since all things have come to be through the Son,
the visible and the invisible. And hear John saying: In the beginning was the
Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. This one was in the
beginning with God. All things came to be through him, and without him nothing
came to be. Do you see that this one is the craftsman of all things. Do you
dare to show me that the Son is numbered together with all things? But
ungrateful men, until when do you go about anthologizing the Scriptures, so
that somewhere you may call the Son a creature and a work? Do you wish to
learn? If they hear concerning the Son a high word or one befitting God, they
go mad; they think that someone is throwing dung into their mouth. I wish to
tell you an example. Just as if someone should be inflamed with sickness and
many fevers, and when a little relief has come to him the physicians should
command that his mouth be smeared with honey; and they do thus as the
physicians command; and while the sick man is being cared for by the
attendants, the sick man thinks that the honey is bitter; and then those
standing by say, it is honey, sir, it is good, encouraging the sick man; but
the honey appears bitter in the mouth of the weak man, not because the honey
is bitter; for what is sweeter than honey? therefore not from the nature of
the honey, but from the weakness of the one not receiving; so also it happens
in the case of these; if they hear consubstantial, they think they are being
given bitterness to drink, and not from the word of the one speaking, but from
the weak hearing of the one not receiving. The Father calls him Son; the
apostles likewise, and these men creature and work. How a creature? Is the
begetting and the creating the same? If everyone who begets creates, and
everyone who creates begets, we find all the creatures to be offspring of God;
for since all things have come to be through the Son, therefore having created
them he also begot them.

Do you see that they are offspring of God, as you say? Until when are you
ungrateful? Tell me, in what manner is the Son honored? Because he is called
Son? You also. But what? Because God? You also. But you have to tell me: That
one in reality, but you in name. If in reality? therefore Son. Do you see,
that your mouth refutes you? not a creature at all, not a work at all. For
that one is Son by nature, but you by appellation; that one was begotten from
an eternal Father, but you were made from underlying matter. And these things
have been said by me toward Jews and Greeks and heretics. But let us glorify
Father and Son and holy Spirit, unto the ages of ages. Amen.
