* Letter to Dracontius
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

Of our father among the saints ATHANASIUS, archbishop of Alexandria, a letter
to Dracontius. I am at a loss what to write. Shall I then blame you as one
declining, or as one seeing the time, and hiding because of the fear of the
Jews? Yet whether otherwise or thus, what has been done by you is worthy of
blame, beloved Dracontius. For it was not fitting that you, having received
the grace, should hide, nor that being prudent you should give to others
pretexts for flight. For many, having heard, are scandalized; not as if you
were doing this simply, but as perceiving the time, and the tribulations laid
upon the Church. And I fear lest, fleeing for yourself, you be found through
others in danger before the Lord. For if he who scandalizes one of the little
ones would choose that a millstone of an ass be hung about his neck, and that
he be drowned, what would you suffer, becoming a scandal to so many? For the
unexpected harmony that came about in the country of the Alexandrians, at your
appointment, will of necessity be split because of your withdrawal; and the
episcopate of that country itself will be seized by many; and of many who are
not orthodox, but of those whom you yourself also know. And Gentiles who
promised to become Christians at your appointment will remain Greeks, your
piety having set at nought the grace given. =25.525= What defense then will be
given by you for such things? With what words will you be able to wash off and
wipe away such charges? How will you heal those who have fallen and are
scandalized because of you? Or how, after the peace has been torn, will you be
able to sew it together? O beloved Dracontius, instead of joy you have made
grief for us, instead of consolation a groan. For we expected to have you as
consolation with us, and now we see you fleeing, and we see that being judged
you will be convicted, and being in danger you will repent. And who will spare
you, as the prophet has said? Who will turn back to you unto peace, seeing the
brothers, for whom Christ died, harmed at your flight? For you must know and
not doubt that before you were appointed you lived for yourself, but having
been appointed, you lived for those for whom you were appointed. And before
you received the grace of the episcopate, no one knew you; but now that you
have become so, the peoples expect you bringing them food, the teaching from
the Scriptures; when therefore those who expect it hunger, and you feed
yourself alone, and our Lord Jesus Christ comes, and we stand before him, what
defense would you have, when he sees his own sheep starving? For if you had
not received the denarii, he would not have blamed you; but having received
them and buried them and hidden them, he would reasonably blame you, saying
those words which may it not be, even for your piety to hear: You ought to
have put my denarii to the bankers, that when I came I also might demand them
from those. I exhort you, spare yourself and us; yourself, that you may not be
in danger; us, that we may not be grieved over you. Take thought for the
Church, lest through you many of the little ones be harmed, and the others
also take a pretext for withdrawing. If then you feared the time, and cowering
did this, the mind is not manly; for one ought in such things to display the
zeal according to Christ, and rather to speak freely in the circumstances, and
to say the words of the blessed Paul: In all these things we more than
conquer; and especially, that it is not fitting to be a slave to the time, but
to the Lord; but if the ordering of the Churches does not please you, and you
do not think the ministry of the episcopate has a reward, you have made
yourself one who despises the Saviour who ordered these things; I exhort you,
do not think such things, nor endure those who counsel these things; for they
are not worthy of Dracontius. For what the Lord has patterned through the
apostles remains good and firm; but the cowardice of the brothers will cease.
=25.528= For if all had the same mind that those who now counsel you have, how
would you have become a Christian, there being no bishops? And if those after
us also take up such a mind, how will the Churches be able to stand? Or do
those who counsel you think you have received nothing, because they despise
it? But this too is false. For it is time for them to think that the grace of
the bath also is nothing, if some despise it. But you have received, O beloved
Dracontius; do not endure those who counsel you, nor deceive yourself. For
this will be demanded by God who gave it. Or have you not heard the Apostle
saying: Do not neglect the gift that is in you? Or have you not read how he
receives the one who doubled, but condemned the one who hid? And may it be
that you return quickly, that you also may become of those who are praised. Or
tell, of whom do those who counsel you want you to be an imitator? For we must
live according to the aim of the saints and of the Fathers, and imitate them;
and know that, departing from these, we become also strangers to their
communion. Whom then do those want you to imitate? The one who hesitates and
wishes to follow, but postpones and takes counsel because of his own people;
or the blessed Paul, who as soon as the stewardship was entrusted to him did
not confer with flesh and blood? For even if he said, I am not sufficient to
be called an apostle, yet knowing what he received, and not being ignorant of
the one who gave, he wrote: For woe is me if I do not preach the gospel; and
as the woe is mine if I do not preach, so teaching and preaching the Gospel he
had those being discipled as joy and a crown; therefore also there was
eagerness of the saints to preach as far as Illyricum, and not to hesitate
even to go to Rome, nor to go up into the Spains, that as much as he toils, so
much the greater reward of the toil he may also receive. He boasted then that
he had fought the good fight, and he was confident that he would receive the
great crown. Therefore, beloved Dracontius, imitating whom do you do this?
This one, or those who are not such? I pray that you and I myself may become
imitators of all the saints. Or perhaps some who counsel you to hide for this
reason, as if you had given a word and an oath not to stand, if you should be
appointed? For I hear them buzzing such things in the ears, and seeming in
these things to be pious; but if they were truly pious, they would have been
pious especially toward God who laid the ministry upon you. And if they had
read the divine =25.529= Scriptures, they would not have counseled contrary to
these. For it is time for them also to blame Jeremiah, and to accuse the great
Moses, because they did not hear their counsel, but fearing God they completed
the ministry, and prophesying they were perfected. For they too, having been
sent, and having already received the grace of prophecy, declined, but
afterward they feared, and did not set at nought the one who sent them.
Whether then you are weak-voiced and slow of tongue, yet fear God who formed
you; whether you say you are too young for the preaching, yet revere him who
knew you before you were formed; whether you have given a word (and the word
to the saints was as an oath), yet read Jeremiah, how he also said, I will not
name the name of the Lord, but afterward fearing the fire that had come to be
in him, he no longer did as he had said, nor as having been caught by the oath
did he hide; but revering the one who entrusted it, he completed the prophecy.
Or do you not know, beloved, that Jonah also, fleeing, became as he became,
but after these things he prophesied again? Do not then take counsel other
than these things. For the Lord knows our things better than we, and he knows
to whom he entrusts his own Churches. For even if someone happens not to be
worthy, yet let him not look to the former life, but let him complete the
ministry; lest in addition to the life he also carry away the curse from
negligence. Do you then, beloved Dracontius, knowing these things and being
intelligent, not pierce your soul? Do you not take thought lest one of those
entrusted to you perish? Are you not burned as by fire of conscience? Do you
not fear the day of judgment, on which none of those now counseling will
become a helper there? For each will also give account of those entrusted to
him. For what did the pretext profit the one who hid the denarius? Or what did
it help Adam to have said, The woman deceived me? Beloved Dracontius, even if
you are truly weak, yet it is fitting that you take up the care, lest, the
Church being deserted, the enemies harm it, having found your flight as a
pretext. It is fitting that you gird yourself, lest you leave us alone in the
contest; it is fitting that you share the toil, that with all you also receive
the reward. Hasten therefore, beloved, delaying no longer, and do not endure
those who hinder you, but remember the one who gave; and come to us who love
you, who counsel you the things of the Scriptures, that you may also be sent
forth by us, and ministering in the =25.532= Churches you may remember us
also. For you were not the only one appointed from monks, nor were you the
only one who presided over a monastery, or the only one loved by monks; but
you know that Serapion also is a monk, and he presided over so many monks; it
has not escaped you of how many monks Apollos became a father. You know
Agathon, and you are not ignorant of Ariston; you remember Ammonius, who went
abroad with Serapion; and perhaps you have also heard concerning Muitus in the
upper Thebaid, and you can learn concerning Paul who is in Latos, and
concerning many others. And yet these, having been appointed, did not
contradict, but having Elisha as a pattern, and knowing the things concerning
Elijah, and having learned the things concerning the disciples and apostles,
they took up the care, and did not despise the ministry, and they have not
become worse than themselves, but they rather expect the reward of the toil,
advancing and admonishing others to advance. How many have they turned from
the idols? How many have they stopped from the demonic custom, admonishing
them? How many have they presented as servants to the Lord, so that even those
who see, seeing these signs, marvel? Or is it not a great sign to make a
maiden live as a virgin, and a young man practice self-control, and an
idolater come to know Christ? Therefore let not monks hinder you, as if you
alone were appointed from monks, nor do you make the pretext that you will
become worse than yourself; for you can also become better, imitating Paul,
zealous for the deeds of the saints. For you know that such men, having been
appointed stewards of the mysteries, rather pursued according to the aim unto
the prize of the upward calling. When did Paul bear witness, and expect to
receive the crown, if not when he was sent to teach? When did Peter confess,
if not when he was preaching the gospel, and became a fisher of men? When was
Elijah taken up, if not when he fulfilled all the prophecy? When did Elisha
have a double portion in the spirit, if not when leaving all he followed
Elijah? And why did the Saviour also choose disciples, if not that he might
also send them? Therefore, having these as a pattern, beloved Dracontius, do
not say, nor be persuaded by those who say that the episcopate is a pretext
for sins, nor that from this there is an occasion of sinning. For it is
possible for you even being a =25.533= bishop to hunger, and to thirst, as
Paul; you are able not to drink wine, as Timothy; and to fast yourself also
continually, as Paul did; that fasting thus according to him, you may fill
others with the words, and thirsting in not drinking, you may give others to
drink by teaching. Therefore let not those who counsel you put these things
forward. For we know also bishops who fast, and monks who eat. We know also
bishops who do not drink wine, and monks who drink; we know also bishops who
work signs, and monks who do not. And many of the bishops have not even
married, and monks have become fathers of children; just as also bishops
fathers of children, and monks being of a whole family. And again we know
clerics who hunger, and monks who fast. For it is possible both thus, and in
that way it is not forbidden. But let a man contend everywhere; for the crown
is given not according to place, but according to the deed. Therefore do not
endure those who counsel other things besides these, but rather make haste,
and do not delay; especially since the holy feast also draws near, lest the
peoples keep the feast without you, and you draw a great danger upon yourself.
For who will proclaim the Pascha to them if you are not present? Who will
announce to them the day of the resurrection, while you are hiding? Who will
counsel them to feast as they ought, while you are fleeing? O how many will be
benefited if you come, and how many will be harmed if you flee! And who will
accept you for these things? And why do they counsel you not to take up the
episcopate, themselves wanting to have presbyters? For if you are worthless,
let them not be with you; but if they recognize that you are fit, let them not
envy the others; for if teaching and presiding is a pretext for sin according
to them, let them themselves not be taught, nor have presbyters, lest they
become worse than themselves, both they and those who teach them. But do not
attend to these human words, nor endure those who counsel these things, as I
have often said before; but rather hasten and turn to the Lord, that, taking
thought for his sheep, you may remember us also. And for this reason I urged
our beloved Hierax the presbyter, and Maximus the reader, to come; that they
may also urge you by words, and that you may be able to learn from this both
the disposition with which I wrote, and the danger from contradicting the
ecclesiastical order.
