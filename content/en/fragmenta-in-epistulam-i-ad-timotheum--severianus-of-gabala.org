* Fragments on the First Epistle to Timothy
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: h1
:END:

1 Tim 1:1 He has not set "according to commandment" in the other epistles. Why
does he set it now? Because he was about to legislate certain things and to
set forth ecclesiastical canons. Taking the lead, he persuades that he is
about to speak according to the commandment of God. 1 Tim 1:8-9 And yet the
law has the things that must be done, and grace the things that must be
forbidden, but he named each from what is in excess, and especially because in
the grace of the savior there are commandments (not laws), but they are left
to free choice. Which things if they do not come about, the one who has not
done them is not altogether condemned, as for instance the possession of
virginity and if there is any other such thing. 1 Tim 2:5-6 But perhaps
someone will say: how do you say that the one who died is God, when Paul has
clearly said in the things he wrote to Timothy: a man, Jesus Christ, who gave
himself a ransom for all. What then? Do you not hear him saying that the Lord
of glory was crucified? And that from Judah is Christ according to the flesh,
who is God over all, blessed unto the ages, amen? And that Jesus Christ
yesterday and today, the same also unto the ages? For he was one from both,
from divinity and from humanity, the natures being complete according to
their own account, being consubstantial with the Father according to the
divinity and the same consubstantial with us men according to the humanity.
For indeed even the man according to us, being from soul and body and coming
to completion into one hypostasis, we say is a mortal rational animal: but
mortal according to the body, and rational according to the soul. Yet the
whole animal is named mortal and the whole is called rational, and neither are
the things from which 337 it has been composed naturally confused, nor indeed
is it divided into two persons. Thus also Emmanuel, being one from two natures
and one hypostasis and one nature of the Word himself incarnate, neither
confused the things from which the ineffable union came to be, nor, remaining
one, does he give to the dyad an entrance from which division [comes]: for
that which is one in the proper sense could never become two, but having
shifted into two it will altogether have ceased from being one. Therefore
Jesus Christ being one, even if someone should name him in part and say man,
he is understood as God incarnate, and conversely if he should call him God,
he is signified as the Word become man. For this reason, knowing him to be one
and undivided, we necessarily use the distinction beforehand, saying
consubstantial with the Father according to the divinity and the same
consubstantial with us according to the humanity, and the same passible in the
flesh and impassible in the divinity. And according to the like figure one may
see also that, as the inspired Scripture goes through: for in one place Christ
is said to have been delivered by the Father, and in another it is written
that he himself delivered himself. And writing to the Romans Paul says
concerning the Father: who did not spare his own Son, but delivered him up for
us all. And to Timothy he writes these things: one God and mediator of God and
men, the man Jesus Christ, who gave himself a ransom for all. And see for me
the polished and exact [character] of the Scripture. For where he said that
Christ is the own Son of God and Father, and said: who did not spare his own
Son, making clear that he is own who exists of the same substance and not
alien, then indeed the humbled and economic [phrase]: he delivered him up for
us all. But where the economic went first, and he said: a man, Jesus Christ,
he added the God-befitting [phrase] and did not say "the one delivered" but
who gave himself a ransom for all. And writing to the Galatians he says thus:
and what I now live in the flesh, I live in faith in the Son of God who loved
me and delivered himself up for me. Why then, running past the things said so
most skillfully and with all most God-befitting harmony, and clinging to
Jewish blindness and pettiness, do the children of Nestorius bring forward
that Christ was delivered or was raised by the Father, and try through these,
as it seems to them, to show him not true God after he became man truly. 1 Tim
2:14-15 Since this woman came to be in transgression, she will be saved
through the children that will be to her and the descendants who will
accomplish 338 piety. What then, if children are not born to her, or they are
born, but not males? But even if they come to be for some, they come to be for
the common race: through whom the transgression is spoken universally of the
race of women. For if the one woman is the cause of the evil to all, how is it
not likely that the many, through the generation of the pious, should dissolve
the evil brought upon them through one woman, Christ having given the
beginning of the dissolution through himself? From what then will the woman be
saved? From the transgression and the disobedience: so that she must also have
her own accomplishments, lest, the disobedience having been forgiven, the
other sins condemn her. And it was not said by him simply: she will be saved
through childbearing, but that the mothers, having their salvation upon the
children, may bring them up in all piety and righteousness. And to be saved
through childbearing. She is not judged through the things in which the woman
transgressed, but she will be saved through those in which she becomes mother
of those who obey, that is according to the account of obedience, since she
has otherwise her own accomplishments through which she is saved. And he says
this, as we said beforehand, that the mothers may bring up the children in
piety. From the reproach of the transgression she will be saved through
childbearing. For the apostle says this, not that he may reproach, but that he
may exhort toward piety and toward bringing up the children in faith. 1 Tim
3:1 He did not say of honor, he did not say of escort, but of a good work,
such as a good course, a good labor on account of the presidency of the
churches. If anyone aspires to the episcopate, he desires a good work. He
yielded not the [matter] of the desire but that of the practice, that one may
furnish himself toward fitness for the episcopate; or rather the concession of
the desire is an exhortation of a good life, and as it were an approach comes
to be toward well-doing and the fairest life, so that having seemed to have
conceded the desire, through the things he adds he exhorts more. He yielded
the [matter] of the desire, not that of the practice, that one may furnish
himself toward fitness for the episcopate through good works and faith. Rather
then the concession of desire is an exhortation of a good life. And the desire
is as it were an entrance into well-doing and the fairest life. He enumerates
so many things as must belong to the bishop, which suffice rather to turn
[one] away, so that having seemed to have conceded the desire, through the
things he lists of what sort the bishop must be, he turns [one] away more. 339
1 Tim 3:2-3 Most instructively he exhorts, bringing on in part the kinds of
the blameless way of life. Not [meaning] to strike while accusing, but the one
moved without reason: for a just accusation puts to shame, but an irrational
blame strikes. 1 Tim 3:16 i.e. he appeared in flesh, but was justified in
spirit, having conducted himself. For even if he was in flesh, yet he did not
live according to flesh, but he subjected the flesh to the spirit, opening for
us a way of righteousness. Or he also says this, that as the Spirit was doing
signs, Christ was being justified according to our confession and faith. He
appeared to angels. Who? The crucified Christ. And he does not say this, that
the angels do not see his divinity: for the savior himself said that the
angels see the face of my Father in the heavens. And when he says: no one has
ever seen God, it is clear that he has not seen as he is, but as far as it is
possible for angel and man to see. God then is invisible both to angels and to
men, but he appeared through the economy. The church upon the earth was
justified in spirit through the confession. This is like: that you may be
justified in your words and overcome when you are judged: for as the Spirit
was doing the signs, he was being justified according to our confession and
the faith of those who believe. The one who has come for the salvation of men
appeared to angels: he showed through this that the divinity of the
Only-begotten is invisible even to angels, but he appeared to them through the
flesh. He was taken up in glory: upon clouds, he says. 1 Tim 5:10 i.e. if she
did not reject, being a widow, the children she had. If she lodged strangers:
the faithful, clearly, and while being counted in marriage. If she washed the
feet of saints. He added this to show that one must also receive them with
care. And it is clear that requiring also the 340 things according to power he
says all these: for she is not more destitute than the one who cast in the two
obols. 1 Tim 5:12 Because having promised to Christ to remain thus they later
married. And he says first faith i.e. first submission, and of old first the
one that is foremost and extreme and highest of all for them. 1 Tim 5:17
Teaching is the simple tradition through the words of Scripture and the
interpretation of the thoughts in the words, and word is that which presents
the thoughts. 1 Tim 5:24-25 Since he said above: lay hands quickly on no one,
nor share in others' sins, now he says the cause on account of which he said
that. Not of all are the sins manifest: for many also sin while hiding. He
says then: test by time: for what was able to escape notice today is exposed
tomorrow, and of the sins some are manifest, leading forth to the judgment of
men (for all know that they sin and judge them unworthy), but some appear
after these, and the things that are otherwise cannot be hidden. Since then
the things concerning men are thus, lay hands quickly on no one, lest he be
found sinning in what has escaped notice and be advanced unworthily and you
share with him in the fault. Likewise also the good works are evident
beforehand. Upon the good works he said this only, that they cannot be hidden
(he did not say that they escape notice), but upon the evil works some are
evident beforehand, and some escape notice. Why? Because one does not quickly
hide the good: for he who does the truth comes to the light, but the one who
does evil hides, wanting to escape notice. 1 Tim 6:2 The devout and faithful
master quickly set himself to receive of the slave's well-doing and goodwill,
and being kind, he becomes well-disposed toward him. 341 Upon "those who
partake of the well-doing," i.e. those who are sensitive toward making a
return. 1 Tim 6:5 Just as from the rubbings of both stone and wood fire is
born, so from the gathering of the worthless impiety is generated. 1 Tim 6:13
It was not said simply "who makes alive," but that he may remind that the one
who makes alive and gives the revival to each will also render to each a
worthy wage at the time of the resurrection, since he was about to charge the
keeping according to the faith. And he did not set the word simply: for if he
brings all things to life, clearly he will also deem the pious men worthy of
the resurrection besides the common bringing to life, giving also the revival
of the dying bodies. Being about then to charge that he keep the commandment
unspotted, he mentions the one who brings to life, that the hope of that
making-alive may exhort toward piety. And it must be noted that he said "who
brings all things to life," not that we should think he also brings the
lifeless to life. Such also is "all things came to be through him": for not
altogether all things, but concerning those of which the word is. 1 Tim 6:16
As the creatures remaining in immortality and incorruption according to his
grace and will and needing the participation from him, even if they obtained
an immortal nature as the angels, but this belonging to him alone in the
proper sense, since he is self-life and the constitution of all things that
are and the supply of every good.
