having heard from them the words spoken against Christ, and of those who have
believed in him, you would blush; but learn also from the other heretics, who
speak and think the same things. Let there be set forth then the dogmas of the
heretics, and the mind of your thinking, and the word of our faith, and of the

Gospel the definition, and the preaching of the apostles, and the testimony of
the prophets, and the understanding of the fulfilled economy. Say then, how do
you say that God has been born from Nazareth, announcing as it were a
beginning of generation of the Godhead according to Paul of Samosata, or
denying the generation of the =26.1140= flesh according to Marcion and the
other heretics, not following the evangelical definition, but wanting to speak
from your own? For this reason you say that God has been begotten from a
Virgin, and not God and man according to the evangelical definition: lest
perhaps, having confessed a generation of flesh, you should say it is natural,
making the word true; but God begotten, yet showing his own flesh as in
appearance. For God is not shown as having a beginning of generation from
Nazareth; but the Word God who exists before the ages was seen from Nazareth
as man, having been born from Mary the Virgin and the Holy Spirit in Bethlehem
of Judea, from the seed of David and Abraham, and of Adam, as it is written;
having taken from the Virgin all things that God from the beginning fashioned
and made for the constitution of man, apart from sin; as also the Apostle
says: "According to likeness in all things, apart from sin," not having shown
a transformation of the Godhead, but having worked a renewal of the manhood,
according to his own will; so that the nations may be fellow-bodied, and
fellow-partakers of Christ, as also the Apostle writes; that both the man may
be God truly, and God may be man truly, that he may be both man truly and God
truly; not of a man being toward God, as you, slandering, say, dragging down
the mystery of the Christians; but of the Only-begotten God having been
well-pleased, by the fullness of his Godhead, to raise up for himself from a
Virgin's womb the fashioning of the archetypal man, and a new making, by a
natural generation and an indissoluble union; that he might accomplish the
saving work on behalf of men, working out the salvation of men in suffering
and death and resurrection. But you say that, if he took all things, then
surely he also had the human reasonings; but it is impossible that in human
reasonings there should not be sin; and how will Christ be without sin? Say
then: If God is creator of the sinful reasonings, his own creation must be
brought to God; for he came that his own making might be brought. But the
judgment condemning the one who sinned will again be unjust; for if God
created sinful reasonings, how does he condemn the one who sinned? and how is
it possible that such a judgment should come from God? But if Adam also was
subject to =26.1141= such reasonings, before he disobeyed the commandment of
God, then how did he not know good and evil? being rational in nature, but
free in reasoning, not knowing the experience of evil, but knowing only the
good, and being as it were of a single manner; but having disobeyed the
commandment of God, he fell under the sinful reasonings; not God having
created the captivating reasonings, but the devil from deceit having sown them
into the rational nature of man, which had come to be in transgression, and
had been thrust away from God; so that the devil both established a law of sin
in the nature of man, and death reigning through the work of sin. For this
reason then the Son of God came, that he might dissolve the works of the
devil. But you say: He dissolved them by not having sinned. But this is not a
dissolution of sin. For the devil did not work sin from the beginning in that
one, so that, when he came into the world, and did not sin, sin might be
dissolved; but having sown it in the rational and intelligent nature of man,
the devil worked sin. For this reason it became impossible for the nature,
being rational and having sinned voluntarily, and having come under a
condemnation of death, to call itself back into freedom, as the Apostle says:
"For the impossibility of the law, in which it was weak through the flesh."
For this reason through himself

the Son of God came to establish this in his own nature from a new beginning
and a wondrous generation; not having divided the original constitution, but
having set aside the sown-in rejection, as the prophet testifies, saying:
"Before the child knows good or evil, he disobeys wickedness so as to choose
the good." But if sinlessness was not shown in the nature that sinned, how was
sin condemned in the flesh, neither the flesh having the practical faculty,
nor the Godhead knowing sin? And how does the Apostle say: "Where sin
abounded, grace superabounded"? not describing a place, but proposing a
nature: "That, as," he says, "through one man sin entered into the world, and
through sin death, so also through one man Jesus Christ grace might reign
through righteousness unto eternal life"; that

through which nature the advance of sin came to be, through it also the
demonstration of righteousness might come to be; and thus the works of the
devil might be dissolved, the nature of men having been set free from sin, and
God might be glorified. =26.1144= But again you say: If Christ is man, he will
be a part of the world; but a part of the world cannot save the world. O
device of deceit, and madness of blasphemy! For let them say of what Scripture
this is the definition, or a sophism of the devil; although the prophet says:
"A brother does not redeem, a man shall redeem"; and elsewhere: "And a man was
born in her, and he himself the Most High founded her." How then does Christ
not save the world, having become man? when indeed it is clear that in the
nature in which sin was practiced, in it also the abundance of grace came to
be. And what is the abundance of grace? that the Word became man, remaining
God; that, even having become man, he might be believed as God; as also being
man, Christ is God, because being God he became man, and saves those who
believe in the form of a man. For if you confess the Lord Jesus with your
mouth, and believe in your heart that God raised him from the dead, you will
be saved. But God does not admit death, nor does he need resurrection, but he
raises from the dead. Whence there was need that God have something which he
might offer on our behalf, whether in death or in life; so that by this rather
the Word saves, that he became man. But again you say: And how can the nature
that has come into a habit of sin, and that has inherited the succession of
sin, be without sin? which is impossible; and Christ too will be as one of
men. And these things Marcion also thought; this opinion Manichaeus also
introduced, ranking the flesh of man and even the generation itself under the
ruler of evil, and inscribing this one as having authority; since "by whatever
one has been overcome, to this he has also been enslaved." You also renew
their opinion, in another manner surrendering in addition the intelligent
nature of man, which is understood as soul, and defining this as inescapable
from sin; even if you have also written a fleshly soul, from where you learned
it I do not also know; for neither is it possible to find this from the holy
Scriptures, nor in the common reasoning of men, the Lord saying: "Do not fear
those who kill the body, but are not able to kill the soul." And how, if the
soul is fleshly according to you, does it not die together with the body, and
perish together with it? And how also did Peter, having named the souls held
=26.1145= in Hades spirits, say: "He went to preach the resurrection to the
spirits shut up in prison"? But you say and name all things contrary to
nature, lest perhaps, recounting the economy according to nature, you should
make the Word true, that the Word also became man truly. For you have said
that it is a voice of God that says: "The mind of man is intent upon the evil
things carefully from youth"; not understanding that, having said "from
youth," he made clear that which is from sowing-in and perishable. For this
reason the Lord swore truth to David, to raise up from the fruit of his belly

Christ according to the flesh; not a man about to be shown from a
transformation of Godhead (since what need was there for the Lord to swear to
David?); but having taken the form of the servant in the allowance of the
generation from woman and of the increase of stature that is according to us,
as the Apostle says: "Since then the children have partaken of blood and
flesh, he himself likewise shared in the same"; "likewise" indeed, because not
from seed, but from Spirit; "of the same," because not from elsewhere, but
from the seed of David and of Abraham, and of Adam, as it is written. How
then, having passed by the holy Scriptures, and the manifestation of the
truth, do you say that if he was not shown as a man from himself, but having
taken a human existence, he became man; he was then joined to a man; and the
crucified one will be a man, the Lord of glory? If then you hear Peter saying:
"This Jesus whom you crucified God made both Christ and Lord," upon what do
you take the making? if upon the Godhead of the Word, why do you still blame
the Arians, thinking the same things as those? but if upon the form of the
servant in the Word, why do you still fight with yourselves? but you have not
believed thus; for again you say: If the nature that sinned has come to be in
God, not having done sin, it is then held by necessity; and that which is held
by necessity is forced. Say then: If not sinning comes about according to
necessity, sinning will be according to nature; you will then give the creator
of the nature as being the worker of sin. But if such a thing is blasphemy,
and sinning has happened according to necessity; it is clear that not sinning
has come about according to nature. For this reason, the form of the servant
having been shown =26.1148= in the Godhead of the Word, not being subject to
necessity, but by nature and power, displays the sinlessness, having dissolved
the definition of necessity, and the law of sin, and having taken captive the
tyrant of the captivity, as the prophet says: "You went up on high, you led
captivity captive." For the Word, putting forward the form of the servant
against the enemy, has made the victory through the one who was once defeated.
For this reason also Jesus completed all the temptation, because he took all
the things that had had experience of temptation, through which he has made
the victory on behalf of men, saying: "Take courage, I have overcome the
world." For the devil did not raise the war against Godhead, which he did not
know, nor did he dare; for this reason he said: "If you are the Son of God";
but against a man, whom of old he was able to deceive, and from that one he
extended the working of his wickedness to all men. And the soul of Adam being
held in a condemnation of death, and crying out to its own master continually,
and those who were well-pleasing to God, and were justified in the natural
law, being held together with Adam, both mourning together and crying out
together, God, having pitied the man whom he made, was well-pleased through a
mystery of manifestation to work a salvation of newness for the race of men,
and to make a taking-down of the enemy who deceived through envy, and to show
forth an unreckonable exaltation of the man by the union and communion with
the Most High in nature and truth. For this reason the Word, being God, and
creator of the first man, came to become man, for the making-alive of the man,
and for the taking-down of the unjust enemy; and he was born from a woman,
having raised up in himself from the first fashioning the form of man, in a
showing of flesh without fleshly wills and human reasonings, in an image of
newness. For the will is of Godhead alone; since also the whole nature of the
Word is, in a showing of the human form and of the seen flesh of the second
Adam, not in a division of persons, but in an existence of Godhead and
manhood. For this reason the devil approached Jesus as a man, but not finding
in him a mark of his old sowing-in, nor a progress of the =26.1149= present
attempt, he was defeated, being put to shame and being overcome; and

being exhausted he said: "Who is this who has come from Edom," that is, from a
land of men, "crossing with force with strength?" Wherefore also the Lord
said: "The ruler of this world comes, and in me he finds nothing." And yet we
have learned that the second Adam has both soul, and body, and the whole
first. For if the "nothing" was concerning an existence of man, how did he
find the body that was seen, when he had said "nothing"? But he did not find
in him the things which he himself worked in the first Adam; and thus sin was
dissolved in Christ; for this reason also the Scripture testifies: "Who did
not do sin, nor was deceit found in his mouth." How then do you say that it is
impossible for the man once taken captive to become not taken captive? that
you may bring the impossible to God, but the possible to the devil, saying
that his sin is undissolved in the nature of men, as also the other heretics;
and that for this reason the Godhead that is not taken captive has come, in a
likeness of flesh and soul, that it itself might remain not taken captive, and
thus a pure righteousness might be shown. When then was the righteousness of
the Godhead not pure? And what benefaction of men is there in these things, if
the Lord was not shown in identity of existence and newness of nature, as the
Apostle says: "Which he dedicated for us, a fresh and living way"; saying: "I
am the way, and the life, and the truth"? But you say that those who believe
are saved by likeness and by imitation, and not by the renewal, and by the
firstfruits. And how did he become firstborn among many brothers, and
firstfruits of those who have fallen asleep is Christ, who is head of the body
of the Church? For neither would faith appearing according to what is clear be
called faith; but faith is that which believes the impossible in power, and
the weak in strength, and the passible in impassibility, and the corruptible
in incorruption, and the mortal in immortality. This mystery is great, as the
Apostle says; but I speak, of Christ, and of the Church. For the Godhead did
not come to justify itself, for it did not sin. But being rich he became poor
for us, that we by that one's poverty might become rich. And how did God
become poor? =26.1152= because he took up in himself the nature that had
become poor, putting this forward in his own righteousness as suffering on
behalf of men and being above men, and having been manifested from men, and
having become wholly God's. For if he had not become firstborn among many
brothers, how would he have been shown firstborn from the dead? How then do
you say, God who suffered through flesh and rose? Alas for the unfittingness
and the blasphemy! Such daring belongs to Arians. For those put forward such
blasphemy without fear, having learned to name the Son of God as God in
pretence; although the Scripture teaches that through God the suffering came
to be in his flesh, and not that God suffered through flesh. You then who
promise beforehand the confession of the consubstantiality, how do you drag
down into suffering the undivided name; dragging down into suffering the
undivided nature, the unspeakable Godhead, the unchangeable and unalterable
consubstantiality, and confessing from resurrection? For if the Word, having
transformed flesh from himself, advanced as far as suffering, having taken on
in himself nothing that admits the passible or resurrection, he himself would
be the one who suffered and rose from the dead; and the suffering will be
common to the Trinity according to Valentinus, the Word being undivided from
the Father according to the divine nature. But if you wish to understand these
things thus, what is the promise through the prophets, or the genealogy of the
evangelists, or the testimony of the apostles, or the presentation of the
mother Mary, or the increase of the stature, or the showing of eating, or the
indication of the fellow-feeling according to all things, or the
bringing-forward of the name, or that the Son of God became son of man, or
that a man, Jesus Christ, is the one who gave himself a ransom on our behalf?
or that the Son of man must suffer many things and be killed, and on the third
day rise from the dead? But if you do not believe that Christ is passible,
since he is man;

but impassible, since he is God; but being straitened you reason that, if you
confess Christ as God and man, you no longer say one, but two; it is necessary
for you either to say that the economy of the suffering and of the death and
of the resurrection is appearance, according to Marcion and the other
heretics, or to name the Godhead of the Word passible, according to Arius and
those after him. =26.1153= For if, reading the divine Scriptures, you
understood how in law and prophets, Gospels and apostles, they everywhere
first address the Lord as man, and then bring on the suffering, lest they ever
send forth a blasphemous word against the Godhead; wherefore they have not
spoken a generation of the Godhead of the Word, but they confess a Father, and
they preach the Son; but from Mary they give the genealogy of Christ according
to the flesh as son of David and of Joseph by the taking-on of the form of the
servant, that his humanity might be believed as from men, and God Word from
God the Father might be confessed, bearing the sufferings on behalf of men,
displaying the impassibility by the passible formation from men in the
suffering body, and the immortality in the dying, and the incorruption in the
one being buried, and the victory in the one tempted, and the newness in the
one that had grown old; that "our old man was crucified together"; for this is
the grace; but nowhere does Godhead admit suffering apart from a suffering
body; nor does it display turmoil and grief apart from a soul grieving and
being troubled; nor is it distressed and does it pray apart from an
intelligence being distressed and praying; but even if the things said did not
happen by a defeat of nature, yet the things that came to be were done by a
showing of existence; how then have you written that God is the one who
suffered through flesh and rose? for if God is the one who suffered through
flesh and rose, you will say that both the Father is passible, and the
Paraclete, the name being one, and the divine nature one. And from this saying
it is possible to see your mind, of what sort it is, as of those not fearing
God, nor being persuaded by the divine Scriptures; Moses writing concerning
God, that "Our God is a consuming fire"; but concerning his sojourn in flesh,
he says that the Lord will be raised up as a prophet from your brothers, and
the life hanging upon a tree as of the body of the Lord, becoming life for us;
and Isaiah also cries, saying concerning God: "The great God, the eternal, who
constructed the ends of the earth, will not hunger nor grow weary," =26.1156=
and the rest; but concerning the suffering: "A man being in a blow, and
knowing how to bear infirmity." And what is the, "Knowing how to bear
infirmity"? that from the one who had suffered the passible was displayed. For
this reason also, "Write," he says, "with a man's stylus in the new volume,"
and not of a formless flesh. And the Apostle also says: "A man, Christ Jesus,
who gave himself on our behalf"; wherefore also he brings the resurrection to
the one from the seed of David, saying "according to the flesh"; but
concerning his Godhead: "For the Word of God is living, and active, and
sharper than every two-edged sword." And the Lord also sets the suffering upon
the Son of man, showing that according to the flesh; but concerning his
Godhead: "I and the Father are one"; and, "No one knows the Son, who he is,
except the Father alone." But nowhere have the Scriptures handed down blood of
God apart from flesh, or God having suffered through flesh and risen. Such
daring belongs to Arians, since they do not confess the Son of God as true
God. But the holy Scriptures, in flesh of God and of flesh of God who became
man, preach blood, and suffering, and resurrection of a body of God, a
resurrection that came to be from the dead. But you say the reverse, as wiser
than the apostles, and more mystical than the prophets, and more authoritative
than the evangelists, or even more authentic than the Lord; by your false
fair-speech denying the truth, but speaking against the Godhead, the economy
being shown clearly even upon the cross, being confirmed in a putting-forth of
blood of flesh; but of a voice

having come to be and a soul being signified, not making clear a separation of
Godhead, but signifying a death of body, neither the Godhead of the body being
left behind in the tomb, nor the soul being separated in Hades. For this is
what was said through the prophets: "You will not abandon my soul to Hades,
nor will you give your holy one to see corruption." Wherefore also the Lord
said: "No one takes it from me; but I lay it down from myself"; that is, being
present I display it. For this reason in the soul of God the hold of death was
dissolved, and the resurrection from Hades came to be, and =26.1157= the
gospel was preached to the souls; but in the body of Christ the corruption was
made void, and the incorruption from a tomb was shown. So that a man of God
was not being separated, nor did God recount an abandonment toward a man, nor
was the death and the withdrawal of the spirit a transfer of God from a body,
but a separation of soul from body; for our death was being described there.
But if God was separated from the body, and thus the death was shown, how did
the body, having been separated from the incorruptible God, display the
incorruption? And how also did the Word make the going into Hades? or how did
he display the resurrection from Hades? unless perhaps he himself was rising
instead of our soul, that he might fit the image of our resurrection? And how
is it possible to think these things concerning God? Your definition then is
out of accord with the holy Scriptures, and your mind is unfitted to the
fulfilled economy. And the, "Sit at my right hand," is not the dignity of a
man, but of God. But since the dignity of God has become a dignity of a man,
that the dignity of the man might be believed as a dignity of God, it has been
said: "Sit at my right hand," and, "Glorify me, Father, with the eternal
glory." He does not say this as having been separated from the glory, but as
having come to be in an inglorious body, that he might show that the form of
the servant is not being separated from the divine glory, but is displaying
this. Wherefore he says: "And I have glorified, and I will glorify again,"
showing one glory before body and in body, as the Apostle says: "Having become
so much better than the angels, as he has inherited a more excellent name than
they. For to which of the angels did he ever say: Sit at my right hand?" By no
means did the Word, the maker of the angels, become better, as being lesser;
but having displayed the form of the servant that arose in him as better than
the angels, or even than all the creation; since, being image of the invisible
God, he also became firstborn of all creation, as it is written also in
Gospels: "Until she bore her son, the firstborn." Wherefore also in him all
things were created, and in him the suffering came to be, and he himself is
the liberator of the suffering and of the death, and through him all things
came to be, and he himself is the head of the body of the Church, who is
firstborn from the dead, that he might become, it says, in all things himself
holding first place. How then do you say that the Word, the creator of the
rational natures, having mixed flesh with himself, has become a rational man?
and how unchangeable and unalterable =26.1160=, and not having constituted the
form of the servant as rational, has he become man, that both the Word might
be unchangeable remaining what he was, and the rational man might be seen upon
earth being God? For the Lord is a heavenly man, not having displayed the
flesh from heaven, but having constituted that from earth as heavenly.
Wherefore also as is the heavenly, such also are the heavenly ones according
to his participation of the holiness. Wherefore also the things of the body
have been made his own. But again you say: How did they crucify the Lord of
glory, but they did not crucify the Word according to you? May it not be! but
they set aside the Word, having nailed the body of the Word to a cross. For
God is the one who was set aside; but of flesh of God and of soul the
suffering, and the death, and the resurrection came to be. For this reason the
Lord said to the Jews: "Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise
it"; as the prophet says: "In return for which his soul was delivered over to
death," and not the Word himself. And John also says: "He laid down his soul
on our behalf." How then

were the Jews able to destroy a temple of God, and to separate the
indissoluble mixing of the flesh that came to be toward the Word, if indeed
according to you the death of the body had this manner. For neither would the
body have died, if it was not separated from something. For if a dissolving
did not come to be, neither death; but if death did not come to be, neither
did resurrection come to be. Give then the dissolving and the separation that
came to be from the body, as it is written in Gospels, that "He expired," and
that "Having bowed the head he delivered over the spirit," that we may see
what spirit you have understood to have withdrawn from the body, and the death
to have come to be. For you have said that the Word, having mixed with himself
the unhypostatic flesh, showed the truly rational and perfect man. If then the
Word withdrew from the body, and thus the death came to be, the Jews prevailed
against God, having dissolved the indissoluble mixing. Neither then did our
death come to be there, if the death of the body came to be when God was
separated. And how also did the body of the incorruptible God, having been
separated, remain in incorruption? And the wounding will be of the body, but
the suffering of the Word. For this reason you also say that God suffered,
speaking consistently with yourselves, or rather in accord with the Arians;
for this those men dogmatize. And the Word according to you will also be
raised from resurrection. For it is necessary that someone have taken the
beginning of the resurrection from Hades, that the resurrection may be
perfect, and the dissolving of the death, and the release of the spirits
there. =26.1161= But if the Word suffered these things, then where is the
unchangeable and unalterable of the Word? And how, having been shown without
covering, was the Word in Hades thought in the death as a man? And how also
did the Lord say to the Jews: "I will raise it," and not, "I rise from Hades"?
For if the Word, having come to be in death, had need of the one raising him
from death, the victory will not be his, but of the one raising him. And how
also was it signified beforehand through the prophets concerning his soul?
How, being present, did the Lord say, fulfilling the promise: "I lay down my
soul for my sheep"? which soul the holy Scriptures clearly teach is a spirit;
and the Lord also having said: the body indeed being killed by men, but they
not being able to kill the soul, inasmuch as it is a spirit. With this Spirit
Jesus was troubled; of this Spirit the withdrawal from the body came to be
upon the cross. And thus the body was being put to death, and of it the
dissolving came to be, God the Word remaining unmovedly toward both the
body, and toward the soul, and toward himself being in the bosom of the Father
in a showing of unchangeableness. But in the form that came to be in him
according to us he was describing our death there; that in it he might also
fit the resurrection on our behalf, displaying the soul from Hades, but
the body from
a tomb; that in death he might dissolve the death in a showing of soul, but in
the tomb he might make void the corruption in a burial of body; displaying the
immortality and the incorruption from Hades and from a tomb, having traveled
our road in a form according to us, and having dissolved the hold that had
come to be against us. And this was the wondrous thing; for this has become
the grace. But you, confessing flesh alone in addition, are able neither to
show the condemnation of sin, nor the dissolving of death, nor the completion
of the resurrection, nor the unchangeableness of the Word; since you have
stepped outside the holy Scriptures, speaking the things which Arians
sophistically invent, the word concerning soul being clear in holy Scriptures,
and the economy having been fulfilled in a showing of all fullness and
completion. But some of the heretics, confessing the one who was seen,
disbelieve his Godhead; but those who confess God deny the generation of his
flesh; =26.1164= but those who confess flesh in addition deny the showing of
his soul; and they have become according to the mad children of the Arians,
who weave together the knotted and crooked propositions, that by these

being at a loss, they may find the simple, themselves being without resource
of the faith. Likewise they also have learned to say: What is the one born
from Mary? God, or man? Then if he should say, Man, he will disbelieve his
Godhead, becoming in accord with the heretics who have disbelieved this; but
if he should say, God, he will deny the generation of his flesh, and will be
gathered with the heretics who deny this. Then again in turn: Who is the one
who suffered? who is the one crucified? God, or man? that, if he should say,
God, he may send forth a blasphemous word according to the impious Arians; but
if he should say, Man, he may speak again with a Jewish supposition. For this
reason the holy Scriptures preach the Word as God unspeakably from the Father,
and this one in the last times born as man from a Virgin; that neither God may
be disbelieved, nor a generation of flesh denied. And where the name of flesh
is, there is the harmony of all the constitution apart from sin. And upon the
name of the man they set the suffering, and they do not go beyond, as it is
written in the holy Scriptures; but concerning the Godhead of the Word they

is theologized, but a man is given in genealogy, that toward both he may be
the same naturally and truly; God indeed toward the eternity of the Godhead
and the creating of the creation; but man toward the generation from woman,
and the increase of the stature. And God indeed toward the life-giving
benefactions, and powerful in the wonder-workings; but man toward the
like-mannered fellow-feelings, and the weaknesses according to us. And God
Word indeed in a showing of the immortality, and incorruption, and
unchangeableness; but man in the nailing of the cross, and a flowing of blood,
and a burial of body, and a descent into Hades, and the resurrection from the
dead. Thus Christ has been raised from the dead, and being God he raises the
dead. =26.1165= Vain then are those who bring suffering upon his Godhead, or
who disbelieve his becoming-man, or who say the one is two, or who attempt to
make the measuring of his flesh, and dare to say the how-much, or the how,
contrary to the Scriptures. For by such suspicions the mind of the heretics
has slipped; of Marcion indeed having turned aside through excess of
blasphemy; but of Manichaeus having been turned through a suspicion of sin;
but of Valentinus having been led astray through a pretext of knowledge; but
of Paul of Samosata, and of the one called Photinus, and of those
